# r4.02.04 Fluid–structure coupling with a free surface

**Summary:**

Fluid/structure coupling is presented here in the case where the fluid has a free surface. Free-surface elements have been implemented in *Code_Aster*  in order to compute the sloshing modes of a fluid coupled to an elastic structure for a three-dimensional problem.

## 2 Introduction

In order to study the behaviour of structures filled with fluid, one may be led to take sloshing phenomena into account, that is to add to the coupled fluid–structure system the effect of gravity at the level of the free surface of the liquid. The structures concerned are, for example, the vessels of nuclear power plants of the fast-reactor line, and the fuel-storage pools [bib4].

The developments already carried out in fluid–structure coupling [bib3] have therefore been completed by the introduction of new surface elements which take into account, in their formulation, the effect of gravity.

## 3 Theoretical formulation of the problem

The interaction problem of a structure with a heavy fluid amounts to solving three problems simultaneously:

- the structure is subjected to a pressure field $P$ imposed by the fluid on the wall $\Sigma$ ;
- the fluid is subjected to a displacement field ${X}_{s}$ imposed by the structure on $\Sigma$ ;
- gravity acts on the free surface, where $p=\rho gz$ .

One will consider at first that the fluid is not heavy, before introducing gravity in paragraph [§3.2].

### 3.1 Reminders on fluid–structure coupling

In order to give a proper account of the fluid–structure interaction, we shall analyse separately the equations governing the behaviour of the fluid and those which govern that of the structure, without considering in this chapter the boundary conditions concerning the free surface.

#### 3.1.1 Description of the fluid

One considers that the system studied is subjected to small perturbations about its equilibrium state, in which the fluid and the structure are at rest: thus, $P={p}_{0}+p$ and ${\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{s}}={\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{s}}({x}_{0}=0)$ . This makes it possible to write [bib2]:

$\rho =-{\rho}_{f}div({\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{f}})$ whence $p=-{\rho}_{f}{c}^{2}div({\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{f}})$ .

With:

- $p$ the fluctuating pressure of the fluid,
- $\rho$ the perturbation of the density of the fluid, ${\rho}_{f}$ the density of the fluid at rest,
- ${x}_{f}(r,t)$ the displacement field of a fluid particle.

The fluid is:

- perfect (that is, non-viscous)
- barotropic:

$p=\rho {c}^{2}$                                 eq 3.1.1-1

- and irrotational: there exists a displacement potential $\varphi$ , such that $p={\rho}_{f}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\varphi }{\partial {t}^{2}}$

The behaviour of the Eulerian fluid volume is therefore described by the following equations:

- constitutive law:

${T}_{ij}=-p{\delta}_{ij}$

- equation of conservation of momentum in the fluid in the absence of a source:

$div(\overline{\overline{T}})={\rho}_{f}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{x}_{f}}{\partial {t}^{2}}$                             eq 3.1.1-2

- equation of conservation of mass:

$\frac{\partial \rho }{\partial t}+{\rho}_{f}div(\frac{\partial {x}_{s}}{\partial t})=0$                         eq 3.1.1-3

By combining the equations of conservation of momentum [eq 3.1.1-2] and of mass [eq 3.1.1-3] written in the harmonic regime at the angular frequency $w$ , one obtains, by virtue of [eq 3.1.1-1], the Helmholtz equation:

$\Delta p+\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}p=0$

#### 3.1.2 Description of the structure

One considers that the structure is linear elastic and that one remains in the domain of small perturbations. In view of these assumptions, one writes:

- the constitutive law in linear elasticity:

${\sigma}_{ij}={C}_{ijkl}{\varepsilon}_{kl}$

- the equation of conservation of momentum in the structure in the absence of body forces other than the inertia forces:

$div(\overline{\overline{\sigma}})={\rho}_{s}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{x}_{s}}{\partial {t}^{2}}$

- the compatibility equation on the strain tensor:

${\varepsilon}_{kl}=\frac{1}{2}({u}_{k,l}+{u}_{l,k})$

#### 3.1.3 Description of the fluid–structure interface

At the interface ($\Sigma$ ) between the fluid and the structure, since the fluid is not viscous, there is continuity of the normal stresses and of the velocities normal to the wall, and vanishing of the tangential stress (absence of viscous friction). These boundary conditions are written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{i}={T}_{ij}{n}_{i}=-p{\delta}_{ij}{n}_{i}\\ \frac{\partial {x}_{f}}{\partial t}.n=\frac{\partial {x}_{s}}{\partial t}.n\end{array}$

##### 3.1.3.1 Formulation of the coupled problem

Finally, the equation of the coupled fluid–structure problem is written, taking $p$ as the variable describing the pressure field in the fluid and ${x}_{s}$ the displacement field in the structure:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,\mathrm{lj}}}+{\omega}^{2}{\rho}_{s}{x}_{{s}_{i}}=0& \text{dans}{V}_{s}\\ \Delta p+\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}p=0& \text{dans}{V}_{f}\\ {\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{i}={C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,l}}{n}_{i}=-p{\delta}_{ij}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}\Sigma \\ \frac{\partial p}{\partial n}={\rho}_{f}{\omega}^{2}{x}_{{f}_{i}}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}\Sigma \end{array}$

The displacement fields ${x}_{s}$ for the structure and the pressure fields $p$ for the fluid which are sought minimise the functional:

$L({x}_{s},p,z)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{{V}_{s}}{\int}\left[{\sigma}_{ij}({x}_{s}){\varepsilon}_{ij}({x}_{s})-{\rho}_{s}{\omega}^{2}{x}_{s}^{2}\right]-\underset{\Sigma}{\int}p{x}_{s}nd\Sigma +\frac{1}{2{\rho}_{f}}\underset{{V}_{f}}{\int}\left[\frac{1}{{\omega}^{2}}(\text{grad}p{)}^{2}-\frac{{p}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}\right]\text{dV}$

### 3.2 Action of gravity on the free surface

#### 3.2.1 Formulation of the problem

The linearised dynamic equations describing the small motions of a perfect fluid are recalled here. An Eulerian description of the fluid is chosen:

$\text{grad}P={\rho}_{f}(\mathrm{g}-\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathrm{s}}}{\partial {t}^{2}})$ in ${V}_{f}$

At equilibrium the fluid particle was at ${M}_{0}$ and therefore: $\text{grad}{P}_{0}={\rho}_{f}\mathrm{g}$ in ${V}_{f}$ .

Motions of small amplitude about the equilibrium state are envisaged (this is the small-perturbation assumption): then $M={M}_{0}+{x}_{f}({M}_{0},t)$

Let $p$ be the Eulerian pressure fluctuation and ${p}_{L}$ the Lagrangian pressure fluctuation; then:

$\begin{array}{}p(M,t)=P({M}_{0},t)-{P}_{0}({M}_{0})\\ {p}_{L}=P(M,t)-{P}_{0}({M}_{0})\end{array}$

In view of the small-displacement assumption:

$\begin{array}{}{p}_{L}-p=\text{gradP}({M}_{o},t){x}_{f}({M}_{o},t)\\ \phantom{{p}_{L}-p}=-{\rho}_{f}g{x}_{f}({M}_{o},t)\end{array}$                     eq 3.2.1-1

If one considers the case of a heavy fluid presenting a free surface in contact with a medium at constant pressure ${P}_{\text{atm}}$ , one may write, neglecting the effects of surface tension:

$P(M,t)={P}_{\text{atm}}$ on the free surface $\text{SL}$ , that is: ${p}_{L}=0$ . Hence, with [eq 3.2.1-1], $p={\rho}_{f}g({x}_{f}.z)$

In view of the small-motion assumption, the instantaneous inclination of the tangent plane is a first-order infinitesimal. ${x}_{f}.z$ therefore coincides, to within a second-order quantity, with the vertical elevation $h$ .

![Figure](/figures/r4.02.04/1000040C000021B100000B44075E40BAD2C6355F.svg)

**Figure 3.2.1-a: approximation on the free surface**

Thus, if one adds to the boundary conditions the gravity condition on the free surface, this amounts to considering at $z=H$ the linearised condition:

$p={\rho}_{f}gz$                             eq 3.2.1-2

The equations of the global problem become:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,\text{lj}}}+{\omega}^{2}{\rho}_{s}{x}_{{s}_{i}}=0& \text{dans}{V}_{s}\\ \Delta p+\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}p=0& \text{dans}{V}_{f}\\ {\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{i}={C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,l}}{n}_{i}=-p{\delta}_{ij}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}\Sigma \\ \frac{\partial p}{\partial n}={\rho}_{f}{\omega}^{2}{x}_{{f}_{i}}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}\Sigma \text{et}\text{sur}\text{SL}\\ p={\rho}_{f}gz& \text{sur}\text{SL}\end{array}$

In order to express the functional, one uses the constitutive law on the free surface. Considering an admissible displacement field $\mathit{dz}$ one obtains [bib2]:

$\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}\rho gz\delta z\text{ds}=\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}p\delta z\text{ds}$

That is, finally, the functional of the total fluid–structure system subjected to gravity:

$\begin{array}{}L({x}_{s},p,z)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{{V}_{s}}{\int}\left[{\sigma}_{ij}({x}_{s}){\varepsilon}_{ij}({x}_{s})-{\rho}_{s}{\omega}^{2}{x}_{s}^{2}\right]-\underset{\Sigma}{\int}p{x}_{s}.nd\Sigma +\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{f}\underset{{V}_{f}}{\int}\left[\frac{1}{{\omega}^{2}}(\text{grad}p{)}^{2}-\frac{{p}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}\right]\text{dV}\\ \phantom{L({x}_{s},p,z)}+\frac{1}{2}\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}\rho g{z}^{2}\text{dS}-\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}pz\text{dS}\end{array}$

This taking into account of gravity implies two additional terms in the functional describing the fluid:

- a potential-energy term linked to the free surface: $\frac{1}{2}\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}\rho g{z}^{2}\text{ds}$
- a term due to the work of the hydrodynamic pressure in the displacement of the free surface: $\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}pz\text{ds}$

However, it must be noted that this is not the only effect of gravity, since at every point of the wall $\Sigma$ there acts a permanent pressure $-\rho gz$ (where $z$ is the altitude of the point $M$ considered: one assumes that $z=0$ at the level of the free surface at equilibrium). The point $M$ is endowed with an infinitesimal motion ${X}_{s}$ , the surface element $d\Sigma$ therefore varies and so does the force due to the permanent pressure. This force is responsible for an additional stiffness term which is added to the structural stiffness in the system. It could cause buckling of the structure by cancelling the structural stiffness. This effect is negligible for the vibratory characteristics ([bib2], [bib1]), and it is therefore not taken into account.

#### 3.2.2 Discretisation by finite elements

In order to obtain the discretised form of the functional, one replaces each integral by a sum of integrals over each element $i$ of the discretised system, then one uses a finite-element approximation of the unknown displacement and pressure functions on each element $i$ [bib18].

The unknowns are ${\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{s}}(u,v,w),p,z$ ; one then has, setting $\text{Ni}$ the shape functions (or nodal interpolation functions on the element $i$ ):

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\sigma =D\varepsilon \\ \varepsilon =Bu\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{s}={N}_{i}u\\ p(x,y,z)={N}_{i}p\\ \nabla p=\overline{{N}_{i}}p\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{s}.n={N}_{\Sigma i}u\\ z={N}_{\mathrm{Si}}z\end{array}$

where $\delta ,p$ , are the unknowns at the structural nodes and at the fluid nodes, and $z$ the unknowns on the free surface.

Whence the discretised expression of the functional associated with the problem:

$L={u}^{t}(K-{\omega}^{2}M)u+{p}^{t}(\frac{H}{{\rho}_{f}{\omega}^{2}}-\frac{Q}{{\rho}_{f}{c}^{2}})p+{z}^{t}{\rho}_{f}g{K}_{z}z-2{p}^{t}{M}_{z}z-2{p}^{t}Cu$

with

| $K=\sum_{i}\underset{\text{Vi}}{\int}{N}_{i}^{t}{B}_{i}^{t}{D}_{i}{B}_{i}{N}_{i}{\text{dV}}_{i}$ | stiffness matrix of the structure |
| --- | --- |
| $M=\sum_{i}\underset{\text{Si}}{\int}{N}_{i}^{t}{\rho}_{f}{N}_{i}{\text{dV}}_{i}$ | mass matrix of the structure |

and

| $Q=\sum_{i}\underset{{\text{Vi}}_{\text{fl}}}{\int}{N}_{i}^{t}{N}_{i}{\text{dV}}_{i}$ | ${M}_{z}=\sum_{i}\underset{\mathrm{Si}}{\int}{N}_{\Sigma i}^{t}{N}_{\Sigma i}{\text{dS}}_{i}$ |
| --- | --- |
| ${K}_{z}=\sum_{i}\underset{\text{Si}}{\int}{N}_{\text{Si}}^{t}{N}_{\text{Si}}{\text{dS}}_{i}$ | ${M}_{z}=\sum_{i}\underset{\text{Si}}{\int}{N}_{i}^{t}{N}_{\text{Si}}{\text{dS}}_{i}$ |
| $H=\sum_{i}\underset{{\text{Vi}}_{\text{fl}}}{\int}\overline{{N}_{i}^{t}}\overline{{N}_{i}}{\text{dV}}_{i}$ |  |

where $c$ is the speed of sound in the fluid, ${\rho}_{f}$ the density of the fluid, and where ${K}_{f}$ corresponds to the potential energy of the fluid, ${K}_{z}$ to the potential energy of the free surface, $H$ to the kinetic energy of the fluid, $M$ to the fluid–solid coupling and ${M}_{z}$ to the coupling $p-z$ on the free surface.

The finite-element approximation of the complete problem then leads to the following matrix system:

$\left[\begin{array}{ccc}K& -C& 0\\ 0& H& 0\\ 0& -{M}_{z}& {K}_{z}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u\\ p\\ z\end{array}\right\rbrace -{\omega}^{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}M& 0& 0\\ {\rho}_{f}C& \frac{Q}{{c}^{2}}& {\rho}_{f}{M}_{z}\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u\\ p\\ z\end{array}\right\rbrace =0$

The first equation corresponds to the motion of the structure subjected to the pressure forces, the second to that of the motion of the fluid coupled to the structure and to the free surface, and the third is the free-surface equation.

However, the problem written in this way possesses non-symmetric mass and stiffness matrices, which prevents the use of the classical solution algorithms of *Code_Aster* .

#### 3.2.3 Introduction of an additional variable

In order to make the problem symmetric and to be able to use the classical solution methods, one introduces an additional variable: the displacement potential in the fluid $j$ [bib2].

${\mathrm{X}}_{\mathrm{f}}=\text{grad}\varphi$ i.e. $\rho {\omega}^{2}\varphi =p$

This additional unknown is linked to the unknowns of the problem, which leads to a singular stiffness matrix.

One reformulates the coupled heavy fluid–structure problem:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,\text{lj}}}+{\omega}^{2}{\rho}_{s}{x}_{{s}_{i}}=0& \text{dans}{V}_{s}\\ \Delta \varphi +\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{\rho}_{f}{c}^{2}}p=0& \text{dans}{V}_{f}\\ p={\rho}_{f}{\omega}^{2}\varphi & \text{dans}{V}_{f}\\ {\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{i}={C}_{ijkl}{x}_{{s}_{k,l}}{n}_{i}=-{\rho}_{f}{\omega}^{2}\varphi {\delta}_{ij}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}S\\ \frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial n}={x}_{{f}_{i}}{n}_{i}& \text{sur}S\\ p={\rho}_{f}gz& \text{sur}\text{SL}\end{array}$

> Which leads to the functional of the coupled system:

$\begin{array}{}L({x}_{s},p,j,z)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{{V}_{s}}{\int}\left[{\sigma}_{ij}({x}_{s}){\varepsilon}_{ij}({x}_{s})-{\rho}_{s}{\omega}^{2}{x}_{s}^{2}\right]+\frac{1}{2}\underset{{V}_{f}}{\int}\frac{{p}^{2}}{{c}^{2}}\text{dV}+\frac{1}{2}\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}\rho g{z}^{2}\text{ds}\\ \phantom{L({x}_{s},p,j,z)}-{\omega}^{2}\left[\underset{\Sigma}{\int}{\rho}_{f}\varphi {x}_{s}nd\Sigma +\underset{\text{SL}}{\int}{\rho}_{f}\varphi z\text{ds}+\underset{{V}_{f}}{\int}\left[\frac{{\rho}_{f}}{2}(\text{grad}\varphi {)}^{2}+\frac{p\varphi }{{c}^{2}}\right]\text{dV}\right]\end{array}$

> That is, upon discretising:

$\begin{array}{}L={\delta}^{t}(K-{\omega}^{2}M)\delta +\frac{1}{{\rho}_{f}{c}^{2}}{p}^{t}Qp+{\rho}_{f}g{z}^{t}{K}_{z}z\\ \phantom{\text{L=}}-2{\omega}^{2}\left[\frac{{\rho}_{f}}{2}{\varphi}^{t}H\varphi +{\rho}_{f}{\varphi}^{t}C\delta +{\rho}_{f}{\varphi}^{t}{M}_{z}z+\frac{1}{{c}^{2}}{p}^{t}Q\varphi \right]\end{array}$

Which is written, in matrix form:

$\left[\begin{array}{cccc}K& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{Q}{{\rho}_{f}{c}^{2}}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& {\rho}_{f}g{K}_{z}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta \\ p\\ \varphi \\ z\end{array}\right]-{\omega}^{2}\left[\begin{array}{cccc}M& 0& {\rho}_{f}C& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{Q}{{c}^{2}}& 0\\ {\rho}_{f}{C}^{t}& \frac{{Q}^{t}}{{c}^{2}}& {\rho}_{f}H& {\rho}_{f}{M}_{z}\\ 0& 0& {\rho}_{f}{M}_{z}^{t}& 0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta \\ p\\ \varphi \\ z\end{array}\right]=0$

## 4 Implementation in Code_Aster

The finite-element library of *Code_Aster*  has been enriched with five isoparametric surface elements having as degrees of freedom the deflection of the free surface and the displacement potential of the fluid at the free surface. They are compatible with the 3D elements which treat the fluid/structure coupling problem [bib3]

One names:

MEFP_FACE3 and MEFP_FACE6 respectively the triangles with 3 or with 6 nodes,

MEFP_FACE4, MEFP_FACE8 and MEFP_FACE9 respectively the quadrilaterals with 4, 8 or with 9 nodes.

These elements belong to the modelling 2D_FLUI_PESA of the phenomenon MECANIQUE.
