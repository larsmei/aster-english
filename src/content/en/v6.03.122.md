# v6.03.122 SSNP122 - Traction. Model of Rousselier in local and non-local versions

**Summary:**

This quasi-static test consists of applying a traction load to a bar. Two versions of the Rousselier model are used with the kinematics of the large deformations of Simo and Miehe: the local and non-local versions (model with gradient of internal variables). For a tensile test, the gradient terms are not activated: so we find the results of the local version, at least as long as the solution of the local version remains homogeneous (no localization).

The bar is modeled by a quadrangular element (QUAD8) in flat deformation.

The results obtained are non-regression results. Both models give identical results until the local version solution locates. The gradient model solution remains homogeneous throughout the loading path.

## 2 Baseline

We don't have reference solution.

- As long as the solution of the local model remains homogeneous for the various points of Gauss, i.e. for $t=\mathrm{0.93s}$ , the results obtained with *Code_Aster* for this model will be taken as reference solution. For the results of the gradient model, the results obtained with the local version must be exactly found. At this moment, the Cauchy ${\sigma}_{yy}$ stress in the $y$ direction will be tested for both models, the Cauchy ${\sigma}_{zz}$ stress in the $z$ direction, the cumulative plastic deformation $p$ and the $f$ porosity , calculated at the point of Gauss #1 of the mesh cell [1,2,3,4].
- For the gradient model, the response must remain homogeneous in the Gauss points throughout the loading path. To test this result, the Cauchy ${\sigma}_{yy}$ stress in the y direction at Gauss No. 1, 2, 3 and 4 points of the mesh cell [1,2,3,4] and at the final moment will be adopted as reference solution.
