# v2.03.200 SDLP200 – Comparison of the holding of a homogeneous embankment between POST_NEWMARK and GeoSlope

**Summary:**

This test compares the static and dynamic safety factor estimates for a homogeneous POST_NEWMARK embankment with GeoSlope software, the solution of which is part of the verification folder of this software for the Newmark method [1].

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 The purpose of this study is to determine the seismic response of a homogeneous embankment and to calculate the evolution of the safety factor during the earthquake along a pre-determined slipline.

### 1.2 To this end, the modelling is broken down into three parts:

- Static calculation by own weight to determine initial static stresses,
- Dynamic calculation of the earthquake to determine the evolution of the mechanical stresses in the embankment over time,
- Call to POST_NEWMARK macro-command for determination of the evolution of the average acceleration, irreversible displacements and the evolution of the safety factor during the earthquake.

### 1.3 Geometry

A uniform embankment of 10 m high and slope 1:1 (45°) is considered. A sliding circle joining the top and bottom of the embankment is considered, with radius 22.43 m and centre (46.11, 31.58).

### 1.5 Boundary conditions and mechanical loadings

#### 1.5.1 boundary condition

Static calculation:

- *Base*: zero displacement in vertical direction (DY)
- *Right and left sides*: zero displacement according to horizontal direction (DX)

Dynamic calculation:

  - *Base,* *right and left edges* add to elements paraaxial (D_PLAN_ABSO)

#### 1.5.2 Loading

Static calculation:

- Gravity type loading applied to the model.

Dynamic calculation:

- *Base* :ONDE_PLANE type loading of a wave S.
- *Right and left bords*: ONDE_PLANE type loading of a S wave with option of adding reflected wave (COOR_REFLECHI)

## 2 reference solution

The result of the POST_NEWMARK macro-command is compared with the GeoSlope software estimate for the same configuration. This solution is available in the case-test database of this software [1].

In static, the safety factor provided by GeoSlope is 1.188.

In dynamically, the following figure shows the evolution of the safety factor obtained by a standard method *Enhanced Limit Equilibrium* (ELE) [2] with seismic loading:

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.200/10000201000002FB00000276DD3EA0B65E585A1C.png)

Figure 2- Evolution temporelle du facteur de sécurité dynamique [1].

In particular, the following are noted:

- A limited choice of the number of points of calculation/visualization of the safety factor, compared to seismic excitation,
- A very high peak of the safety factor after 2s,
- A safety factor fluctuation between 6 and 8 seconds, not directly related to the acceleration signal variations.

Furthermore, the reference calculation does not appear to use advanced modelling techniques of boundary conditions paraaxial type with the addition of reflected wave. Therefore, the reference calculation is expected to overestimate the acceleration and stress variations in the embankment compared to the reference model for this type of problem with code_aster.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Model A uses a linear type embankment behavior model, the calculation of initial stresses from the MECA_STATIQUE operator and the dynamic calculation by DYNA_VIBRA. The safety factor assessment is performed by the POST_NEWMARK macro-command directly by providing the radius and coordinates of the sliding circle.

The check option of the sliding area mass is activated, with a relative error of RESI_RELA of 2%.

## 5 Analysis of results

In static, the estimate by POST_NEWMARK is very similar to that obtained by GeoSlope (1.189).

In dynamic terms, the results between the two software programs are different, as the boundary conditions is not the same. Figure 3 shows the average acceleration of the slip zone between the GeoSlope and code_aster calculations. One can see that the acceleration peaks are attenuated by the boundary conditions in the solution obtained by code_aster, as no material damping hypothesis is integrated into the modelling.

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.200/100002010000032000000258916EF3B456B8ABF1.png)

Figure 3- Accélération moyenne de la zone qui glisse

By the same reasoning, it is observed that the changes in the safety factor in dynamic calculations are less than those obtained by GeoSlope, an index of large variations in normal and tangential stresses. The trends are similar, except in particular between 6 and 8 seconds: in fact, the Modelling with code_aster and POST_NEWMARK predicts small variations in stress, in connection with an acceleration of the almost zero input signal (Figure 1). However, there are large variations in the GeoSlope solution, which is an index of a wave trapped in the model by boundary conditions.

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.200/100002010000032000000258AD177BCCB595B8B8.png)

Figure 4- Evolution temporelle du facteur de sécurité en dynamique

The reference case also analyses the evolution of the safety factor with mean acceleration. In this case, it is observed that the Modelling with code_aster and GeoSlope gives close results, with a similar curvature despite a greater dispersion with code_aster (although the small number of points of post-processing presented in the reference case with GeoSlope suggests that there is an internal choice made for the moments of analysis, different from the simulation step of the earthquake at the beginning of the calculation).

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.200/10000201000003200000025894D4AF41CC08FBE0.png)

Figure 5- Evolution du facteur de sécurité avec l’accélération moyenne.

Considering a critical acceleration value of 0.1g (equal to that which induces a unit safety factor in Figure 5), the irreversible displacement predicted by the GeoSlope and POST_NEWMARK modelling is shown in Figure 6. The displacements obtained in code_aster are lower, as the average acceleration is lower. Having efficient boundary conditions is a significant margin source for this type of approach to justify seismic holding of the embankments without taking into account water pressures.

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.200/100002010000032000000258FEE1BA205C1B55D5.png)

Figure 6- Evolution temporelle du déplacement irréversible estimé par la méthode de Newmark
