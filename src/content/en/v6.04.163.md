# v6.04.163 SSNV163 - Clean creep calculation with BETON_UMLV and BETON_BURGER models

**Summary:**

This test validates the BETON_UMLV and BETON_BURGER own creep models. The results of this test are compared with the analytical solutions (BETON_UMLV) or obtained using an explicit integration scheme (BETON_BURGER) for three types of modellings: $\mathrm{3D}$ , axisymmetric and flat stresses.

Modelling A: Clean creep test with BETON_UMLV and 3D Modelling.

Modelling B: Clean creep test with BETON_UMLV model and AXIS Modelling.

Modelling C: Clean creep test with BETON_UMLV model and C_PLAN Modelling.

Modelling D: Clean creep test with BETON_BURGER and 3D Modelling.

Modelling E: Clean creep test with BETON_BURGER model and AXIS Modelling.

Modelling F: Clean creep test with BETON_BURGER model and C_PLAN Modelling.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In this test, a homogeneous drying field is created which varies in structure, the humidity is $\text{100\%}$ (condition of a sealed test piece). The mechanical loading corresponds to a unidirectional compression in the vertical direction ($z$ in $\mathrm{3D}$ or $y$ in $\mathrm{2D}$); its intensity is $1[\mathrm{MPa}]$ . The load is applied in $\mathrm{1s}$ and is kept constant for 100 days.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The beginning of the calculation is assumed $–1$ moment. At this moment there is no drying field, no mechanical stress.

At moment 0, a drying field corresponding to $\text{100 \%}$ d-hygrometry is applied.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Solutions obtained for BETON_UMLV

#### 2.1.1 Method of calculation

This section presents the complete analytical resolution of the problem of a test body subjected to a uniform and unidirectional stress field applied instantly at the initial moment and maintained constant thereafter (case of a simple compression creep test):

$$
\sigma ={\sigma}_{0}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}
$$

Of which the decomposition partly spherical and deviatoric ss.

$$
\sigma =\underset{\text{partie sphérique}}{\underset{⏟}{\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{0}I}}+\underset{\text{partie déviatorique}}{\underset{⏟}{\frac{2}{3}{\sigma}_{0}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}-\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{0}\left({e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}+{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\right)}}
$$

By operating a spherical/deviatoric decomposition identical to that of the stresses, the axial deformation ss written in the form:

$$
{\epsilon}_{zz}={\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs}}\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{0}}{3}\right)+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fd}}\left(\frac{2{\sigma}_{0}}{3}\right)
$$

The response to a spherical stress step and a deviatoric stress step must therefore be resolved successively.

#### 2.1.2 Solving the equations of spherical creep [bib2]

The spherical creep deformation process is governed by the system of coupled equations (equations () and (), cf. [R7.01.06](doc:r7.01.06)):

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{fs}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}\left(h{\sigma}^{s}-{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}\right)-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}
$$

where ${k}_{r}^{s}$ is the apparent rigidity associated with the skeleton formed by mesoscopically-scaled hydrate blocks and ${\eta}_{r}^{s}$ is the apparent viscosity associated with the diffusion mechanism within the capillary porosity.

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}{⟨\left[{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs}}-\left({k}_{r}^{s}+{k}_{i}^{s}\right){\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}\right]-\left[h{\sigma}^{s}-{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}\right]⟩}^{+}
$$

where ${k}_{i}^{s}$ is the apparent rigidity associated intrinsically with microscopic hydrates and ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ is the apparent viscosity associated with the interfolia diffusion mechanism. In (), ${⟨⟩}^{+}$ is the Mac Cauley operator: ${⟨x⟩}^{+}=\frac{1}{2}\left(x+|x|\right)$

The resolution of the previous coupled equation system requires a distinction between two cases according to the sign of the quantity between the Mac Cauley hooks. In the next, the analytical resolution of the response is presented at a stress step ${\sigma}^{s}$. Relative humidity is assumed to be invariant; the medium is saturated with water.

##### 2.1.2.1 Short-term creep

At the initial moment, $t=0$ , a positive ${\sigma}^{s}$ spherical stress is applied. Reversible and irreversible creep deformations are equal to zero (initial conditions). The system equation () therefore sss.

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}(t=0)=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}{⟨2{k}_{r}^{s}0-{k}_{i}^{s}0-{\sigma}^{s}⟩}^{+}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}{⟨-{\sigma}^{s}⟩}^{+}=0
$$

The rate of irreversible creep deformation is therefore equal to zero. It is deduced that irreversible creep deformation is also equal to zero. The irreversible deformation rate remains equal to zero until instant

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.163/Object_29.svg)

, defined by the relationship ():

$$
2{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}({t}_{0})-{\sigma}^{s}=0\Rightarrow {\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}({t}_{0})=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{2{k}_{r}^{s}}
$$

Up to the $t={t}_{0}$ moment, the reversible creep deformation is defined by the following relationship:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{s}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}\left({\sigma}^{s}-{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{s}\right)\Rightarrow {\epsilon}_{r}^{s}(t)=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{t}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}\right)\right]
$$

The ${\tau}_{r}^{s}=\frac{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}$ quantity is the characteristic time associated with reversible creep deformation. L-snap ${t}_{0}$ is therefore defined by the relation ():

$$
{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{2{k}_{r}^{s}}=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{{t}_{0}}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}\right)\right]\Rightarrow {t}_{0}=\ln(2){\tau}_{r}^{s}\approx 0.69{\tau}_{r}^{s}
$$

Reversible and irreversible creep deformations are therefore determined by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}(t)=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{t}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}\right)\right]\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}(t)=0\end{array}
$$

When calculating the creep deformations for $t>{t}_{0}$ , the new initial conditions are therefore:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}({t}_{0})=\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{2{k}_{r}^{s}}\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}({t}_{0})=0\end{array}
$$

##### 2.1.2.2 Long-term creeping

The following relationship is obtained by expressing the reversible and irreversible creeping deformation rates as a function of creep deformations:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}=\left(-\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}-4\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}+\left(2\frac{{k}_{i}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}+\left(\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}+\frac{2}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\sigma}^{s}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}=\left(2\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}+\left(-\frac{{k}_{i}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}+\left(-\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\right){\sigma}^{s}\end{array}
$$

To simplify the calculations, the following intermediate variables are defined:

$$
{u}_{\mathit{rr}}\equiv \frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}=\frac{1}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}
$$

The system of equations () can then be put into the following matrix form:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{fs}}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left[\begin{array}{cc}-{u}_{\mathit{rr}}-4{u}_{\mathit{ri}}& 2{u}_{ii}\\ 2{u}_{\mathit{ri}}& -{u}_{ii}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{\mathit{rr}}+2{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\\ -{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

That is:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{fs}}=A\times {\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs}}+{\sigma}^{s}B
$$

Suppose the $A$ matrix is *diagonalizable* (this property will be verified later):

$$
A=PD{P}^{-1}
$$

Where $D$ means the diagonal matrix of the $A$ matrix values, $P$ means the matrix of the $A$ matrix vectors and ${P}^{-1}$ means the $P$ matrix matrix inverse matrix. By eventually completing the product by the quantity ${P}^{-1}$, the relationship () can be put into the form:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{fs},\text{*}}=D\times {\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs},\text{*}}+{\sigma}^{s}{B}^{\text{*}}
$$

${\lambda}_{1}$ and ${\lambda}_{2}$ are the values of the $A$ matrix.

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs},\text{*}}\equiv \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{1}^{\text{*}}\\ {\epsilon}_{2}^{\text{*}}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The relationship () then reads:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{1}^{\text{*}}(t)={\lambda}_{1}{\epsilon}_{1}^{\text{*}}+{\sigma}^{s}{b}_{1}^{\text{*}}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{2}^{\text{*}}(t)={\lambda}_{2}{\epsilon}_{2}^{\text{*}}+{\sigma}^{s}{b}_{2}^{\text{*}}\end{array}
$$

System whose solution is written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{1}^{\text{*}}(t)=-\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{\lambda}_{1}}{b}_{1}^{\text{*}}+{\mu}_{1}\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}t\right)\\ {\epsilon}_{2}^{\text{*}}(t)=-\frac{{\sigma}^{s}}{{\lambda}_{2}}{b}_{2}^{\text{*}}+{\mu}_{2}\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}t\right)\end{array}
$$

The initial space can then be returned through the passing matrix; reversible and irreversible creep deformations are linear combinations of ${\epsilon}_{1}^{\text{*}}$ and ${\epsilon}_{2}^{\text{*}}$. The clean values of the $A$ matrix are obtained by solving:

$$
det\left(A-{\lambda}_{i}I\right)=0
$$

Again:

| :math:left | begin{array}{cc}-{u}_{mathit{rr}}-4{u}_{mathit{ri}}-{lambda}_{i}& 2{u}_{ii}\ 2{u}_{mathit{ri}}}& -{u}_{ii}-{lambda}_{i}end{array}right | =0iff {lambda}_{i}{2}+left({u}_{mathit{rr}}+4{u}_{mathit{ri}}+{u}_{ii}right){lambda}_{i}+{u}_{mathit{rr}}{u}_{ii}=0` |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

Noting that ${u}_{\mathit{rr}}$ , ${u}_{\mathit{ri}}$ and ${u}_{ii}$ are strictly positive, the discriminant is therefore always strictly positive. Therefore, the own values are real and distinct, so the $A$ matrix is *diagonalizable* . Moreover, neither of the two values is equal to zero (${\lambda}_{1}{\lambda}_{2}={u}_{\mathit{rr}}{u}_{ii}\ne 0$ ). The two values are defined by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\lambda}_{1}=\frac{-\left({u}_{\mathit{rr}}+4{u}_{\mathit{ri}}+{u}_{ii}\right)-\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}\\ {\lambda}_{2}=\frac{-\left({u}_{\mathit{rr}}+4{u}_{\mathit{ri}}+{u}_{ii}\right)+\sqrt{\Delta}}{2}\end{array}
$$

The two values of the spherical creep deformation can be shown to be negative. Let us show that the second value of the spherical creep deformation is negative. Therefore, the spherical creep deformation is asymptotic, a hypothesis that is given in the spherical own creep model [bib1]. Let us now determine a basis for the $\left({X}_{1},{X}_{2}\right)$ own vectors associated with the ${\lambda}_{1}$ and ${\lambda}_{2}$ own values. It is determined by solving the $\left(A-{\lambda}_{i}I\right){X}_{i}=0$ equation. A particular basis of clean vectors swritten:

$$
{X}_{1}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{1}\\ 1\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

After checking that $P$ can actually be reversed, the solution is derived in physical space:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fs}}(t)=\left[-{\sigma}^{s}\left({x}_{1}\frac{{b}_{1}^{\text{*}}}{{\lambda}_{1}}+\frac{{b}_{2}^{\text{*}}}{{\lambda}_{2}}\right)+{x}_{1}{\mu}_{1}\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}t\right)+{\mu}_{2}\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}t\right)\right]\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fs}}(t)=\left[-{\sigma}^{s}\left(\frac{{b}_{1}^{\text{*}}}{{\lambda}_{1}}+{x}_{2}\frac{{b}_{2}^{\text{*}}}{{\lambda}_{2}}\right)+{\mu}_{1}\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}t\right)+{x}_{2}{\mu}_{2}\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}t\right)\right]\end{array}
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{b}_{1}^{\text{*}}=\frac{1}{{x}_{1}{x}_{2}-1}\left[{x}_{2}\left({u}_{\mathit{rr}}+2{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\right)+{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\right]\\ {b}_{2}^{\text{*}}=\frac{1}{{x}_{1}{x}_{2}-1}\left[-\left({u}_{\mathit{rr}}+2{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\right)-{x}_{1}{u}_{\mathit{ri}}\right]\end{array}
$$

Finally, ${\mu}_{1}$ and ${\mu}_{2}$ are defined by the relationships:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\mu}_{1}=-\frac{1}{\left({x}_{1}{x}_{2}-1\right)\exp\left[\left({\lambda}_{1}+{\lambda}_{2}\right){t}_{0}\right]}\left[\frac{1}{2{k}_{r}}{x}_{2}\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}{t}_{0}\right)-\frac{1}{{k}_{i}}\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}{t}_{0}\right)\right]\\ {\mu}_{2}=-\frac{1}{\left({x}_{1}{x}_{2}-1\right)\exp\left[\left({\lambda}_{1}+{\lambda}_{2}\right){t}_{0}\right]}\left[-\frac{1}{2{k}_{r}}\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}{t}_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{{k}_{i}}{x}_{1}\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}{t}_{0}\right)\right]\end{array}
$$

#### 2.1.3 Solving the equations of deviatoric creep

Deviatoric deformations have a reversible and irreversible part (see [R7.01.06]):

$$
\underset{\text{déformation déviatorique totale}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fd}}}}=\underset{\text{contribution eau adsorbée}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathit{fd}}}}+\underset{\text{contribution eau libre}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathit{fd}}}}
$$

The main *jème* component of total deviatoric deformation is governed by equations () and ():

$$
{\eta}_{r}^{d}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{d,j}+{k}_{r}^{d}{\epsilon}_{r}^{d,j}=h{\sigma}^{d,j}
$$

where ${k}_{r}^{d}$ is the rigidity associated with the capacity of adsorbed water to transmit loads ( *load bearing water* ) and ${\eta}_{r}^{d}$ is the viscosity associated with water adsorbed by the hydrated sheets.

$$
{\eta}_{i}^{d}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{d,j}=h{\sigma}^{d,j}
$$

où

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.163/Object_86.svg)

The equation () is more simple to resolve than the one governing the spherical behaviour because it is decoupled. It is always assumed that the humidity remains equal to 1 throughout the load. The equation () corresponds to the visco-elastic Kelvin model, whose response to an exponential stress step is of the type. As for equation (), the deformation response is linear over time. The total creep deformation is therefore written as the sum of the contribution of a Kelvin chain and the contribution of a shock absorber and series:

$$
{\epsilon}^{d,j}(t)=\left[\frac{t}{{\eta}_{i}^{d}}+\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{d}}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{{k}_{r}^{d}}{{\eta}_{r}^{d}}t\right)\right)\right]{\sigma}^{d,j}H(t)
$$

#### 2.1.4 Analytical solution Summary

For uniaxial loading the analytical solutions of the two deformation components are known. The contribution of the deviator part ssss written:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fd}}(t)=\frac{2}{3}{\sigma}_{0}\left[\frac{t}{{\eta}_{i}^{d}}+\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{d}}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{{k}_{r}^{d}}{{\eta}_{r}^{d}}t\right)\right)\right]
$$

The spherical part contribution is defined over two intervals:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{{\sigma}_{0}}{3{k}_{r}^{s}}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}t\right)\right]\text{pour}t\le \frac{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\ln2\\ \frac{{\sigma}_{0}}{3}\left[\left(\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{s}}+\frac{1}{{k}_{i}^{s}}\right)+{\mu}_{1}\left(1+{x}_{1}\right)\exp\left({\lambda}_{1}t\right)+{\mu}_{2}\left(1+{x}_{2}\right)\exp\left({\lambda}_{2}t\right)\right]\text{pour}t>\frac{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\ln2\end{array}
$$

Axial deformation is a linear function of the two previous contributions:

$$
{\epsilon}_{zz}={\epsilon}^{\mathit{fs}}\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{0}}{3}\right)+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fd}}\left(\frac{2{\sigma}_{0}}{3}\right)
$$
