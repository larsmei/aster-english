# r4.07.03 Computation of an added-mass matrix on a modal basis

**Summary:**

This document presents one aspect of fluid/structure coupling: when a vibrating structure is immersed in a fluid assumed to be without flow, incompressible and inviscid, it experiences pressure forces whose resultant is proportional to the acceleration of the structure in the fluid: the proportionality coefficient is homogeneous to a mass: it is called the **added mass**. The means of building an added-mass matrix for one (or several) structure(s) with several degrees of freedom, on the modal basis of the structure(s) established beforehand in the absence of fluid, is specified here. This added-mass matrix is obtained under the assumption of small motions, both for the structure and for the fluid.

## 2 Introduction

Many industrial components or structures are in contact with fluid media, which may moreover be in flow. These surrounding fluid media disturb the vibratory characteristics of the structures, in particular their modal characteristics. This action of the fluid on the structure results in fluid/structure coupling effects.

The surrounding fluid medium is assumed here to be incompressible, perfect and at rest (without flow). It will be shown that, then, a structure which vibrates with a small amplitude while sharing a boundary with this fluid domain modifies the pressure field in the fluid at rest, and therefore experiences a fluctuating pressure force, proportional to its acceleration. The proportionality coefficient is a mass. It describes the inertial effect of the fluid on the structure: this is why this mass is called the **added mass** of the fluid on the structure.

The fluid domain may also have fixed boundaries or free surfaces, but in what follows the action of gravity on the free surface will not be considered, and therefore neither will the sloshing modes.

When several structures are in contact with the same fluid domain, when one of the structures starts to vibrate, not only does it experience the inertia of the fluid, but it also modifies the fluctuating pressure field around the interfaces with the fluid of all the other structures. The forces experienced by each of them are proportional to the acceleration of the vibrating structure: here again the proportionality coefficients are masses called **coupling added masses**.

The added masses are established in the basis of the eigenmodes of the structures considered in the absence of fluid. The modal analysis of the structures can then be carried out again taking these added masses into account, and one may possibly return to the finite-element basis (“physical” basis).

## 3 Recall of the equations of the problem

### 3.1 Equations in the fluid

It is assumed that $K$ vibrating structures are immersed in a perfect (inviscid), incompressible fluid domain at rest (without flow). The effect of gravity is neglected. The Euler equations associated with the fluctuations in the fluid without flow can therefore be written:

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.03/1000055400001E7D000011FCABC6687AD3DA94FD.svg)

- conservation of mass:

$\frac{\partial {\rho}_{f}}{\partial t}+div({\rho}_{f}\mathrm{v})=0$                             eq 3.1-1

- conservation of momentum (under the linearised assumption):

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{v}}{\partial t}+(\mathrm{v}.\mathrm{grad})\mathrm{v}+\frac{1}{{\rho}_{f}}\mathrm{grad}p=0$                             eq 3.1-2

Because of the incompressibility of the fluid, equation [eq 3.1-1] becomes:

$divv=0$                                     eq 3.1-3

In the fluid volume ${\Omega}_{f}$ , the convection induced by the motion of the structure, assumed to be of small amplitude, is neglected. Equation [eq 3.1-2] therefore becomes:

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{v}}{\partial t}+\frac{1}{{\rho}_{f}}\mathrm{grad}p=0$                                 eq 3.1-4

Differentiating [eq 3.1-3] with respect to time and substituting the expression for $\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}$ as a function of the pressure into this equation, one obtains:

$div\mathrm{grad}p=0$

that is:

$\Deltap =0$ in ${\Omega}_{f}$

which is the Laplace equation in a fluid without flow.

At the fluid/structure interface, assumed to be watertight, the normal acceleration of the wall of the structure can be written as equal to the normal acceleration of the fluid (continuity of the normal accelerations — impermeability condition of the structure). The following convention is used here for the normal: it is the **outward** normal to the structure, **oriented from the structure towards the fluid.**

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{v}}{\partial t}.\mathrm{n}={\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{{S}_{l}}.\mathrm{n}$

With equation [eq 3.1-4], one obtains:

$\text{grad}p.\mathrm{n}=-{\rho}_{f}\frac{\partial \mathrm{v}}{\partial t}.\mathrm{n}=-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{{S}_{l}}.\mathrm{n}$

That is:

$(\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}=-{\rho}_{f}\ddot{{\mathrm{x}}_{{S}_{l}}}.\mathrm{n}$ on ${\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}$ , the fluid/structure interface of the structure indexed by $l$ .

In summary, the fluid problem consists in solving a Laplace equation in the fluid domain with von Neumann boundary conditions on the moving walls ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{fs}}$ and the fixed walls ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{fix}}$ and Dirichlet conditions on the free surface ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{lib}}$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta p=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{{\Gamma}_{\mathit{fs}}}=-{\rho}_{f}\ddot{{\mathrm{x}}_{s}}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{\mathit{fs}},\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{\mathit{fs}}=\text{}\underset{l=1,K}{\cup}{\gamma}_{l}\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{{\Gamma}_{\mathit{imp}}}=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{2},\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{2}=\partial {\Omega}_{f}-{\Gamma}_{\mathit{fs}}\end{array}$                 eq 3.1-5

### 3.2 Equations in the structures

Consider $K$ elastic structures immersed in a fluid medium. The equation of their motion in the presence of fluid is written, having denoted their degrees of freedom by $\mathrm{U}$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\forall l\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{indice de structure},l\in \lbrace 0,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\mathrm{M}}_{l}.{\ddot{\mathrm{U}}}_{l}+{\mathrm{K}}_{l}.{\mathrm{U}}_{l}={\mathrm{F}}_{l}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{{S}_{l}},\text{volume de la structure}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}l\\ \forall l,\sigma .\mathrm{n}=-p.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}},\text{contour de la structure}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}l\end{array}$

${M}_{l}$ is the mass matrix of the structure, ${\mathrm{K}}_{l}$ its stiffness matrix. The boundary condition on the contour of the structures expresses the continuity of the normal stress at the fluid/structure interface (the fluid stress tensor being reduced to its non-deviatoric part, the fluid being perfect). Integrating this normal stress over the contour of each structure gives a force ${\mathrm{F}}_{l}$ , the resultant of the fluid pressure forces at the fluid/structure interface. This force is the integral of the pressure field over the contour ${\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}$ of each structure:

$\forall l\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{indice}\text{de}\text{structure},l\in \lbrace 0,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\mathrm{F}}_{l}=-\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}p\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}$

The pressure field satisfies problem [eq 3.1-5].

### 3.3 Equations of the coupled problem — highlighting the added-mass matrix

Ultimately, the coupled fluid/structure problem is written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta p=0\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ \forall l\in \lbrace 0,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,{\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}\right)}_{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}=-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{{S}_{l}}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}\\ \forall l\in \lbrace 0,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\mathrm{M}}_{l}.{\ddot{\mathrm{U}}}_{l}+{\mathrm{K}}_{l}.{\mathrm{U}}_{l}={\mathrm{F}}_{l}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{{S}_{l}}\\ \forall l\in \lbrace 0,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\mathrm{F}}_{l}=-\underset{{\gamma}_{l}}{\int}p.\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}\end{array}$                 eq 3.3-1

It will now be shown that the force experienced by the immersed structures is proportional to their acceleration. A good way of demonstrating this is to place oneself in the modal basis of the structures in vacuum. The acceleration can thus be decomposed on this basis (which is in fact the union of the modal bases of each of the structures). Thus:

${{x}_{{S}_{l}}}_{}(r,t)=\sum_{\text{i=1}}^{\infty}{a}_{\mathrm{il}}(t){X}_{\mathrm{il}}(r)$

Substituting this expression into the second equation of system [eq 3.3-1], one is led to seek the pressure field in the form:

$p=\sum_{l=1,\mathrm{...},K}\sum_{i=1,\mathrm{...},\infty }{\ddot{a}}_{\mathrm{il}}(t){p}_{\mathrm{il}}(r)$

Substituting these expressions into problem [eq 3.3-1], one has to solve in the fluid as many Laplace problems as modes have been chosen for each of the structures. This is expressed by:

$\forall l\in \lbrace 1,\mathrm{...},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\forall i\lbrace 1,\mathrm{...},\infty \rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {p}_{\mathit{il}}=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\text{}{\Omega}_{f}\\ {\left(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathit{il}}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}\right)}_{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}=-{\rho}_{f}{\mathrm{x}}_{\mathit{il}}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}\\ \left[\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathit{il}}\end{array}\right]({\ddot{\mathrm{a}}}_{l})+\left[\begin{array}{c}{k}_{\mathit{il}}\end{array}\right]({\mathrm{a}}_{l})=({\mathrm{f}}_{\mathrm{il}})\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{l}\end{array}$

The generalised mass and stiffness “matrices” written in these bases are diagonal.

Each component of the resultant pressure force projected onto the modal basis is written:

$\forall i\lbrace 1,\mathrm{....},\infty \rbrace ,\forall l\lbrace 1,\mathrm{....},K\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left({f}_{\mathit{il}}\right)=-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}{\ddot{a}}_{\text{jk}}\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{p}_{\text{jk}}{X}_{\mathit{il}}.\mathrm{n}.{N}_{j}\mathit{dS}$

The vector of the generalised pressure force on an immersed structure can then be written in matrix form:

$({\mathrm{f}}_{\mathit{il}})=-\left[\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}\end{array}\right].{\ddot{a}}_{\text{jk}}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{avec}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{m}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}=\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{p}_{\text{jk}}{X}_{\mathit{il}}.\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}$

Here, $l$ is fixed: the matrix made up of the components $\left[{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}\right]$ is called the **added-mass matrix** of the fluid on the structure of contour ${\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}$ . When the modal basis of the whole set of $K$ structures is considered, the notation of the **added-mass matrix** of components $\left[{\mathrm{m}}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}\right]$ on the modal basis in vacuum is generalised, $l$ varying from $1$ to $K$ . This matrix is in general non-diagonal.

### 3.4 A few definitions

#### 3.4.1 Definition 1

When $l=k$ (same structure) and $i=j$ (same mode order), the coefficient ${m}_{\mathrm{ilil}}$ is the **added self-mass** of mode $i$ of the structure $l$ . It is the additional inertia due to the fluid displaced by the mode of order $i$ of the structure, taking into account the geometric confinements induced in the fluid by the presence of the other structures, assumed fixed.

#### 3.4.2 Definition 2

When $l=k$ (same structure) and $i\mathrm{\ne }j$ (different mode orders), the coefficient ${m}_{\mathrm{iljl}}$ is the **coupling added mass** between the modes of order $i$ and $j$ of the structure $l$ . In air, these off-diagonal mass terms are zero, because the modes are orthogonal to one another. In view of the general expression of the coefficient ${m}_{\mathrm{il}\text{jk}}$ , the modes $i$ and $j$ may be coupled in mass, because the pressure field ${p}_{\mathit{jl}}$ created by mode $j$ of the structure $l$ is not necessarily orthogonal to the mode of order $i$ of this same structure. It is sufficient that this structure be immersed in an environment with no geometric symmetry for this coefficient to be non-zero. In a symmetric environment, on the other hand, the orthogonality of the pressure field with the mode is observed.

#### 3.4.3 Definition 3

When $l\mathrm{\ne }k$ (different structures) and $i\ne j$ (different mode orders), the coefficient ${m}_{\mathrm{il}\text{jk}}$ is the **coupling added mass** between the modes of order $i$ and $j$ of the structures $l$ and $k$ respectively. This coefficient expresses the inertial force exerted by the structure $k$ vibrating on its mode of order $j$ on the structure $l$ vibrating on its mode $i$ .

### 3.5 Properties of the added-mass matrix

#### 3.5.1 Theorem 1: the added-mass matrix is symmetric

To simplify the proof, a single structure immersed in a perfect, incompressible and inviscid fluid will be considered. The motion of the structure is decomposed on its modal basis (truncated to $n$ modes), but the result can just as well be proved in the “physical” basis (*i.e.* the basis of the nodal interpolation functions). Finally, the result generalises to the case of $K$ structures immersed in the same fluid.

It must be proved that: ${m}_{ij}=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}{\mathrm{X}}_{j}.\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}={m}_{ji}=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{j}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}.\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}$

- ${p}_{i}$ (respectively ${p}_{j}$ ) represents the pressure field created in the fluid and at the interface with the structure by the mode of order $i$ (respectively of order $j$ ) of the structure,
- ${\mathrm{X}}_{j}$ (respectively ${\mathrm{X}}_{i}$ ) represents the modal shape of the mode of order $j$ (respectively of order $i$ ).

Now:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {p}_{i}=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ \frac{\partial {p}_{i}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}=-{\rho}_{f}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\Gamma \end{array}$ and $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {p}_{j}=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ \frac{\partial {p}_{j}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}=-{\rho}_{f}{\mathrm{X}}_{j}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\Gamma \end{array}$

Whence, using Green's formula with a normal oriented from the structure towards the fluid and the harmonicity of ${p}_{i}$ and of ${p}_{j}$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{m}_{ij}=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}{\mathrm{X}}_{j}.\mathrm{n}d\Gamma =-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}\frac{\partial {p}_{j}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}.\mathrm{n}dS\\ \phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}=-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}(\underset{0}{\underset{⏟}{\underset{\Omega}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}\Delta {p}_{j}d\Omega }}-\underset{\Omega}{\overset{}{\int}}\mathrm{grad}{p}_{i}.\mathrm{grad}{p}_{j}d\Omega )\\ \phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}=-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}(\underset{0}{\underset{⏟}{\underset{\Omega}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{j}\Delta {p}_{i}d\Omega }}-\underset{\Omega}{\overset{}{\int}}\mathrm{grad}{p}_{j}.\mathrm{grad}{p}_{i}d\Omega )\\ \phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}=-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{j}\frac{\partial {p}_{i}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}.\mathrm{n}dS=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{j}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}.\mathrm{n}dS\\ \phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}={m}_{ji}\end{array}$

#### 3.5.2 Theorem 2: the added-mass matrix is positive definite

The reader is referred to reference [bib1] for the complete proof.

#### 3.5.3 Theorem 3

Suppose that there are $K$ structures with identical linear elastic properties and which are immersed in the same fluid. Moreover, these structures admit two displacement degrees of freedom in the plane $\mathit{Oxy}$ (cf. the diagram in § 3.1). Each of these structures admits the same spectrum ${f}_{1},\mathrm{...},{f}_{n},\mathrm{...}$ of eigenfrequencies in vacuum.

For every eigenfrequency ${f}_{n}$ , there exist $2K$ eigenfrequencies $\left\lbrace {\omega}_{1},\mathrm{...},{\omega}_{\mathrm{2K}}\right\rbrace$ of the coupled fluid/structure system satisfying $\forall i\in \left\lbrace 1,\mathrm{...},2K\right\rbrace ,{\omega}_{i}\le {f}_{n}$

The reader is referred to reference [bib1] for the complete proof.

#### 3.5.4 Other properties

> The added self-mass coefficients are always positive.

It is still assumed that there is a single structure immersed in a perfect, incompressible fluid without flow. The proof generalises without difficulty to $K$ immersed structures.

It must be proved that:

$\forall i\text{indice}\text{de}\text{mode}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\in \left\lbrace 1,\mathrm{...},n\right\rbrace ,\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{m}_{ii}=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}.\mathrm{n}dS\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\ge \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}0$

Now:

$\begin{array}{c}{m}_{ii}=\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}{\mathrm{X}}_{i}.\mathrm{n}dS=-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}\underset{\Gamma}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}\frac{\partial {p}_{i}}{\partial \mathrm{n}}dS\\ \phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}=-\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}(\underset{0}{\underset{⏟}{\underset{{\Omega}_{f}}{\overset{}{\int}}{p}_{i}\Delta {p}_{i}d\Omega }}-\underset{\Omega}{\overset{}{\int}}\mathrm{grad}{p}_{i}.\mathrm{grad}{p}_{i}d\Omega )\\ \phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}=\frac{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}1}{{\rho}_{f}}\underset{{\Omega}_{f}}{\overset{}{\int}}(\mathrm{grad}{p}_{i}{)}^{2}d\Omega \\ \phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}\ge 0\end{array}$

Suppose that there are $K$ structures immersed in the same fluid. It is assumed that they have a single translational degree of freedom along $\mathit{Ox}$ . Then the sum of all the added-mass coefficients of this matrix gives the added self-mass on the set of $K$ structures all moving with the same rectilinear sinusoidal motion.

The reader is referred to reference [bib2] for the complete proof.

## 4 Numerical implementation

### 4.1 Solution of the Laplace equation by volume finite elements

Let us return to the fluid Laplace problem with von Neumann boundary conditions:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{c}\Delta p=0\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{{\Gamma}_{1}}=-{\rho}_{f}\ddot{{\mathrm{x}}_{s}}.\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{1},\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{1}=\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\underset{l=1,K}{\cup}{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}\end{array}\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}=0\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{2},\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{\Gamma}_{2}=\partial \Omega -{\Gamma}_{1}\end{array}$

Let us write a variational formulation of this problem:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}v.\Delta pd\Omega =0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\forall v$

Using Green's formula with a normal assumed to be oriented from the structure towards the fluid (hence inward to the fluid volume) and setting $\Gamma ={\Gamma}_{1}\cup {\Gamma}_{2}$ :

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\mathrm{grad}v.\mathrm{grad}pd\Omega +{\int}_{\Gamma}v\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}.\mathrm{n}dS=0$

That is:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\mathrm{grad}v.\mathrm{grad}pd\Omega ={\rho}_{f}{\int}_{\Gamma}v{\ddot{x}}_{n}dS$                         eq 4.1-1

A partition of the fluid volume ${\Omega}_{f}$ into a finite number of elements is considered. On this discretisation of the domain, an approximate form of the hydrodynamic pressure field can be written:

${p}_{}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}(\mathrm{r}){p}_{i}$

${N}_{i}$ represents the nodal interpolation functions defined on the elements: they are equal to 1 at node number $i$ , and to $0$ on all the others for P1 finite elements.

Then, taking as test functions $v$ successively the nodal interpolation functions, a system of $N$ equations is obtained by substitution into [eq 4.1-1]:

$j=1,\mathrm{...},N;\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{p}_{i}\mathrm{grad}{N}_{i}\left(\mathrm{r}\right).\Delta {N}_{j}\left(\mathrm{r}\right)d\Omega ={\rho}_{f}{\int}_{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{N}_{j}{\ddot{x}}_{n}\mathit{dS}$

which can be written in the form of the linear system, whose solutions are the pressure degrees of freedom $\mathrm{P}$ :

| $\mathrm{H}.\mathrm{P}=\Phi ` with :math:$Phi ` vector of components ${\Phi}_{j}={\rho}_{f}\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{N}_{j}{\ddot{x}}_{n}\mathit{dS}$ |
| --- |

| with $\mathrm{H}$ matrix of components :math:{H}_{ij}=underset{{Omega}_{f}}{int}mathrm{grad}{N}_{i}.mathrm{grad}{N}_{j}dOmega ` eq 4.1-2 |
| --- |

Strictly speaking, this system is singular. It admits an infinity of solutions differing by a constant. A pressure must therefore be imposed (Dirichlet boundary condition) at one point of the fluid in order to remove the indeterminacy of the solution.

These precautions having been taken, inverting the system [eq 4.1-2] gives the pressure field throughout the fluid volume ${\Omega}_{f}$ , including at the fluid/structure interface, where it is of course of interest.

### 4.2 Computation of the coefficients of the added-mass matrix on a modal basis

The value of the integral must be estimated numerically:

${m}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}=\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{p}_{\text{jk}}{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathit{il}}.\mathrm{n}\mathit{dS}$                             eq 4.2-1

from a nodal pressure field represented by a column vector denoted ${\mathrm{P}}_{\mathit{jk}}$ and a nodal displacement field corresponding to a structural modal shape in air and represented by the column vector ${\mathrm{X}}_{\mathit{il}}$ .

Now, on the fluid/structure interface, the approximate pressure field ${p}_{\mathrm{jk}}$ due to the discretisation of the interface into $N$ boundary elements can be written:

${p}_{\text{jk}}={\sum^{N}}_{m=1}{N}_{m}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){p}_{{\text{jk}}_{m}}$

while the “modal” displacement field is written on this same discretisation:

${\mathrm{X}}_{\mathit{il}}=\sum_{n=1}^{N}{N}_{n}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){\mathrm{X}}_{{\mathit{il}}_{n}}$

Thus, substituting these two expressions into the integral [eq 4.2-1], one obtains:

$\begin{array}{c}{m}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}\simeq \underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}(\sum_{m=1}^{N}{N}_{m}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){p}_{{\text{jk}}_{m}})\left[\begin{array}{c}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{N}_{n}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){X}_{{\mathit{ilx}}_{n}}\mathrm{{\rm A}}{n}_{x}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}{N}_{n}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){X}_{{\mathit{ily}}_{n}}\mathrm{{\rm A}}{n}_{y}\end{array}\right]\mathit{dS}\\ {m}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}\simeq \sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{p}_{{\text{jk}}_{m}}(\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{N}_{m}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){N}_{n}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){n}_{x}\mathit{dS}){X}_{{\mathit{ilx}}_{n}}+\sum_{m=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}{p}_{{\text{jk}}_{m}}(\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{N}_{m}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){N}_{n}\left(\mathrm{r}\right){n}_{y}\mathit{dS}){X}_{{\mathit{ily}}_{n}}\end{array}$

It has been assumed in the proof above that the problem is two-dimensional.

This can be put in the form of a scalar product, involving a matrix–vector product:

| ${m}_{\mathit{il}\text{jk}}={\mathrm{P}}_{\text{jk}}^{T}{\mathrm{A}}_{x}{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathit{ilx}}+{\mathrm{P}}_{\text{jk}}^{T}{\mathrm{A}}_{y}{\mathrm{X}}_{\mathit{ily}}$ with ${\mathrm{A}}_{x}$ the matrix of coefficients $\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{N}_{i}{N}_{j}{n}_{x}\mathit{dS}$ |
| --- |
| and ${\mathrm{A}}_{y}$ the matrix of coefficients $\underset{{\gamma}_{l}^{\mathit{fs}}}{\int}{N}_{i}{N}_{j}{n}_{y}\mathit{dS}$ |

## 5 Implementation in Code_Aster

### 5.1 Thermal analogy

To solve the Laplace problem in pressure, a thermal analogy is used: the steady heat equation is solved with a material of thermal conductivity equal to unity. Thus:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta p=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{\Gamma}=-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{s}·\mathrm{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\Gamma \end{array}\iff \lbrace \begin{array}{c}div(\lambda \mathrm{grad}T)=0\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}\text{dans}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{\Omega}_{f}\Leftarrow \Delta T=0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{si}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\lambda =1\\ (\frac{\partial T}{\partial \mathrm{n}}{)}_{\Gamma}={\varphi}_{n}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\text{sur}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\Gamma \end{array}$

$T$ represents the temperature in the medium; it plays the role of the pressure in the fluid medium. ${\phi }_{n}$ is the heat flux normal to the wall; it plays the role of the term $-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{s}.\mathrm{n}$ which can be likened to the variation with time of the (fluid) mass flux at the wall of the structure. This quantity $-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{\mathrm{x}}}_{s}.\mathrm{n}$ is indeed homogeneous to a mass divided by an area and a time squared.

### 5.2 Practical implementation

The operator CALC_MATR_AJOU [U4.66.01](doc:u4.66.01) was developed to take into account the inertial coupling (added mass: OPTION = 'MASS_AJOU') between structures immersed in the same perfect, incompressible fluid at rest. The fluid is described by equivalent thermal characteristics (operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)) and the part of the mesh which represents it is assigned thermal elements (operator AFFE_MODELE [U4.41.01](doc:u4.41.01)). This operator CALC_MATR_AJOU also makes it possible to compute the added stiffness or the added damping. In order to facilitate its use in certain cases, the macro-command MACRO_MATR_AJOU [U4.66.11](doc:u4.66.11) also exists.

The operator uses five mandatory keywords:

- the keyword MODELE_FLUIDE: it is on this model that the Laplace problem with von Neumann boundary conditions (or its equivalent thermal problem) is solved,
- the keyword MODE_MECA (or CHAM_NO, or MODELE_GENE): this keyword makes it possible to compute the flux-type boundary conditions at the wall of the structure,
- the keyword MODELE_INTERFACE: it is on this model, which comprises all the thermal boundary elements of the fluid/structure interface, that the scalar product mentioned in paragraph [§4.2] is computed,
- the keyword CHAM_MATER: this is the fluid material (described by equivalent thermal characteristics),
- the keyword CHARGE: this is a thermal load (temperature imposed at any node of the fluid mesh) which corresponds to the Dirichlet boundary condition used to remove the singularity of the Laplace problem (see [§4.1]).

A generalised added-mass matrix is thus obtained. This matrix, which has a skyline profile but is full (operator NUME_DDL_GENE [U4.65.03](doc:u4.65.03)), can be added to the generalised mass matrix of the structure using the operator COMB_MATR_ASSE [U4.72.01](doc:u4.72.01). This makes it possible to compute the coupled fluid/structure modes of the immersed structures (“wet” modes) (operator CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02)).

It is advisable to consult the test case FDLV114 — *Seismic response of a cylindrical tank* [V8.01.114](doc:v8.01.114).
