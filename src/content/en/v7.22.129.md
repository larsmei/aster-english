# v7.22.129 HSNV129 - Compression-Dilation Test for Thermal-Fissure Coupling Study

**Summary:**

A thermo-mechanical load is applied to a volume element according to the Mazars law in order to check the correct consideration of the dependence of material parameters with temperature and the consideration of thermal expansion. The load is homogeneous and thus breaks down: compression at a forced displacement and constant temperature, then application of a heating-cooling cycle.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and boundary conditions

Element volume materialized by a unit side cube ($m$ ):

![Figure](/figures/v7.22.129/10000FE4000069BB0000437DD411376829BB2C70.svg)

*Geometry*

The load is such that a uniform stress and deformation state is obtained in the volume.

Blockages are as follows:

- $\mathit{ABCD}$ face : $\mathit{DZ}=0$
- $\mathit{BCGF}$ face : $\mathit{DX}=0$
- $\mathit{ABFE}$ face : $\mathit{DY}=0$
- $\mathit{EFGH}$ face: $\mathit{Uz}(t)$ displacement

The temperature $T(t)$ is assumed to be uniform on the cube; the reference temperature is $0°C$.

$\mathit{Uz}$ and $T$ vary with time as follows:

| ** moment** $t$ | 0 | 100 | 200 | 300 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathit{Uz}(t)$ | $0\mathit{m.}$ | $–{10}^{-3}\mathit{m.}$ | $–{10}^{-3}\mathit{m.}$ | $–{10}^{-3}\mathit{m.}$ |
| $T(t)$ | $0°C$ | $0°C$ | $200°C$ | $0°C$ |

So a purely mechanical load is performed, then heats up by blocking the $\mathit{Uz}$ direction before cooling. This allows to check the separation of thermal and mechanical deformations as well as the non-recovery of mechanical properties after heating.

## 2 reference solution

The solution to the problem can be determined analytically.

It is noted:

- ${\varepsilon}_{0}$ the deformation applied in the direction $z$ ,
- ${\varepsilon}_{1}$ , ${\varepsilon}_{2}$ and ${\varepsilon}_{3}$ main deformations

### 2.1 First step of loading: simple compression

- The strain tensor is:

$\begin{bmatrix} -\nu\varepsilon_{0} & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -\nu\varepsilon_{0} & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \varepsilon_{0} \end{bmatrix} \text{ avec } \varepsilon_{0}<0$

with ${\varepsilon}_{0}<0$

- The equivalent deformation therefore applies to:

$\tilde{\varepsilon} = \sqrt{\langle \varepsilon^{e}_{1} \rangle_{+}^{2} + \langle \varepsilon^{e}_{2} \rangle_{+}^{2} + \langle \varepsilon^{e}_{3} \rangle_{+}^{2}} = -\nu\varepsilon_{0}\sqrt{2}$

- As soon as $\tilde{\varepsilon} > \varepsilon_{d0}$, there is a change in damage which is worth:

$D = 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_{d0}(1 - A_{c})}{\tilde{\varepsilon}} - \frac{A_{c}}{\exp\!\left[ B_{c}(\tilde{\varepsilon} - \varepsilon_{d0}) \right]}$

- Finally, the $\sigma_{zz}$ constraint is:

$\sigma_{zz} = E(1 - D)\,\varepsilon_{0}$

### 2.2 Second stage of loading: thermal expansion in flat deformations

- The tensor of total deformations is:

$\begin{bmatrix} -\nu\varepsilon_{0} + \alpha(T - T_{\mathrm{ref}})(1+\nu) & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & -\nu\varepsilon_{0} + \alpha(T - T_{\mathrm{ref}})(1+\nu) & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & \varepsilon_{0} \end{bmatrix} \text{ avec } \varepsilon_{0}<0 \text{ fixe}$

- The elastic deformation of $\varepsilon^{e} = \varepsilon - \alpha(T - T_{\mathrm{ref}})I_{d}$, the equivalent deformation is:

$\tilde{\varepsilon} = \sqrt{2} \nu(\alpha(T - T_{\mathrm{ref}}) - \varepsilon_{0})$

- Damage shall be:

$D = \max\!\left[ D^{-},\ 1 - \frac{\varepsilon_{d0}(1 - A_{c})}{\tilde{\varepsilon}} - \frac{A_{c}}{\exp\!\left[ B_{c}(\tilde{\varepsilon} - \varepsilon_{d0}) \right]} \right]$

- Finally, the $\sigma_{zz}$ constraint is:

$\sigma_{zz} = E(1 - D)\left[\varepsilon_{0} - \alpha(T - T_{\mathrm{ref}})\right]$

**Note**

At a given state, the material parameters used are those defined at the maximum temperature seen by the material and not at the current temperature.

The evaluation of damage $D$ involves the notion of maximum reached during the loading history; the solution is therefore not completely analytical but involves discretization. In the case where there is no influence of thermal, it is sufficient to take $\stackrel{~}{\epsilon}$ equivalent to the maximum equivalent deformation achieved. When considering the thermal aspect, heating can contribute to "decrease" or "retard" the damage with given deformation; this is the case with the evolution of ${B}_{c}$ retained. In this case, the load must be carefully discretized to have the correct damage value $D$ (which indeed has a maximum in our case).

## 5 Summary of the results

The analytical solution is obtained with an accuracy of less than 0.02%, which allows us to be assured of the correct implantation of the Mazars model including when the temperature is involved. Let us remember the choices that were made for the cracking-thermal coupling and which are verified here:

- linear thermal expansion,
- the evolution of damage only under the effect of elastic deformation and not thermal,
- Dependence of material parameters on maximum temperature, i.e. non-reversibility of changes in mechanical properties when the concrete is heated and cooled.
