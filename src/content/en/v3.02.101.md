# v3.02.101 SSLP101 - Rate of return of energy in flat stresses

**Summary:**

This is a static break mechanical test for a two-dimensional problem. One considers a plate cracked in flat constraints, the functions tested are:

- the $G$ energy return rate ,
- the energy return rate calculated from the calculation of the stress coefficients ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ .

The test is useful in comparing the standard $G$ value and the $G$ (IRWIN) value obtained from ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$. It also allows testing the invariance of the calculation with the integration crowns.

This test contains 3 different modellings: Model A that dealt with the Rice integral calculation is no longer supported since version 3.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Rectangular plate with $\mathrm{OC}$ crack opening.

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.101/10000000000002E3000002A0B2622266A6ABF062.png)

For symmetry reasons, the model is reduced to the $y\ge 0$ half-structure.

Plate height : $h=250\mathrm{mm}$

Width plate : $I=100\mathrm{mm}$

Crack depth: $a=37.5\mathrm{mm}$ ($\mathrm{OC}$ )

### 1.2 Material properties

$E=200000\mathit{MPa}$ $\nu =0.3$

Assumption of flat constraints.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

| Constraint imposed in $Y=h$: | $\sigma =1\mathit{MPa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Moving for $\mathrm{CA}$ edge defined by: $a\le X\le l$ and $y=0$ | $v=0.$ |
| Point fixed $A$: | $u=v=0.$ |

For $C$ Modelling, the stress imposed by a pressure on the crack lips is replaced.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Reference solution of BROWN & STRAWLEY [bib1]:

| $J={F}^{2}\pi a{\sigma}^{2}/E$ with $F=1.98$ |
| --- |
| $a$ en $\mathit{mm}$ |
| $\sigma$ et $E$ en $N/{\mathrm{mm}}^{2}$ |

### 2.2 Reference results for $G$

Reference results $G={1.98}^{2}\times \pi \times 37.5\times 0.5{10}^{-5}=2.3093{10}^{-3}\mathrm{Mpa.mm}$

The formula

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.101/Object_3.svg)

(IRWIN) = $\frac{1}{E}({K}_{1}^{2}+{K}_{2}^{2})$ drives, as

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.101/Object_5.svg)

, à ${K}_{1}=21.491{\mathrm{MPa.mm}}^{1/2}$

### 2.3 Reference results for $G$ derivatives

By varying the Young's modulus and the $\mathrm{Fy}$ load, it is apparent that:

$G=\alpha {F}_{Y}^{2}$ with $\alpha =2.3{10}^{-3}$ or $\frac{\partial G}{\partial {F}_{Y}}=2\alpha {F}_{Y}$

$G=\frac{\beta}{E}$ with $\beta =460.$ or $\frac{\partial G}{\partial E}=-\frac{G}{E}$

### 2.4 Bibliographic reference

1. BROWN-STAWLEY ASTM Special Technical Publication No. 410 (1966)

## 3 Modelling B

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.101/100000000000045F0000030353DEDBF695FE6177.png)

The $\theta$ field is calculated and the $G$ energy return rate , the ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ stress coefficients , the energy return rate obtained by the IRWIN formula, the crack propagation direction.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes : 673

Number of mesh cells and types: 112 mesh cells QUAD8 and 142 mesh cells TRIA6

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

The tested values are the energy release rate calculated by the theta method and the energy release rate calculated by the IRWIN formula from the stress intensity coefficients ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ .

| **Identification** | **Reference** | **Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Crowns 1 to 6 $G$ | $2.3093{10}^{-3}$ | <1% |
| Crowns 1 to 6 $G$ (IRWIN) | $2.3093{10}^{-3}$ | <1% |
| Crowns 1 to 6 ${K}_{1}$ | $24.491$ | <1% |
| Crowns 1 to 6 ${K}_{2}$ | $0.$ | absolute |

### 3.4 Note

The calculation of $G$ , ${K}_{1}$ , ${K}_{2}$ , $G$ (IRWIN) $=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{1}^{2}+{K}_{2}^{2})$ was performed from 6 different $\theta$ fields, each corresponding to a circular crown centered in $C$ .

## 5 Modelling E

## 6 Modelling F

This Modelling is identical to Modelling B, except that the formalism of the lips taken off (CONFIG_INIT key word DEFI_FOND_FISS indicated at the DECOLLEE value) is used here. There is no physical reason for this choice of formalism, the test being performed only for computer coverage purposes.

The results are identical to those of model B.

## 7 Summary of the results

$G$ calculation is not sensitive to the choice of integration domain.
