# v6.04.173 SSNV173 – Bar cracked with X-FEM

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to validate two aspects of the elementary calculation in the X-FEM [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12):

- integration of a discontinuous quantity by sub-cutting of the element,
- The enrichment of form functions by the Heaviside function.

This test involves a parallelepipedic bar cracked all over its section (we will then speak of an interface), subject to a forced displacement, which results in the separation of both parts of the structure.

The influence of mesh and boundary conditions is also studied.

$\mathrm{2D}$ also studies the case of a plate.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 3D geometry

The structure is a straight parallelepipeda with square base. The dimensions of the bar (see []) are: $\mathit{LX}=5m$ , $\mathit{LY}=5m$ and $\mathit{LZ}=25m$ .

The crack (or rather the interface) is introduced by level set functions directly into the command file using DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator. The interface is present in the middle of the structure through its representation by a level set $\mathit{LSN}$ (see []) equation:

$\mathrm{LSN}$ (for interface plane): $Z-\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}$

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Mesh characteristics

The structure is meshed by a single mesh cell of the HEXA8 type. The interface is present within this element via the level sets.

### 2.2 boundary conditions

Remember that moving under X-FEM is the sum of continuous displacement and discontinuous displacement. In the case of an interface, without crack bottom, the displacement approximation is written as follows:

${u}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i\in {N}_{n}(x)}{a}_{i}{\varphi}_{i}(x)+\sum_{j\in {N}_{n}(x)\cap {K}_{+}}{b}_{j}{\varphi}_{j}(x)\left(\text{-2}{\chi }_{-}\left(\mathit{lsn}(x)\right)\right)+\sum_{j\in {N}_{n}(x)\cap {K}_{-}}{b}_{j}{\varphi}_{j}(x)\left(2{\chi }_{+}\left(\mathit{lsn}(x)\right)\right)$

Où:

${a}_{i}$ and ${b}_{i}$ are the degrees of freedom moving node $i$ ,

${\phi }_{i}$ the form functions associated with node $i$ ,

${N}_{n}(x)$ is all nodes with point $x$ in its support,

${K}_{-}$ is all nodes located in the $\mathit{lsn}(x)<0$ domain, and the support is completely cut by the interface,

${K}_{+}$ is all nodes located in the $\mathit{lsn}(x)>0$ domain, and the support is completely cut by the interface,

${\chi }_{-}(x)$ is a domain characteristic function defined by ${\chi }_{-}(x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}1\text{si}x<0\\ 0\text{si}x\ge 0\end{array}$ ,

${\chi }_{+}(x)$ is a domain characteristic function defined by ${\chi }_{+}(x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\text{si}x<0\\ 1\text{si}x\ge 0\end{array}$ ,

$\mathit{lsn}(x)$ is the normal level-set value at point $x$

For more details, refer to the X-FEM [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) reference documentation.

Since nodes near the interface, i.e. the 8 nodes of mesh are enriched by additional degrees of freedom, boundary conditions write a little differently. This is related to the enrichment of [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) classic form functions by ${\chi }_{-}(x)$ and ${\chi }_{+}(x)$ domain characteristic functions.

Imposing a zero displacement on the nodes of the lower side is like writing a linear relationship between the degrees of freedom. For each node, you impose ${a}_{\mathit{ix}}=0$ (idem following $y$ and $z$ ) when the interface is not in line with the nodes of the mesh. However, if the interface was nodes compliant, imposing zero displacement on the interface, also underlies a hypothesis regarding the displacement jump through the interface. This introduces an additional relationship due to the continuity/saut displacement. The following condition should be imposed on degrees of freedom discontinuity ${\mathrm{2b}}_{\mathit{ix}}=\Delta u=0$ .

For the nodes on the top side, a $z$ displacement is required, and zero in the other two directions, i.e. ${a}_{\mathit{ix}}=0$, ${a}_{\mathit{iy}}=0$ and ${a}_{\mathit{iz}}={10}^{-6}$.

These relationships are imposed **automatically** when keyword DDL_IMPO is used on a node X-FEM. For example, the taxation of the $X$ zero displacement of node X-FEM $\mathit{N1}$ is therefore done in the classic way:

DDL_IMPO=_F(node='N1',DX=0)

### 2.3 Analytical resolution

The solution to such a problem is of course obvious. It is clear that mechanically speaking, the two parts of the structure will detach: the lower part will have a zero displacement and the upper part will have a whole movement equal to the required displacement (see []).

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.173/1000000000000270000001951FCF8F7EDC3D9A8A.png)

**Figure** 2.3-a **: Initial and final status of the structure**

The analytical solution is then the following: all the following $x$ and $y$ moves are zero, all the following $z$ moves below the level set are zero and all the following $z$ moves above the level set are equal to the ${u}_{z}$ displacement at the top of the structure.

### 2.4 Tested quantities and results

The POST_MAIL_XFEM operator allows the cracks to be broken by the X-FEM method. The POST_CHAM_XFEM operator then allows the X-FEM results to be exported to this new mesh. Both operators are only to be used after the calculation at post-processing views. They allow the nodes to be generated just below and above the interface and to display their movements.

The values of the displacement are therefore tested just below and above the interface after convergence of the iterations of the STAT_NON_LINE operator. It is verified that the values determined in [§ 2.3 ] are well reflected.
