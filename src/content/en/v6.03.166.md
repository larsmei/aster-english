# v6.03.166 SSNP166 - Three-point bending beam

**Summary:**

The study of this problem allows to characterize the energy required to propagate a crack in a concrete beam. It allows to validate the constitutive law CZM_EXP_MIX by comparison with the experience of the overall Force-Movement behavior of a beam cut on two simple supports.

It corresponds to one of the experimental essays of Per-Erik Peterson's thesis [bib1].

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

To validate the CZM_EXP_MIX cohesive law more specific to quasi-fragile materials, a concrete-cut beam subjected to 3-point bending is modelled, using experimental data from P.-E. Peterson's thesis [bib1]. This trial is one of the most frequent experimental references to validate models of surface or volume damage of concrete.

A $2m$ length, $200\mathit{mm}$ height and $50\mathit{mm}$ thickness beam, cut in its middle on $100\mathit{mm}$ (half the height of the beam) is in single support on both ends.

**clamped support**: The following Oy is zero at the 2 support points (GROUP_NO: CL1 and CL2). The following 0x displacement is blocked on one of the two supports.

**Movement:** A forced displacement following Oy is applied over the crack (GROUP_NO: Dpl).

## 4 Summary of the results

The exponential law allows to find the peak of the experimental test with an error of less than 2% and the post-pic curve in contrast to a linear cohesive law.
