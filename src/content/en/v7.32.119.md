# v7.32.119 WTNP119 – Flat Modelling of clay swelling with GonfElas

**Summary:**

This test validates the so-called "GonfElas" model. This nonlinear suction-dependent elastic model describes the swelling behaviour of certain types of clay. Typically it is used to model the behavior of compacted clay caps - or bentonite - used to close radioactive waste storage cells. It is a very simplified version (elastic part) of the Barcelona model.

This model is written according to the following couple of variables: net stress and suction (suction is capillary pressure).

This test represents the swelling pressure of a clay cell that is filled with water. This test case is the test case WTNA110 declination to a flat geometry.

### 1.3 Initial conditions

A $t=0$ :

- $\mathit{Pgaz}=1\mathit{atm}$
- $S=0,5$ (i.e. $\mathit{Pc}=44,7\mathit{Mpa}$ and ${p}_{w}=-44.6\mathit{Mpa}$ )
- Total compression stress equal to -1 atm.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

All movements are blocked at the edge ($\mathit{DX}=\mathit{DY}=0$ ).

The flow is zero.

The initial saturation is $\text{50 \%}$: saturation is increased and the evolution of total stress is monitored. By definition, the swelling pressure is the stress obtained with complete resaturation.

For this purpose, a linear downward hair pressure load is imposed on the entire domain in $\mathrm{1s}$ between $44,7\mathit{Mpa}$ and $-10\mathit{Mpa}$.

### 3.2 Results

Figure 3.2-a shows the evolution of total stress as a function of capillary pressure (homogenous in all point, the post-processing is here made to node $\mathit{N3}$). In the saturated part (${P}_{c}\le 0$ ) the decrease of capillary pressure corresponds to an increase in water pressure and the total stress increases linearly. The slope of the curve is observed to be continuous.

The parameters $A$ and ${\beta}_{m}$ were calculated to recover a swelling pressure of $7\mathit{MPa}$. When saturation reaches 1 (or capillary pressure 0), the swelling pressure is given by the following formula:

$\frac{{P}_{\mathit{gf}}}{A}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{{\beta}_{m}}}+\frac{1}{2{\beta}_{m}}$

So we find the classic look of the swelling stress and we check that the curve cuts well the axis of the ordered (${P}_{c}=0$ ) with a value of $7\mathit{Mpa}$.
