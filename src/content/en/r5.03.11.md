# r5.03.11 Single-crystal and polycrystalline elastoviscoplastic behaviours

**Summary:**

The aim of this document is to describe the integration of single-crystal and polycrystalline behaviours.

This integration may be carried out explicitly (Runge–Kutta method with accuracy control and local subdivision of the time step) or implicitly (Newton method with local subdivision of the time step).

These behaviours may be used for the analysis of microstructures (mesh of an aggregate, with a geometric representation of each physical single-crystal grain) or for an analysis of POLYCRISTAL type, a “homogenised” medium which, at every material point (integration point), contains several phases at once, in variable proportions.

We also describe the types of analyses possible with these behaviours as well as the mesh generation procedure.

## 1 Introduction

The general objective of the development of “micro-macro” functionalities in *Code_Aster* is to be able to modularly integrate models at several scales (with the possibility of choosing constitutive laws, localisation rules, types of microstructures). Which can lead to different types of analysis (polycrystalline analyses, use of a *Berveiller-Zaoui* type law or a *“rule in* $\beta$ *”* type law, analyses of multi-crystal aggregates with mesh of a microstructure, etc.).

The approach presented here consists of allowing the decoupling, by modularity, of the different elements which constitute a constitutive law. This flexibility is accessible directly to the user. Furthermore, for the developer, it is possible to easily add a crystalline constitutive law by simply defining the partial derivatives of the problem, in terms of internal variables. This is sufficient if we are content with explicit integration; for implicit integration, the tangent operator must also be defined.

More precisely, for the MONOCRISTAL behaviour aspect, at each integration point of a given finite element, the behaviour is that of a single crystal having a given orientation, and a certain number of slip systems. Each family of slip systems has its own local constitutive law.

In the case of a polycrystalline model, it is assumed that at a material point (integration point of a finite element), several metallurgical phases are present simultaneously, each phase being able to consist of grains with given orientations, each grain having a certain number of slip systems (not necessarily the same for each phase). The representation of the material is based on a simplified self-consistent approach 5 or possibly extended for non-radial or cyclic loadings 4. Each family of slip systems has its own local constitutive law. We thus find separately the crystallographic structure, the law of crystal viscoplasticity and the scale transition rules. This mode of separation is also extended to the law of viscoplasticity itself, with a separation between elasticity, the criterion and the flow rule.

## 2 Formulation of single-crystal and polycrystalline behaviours

### 2.1 Principle of the constitutive laws of the single crystal

The behaviours relating to the slip systems of a single crystal are (in all the behaviours considered) of the elasto-visco-plastic type. They can be constructed on phenomenological bases or on physical bases (dislocation dynamics). For each of the slip directions, the behaviour is *one-dimensional*. It can be broken down into three types of equations:

- Flow relation: $\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}$ represents the plastic slip of the system $s$

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$, with $\dot{{p}_{s}}=∣\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}∣$ and:

- elastoplastic case, a criterion of the type: $F({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})\le 0$ and $F\cdot \dot{{p}_{s}}=0$
- elasto-viscoplastic case, $\dot{{p}_{s}}=f({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$
- $\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}$ represents the kinematic hardening in the case of a phenomenological law and the dislocation density for a physically based behaviour.

Its evolution is the form: $\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$

- for phenomenological laws, isotropic hardening is defined by a function: $R({p}_{s})$.

**These relationships become, after discretisation in time:**

- Flow rule:

$\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$, with, $\Delta {p}_{s}=∣\Delta {\gamma}_{s}∣$

for elastoplastic behaviour a criterion of the type: $F({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})\le 0$ and $F\cdot \Delta {p}_{s}=0$

and for elasto-viscoplastic behaviour, $\Delta {p}_{s}=f({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})\Delta t$

- Evolution of $\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}$: $\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$
- Evolution of isotropic hardening: $R({p}_{s})$

The quantities $({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ are evaluated at the current time for an implicit discretisation and at the previous time for an explicit discretisation.

The available single crystal laws are described below. The names of these relationships correspond to their name in the command DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

### 2.2 Single crystal constitutive laws available

#### 2.2.1 Phenomenological laws

They are constructed from a flow rule, an isotropic hardening law and a kinematic hardening law. The combination of the laws **MONO_VISC1, MONO_ISOT1, MONO_CINE1** corresponds to the crystal law known as Meric-Cailletaud 1.

##### 2.2.1.1 Flow rule MONO_VISC1

$\begin{array}{}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=\Delta {p}_{s}\frac{{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}∣}\\ \Delta {p}_{s}=\Delta t{\langle \frac{∣{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}∣-{R}_{s}({p}_{s})}{k}\rangle }^{n}\end{array}$

The parameters are: $(c,k,n)$ .

##### 2.2.1.2 Flow rule MONO_VISC2

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=\Delta {p}_{s}\frac{{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}∣}\\ \Delta {p}_{s}=\Delta t{\langle \frac{∣{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}∣-{R}_{s}({p}_{s})+\frac{d}{\mathrm{2c}}{(c{\alpha}_{s})}^{2}}{k}\rangle }^{n}\end{array}$

The parameters are: $c,k,n,a,d$.

##### 2.2.1.3 Law of kinematic hardening MONO_CINE1

$\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=\Delta {\gamma}_{s}-d{\alpha}_{s}\Delta {p}_{s}$

The parameter is: $d$.

##### 2.2.1.4 Law of kinematic hardening MONO_CINE2

$\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=\Delta {\gamma}_{s}-d{\alpha}_{s}\Delta {p}_{s}-{(\frac{∣c{\alpha}_{s}∣}{M})}^{m}\frac{{\alpha}_{s}}{∣{\alpha}_{s}∣}$

The parameters then being: $d,M,m,c$.

##### 2.2.1.5 Isotropic hardening law MONO_ISOT1

${R}_{s}(p)={R}_{0}+Q(\sum_{r=1}^{\mathrm{ns}}{h}_{\mathrm{sr}}(1-{e}^{{\mathrm{bp}}_{r}}))$ ; the parameters are: ${R}_{0,}Q,b,{h}_{\mathrm{sr}}$ .

##### 2.2.1.6 Law of isotropic hardening MONO_ISOT2

${R}_{s}(p)={R}_{0}+{Q}_{1}\sum_{r}{h}_{\mathrm{sr}}(1-{e}^{-{b}_{1}{p}_{r}})+{Q}_{2}(1-{e}^{-{b}_{2}{p}_{s}})$ ; the parameters are: ${R}_{0,}{Q}_{1,}{b}_{1,}{b}_{2,}{h}_{\mathrm{sr}},{Q}_{2}$ .

##### 2.2.1.7 Interaction matrix

${h}_{\mathrm{sr}}$ a symmetric square matrix of interaction between slip systems (see appendix 4).

In the case where only one coefficient $H$ is provided, the matrix ${h}_{\mathrm{sr}}$ takes a very simple form:

${h}_{\mathrm{rs}}=1\mathrm{si}r=s$ and ${h}_{\mathrm{rs}}=H\mathrm{si}r\ne s$, with default $H=0$ (identity matrix).

#### 2.2.2 Law resulting from Dislocation Dynamics: MONO_DD_KR

The MONO_DD_KR (Kocks-Rauch) model is applicable to $\mathit{CC}$ type steels (body-centred cubic crystal structure). Its main internal variable is the density of dislocations on each slip system. It also offers the advantage of being valid for a wide range of temperatures [bib7]. The flow is formulated as follows:

If $∣{\tau}^{s}∣>{\tau}_{0}$

${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}=\langle ∣{\tau}^{s}∣-{\tau}_{0}-{\tau}_{\mu}^{s}\rangle$ $\langle \rangle$ positive part

${\tau}_{\mu}^{s}=\frac{{(\mu )}^{2}{\sum}_{u}{a}^{\mathrm{su}}{\alpha}^{u}}{∣{\tau}^{s}∣-{\tau}_{0}}$ with ${\alpha}^{u}={\rho}^{u}{b}^{2}$

otherwise ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}=0$ . In these expressions, $\mu$ designates the shear modulus of the material, $b$ is the Burgers constant, ${\rho}^{u}$ is the dislocation density, ${a}^{\mathrm{su}}$ is the interaction matrix between the 24 slip systems (its particular form, depending on 5 coefficients, is provided in the appendix). The diagonal terms represent the self-hardening of a slip system, the other terms represent the latent hardening.

If $∣{\tau}^{s}∣>{\tau}_{0}$ and ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}>0$

$\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s},{\gamma}_{s})=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}\exp(\frac{-\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})}{{k}_{B}T})\cdot \frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}\cdot \Delta t$

with $\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathit{eff}}^{s})=\Delta {G}_{0}{(1-{(\frac{\langle {\tau}_{\mathit{eff}}^{s}\rangle }{{\tau}_{R}})}^{p})}^{q}$

otherwise

$\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=0$

$\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=0$

$\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})$ is an enthalpy activation corresponding to a free activation in an athermal domain. ${\gamma}_{0,}{\tau}_{R},p,q$ are material data, ${k}_{B}$ the Boltzman constant and $T$ the temperature.

The evolution of the dislocation density is proportional to the square root of the sum of the dislocation densities of all other slip systems:

$\dot{{\rho}_{s}}=\frac{∣\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}∣}{b}(\frac{1}{{\Lambda}^{s}}-{g}_{c}(T){\rho}^{s})$ with $\frac{1}{{\Lambda}^{s}}=\frac{1}{d}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\ne s}{\rho}^{u}}}{K(T)}$

and ${g}_{c}(T)={g}_{\mathrm{c0}}\exp\left[-\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{gc}}}{{k}_{B}T}\right]$ where ${E}_{\mathrm{gc}},{k}_{B},b,d,{g}_{\mathrm{c0}},K(T)$ are material parameters,

hence

$\Delta {\rho}^{s}=∣\Delta {\gamma}^{s}∣(\frac{1}{\mathrm{bd}}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\ne s}{\alpha}^{u}}}{{b}^{2}K}-\frac{{g}_{c}{\alpha}^{s}}{{b}^{3}})$ and therefore:

$\Delta {\alpha}^{s}=∣\Delta {\gamma}^{s}∣(\frac{b}{d}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\ne s}{\alpha}^{u}}}{K}-\frac{{g}_{c}{\alpha}^{s}}{b})=∣g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s})∣\cdot h({\alpha}_{s})$

The parameters are: $\dot{{\gamma}_{0,}}{k}_{B},T,{\tau}_{R},\Delta {G}_{0,}p,q,{\tau}_{0,}\mu ,b,d,K,{g}_{c}$

${k}_{B}$ is the Boltzman constant.

Note: Newton's resolution can lead to negative values for $\Delta {\alpha}^{u}$, so the terms under the root must be protected. To do this, we choose the following modification: $\sqrt{\sum_{u\ne s}\langle {\alpha}^{u}\rangle }$ where $\langle .\rangle$ represents the positive side: $\langle x\rangle =\lbrace \begin{array}{}x\text{si}x>0\\ 0\text{sinon}\end{array}$

Note also that the expression of $\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$, which is a function of $\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})$, leads to a discontinuity in the vicinity of ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}=0$: for ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}<0$, $\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=0$, and $\underset{{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}\to 0\text{*}}{\lim}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}\exp(\frac{-\Delta {G}_{0}}{{k}_{B}T})\cdot \frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}\cdot \Delta t$

#### 2.2.3 Laws from Dislocation Dynamics: MONO_DD_CFC / MONO_DD_CFC_IRRA /MONO_DD_FAT

The formulation of the DD_CFC [8], [9] and DD_FAT [18] laws is constructed from dislocation-dynamics computations. It applies to materials with a face-centred cubic crystal structure ($\mathit{CFC}$) such as austenitic steels. A priori the DD_CFC model is not compatible with a change in loading path, in particular the parameters are not adapted to cyclic loading (when a dislocation “returns” its kinetics are different due to a different interaction with the obstacles).

The equations below are written for each slip system $s$.

The direction of flow is given by:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}=\dot{{p}_{s}}\text{signe}({\tau}_{s})$ and the intensity of the flow by:

$\dot{{p}_{s}}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}({(\frac{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}{{\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}})}^{n}-1)\text{si}∣{\tau}_{s}∣\ge {\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}},\text{sinon}\dot{{p}_{s}}=0$

with $n$ large (to represent a quasi-elastoplastic model: for the CFC, the time dependence is indeed negligible).

Compared to the initial model [8], the threshold was introduced as well as the term $–\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}$ to ensure continuity when the threshold is crossed.

In practice, the parameter $\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}$ is small and the term ${(\frac{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}{{\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}})}^{n}$ very quickly becomes much greater than $1$, because of the large values of the exponent $n$.

${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}$ is a hardening function whose coefficients are provided by the keyword MONO_DD_* (*=CFC, FAT or CC), and which is described in the following §.

In DD_CFC, the evolution of the dislocation density is given by:

$\dot{{\rho}_{s}}=\frac{\dot{{p}_{s}}}{b}(A\frac{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}^{\mathrm{eff}}}{\rho}_{j}}{\sum_{j=1,12}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}^{\mathrm{eff}}{\rho}_{j}}}+B\sum_{j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}^{\mathrm{eff}}{\rho}_{j}}-y{\rho}_{s})$

- $j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)$ are the systems of distinct normals ${n}_{j}\ne {n}_{s}$
- $j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)$ are the identical normal systems ${n}_{j}={n}_{s}$

The initial value of the dislocation density is: ${\rho}_{s}(t=0)={\rho}_{s}^{0}$ (field provided by the user. Check carefully whether it is a global density (for all 12 slip systems) or a density per system).

Like ${a}_{ij}^{\mathrm{eff}}=C{(\rho )}^{2}.{a}_{ij}$, ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\rho )=\mu bC(\rho )\sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\rho}_{j}}$ and $\dot{{\rho}_{s}}=\frac{\dot{{p}_{s}}}{b}(A\frac{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}}{\rho}_{j}}{\sum_{j=1,12}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\rho}_{j}}}+BC(\rho )\sum_{j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\rho}_{j}}-y{\rho}_{s})$

To avoid any problem of conditioning the Jacobian matrix, we choose to scale the system by replacing the dislocation density by: ${\omega}_{s}={b}^{2}\ast {\rho}_{s}$ and solve in ${\omega}_{s}$. This gives:

$\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\omega )\text{avec}{h}_{s}(\omega )=(A\frac{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}}{\omega}_{j}}{\sum_{j=1,12}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\omega}_{j}}}+BC(\omega )\sum_{j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\omega}_{j}}-\frac{y}{b}{\omega}_{s})$

**Note**: Physically, the dislocation density must remain positive. Numerically, for all the terms in $\sqrt{\rho}$ to be legal, it is necessary to test the positivity of $\rho$ and either re-divide the time step if this value becomes negative (by “hesitation” of the algorithm during the iterations) or introduce a positive part into the evolution equations. This last solution is chosen for explicit integration as well as for implicit integration.

The set of differential equations governing the viscoplastic flow of the DD_CFC model is therefore written:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}$ (12 equations)

$\dot{{p}_{s}}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}({(\frac{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}{{\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}})}^{n}-1)\text{si}∣{\tau}_{s}∣\ge {\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}},\text{sinon}\dot{{p}_{s}}=0$ (12 equations)

$\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\langle \omega \rangle )$ (12 equations) with: $\langle {\omega}_{i}\rangle =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\omega}_{i}\text{si}{\omega}_{i}>0\\ 0\text{sinon}\end{array}$

$C(\omega )=0.2+0.8\frac{\ln(\alpha \sqrt{\sum_{i=1,12}\langle {\omega}_{i}\rangle })}{\ln(\alpha b\sqrt{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ref}}})}$

${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\omega )=\mu C(\omega )\sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }$

${h}_{s}(\omega )=(A\frac{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }{\sum_{j=1,12}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }}+BC(\omega )\sum_{j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }-\frac{y}{b}\langle {\omega}_{s}\rangle )$

with ${\omega}_{s}={b}^{2}\ast {\rho}_{s}$

In the MONO_DD_CFC_IRRA case, the evolution of the variables linked to irradiation is given by:

${\dot{\rho}}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}=-\xi \left(\sum_{j\in \mathit{copla}(s)}∣{\dot{\gamma}}_{j}∣\right)\left({\rho}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}-{\rho}^{\mathit{sat}}\right)$         ${\dot{\varphi}}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}=-\zeta \left(\sum_{j\in \mathit{copla}(s)}∣{\dot{\gamma}}_{j}∣\right)\left({\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}-{\varphi}^{\mathit{sat}}\right)$

the expression of ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathit{forest}}$ becomes: ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathit{forest}}=\mu \sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathit{sj}}^{\mathit{eff}}{\omega}_{j}+{\alpha}^{\mathit{loops}}{\varphi}^{\mathit{loops}}{\rho}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}{b}^{2}+{\alpha}^{\mathit{voids}}{\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}{\rho}^{\mathit{voids}}{b}^{2}}$

which, after multiplication by ${b}^{\mathrm{2 }}$: ${\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}={b}^{2}\ast {\rho}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}$ gives:

${\dot{\omega}}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}=-\xi \left(\sum_{j\in \mathit{copla}(s)}∣{\dot{\gamma}}_{j}∣\right)\left({\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}-{\omega}^{\mathit{sat}}\right)$ and ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathit{forest}}=\mu \sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathit{sj}}^{\mathit{eff}}{\omega}_{j}+{\alpha}^{\mathit{loops}}{\varphi}^{\mathit{loops}}{\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}+{\alpha}^{\mathit{voids}}{\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}{\omega}^{\mathit{voids}}}$

> we can notice in passing that the evolution equations of ${\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loop}}$ and ${\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}$ are analytically integrated:
> ${\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}(t+\Delta t)={\omega}^{\mathit{sat}}+\left({\omega}_{s}^{\mathit{loops}}(t)-{\omega}^{\mathit{sat}}\right)\exp\left(-\xi \sum_{j\in \mathit{copla}(s)}∣{\Delta \gamma }_{j}∣\right)$

${\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}(t+\Delta t)={\varphi}^{\mathit{sat}}+\left({\varphi}_{s}^{\mathit{voids}}(t)-{\varphi}^{\mathit{sat}}\right)\exp\left(-\zeta \sum_{j\in \mathit{copla}(s)}∣{\Delta \gamma }_{j}∣\right)$

In the DD_FAT case, the evolution of the dislocation density is given by:

$\dot{{\rho}_{s}}=\frac{\dot{{p}_{s}}}{b}(\frac{1}{d}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\mathrm{\ne }s}{\rho}_{u}}}{K}-{g}_{\mathit{c0}}{\rho}_{s})$

By dimensioning as above, i.e. by setting:

${\omega}_{s}={b}^{2}\times {\rho}_{s}$

we obtain:

$\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\omega )$ with ${h}_{s}(\omega )=(\frac{b}{d}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\mathrm{\ne }s}{\omega}_{u}}}{K}-{g}_{\mathit{c0}}\frac{{\omega}_{s}}{b})$

Hence by introducing the positive parts:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}$ (12 equations)

$\dot{{p}_{s}}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}({(\frac{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}{{\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}})}^{n}-1)\text{si}∣{\tau}_{s}∣\ge {\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}},\text{sinon}\dot{{p}_{s}}=0$ (12 equations)

$\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\langle \omega \rangle )$ (12 equations) with: $\langle {\omega}_{i}\rangle =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\omega}_{i}\text{si}{\omega}_{i}>0\\ 0\text{sinon}\end{array}$

$C(\omega )=1.$

${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\omega )=\mu \sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }$

${h}_{s}(\omega )=(\frac{b}{d}+\frac{\sqrt{\sum_{u\ne s}\langle {\omega}_{u}\rangle }}{K}-{g}_{\mathrm{c0}}\frac{\langle {\omega}_{s}\rangle }{b})$

with ${\omega}_{s}={b}^{2}\ast {\rho}_{s}$

The hardening law of the DD_CFC models is: ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}=\mu b\sqrt{\sum_{j=1,{n}_{s}}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}^{\mathrm{eff}}{\rho}_{j}}$

and ${a}_{ij}^{\mathrm{eff}}=C{(\rho )}^{2}.{a}_{ij}\text{avec}C(\rho )=0.2+0.8\frac{\ln(\alpha b\sqrt{\sum_{i=1,12}{\rho}_{i}})}{\ln(\alpha b\sqrt{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ref}}})}$

where ${a}_{ij}$ represents the interaction matrix between slip systems, the particular form of which is provided in appendix 4.

The hardening law of the DD_FAT model is: ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\rho )=\mu b\sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}{\rho}_{j}}$, which corresponds in the previous model to $C(\rho )=1.$

#### 2.2.4 Laws from Dislocation Dynamics: MONO_DD_CC / MONO_DD_CC_IRRA

The formulation of the DD_CC laws (with or without irradiation) is constructed from computations of dislocation dynamics [17]. It applies to materials with a body-centred cubic crystal structure ($\mathit{CC}$)

The equations below are written for each slip system $s$.

The visco-plastic flow is obtained by harmonic average of two regimes (low temperature and high temperature).

We choose here to reveal the unknowns $\rho$ (representing all the dislocation densities ${\rho}^{s}\text{et}{\rho}_{\mathit{irr}}^{s}$) and ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}$ (which is an additional a priori unknown. We will see that the proposed resolution algorithm allows everything to be expressed as a function of $\rho$).

${\rho}_{\mathit{irr}}^{s}$ designates the density of dislocations induced by irradiation (case of **MONO_DD_CC_IRRA**), and is not used for the behaviour **MONO_DD_CC** in order to limit the number of internal variables.

${\tau}^{s}$ being the split resolved on the system $s$:

- plastic slip is obtained by: $\frac{1}{{\dot{\gamma}}^{s}}=\frac{1}{{\dot{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{nuc}}^{s}}+\frac{1}{{\dot{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{prob}}^{s}}$
- ${\dot{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{nuc}}^{S}={\rho}_{m}^{s}bH\cdot {l}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})\cdot \exp(\frac{-\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})}{{k}_{B}T})\mathrm{sgn}({\tau}_{s})$
- $\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})=\Delta {G}_{0}(1-{(\frac{\langle {\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}\rangle }{{\tau}_{0}})}^{0.5})$ (with: ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}<{\tau}_{0}$ and $0<\Delta G<\Delta {G}_{0}$)
- ${\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}({\tau}^{s},\rho )=∣{\tau}^{s}∣-{\tau}_{c}$     ${\tau}_{c}={\tau}_{F}+\sqrt{({\tau}_{\mathit{LR}}^{s}{(\rho )}^{2}+{\tau}_{\text{LT}}^{s}{(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathit{eff}}^{s})}^{2})}$
- ${\tau}_{\mathrm{LR}}^{s}(\rho )=\mu b\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{s}(\rho ){\rho}^{s}}$
- ${\tau}_{\text{LT}}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})=\max(0;{\alpha}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{s}(\rho )\mu b(\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})}-\frac{1}{2{\alpha}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{s}(\rho ){R}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})+{l}_{c}(T)}))$
- ${\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{s}(\rho )=\sum_{j\ne s}{\rho}^{j}+{\rho}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{s}$
- ${\alpha}_{\mathit{AT}}^{s}(\rho )=\sqrt{\sum_{j\ne s}{a}_{\mathit{AT}}^{\mathit{sj}}\frac{{\rho}^{j}}{{\rho}_{\mathit{tot}}^{s}}+{a}_{\mathit{irr}}\frac{{\rho}_{\mathit{irr}}^{s}}{{\rho}_{\mathit{tot}}^{s}}}$
- $\frac{1}{{\lambda}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})+D}=\min(\sqrt{{\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{s}(\rho )};(D+2{R}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})){\rho}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{s}(\rho ))$
- ${R}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})=\frac{\mu b}{2{\tau}_{0}{(1-\frac{\Delta G({\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})}{\Delta {G}_{0}})}^{2}}$
- ${l}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})=\max({\lambda}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s})-2{\alpha}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{s}(\rho ){R}^{s}(\rho ,{\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s});{l}_{c})$
- ${\dot{\gamma}}_{\mathrm{prob}}^{s}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}{(\frac{∣{\tau}^{s}∣}{{\tau}_{c}})}^{n}\mathrm{sgn}({\tau}^{s})$

The parameters are: ${\rho}_{m}^{s},b,H,\Delta {G}_{0,}{\tau}_{0,}{\tau}_{F},\mu ,{a}_{\mathit{irr}},{l}_{c}(T),D,\dot{{\gamma}_{0,}}n$ and the interaction matrix ${\alpha}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{\mathrm{sr}}$

#### 2.2.5 The laws of evolution of dislocation densities (${\rho}^{s}`et :math:`{\rho}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{s}$) are:

- $\dot{{\rho}^{s}}=\frac{∣\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}∣}{b}\left(\frac{1}{{d}_{\mathit{lath}}}+\frac{{c}_{\mathit{eff}}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathit{AT}}^{\mathit{ss}}{\rho}^{s}}}{{K}_{\mathit{self}}}+\frac{{\alpha}_{\mathit{AT}}^{s}{c}_{\mathit{eff}}{\lambda}^{s}{\rho}^{s}}{{K}_{f}}-{y}^{s}{\rho}^{s}\right)$
- $\dot{{\rho}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{s}}=-\xi {\rho}_{\mathrm{irr}}^{s}∣\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}∣$

with:

$\frac{1}{{y}^{s}}=\frac{1}{{y}_{\mathrm{AT}}^{s}}+\frac{2\pi {\tau}_{\mathrm{eff}}^{s}(\rho )}{\mu b}$ and ${c}_{\mathit{eff}}^{s}=\left(1-\frac{{\tau}_{\mathit{eff}}^{s}}{{\tau}_{0}}\right)$,

with parameters ${d}_{\mathit{lath}}{K}_{\mathit{self}}{K}_{f}\xi {y}_{\mathit{AT}}^{s}$

**Notes**:

- Physically, the dislocation density must remain positive. Numerically, for all the terms in $\sqrt{\rho}$ to be legal, it is necessary to test the positivity of $\rho$ and either re-divide the time step if this value becomes negative (by “hesitation” of the algorithm during the iterations) or introduce a positive part into the evolution equations. - In the case where the elasticity is not isotropic, the value of $\mu$ used above is independent of the elasticity coefficients, because it is obtained by homogenisation.

### 2.3 Slip systems and overall behaviour of the single crystal

A single crystal is composed of one or more families of slip systems (cubic, octahedral, basal, prismatic, etc.), each family comprising a certain number of systems (12 for the octahedral family for example).

Each family of slip system is associated with a flow rule, a type of kinematic and isotropic hardening, and parameter values for these laws. In other words, we do not plan to vary the behavioural relationships or the coefficients within the same family of slip systems. On the other hand, from one family to another, the constitutive laws can change, as well as the values of the parameters.

A slip system is determined by an orientation tensor (or Schmid tensor) constructed from the crystallographic definitions of:

- the slip direction of the system $s$ (defined by the unit vector ${m}_{s}$)
- and the normal to the slip plane (defined by the unit vector ${n}_{s}$).

#### 2.3.1 Small strains, initial configuration

In small strains, the orientation tensor is defined by:

${\mu}_{ij}^{s}=\frac{1}{2}({m}_{i}\otimes {n}_{j}+{n}_{j}\otimes {m}_{i})$

From the point of view of behaviour at the material point, this tensor is used for the calculation of the resolved shear stress.

${\tau}^{s}=\Sigma :{\mu}^{s}$

and that of the overall viscoplastic strain rate ${E}^{\mathrm{vp}}$, defined from the knowledge of the slip rates $\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}$ for all slip systems:

${\dot{E}}_{ij}^{\mathrm{vp}}=\sum_{s\in g}\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}{\mu}_{ij}^{s}$

In addition, the single crystal can be oriented with respect to the global coordinate definition axes. This orientation is defined for each mesh cell or mesh-cell group (typically for each grain) by the data of 3 nautical angles or 3 Euler angles.

The components of the orientation tensor ${\mu}^{s}$, defined in the frame linked to the single crystal, are then expressed in the global frame using these nautical angles. In small strains, they are fixed in the initial configuration.

Furthermore, at the level of a Gaussian point, we postulate an elasticity relation on the global tensors:

- macroscopic total strain $E$
- macroscopic viscoplastic strain ${E}^{\mathrm{vp}}$
- macroscopic constraint: $\Sigma$

$\Sigma =\Lambda (E-{E}^{\mathrm{th}}-{E}^{\mathrm{vp}})$

where $\Lambda$ represents the elasticity operator (isotropic or orthotropic)

#### 2.3.2 Small strains, rotation of the crystal lattice

An approximation of the effect of the strain on the behaviour of the single crystal consists of taking into account the rotation of the crystal lattice. This rotation is interesting for two reasons:

- its effective consideration in the analyses is consistent with strains that are not infinitely small (in current analyses, it is not uncommon to reach 10%). It is a preliminary to a complete model of large strains on the single crystal.
- This is interesting information for post-processing, because it can be compared with experimental results.

We describe here (following 9, 10, 11) the method of taking into account the rotation of crystal lattice in the general framework of small strains. The main physical hypotheses are:

- knowing the vorticity tensor $\stackrel{ˆ}{\dot{\omega}}$ or rotation rate, which is the antisymmetric part of the tensor $L=\dot{F}{F}^{\text{-1}}$), we postulate an additive decomposition into a part called plastic rotation $\stackrel{ˆ}{\dot{{\omega}^{\mathrm{p}}}}$ (antisymmetric part of the plastic rotation) and an “elastic” part $\stackrel{ˆ}{\dot{{\omega}^{e}}}$, which will make it possible to calculate the rotation of the crystal lattice;
- we admit for the moment that we can calculate the rotation rate tensor, while integrating the behaviour with a linearised strain tensor. This limitation naturally falls with the complete model in large strains, but we assume here that we can reason in a decoupled way. We therefore have, in incremental form:

$\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{\omega}=\frac{1}{2}(\Delta F{F}^{\text{-1}}-{(\Delta F{F}^{\text{-1}})}^{T})$ and $\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{e}}=\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{\omega}-\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{p}}$ which are antisymmetric tensors.

The calculation of $\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{p}}$ is carried out at the end of the integration: it is stored and used in the following iteration (a purely implicit calculation would involve solving the system of equations described in paragraph 3 by adding all the equations on the orientation of the network, which would greatly increase the calculation).

Once the rotation increment $\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{e}}$ is known, we extract the “axial” vector [see for example R5.03.40]: $\Delta {\omega}^{e}$ such that $\Delta {\omega}^{e}\wedge U=\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{e}}\cdot U$ and we calculate the angle of rotation and the vector which corresponds to the axis of rotation:

$\Delta \theta =\parallel \Delta {\omega}^{e}\parallel$ and ${n}_{\omega}=\frac{\Delta {\omega}^{e}}{\Delta \theta }$.

The rotation increment matrix is then obtained by the Euler-Rodrigues formula:

$\Delta Q={I}_{d}+\sin(\Delta \theta )\stackrel{ˆ}{{n}_{\omega}}+(1-\cos(\Delta \theta ))\stackrel{ˆ}{{n}_{\omega}}\cdot \stackrel{ˆ}{{n}_{\omega}}$ where $\stackrel{ˆ}{{n}_{\omega}}$ is the antisymmetric matrix resulting from ${\mathrm{n}}_{\omega}$

Which makes it possible to update the lattice rotation matrix: ${Q}_{+}=\Delta Q\cdot {Q}_{-}$

It allows the orientation tensor to be updated by: $\begin{array}{}{n}^{s}={Q}_{+}{n}_{0}^{s}\\ {m}^{s}={Q}_{+}{m}_{0}^{s}\end{array}$.

The behaviour is then integrated with this definition of the crystal lattice.

It then remains to update the plastic rotation increment: $\Delta \stackrel{ˆ}{{\omega}^{p}}=\frac{1}{2}{\sum}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}^{s}({m}^{s}\otimes {n}^{s}-{n}^{s}\otimes {m}^{s})$

Additional internal variables are: $Q,\Delta {\omega}^{p},\Delta {\omega}^{e},\theta$

In practice lattice rotation is taken into account via the keyword ROTA_RESEAU of the DEFI_COMPOR [U4.43.06](doc:u4.43.06) command. It is only effective for the implicit integration of MONOCRISTAL behaviour.

#### 2.3.3 Finite strains

Expected properties: the chosen formalism must be objective, and must preserve plastic incompressibility ($\det({F}^{p})=1$). We use the multiplicative decomposition $F={F}^{e}{F}^{p}$ with the Mandel relaxed isocline intermediate configuration.

The chosen (hyper-)elasticity law uses the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor $S={J}^{e}{F}^{e-1}\sigma {F}^{e-T}$ and the Green–Lagrange strain associated with the elastic deformation gradient:

${E}_{\mathrm{GL}}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}({{F}^{e}}^{T}{F}^{e}–{I}_{d})$     $S=\Lambda :{E}_{\mathrm{GL}}^{e}$ .

On each slip system $s$, the resolved shear stress is written with the Mandel tensor $M$ defined by $M={J}_{e}{{F}^{e}}^{T}\sigma {{F}^{e}}^{\text{-T}}$ in the following way: ${\tau}_{s}=M:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}$ (cf. 7, 14).

Indeed, the power of the internal efforts can be written: $\frac{1}{\rho}\sigma :D=\frac{1}{\rho}\sigma :L=\frac{1}{\rho}\sigma :\dot{F}{F}^{\text{-1}}$

and $\dot{F}{F}^{\text{-1}}=(\dot{{F}^{\text{e}}}{F}^{p}+{F}^{\text{e}}\dot{{F}^{p}}){({F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}{({F}^{e})}^{\text{-1}}={L}^{\text{e}}+{F}^{\text{e}}{L}^{p}{({F}^{e})}^{\text{-1}}$

therefore $\frac{1}{\rho}\sigma :D=\frac{1}{\rho}\left[\sigma :{L}^{\text{e}}+\sigma :({F}^{\text{e}}{L}^{p}{({F}^{\text{e}})}^{\text{-1}})\right]=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}S:\dot{{E}_{\mathrm{GL}}^{\text{e}}}+\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}M:{L}^{p}$

The power dissipated plastically is written: $M:{L}^{p}$ and also: $\sum_{s}\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}{\tau}_{s}$, therefore $M:{L}^{p}=M:\sum_{s}\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}{\mu}_{s}=\sum_{s}\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}M:{\mu}_{s}$, hence ${\tau}_{s}=M:{\mu}_{s}$

For each crystal constitutive law, the flow and evolution equations of the internal variables are classically written for each slip system:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ or $\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$

On the other hand, the relationship linking plastic strain to the slip of active systems is now written: ${\dot{F}}^{p}=(\sum_{s}\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}){F}^{p}$

### 2.4 Behaviour of the homogenised polycrystal

In the case of homogenised polycrystal, each single-crystalline phase must be defined by its orientation, its proportion (volume fraction) and the associated behaviour. An overall orientation of the polycrystal can be defined. It is also necessary to define a localisation rule.

The single-crystal behaviour is constructed as before from the previous elasto-visco-plastic behaviour and data from families of slip systems.

In addition to the single-crystal behaviour described previously, we add a modelling scale, which represents the assembly of the phases.

At the level of a Gauss point, we postulate an elasticity relation on the global tensors:

- macroscopic total strain $E$
- macroscopic viscoplastic strain ${E}^{\mathrm{VP}}$
- macroscopic constraint: $\Sigma$

$\Sigma =\Lambda (E-{E}^{\mathrm{th}}-{E}^{\mathrm{VP}})$

$\Lambda$ represents the elasticity operator (isotropic or orthotropic)

- In addition, knowing all the internal variables relating to the slip systems of each phase, the behavioural parameters of each phase, the orientations and volume fractions of each phase, and the type of localisation method,
- for each single-crystal phase (or “grain”), defined by an orientation and a proportion ${f}_{g}$, a stress location relation, of the general form (to be expressed in the local reference frame of each phase)

${\sigma}_{g}=L(\Sigma ,{E}^{\mathrm{vp}},{\varepsilon}_{g}^{\mathrm{vp}},{\beta}_{g})$

and for each slip system of each phase, behavioural relations relating to each slip system, similar to the case of the single crystal:

- Flow rule:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$, with, $\dot{{p}_{s}}=∣\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}∣$ and $\dot{{p}_{s}}=f({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$

- Evolution of kinematic hardening or dislocation density: $\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$
- Evolution of isotropic hardening defined by a function: $R({p}_{s})$
- Viscoplastic strains of the phase: ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{v{p}_{g}}=\sum_{s\in g}\dot{{\gamma}^{s}}{\mu}_{ij}^{s}$

What then remains are the homogenisation equations: ${\dot{E}}^{\mathrm{vp}}=\sum_{g}{f}_{g}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{g}^{\mathrm{vp}}$

Two location relations of type ${\sigma}_{g}=L(\Sigma ,{E}^{\mathrm{vp}},{\varepsilon}_{g}^{\mathrm{vp}},{\beta}_{g})$ are available:

- The Berveiller-Zaoui relation [bib5] established on the notion of self-consistency. This relationship is validated under certain conditions, namely: isotropy of the material (recent work implements extensions to cubic elasticity), homogeneous elastic behaviour and monotonic loading:

${\sigma}_{ij}^{g}={\Sigma}_{ij}+\alpha {\mu}^{\mathit{loca}}\left({E}_{ij}^{\mathit{vp}}-{\epsilon}_{ij}^{v{p}_{g}}\right)$ $\frac{1}{\alpha}=1+\frac{3}{2}{\mu}^{\mathit{loca}}\frac{∥{E}_{ij}^{\mathit{vp}}∥}{{J}_{2}({\Sigma}_{ij})}$

- The second relation, inspired by the previous one, and developed more particularly for cyclic loadings [bib4] makes it possible to give a good description to schematize the interactions between the grains:
- ${\sigma}_{ij}^{g}={\Sigma}_{ij}+{\mu}^{\mathit{loca}}\left({B}_{ij}-{\beta}_{ij}^{g}\right)$ ${B}_{ij}=\sum_{g}{f}_{g}{\beta}_{ij}^{g}$
- ${\dot{\beta}}_{ij}^{g}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{v{p}_{g}}-D({\beta}_{ij}^{g}-\delta {\varepsilon}_{ij}^{v{p}_{g}})∥{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{v{p}_{g}}∥$

where ${\mu}^{\mathit{loca}}$, $D$ and $\delta$ are characteristic parameters of the material and the temperature.

## 3 Local integration and numerical implementation

### 3.1 System of equations to solve

#### 3.1.1 MONOCRISTAL type behaviour

The local behaviour of the single crystal is defined, at a given instant of the discretisation in time and at the level of an integration point of a finite element, by the data:

- the macroscopic stress tensor at the previous instant $\Sigma ({t}_{i-1})={\Sigma}^{-}$,
- internal variables at the previous instant, for each slip system: ${\alpha}_{s}({t}_{i-1}),{\gamma}_{s}({t}_{i-1}),{p}_{s}({t}_{i-1})$,
- and the total strain increase tensor provided by iteration n of the global resolution algorithm $\Delta E=\Delta {E}_{i}^{n}$ (with the notations of [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)).

Integration consists of finding:

- the macroscopic stress tensor $\Sigma =\Sigma ({t}_{i})$
- and the internal variables ${\alpha}_{s}={\alpha}_{s}({t}_{i})$, ${\gamma}_{s}={\gamma}_{s}({t}_{i})$, ${p}_{s}={p}_{s}({t}_{i})$

verifying the behavioural equations in each slip system (which are one-dimensional relations), and the transition relations between the macroscopic tensors and all the slip directions. Notation: we write the equations in discretised form in the following way:

- explicit, if the quantities noted ${A}^{\text{+/ -}}$ are evaluated at the instant ${t}_{i-1}$: ${A}^{\text{+/ -}}={A}^{-}=A({t}_{i-1})$
- implicit, if they are evaluated at the moment ${t}_{i}$: ${A}^{\text{+/ -}}={A}^{+}=A({t}_{i})$

The equations to be integrated can be put in the following general form:

Given, at a Gaussian point, the tensors:

$\Delta E$: strain variation at instant ${t}_{i}$,

$E({t}_{i-1})={E}^{-}$: deformation at instant ${t}_{i-1}$,

$\Sigma ({t}_{i-1})={\Sigma}^{-}$: macroscopic stress at time ${t}_{i-1}$,

${\alpha}_{s}({t}_{i-1}),{\gamma}_{s}({t}_{i-1}),{p}_{s}({t}_{i-1})$: internal variables for each slip system at ${t}_{i-1}$,

You need to find:

$\Sigma =\Sigma ({t}_{i})$: macroscopic constraint at instant ${t}_{i}$, in the reference frame corresponding to the global orientation

${\alpha}_{s}={\alpha}_{s}({t}_{i})$

${\gamma}_{s}={\gamma}_{s}({t}_{i})$

${p}_{s}={p}_{s}({t}_{i})$

checking:

${D}^{-1}\Sigma =({D}_{-}^{-1}){\Sigma}^{-}+(\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\text{th}}-\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}})$, where $\Lambda$ can depend on temperature, and can correspond to orthotropic elasticity.

$\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}=\sum_{s}{\mu}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$

for each slip system (of all system families):

${n}_{s}$ flow relations: either in viscoplasticity $\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\alpha}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\gamma}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{p}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}})$

with $\Delta {p}_{s}=f({\tau}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\alpha}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\gamma}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{p}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}})$

either in plasticity $F({\tau}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\alpha}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\gamma}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{p}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}})\le 0$, $F\Delta {p}_{s}=0$,

with $\Delta {p}_{s}=\mid \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\mid$

${n}_{s}$ equations of evolution of kinematic hardening: $\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=h({\tau}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\alpha}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{\gamma}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}},{p}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}})$

${n}_{s}$ evolution equations of isotropic hardening: ${R}_{s}({p}_{s}^{\text{+/ -}})$

This is resolved either explicitly ( *Runge_Kutta* ) or implicitly ( *Newton* ).

#### 3.1.2 POLYCRISTAL type behaviour

The discretised behavioural relations are:

Given (at a Gaussian point) the global tensors:

- total-strain increment $\Delta E$,
- total strain at the previous instant, $E({t}_{i-1})={E}^{-}$
- constraint at the previous moment: $\Sigma (t{}_{i-1}\text{})={\Sigma}^{-},$
- all internal variables ${\alpha}_{s}^{-},{\gamma}_{s}^{-},{p}_{s}^{-}$ relating to the slip systems of each phase,
- the behavioural parameters of each phase, the orientations and volume fractions of each phase, and the type of localisation method.

We must find $\Sigma =\Sigma ({t}_{i}),{\alpha}_{s}=\alpha {}_{s}\text{}({t}_{i}),{\gamma}_{s}={\gamma}_{s}({t}_{i}),{p}_{s}={p}_{s}({t}_{i})$ checking:

- at the level of the Gauss point: $\Sigma =\Lambda ({\Lambda}_{-}^{-1}){\Sigma}^{-}+\Lambda (\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\text{th}}-\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}})$, in the global reference frame,
- for each phase (or “grain”), defined by an orientation and a proportion ${f}_{g}$, a stress location relation, of the general form (to be expressed in the local reference frame of each phase)

${\sigma}_{g}=L(\Sigma ,{E}^{\text{vp}},{\varepsilon}_{g}^{\text{vp}},{\beta}_{g})$

- and for each slip system of each phase:
- $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{g}^{\text{vp}}}=\sum_{s}{m}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$
- ${n}_{s}$ equations: ${\tau}^{s}={\Sigma}_{ij}:{\mu}_{ij}^{s}$
- ${n}_{s}$ flow relations: $\Delta {\gamma}_{s}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ with $\Delta {p}_{s}=\mid \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\mid$
- ${n}_{s}$ evolutions of hardening: $\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$
- $F({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})\le 0,F\cdot \Delta {p}_{s}=0$, (in time-independent plasticity)

What then remains are the homogenisation equations: $\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}=\sum_{g}{f}_{g}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{g}^{\text{vp}}$

The viscoplastic behaviours relating to each slip system are identical to the case of the microstructure.

In the current version of *Code_Aster*, these behavioural relationships are integrated only explicitly.

### 3.2 Implicit solution of MONOCRISTAL behaviour

#### 3.2.1 Implicit solution of phenomenological laws

We must solve a system of the following general form:

$R(Y)=\lbrace \begin{array}{}s(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ e(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ {n}_{s}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}a(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ g(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ p(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$ $=\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}\Sigma -({\Lambda}_{-}^{\text{- 1}}){\Sigma}^{-}-(\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\text{th}}-\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}})\\ \Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}-{\sum}_{s}{\mu}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}\\ {n}_{s}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\Delta {\alpha}_{s}-h({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ \Delta {\gamma}_{s}-g({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}-f({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$ =0

In a more contracted way, we pose:

$R(\Delta y)=0=\left[\begin{array}{c}s(\Delta y)\\ e(\Delta y)\\ a(\Delta y)\\ g(\Delta y)\\ p(\Delta y)\end{array}\right]$ with $\Delta y=(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \Sigma \\ \Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}\\ \Delta {\alpha}_{s}\\ \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\\ \Delta {p}_{s}\end{array})$

To solve this system of $6+6+3{n}_{s}$ nonlinear equations (in 3D), we use a Newton method: we construct a sequence of solution vectors as follows:

${Y}_{k+1}={Y}_{k}-(\frac{\text{dR}}{{\text{dY}}_{k}}{)}^{-1}R({Y}_{k})$

It is therefore necessary to define the initial values ${Y}_{0}$, and calculate the Jacobian matrix of the system: $\frac{\text{dR}}{{\text{dY}}_{k}}$ (this is detailed in the appendix for the viscoplastic behaviours described previously). It has the following form:

$J=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}\frac{\partial s}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial e}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial a}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial p}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\end{array}\right]$

However, we can simplify this system of equations by reducing its size, in order to improve the performance of the integration.

In the expression of the 6 components of the stress tensor, $\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}$ can be expressed as a function of $\sum_{s}{\mu}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$ therefore an equation can be eliminated from the overall system to be solved.

Like $\Delta p=\mid \Delta \gamma \mid$, the equation in $\Delta p$ can be eliminated.

In the case of **MONO_CINE1**, $\Delta \alpha$ is expressed directly as a function of $\Delta \gamma$. The size of the system to be solved is therefore reduced to $6+{n}_{s}$ equations in $\Delta \gamma$, with ${n}_{s}$ the number of slip systems.

In the case of **MONO_CINE2**, we calculate $\Delta \alpha$ numerically as a function of $\Delta \gamma$, by a secant method.

The equation relating to the stress components reduces to:

${R}_{1}^{i}({\Sigma}_{i},{\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},{R}_{s}({p}_{r}))={\Lambda}^{-1}{\Sigma}_{i}-({\Lambda}_{-}^{-1}{\Sigma}_{{i}_{-}})-\Delta \varepsilon +\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}+\sum_{s}{\mu}_{s}{g}_{s}({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},{R}_{s}({p}_{r}))=0$

The equation relating to the hardening parameter $\Delta \gamma$ is written in the form:

${R}_{2}^{s}({\Sigma}_{i},{\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},{R}_{s}({p}_{r}))=\Delta {\gamma}_{s}-{g}_{s}({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},{R}_{s}({p}_{r}))=0$

with $i=1à6$, $s=1$ to ${n}_{s}$ and $r=1$ to ${n}_{s}$

In the case of **MONO_VISC1**:

${g}_{s}=\Delta t{\langle \frac{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}\mid -{R}_{s}({p}_{r})}{k}\rangle }^{n}\frac{{\tau}_{s}-{\mathrm{c\alpha }}_{s}}{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}\mid }$ with ${\tau}_{s}=\Sigma :{\mu}_{s}$

In the case of **MONO_VISC2**:

${g}_{s}=\Delta t{\langle \frac{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}\mid -{R}_{s}({p}_{r})+\frac{d}{\mathrm{2c}}{(c{\alpha}_{s})}^{2}}{k}\rangle }^{n}\frac{{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}}{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}\mid }$

The Jacobian matrix of the reduced system is written in the form:

$J=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{\partial {R}_{1}^{i}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{j}}& \frac{\partial {R}_{1}^{i}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial {R}_{2}^{s}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{i}}& \frac{\partial {R}_{2}^{s}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{r}}\end{array}\right]$ of dimension $(6+{n}_{s})\times (6+{n}_{s})$

The different terms of the Jacobian matrix are:

$\frac{\partial {R}_{1}^{i}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{j}}={({\Lambda}^{-1})}_{ij}+\sum_{s}{({\mu}_{s})}_{i}\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{j}}={({\Lambda}^{-1})}_{ij}+\sum_{s}{({\mu}_{s})}_{i}\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}\frac{\partial {\tau}_{s}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{j}}={({\Lambda}^{-1})}_{ij}+\sum_{s}{({\mu}_{s})}_{i}{({\mu}_{s})}_{j}\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}$

$\frac{\partial {R}_{2}^{s}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{i}}=\frac{\partial (-{g}_{s}({\tau}_{s},{\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s},{\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s},{R}_{s}({p}_{r})))}{\partial {\Sigma}_{i}}=\frac{\partial (-{g}_{s})}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}.\frac{\partial {\tau}_{s}}{\partial {\Sigma}_{i}}=-{\mu}_{s}^{i}.\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}$

$\frac{\partial {R}_{1}^{i}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}}=\sum_{s}{\mu}_{s}^{i}.\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}}$

$\frac{\partial {R}_{2}^{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{\mathrm{rs}}}={\delta}_{\mathrm{sr}}-\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}$ with $\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}=\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}.\frac{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}}{\delta}_{\mathit{sr}}+\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {R}_{s}}.\frac{\partial {R}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}$

In the expressions appearing above, the terms $\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}},\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}},\frac{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}},\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {R}_{s}},\frac{\partial {R}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {p}_{r}}$ depend on the laws of flow and hardening:

- In the case of **MONO_VISC1**:

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}=\frac{n\Delta t}{{k}^{n}}{\langle \mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}\mid -{R}_{s}\left({p}_{r}\right)\rangle }^{n-1}={H}_{s}\left({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s}\left({p}_{r}\right)\right)$

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}=-c.{H}_{s}({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s})$

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {R}_{s}}=-{H}_{s}\left({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s}\right)\text{sgn}s$ with $\text{sgn}s=\frac{{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}}{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}\mid }$

- In the case of **MONO_VISC2**:

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {\tau}_{s}}={H}_{s}\left({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\alpha}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s}\left({p}_{r}\right)\right)=\frac{n}{{k}^{n}}{\langle \mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}\mid -{R}_{s}\left({p}_{r}\right)+\frac{d}{\mathrm{2c}}{\left(c{\alpha}_{s}\right)}^{2}\rangle }^{n-1}$

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}={H}_{s}({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s})(-c\text{sgn}s+d{\alpha}_{s}\Delta {\alpha}_{s})$

$\frac{\partial {g}_{s}}{\partial {R}_{s}}=-{H}_{s}\left({\tau}_{s},\Delta {\gamma}_{s},{R}_{s}\right)\text{sgn}s$ with $\text{sgn}s=\frac{{\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}}{\mid {\tau}_{s}-c{\alpha}_{s}-a{\gamma}_{s}\mid }$

- In the case of **MONO_CINE1:**

$\frac{\partial \Delta {\alpha}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}=\frac{1-d{\alpha}_{s}^{-}\text{sgn}s}{{\left(1+d\mid \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\mid \right)}^{2}}$

- In the case of **MONO_CINE2:**

$\frac{\partial {\alpha}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}$ **;** This derivation requires the solution of a non-linear equation using Newton's method

- In the case of **MONO_ISOT1:**

$\frac{\partial {R}_{s}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}=\frac{\partial \left[Q\sum_{k}{h}_{\text{sk}}(1-{e}^{-{\text{bp}}_{k}})\right]}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial {p}_{r}}\left[Q\sum_{k}{h}_{\text{sk}}(1-{e}^{-{\text{bp}}_{k}})\right]\frac{\partial {p}_{r}}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{r}}={\text{bQh}}_{\text{sr}}{e}^{-{\text{bp}}_{r}}.\text{sgn}r$

#### 3.2.5 Implicit integration algorithm in large strains

From ${U}_{n}$ and $\Delta U$, we can calculate ${F}_{n}$, $\Delta F$ and ${F}_{n+1}=\Delta F{F}_{n}$. moreover, we know the results of the time increment $n$: Cauchy constraints ${\sigma}_{n}$ internal variables.

This involves determining ${\sigma}_{n+1}$ and the internal variables ${({\gamma}_{s})}_{n+1}$ (or ${({\alpha}_{s})}_{n+1}$) solutions of the system of $6+{n}_{s}$ equations:

${S}_{n+1}=\Lambda .\frac{1}{2}({{F}_{n+1}^{e}}^{T}{F}_{n+1}^{e}–{I}_{d})$

and for each slip system:

${(\Delta {\gamma}_{s})}_{n+1}=\Delta tg({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ or $\Delta {\alpha}_{s}=\Delta th({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$

with ${\tau}_{s}(S)={M}_{n+1}:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}=({{F}^{e}}_{n+1}^{T}{{F}^{e}}_{n+1}{(S)}_{n+1}):{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}=\left[(2{\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}S+{I}_{d})S\right]:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}$

in fact following 7 and 13, we consider here Piola-Kirchhoff constraints measured from the intermediate configuration, therefore only the elastic part of the transformation gradient intervenes.

${F}_{n+1}^{\text{e}}={F}_{n+1}{({F}_{n+1}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}=\Delta F{F}_{n}^{\text{e}}{(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$

Several choices are possible for the calculation of ${(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$:

- ${(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}=\exp(-\sum\Delta {\gamma}_{s}(S,{\omega}_{s}){m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s})$ 12 which has the advantage of preserving zero trace of plastic strains, but which can present an additional cost;
- ${(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}={({I}_{d}+\sum\Delta {\gamma}_{s}(S,{\omega}_{s}){m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s})}^{\text{-1}}{\left(det({I}_{d}+\sum\Delta {\gamma}_{s}(S,{\omega}_{s}){m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s})\right)}^{-\frac{1}{3}}$  13
- ${(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}=({I}_{d}-\sum\Delta {\gamma}_{s}(S,{\omega}_{s}){m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}){\left(det({I}_{d}-\sum\Delta {\gamma}_{s}(S,{\omega}_{s}){m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s})\right)}^{-\frac{1}{3}}$

The gradients ${F}^{\text{e}}$ are stored as internal variables (removing the identity tensor)

In post-processing we calculate the Cauchy constraints: ${\sigma}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{\det{F}_{n+1}^{e}}{F}_{n+1}^{e}{(S)}_{n+1}{{F}_{n+1}^{e}}^{\text{T}}$

or Kirchhoff constraints: ${\tau}_{n+1}=\det{F}_{n+1}{\sigma}_{n+1}={F}_{n+1}^{e}{(S)}_{n+1}{{F}_{n+1}^{e}}^{\text{T}}$

because $\det{F}_{n+1}=\det{F}_{n+1}^{e}\det{F}_{n+1}^{p}=\det{F}_{n+1}^{e}$

The problem to be solved at each integration point therefore has the goal of knowing the internal variables. ${({\gamma}_{s})}_{n}$ ${({\alpha}_{s})}_{n}$ ${({p}_{s})}_{n}$ ${F}_{n}^{e}$ to determine ${\sigma}_{n+1}$ and the internal variables ${({\gamma}_{s})}_{n+1}$ (${({\alpha}_{s})}_{n+1}$, ${F}_{n+1}^{e}$) solutions of the system to $6+{n}_{s}$ equations:

${R}_{1}={(\Lambda )}^{\text{-1}}{S}_{n+1}-\frac{1}{2}({{F}_{n+1}^{e}}^{T}{F}_{n+1}^{e}–{I}_{d})=0$

and for each slip system:

${R}_{2}={(\Delta {\gamma}_{s})}_{n+1}-\Delta tg({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=0$ or ${R}_{2}=\Delta {\alpha}_{s}-\Delta t\cdot h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})=0$

with ${\tau}_{s}(S)=\left[(2{\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}S+{I}_{d})S\right]:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}$

> ${F}_{n+1}^{e}=\Delta F{F}_{n}^{e}{(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$

The resolution of this system of nonlinear equations can be done classically by Newton's algorithm [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14).

The vector of unknowns is composed of: ${S}_{n+1}$ (6 components) and $\Delta {\alpha}_{s}$ (${n}_{s}$ components).

The calculation of the Jacobian matrix is described in the appendix.

**Initialisation**,

The algorithm is initialised as follows:

We put ${F}_{n+1}^{\text{e-trial}}={F}_{n+1}{({F}_{n}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$ which amounts to supposing that ${F}_{n+1}^{p}=\Delta {F}^{p}{F}_{n}^{p}={I}_{d}{F}_{n}^{p}$

We calculate ${R}_{1}$ with ${F}_{n+1}^{\text{e}}={F}_{n+1}^{\text{e-trial}}$ then all the equations ${R}_{2}$ If no threshold is reached, then ${F}_{n+1}^{\text{e}}={F}_{n+1}^{\text{e-trial}}$. Otherwise it is necessary to solve the system ${R}_{1}$ ${R}_{2}$ .

We can choose to keep the tensor ${F}_{n+1}^{e}$ as an internal variable. to more easily calculate ${F}_{n+1}^{e}=\Delta F{F}_{n}^{e}{(\Delta {F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$

In practice we store ${F}^{e}-{I}_{d}$ in the internal variables $V(6+3\ast {n}_{s}+1)$ to $V(6+3\ast {n}_{s}+9)$

We also keep ${F}^{p}-{I}_{d}$ in the internal variables $V(6+3\ast {n}_{s}+10)$ to $V(6+3\ast {n}_{s}+19)$

In post-processing, the tensors ${m}_{s}$ and ${n}_{s}$ can be updated using the rotation resulting from the polar decomposition ${F}^{e}={R}^{e}{U}^{e}$: ${m}_{s}={R}^{e}{{m}_{s}}_{0}$ ${n}_{s}={R}^{e}{{n}_{s}}_{0}$

For the calculation of the polar decomposition, an exact algorithm is proposed in 12.

Next 13, they can also be transported in the current configuration by:

${m}_{s}={F}^{e}{{m}_{s}}_{0}$ and ${n}_{s}={F}^{e-T}{{n}_{s}}_{0}$

#### 3.2.6 Convergence criteria used for implicit solutions

The criterion for stopping the iterations concerns the relative nullity of the residue: $\frac{{∣∣R(Y)∣∣}_{h}}{{∣∣{R}_{\mathrm{ref}}∣∣}_{h}}<{\varepsilon}_{c}$. where ${\varepsilon}_{c}$ represents the tolerance given by RESI_INTE_RELA. The problem is to choose ${R}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ as well as the standard ${∣∣.∣∣}_{h}$

For homogeneity with the global Newton algorithm used in STAT_NON_LINE[R5.03.02], and to avoid unnecessary iterations when the initial residue is very small, we choose:

$\frac{\underset{i=1,6}{\max}∣{R}_{i}(Y)∣}{\underset{i=1,6}{\max}∣{E}_{i}^{\mathrm{tr}}∣}<{\varepsilon}_{c}$ with ${E}^{\mathrm{tr}}={\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}{\Sigma}^{-}+\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{th}}$

$\frac{\underset{i=7,n}{\max}∣{R}_{i}(Y)∣}{\underset{i=7,n}{\max}∣{Y}_{i}^{-}+\Delta {Y}_{i}∣}<{\varepsilon}_{c}$

The two previous criteria must be verified to obtain convergence.

If convergence is not reached after the maximum number of iterations, we also test the stationarity of the solution:

$\mid {Y}_{k+1}-{Y}_{k}\mid <\varepsilon$

The method used allows a local subdivision of the time step, either systematically, or in the event of non-convergence (keyword ITER_INTE_PAS): this allows easier integration, but then losing the benefit of a consistent tangent operator at the end of the integration of the behaviour.

It is often preferable, in the event of local non-convergence at the end of ITER_INTE_MAXI iterations, to proceed with a global subdivision of the time step (using DEFI_LIST_INST), in order to maintain a consistent tangent matrix.

### 3.3 Explicit solution

Another solution method, very simple to implement to solve the differential equations of single-crystal behaviour is explicit solution. For it to be numerically efficient, it is essential to associate it with automatic step control. As in [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14), we use the Runge and Kutta method. Calculation of internal variables at the moment

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.11/Object_264.svg)

is only a function of the values of their derivatives $\frac{\text{dY}}{\text{dt}}=F(Y,t)$:

$\Delta Y=\lbrace \begin{array}{}\Delta {\alpha}_{s}\\ \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\\ \Delta {p}_{s}\\ \Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}}\end{array}=\lbrace \begin{array}{}h({\tau}_{s}^{-},{\alpha}_{s}^{-},{\gamma}_{s}^{-},{p}_{s}^{-})\\ g({\tau}_{s}^{-},{\alpha}_{s}^{-},{\gamma}_{s}^{-},{p}_{s}^{-})\\ ∣\Delta {\gamma}_{s}∣=f({\tau}_{s}^{-},{\alpha}_{s}^{-},{\gamma}_{s}^{-},{p}_{s}^{-})\\ \sum_{s}{m}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}\end{array}$

with ${\tau}_{s}=\Sigma :{\mu}_{s}=({\Sigma}^{-}+\Lambda (\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{th}}-\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}})):{\mu}_{s}$

We integrate according to the following diagram:

${Y}_{t+h}={Y}^{\text{(2)}}$ if the precision criterion is satisfied

${Y}^{(2)}=Y+\frac{h}{2}\left[F(Y,t)+F({Y}^{(1)},t+h)\right]$ with ${Y}^{(1)}=Y+hF(Y,t)$

The difference between ${Y}^{(2)}$ (order 2 scheme) and ${Y}^{(1)}$ (order 1 scheme, Euler) provides an estimate of the integration error and makes it possible to control the size of the time step

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.11/Object_272.svg)

which is initialised to $\Delta {t}_{i}$ for the first attempt. The step-control strategy is defined from a norm of the discrepancy between the two integration methods: $∥{Y}^{(2)}-{Y}^{(1)}∥$ and the precision required by the user $\eta$ (keyword: RESI_INTE_RELA). The criterion retained is the following, where we note

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.11/Object_276.svg)

:

$\delta Y(t)=\underset{j=1,N}{\sup}\left\lbrace \frac{∣{y}_{i}^{(2)}-{y}_{i}^{(1)}∣}{\max\left[\varepsilon ,∣{y}_{j}^{(t)}∣\right]}\right\rbrace <\eta$

The $\varepsilon$ parameter is set to 0.001. The desired integration precision

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.11/Object_279.png)

must be consistent with the level of precision required for the overall step.

If the criterion is not verified, the time step is re-divided according to a heuristic method (number of sub-steps defined by the user via the keyword ITER_INTER_PAS). When the time step becomes too small (h < 1.10–20), the calculation is stopped with an error message.

**Explicit integration algorithm in large strains**

At each moment and at each integration point, we can calculate ${F}_{n}$, $\Delta F$ and ${F}_{n+1}=\Delta F{F}_{n}$. We know the Cauchy constraints ${\sigma}_{n}$ and the internal time variables $n$

This involves determining $S$ and the internal variables $({\gamma}_{s})$, $({\alpha}_{s})$ ${F}^{p}$ at time $n+1$. To do this, we can use a Runge-Kutta method [cf. R5.03.14] on the system of differential equations:

${\dot{F}}^{p}=(\sum_{s}\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}){F}^{p}$

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=g({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ for each slip system

$\dot{{\alpha}_{s}}=h({\tau}_{s},{\alpha}_{s},{\gamma}_{s},{p}_{s})$ for each slip system

with: ${\tau}_{s}=M:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}=({{F}^{e}}^{T}{F}^{e}S):{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}=\left[(2{\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}S+{I}_{d})S\right]:{m}_{s}\otimes {n}_{s}$

$(S)=\Lambda .\frac{1}{2}({{F}^{e}}^{T}{F}^{e}–{I}_{d})$ and ${F}^{e}=F{({F}^{p})}^{\text{-1}}$

Taking into account irradiation:

- in the DD_CC_IRRA case, it is necessary to add ${n}_{\mathrm{irra}}=12$ internal variables: ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ contain for each slip system the density of dislocations linked to irradiation ${\rho}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}$
- in the DD_CFC_IRRA case, it is necessary to add ${n}_{\mathrm{irra}}=24$ internal variables: ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ contain for each slip system ${\rho}_{s}^{\mathrm{loops}}\ast {b}^{2}$ ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+13}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+24}$ contain for each slip system ${\phi }_{s}^{\mathrm{voids}}$

We then store the cissions for each slip system: ${\tau}_{1}$, … ${\tau}_{{n}_{s}}$

In the case where we take into account the rotation of the crystal lattice, we must add ${n}_{\mathrm{rota}}=16$ internal variables:

- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+9}$ are the 9 components of the rotation matrix $Q$,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+10}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ are the 3 components of $\Delta {\omega}^{p}$,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+13}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+15}$ are the 3 components of $\Delta {\omega}^{e}$,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+16}$ represents $\Theta$

The antepenultimate internal variable is the cleavage constraint: $\underset{s}{\max}(\Sigma .n):n$

The penultimate internal variable contains the overall cumulative plastic strain, defined by:

${V}_{p-1}=\sum\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ with $\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}}:\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}})}$

The last internal variable, $\mathit{Vp}$, ($p=6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+{n}_{\mathrm{rota}}+3$, ${n}_{s}$ being the total number of slip systems) is an indicator of plasticity (threshold exceeded in at least one slip system at the current time step). If it is zero, there has been no increase in internal variables at the current time. Otherwise, it contains the number of local Newton iterations (for implicit solution) that were necessary to obtain convergence.

### 4.2 Case of polycrystal

The internal variables in *Code_Aster* are named ${V}_{1}$, ${V}_{2}$, ****** ${V}_{p}$.

The number of internal variables is $p=7+\mathrm{6m}+\sum_{g=1,m}({\mathrm{3n}}_{s}(g))+\mathrm{6m}+1$, $m$ being the number of phases and ${n}_{s}(g)$ being the number of slip systems of the phase $g$).

In the case where irradiation is taken into account, the total number of internal variables is:

$p=7+\mathrm{6m}+\sum_{g=1,m}({\mathrm{3n}}_{s}(g))+\mathrm{12m}+\mathrm{6m}+1$

- The first six internal variables are the components of the macroscopic viscoplastic strain ${E}^{\mathrm{vp}}$:

${V}_{1}={E}_{xx}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{2}={E}_{yy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{3}={E}_{zz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{4}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{5}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{6}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{yz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ ;

- the seventh is the macroscopic cumulative equivalent viscoplastic strain $P$:

${V}_{7}=\sum\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ with $\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}}:\Delta {E}^{\mathrm{vp}})}$;

- then, for each phase, we find the 6 components of the viscoplastic strains or the tensor $\beta$ of the phase: ${\left\lbrace {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),{\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{xy}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{xz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{yz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g)\right\rbrace }_{g=1,m}$;
- then, for each phase, and for each phase slip system, we find the values of ${\alpha}_{s}$ ${\gamma}_{s}$ ${p}_{s}$;
- if irradiation is taken into account, for each phase, we find the 12 dislocation densities linked to irradiation ${\rho}_{\mathit{irr}}^{s}$;
- then, for each phase, we find the 6 components of the phase constraints: ${\left\lbrace {\sigma}_{xx}(g),{\sigma}_{yy}(g),{\sigma}_{zz}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{xy}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{xz}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{yz}(g)\right\rbrace }_{g=1,m}$;
- the last internal variable is a plasticity indicator (threshold exceeded in at least one slip system at the current time step).

## 5 Numerical implementation in Code_Aster

Generally speaking, single-crystal behaviours are integrated into Runge-Kutta methods for explicit integration, and into the “plasti” environment for implicit integration [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14). The orientation tensors of the slip systems are all defined in the LCMMSG routine providing the global reference tensor for the nth system of the family provided whose name is provided by the calling routine.

To add a new single-crystal behaviour, or simply a new flow or hardening law, the parameters must be defined in DEFI_MATERIAU. Depending on the case (flow, isotropic or kinematic hardening), the reading of these parameters must be added in the routines LCMAFL, LCMAEI and LCMAEC. For the integration, it is enough to write the increments of the internal variables in the routines LCMMFE (flow), LCMMFC (kinematic hardening) and LCMMFI (isotropic hardening), for the explicit integration to work.

Implicit integration also uses the routines LCMMFE, LCMMFC and LCMMFI. It also requires the derivatives of the equations with respect to the different variables. The derivatives are written in the routines LCMMJF (derivative of the flow equation), LCMMJI (derivative of the isotropic-hardening relation) and LCMMJC (derivative of the kinematic-hardening relation).

For more details on the architecture of the solution of the crystal behaviours, see [D5.04.02](doc:d5.04.02).

## 6 Use

These models are accessible in *Code_Aster* in **3D**, plane strains (**D_PLAN**), plane stresses (**C_PLAN**, by the De Borst method [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03)) and axisymmetry (**AXIS**).

### 6.1 Case of single crystal

In the case of meshed microstructures, the different grains of a single crystal being represented by mesh-cell groups, it is necessary to assign the material parameters and the behaviours of the single crystals as well as their orientations to the different grains.

The values of the parameters of the behavioural relations are provided using the DEFI_MATERIAU command. This is defined from the keywords MONO_VISC1, MONO_VISC2, MONO_DD_KR, MONO_DD_CFC for the flow, MONO_ISOT1, MONO_ISOT2, MONO_DD_CFC for isotropic hardening and MONO_CINE1, MONO_CINE2 for kinematic hardening [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01). For example [V6.04.172](doc:v6.04.172):

```text

/ MONO_VISC2=_F(N=10, K=40,C=6333,D=37,A=121),

/ MONO_DD_KR=_F(...),

# ECROUISSAGE ISOTROPE

/ MONO_ISOT1=_F(R_0=75.5,Q=9.77,B=19.34,H=2.54),

/ MONO_ISOT2=_F(R_0=75.5,Q1=9.77,B1=19.34,H=2.54,Q2=-33.27, B2=5.345,),

/ MONO_CINE2=_F(D=36.68, GM=, PM=,),

);

On peut ainsi dissocier, au niveau des données, l’écoulement de l’écrouissage isotrope et de l’écrouissage cinématique.

Il faut maintenant définir le (ou les) type de monocristal étudié. Pour cela, on définit le comportement de façon externe à STAT_NON_LINE, par l’intermédiaire de l’opérateur DEFI_COMPOR, par exemple:
```

The DEFI_COMPOR operator calculates the total number of internal variables associated with the single crystal.

### 6.2 Case of polycrystal

In the case of multiphase polycrystals, each phase corresponds to a single crystal. We will therefore use the parameters defined previously in DEFI_MATERIAU for the single crystal. POLY1=DEFI_COMPOR(POLYCRISTAL=(_F(MONOCRISTAL=MONO1,

FRAC_VOL=0.025,

ANGL_REP=(-149.676,15.61819,154.676,),),

_F(MONOCRISTAL=MONO1,

FRAC_VOL=0.025,

ANGL_REP=(-150.646,33.864,55.646,),),

_F(MONOCRISTAL=MONO1,

FRAC_VOL=0.025,

ANGL_REP=(-137.138,41.5917,142.138,),),

……

_F(MONOCRISTAL=MONO1,

FRAC_VOL=0.025,

ANGL_REP=(-481.729,35.46958,188.729,),),),

The keyword POLYCRISTAL makes it possible to define each phase by the data of an orientation, (provided by ANGL_REP or ANGL_EULER), of a volume fraction, of a mono-crystal (that is to say a model of behaviour and slip systems).

**Expression of the Jacobian of the integrated elasto-visco-plastic equations**

The system to be solved is of the form:

$R(Y)=R(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})=\lbrace \begin{array}{}s(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ e(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ {n}_{s}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}a(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ g(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ p(\Sigma ,\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$

$=\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\Lambda}^{\text{-1}}\Sigma -({\Lambda}_{-}^{\text{- 1}}){\Sigma}^{-}-(\Delta E-\Delta {E}^{\text{th}}-\Delta {E}^{\text{vp}})\\ \Delta {E}^{\text{vp}}-{\sum}_{s}{\mu}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}\\ {n}_{s}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\Delta {\alpha}_{s}-h({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ \Delta {\gamma}_{s}-g({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\\ {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}-f({\tau}_{s}^{+},{\alpha}_{s}^{+},{\gamma}_{s}^{+},{p}_{s}^{+})\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$

$=0\text{avec}{\tau}_{s}^{+}={\Sigma}^{+}:{\mu}_{s}$

Let us therefore evaluate the terms of the Jacobian hypermatrix $J$ at the instant $t+\Delta t$

$J=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}\frac{\partial s}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial e}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial a}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\\ \frac{\partial p}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}& \frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\end{array}\right]$

Concerning the first line of the matrix, independently of the hardening and flow equations, we have:

$\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{\partial s}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}={D}^{-1}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}=\text{Id}& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}=& \frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}\end{array}=0$

The second line can also be written independently of flow and hardening:

$\begin{array}{ccc}\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{\partial e}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}=0& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}}=\text{Id}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \alpha }}_{s}}=0\end{array}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{s}}=-{\mu}_{s}& \frac{\partial e}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta p}}_{s}}=0\end{array}$

The first column of the lines corresponding to equations (a), (g) and (p) is written:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial a}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}=\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}{\text{ΔΣ}}\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}{\text{ΔΣ}}\\ \frac{\partial p}{\partial \text{ΔΣ}}=\frac{\partial p}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}{\text{ΔΣ}}\end{array}$

with

$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \tau }}_{s}}{\text{ΔΣ}}={({\mu}_{s})}^{T}$

The second column is identically zero (due to equation (e): the flow and hardening relationships can only be expressed as a function of $\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$ and not of ${\mathrm{\Delta E}}^{\text{vp}}$.

The last block of equations depends on the chosen behaviours:

**Evaluation of the consistent tangent operator**

This involves finding the consistent tangent operator, i.e. calculated from the solution of $(R(Y)=0)$ at the end of the increment. For a small variation of $R$, this time considering $\Delta E$ as a variable and not as a parameter, we obtain:

$\frac{\partial R}{\partial \Sigma }\delta \Sigma +\frac{\partial R}{\partial \Delta E}\delta \Delta E+\frac{\partial R}{\partial \Delta {\gamma}_{s}}\delta \Delta {\gamma}_{s}=0$

This system can be written:

$\frac{\partial R}{\partial Y}\delta (Y)=X$ with $Y=\left[\begin{array}{c}\Sigma \\ \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\end{array}\right]$ and $X=\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta \Delta E\\ 0\end{array}\right]$

By writing the Jacobian matrix in the form:

$J.\delta Y=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{Y}_{0}& {Y}_{1}\\ {Y}_{2}& {Y}_{3}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\Sigma \\ \Delta Z\end{array}\right]$

With:

$\begin{array}{}{Y}_{0}={\Lambda}^{-1}\\ \Delta Z=\left\lbrace \Delta {\gamma}_{s}\right\rbrace \times {n}_{s}\end{array}$

The dimensions of the submatrices are:

$\begin{array}{}\text{dim}({Y}_{0}={\Lambda}^{-1})=\left[6,6\right]\\ \text{dim}{Y}_{1}=\left[6,{n}_{s}\right]\\ \text{dim}{Y}_{2}=\left[{n}_{s},6\right]\\ \text{dim}{Y}_{3}=\left[{n}_{s},{n}_{s}\right]\end{array}$

By operating by successive eliminations and substitutions, the third block of the equation system gives:

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta Z=-{({Y}_{3})}^{-1}{Y}_{2}\Sigma \\ ({Y}_{0}-{Y}_{1}{({Y}_{3})}^{-1}{Y}_{2})\Sigma =\Delta E\end{array}$

the tangent operator sought can therefore be written directly:

${(\frac{\partial \Sigma }{\partial E})}_{t+\Delta t}={(\frac{\partial \Sigma }{\partial \Delta E})}_{t+\Delta t}={({Y}_{0}-{Y}_{1}{Y}_{3}^{-1}{Y}_{2})}^{-1}$
