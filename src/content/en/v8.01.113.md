# v8.01.113 FDLV113 - Pressure source in a fluid-filled ball in soil interaction - fluid - structure

**Summary:**

This test contributes to the validation of the *Code_Aster* - MISS3D chaining by the frequent method of coupling in soil-fluid-structure interaction (ISFS).

This test allows you to consider all types of interface: soil-structure, fluid-structure, soil-fluid, free soil. It also allows you to test the loading by point pressure source in the fluid.

It represents a ball, a hollow sphere of finite dimensions, filled with water.

To have all types of interface, the lower half of the hollow sphere is modelled by *Code_Aster* as the domain "structure"; the upper half representing the domain "ground" of the same characteristics as the structure and the domain "fluid" is modelled by MISS3D. A source of harmonic pressure, of constant unit module for each frequency between $1\mathrm{Hz}$ and $30\mathrm{Hz}$, is imposed on the center of the ball in the fluid medium.

The radial displacement module obtained from the outside and inside the ball is tested against a calculated analytical solution. The concordance is correct provided that the effect of a parasitic resonance corresponding to the first proper frequency of the sphere with water mass added to the center is removed. This is possible by the introduction of a parameter RFIC in MISS3D.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Miss3D uses the frequent coupling method to account for the soil-fluid-structure interaction. This method, based on dynamic substructure, consists of cutting the study field into three sub-domains:

- soil,
- fluid,
- structure.

The result is 4 types of interface possible:

- the soil-structure interface,
- fluid-structure interface,
- the ground-free interface.

**The soil, fluid, and structure**

The soil and structure are made of the same homogeneous material.

In the coupled calculation *Code_Aster* -MISS3D, to represent the case of a hollow sphere of finite dimension filled with fluid, one models half of the solid medium as domain

The fluid medium, which is inside the $r=5m$ ray sphere, is taken into account with MISS3D. The solid medium occupies the volume between the ${r}_{\mathrm{interne}}=5m$ and ${r}_{\mathrm{externe}}=7m$ ray spheres.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and mechanical loadings

A point fluid source condition is applied to the centre of the $(000)$ coordinate ball with a $P={P}_{o}\sin\omega t$ harmonic load whose ${p}_{o}$ pressure module is unitary with a pulse that varies from $1\mathrm{Hz}$ to $30\mathrm{Hz}$ by $1\mathrm{Hz}$ steps. This corresponds to a Dirac at the initial moment in time. This returns to *Code_Aster* to insert the source coordinates into CALC_MISS under the SOURCE_FLUIDE key word.

In order not to have a rigid body motion problem, the nodes of the mesh structure, which is located on the $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ axes respectively, were blocked ($Y$ and $Z$ ), ($X$ and $Z$ ), ($X$ and $Y$ ). Thus, rigid body movements are prevented, while allowing radial movement.

With respect to the ground-structure interface modes, it was noted that the static forced-type modes calculated with this boundary condition d-clamped support at the ground-structure interface cannot be a complete base for representing a spherical symmetry deformed.

For this purpose, new static modes have been introduced into the modal base. The modes introduced are mode of type forced on the outer envelope of the lower half-sphere (in yellow in Figure 1.1-b). They correspond to a new limit condition of blocking according to the 3 degrees of freedom of all nodes of this surface, with the exception of its nodes intersection with the axes. For these nodes, the unit mode corresponding to the degree of freedom which is tangential to the surface is not used, because the displacement that is being sought does not have a component in this direction.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Baseline

The frequent coupling method between Miss3D and *Code_Aster* is described in the reference document [bib1].

The radial displacement module obtained from the outside and inside the ball is tested against a calculated and detailed analytical solution in an application study [bib2]. Pressure and development solutions depend only on radius and time. Total fluid pressure is considered to be due to the sum of two contributions:

- ${p}_{d}$ pressure due to the vibration of the wall at the interface with the solid medium,
- ${p}_{0}$ pressure due to the action of Dirac mass in the center of the sphere, in an infinite fluid
- $p={p}_{d}+{p}_{0}$

The Helmholtz equation of wave propagation in the fluid in the absence of a source is written in spherical coordinates after transformation of Fourier:

$\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\frac{\delta}{\delta r}({r}^{2}\frac{\delta {p}_{d}}{\delta r})(r,\omega )+\frac{\omega}{{c}_{f}^{2}}{p}_{d}(r,\omega )=0$

- By posing: ${k}_{f}(\omega )=\frac{\omega}{{c}_{f}}$ , one obtains a solution of the form ${p}_{d}=A(\sin\frac{({k}_{f}r)}{4\pi r})$ ,

The solution for the ${p}_{0}$ pressure is given by a function of Green and the pressure in the fluid is written: $p=\frac{{e}^{i{k}_{f}r}}{4\pi r}+A(\sin\frac{({k}_{f}r)}{4\pi r})$

The Navier equation of wave propagation in the solid in the absence of a source is written in spherical coordinates after transformation of Fourier and by changing variables $u=\frac{\delta \phi }{\delta r}$:

$\frac{{\delta}^{2}}{\delta {r}^{2}}(r\phi )(r,\omega )+{k}_{p}^{2}(r\phi )(r,\omega )=0$

- By posing ${k}_{p}(\omega )=\frac{\omega}{{c}_{p}}$, one obtains a solution of the form:

$u=B\frac{{e}^{i{k}_{p}r}}{4\pi r}(\frac{i{k}_{p}r-1}{r})+C\frac{{e}^{-i{k}_{p}r}}{4\pi r}(\frac{-i{k}_{p}r-1}{r})$ .

The 3 unknown coefficients $A$ , $B$ and $C$ are then determined from 3 limit conditions:

- Continuity of normal movements at the $\rho {\omega}^{2}u=\mathrm{grad}(p)$ ground-fluid interface for $r={r}_{1}$ ,
- Continuity of normal stresses at the ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}+p=0$ ground-fluid interface for $r={r}_{1}$ ,
- Zero radial stress on the ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}=0$ outer surface for $r={r}_{2}$ ,

## 4 Summary of the results

The radial frequency-analytical displacement of $A$ , $B$ and $C$ (positioned in Figure 1.3-a above) for $7m$ outside the post-contractor structure domain is represented in Figures 4a and 4b.

The correct results are consistent overall. In particular, resonance frequencies generally are found to $19\mathrm{Hz}$ and $25\mathrm{Hz}$ despite a slight offset and the nonresonance displacements at points A and C in the equatorial and vertical planes. However, there is a significant difference in resonance amplitudes due to at least the previous slight offset. On the other hand, there is at least another disturbance with a parasitic resonance to $10\mathrm{Hz}$. This frequency is the first specific frequency of the sphere with water mass added to the centre that can be found by a modal analysis calculation. The corresponding mode is a different incompressible fluid mode from the bulging modes sought here. This disturbance can be mitigated by the introduction of the RFIC parameter (here being 0.5) as given by MISS3D.

The use of static modes forced on the outer envelope of the sphere is therefore exhaustive to represent spherical symmetry inflating modes, but it is therefore also likely to represent modes of another nature that may disturb the solution, notably at point $B$ where no explicit limit conditions have been imposed to recover this spherical symmetry of displacements. The introduction of structural damping would also reduce the amplitude differences at resonance frequencies between analytical results and calculation.
