# v2.01.021 SDLD21 - 8 degrees of freedom spring mass-system with viscous shock absorber

**Summary:**

This unidirectional problem consists in a harmonic analysis of a mechanical structure composed of a set of spring masses with viscous shock absorbers and subjected to sinusoidal excitation. This mechanical test of the structures corresponds to a dynamic analysis of a discrete model with linear behavior.
For Modelling C, the modal calculation (QEP quadratic problem) is checked by varying the modal solvers (SORENSEN and QZ) and the blocking factor: DDL_IMPO alone, MECA_IMPO alone or a paning of both.
The results obtained (field of displacement, speed and acceleration for different excitation frequencies) are in good agreement with the results of the VPCS guide.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The system of differential equations of the second coupled order is of the form:

$M\ddot{u}+C\dot{u}+Ku=F$

with $M=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}10& & & & \\ & 10& & & \\ & & .& & \\ & & & & 10\end{array}\right]$ $C=50\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}2& -1& & & & \\ -1& 2& -1& & & \\ & -1& 2& .& & \\ & & .& .& .& \\ & & & .& .& -1\\ & & & & -1& 2\end{array}\right]$

$K={10}^{+5}\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}2& -1& & & & \\ -1& 2& -1& & & \\ & -1& 2& .& & \\ & & .& .& .& \\ & & & .& .& -1\\ & & & & -1& 2\end{array}\right]$

$\omega$ solution with $F={F}_{0}{e}^{j\omega t}({j}^{2}=-1)$ harmonic excitation is of the $u={u}_{0}{e}^{j\omega t}$ form, which leads to: $(K-M{\omega}^{2}+j\omega C){u}_{0}={F}_{0}$

This system can be solved for any $\omega$ , either directly or using modal transformation from the actual eigenmodes obtained by the associated $(K-M{\omega}^{2})\phi =0$ conservative system.

It admits $n$ clean solutions (8 in this case) ${\omega}_{i}^{2}$ and associated vectors ${\phi }_{i}$ grouped in the $\Lambda =[{\omega}_{i}^{2}]$ spectral matrix and $\Phi =[{\phi }_{i}]$ modal matrix.

The modal transformation consists of writing:

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.021/Object_13.svg)

which leads to:

$\left[\Lambda -{\omega}^{2}I+j\omega \xi \right]q={}^{t}\Phi {F}_{0}$

$I$ is the identity,

$\xi$ is diagonal $\xi =\left[{\xi}_{ii}\right]$ because the damping is proportional $(C=\alpha K)$.

The answer is: ${u}_{0}=\underset{i=1}{\overset{n}{\Sigma}}\frac{{\phi }_{i}^{t}{\phi }_{i}}{{\omega}_{i}^{2}-{\omega}^{2}+j\omega {\xi}_{ii}}{F}_{0}$

The exact solution is obtained by taking all the modes of the environment.

Deduced from this: $\dot{{u}_{0}}=j\omega {u}_{0}$ and $\ddot{{u}_{0}}=-{\omega}^{2}{u}_{0}$

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes: 10

Number of mesh cells and types : 9 SEG2

To test REST_SPEC_TEMP, we will compare several approaches (on a modal and physical basis) by testing the $\mathrm{DX}$ component to the point ${P}_{4}$ of displacement, speed and acceleration.

Two methods are tested:

- modal basis calculation, then after REST_SPEC_TEMP, physical basis return with RECU_FONCTION on the RESU_GENE,
- calculation on a physical basis directly.

Each time the option TOUT_CHAM='OUI' is tested or the three kinematic fields are calculated separately with NOM_CHAM = 'DEPL', 'VITE' or 'ACCE'.

By these different paths we have to find the same results because the modal base is complete (this is not problematic because we have a small number of physical degrees of freedom).

Rather than testing only in specific moments, comparisons (on a physical basis) are made by analyzing the sum of the absolute values of the maximum (at each step) of the differences between the temporal solutions (obtained by FFT inverse with REST_SPEC_TEMP) at all times. This standard must be strictly zero each time.

Given the number of additional REST_SPEC_TEMP-related operations, the CPU time of Modelling B is significantly increased compared to the other modellingss that do not have these tests.
