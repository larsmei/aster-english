# 1 **u2.08.09** Mesh adaptation in the non-linear regime

**Summary:**

This document presents the methodology for carrying out a non-linear study with mesh adaptation. This adaptation of the mesh in the course of the transient is obtained with the help of the HOMARD software, called via the command MACR_ADAP_MAIL [U7.03.01](doc:u7.03.01).

An example illustrates the possibilities and the implementation of remeshing strategies.

## 1 Introduction

Mesh adaptation provides assistance to the user in supplying analyses which are as reliable as possible with respect to the discretisation errors induced by the finite-element solution method.

We shall not detail in this document the principle of adaptation, and we refer the reader to the document [U2.08.01](doc:u2.08.01). We merely recall a few elements.

Schematically, an iteration of mesh adaptation appears as in the figure below. The software computes the numerical solution on mesh number $k$ , then deduces from it the values of the error indicator over the whole mesh. From the knowledge of mesh number $k$ and of indicator number $k$ , the mesh-adaptation software HOMARD creates the new mesh number $k+1$ .

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.09/100000000000054A0000019DF3574E3B6F724E9E.png)

Illustration 1: Iteration of mesh adaptation

For a static analysis, this amounts to seeking to improve the solution by a succession of analyses on different meshes. For a transient analysis in time, the analysis is suspended at a given time instant; the mesh is adapted; the analysis resumes on the new mesh. It is this case that we tackle here. Indeed, in the case of the solution of a non-linear transient, restarting the analysis is not necessarily a straightforward matter. The aim of this document is to help the user to implement this type of modelling.

## 2 Adaptation in the non-linear regime

In this part, one describes the typical problem that one seeks to solve, as well as the essential difficulties that it presents.

### 2.1 Problem considered

The typical problem of interest is a transient involving a model with a non-linear constitutive law. One wishes to adapt the support mesh in the course of the solution according to the diagram of the figure . The principle is the following:

- one solves the transient over one or several time steps
- one adapts the mesh on the basis of the computation of an error indicator
- one resumes the transient

In fact, this diagram conceals a large part of the complexity of the problem. Let us take the example of the use of an elastoplastic von Mises constitutive law with isotropic hardening. At each time step, one stores the state of the analysis domain $\Omega$ , given by the displacement field, the stress field and the field of internal variables $(u,\sigma ,\alpha )$ . It is this triplet, carried by the mesh $M$ , which completely determines the equilibrium of $\Omega$ . Now, in the case of the use of mesh adaptation, the mesh becomes an evolving element. If one assumes that the mesh is adapted at each time step, one must also transfer the triplet $(u,\sigma ,\alpha )$ from one mesh to the other. This is the projection phase. Unfortunately, once the projection has been carried out, nothing ensures that the new triplet ensures the equilibrium of the domain on the new mesh. One must for this purpose correct the new triplet. This is the rebalancing phase.

### 2.2 Rebalancing phase

In what follows, one will use the following assumptions:

- use of an elastoplastic von Mises constitutive law with isotropic hardening
- adaptation of the mesh at each loading step

One sets the following notations:

- ${M}_{i}$ denotes the mesh at loading step $i$
- $({u}_{i},{\sigma}_{i},{\alpha}_{i})$ denote the triplet displacement field, stress field and field of internal variables at loading step $i$
- ${(x)}_{{M}_{i}}$ denotes the quantity $x$ carried by the mesh ${M}_{i}$

One now details the analysis process. One starts from a balanced state ${({u}_{i},{\sigma}_{i},{\alpha}_{i})}_{{M}_{i}}$ carried by the mesh ${M}_{i}$ at loading step $i$ . One proceeds to the solution of the problem at loading step $i+1$ . One obtains the balanced state ${({u}_{i+1},{\sigma}_{i+1},{\alpha}_{i+1})}_{{M}_{i}}$ . On the basis of the computation of an error indicator, one produces a new mesh ${M}_{i+1}$ .

To continue the analysis, one must project the balanced state ${({u}_{i+1},{\sigma}_{i+1},{\alpha}_{i+1})}_{{M}_{i}}$ onto the new mesh ${M}_{i+1}$ . We shall detail this projection later. One then obtains the state ${({u}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\alpha}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}})}_{{M}_{i+1}}$ but which is not necessarily balanced. Indeed, the projection phase can induce perturbations on each of the 3 fields and remove their consistency. It is therefore a matter of rebalancing the mechanical state at time instant $i+1$ of the domain $\Omega$ discretised by ${M}_{i+1}$ .

To do this, one refers to the documentation [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) “Quasi-static non-linear algorithm” and, not taking into account the command variables, one finds that the system which one solves at the first iteration of each time step is the following:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{i}.\Delta {u}_{i+1}^{0}+{B}^{T}.\Delta {\lambda}_{i+1}^{0}={L}_{i+1}^{\text{méca}}-{Q}_{i}^{T}.{\sigma}_{i}\\ B.\Delta {u}_{i+1}^{0}={u}_{i+1}^{d}-B.{u}_{i}\end{array}
$$

The right-hand sides ${L}_{i+1}^{\text{méca}}-{Q}_{i}^{T}.{\sigma}_{i}$ and ${\mathrm{u}}_{i+1}^{d}-\mathrm{B}.{\mathrm{u}}_{i}$ measure respectively:

- the deviation from equilibrium between the loading and a mechanical stress state
- the deviation from the verification of the boundary conditions

The solution of the underlying non-linear system leads to the cancellation of these deviations and to the production of a balanced mechanical state. This is exactly what one seeks to do, not starting from the mechanical state of the previous time instant ${({u}_{i},{\sigma}_{i},{\alpha}_{i})}_{{M}_{i}}$ as in () but from the projected mechanical state ${({u}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}},{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}},{\alpha}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}})}_{{M}_{i+1}}$ .

If one transposes it to the case of interest, the system to be solved is therefore the following:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{i}.\Delta {u}_{i+1}^{0}+{B}^{T}.\Delta {\lambda}_{i+1}^{0}={L}_{i+1}^{\text{méca}}-{Q}_{i+1}^{T}.{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}}\\ B.\Delta {u}_{i+1}^{0}={u}_{i+1}^{d}-B.{u}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}}\end{array}
$$

From the point of view of the modelling, this therefore amounts to relaunching the solution of loading step $i+1$ using as initial state the projected mechanical state ${({u}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\alpha}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}})}_{{M}_{i+1}}$ .

The use of the projected mechanical state ${({u}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}},{\alpha}_{i+1}^{\mathrm{proj}})}_{{M}_{i+1}}$ as initial state is essential. It first makes it possible to start from a starting point as close as possible to the arrival point and therefore to increase the chances of seeing the Newton method converge. But there is a much more important reason. In the context of the use of irreversible constitutive laws, the internal variables, such as the cumulative plastic strain, carry the mechanical history of each point of the structure. One must start from a state of internal variables as faithful as possible to the real state of the structure in order to represent its mechanical history properly.

This leads us to a new observation: it is essential to produce a projected mechanical state ${({u}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}},{\sigma}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}},{\alpha}_{i+1}^{\mathit{proj}})}_{{M}_{i+1}}$ which is as faithful as possible. This is what we shall now address.

### 2.3 Projection

The projection of the fields is carried out with the operator PROJ_CHAMP; for any information on its syntax and its complete functionalities, the reader is referred to [U4.72.05](doc:u4.72.05).

We are interested in the projection of the mechanical state, which is composed of 3 fields. We shall examine the projection of each of these fields.

#### 2.3.1 Projection of the displacement field

The displacement field DEPL is a continuous field at the nodes. Its projection poses no particular problem. The collocation method, which is used by default for this type of field, is perfectly suited.

#### 2.3.2 Projection of the stress field

The stress (and/or force) field SIEF_ELGA is natively computed at the integration points. In order to minimise the perturbation induced by the projection, it is preferable to exploit directly the field at the integration point. This is the method that the projection operator will choose automatically for this field.

It is possible to carry out the projection using a field by element at the nodes or a field at the nodes. This implies additional manipulations which are fairly technical. This approach is not recommended.

#### 2.3.3 Projection of the field of internal variables

The field of internal variables VARI_ELGA is natively computed at the integration points. In order to minimise the perturbation induced by the projection, it is preferable to exploit directly the field at the integration point. This is the method that the projection operator will choose automatically for this field.

As for the stress field, it is possible to use a field by element at the nodes or a field at the nodes, but this poses more of a problem. Indeed, if one considers the case of von Mises plasticity, the cumulative plastic strain $p$ must always be positive, $p\ge 0$ . Consider the figure . It represents a SEG2 element with 2 nodes $\mathit{N1}$ and $\mathit{N2}$ and with 2 integration points $\mathit{PG1}$ and $\mathit{PG2}$ . This element carries a field at its integration points of value $\mathit{VG1}$ and $\mathit{VG2}$ , which are both positive. Now, following the extrapolation to the vertices, one observes that the value $\mathit{VN1}$ at the vertex $\mathit{N1}$ indeed remains positive whereas the value $\mathit{VN2}$ at the vertex $\mathit{N2}$ becomes negative. In order to be used as an initial value, the cumulative plastic strain $p$ must be positive or zero; the value $\mathit{VN2}$ will therefore be set to zero.

The use of a field of internal variables at the nodes is strongly discouraged because, in addition to the perturbations inherent in the projection as in the previous paragraph, it imposes an additional treatment to make the field admissible, which perturbs it noticeably.

## 3 Implementation

### 3.1 General diagram

The general diagram of the adaptation is presented in the figure . It comprises the 4 phases described previously:

- solution over one or several loading steps
- adaptation of the mesh on the basis of the computation of an error indicator
- projection of the fields at the end of the last time step onto the new mesh
- balancing of the fields at the end of the last time step on the new mesh
