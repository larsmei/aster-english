# v6.04.202 SSNV202 – Drained oedometric test with CAM_CLAY and MCC behavior models

**Summary**

This drained oedometric compression test (in pure mechanical,* equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) validates the elastoplastic laws CAM_CLAY [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14)_ and MCC [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48)_, specific to soil behavior, presenting under this load a nonlinear elastic regime and a hardening plastic regime.

Modelling A uses the CAM_CLAY model and pilotes the test by a monotonous, imposed displacement. Modelling C uses the MCC model and controls the test by a pressure imposed. For each of the two laws, the quantities calculated are compared with the results obtained either from the FLAC software for soil mechanics in Modelling A or from the results of a MTest calculation at the point hardware in Modelling C.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

An oedometric test consists of imposing a vertical load variation on the test piece while maintaining the lateral deformations zero. It can be drained (the fluid pore pressure does not vary during the test) or undrained (the fluid pore pressure changes in the sample). The case is examined here. The variation of the effective mean pressure, noted $p'$, is therefore equal to that of the total pressure $p$.

In model A, the oedometric test is performed with a stress state initiated at $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-P_{conso}^0\boldsymbol{I}$ with ${P}_{conso}^0=2P_{cr}^{0}-P_{trac}$ preconsolidation. The vertical load is controlled on a displacement from ${U}_{z}=0$ to ${U}_{z}=-0.1$ m (which corresponds to a maximum axial compression deformation equal to $10\%$ on the upper side of the cubic sample.

In model C, the oedometer test is performed with an initially zero stress state. The vertical load is controlled by increasing the pressure force $-\sigma_{zz}$ to $10$ MPa before performing a discharge up to $10$ kPa.

> **Note:**
> In Modelling A, the CREA_CHAMP operator defines the non-zero initial stress state $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-P_{conso}^0\boldsymbol{I}$ on all the element at Gauss points.

Modelling A with the CAM_CLAY 3D type is *tridimensional* and *static nonlinear* .

The initial preconsolidation of the sample is equal to ${p}_{\mathrm{co}}={100}$ kPa. It is checked that the Young's modulus respects the inequality: $E<1.8$ MPa.

In this model A, on the mesh-cell group *HAUT*, $DZ$ therefore varies between $0$ and $-0.1$ m in 70 time step. During the calculation, the automatic subdivision of the time step is activated to manage non-convergence situations of local integration.

In integrating equilibrium equations, a re-update of the tangent matrix is required, which is provided by CAM_CLAY law routines and significantly accelerates convergence. Also, the time step (DEFI_LIST_INST command) subdivision is required to treat local integration failure situations due to too large load increments.

To represent an oedometric test, the common boundary conditions are (symmetry conditions, because cubic l-element represents one eighth of the sample):

- On mesh cells *DEVANT* and *DERRIERE*: $DX=0$;
- On mesh cells *GAUCHE* and *DROITE*: $DY=0$;
- On the mesh cell *BAS* : $DZ=0$.

The following figures show the different comparisons between Code_Aster and FLAC in terms of the loading path in the $(p,q)$ plane (with $p'=p$ in this drained test), and the evolution of the empty index. In the latter two Code_Aster solutions are shown:

- The one-phase solution, which corresponds to Modelling A.
- The two-phase solution, where the assignment of the initial stress field ${P}_{\mathit{cons}}^{0}$ (CREA_CHAM) was replaced by the calculation of the CAM_CLAY elastoplastic law under isotropic consolidation up to the ${P}_{\mathit{cons}}^{0}$ pressure.

The procedure used in Modelling A is that used for FLAC calculation. In terms of the loading path in the $(p,q)$ plane, there is an excellent convergence of the solutions between FLAC and Code_Aster, both in one phase and in two. In terms of the empty index, however, if the Code_Aster solution in one phase coincides well with the FLAC solution, it is quite different for the Code_Aster solution in two phases. This is easily explained: when loading isotropic with the CAM_CLAY law, the empty index has evolved, from $e_{0}=2$ to $e\approx 1.82$. This result therefore shows how the two approaches (in one or two phases) are not entirely equivalent, and that it is necessary to be aware of this.
