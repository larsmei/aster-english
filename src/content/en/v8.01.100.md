# v8.01.100 FDLV100 - Piston coupled to an incompressible fluid column

**Summary:**

This fluid field test (fluid-structure coupling) validates the modal-based mass calculation and performs, as part of a modal analysis, the calculation of the proper frequency of a piston-spring system coupled with an incompressible fluid column. To model the fluid, the elements thermal plane is used; to model the piston, 2D mechanical elements in flat deformation and a discrete element to model a spring. Finally, the fluid-structure interface is modelled by modified thermal linear elements to introduce an "acceleration" boundary condition into the fluid. The test case has only one model, two-dimensional. The specific frequency of the coupled system is found at 0.01% of the analytical result.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

**Analytical calculus:**

When the structure vibrates in the fluid, it modifies the pressure field that obeys an equation of Laplace with boundary conditions from Von Neuman [R4.07.03](doc:r4.07.03).

In our case, given the symmetries of the problem, the pressure field depends only on the variable $x$ and checks:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}\frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {x}^{2}}=0& {(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x})}_{x=0}=-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{x}}_{S}.n\\ p=0\text{en}x=L& \end{array}$

Thus, the pressure field is a function of the $x$ affine. The two boundary conditions on the pressure involve: $p=-{\rho}_{f}{\ddot{x}}_{S}.n(x-L)$

The pressure force on the structure is written:

$F=\underset{\Gamma}{\int}p(0)nd\Gamma =\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{\rho}_{f}L({\ddot{x}}_{S}.n)nd\Gamma$

Since the problem is one-dimensional, this force can be expressed algebraically according to the following acceleration component $\mathrm{Ox}$ of the structure:

$F=-\ddot{x}\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{\rho}_{f}Ld\Gamma =-{\rho}_{f}Ld\ddot{x}=-{m}_{a}\ddot{x}$ with ${m}_{a}={\rho}_{f}Ld$

This is the added linear mass of the fluid on the structure: it is noticed that it corresponds to the fluid mass in the column, that is to say the fluid mass displaced by the piston.

The equation of the movement of the piston projected on $\mathrm{Ox}$ is written (free vibration not damped due to the presence of the fluid):

$m\ddot{x}+Kx=F=-{m}_{a}\ddot{x}\mathrm{\iff }(m+{m}_{a})\ddot{x}+Kx=0$

The proper frequency of this submerged system is thus written:

$f=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{K}{m+{m}_{a}}}$

The effect of the fluid is therefore to lower the system's eigenfrequency in air.

In Aster, the added mass matrix is determined on the modal basis of the structure in the vacuum: To calculate the added mass given above, the calculation of the proper mode of the piston-spring system is restricted to a unit-nominated translation movement: the modal base of the structure is therefore truncated to one mode in air (operator CALC_MODES option 'PLUS_PETITE'). This mode determines the added mass on the piston.

$K={10}^{5}N/m$ ${m}_{a}=200\mathrm{kg}/m$ $m=78\mathrm{kg}/m$

The proper frequency of the plunger-spring system is therefore $f=3.018\mathit{Hz}$
