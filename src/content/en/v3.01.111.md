# v3.01.111 SSLL111 - Static response of a reinforced concrete beam (T-section) with linear behaviour

**Summary:**

The problem is to analyze the response of a reinforced concrete beam through multifibre beam modelling, which is a static analysis of a beam with linear behavior.

Three successive load cases are tested: one point load, the clean weight and a temperature rise. For the first load case, two cross-section meshes, one coarser and the other finer are tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Three-point bending beam, defined by:

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/Object_1.svg)

With a $T$ double section:

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/Object_2.svg)

On this diagram, $O$ is located at mid-height of the section.

The total cross section of the top steels is ${3.10}^{-4}{m}^{2}$ and the lower steels is ${4.10}^{-4}{m}^{2}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

- concrete: $E=2.{10}^{10}\mathit{Pa}$; $\nu =0.2$; $\rho =2400{\mathit{kg.m}}^{-3}$; $\alpha =10-5{K}^{-1}$
- steel: $E=2,1.{10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$; $\nu =0.33$; $\rho =7800{\mathit{kg.m}}^{-3}$; $\alpha =10-5{K}^{-1}$

### 1.3 boundary conditions

Simple support in $B$ : $\mathit{dy}=0$

"Double" support in $A$: $\mathit{dx}=\mathit{dy}=\mathit{dz}=0$ as well as $\mathit{rx}=\mathit{ry}=0$.

### 1.4 Loading

Three load cases are tested successively:

- Loading 1: concentrated effort in beam media, $F=10000N$
- Loading 2: own beam weight, $g=9,8{\mathit{m.s}}^{-2}$
- Loading 3: Uniform heating of the $\Delta T=100K$ beam

## 2 reference solution

The reference calculations are based on a simple elastic calculation in RdM.

### 2.1 Elastic centre

In simple bending, for elastic behaviour, the neutral axis passes through the elastic centre (barycentre of sections weighted by the material modules):

$C$ such as ${\int}_{S}E\overrightarrow{\mathit{CM}}\mathit{dS}=\overrightarrow{0}$

The position of the centres of gravity of the concrete alone is determined first

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/1000018E0000018D0000017216A7F0FABA3D7859.svg)

and steel alone

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/1000018E0000018D0000017233AD886678DD09D8.svg)

relative to point $O$ .

$\begin{array}{c}{y}_{{G}_{b}}=\frac{0.125\times 0.3\times 0.05-0.125\times 0.2\times 0.05}{0.2\times 0.05+0.1\times 0.2+0.3\times 0.05}=1.38888{10}^{-2}m\\ {y}_{{G}_{a}}=\frac{0.125\times 3-0.125\times 4}{3+4}=-1.78571{10}^{-2}m\\ {z}_{{G}_{a}}={z}_{{G}_{b}}=0m\end{array}$

The position of the $C$ elastic centre can then be determined.

$\overrightarrow{\mathit{OC}}=\frac{{E}_{a}{S}_{a}\overrightarrow{{\mathit{OG}}_{a}}+{E}_{b}{S}_{b}\overrightarrow{{\mathit{OG}}_{b}}}{{E}_{a}{S}_{a}+{E}_{b}{S}_{b}}$

The ${S}_{b}$ concrete section is $0,045{m}^{2}$ and the ${S}_{a}$ steel section is ${7.10}^{-4}{m}^{2}$. The Young's modulus of the concrete is ${2.10}^{10}\mathit{MPa}$ and the ${21.10}^{10}\mathit{MPa}$ steel section is ${21.10}^{10}\mathit{MPa}$.

$\begin{array}{c}{y}_{c}=\frac{2\times 0.045\times 1.38888-21\times 7{10}^{-4}\times 1.78571}{2\times 0.045+21\times 7{10}^{-4}}=0.94317{10}^{-2}m\\ {z}_{c}=0m\end{array}$

### 2.2 Quadratic times

The quadratic moments of rectangular concrete sections are calculated by the following formula:

$\frac{b{h}^{3}}{12}+b\times h\times {d}^{2}$

Where, b represents the width, h the height and distance from the centre of gravity of the section from the axis for which the moment is calculated.

The quadratic moment of the concrete cross section is then obtained in relation to the $z$ axis through the elastic centre:

$\begin{array}{cc}{I}_{\mathit{béton}}& =\frac{0.3\times {0.05}^{3}}{12}+(0.3\times 0.05)\cdot {(0.125-0.94317\cdot {10}^{-2})}^{2}+\frac{0.1\times {0.2}^{3}}{12}+(0.1\times 0.2)\cdot {(0.94317\cdot {10}^{-2})}^{2}\\ & +\frac{0.2\times {0.05}^{3}}{12}+(0.2\times 0.05)\cdot {(0.125+0.94317\cdot {10}^{-2})}^{2}=0.4547\cdot {10}^{-3}{m}^{4}\end{array}$

Inertials of steels are calculated by the following formula:

$\frac{\pi {\phi }^{4}}{64}+S\times {d}^{2}\approx S\times {d}^{2}$

Where, $\phi$ represents the diameter of the steel, $S$ the cross section of steel and the distance from the centre of gravity of the cross section to the axis for which the moment is calculated. The diameter of the steels being small, the first term is neglected.

The quadratic moment of the steel sections is obtained from the $z$ axis through the elastic centre:

$3{10}^{-4}\times {(0.125-0.94317{10}^{-2})}^{2}+4{10}^{-4}\times {(0.125+0.94317{10}^{-2})}^{2}=0.1124{10}^{-4}{m}^{4}$

For the complete section of the beam, the quadratic moment weighted by the d-Young modules of the material is:

$\mathit{EI}=2{10}^{10}\times 0.4547{10}^{-3}+21{10}^{10}\times 0.1124{10}^{-4}=11.4544{10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}.{m}^{4}$

### 2.3 Load case 1

For load 1 (concentrated load in the middle of the beam), the deflection is calculated by the following RDM formula:

$f=\frac{F\times {l}^{3}}{48\mathit{EI}}$

Which gives the deflection:

$f=\frac{10000\times {5}^{3}}{48\times 11.4544{10}^{6}}=2.2735{10}^{-3}m$

The following generalised efforts can also be calculated:

- l-shear force at the beginning of the beam (left part) is $\frac{F}{2}=5000N$ ,
- the bending moment in the middle of the beam is $\frac{F\times l}{4}=1.25{10}^{4}\mathit{N.m}$.

### 2.4 Load case 2

For load case 2 (clean beam weight), the deflection is calculated by the strength formula of the following materials:

$f=\frac{5\times p\times {l}^{4}}{384\mathit{EI}}$

where $p$ is the linear load due to the weight of the materials:

$p=g({\rho}_{a}{S}_{a}+{\rho}_{b}{S}_{b})=9.8\times (2800\times 7{10}^{-4}+2400\times 0.045)=1111.9{\mathit{N.m}}^{-1}$

Which gives the deflection:

$f=\frac{5\times 1111.9\times {5}^{4}}{384\times 11.4544{10}^{6}}=7.9{10}^{-4}m$

### 2.5 Load case 3

For load 3 (homogeneous temperature rise), the beam being isostatic and the coefficients of expansion of concrete and steel being identical, the solution is simple:

The general constraints and efforts are zero.

The lengthening of the beam is: $\Delta l=\alpha \times l\times \Delta T$

This gives with the values of our case:

$\Delta l={10}^{-5}\times 5\times 100=5.{10}^{-3}m$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

POU_D_EM modelling is used.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Longitudinal mesh of the beam:

We have 3 nodes and 2 elements (POU_D_EM).

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/Object_6.svg)

The concrete part of the cross-section of the beam is meshed (DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE / SECT) while the steels are given directly as 4 point fibers in DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE / FIBRE.

Two meshes of the concrete part are tested for the load case 1. The fine mesh is made of 120 fibres and the coarse mesh consists of 16 fibres:

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/10003DD000001E6300001565C50364C9FFE09809.svg)

![Figure](/figures/v3.01.111/10001DFE00001E63000015659D6A523892DA6358.svg)

**Note:**

The problem being $\mathrm{2D}$ , only one fiber in width might seem sufficient (multilayers), but this would lead to zero terms in the rigidity matrix (the own inertia of the fibres not being taken into account) and to an error in the resolution of the equations system.
