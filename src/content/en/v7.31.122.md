# v7.31.122 WTNV122 - Undrained triaxial test with CAM_CLAY, MCC and CSSM behavioral models

**Summary:**

This test validates the specific CAM_CLAY, MCC and CSSM elasto-plastic mechanical laws
Five different modellings are made in 3D,
the first three using the CAM_CLAY [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14) model,
the fourth with the MCC [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48) model and
the conquer with the CSSM [r7.01.44](doc:r7.01.49) model.
In each model, the test is performed in hydro-mechanical coupling and includes two loading routes:

- A hydrostatic compression path in drained condition.
- A vertical compression path without dredging by maintaining the lateral stresses constant.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

For modellings A, B and C, the first loading path for the CAM_CLAY model is a drained isotropic compression ($PRE1=0$) to the ${P}_{sup}$ pressure. For the second route, the ${P}_{sup}$ pressure is kept constant on the side faces and, simultaneously, a vertical pressure displacement is imposed. This second load is undrained, which corresponds to a zero hydrostatic flux on all sides.

- For modelling A: ${P}_{sup}={P}_{conso}=2{P}^0_{cr}-P_{trac}=600$ kPa (final contracting status);
- For model B: ${P}_{sup}={P}^0_{cr}=300$ kPa (critical final state with zero volume change);
- For Modelling C: ${P}_{sup}=220$ kPa $<P^0_{cr}$ (final dilating state).

For Modelling D, for model MCC, the first load is also isotropic compression, in drained condition,
up to ${P}_{sup}=2$ MPa. The second load is the same as the one previously described in the modellings B, C and D.

For model E, for model CSSM, the first load is isotropic compression, in drained condition,
The second load is identical to those described in the previous modellings.

> **Note:**
> For modellings A, B and C, for CAM_CLAY, it is required that in the initial state
the hydrostatic stress is strictly greater than zero (see [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14)).
For this purpose, a purely elastic calculation by changing the pressure from $0$ to $1$ Pa is performed.
From this calculation is extracted the stress field at the gauss points. This stress field resulting from the elastic calculation
is considered as the initial state of the hydrostatic stress required by the CAM_CLAY law of the following calculation
(isotropic compression in drained conditions up to ${P}_{sup}$).

- A hydrostatic compression path in drained condition to the consolidation pressure.

- On the face *BAS* : $DZ=0$.
- On the face *GAUCHE* : $DY=0$.
- On the face *DERRIERE* : $DX=0$.

The load consists of the same pressure distributed in compression on the
3 mesh cells: *HAUT*, *DROITE* and *DEVANT* to simulate a hydrostatic test,
and a zero water pressure to simulate drainage condition ($PRE1=0$).
Then, the distributed pressure is kept constant on the side faces *DROITE* and *DEVANT*, a $DZ$ displacement is imposed on the *HAUT* face variable over time, and the hydraulic load (zero flow) is changed to simulate the undrained condition.

## 5 Summary of the results of modellings A, B and C

By interpreting the $(P,Q)$ diagram, with $P=-(2\sigma'_{xx}+\sigma'_{zz})/3$ and $Q=|\sigma'_{xx}-\sigma'_{zz}|$
for the three modellings A, B and C of this test case, it is clear that in Modelling A,
the load remains hydrostatic until an effective pressure value equal to $600$ kPa.
Once vertical displacement is imposed and varies over time, the pressures on the side faces are maintained
a stress deviator is induced and increases with time with positive hardening.
When you get closer to the point $Q=MP$, you tend to the perfect plasticity with plastic discharge without nutring
and without any change in stress (see [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14)).

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.122/10000000000002D00000022C9905C25678953337.png)

*$Q$ based on $P$ (modelization A).*

In model B, after a hydrostatic load reaching critical pressure at $300$ kPa,
the second load is only deviatoric with a hydrostatic pressure maintained at $300$ kPa.
When you reach the critical point, you touch the critical slope, where plasticity is perfect with plastic flow
without nutting and without variation of stresses.

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.122/10000000000002C7000002259B23C2EB5ABB3E22.png)

*$Q$ based on $P$ (modelization B).*

In model C, vertical displacement is imposed before the hydrostatic load reaches critical pressure.
The stress deviator varies over time, while the pressures on the side faces are maintained constant.
As the criterion of plasticity is met in the field of dilatance,
The hardening is negative and the stress deviator decreases with time.

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.122/10000000000002C7000002257EFD43449D18A342.png)

*$Q$ based on $P$ (modelization C).*

The v7.31.122-modelization_D shows the path of the actual constraints in their meridian plane, where:

The load is hydrostatic until the $-\sigma'_m=2$ MPa stress (drained isotropic compression phase). Then, when the load is undrained, the deviator becomes non-zero and the plastic flow occurs very early. The stress path reaches the critical state line of $\sigma'_{eq}+M(\sigma'_m-\sigma_0)=0$ [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48)_ equation when the effective average pressure $-\sigma'_m$ decreases. This path is similar to that obtained with model A.
