# v8.01.105 FDLV105 - Adding mass on axisymmetric piston coupled to an incompressible fluid column

**Summary:**

This fluid field test implements modal analysis on a coupled fluid structure incompressible fluid piston-column type with free surface. The piston and fluid are modeled respectively by mechanical and thermal elements **axisymmetric**. Thus, the mass calculation added in axisymmetric configuration is validated.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.105/1000095400002C57000008E3C131B213B2275130.svg)

The system consists of a circular vibrating piston when in contact with a column of ring fluid, which is completed by a free surface:

Length: $1m$

width: $0.03m$

height : $0.025m$

The revolution axis is the $\mathrm{OY}$ axis of the reference point. $\mathrm{OX}$ is the radial axis.

### 1.2 Material properties

Structure: steel - elastic material

$E={2.10}^{13}\mathrm{Pa}$

$\nu =0.3$

${\rho}_{s}=7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

The spring has a stiffness per $K=\frac{{10}^{5}}{2\pi }N/m/\mathrm{rad}$ radian

Fluid: equivalent thermal material

$\lambda =1.$

${\rho}_{f}{C}_{p}=1000.$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Structure side: degree of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$ of all nodes of the structure is blocked: DX: 0.0

Fluid side: zero pressure (i.e. temperature) is imposed on the nodes of the free surface.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The following coupled problem is resolved analytically:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}m\ddot{y}+ky=F\\ \frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {y}^{2}}=0\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial y})={\rho}_{f}\ddot{y}\end{array}$

with

| $F$ hydrodynamic pressure force on the piston |
| --- |
| $P$ hydrodynamic pressure in the fluid |
| $m,k$: mass and stiffness of piston by radian |

The hydrodynamic pressure field in the fluid is written:

$p=-{\rho}_{f}\ddot{y}(y-l)$

from which the pressure force applied to the piston:

$F={\int}_{0}^{e}pnr\mathit{dr}=-{\rho}_{f}\ddot{y}l\frac{{e}^{2}}{2}$

the weight added per radian is: ${m}_{a}={\rho}_{f}l\frac{{e}^{2}}{2}$

the mode of the coupled system is: $f=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{k}{m+{m}_{a}}}=27,25\mathrm{Hz}$

### 2.2 Baseline

Analytic.
