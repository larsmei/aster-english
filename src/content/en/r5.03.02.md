# r5.03.02 Integration of the elastoplastic constitutive relations of Von Mises

**Summary:**

This document describes the quantities computed by the operator STAT_NON_LINE that are required for the implementation of the quasi-static non-linear algorithm described in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) in the case of elastoplastic constitutive behaviours. These quantities are computed by the same subroutines in the operator DYNA_NON_LINE in the case of a dynamic loading [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05).

This description is presented according to the various keywords that allow the user to choose the desired constitutive relation. The constitutive relations treated here are:

- the Von Mises constitutive behaviour with isotropic hardening (linear or non-linear)
- the Von Mises constitutive behaviour with linear kinematic hardening (Prager model)

The integration method used is based on a direct implicit formulation. Starting from the initial state, or from the previous computation time instant, one computes the stress field resulting from a strain increment. One also computes the tangent operator.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Constitutive relations described in this document

In the operator STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) (or DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01)), two types of constitutive behaviour can be treated:

- incremental constitutive behaviour: factor keyword COMPORTEMENT,
- non-linear elastic constitutive behaviour: factor keyword COMPORTEMENT.

For each constitutive behaviour one may choose:

- the constitutive relation: keyword RELATION,
- the mode of computation of the strains: keyword DEFORMATION.

For further details, consult the document [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) of the User's manual; the constitutive behaviours described here fall only under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT.

The relations treated in this document are:

| VMIS_ISOT_LINE : | Von Mises with linear isotropic hardening, |
| --- | --- |
| VMIS_ISOT_TRAC : | Von Mises with isotropic hardening given by a tensile curve, |
| VMIS_ISOT_PUIS : | Von Mises with isotropic hardening given by an analytical curve, |
| VMIS_JOHN_COOK : | Von Mises with Johnson-Cook isotropic hardening, |
| VMIS_CINE_LINE : | Von Mises with linear kinematic hardening. |

### 1.2 Purpose of the integration

To solve the global non-linear problem posed on the structure, the document [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) describes the algorithm used in **code_aster**  for non-linear statics and the document [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05) describes the method used for non-linear dynamics.

These two algorithms rely on the computation of local quantities (at each integration point of each finite element) that result from the integration of the constitutive relations.

At each iteration $n$ of Newton's method [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) one must compute the nodal forces $R({{u}_{i}}^{n})={Q}^{T}{\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ (options RAPH_MECA and FULL_MECA), the stresses ${\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ being computed at each integration point of each element from the displacements ${u}_{i}^{n}$ by means of the constitutive relation. One must also build the tangent operator in order to compute ${K}_{i}^{n}$ (option FULL_MECA).

Before the first iteration, for the prediction phase, one computes ${K}_{i-1}$ (option RIGI_MECA_TANG). The computation of ${K}_{i-1}$ , which is required for the initialisation phase [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01), corresponds to the computation of the tangent operator deduced from the rate problem.

This operator is not identical to the one used to compute ${K}_{i}^{n}$ by the option FULL_MECA, during the Newton iterations. Indeed, the latter operator is tangent to the problem discretised in an implicit manner.

One describes here, for the constitutive relations VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, VMIS_ISOT_PUIS, VMIS_JOHN_COOK and VMIS_CINE_LINE, the computation of the tangent matrix of the prediction phase, ${K}_{i-1}$ , then the computation of the stress field from a strain increment, the computation of the **nodal** forces $R$ and of the tangent matrix ${K}_{i}^{n}$ .

## 2 General notation and assumptions on the strains

All quantities evaluated at the previous time instant are indexed by ${}^{-}$ .

Quantities evaluated at the time instant $t+\Delta t$ are not indexed.

Increments are denoted by $\Delta$ . One thus has:

$\mathrm{Q}=\mathrm{Q}(t+\Delta t)=\mathrm{Q}(t)+\Delta \mathrm{Q}={\mathrm{Q}}^{-}+\Delta \mathrm{Q}$ .

For the computation of the derivatives, one will write: $\dot{Q}$  derivative of $Q$ with respect to time

| $\sigma$ | stress tensor. |
| --- | --- |
| $\text{~}$ | deviatoric operator: ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{ij}=\sigma -\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{kk}{\delta}_{ij}$ . |
| ${()}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ | Von Mises equivalent value: ${\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{ij}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{ij}}$ |
| $\Delta \varepsilon$ | strain increment. |
| $A$ | elasticity tensor. |
| $\lambda ,\mu ,E,\nu ,K$ | coefficients of isotropic elasticity, respectively: Lamé coefficients, Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio and bulk modulus. |
| $3K=3\lambda +2\mu$ | bulk modulus |
| $\alpha$ | mean thermal expansion coefficient. |
| $t$ | time. |
| $T$ | temperature. |
| ${()}_{+}$ | positive part. |

To compute the tangent operators, one will adopt the convention of writing symmetric second-order tensors in the form of vectors with six components. Thus, for a tensor $a$ :

$\vec{a}={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{a}_{xx}& {a}_{yy}& {a}_{zz}\end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}\sqrt{2}{a}_{xy}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{xz}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{yz}\end{array}\right]$

One introduces the hydrostatic vector $\vec{1}$ and the deviatoric projection matrix $\mathrm{P}$ :

$\vec{1}={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 1& 1\end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$

$\mathrm{P}=\text{Id}-\frac{1}{3}\vec{1}\otimes \vec{1}$

### 2.1 Partition of the strains (small strains)

One writes for every time instant:

$\epsilon (t)={\epsilon}^{e}(t)+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}(t)+{\epsilon}^{p}(t)$

with the elastic strain

${\epsilon}^{e}(t)={\mathrm{A}}^{-1}(T(t))\sigma (t)$

with the thermal strain

${\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}(t)=\alpha (T(t))\left(T(t)-{T}_{\mathit{ref}}\right)\mathrm{Id}$

or, more generally:

$\begin{array}{cccc}& {\epsilon}^{\text{th}}\left(T\right)& =& \alpha \left(T\right)\left(T-{T}_{\text{def}}\right)-\alpha \left({T}_{\text{ref}}\right)\left({T}_{\text{ref}}-{T}_{\text{def}}\right)\\ & & =& \widehat{\alpha}\left(T\right)\left(T-{T}_{\text{ref}}\right)\\ \text{et}& {\epsilon}^{\text{th}}\left({T}_{\text{ref}}\right)& =& 0\end{array}$

The elasticity matrix $A$ depends on the time instant $t$ through the temperature. The thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha (T(t))$ is a mean expansion coefficient that may depend on the temperature $T$ . The temperature ${T}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the reference temperature, that is the one for which the thermal expansion is assumed to be zero; if the mean expansion coefficient is not known with respect to ${T}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ , one may use a definition temperature of the mean expansion coefficient ${T}_{\text{def}}$ (defined by the keyword TEMP_DEF_ALPHA of DEFI_MATERIAU) different from the reference temperature [R4.08.01](doc:r4.08.01).

This leads to: $\dot{\epsilon}(t)=\stackrel{.}{\stackrel{⏞}{{\mathrm{A}}^{-1}(T(t))\sigma (t)}}+{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{th}}(t)+{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}(t)$

This choice is made for the sake of consistency with elasticity: one must be able to find the same solution in elasticity (operator MECA_STATIQUE) and in elastoplasticity (operator STAT_NON_LINE) when the material characteristics remain elastic. This choice leads to the discretisation:

$\Delta \varepsilon =\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}+\Delta ({A}^{-1}\sigma )+\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}$

with:

$\Delta ({A}^{-1}\sigma )={A}^{-1}({t}^{-}+\Delta t)({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{A}^{-1}({t}^{-}){\sigma}^{-}$

and

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=(\alpha ({t}^{-}+\Delta t)(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})-\alpha ({t}^{-})({T}^{-}-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}))\mathrm{Id}$

## 3 Von Mises relation with isotropic hardening

### 3.1 Expression of the constitutive relations

These relations are obtained by the keywords VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC and VMIS_ISOT_PUIS.

One describes here these relations in small strains (DEFORMATION='PETIT'):

$\begin{array}{c}\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}.\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}=\dot{\epsilon}-\stackrel{·}{\stackrel{⏞}{{A}^{-1}\sigma }}-{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\text{th}}\\ {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p\right)\le 0\\ (\begin{array}{c}\dot{p}=0\text{si}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p\right)<0\\ \dot{p}\ge 0\text{si}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p\right)=0\end{array}\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cc}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}:& \text{vitesse de déformation plastique,}\\ p:& \text{déformation plastique cumulée}:p=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}:{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}}\\ {\epsilon}^{\text{th}}:& \text{déformation d'origine thermique}:{\epsilon}^{\text{th}}=\alpha \left(T-{T}_{\text{ref}}\right)\text{Id}\end{array}\end{array}$

The hardening function $R(p)$ is deduced from a simple monotonic isothermal tensile test.

For the von Mises relation with hardening, the equivalent plastic strain ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{p}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{p}}$ , the cumulative plastic strain $p$ and the hardening variable $R(p)$ coincide. **code_aster**  stores the same value of these three terms in the internal variable «EPSPEQ» (equivalent plastic strain).

The user may choose a linear hardening (relation VMIS_ISOT_LINE) or a tensile curve given either point by point (relation VMIS_ISOT_TRAC), or by an analytical expression (relation

VMIS_ISOT_PUIS).

The constitutive law VMIS_JOHN_COOK differs from the previous ones in that the hardening function depends on the rate of the cumulative plastic strain and on the temperature.

One describes here these relations in small strains (DEFORMATION='PETIT'):

$\begin{array}{c}\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}=\dot{\epsilon}-\stackrel{·}{\stackrel{⏞}{{A}^{-1}\sigma }}-{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\text{th}}\\ {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p,\dot{p},T\right)\le 0\\ \lbrace \begin{array}{c}\dot{p}=0\text{si}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p,\dot{p},T\right)<0\\ \dot{p}\ge 0\text{si}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p,\dot{p},T\right)=0\end{array}\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cc}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}:& \text{vitesse de déformation plastique,}\\ p:& \text{déformation plastique cumulée,}\\ \dot{p}:& \text{vitesse de déformation plastique cumulée,}\\ {\epsilon}^{\text{th}}:& \text{déformation d'origine thermique}:{\epsilon}^{\text{th}}=\alpha \left(T-{T}_{\text{ref}}\right)\text{Id}\end{array}\end{array}$

The hardening function $R(p,\dot{p},T)$ is deduced from a series of simple monotonic tensile tests at different strain rates and at different temperatures.

#### 3.1.1 Relation VMIS_ISOT_LINE

The data for the material characteristics are those supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_LINE or ECRO_LINE_FO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

/ ECRO_LINE =_F ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

/ ECRO_LINE_FO =_F ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

ECRO_LINE_FO corresponds to the case where ${E}_{T}$ and ${\sigma}_{y}$ depend on the temperature and are then computed for the temperature of the current Gauss point.

Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ are those supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO.

In this case the tensile curve is the following:

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.02/10000200000001830000010F00B159002C963889.png)

That is:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{L}=E{\varepsilon}_{L}\text{si}{\varepsilon}_{L}\le \frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E}\\ {\sigma}_{L}={\sigma}_{y}+{E}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{L}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E})\text{si}{\varepsilon}_{L}\ge \frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E}\end{array}$

**Note:**

${\sigma}_{y}$ *is the elastic limit (the choice of* ${\sigma}_{y}$ *is the user's responsibility: it may correspond to the end of linearity of the real tensile curve, or else to a regulatory or conventional elastic limit. In any case, one uses here the single value defined under* *ECRO_LINE).*

When the criterion is reached one has: ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}-R(p)=0$ . To identify $R(p)$ , one uses the properties of the uniaxial stress state:

$\sigma =(\begin{array}{ccc}{\sigma}_{L}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})$ .    hence $\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{L}\\ p={\varepsilon}_{L}^{P}={\varepsilon}_{L}-\frac{{\sigma}_{L}}{E}\end{array}$ and the criterion is written: ${\sigma}_{L}-R(p)=0$

${\sigma}_{L}-{\sigma}_{y}={E}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{L}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E})={E}_{T}(\frac{{\sigma}_{L}}{E}+p-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E})$

hence

$({\sigma}_{L}-{\sigma}_{y})(1-\frac{{E}_{T}}{E})={E}_{T}p$ that is $({\sigma}_{L}-{\sigma}_{y})=\frac{{E}_{T}.E}{(E-{E}_{T})}p$

whence the linear hardening function: $R(p)=\frac{{E}_{T}E}{E-{E}_{T}}p+{\sigma}_{y}$

#### 3.1.2 Relation VMIS_ISOT_TRAC

The material data are those supplied under the factor keyword TRACTION =_F (SIGM = f), of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU.

f is a function of one or two variables representing the simple tensile curves. The first variable is necessarily the strain; the second, if it exists, is the temperature (parameter of a nappe). For each temperature, the tensile curve must be such that:

- the abscissae (true strains) are strictly increasing,
- the first point of the curve corresponds to $({ϵ}_{1,}{\sigma}_{y})$ where ${ϵ}_{1}\ne 0$ and ${\sigma}_{y}$ is the elastic limit,
- the slope between 2 successive points is less than the elastic slope between 0 and the first point of the curve $(E={\sigma}_{y}/{ϵ}_{1})$ .

To interpolate with respect to temperature, *Code_Aster*  first transforms all the curves $\sigma =f(ϵ)$ given by the user into curves $\sigma (p)=R(p)$ in the following way: if $E$ is the elastic slope between 0 and the first point of the curve $({ϵ}_{1,}{\sigma}_{y})$ (where ${ϵ}_{1}\ne 0$ and ${\sigma}_{y}$ is the elastic limit), every point of the curve $({ϵ}_{i},{\sigma}_{i})$ becomes the point $({p}_{i},{\sigma}_{i})$ with ${p}_{i}={ϵ}_{i}-{\sigma}_{i}/E$ (whence ${p}_{1}=0$ ). Let $\theta$ be the temperature considered; if there exists $k$ such that $\theta \in [:ref:`{\theta}_{k},{\theta}_{k+1} <{\theta}_{k},{\theta}_{k+1}>$]` where $k$ denotes the index of the tensile curves contained in the nappe, one builds the curve $R(p,\theta )$ point by point by interpolating from $R(p,{\theta}_{k})$ and $R(p,{\theta}_{k+1})$ for all the values of $p$ of the union of the values of the abscissae of the curves $k$ and $k+1$ (in the case where these two curves are extended linearly or by a constant function):

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.02/10000000000002C900000248597B1EB23C133FED.png)

If ${n}_{k}$ and ${n}_{k+1}$ are the numbers of points of the curves $k$ and $k+1$ , the number of points $n$ of the curve $R(p,\theta )$ is in the general case ${n}_{k}+{n}_{k+1}-1$ (case where all the non-zero abscissae are distinct).

If $\theta$ lies outside the intervals of definition of the tensile curves, one extrapolates in accordance with the extensions specified by the user in DEFI_NAPPE [U4.31.03](doc:u4.31.03) and according to the previous principle.

*Note:*

To avoid generating significant approximation errors or even obtaining poor tensile curves by extrapolation, it is better not to use a linear extension in DEFI_NAPPE.

In the case where the extension of the shortest curve is «EXCLU», one stops the interpolation at that point and the extension of the interpolated curve is also «EXCLU».

One therefore obtains in all cases a piecewise linear hardening function:

$R(p,q)={s}_{i}+\frac{{s}_{i+1}-{s}_{i}}{{p}_{i+1}-{p}_{i}}(p-{p}_{i})$ for $p\in [:ref:`{p}_{i},{p}_{i+1} <{p}_{i},{p}_{i+1}>$]` for $i+1\le n$ , with ${p}_{1}=0$

Young's modulus corresponding to the temperature $\theta$ is computed in the following way:

$E={E}_{k}+\frac{q-{q}_{k}}{{q}_{k+1}-{q}_{k}}({E}_{k+1}-{E}_{k})$

where, for $i=k$ or $i=k+1$ , ${E}_{i}$ is the elastic slope between $0$ and the first point of the curve $\sigma =f(\epsilon )$ corresponding to the temperature ${\theta}_{i}$ .

It is this Young's modulus that is used in the integration of the constitutive relation.

The elastic limit at the temperature $\theta$ is:

${\sigma}_{y}=R(0,\theta )={\sigma}_{1}$

The user must also give Poisson's ratio $\nu$ and a fictitious Young's modulus (which is used only to compute the elastic stiffness matrix if the keyword NEWTON=_F(MATRICE='ELASTIQUE') is present in STAT_NON_LINE) by the keywords:

/ ELAS =_F (NU = $\nu$ ,    E = $E$ )

/ ELAS_FO =_F (NU = $\nu$ ,    E = $E$ )

### 3.2 Tangent operator. Option RIGI_MECA_TANG

The aim of this paragraph is to compute the tangent operator ${K}_{i-1}$ (computation option RIGI_MECA_TANG called at the first iteration of a new load increment) from the results known at the previous time instant ${t}_{i-1}$ .

To that end, if the stress tensor at ${t}_{i-1}$ lies on the boundary of the elastic domain, one writes the condition:

$\dot{f}=0$

which must be satisfied (for the time-continuous problem) jointly with the condition:

$f=0$

with:

$f(\sigma ,p)={\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}-R(p)$

If on the other hand the stress tensor at ${t}_{i-1}$ lies inside the domain, $f<0$ , then the tangent operator is the elasticity operator.

The quantities appearing in this expression are computed at the previous time instant ${t}_{i-1}$ , which are the only ones known at the time of the prediction phase. By computing the differential:

$\dot{f}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\dot{\sigma}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\dot{p}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\tilde{\dot{\sigma}}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\dot{p}$

since $\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }$ is deviatoric. And therefore

$\dot{f}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\left(2\mu \tilde{\dot{\epsilon}}-2\mu {\dot{\epsilon}}^{P}\right)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\dot{p}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\left(2\mu \dot{\epsilon}-2\mu {\dot{\epsilon}}^{P}\right)+\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}\dot{p}=0$

With $\sigma ={\sigma}^{-}=\sigma \left({t}_{\text{i-}1}\right)$ , $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{-}=\epsilon ({t}_{i-1})$ , ${\epsilon}^{p}={\epsilon}^{{p}^{-}}={\epsilon}^{p}\left({t}_{\text{i-}1}\right)$ and $p{\text{=p}}^{-}=p\left({t}_{\text{i-}1}\right)$

**Note:**

One does not take into account in this expression the variation of the elastic coefficients with temperature. This is an approximation, without important consequence, since this operator serves only to initialise the Newton iterations. On the other hand, the dependence of the tangent operator on the thermal strains is indeed taken into account at the level of the global algorithm [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01).

When one differentiates with respect to $p$ the threshold function $f$ , one has

$\frac{\partial f}{\partial p}=-\frac{dR}{dp}=-{R}^{'}\left(p\right)$

One also has ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}$ and $\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}$ , the expression of $\dot{f}=0$ is therefore rewritten

$\frac{3}{2}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\left(2\mu \dot{\epsilon}-2\mu \dot{p}\frac{3}{2}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\right)-{R}^{'}\left(p\right)\dot{p}=0$

This leads to the following expression of the plastic multiplier:

$\dot{p}=\frac{\left(3\mu \right)}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\frac{\left(\tilde{\sigma}.\dot{\tilde{\epsilon}}\right)}{3\mu +{R}^{'}\left(p\right)}$

hence

${\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{9\mu }{2}\frac{\left(\tilde{\sigma}.\dot{\tilde{\epsilon}}\right)}{3\mu +{R}^{'}\left(p\right)}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}\text{si}f(\sigma ,p)=0\text{(plasticité)}\\ 0\text{si}f<0\text{(élasticité)}\end{array}$

the stress is written

${\dot{\sigma}}_{ij}=3K{\dot{\epsilon}}_{kk}{\delta}_{ij}+2\mu \left({\dot{\tilde{\epsilon}}}_{ij}-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{p}\right)$

one recalls that $2\mu =\frac{E}{1+\nu }$ and $3K=\frac{E}{1-2\nu }$ .

**Note:**

*The information* ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{-}-R\left({p}^{-}\right)=0$ *is stored in the form of an internal variable* $\xi$ *which equals 1 in this case and 0 if* ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{-}<R\left({p}^{-}\right)$ *.*

The tangent operator relates the virtual strain vector ${\epsilon}^{\ast }$ to a virtual stress vector ${\sigma}^{\ast }$ .

The tangent stiffness matrix is written for an elastic constitutive behaviour:

${\sigma}^{\ast }=\left(3K\vec{1}\otimes \vec{1}+2\mu \mathrm{P}\right){\epsilon}^{\ast }$

and for a plastic constitutive behaviour:

${\sigma}^{\ast }=\left(3K\vec{1}\otimes \vec{1}+2\mu \mathrm{P}-{C}_{p}\mathrm{s}\otimes \mathrm{s}\right){\epsilon}^{\ast }$

with $s$ the vector of the deviatoric stresses associated with ${\sigma}^{-}$ defined by:

${s}^{T}=\left({\tilde{s}}_{11}^{-},{\tilde{s}}_{22}^{-},{\tilde{s}}_{33}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{s}}_{12}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{s}}_{23}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{s}}_{31}^{-}\right)$

and:

${C}_{p}=\xi \frac{{\left(3\mu \right)}^{2}}{{\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\right)}^{2}}\frac{1}{3\mu +{R}^{'}}$

$\xi =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}1\text{si}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{-}-R\left({p}^{-}\right)=0\\ 0\text{sinon}\end{array}$

In the case of the first load increment, therefore if the state at the previous time instant corresponds to an unstressed initial state, the tangent operator is identical to the elasticity operator.

### 3.3 Computation of the stresses and of the internal variables

The decomposition of the strains makes it possible to write:

$\Delta \varepsilon =\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}+\Delta ({A}^{-1}\sigma )+\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}$

That is, taking the spherical and deviatoric parts:

$D\tilde{e}={\mathrm{De}}^{p}+D(\frac{\tilde{s}}{\mathrm{2m}})$ since $\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{\text{th}}=0.$

$\text{tr}\Delta \varepsilon =\Delta (\frac{\text{tr}\sigma }{\mathrm{3K}})+\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}$ since $\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=0.$

By direct implicit discretisation of the constitutive relations for isotropic hardening, one then obtains:

$2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-({\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma})=\frac{3}{2}2\mu \Delta p\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}}{{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma})}_{\mathrm{eq}}}-2\frac{\mu}{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}$

$\mathrm{tr}\sigma =\frac{\mathrm{3K}}{{\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}\mathrm{tr}{\sigma}^{-}+3K\mathrm{tr}\Delta \varepsilon -3K\mathrm{tr}{\Delta \varepsilon }^{\mathrm{th}}$

$\begin{array}{c}{({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )\le 0\\ \Delta p =0\text{si}{({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}<R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )\\ \Delta p \ge 0\text{si}{({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )\end{array}$

One defines, in order to simplify the notation, the tensor ${\sigma}^{e}$ such that:

${\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}=\frac{2\mu }{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}$ and $\text{tr}{\sigma}^{e}=\text{tr}\sigma$ .

Two cases arise:

- ${({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}<R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )$

in this case: $\Delta p =0\text{soit}\tilde{\sigma}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}$ , hence: ${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}_{\text{eq}}<R({p}^{-})$

- ${({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )$

in this case: $\Delta p \ge 0$ hence: ${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}_{\text{eq}}\ge R({p}^{-})$

One deduces from this the solution algorithm:

- if ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{\text{eq}}^{e}\le R({p}^{-})$ then $\Delta p=0\text{soit}\tilde{\sigma}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}$
- if ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{\text{eq}}^{e}>R({p}^{-})$

then one must solve: $\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}=\tilde{{\sigma}^{-}}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}+\frac{3}{2}2\mu \Delta p\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}}{{({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\mathrm{eq}}}$

hence, by factoring ${\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}$ and taking the Von Mises equivalent value:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=(1+\frac{3}{2}\frac{2\mu \Delta p }{{({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}}){({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}$

that is:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )+3\mu \Delta p$

since: ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}_{\text{eq}}=R(\overline{p}+\Delta p )$

This is a scalar equation in $\Delta p$ , linear or not according to $R(p)$ . $\Delta p$ is computed in the following way:

- in the case where the hardening is linear (relation VMIS_ISOT_LINE), one obtains directly:

$\Delta p =\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}-{\sigma}_{y}-R'{p}^{-}}{R'+3\mu }$ $R'=\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}$ with

- in the case where the hardening is given by a piecewise affine tensile curve (relation VMIS_ISOT_TRAC), one takes advantage of the piecewise linearity to determine $\Delta p$ exactly (see § Appendix 1 );
- in the case of a hardening defined by a power law (relation VMIS_ISOT_PUIS), $\Delta p$ is the solution of the non-linear equation: $R({p}^{-}+\Delta p )+3\mu \Delta p -{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=0$ . This equation is solved by an iterative method (secant-type algorithm). In the neighbourhood of the origin, one linearises $R(p)$ , because the derivative ${R}^{'}=\frac{E}{\text{an}}{(\frac{E}{a{\sigma}_{y}}p)}^{\frac{1}{n}-1}$ is infinite at $p=0$ . Therefore if $p<{p}_{0}$ , one replaces $R(p)$ by ${R}^{\text{lin}}(p)={\sigma}_{y}+\frac{p}{{p}_{0}}(R({p}_{0})-{\sigma}_{y})$ , which avoids the search for a numerically almost zero solution. In practice, one chooses ${p}_{0}={10}^{-10}$ .

Once $\Delta p$ has been determined, one computes $\sigma$ by:

${\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-3\mu \Delta p }{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}.{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}$

and

$\text{tr}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )=\text{tr}{\sigma}^{e}$ .

The options RAPH_MECA and FULL_MECA both perform the previous computation, which makes explicit the computation of $R({u}_{i}^{n})$ . One notes that in reality, $R({u}_{i}^{n})={Q}^{T}{\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ where ${\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ is computed not as a function of ${u}_{i}^{n}$ , but of ${\sigma}_{i-1}\text{et}{\Delta u }_{i}^{n}$ .

### 3.4 Tangent operator. Option FULL_MECA

The option FULL_MECA makes it possible to compute the tangent matrix ${K}_{i}^{n}$ at each iteration. The tangent operator used to build it is computed directly on the previous discretised system (one writes, for simplicity: $\tilde{\sigma}=\tilde{{\sigma}^{-}}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma},p={p}^{-}+\Delta p$ ) and one writes the expressions only in the isothermal case.

- If the stress tensor lies on the boundary of the domain, $f=0$ then one has, by differentiating the expression of the normality rule in $\tilde{\sigma}=\tilde{{\sigma}^{-}}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma}$ :

$2\mu {\delta \varepsilon }^{p}=2\mu \delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\delta \tilde{\sigma}=\frac{3}{2}2\mu (\deltap \frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}+\Delta p \frac{\delta \tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}-\frac{3}{2}\Delta p \frac{\tilde{\sigma}:d\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{{\text{eq}}^{3}}}.\tilde{\sigma})$

where ${\delta \varepsilon }^{p},\delta \tilde{\varepsilon},\delta \tilde{\sigma}$ represent infinitesimal increments about the solution of the incremental elastoplastic problem obtained previously.

Since:

$\frac{3}{2}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}:d\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}={R}^{'}(p)\mathrm{dp}$

by performing the tensor product of the first equation by $\tilde{\sigma}$ one has:

$2\mu \tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\sigma}=2\mu \deltap .{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$

by eliminating $\deltap$ from the last two equations:

$\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\sigma}=\frac{2\mu \tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\varepsilon}}{1+\frac{3\mu }{{R}^{'}(p)}}$ .

- If on the other hand the stress tensor lies inside the domain, $f<0$ , then the tangent operator is the elasticity operator.

By expressing $\deltap$ and $\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\sigma}$ in the first equation, one obtains:

$2\mu \delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\delta \tilde{\sigma}=\frac{3\mu \Delta p }{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\delta \tilde{\sigma}+{C}_{p}.{(\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\varepsilon})}_{+}\tilde{\sigma},$

with:

${C}_{p}=\frac{9{\mu}^{2}}{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{2}}(1-\frac{{R}^{'}(p)\Delta p}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}})\frac{1}{{R}^{'}(p)+3\mu }$

The positive part ${(\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\varepsilon})}_{+}$ makes it possible to gather into a single equation the two conditions:

- either $f<0$ , which implies $\Delta p =0$
- or $f=0$

One then obtains:

$\delta \tilde{\sigma}=\frac{2\mu }{a}\delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\frac{{C}_{p}}{a}{(\tilde{\sigma}:\delta \tilde{\varepsilon})}_{+}\tilde{\sigma}$

by setting:

$a=1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p }{R(p+\Delta p )}$

The tangent operator relates the virtual strain vector ${\varepsilon}^{\ast }$ to a virtual stress vector ${\sigma}^{\ast }$ .

The tangent stiffness matrix is written for an elastic constitutive behaviour:

${\sigma}^{\ast }=(K\overrightarrow{1}\otimes \overrightarrow{1}+2\mup ){\varepsilon}^{\ast }$

and for a plastic constitutive behaviour:

${\sigma}^{\ast }=(K\overrightarrow{1}\otimes \overrightarrow{1}+\frac{2\mu }{a}P-\frac{{C}_{p}}{a}s\otimes s){\varepsilon}^{\ast }$

with $s$ the vector of the deviatoric stresses associated with ${\sigma}^{-}$ defined by:

${s}^{T}=({\tilde{\sigma}}_{11}^{-},{\tilde{\sigma}}_{22}^{-},{\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{12}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{23}^{-},\sqrt{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{31}^{-})$

and:

$\xi =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}1\text{si}\Delta \varepsilon \text{conduit à une plastification}\text{et}\tilde{\sigma}.\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}\ge 0\\ 0\text{sinon}\end{array}$

One observes that the tangent operator of the system arising from the implicit discretisation differs from the tangent operator of the rate problem (RIGI_MECA_TANG). One recovers it by setting: $\Delta p =0$ in the expressions of ${C}_{p}$ and $a$ .

- If ${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta \tilde{\sigma})}_{\text{eq}}<R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)\text{alors}\Delta p=0$ then $2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}=\Delta \tilde{\sigma},$ that is $\Delta y =0.$
- Otherwise, the solution technique consists in expressing $\Delta y$ as a function of $\Delta p$ . One then obtains a non-linear scalar equation in $\Delta p$ .

One sets: ${\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}=\frac{2\mu }{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{q}$ . In the same way as for the integration outside plane stress, one obtains:

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{e}+2\mu {\stackrel{}{\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}}}^{y}=({\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+\stackrel{}{\Delta \tilde{\sigma}})(1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p}{R(p+\Delta p)})$ .

But this expression involves an additional unknown $\Delta y$ : In particular:

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{33}^{y}=({\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{-}+\Delta {\tilde{\sigma}}_{33})(1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p}{R(p+\Delta p)})$

now

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{33}^{y}=\frac{2}{3}\Delta y$

and $\mathrm{tr}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )=3K\mathrm{tr}{\Delta \varepsilon }^{q}+3{K}^{+}\Delta y+\frac{3{K}^{+}}{3{K}^{-}}\mathrm{tr}{\sigma}^{-}-3{K}^{+}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}$

Since:

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{e}+\Delta {\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}={\sigma}_{33}^{e}+\Delta {\sigma}_{33}=\frac{\mathrm{tr}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}{3}=0-\frac{\mathrm{tr}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )}{3}$

One obtains an equation linking $\Delta y$ and $\Delta p$ :

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}+2\frac{2}{3}\Delta y=(1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p}{R(p+\Delta p)})(\frac{-\mathrm{tr}{\sigma}_{e}-3K\Delta y}{3})$

with:

$\mathrm{tr}{\sigma}_{e}=\frac{3K}{3{K}^{-}}\mathrm{tr}{\sigma}^{-}+3K\mathrm{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{q}-3K\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}$

That is:

$\Delta y(\frac{4\mu }{3}+K(1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p}{R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)}))={\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{e}-\frac{\text{tr}{\sigma}_{e}}{3}(1+\frac{3\mu \Delta p}{R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)})$

noting that:

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{e}={\sigma}_{33}^{e}-\frac{\text{tr}{\sigma}_{e}}{3}=0-\frac{\text{tr}{\sigma}_{e}}{3}$

and by making $\mu ,K$ explicit, one obtains:

$\Delta y =\frac{3(1-2\nu )\Delta p }{E\Delta p +2(1-\nu )R(p+\Delta p )}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}^{e}$

to be substituted into the equation in $\Delta p$ (identical to the previous cases):

${(\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}+2\mu {\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}}^{y})}_{\mathrm{eq}}-3\mu \Delta p-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)=0$

where $\Delta y$ is expressed as a function of $\Delta p$ since: ${\stackrel{}{\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}}}^{y}=\frac{\Delta y }{3}(\begin{array}{ccc}-1& & \\ & -1& \\ & & 2\end{array})$

The scalar equation in $\Delta p$ thus obtained is always non-linear. This equation is solved by a method of searching for zeros of functions, based on a secant algorithm. Once the solution $\Delta p$ is known one computes $\Delta y$ then $\sigma$ .
