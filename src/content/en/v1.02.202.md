# v1.02.202 RTOOL01 – Release of DDL s on SMART with Rtool

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the kinematic relationships produced by the Rtool tool of salome_meca. This tool, allows to easily release rotational degrees of freedom (DDL) between two elements structures. The modal analysis of the model of the reinforced concrete building carried out during the SMART2013 (Seismic design and best-estimate Methods Assessment of Reinforced concrete buildings subject to Torsion and non-linear effects) campaign conducted by the CEA and EDF. This campaign focuses on the seismic study of irregular reinforced concrete buildings whose response is strongly affected by the torsion movements of the whole. The model represents an electric concrete building reinforced with three floors on a 1/4 scale. It is carried out in the law of similarity of Cauchy-Froud (i.e. the eigenfrequencies increased by a ratio 2).

The objective is to perform modal analysis under different release and orientation configurations, and to compare the first three eigenfrequencies obtained.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The model model model is modeled at the 1/4th scale of a typical nuclear facility building. It consists of three V01/02 V03 and V04 U-shaped sails and three trapezoidal floors. Openings are pierced in the V01/02 and V03 sails. Each floor is supported by a horizontal beam as well as a vertical pole. The model is asymmetrical to facilitate twisting movements. ## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This analysis consists of 5 modellings:

**•** **Modelization A**: In this Modelling the first three frequencies are calculated on the original mesh. These values are used as a reference to verify the operation of the Rtool tool;

**•** **Modelization B**: In this Modelling the first three eigenfrequencies will be calculated on a modified mesh. Rotation will be released on an internal node of the pole. It will be released node/node;

**•** **Modelization C**: In this Modelling the first three frequencies are calculated on a modified mesh. Rotation on the three restraintss between poles and floors will be released. It will be node/plane release;

**•** **Modelization D**: In this Modelling the first three frequencies are calculated on a modified mesh. Rotation will be released on the common stops between sails and floors. It will be a plan/plan release;

**•** **Modelling E**: In this Modelling, before releasing DDL as in **D** Modelling, a rigid rotation of mesh is made according to the global axes X, Y and Z. Then, the operations performed in the Modelling **D** are repeated. This last study will confirm the correct writing of kinematic relations by the Rtool tool.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

As already mentioned, **A** Modelling gives us a reference for modellings **B, C, D** . The simple release of DDL results in a change of stiffness, which causes a change in the model's eigenmodes and frequencies. Furthermore, the relaxation is done in directions parallel to the global axes, this allows to verify the kinematic relationships produced by the Rtool tool.

In order to test the tool in a more complex case, a rotation of the model is performed for the model **E** . Once the rotation is carried out on the three main axes (rotation of **35°** around the **X** axis, **50°** around the **Y** axis and **65°** around the **Z** axis), a new calculation of the eigenfrequencies is made, after having released the DDL sails/floors. In this case, the verification of the kinematic relations produced by Rtool is much more complex, but a simple comparison with the **D** Modelling is sufficient to verify the writing of the kinematic relations produced by the Rtool tool.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The reinforced concrete walls and floors are modeled by elements plates DKT. The reinforced concrete is modeled with a simple elastic law. The beams and poles are modeled by elements POU_D_E. These are considered elastic. The sole is modeled by elements 3D and is considered elastic.

Depreciation is not considered in modelling.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh contains about 2600 mesh cells and 2700 nodes (without considering the sole). The mesh is the SMART model.
