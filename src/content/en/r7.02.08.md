# r7.02.08 Computation of the stress intensity factors by extrapolation of the displacement field

**Summary:**

One describes here a method for computing $\mathit{K1}$ , $\mathit{K2}$ and $\mathit{K3}$ in 2D (plane and axisymmetric) and in 3D by extrapolation of the displacement jumps on the crack lips. It can be used by means of the command POST_K1_K2_K3, both for a meshed crack (classical finite elements) and for a non-meshed crack (enriched finite elements: X-FEM method).

If the crack is meshed, it must necessarily be plane; if the crack is not meshed (X-FEM method), it may be non-plane (but sufficiently regular). In both cases, the method is applicable only for linear elastic, homogeneous and isotropic materials.

The method used is theoretically less accurate than the computation from the bilinear form of the energy release rate and of the singular displacements [R7.02.01 and R7.02.05] (operator CALC_G). It nevertheless makes it possible to obtain easily relatively reliable values of the stress intensity factors. The comparison of the different computation methods is useful for estimating the accuracy of the results obtained.

The accuracy of the results of the method of extrapolation of the displacement jumps is distinctly improved if the mesh is quadratic. For a meshed crack, it is recommended to use so-called “Barsoum” elements at the crack front (elements whose mid-side nodes are located at the quarter of the edges). For a non-meshed crack, it is recommended to enrich several layers of elements around the crack front.

## 1 Statement of the problem

The method for computing the stress intensity factors by extrapolation of the displacement is based on the asymptotic expansion of the displacement field at the crack front.

In 2D, in an elastic, linear, isotropic and homogeneous medium, the stress and displacement fields are known analytically for the opening modes of the crack (characterised by $\mathit{K1}$ ), of in-plane sliding ($\mathit{K2}$ ) and of anti-plane sliding ($\mathit{K3}$ ), cf.  and . In the general case in 3D, one can show that the asymptotic behaviour of the displacements and of the stresses is the sum of the solutions corresponding to modes 1 and 2 (in plane strains) and to mode 3 (anti-plane), and of four other particular solutions, but which are more regular than the preceding ones.

In all cases, the singularity is therefore the same and one can write the following relations in the plane normal to the crack front, at a point $M$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{1}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1-{\nu}^{2})}[{U}_{m}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\\ {K}_{2}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1-{\nu}^{2})}[{U}_{n}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\\ {K}_{3}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1+\nu )}[{U}_{t}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\end{array}$

with:
$t,n$ in the plane of the crack at $M$ ,
$t$ vector tangent to the crack front at $M$ ,
$n$ vector normal to the crack front at $M$ ,
$m$ vector normal to the plane of the crack at $M$ ,
$[U]$ displacement jump between the crack lips:
$[{U}_{m}]=({U}^{\mathrm{lèvre}\mathrm{supérieure}}-{U}^{\mathrm{lèvre}\mathrm{inférieure}})\cdot m$
$r=\parallel \text{MP}\parallel$ where $P$ is a point of the plane normal to the crack front at $M$ , located on one of the lips.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.08/Shape1.gif)

If the crack is not plane, the three vectors are defined locally at the point $M$ of the front considered. The preceding relations therefore supply a method for identifying numerically $\mathit{K1}$ , $\mathit{K2}$ and $\mathit{K3}$ . From the stress intensity factors, the Irwin formula then makes it possible to compute the energy release rate $G$ :

$G=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{1}^{2}+{K}_{2}^{2})$                     in plane stresses

$G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{1}^{2}+{K}_{2}^{2})(+\frac{1+\nu }{E}{K}_{3}^{2})$     in plane strains (and in 3D)

- *One can observe that the signs of* $\mathit{K2}$ *and* $\mathit{K3}$ *depend on the orientation of* $\text{t}$ *and* $\text{n}$ *. This is not too troublesome insofar as the fracture or fatigue criteria use only the absolute values of* $\mathit{K2}$ *and* $\mathit{K3}$ *.*
- *One can also give expressions as a function of the stress fields, but the values of the stress vectors on the crack lips are less accurate than the displacements (because they come from a transfer from the Gauss points to the nodes).*
- *The expression of the asymptotic fields is valid for non-plane cracks (curved cracks, for example), but these must still be sufficiently regular. The user must ensure at least that a normal can be defined at every point of the front.*
- *The method used here is theoretically less accurate than the computation from the bilinear form* of the energy release rate and of the singular displacements [R7.02.01 and R7.02.05] ( *operator* *CALC_G). It nevertheless makes it possible to obtain easily relatively reliable values of the stress intensity factors. The comparison of the different computation methods is always useful for estimating the accuracy of the results obtained.*

## 2 Implementation of the extrapolation methods

The methods of extrapolation of the displacements are implemented in the operator POST_K1_K2_K3, from the displacement field computed on the whole structure. The definitions of the stress intensity factors are true only asymptotically; the extrapolation is therefore carried out by restricting oneself to the neighbourhood of the crack front limited by a maximum distance $\mathit{dmax}$ from the front. $\mathit{dmax}$ is the parameter ABSC_CURV_MAXI of the operator. In the case of a meshed crack ABSC_CURV_MAXI is optional. If it is not entered, $\mathit{dmax}$ is computed automatically in POST_K1_K2_K3 and is equal to four times the maximum size of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the front.

The general principle of the computation is the following:

**Loop over these nodes:**

If ${r}_{i}^{\sup}=\parallel {\text{MP}}_{i}^{\sup}\parallel \text{dmax}$ : extraction of the displacement at ${P}_{i}^{\sup}$
If ${r}_{i}^{\inf}=\parallel {\text{MP}}_{i}^{\inf}\parallel \text{dmax}$ : extraction of the displacement at ${P}_{i}^{\inf}$

> Computation of the displacement jump in the three directions

**Loop over the nodes of the crack front (current point: $M$ )**

Definition of the plane $\Gamma$ normal to the crack and to the crack front, at the point $M$ (plane of normal $\text{t}$ )
Identification of the nodes of the two lips which belong to $\Gamma$ : ${P}_{i}^{\sup}$ and ${P}_{i}^{\inf}$

Extrapolation of the displacement jump

Three extrapolation methods are programmed. They are illustrated in this paragraph for a meshed crack (quadratic mesh), with or without elements of “Barsoum” type. The Barsoum elements are such that the non-vertex nodes on the sides of the quadratic elements touching the crack front are moved to the quarter of the side. They make it possible to capture better the singularity of the stress field at the crack front

| ![Figure](images/Object_27.png) Classical finite element (M being a node of the crack front) | ![Figure](images/Object_28.png) Finite element of Barsoum type |
| --- | --- |

- **Method 1:** one computes the square of the jump of the displacement field and one divides it by $r$ . Different values of ${K}^{2}$ are obtained (up to a multiplicative factor) by extrapolation to $r=0$ of the straight-line segments thus obtained. If the solution were perfect (analytical asymptotic field everywhere), one should obtain a straight line. In reality, one obtains almost a straight line with a mesh of “Barsoum” type, and a non-straight curve otherwise:

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.08/Object_30.svg)

- **Method 2:** one plots the square of the jump of the displacement field as a function of $r$ . The approximations of $K$ are (still up to a multiplicative factor) equal to the square root of the slope of the segments connecting the origin to the different points of the curve.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.08/Object_32.png)

- **Method 3:** one identifies the stress intensity factor $K$ from the displacement jump $[U]$ by a least-squares method. The fitting is carried out on a segment of length $\mathit{dmax}$ , where $\mathit{dmax}$ is the parameter fixed in the operand ABSC_CURV_MAXI of the operator POST_K1_K2_K3 or, in the case of a meshed crack, if ABSC_CURV_MAXI is not given, $\mathit{dmax}$ is equal to four times the maximum size of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the front:

$K$ minimises $J(k)=\frac{1}{2}\underset{0}{\overset{\text{dmax}}{\int}}{([U(r)]-k\sqrt{r})}^{2}\mathrm{dr}$

Hence the explicit formula for computing $K$ :

$K=\frac{2}{{r}_{m}^{2}}\underset{0}{\overset{\text{dmax}}{\int}}[U(r)]\sqrt{r}\mathrm{dr}=\frac{1}{{r}_{m}^{2}}\sum_{i=0}^{\mathrm{nbno}-1}({r}_{i+1}-{r}_{i})({[U]}_{i+1}\sqrt{{r}_{i+1}}-{[U]}_{i}\sqrt{{r}_{i}})$

where $\mathit{nbno}$ is the number of nodes on the fitting segment $[0,\mathit{dmax}]$ . One notes that in this expression $K$ is, for a fixed $\mathit{dmax}$ , a linear form of the displacement field.

## 3 Accuracy of the proposed methods

The method of extrapolation of the displacement jumps has been validated on tests whose analytical solutions are known. One presents below certain results, in 2D and in 3D, for a meshed or non-meshed crack. One also compares the results with the theoretically more accurate method based on the computation of the energy release rate and on the singular functions (operator CALC_G: theta method).

## 4 Conclusion

The results obtained with the method of extrapolation of the displacement are on the whole satisfactory, with less than 5% error on $G$ , especially if the elements of the crack front are of Barsoum type (meshed crack case) or if several layers of elements are enriched around the front (non-meshed crack case, X-FEM method). In both cases, it is a matter of capturing as well as possible the asymptotic behaviour of the displacement.

One must indeed note that the asymptotic expression of the displacements is valid only for $r$ tending to 0. This is why one must take care not to choose too large an extrapolation domain (distance $\mathit{dmax}$ of the operator POST_K1_K2_K3 of the order of 4 to 5 elements).

On the tests presented for a meshed crack, method 1 gives the most accurate and the most stable results, whether in 2D or in 3D, if there are Barsoum elements. If the mesh does not contain Barsoum elements, one then advises using the results of method 3. For a non-meshed crack, method 1 also seems the most accurate.

On a study for which one does not know a reference solution, it is possible to estimate a posteriori the quality of the computation. Indeed, POST_K1_K2_K3 systematically supplies, for the first two methods, the maximum values and the minimum values (over the set of the points computed) of the stress intensity factors, as well as the value of G recomputed by the Irwin formula. Method 3 for its part supplies only a single value for each stress intensity factor. This method is a weighted average of the stress intensity factors extrapolated at each node.

A result may be considered satisfactory if the 5 values thus supplied (min and max of methods 1 and 2, and method 3) are close. One can also recommend comparing the results obtained with this method with those coming from the computation of the energy release rate and of the singular functions (operator CALC_G).
