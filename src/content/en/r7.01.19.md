# r7.01.19 Modelling of the creep/plasticity coupling for concrete

**Summary**

This is a coupling model between the constitutive behaviours modelling creep, of GRANGER type, and the plasticity models, either general (von Mises law with isotropic hardening) or specific to concrete, of BETON_DOUBLE_DP type, implemented in the general solution algorithm of DYNA/STAT_NON_LINE. The coupling is carried out by a chaining of the solution of the equations of the two models during an iterative algorithm, which will take place at each Gauss point, at each iteration. It is therefore a "strong" coupling, since the equations of the creep law and of the plasticity law are satisfied rigorously at each instant.

This chaining makes it possible to dispense with the complete development of a genuine coupled creep/plasticity solution, by using the existing models and without intervening in their solution, but it has the disadvantage of a higher computational cost.

## 1 Introduction

This is a coupling model between the constitutive behaviours modelling creep, of GRANGER type, and the concrete models modelling the phenomena of plasticity, either general (von Mises) or specific to concrete, of BETON_DOUBLE_DP type, implemented in the general solution algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01).

The existing models are used, with the objective of chaining the solution of the internal equations of the two models during an iterative algorithm, which takes place at each Gauss point, at each iteration. It is therefore a "strong" coupling, since the internal equilibrium equations of creep and of plasticity are satisfied rigorously at each instant.

This chaining makes it possible to dispense with the complete development of a genuine coupled creep/plasticity solution, but it has the disadvantage of a higher computational cost. Indeed, at each Gauss point, and at each iteration, one must a priori solve several times the equilibrium equations of plasticity and those of creep, until the equilibrium between creep and plasticity is obtained. But this makes it possible to couple a creep law with any plasticity law, for a moderate development cost. On the other hand, in the case of the law BETON_DOUBLE_DP, in post-peak tension, the hardening curve is linear, and the local solution algorithm converges in a single iteration. The computational cost therefore remains limited.

The couplings available by this procedure are, in the version of Code_Aster relating to this document:

- for the creep law: GRANGER_FP, GRANGER_FP_V, GRANGER_FP_INDT
- for the plasticity law: ELAS, VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, VMIS_ISOT_PUIS, ROUSS_PR, BETON_DOUBLE_DP

Note: the coupling between the creep law BETON_UMLV_FP and the damage laws MAZARS and ENDO_ISOT_BETON is in fact a constitutive law written specifically in order to take into account the coupling between these two models, which does not fall under the principle described in this document. This coupling is described in R7.01.06.

## 2 Principle of the creep/plasticity coupling

The coupling is carried out during the solution internal to the Gauss point, within the framework of an iterative algorithm in which the creep and plasticity computations are chained.

In order to carry out such a coupling, one integrates into the local solution of the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE a module making it possible to compute successively, from the same initial state (stresses and internal variables, strains and strain increments), the solution of creep and of plasticity. The principle is the following: during the solution at the Gauss point, from the initial state in stresses, internal variables and strains, a creep computation is performed. The creep strain increment computed during the solution of creep is then subtracted from the total strain increment, and supplied as an argument of the solution of plasticity. The solution of plasticity is carried out from the same initial state in stresses, internal variables and strains, except for the total strain increment, from which the creep strain increment has been removed. When the plastic strain increment has been computed, after solution of plasticity, one can correct the value of the strain at the input of the creep computation, and reiterate the chained creep/plasticity computation, until convergence of the solution is obtained, that is, the stability of the stresses and of the creep and plasticity strains.

The stresses computed by means of the creep equations are a priori not identical to those of the plasticity equations. Their equality is not formally written in the equations. But when the creep/plasticity equilibrium is reached, one can show that the stresses at the output of creep are indeed equal to the stresses at the output of plasticity (cf. paragraph 3.2).

Since the coupling is solved by chaining the solutions of creep and of plasticity during an iterative algorithm, this allowing a low cost of software development, computation of the tangent matrix is carried out exclusively from the plasticity model, without taking creep or the creep/plasticity coupling into account, as a simplification. The creep strains then constitute a loading for the plasticity computation. This assumes that the evolution of creep is slower than the evolution of plasticity.

This simplification, all the more relevant when the analysis step is judiciously chosen, makes it possible to converge towards the (global) solution with a number of global iterations slightly larger than in the case where the tangent matrix is computed exactly, but it in no way alters the result of the (converged) analysis.

## 3 Formulation of the equations

### 3.1 Formulation of Granger creep in Code_Aster (Reminder)

The modelling of creep in *Code_Aster* , according to the model of *Granger* , is presented briefly in this paragraph. For more detail, one will refer to [R7.01.01 and V6.04.142].

The expression for the creep strain applied to a linear viscoelastic material, by applying the Boltzmann superposition principle for a non-constant loading history $\sigma (t)$ , can be written in 1D:

${\epsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)=\underset{\tau =0}{\overset{t}{\int}}f(t-\tau )\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \tau }\mathrm{d\tau }=f\ast \frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial t}$

$\ast$ represents the convolution product

$J(t,{t}_{c})=f(t-{t}_{c})$ is the creep function, an increasing function of $(t-{t}_{c})$ and zero for $(t-{t}_{c})$ negative. One can show that any linear viscoelastic body can be modelled by a series arrangement of Kelvin models and that the creep function can then be put in the form:

$f(t)=\sum_{s=1}^{r}{J}_{s}(1-\exp(-\frac{t}{{\tau}_{s}}))$

${\tau}_{s}$ and ${J}_{s}$ are positive coefficients, identified on the experimental creep curves.

A series arrangement of Kelvin models is used, whose coefficients are identified from experimental creep curves. It is shown in practice that the creep curves of concrete are reproduced satisfactorily with a series of 8 models.

The following creep function is therefore used: $J(t,{t}_{c})=\sum_{s=1}^{s=8}{J}_{s}(1-\exp(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}))$

which leads to the expression:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)=\underset{\tau =0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\left[\sum_{s=1}^{s=8}{J}_{s}(1-\exp(-\frac{t-\tau }{{\tau}_{s}}))\right]\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \tau }d\tau$

By differentiating this expression in the case of a single Kelvin model ( *s=1* ), one easily obtains the following equation: ${\tau}_{s}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)={J}_{s}\sigma (t)$

which can also be written: $\sigma (t)=\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{{J}_{s}}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+\frac{1}{{J}_{s}}{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)$

or again: $\sigma (t)=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)$     with    $\mu =\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{{J}_{s}}$     and    $K=\frac{1}{{J}_{s}}$

This formulation can be extended to the case of the series expansion in 8 Kelvin models (or more), subject to a few mathematical developments, which gives:

$\sigma (t)=\sum_{s=1}^{s=8}\left[{K}_{s}{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{s}(t)+{m}_{s}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}^{s}(t)\right]$

The rheological model of creep is represented in the figure below, in the case of a single Kelvin model:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.19/100000000000025D000000F05876084D9268F97C.png)

In order to simplify the presentation of the coupling in the paragraph which follows, only the first term of the Kelvin series decomposition is taken into account, and one writes: $\sigma (t)=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)$

Moreover, for the presentation, $\mu$ and $K$ are assumed constant, that is $\mu ={\mu}^{-}$ and $K={K}^{-}$ *.*

### 3.2 Formulation of the creep/plasticity coupling in 1D

The creep/plasticity coupling corresponds to the rheological model below, in which creep, characterised by the coefficients $K$ and $\mu$ (stiffness and viscosity), and plasticity are modelled in series.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.19/100000000000024F000000B51979BC7A9B8E013B.png)

*The equations governing creep are the following:*

$\sigma =\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

$\sigma =E{e}_{\varepsilon}$

which can be written at the current instant (the exponent ‘-‘ denoting the previous instant):

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}={\varepsilon}^{-}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{e}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

$\sigma =\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{e}\right]$

$\sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{e}$

*The equation governing the plasticity model is the following:*

$\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}\right]$

Which leads, in the case of the *creep/plasticity coupling* , to the following system of equations:

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}={\varepsilon}^{-}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{e}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}$

$\sigma =\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{e}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}\right]$

$\sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{e}$

$\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]$

In the solution of the constitutive equations at the Gauss point, in the elementary routines of *Code_Aster* , the equality of the stresses coming from creep and from plasticity is not formally written, which in fact corresponds to the following system of equations (denoting by ${\sigma}_{1}$ the stress coming from the solution of creep and by ${\sigma}_{2}$ the stress coming from the solution of plasticity at the current instant):

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}={\varepsilon}^{-}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{e}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}$

${\sigma}_{1}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{e}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}\right]$

${\sigma}_{1}=E{\varepsilon}_{e}$

${\sigma}_{2}=E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]$

Now, at equilibrium of the creep/plasticity coupling, one does indeed have equality of the creep strain and of the plastic strain in the equations of the Granger creep model and in the equations of plasticity, which implies, by modifying the equation governing plasticity:

${\varepsilon}_{e}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

then         ${\sigma}_{2}=E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]=E{\varepsilon}_{e}$

whence        ${\sigma}_{2}=E{\varepsilon}_{e}={\sigma}_{1}$

that is, the equality of the stresses computed during the solution of creep with the stresses computed during the solution of plasticity: ${\sigma}_{2}={\sigma}_{1}$

It is the partition of the strains in the form $\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ , and the identity of the Young's moduli of the creep and plasticity models, which are at the origin of this result. Indeed, the same Young's modulus enters the creep model and the plasticity model, and leads, with the identity of the elastic strain, to the equality of the stresses.

*Notes:*  More generally, this kind of algorithm requires that the two laws compute in an **identical**  manner the elastic stress.

Under this assumption, a generalisation of this coupling to models whose viscous and plastic strains are additive is possible [1]. One will then write:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\end{array})={F}_{1}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}};{\sigma}^{-};{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}^{-},...)$

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{2}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}\end{array})={F}_{2}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}};{\sigma}^{-};{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{-},...)$

A theoretical study of the convergence of such a model, under the assumption that the two constitutive behaviours fall within the framework of generalised standard materials, is described in the appendix.

Other possibilities of coupling, concerning in particular the damage and plasticity laws, exist [7]. In particular the coupling BETON_UMLV_FP / ENDO_ISOT_BETON is described in [R7.01.06](doc:r7.01.06).

## 4 Integration algorithm of the creep/plasticity coupling

### 4.1 Algorithm at the Gauss points

In order to introduce the creep/plasticity coupling into *Code_Aster* , the following approach is adopted: an iterative algorithm is used in which, at each Gauss point, creep is chained to plasticity. The creep equation is solved at each iteration $(k)$ by means of an estimated value of the plastic strain increment, which is subtracted from the total strain increment. An estimated value of the creep strain increment is thus obtained, which is subtracted from the total strain for the solution of plasticity at iteration $k$ . A new estimate of the plastic strain increment is thus obtained, which makes it possible to carry out the creep computation at iteration $k+1$ .

The creep/plasticity chaining is carried out iteratively, until a stable solution is obtained, that is, a variation in relative value of the stress lower than a convergence criterion.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.19/1000000000000255000001D1E697489CAEB540DE.png)

Insofar as **the solution of creep in** **Code_Aster is programmed in an incremental manner** , the simplification consists in replacing $\Delta \varepsilon$ by $\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}^{k}$ in the calling arguments of the solution of creep. There is therefore no intervention to be made in the creep solution module. The same is true of the solution of plasticity, in which $\Delta \varepsilon$ is replaced by $\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{k}$ in the calling arguments of the solution of plasticity.

### 4.2 General flow chart

The flow chart of the solution can be outlined as follows:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.19/100000000000029900000265F67FC00583D938DB.png)

*Note* :     $\mathrm{Vi}$  denotes the internal variables

The creep computation has as its only objective to supply the creep strain, which enters as an additional loading of the plasticity computation. The stresses computed during the solution of creep are neither stored in memory nor used for the plasticity computation. At creep/plasticity equilibrium, they are equal to the stresses coming from the plasticity computation.

The internal variables of the creep computation are on the other hand retained from one iteration to the next, because the creep computation at iteration $n$ requires knowledge of the internal variables at iteration $n-1$ . These variables also make it possible to compute, at each time step, the creep strain whose increment is used for the plasticity computation. The same is true of the internal variables of the plasticity model.

### 4.3 Treatment of the internal variables

The simultaneous solution of the two models, creep and plasticity, requires a particular treatment of the internal variables, analogous to what is done in the THM environment (cf [R7.01.10](doc:r7.01.10)): the dimension of the array of internal variables is the sum of the numbers of variables of each model. The information required in order to recover these variables at each computation instant is transmitted to the lower-level routines.

For the accumulation of two coupled mechanical laws, the total number of internal variables is stored in the behaviour map, as well as the name of the laws.

The internal variables of the various laws follow one another in the array of internal variables: first those which concern creep, then those relating to plasticity.

### 4.4 Management of the constitutive behaviours and taking into account the subdivision of the time step

The chaining of the constitutive laws requires computation of the creep strain increment from the internal variables of the model, which is then subtracted from the total strain increment before calling another law. The constitutive behaviours therefore cannot be permuted.

The first law to be coupled must be a creep law of type GRANGER_FP (*Granger* law) or GRANGER_FP_V (*Granger* law with ageing) or GRANGER_FP_INDT (*Granger* law independent of temperature). The order of the constitutive laws defined by the user must respect this logic.

The second law must be a non-linear mechanical law, of type BETON_DOUBLE_DP, or more generally an elastic law ELAS, or an elastoplastic law *.

The number of laws of the coupling is limited to two, the first being a creep law, and failure to respect these modelling choices leads to a «Fatal» stop.

The solution module of the coupled creep/plasticity constitutive behaviour is presented as a new law.

At present, the creep law is not integrated into the automatic subdivision of the time step of *Code_Aster* , because of an explicit solution, unlike the plasticity laws. During the solution of the coupling, the plasticity law benefits from the automatic subdivision of the time step, according to the same conventions as the subdivision applied to the uncoupled plasticity laws (systematic subdivision, or only in the event of non-convergence, or no subdivision).

#### 4.4.1 Convergence criterion

The creep/plasticity chaining is carried out in a loop, in the coupling module, until convergence of the solution. When the creep strain increment and the plasticity strain increment are stable from one iteration to the next, the stresses coming from the creep computation are equal to the stresses coming from the plasticity computation. One could establish a convergence criterion in strain, but it is more logical that the convergence criterion be computed from the stresses, which constitute the output argument of the elementary computations at the Gauss points (together with the internal variables).

Finally, the convergence criterion of the coupling is of the form:

$\frac{∣∣{\sigma}_{1}∣∣-∣∣{\sigma}_{2}∣∣}{∣{\sigma}_{1}∣}<\text{tolérance}$

denoting by ${\sigma}_{1}$ the stress coming from the solution of creep and by ${\sigma}_{2}$ the stress coming from the solution of plasticity at the current instant. It is therefore a relative criterion in stress.

The maximum number of creep/plasticity coupling iterations is defined by the parameter giving the maximum number of iterations of the local solution, ITER_INTE_MAXI. Likewise, the convergence criterion of the coupling, defined by the relative residual in stress, is defined by the maximum residual of the local solutions, RESI_INTE_RELA. These two parameters therefore make it possible to manage the convergence of each of the two local laws, and the convergence of the coupling.

#### 4.4.2 Creep strain increment

Computation of the creep strain increment is carried out in the coupling solution module. Indeed, it is not stored in the internal variables.

From the internal variables corresponding to creep, one can recompute in a simple manner the creep strain at the current and previous instants, and thus deduce from it the creep strain increment to be subtracted from the total strain increment. (One will refer to the reference documentation of the Granger constitutive relation, for further information [R7.01.01 and V6.04.142]).

Within the framework of a one-dimensional expression, the creep strain of the Granger model is the following:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{\mathrm{1D}}({t}_{n})=\sum_{s=1}^{8}{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{s}({t}_{n})$ $=J\cdot {A}_{n}^{0}-\sum_{s=1}^{8}{A}_{n}^{s}$     with $J=\sum_{s}{J}_{s}$

and

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{A}_{n+1}^{0}={A}_{n}^{0}+\Delta (S{)}_{n+1}\\ {A}_{n+1}^{s}={A}_{n}^{s}\cdot \exp(-\frac{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}{{\tau}_{s}})+\Delta (S{)}_{n+1}\cdot \frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}\cdot {J}_{s}\cdot (1-\exp-\frac{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}{{\tau}_{s}})\end{array}$

where     $S=\sigma .h.\frac{T-248}{45}$

$\Delta {t}_{n}$ represents the time increment between the instants ${t}_{n}$ and ${t}_{n+1}$ .

${J}_{s}$ and ${\tau}_{S}$ are coefficients of the series decomposition of the Kelvin model.

The coefficients $J\cdot {A}_{n}^{0}$ and ${A}_{\begin{array}{cc}n& (s=1,8)\end{array}}^{s}$ are in fact tensors of the same dimension as the strain tensor, whose various components are stored as internal variables of the model.

In 3D, the classical assumption consists in supposing the existence of a creep Poisson's ratio, constant and equal to the elastic Poisson's ratio. This creep Poisson's ratio is not taken into account in the 1D formulation: ${e}_{\text{fl}}^{\mathrm{1D}}({t}_{n})=J\cdot {A}_{n}^{0}-\sum_{s=1}^{8}{A}_{n}^{s}$ .

It is therefore necessary to multiply the strain tensor obtained from the internal variables by the matrix:

${H}_{0}=\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}1& -\nu & -\nu & 0& 0& 0\\ -\nu & 1& -\nu & 0& 0& 0\\ -\nu & -\nu & 1& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 1+\nu & 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 1+\nu & 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 1+\nu \end{array}\right]$     in order to obtain the 3D creep strain.

That is    ${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}({t}_{n})={H}_{0}.{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{\mathrm{1D}}({t}_{n})={H}_{0}.(J\cdot {A}_{n}^{0}-\sum_{s=1}^{8}{A}_{n}^{s})$

(One has an analogous formulation in 2D).

One then transmits, as the strain increment at the input of the solution of plasticity, the total strain increment, from which the creep strain increment computed by difference of the creep strains at the current and previous instants has been subtracted.

#### 4.4.3 Plastic strain increment

Computation of the plastic strain increment is carried out in the coupling solution module. Indeed, it is not stored in the internal variables.

The plastic strain increment is obtained simply by subtracting the creep strain increment and the elastic strain increment from the total strain increment.

## 7 APPENDIX: convergence of the coupling method

### 7.1 Notation and assumptions

In this paragraph and the following one, two non-linear, dissipative constitutive laws $\mathrm{L1}$ and $\mathrm{L2}$ associated with one and the same elasticity tensor $H$ will be considered. Integration of the law $\mathrm{Li}$ consists, at each Gauss point, on the one hand in computing the stress increment $\mathrm{Ds}$ and the anelastic strain increment $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{i}}$ as a function of a total strain increment $\Delta \varepsilon$ and of quantities at the beginning of the time step (known), and on the other hand in computing at the point $\Delta \varepsilon$ the contribution to the tangent operator ${(\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon })}_{\Delta \varepsilon }^{{L}_{i}}$ .

One can describe in a synthetic manner the «local incremental problem» constituted by the integration of the law ${L}_{i}$ by introducing the operator ${f}_{i}$ defined by:

$(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{i}}\end{array})={f}_{i}(\Delta \varepsilon )$

One can then define the constitutive law $L$ resulting from the coupling of $\mathrm{L1}$ and $\mathrm{L2}$ . Its anelastic strain ${\epsilon}_{a}$ is: ${\varepsilon}_{a}={\varepsilon}_{{a}_{1}}+{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{2}}$ and the elasticity relation associated with $L$ is written:

$\sigma =H(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{1}}-{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{2}})$

Integration of the law $L$ is carried out by the successive solutions of the following «local incremental problems» (laws ${L}_{1}$ and ${L}_{2}$ ) in a loop until convergence on $\Delta \sigma$ (the tangent operator ${(\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon })}_{\Delta \varepsilon }^{L}$ for the law $L$ is computed in paragraph 3):

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.19/100002000000061500000800D8B8E72AA8FD86C1.png)

The theoretical approach to this type of coupling comprises two parts:

- The study of the convergence of the loop above
- The study of the possibility of putting the global incremental problem (constituted by the structural analysis with the law $L$ ) in the form of a minimisation problem having a unique solution.

### 7.2 Writing of the global incremental problem in the form of a minimisation

**We assume that the two constitutive laws** ${L}_{1}$ **and** ${L}_{2}$ **fall within the framework of generalised standard materials** . In this case, the «local incremental problem» constituted by the integration of the law ${L}_{i}$ , described by the equality:

$(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{i}}\end{array})={f}_{i}(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{j}})\begin{array}{ccc}& & \end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}& & \end{array}({E}_{i})$

where $i\in \left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace$ and $j=3-i$ , can also be written in the form:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{i}}=\text{Arg}\underset{{e}_{i}}{\text{Min}}\left[\phi (\varepsilon +\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{j}},{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{i}}+{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{j}}+{e}_{i})+{G}_{i}({e}_{i})\right]({P}_{i})$

where $\varphi$ is the elastic strain energy:

$\varphi (\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}_{a})=\frac{1}{2}H(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{a}):(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{a})$

and where ${G}_{i}$ is the «complementary potential» of the law ${L}_{i}$ . This potential ${G}_{i}$ is defined below from ${f}_{i}$ , the stored part of the free energy and a function of the internal variables ${a}_{i}$ , and from the dissipation potential ${D}_{i}$ , a function of the anelastic strain rate ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{{a}_{i}}$ and of the rates of evolution of the internal variables ${\dot{\alpha}}_{i}$ . With an incremental formulation, one has:

${G}_{i}({e}_{i})=\underset{\Delta \alpha }{\text{Min}}\left[{\varphi}_{i}({\alpha}_{i}+\Delta \alpha )+{D}_{i}({e}_{i},\Delta \alpha )\right]$

The vector ${a}_{i}$ of the internal variables of the law ${L}_{i}$ at the beginning of the time step therefore enters as a parameter in the expression for ${G}_{i}$ . For each value of this parameter ${a}_{i}$ , the function ${G}_{i}$ is positive and convex.

*Note* : If there are no internal variables, one simply has ${G}_{i}={D}_{i}$

**It is furthermore assumed that one of the two complementary potentials,** ${G}_{1}$ **or** ${G}_{2}$ **, is strictly convex and coercive.**

Setting $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{1}}=\stackrel{ˉ}{x}$ , $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{a}_{2}}=\stackrel{ˉ}{y}$ and $F(x,y)=\varphi (\varepsilon +\Delta \varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{1}}+{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{2}}+x+y)$ , the system constituted by the equations $({P}_{1})$ and $({P}_{2})$ is equivalent to:

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\stackrel{ˉ}{x}=\text{Arg}\underset{x}{\text{Min}}\left[F(x,\stackrel{ˉ}{y})+{G}_{1}(x)\right]\\ \stackrel{ˉ}{y}=\text{Arg}\underset{y}{\text{Min}}\left[F(\stackrel{ˉ}{x},y)+{G}_{2}(y)\right]\end{array}$     (S)

${G}_{1}$ and ${G}_{2}$ being convex and $F$ being Gâteaux-differentiable and convex, one has, according to property $\mathrm{P7}$ of [4] (which follows from proposition 2.2 of chapter II of [2]):

$(S)\mathrm{\iff }(\stackrel{ˉ}{x},\stackrel{ˉ}{y})=\text{Arg}\underset{(x,y)}{\text{Min}}\left[F(x,y)+{G}_{1}(x)+{G}_{2}(y)\right]$

Finding an exact solution of the system constituted by the equations $({E}_{1})$ and $({E}_{2})$ is therefore equivalent to finding the minimum of a continuous, coercive and strictly convex function. According to proposition 1.2 of chapter II of [2], this minimum exists and is unique.

The local solution of the law $L$ resulting from the coupling of ${L}_{1}$ and ${L}_{2}$ is therefore equivalent to the minimisation problem above. Consequently, $L$ is a generalised standard constitutive law with j as elastic strain energy and $G$ as complementary potential, where $G$ is defined by:

$G({\dot{\varepsilon}}_{a})={G}_{1}({\dot{\varepsilon}}_{{a}_{1}})+{G}_{2}({\dot{\varepsilon}}_{{a}_{2}})$

with ${\varepsilon}_{a}={\varepsilon}_{{a}_{1}}+{\varepsilon}_{{a}_{2}}$ .

By proceeding in the same manner as in §3 of [5], one can therefore put the global incremental problem (constituted by the structural analysis with the law $L$ ) in the form of a minimisation problem having a unique solution.

### 7.3 Convergence of the algorithm

It must now be proved that the algorithm described above does indeed converge.

One denotes by ${z}_{n}=({x}_{n},{y}_{n})=(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{a}^{{n}_{1}},\Delta {\varepsilon}_{a}^{{n}_{2}})$ the result coming from iteration $n$ and one sets:

$K(z)=K(x,y)=F(x,y)+{G}_{1}(x)+{G}_{2}(y)$

${z}_{n+1}$ is computed as a function of ${z}_{n}$ in the following manner:

$\begin{array}{}{x}_{n+1}=\text{Arg}\underset{x}{\text{Min}}K(x,{y}_{n})\\ {y}_{n+1}=\text{Arg}\underset{y}{\text{Min}}K({x}_{n+1},y)\end{array}$

One denotes:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{z}=(\stackrel{ˉ}{x},\stackrel{ˉ}{y})=\text{Arg}\underset{(x,y)}{\text{Min}}K(x,y)$

In order to show the convergence of the sequence $({z}_{n})$ towards $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , the global convergence theorem of §6.5 of[3] will be used.

One considers $A$ the algorithm which, from a given ${z}_{0}$ , generates the sequence $({z}_{n})$ defined above:

${z}_{n+1}=A({z}_{n})$

The solution set $\Gamma$ is constituted by the singleton { $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ }.

*Let us verify the assumptions of this theorem:*

**H1: all the points** ${z}_{n}$ **are contained in a compact set** $S$ **.**

One has by construction:

$\forall n\in \mathrm{\mathbb{N}},K({z}_{n})\le K({z}_{0})$

Now, $K$ is coercive, hence $K(z)\underset{\mathrm{\parallel }z\mathrm{\parallel }\to +\infty }{\to }+\infty ` with :math:`{\mathrm{\parallel }z\mathrm{\parallel }}^{2}={x}_{\text{eq}}^{2}+{y}_{\text{eq}}^{2})$

Therefore:

$\forall R>0$ , $\exists M>0$ , $\forall z$ , $\mathrm{\parallel }z\mathrm{\parallel }>M\mathrm{\Rightarrow }K(z)>R$

$\forall R>0$ , $\exists M>0$ , $\forall z$ , $K(z)\le R\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\mathrm{\parallel }z\mathrm{\parallel }\le M$

Taking $R=K({z}_{0})$ , one therefore has:

$\forall n\in \mathbb{N},∣∣{z}_{n}∣∣\le M$

Therefore all the points ${z}_{n}$ are contained in a bounded set, hence in a compact set since the vector space is of finite dimension, and the assumption is therefore satisfied.

**H2. There exists a descent function** $Z$ **for** $\Gamma$ **and** $A$

$Z$ must be continuous and satisfy the following two properties:

> . if $z\mathrm{\ne }\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , then $Z(A(z))<Z(z)$
> . if $z=\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , then $Z(A(z))\le Z(z)$

One takes $Z=K$

$K$ is indeed continuous and one has:

> . $K(A(\stackrel{ˉ}{z}))=K(\stackrel{ˉ}{z})$
> . If $z\mathrm{\ne }\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , then $A(z)=z’=(x’,y’)$ such that:

> $\begin{array}{c}x'=\text{Arg}\underset{x}{\text{Min}}K(x,y)\\ y'=\text{Arg}\underset{y}{\text{Min}}K(x\text{',}y)\end{array}$

If one has $x’\mathrm{\ne }x$ , then, $K$ being continuous, coercive and strictly convex, the minimum $x’$ is unique and one has

$K(x’,y)<K(x,y)$ . Now, one has $K(x’,y’)\le K(x’,y)$ hence $K(z’)<K(z)$ .

If one has $x’=x$ , then one has $y’\mathrm{\ne }y$ (otherwise, one would have $z=\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ ).

$K$ being continuous, coercive and strictly convex, the minimum $y’$ is unique and one has:

$K(x’,y’)<K(x’,y)\mathit{et}K(x’,y)=K(x,y)$ hence $K(x’,y’)<K(x,y)$

One therefore has in both cases:

$K(A(z))<K(z)$

There therefore does indeed exist a descent function for $\Gamma$ and $A$ . The assumption is satisfied.

**H3. The algorithm** $A$ **is closed for every** $z$ **not belonging to** $\Gamma$

In order to verify this third assumption, the same method as that used in §7.8 of [3] to show the global convergence of a cyclic coordinate descent algorithm (cyclic coordinate descent algorithm) will be adopted.

Consider the following two algorithms:

- ${C}^{1}$ which associates with $(x,y)$ the set of points $(X,y)$ with $X$ arbitrary
- ${C}^{2}$ which associates with $(x,y)$ the set of points $(x,Y)$ with $Y$ arbitrary

One furthermore considers $M$ the minimisation algorithm on each of these two sets (which associates with an element of the set the point realising the minimum over the set). The algorithm $A$ is then the composition of 4 algorithms:

$A=M{C}^{2}M{C}^{1}$

The algorithms ${C}^{1}$ and ${C}^{2}$ are continuous and $M$ is closed. Since all the points are contained in a compact set $S$ (cf the first assumption), one deduces from it, according to corollary 1 of §6.5 of [3], that $A$ is closed, and the assumption is therefore satisfied.

The three assumptions of the global convergence theorem being verified, the following result has therefore been shown:

> **The limit of every convergent subsequence of** $({z}_{n})$ **is** $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ **.**         $(P)$

Now, it has also been shown that all the points ${z}_{n}$ are contained in a compact set $S$ . Suppose that $({z}_{n})$ does not converge towards $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , then one has:

$\exists \varepsilon >0,\forall N\in \mathrm{\mathbb{N}},\exists n=\psi (N)>N,\mathrm{\parallel }{z}_{n}-\stackrel{ˉ}{z}\mathrm{\parallel }>\varepsilon$

One can construct a subsequence $({z}_{b(n)})$ defined by:

$b(0)=0$

$\forall n\in \mathrm{\mathbb{N}},\beta (n+1)=\psi (\beta (n))$

One then has: $\forall n\in \mathrm{\mathbb{N}},∣∣{z}_{\beta (n)}-\stackrel{ˉ}{z}∣∣\le M$

Now, the set $(S–B(\stackrel{ˉ}{z},e))$ is compact, hence the sequence $({z}_{b(n)})$ admits a convergent subsequence which, according to property $(P)$ above, converges towards $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ , which is absurd.

The desired result is therefore obtained:

**The sequence** $({z}_{n})$ **converges towards** $\stackrel{ˉ}{z}$ **.**
