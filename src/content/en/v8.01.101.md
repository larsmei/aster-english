# v8.01.101 FDLV101 - Two cylinders separated by an incompressible fluid

**Summary:**

This fluid field test (fluid coupling/structure) validates the calculation of added mass matrix in the case of several structures immersed in the same fluid.

The coupled modes of the two structures are determined by modal analysis because of the mass of fluid that separates them. Plane Modelling (thermal for the fluid, and flat deformation for the cylinders) is adopted.

The coupled modes of the system are found at less than $0.1\text{\%}$ of the analytical result.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

**Analytical calculus:**

It is assumed that the movements of the cylinders and the fluid are essentially plane. The longitudinal effects will be neglected in front of the transverse effects. The problem is two-dimensional. Given the symmetry, the reference used is a cylindrical $(r,\theta )$ marker linked to the central cylinder (see figure above). In this coordinate system and with this particular geometry, the normal $\frac{\partial .}{\partial n}$ derivative is equal to the $\frac{\partial .}{\partial r}$ derivative compared to $r$.

In this part, the variable $p$ refers to the hydrodynamic pressure field in the fluid created by the clean vibrations of the structures, ${X}_{1/2}$ refers to the eigenmodes of the $1$ or $2$ cylinder, respectively.

The eigenmodes of $({\Gamma}_{1})$ and $({\Gamma}_{2})$ border hulls in the absence of fluid are of the shape ($n$ refers to the order of mode):

${X}_{\mathrm{1n}}(r)=\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \text{ou}\sinn\theta \\ 0\end{array}$ et ${X}_{\mathrm{2n}}(r)=\lbrace \begin{array}{}0\\ \cosn\theta \text{ou}\sinn\theta \end{array}$

$\theta$ is the azimutal angle. These modes are uncoupled of course. The first component corresponds to the normal displacement of the inner shell, the second to the outer shell. In the fluid volume, therefore, there are two problems to solve:

> $\Delta {p}_{\mathrm{1n}}=0$ ${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{1n}}}{\partial n})}_{{\Gamma}_{1}}=-{\rho}_{f}\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}$ ${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{1n}}}{\partial n})}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}=0$             éq 2.1-1

et:

> $\Delta {p}_{\mathrm{2n}}=0$ ${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{2n}}}{\partial n})}_{{\Gamma}_{1}}=0$ ${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{2n}}}{\partial n})}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}=-{\rho}_{f}\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}$             éq 2.1-2

The ${p}_{\mathrm{1n}}$ field corresponds to the pressure field generated in the fluid if the ${\Gamma}_{1}$ central shell vibrates alone, the ${p}_{\mathrm{2n}}$ field is created by the ${\Gamma}_{2}$ outer shell if it vibrates alone. The linearity of the Laplace equation allows each problem to be solved independently and then superimposed to find the total pressure field.

The solution to the problem [eq2.1-1-o] is, in polar coordinates, of the [bib1] type:

${p}_{\mathrm{1n}}(r,\theta )=\left\lbrace A{r}^{n}+B{(\frac{1}{r})}^{n}\right\rbrace \left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}\right\rbrace$

We must have $n\ne 0$, because otherwise we have the non-conservation of fluid volume.

$A$ and $B$ constants are determined by the boundary conditions:

${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{1n}}}{\partial n})}_{{R}_{1}}=-{\rho}_{f}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}\right\rbrace$ et ${(\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathrm{1n}}}{\partial n})}_{{R}_{2}}=0$

The pressure field for each of the two problems is then found to be:

${p}_{\mathrm{1n}}(r,\theta )=\frac{{\rho}_{f}{R}_{1}}{n}\frac{{(r/{R}_{1})}^{n}+{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{n}{({R}_{2}/r)}^{n}}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}-1}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}\right\rbrace$

et:

${p}_{\mathrm{2n}}(r,\theta )=\frac{{\rho}_{f}{R}_{2}}{n}\frac{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{n}{(r/{R}_{1})}^{n}+{({R}_{2}/r)}^{n}}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}-1}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}\right\rbrace$

The modal mass added coefficients ${m}_{\mathrm{ijnm}}^{A}$ are calculated from the following formula [R4.07.03](doc:r4.07.03) if $i=1$ or $2$ , $j=1$ or $2$ , $(n,m)$ belongs to ${i}^{2}$ .

${m}_{\mathrm{ijnm}}^{A}={\int}_{{\Gamma}_{j}}{p}_{\mathrm{jn}}{X}_{\text{im}}(r).n({\Gamma}_{j})d{\Gamma}_{j}$

Indexation is a little more complex here than in the [R4.07.03](doc:r4.07.03) formula: indices i and j refer to hulls

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.101/Object_38.svg)

et

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.101/Object_39.svg)

, and $m$ and $n$ indices are associated with the hull modes. It is noted that there are coupling modes of the various hulls, external and internal.

On the one hand, the fluid does not couple different $n$ index modes because the ${\int}_{\Gamma}\cosn\theta \cosm\theta d\Gamma$ integrals are canceled; on the other hand, the fluid does not couple $\cosn\theta$ and $\sinn\theta$ modes because ${\int}_{(\Gamma )}\cosn\theta \sinn\theta d\Gamma =0$ . The only existing coupling is a coupling between the two hulls for the same modes.

Each $n$ mode is combined with a symmetrical 4 order matrix. A submatrix corresponding to the projection on $n$ mode is written:

${M}_{1}^{A}=(\begin{array}{cc}{m}_{\mathrm{11nn}}^{A}& {m}_{\mathrm{12nn}}^{A}\\ {m}_{\mathrm{21nn}}^{A}& {m}_{\mathrm{22nn}}^{A}\end{array})$

The global matrix is written: $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{1}^{A}& 0\\ 0& {M}_{2}^{A}\end{array}\right]$ with ${M}_{1}^{A}={M}_{2}^{A}$

with ${m}_{\mathrm{11nn}}^{A}=L{R}_{1}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{p}_{1}({R}_{1,}\theta )\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cosn\theta \\ \text{ou}\\ \sinn\theta \end{array}\right\rbrace d\theta$

Either:

${m}_{\mathrm{11nn}}^{A}=\frac{\pi}{n}{\rho}_{f}{R}_{1}^{2}L\frac{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}+1}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}-1}$                             éq 2.1-3

We will get:

${m}_{\mathrm{22nn}}^{A}=\frac{\pi}{n}{\rho}_{f}{R}_{2}^{2}L\frac{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}+1}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}-1}$                             éq 2.1-4

et:

> ${m}_{\mathrm{21nn}}^{A}={m}_{\mathrm{12nn}}^{A}=-\frac{\pi}{n}{\rho}_{f}{R}_{1}{R}_{2}L\frac{2({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{\mathrm{2n}}-1}$                 éq 2.1-5

$L$ is the height of the cylinder shells in the longitudinal direction.

In our case, the $n=1$ modes of order of the hulls are considered only: they correspond respectively to the modes of translation of each of the hulls along an axis passing through the centre of the central tube: one takes those corresponding to the $\mathrm{Ox}$ axis arbitrarily: the coefficients of added linear mass are written:

${m}_{11}^{A}=\pi {\rho}_{f}{R}_{1}^{2}\frac{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{2}+1}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{2}-1}$

${m}_{22}^{A}=\pi {\rho}_{f}{R}_{2}^{2}\frac{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{2}+1}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{2}-1}$

${m}_{21}^{A}={m}_{12}^{A}=-\pi {\rho}_{f}{R}_{1}{R}_{2}\frac{2({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}{{({R}_{2}/{R}_{1})}^{2}-1}$

The equation of the generalized movement of the two coupled hulls is written:

$(\begin{array}{cc}{m}_{1}& 0\\ 0& {m}_{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{}{\ddot{x}}_{1}\\ {\ddot{x}}_{2}\end{array})+(\begin{array}{cc}{k}_{1}& 0\\ 0& {k}_{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{}{x}_{1}\\ {x}_{2}\end{array})=-(\begin{array}{cc}{m}_{11}& {m}_{12}\\ {m}_{12}& {m}_{22}\end{array})(\begin{array}{}{\ddot{x}}_{1}\\ {\ddot{x}}_{2}\end{array})$

The pulses of the coupled system are given by the degree 4 equation:

$\det\left[(\begin{array}{cc}{m}_{1}+{m}_{11}& {m}_{12}\\ {m}_{12}& {m}_{2}+{m}_{22}\end{array}){\Omega}^{2}-(\begin{array}{cc}{k}_{1}& 0\\ 0& {k}_{2}\end{array})\right]=0$

**Digital application:**

${m}_{11}=33060\mathrm{kg}/m$

${m}_{22}=40004\mathrm{kg}/m$

${m}_{12}=–36200\mathrm{kg}/m$
