# v7.22.132 HSNV132 - X-FEM thermal elasticity

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to validate the consideration of a thermal load for a cracking calculation by the X-FEM [bib1] method on an academic case $\mathrm{2D}/\mathrm{3D}$.

This test involves a square plate with a straight crack opening, clamped on the lower edge, and subjected to a horizontal temperature gradient. This load opens the crack. The displacement for the node end at the top right, for FEM and X-FEM, is compared. This test includes a call to THER_LINEAIRE, then MECA_STATIQUE.

Four modellings are considered:

- $A$ Modelling: $\text{FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ (as reference),
- $B$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$, crack in the middle of elements,
- $C$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{3D}$ (the following $z$ moves are blocked), cracking in the middle of elements,
- $D$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{3D}$ , real $\mathrm{3d}$ Modelling, without putting $\mathit{DZ}=0$ , to validate the $G$ calculation to $\mathrm{3D}$ .
- $E$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{3D}$ , true $\mathrm{3d}$ Modelling, without putting $\mathrm{DZ}=0$ , to validate $G$ calculation to $\mathrm{3D}$ . Similar to $D$ Modelling except that the top and bottom surfaces were clamped to induce significant stresses.

There is a difference less than $\text{1\%}$ for moving, and a difference of $\text{3,5\%}$ on ${K}_{I}$ and $\text{1,4 \%}$ on ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The $\mathrm{2d}$ structure is a unit square ($\mathrm{LX}=1m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=1m$ ), with a straight crack opening to the right, located at mid-height. [ Figure 1.1-1 ]. The left line is called the $x=0$ line , the right line is called the $x=\mathrm{LX}$ line and the bottom line is called the $y=0$ line .

![Figure](/figures/v7.22.132/10000000000001B80000018CEBBE8AB3F1FBC801.png)

**Figure** 1.1-1 **: geometry of cracked square plate**

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus:        $E=205000\mathit{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio:        $\nu =0.3$

Thermal expansion coefficient: $\alpha =1,282{10}^{-5}$

Thermal conductivity: $\lambda =1{\mathit{W.m}}^{\text{-1}}.{K}^{\text{-1}}$

Volumetric heating capacity: $\rho {C}_{p}=0\mathit{J.m}-{\mathrm{3.K}}^{-1}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 For the thermal part

The thermal loading consists of applying an imposed temperature $T=20°C$ to the nodes of the left line and an imposed temperature $T=220°C$ to the nodes of the right line. The temperature gradient is uniform and opens the crack in mixed mode.

#### 1.3.2 For the mechanical part

The nodes of the lower line is clamped.

## 3 Modelization B: dimension 2 non-grinded crack

In this Modelling, the $\mathrm{2D}$ structure is considered. The extended elements (X-FEM) method is used.

This Modelling involves two command files (hsnv132b.comm and hsnv132b.com1). In each file, the same problem is strictly modelled, but with a different strategy (only for validation purposes). The quantities tested, as well as the non-regression values, are identical from one file to another.

* file hsnv132b.comm:*

Elements X-FEM only intervene at the mechanical calculation level to represent the discontinuity of the displacement through the crack. For the thermal part, the temperature is calculated on a healthy thermal model, the temperature is actually continuous through the crack lips.

*file hsnv132b.com1:*

Elements X-FEM is used in the thermal calculation and in the mechanical calculation. For the thermal part, a continuous temperature is imposed through the interface (via AFFE_CHAR_THER / ECHANGE_PAROI / TEMP_CONTINUE = 'OUI'). Therefore, the same problem is modelled as in the previous control file, but with a different discretization.

### 3.1 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular mesh composed of $101\times 101$ QUAD4, respectively, along the $x$ , $y$ axes. The crack is not meshed.

## 4 Modelization C: non-mesh crack in false 3D

In this Modelling, the $\mathrm{3d}$ structure is considered, but all degrees of freedom following $z$ (not just displacements) are zeroed to the $\mathrm{2d}$ case. The extended elements finite ($\text{X-FEM}$) method is used.

This Modelling involves two command files (hsnv132c.comm and hsnv132c.com1). In each file, the same problem is strictly modelled, but with a different strategy (only for validation purposes). The quantities tested, as well as the non-regression values, are identical from one file to another.

* file hsnv132c.comm:*

Elements X-FEM only intervene at the mechanical calculation level to represent the discontinuity of the displacement through the crack. For the thermal part, the temperature is calculated on a healthy thermal model, the temperature is actually continuous through the crack lips.

*file hsnv132c.com1:*

Elements X-FEM is used in the thermal calculation and in the mechanical calculation. For the thermal part, a continuous temperature is imposed through the interface (via AFFE_CHAR_THER / ECHANGE_PAROI / TEMP_CONTINUE = 'OUI'). Therefore, the same problem is modelled as in the previous control file, but with a different discretization.

### 4.1 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular mesh composed of $11\times 11\times 1$ HEXA8, respectively, along the $x$ , $y$ and $z$ axes. The crack is not meshed.

### 4.2 Boundary conditions and loads

To get back to the $\mathrm{2D}$ case, it is necessary to block all the following degrees of freedom $z$.

Blocking the movements following $z$ is not enough, the enriched degrees of freedom have a high importance. Therefore, you have to impose $\mathrm{DZ}=0$ on all nodes, and also impose $\mathrm{H1Z}=0$ on the nodes enriched by Heaviside and $\mathrm{E1Z}=\mathrm{E2Z}=\mathrm{E3Z}=\mathrm{E4Z}=0$ on all nodes enriched by asymptotic functions.

### 4.3 Tested quantities and results

The $X$ and $Y$ displacement values of the point end ‘PTEXTR' coordinates are tested.

The $G$ energy return rate given by CALC_G_XFEM is not tested, nor is the ${K}_{I}$ stress factor given by ${K}_{I}$ POST_K1_K2_K3 because forcing the $Z$ displacements is not in accordance with the $\mathrm{2D}$ energy view point.

Reference values are those obtained by Modelling A.

## 7 Summary of results

The objectives of this test are achieved:

- validate the temperature consideration on the mechanical calculation with $\text{X-FEM}$ on a simple case,
- validate the calculation of stress intensity factors for elements $\text{X-FEM}$ , including thermal-related terms.
