# v2.02.113 SDLL113 - Transitional dynamic substructure: single-tension beam

**Summary:**

The structure is a single-trail ring section beam, clamped on one side, and subjected to a Heaviside-type force at its end. Its transient dynamic response is calculated by substructure.

The beam is modelled by Timoshenko beam-type elements (linear model). Two modellings are offered depending on whether the beam is damped or not. The damping tested is RAYLEIGH type (proportional damping).

The reference results are derived from a direct transient calculation by modal recombination without sub-structure. This test therefore allows the validation of the tools for calculating transient response by sub-structure, in the linear case.

A third model dealing with exactly the same amortized problem as the second one was added to address a case of using a dynamic condensation method where the resolution of the dynamic problem on a physical basis ssseoper on a mixed model consisting of the macro-element of the right part of the structure and a reduced physical model to its left part. The results are exactly the same as those of the second model as expected.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

There is a detailed analytical solution in the reference [bib1].

The following ratings are adopted:

| $E$ | : | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\rho$ | : | density |
| $L$ | : | length of bar |
| $A$ | : | section of the bar |
| $N$ | : | normal effort directed to $X$ axis |
| $\alpha ,\beta$ | : | Rayleigh depreciation coefficients |

The following are also proposed:

${\omega}_{n}=(\mathrm{2n}-1)\frac{\pi}{2}$ où $n=1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$

${\varepsilon}_{n}=\frac{1}{2}(\alpha {\omega}_{n}+\beta /{\omega}_{n})$

The displacement in any point $M(x)$ is given by:

$u(x,t)=\frac{\mathrm{Nx}}{\mathrm{EA}}+\frac{\mathrm{8NL}}{{\pi}^{2}\mathrm{EA}}\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\Sigma}}{(-1)}^{n}\frac{{e}^{-{\omega}_{n}{\varepsilon}_{n}t}}{{(\mathrm{2n}-1)}^{2}}\left\lbrace \cos(\sqrt{1-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}}{\omega}_{n}t)+\frac{{\varepsilon}_{n}}{\sqrt{1-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}}}\sin(\sqrt{1-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{2}}{\omega}_{n}t)\right\rbrace$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The beam is cut into two parts of equal size. Each substructure is meshed into segments to which elements of the POU_D_T type are assigned.

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.113/1000020000000615000008006BB4092584663A5C.png)

The structure is studied using the transitional substructure method with "Craig-Bampton" interfaces (blocked interfaces).

The modal base used is composed of 4 modes for the left-hand substructure, 5 modes for the right-hand substructure, plus the forced modes associated with the degrees of freedom interface (number of 2).

### 3.5 Remarks

It is surprising that the adopted reference corresponds to the complete beam modeled by 10 elements and not to the analytical solution. It turns out that the development of the serial solution of eigenmodes converges very slowly: the modal solution is here very far from the theoretical solution. The relevant comparison is therefore that chosen.

The modal recombination calculation is performed on the complete modal basis of the structure (11 modes) taking into account the discretization adopted. Similarly, the dimension of the projection base used for dynamic substructure calculation is 11 (left substructure: 4 eigenmodes + 1 constrained mode; right substructure: 5 eigenmodes + 1 constrained mode). It is therefore normal to obtain an excellent agreement between the Modelling of the complete beam and that of the beam divided into two substructures.

### 4.5 Remarks

It is surprising that the reference adopted corresponds to the complete beam modeled by 10 elements and not the analytical solution. The differences important between numerical and theoretical solutions is due to the reduced number of d-elements. Using 50 elements instead of 10 would have allowed to approach theoretical acceleration to within 1%. Apart from this, it can be seen that the use of a substructure method provides the same results as the complete beam.

The modal superposition calculation is performed on the complete modal basis of the structure (11 modes). Similarly, the projection base dimension used for dynamic substructure calculation is 11 (left substructure: 4 eigenmodes + 1 constrained mode; right substructure: 5 eigenmodes + 1 constrained mode). It is normal to obtain an excellent agreement between the Modelling of the complete beam and its Modelling in two substructures.

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

As in the previous model (modelization B), the structure is amortized with the same proportional damping and the same Rayleigh coefficients (see [§ 5.1]).

The difference lies in the use of a dynamic condensation method of the right part of the structure (this time including 6 elements) which is then represented by a dynamic substructure macro-element. The resolution of the dynamic problem is based on a mixed model consisting of the macro-element of the right part of the structure where applied force is exercised and a reduced physical model to the left part comprising 4 elements where there is l-clamped support.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh used is the initial complete mesh (see §1.1) obtained by duplication and then assembly of the mesh model A, which gives 10 elements and 11 nodes.

### 5.3 Tested Features

The dynamic computational features with condensation of part of the structure and the restitution in the physical space are tested.

## 6 Summary of the results

Both unamortized and amortized results obtained by the complete model and by substructure are not significant in the case of differences. Therefore, the linear transition calculation operators by substructure are validated.

In the amortized case, the agreement between digital and analytical solutions would have been better by taking more d-elements (50 instead of 10 for example).
