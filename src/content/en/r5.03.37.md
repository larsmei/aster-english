# r5.03.37 Homogenisation of periodic heterogeneous structures

**Summary:**

The assumptions and the methods used in the command [CALC_MATE_HOMO](doc:u4.43.07) are set out; this command computes the homogenised linear thermal and thermoelastic properties for periodic heterogeneous structures with a rectangular pitch, with basic cells possessing symmetries with respect to orthogonal planes in Cartesian space. The case with hexagonal symmetry can also be treated. The framework adopted is that of the three-dimensional homogenisation of heterogeneous solids and also that of homogenisation in the theory of heterogeneous plates.

A table of the homogenised linear thermal and thermoelastic coefficients is obtained, which may be used to define equivalent orthotropic materials, which may be parameterised by command variables, such as temperature or irradiation.

All the thermal and thermoelastic corrector fields are also produced, so as to allow a subsequent use with a view to reconstructing the complete solution localised at the microscopic scale from the macroscopic solution.

## 1 Introduction

Continuous media or heterogeneous structures are considered, whose geometric and/or physical properties vary “rapidly” and periodically in space with respect to the scale of the loadings applied, here with a rectangular pitch. They are therefore characterised by a three-dimensional “basic cell”, also called a “representative elementary volume”, possessing
in the framework studied here geometric and material symmetries with respect to orthogonal planes in Cartesian space. One is interested in the “macroscopic” behaviour of these structures, in order to determine the overall response, but one also wishes to return to the “microscopic” scale on a “basic cell”, in order to access quantities of interest at that scale.

These may be multi-perforated plates, fibre composites, made up of a repetitive pattern...

The objective is to establish the homogenised properties in three-dimensional linear thermal analysis and, depending on the type of heterogeneous structure, cf. , in three-dimensional linear thermoelasticity, cf. , or in linear elasticity of a Love–Kirchhoff plate in bending–membrane, cf. . The “method of averages” is used for this,
justified by an asymptotic expansion, cf. .

In three-dimensional linear thermal analysis, it is accepted that sufficiently slow transients may be considered for the heterogeneous structure studied, so as to be able to separate the spatial scales as regards the effect of the time derivative of the temperature on the evolution of the macroscopic volumetric enthalpy. The effect of a thermal shock cannot therefore be treated in this way.
Indeed, in one-dimensional stationary thermal analysis, under the action of a temperature imposed on the boundary ${x=0}$ at ${t=0}$ the solution is written:

this solution brings out the thermal diffusivity coefficient $\frac{K_{cond}}{\rho_{cp}}$ , which has the dimension of the square of a distance divided by a time.
Take for example an ordinary steel of conductivity $K_{cond} = 35 \frac{W}{m \cdot K}$ and heat capacity $\rho_{cp} = 3.5 \frac{MJ}{m^3 \cdot K}$, with a length of $3~cm$; the time derivative of the temperature becomes very small ($0.01~\frac{°C}{s}$) beyond a duration of $90~s$. This means that taking into account a transient whose duration is shorter will produce a solution that cannot be represented correctly at the homogenised scale for a basic cell of size $3~cm$.

In thermoelasticity, the following situations are distinguished:

- three-dimensional heterogeneous solids, periodic in the three directions of Cartesian space;
- slender three-dimensional heterogeneous solids, of plane or curved geometry: plate or shell, periodic in the two directions of the tangent plane. The standard case is adopted, cf.  and r5.03.37-fig-introduction, where the periodicity pitch is of the same order of magnitude as the thickness of the plate or of the shell, and where this periodicity pitch is considered to be small compared with the mean curvature of the shell, so as to admit a Cartesian metric in the basic cell.

## 2 Assumptions

The assumptions adopted in the treatment proposed by the command [CALC_MATE_HOMO](doc:u4.43.07) are presented. The three-dimensional heterogeneous solid studied may be:

- of arbitrary dimensions in the three spatial directions: a three-dimensional homogenisation is then carried out, cf. , .
- slender, with one dimension small compared with the other two, of thin-plate or thin-shell type: a homogenisation is then carried out within the framework of the theory of heterogeneous plates, in Love–Kirchhoff kinematics, in bending–membrane, cf. , .

The three-dimensional “basic cell”, or also “representative elementary volume”, corresponds to a periodicity pattern with rectangular pitch, constituting the geometry of the heterogeneous solid studied:

- in the three spatial directions, for a three-dimensional homogenisation. The mesh of the three-dimensional basic cell is made up of three-dimensional mesh cells, on which isoparametric solid finite elements P1 or P2 will be assigned. Since the case is restricted to symmetries with respect to the planes of the spatial reference frame, the mesh is limited to the octant of the basic cell $(X>0,Y>0,Z>0)$ ;
- in two spatial directions, and over the half-thickness of the plate, along the third spatial direction ($Z$), for a homogenisation of plate or shell type. The mesh of the basic cell is made up of three-dimensional mesh cells, on which isoparametric solid finite elements P1 or P2 will be assigned. The dimension of the basic cell along the third spatial direction is used to define its thickness. It is however possible, in the case of a basic cell of the heterogeneous plate or shell, to treat a situation of a free boundary along the third spatial direction whose thickness is not plane: for this, a fictitious material will be added, with physical properties negligible compared with those of the other parts of the basic cell, in order to “fill” these voids so as to obtain a plane boundary. Since the case is restricted to symmetries with respect to the planes of the spatial reference frame, the mesh is limited to the octant of the basic cell $(X>0,Y>0,Z>0)$ .

Cases of periodic type with “hexagonal pitch” can also be treated by defining a periodic and symmetric “sub-cell” with square pitch.

The basic cell, denoted $C$, is therefore bounded by plane boundaries, see r5.03.37-fig-celluleBase. It is made up of subdomains each carrying a given material and possibly one or more unmeshed cavities: see r5.03.37-fig-reseauPeriodique, which may intersect the boundaries of the basic cell.

The various materials constituting the basic cell are defined by characteristics of density, of isotropic thermal conduction, of heat capacity and of isotropic linear thermoelastic characteristics, which are defined as functions of command variables: temperature, or irradiation.

The homogenised constitutive model produced will be, depending on the case:

- homogenised three-dimensional non-linear thermal,
- homogenised three-dimensional orthotropic linear thermoelasticity,
- homogenised linear thermoelasticity in bending–membrane of a Love–Kirchhoff plate, without bending–membrane coupling because of the symmetry with respect to the mid-surface.
- homogenised linear thermoelasticity in bending–membrane of a Mindlin plate with transverse shear, without bending–membrane coupling because of the symmetry with respect to the mid-surface.
- homogenised linear thermoelasticity in bending–membrane of a Touratier plate with transverse shear, without bending–membrane coupling because of the symmetry with respect to the mid-surface.

## 3 Methods

### 3.1 Homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermal analysis

For homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermal analysis, three thermal corrector fields ${\zeta}^{i}$ are computed, each associated with three unit temperature gradients in each of the three spatial directions, homogeneous in the volume of the three-dimensional basic cell $C$.

Since the mesh of the basic cell is restricted to the octant $(X>0,Y>0,Z>0)$, the conditions of material and geometric symmetry and of periodicity impose the following Dirichlet conditions:

| Corrector | Dirichlet conditions | Wall considered | Imposed temperature gradient $g$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\zeta}^{1}$ | $T=0$ | ${x}_{\min}$ and ${x}_{\max}$ | `PRE_GRAD_TEMP=_F(FLUX_X=-1)` |
| ${\zeta}^{2}$ | $T=0$ | ${y}_{\min}$ and ${y}_{\max}$ | `PRE_GRAD_TEMP=_F(FLUX_Y=-1)` |
| ${\zeta}^{3}$ | $T=0$ | ${z}_{\min}$ and ${z}_{\max}$ | `PRE_GRAD_TEMP=_F(FLUX_Z=-1)` |

The three elementary heat-conduction problems on the basic cell $C$, which provide the three corrector fields, are written in variational form:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\text{Trouver }{{\zeta}}^{i}(x,y,z)\in {V}_{\text{pér}} \text{ tel que :}\hfill \\
{\int}_{C}{\tensTwo{K}}\cdot{\zeta}_{,j}^{i}\,{v}_{,j}\,{dV}
=
-{\int}_{C}{\tensTwo{K}}\cdot{g}_{j}^{i}\,{v}_{,j}\,{dV} \qquad \forall v\in {V}_{\text{pér}}
\end{array}
$$

the space of admissible periodic fields ${V}_{\text{pér}}$ being restricted here to those satisfying the Dirichlet conditions reported in the table above.

These terms correspond to the “rule of mixtures”, from which the contribution of the correctors is subtracted in order to obtain the components of the homogenised conduction tensor.

$$

### 3.3 Homogenisation in linear elasticity of a Love–Kirchhoff plate in bending–membrane

- three elastic corrector fields, each associated with the three components of the symmetric second-order tensor of unit *membrane* strain, homogeneous in the volume of the three-dimensional basic cell,
- three elastic corrector fields, each associated with the three components of the symmetric second-order tensor of unit *bending* strain, homogeneous in the volume of the three-dimensional basic cell,

### 3.4 Homogenisation in linear elasticity of a plate with transverse shear taken into account

When transverse shear is taken into account, an additional condition is imposed for the computation of the correctors: the mean displacement of the upper surface must be equal to that of the lower surface, cf. .

Two additional correctors are computed:

| Corrector | Dirichlet conditions | Wall considered | Imposed strain |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\chi}_{31}^{ct}$ | ${U}_{y}=0$ | ${y}_{\min}$ and ${y}_{\max}$ | `PRE_EPSI=_F(EPXZ=-0.5*f(Z))` |
| ${U}_{z}=0$ | ${x}_{\min}$ and ${x}_{\max}$ |  |  |
| ${\chi}_{23}^{ct}$ | ${U}_{x}=0$ | ${x}_{\min}$ and ${x}_{\max}$ | `PRE_EPSI=_F(EPYZ=-0.5*f(Z))` |
| ${U}_{z}=0$ | ${y}_{\min}$ and ${y}_{\max}$ |  |  |

Within the framework of a Mindlin kinematics, a uniform mean distortion, constant throughout the section $f(Z)=1$, is considered, although this assumption may produce a response that is too stiff in transverse shear. In the article by Touratier, cf. , a kinematics is proposed with a transverse distortion that is non-uniform in the section $f(Z)=\frac{\pi}{2} \cdot cos(\frac{\pi \cdot Z}{h})$ with $h$ the thickness of the plate, which makes it possible to approach a plate theory of Reissner type, in which the transverse-shear energy is controlled in stress.

The two approaches mentioned, of Mindlin and of Touratier, are strain-based approaches, and they do not ensure that the transverse shear is zero at the wall.

## 4 Results

The command produces the set of corrector fields, solutions of the linear thermal problems and then of the linear elasticity problems carried out on the basic cell.

The coefficients homogenised in linear thermal analysis and in linear thermoelasticity are given along the axes of the Cartesian reference frame of the mesh of the basic cell.

The correspondences of directions are the following:

### 4.1 Homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermal analysis

For homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermal analysis, the homogenised linear thermal characteristics are computed with the three corrector fields:

- of orthotropic thermal conduction: `LAMBDA_L`, `LAMBDA_T`, `LAMBDA_N`,
- of equivalent volumetric heat capacity: `RHO_CP`.

### 4.2 Homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermoelasticity

For homogenisation in three-dimensional linear thermoelasticity, the homogenised linear thermoelastic characteristics are computed with the seven corrector fields:

- of orthotropic elasticity (9 independent coefficients that define the elastic compliances): `E_L`, `E_T`, `E_N`, `NU_LT`, `NU_LN`, `NU_TN`, `G_LT`, `G_LN`, `G_TN`,
- of orthotropic elasticity (9 independent coefficients of the elastic stiffness tensor): `A1111`, `A2222`, `A3333`, `A1122`, `A1133`, `A2233`, `A1212`, `A2323`, `A3131`,
- of orthotropic thermal expansion: `ALPHA_L`, `ALPHA_T`, `ALPHA_N`
- of equivalent density: `RHO`.

Finally a scalar `ISOTRANS` is computed, which makes it possible to make sure, if it is zero, of the case where a transverse isotropy is obtained (for example in the case of hexagonal symmetry of the basic cell), in which there are then only 5 independent elastic coefficients.

### 4.3 Homogenisation in linear elasticity of a Love–Kirchhoff plate in bending–membrane

For homogenisation in linear elasticity of a Love–Kirchhoff plate in bending–membrane, the homogenised linear thermoelastic characteristics in bending–membrane are computed with the seven corrector fields:

- of orthotropic elasticity in membrane (4 independent coefficients that define the elastic stiffnesses): `MEMB_L`, `MEMB_T`, `MEMB_LT`, `MEMB_G_LT`,
- of orthotropic elasticity in bending (4 independent coefficients that define the elastic stiffnesses): `FLEX_L`, `FLEX_T`, `FLEX_LT`, `FLEX_G_LT`,
- of orthotropic elasticity in transverse shear (2 independent coefficients that define the elastic stiffnesses): `CISA_L`, `CISA_T`,
- of thermal expansion in membrane: `ALPHA`,
- of equivalent density: `RHO`.

### 5.1 Stratified elastic plate

A material stratified in 3 layers of different thicknesses of isotropic materials is considered. In particular, the thicknesses of the successive layers are denoted: ${e}_{K}={z}_{K}-{z}_{K-1}$, see r5.03.37-fig-stratifie. The layers are assumed to be symmetric with respect to the mid-surface, so as to cancel the bending–membrane coupling.

In this case, the basic cell has an arbitrary periodicity in the directions of the tangent plane $(X,Y)$. Consequently, the membrane corrector fields ${\chi}_{11}^{m}$, ${\chi}_{22}^{m}$ depend in a piecewise affine manner on the position $Z$ through the thickness of the plate, whereas the membrane corrector ${\chi}_{12}^{m}$ associated with the distortion is zero everywhere. And the bending corrector fields ${\chi}_{11}^{f}$, ${\chi}_{22}^{f}$ are piecewise parabolic in $Z$ through the thickness of the plate, whereas the bending corrector ${\chi}_{12}^{f}$ is zero everywhere.
