# v2.02.141 SDLL141 - Eigenfrequencies of a beam alone, subject to gyroscopic effect.

**Summary:**

This problem consists in searching for the vibration frequencies of a beam supported at each end, on infinitely rigid supports. The beam is full, circular cross section and subjected to a constant rotation speed. It does not have any discs.

Five modellings are being studied:

- Modelization A: the beam is on the $x$ axis ,
- Modelization B: the beam is on $t$ axis such as $t$ as the master vector of the $(x,y)$ bisectress.
- Modelling C: the beam is on the $t$ axis such as $t$ the master vector of the bisectress, and the $(x,y)$ mass is distributed by discrete elements installed on each nodes.
- Modelization D: the beam is on the $x$ axis. The section is circular and variable with the same $\mathrm{R1}$ and $\mathrm{R2}$ rays.
- Modelling E: Resumes Modelling C with reporting of the characteristics of discrete elements in the local marker.

This problem allows us to test the effect of the gyroscopic matrix that was developed for an straight beam.

The gyroscopic effect leads to the duplication of modes. The evolution of the frequencies of the individual as a function of the rotation speed allows to build the Campbell diagram.

The references are based on the theory of Euler-Bernouilli.

The results obtained are in good agreement with those given in reference.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is the one presented in René-Jean GIBERT's book.

By adopting the following ratings:

- Next beam $x$
- $y$ and $z$ bending movements in $\mathrm{xOz}$ and $\mathrm{xOy}$ planes
- $S$: section of the beam
- $I$: bending inertia moment in relation to $y$ and $z$ axes
- ${I}_{x}$: inertia moment per unit length compared to $\mathrm{Ox}$ axis
- $\rho$ , $E$ material characteristics
- $\Omega$ beam rotation speed

The singular solutions are governed by the following system of equations:

$\mathrm{EI}\frac{{\partial}^{4}Y}{\partial {x}^{4}}-\rho S{\omega}^{2}Y+i\omega \Omega {I}_{x}\frac{{\partial}^{2}Z}{\partial {z}^{2}}=0$

$\mathrm{EI}\frac{{\partial}^{4}Z}{\partial {x}^{4}}-\rho S{\omega}^{2}Z-i\omega \Omega {I}_{x}\frac{{\partial}^{2}Y}{\partial {z}^{2}}=0$

by following the following boundary conditions:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}Y=Z=0\\ \frac{{\partial}^{2}Y}{\partial {z}^{2}}=\frac{{\partial}^{2}Z}{\partial {z}^{2}}=0\end{array}$ en $\lbrace \begin{array}{}x=0\\ x=L\end{array}$

Two families of eigenmodes are obtained:

1. Retrograde mode:

${Y}_{1}=-{\mathrm{i.Z}}_{1}=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ with $(\frac{{\omega}_{1}}{{\omega}_{0}})=\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}+1}-\lambda$

1. Direct mode:

${Y}_{2}=-{\mathrm{i.Z}}_{2}=\sin\frac{n\pi x}{L}$ with $(\frac{{\omega}_{2}}{{\omega}_{0}})=\sqrt{{\lambda}^{2}+1}+\lambda$

by setting:

clean pulse without rotation: ${\omega}_{0}={(\frac{n\pi }{L})}^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{EI}}{\rho S}}$

$\lambda =\frac{1}{2}.\frac{\Omega {I}_{x}}{\sqrt{\mathrm{EI}\rho S}}$ with ${I}_{x}=\frac{\rho S{D}^{2}}{8}$ and $I=\frac{\pi {D}^{4}}{64}$

### 2.2 Baseline

4 first clean bending modes.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

Analytical solution with the Euler beam hypothesis.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

René-Jean GIBERT, Vibration of structures, No.69 of the R&D collection d

## 8 Summary of the results

Good implantation of the gyroscopic effect for the element beam. The $x$ beam axis change (direction in which elements was implanted) (modelization A) at a $x+y$ direction (modelization B) does not generate any differences results.

In the absence of an analytical reference for the validation of discrete elements subjected to gyroscopic effect, modellings C and E still allow to verify the implantation of the gyroscopic matrix of a disc supposedly indeformable. The movement of each disc is fixed by that of the nodes and thus follows the deformity of the neutral fiber only discreetly. This explains the differences observed on model C, especially for the higher modes characterized by a greater concavity of the modal deformity.
