# v3.01.018 SSLL18- CALC_FERRAILLAGE Operator Validation Compared to SECTION_ARMATURES Tool

**Summary:**

This test case is designed to validate the `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` operator by comparing it to a qualified tool used by engineering, called `SECTION_ARMATURES` [bib1].
The test consists of two distinct modellingss:

- **Model A**: This first Modelling consists of a comparison between `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` and `SECTION_ARMATURES` on rectangular sections subjected to simple and composite bending for common concrete classes (C25/30). These cases are already part of the validation of the `SECTION_ARMATURES` tool and are also confronted with analytical results as well as other engineering tools.
- **Modelling B**: This second Modelling is designed to test the `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` operator for higher concrete classes ($f_{ck} > 50\,\text{MPa}$), with specific characteristics in the calculation of the railing. It takes the same cases as Modelling A, but for a higher concrete class (C55/67), thus allowing to verify the robustness of the operator in this field.

## 1 Reference problem

The structure studied is a rectangular section beam of $H_y \times H_z$ dimensions.
No mechanical resolution is made in this test case; a generalized field of effort is directly imposed for each configuration.
The dimensions, the forces applied, and the properties of the materials (beton and steel) and the type of scenario considered are summarized for each model in the tables below.

## 4 Summary of the results

The results obtained with the `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` operator were compared with those of the qualified reference tool `SECTION_ARMATURES` through two sets of tests (modellings A and B), covering different concrete classes (C25/30 and C55/67), various sections, and single or composite bending stresses.

For **modelization A** (C25/30 concrete), differences between the two tools is consistently below 5%, with a variation ranging from 0.8 % to 4.7 %. These cases had already been used for the validation of the `SECTION_ARMATURES` tool, thus enhancing the relevance of the comparison.

For **modelization B** (C55/67 concrete), differences also remains very low, all below 3%, showing good robustness of `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` operator for high concrete classes ($f_{ck} > 50\,\text{MPa}$), with a special feature in the ferralization calculation.

In general, these results demonstrate the **coherence, accuracy and reliability of the `CALC_FERRAILLAGE` operator** in standard dimensioning configurations, both for standard concretes and for higher performance concretes.
