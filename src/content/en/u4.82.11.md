# u4.82.11 Operator PROPA_FISS

## 1 Purpose

The purpose of the operator PROPA_FISS is to propagate a crack, this crack being defined with the X-FEM method. Three types of method are available:

- a method based on a *fast marching* algorithm (METHODE_PROPA='SIMPLEXE', 'UPWIND'): this method is available in 2D and in 3D for all types of finite element. The initial crack may be defined on the mesh of the model or on an auxiliary mesh.
- a purely geometric method by computation of distances to the crack front (METHODE_PROPA='GEOMETRIQUE')
- a method using an intermediate surface mesh of the lip of the crack (METHODE_PROPA='MAILLAGE'): this method is available in 2D and in 3D for all types of finite element. The initial crack must itself also be defined by means of a mesh.

The operator PROPA_FISS thus computes the crack resulting from a fatigue propagation according to a local Paris law, from the previous crack and from the table of stress intensity factors coming from CALC_G or from POST_K1_K2_K3.

For half-line cracks (in 2D) or plane cracks with a straight front (in 3D), the surface mesh of the initial crack may be created by the operator PROPA_FISS (METHODE_PROPA = 'INITIALISATION').

The operator produces:

- either a concept of type fiss_xfem defining the propagated crack (case METHODE_PROPA='SIMPLEXE', 'UPWIND' or 'GEOMETRIQUE');
- or the mesh of the initial crack (case METHODE_PROPA='INITIALISATION');
- or the mesh of the propagated crack (case METHODE_PROPA='MAILLAGE').
- The theoretical aspect of the propagation of the level sets for the X-FEM method is dealt with in [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12).

## 3 Operands common to all the options

### 3.1 Operand METHODE_PROPA

♦        METHODE_PROPA    =    /    'SIMPLEXE',

```text
/    'UPWIND',
/    'MAILLAGE',
/    'INITIALISATION',
/    'GEOMETRIQUE',
```

Three methods are available for propagating a crack defined with the X-FEM method:

    - **Methods based on a fast marching algorithm**
- (METHODE_PROPA='UPWIND'): a discretisation of the Eikonal equation is solved in order to reinitialise lsn and lst. The use of the auxiliary grid is indispensable for irregular meshes. For further details concerning the algorithms relating to this method, one may refer to the reference documentation [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12).

(METHODE_PROPA='SIMPLEXE'): Robust method, which may be applied to arbitrary meshes. For further details concerning the algorithms relating to this method, one may refer to the reference documentation [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12).

  - **a method using an intermediate surface mesh of the lip of the crack** (METHODE_PROPA='MAILLAGE'): this method is available in 2D and in 3D for all types of finite element.

The initial crack must itself also be defined by means of a mesh, with specific naming rules. For example in 3D, the front is called $\text{FOND\_0}$ and contains the nodes $\text{NXA1}$, $\text{NXB1}$, …; the lip is called $\text{FISS\_0}$ and contains the surface mesh cells $\text{MXA1}$, $\text{MXB1}$, …; the surface mesh cell $\text{MXA1}$ contains the nodes $\text{NXA0}$, $\text{NXB0}$, $\text{NXA1}$ and $\text{NXB1}$.

  - **a method using a purely geometric approach** (METHODE_PROPA='GEOMETRIQUE'): this method, which uses a vector propagation of the crack front and of its local basis as well as an estimate of the distances with respect to the latter in this local basis, is available in 2D and in 3D for all types of finite element without any restriction.

In order to facilitate the definition of this initial crack in the most usual cases (half-line crack (in 2D) or plane crack with a straight front (in 3D)), the method 'INITIALISATION' must be used.

**Note on the various methods:**

*The method of projection onto an intermediate mesh is for its part more approximate, but it makes it possible to have very satisfactory results, in particular for propagations in pure mode I.*

*The fast marching methods are robust and give very satisfactory results for propagations in mixed mode. The simplex method does not require an auxiliary grid.*

*The purely geometric method is a* *method that* does not require an auxiliary grid and offers good performance.

### 3.2 Operand OPERATION

```text
#    Si METHODE_PROPA    = 'GEOMETRIQUE'
◊OPERATION    =    / 'RIEN' [DEFAUT]
/ 'DETEC_COHESIF'
/ 'PROPA_COHESIF'
```

This keyword defines the nature of the operation desired when the crack is updated:

If 'RIEN', a classical propagation of the normal and tangent level sets is desired, according to a certain angle and a certain advance. This is the operation that must be selected in the absence of cohesive forces.

The operation 'DETEC_COHESIF' carries out a detection of the front by post-processing of a result with cohesive forces. Given a possible cracking surface, on which cohesive forces are defined, it makes it possible to detect the new position of the propagation front, by post-processing of the result, which must be given under the keyword RESULTAT. It is therefore an update of the tangent level set only.

The operation 'PROPA_COHESIF' extends the possible cracking surface according to an angle given as an argument in TABLE, and by a uniform length specified by DA_MAX. Once the propagation direction has been determined, the point is to extend the possible cracking zone in order to carry out the next mechanical analysis. This extension must be made over a length greater than the effective advance of the crack. This may be quantified by a simple dimensional analysis, and must be checked a posteriori during the analysis.

The algorithms for crack propagation with cohesive elements are described in the reference documentation [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19).

### 3.3 Keyword LOI_PROPA

```text
# ou (METHODE_PROPA='MAILLAGE' ou 'INITIALISATION')

♦    LOI_PROPA    =    _F    (
♦    LOI        =    /‘PARIS’,
# Si LOI='PARIS'

◊    DELTA_K_SEUIL    =    /    0.                                [DEFAUT]
/    val_seuil,                    [R]
# Fin si

♦ MATER    = ma,                                                        [mater] ),
```

The factor keyword LOI_PROPA defines the propagation law used.

For the moment, the only law available is a Paris law, in the following form:

$\frac{\mathit{da}}{\mathit{dN}}=\mathit{C.}{(\Delta {K}_{\mathit{eq}}-\Delta {K}_{\mathit{seuil}})}^{m}$

The coefficients $C$ and $m$ of this law must necessarily be supplied. The propagation threshold $\Delta {K}_{\mathit{seuil}}$ can be set via the optional parameter DELTA_K_SEUIL, whose default value is 0. In the case $\Delta {K}_{\mathit{eq}}<\Delta {K}_{\mathit{seuil}}$, the advance is zero.

*Note:*

*For the moment all the cracks of the model must propagate in the same material. In fact only one material can be given and it is used for all the cracks of the model.*

*In addition Paris's law does not make it possible to consider the propagation threshold of the crack.*

*Warning:*

*The constants of the propagation law must be given in such a way that the unit of the advance rate* $\mathit{da}/\mathit{dN}$ *is equal to* $L/\mathit{cycles}$ *, where* $L$ *is the unit used in the model for the length.*

### 3.5 Operand DA_MAX

> ♦    DA_MAX    =    da,                                                        [R]

In the case where there is a single crack in the model mo, this operand defines the maximum advance of the crack.

In the case where there are several cracks in the model mo, this operand defines the maximum advance of the crack that propagates fastest. The number of fatigue cycles is computed using the speed of the point of the crack front that propagates fastest. This number of cycles is used for all the cracks of the model.

If OPERATION='PROPA_COHESIF', this keyword defines the uniform length over which the new possible cracking surface is extended with a view to the next propagation step.

# If TEST_MAIL='NON',

```text
♦ COEF_MULT_MAXI    = cmax,                [R]
```

The parameters COEF_MULT_MINI and COEF_MULT_MAXI of the operand COMP_LINE define the minimum and maximum loading conditions of the fatigue cycle. The two values are the constants by which the loadings of the reference configuration must be multiplied in order to obtain the minimum and maximum loading conditions of the fatigue cycle. Thus if the reference configuration chosen coincides with the minimum or maximum loading condition, the value of COEF_MULT_MINI or COEF_MULT_MAXI, respectively, is equal to 1.

### 3.7 Factor keyword CRIT_ANGL_BIFURCATION

```text
◊    CRIT_ANGL_BIFURCATION    =    /    'SITT_MAX    '                    [DEFAUT]
/ 'SITT_MAX_DEVER'

/    'K1_MAX'
/    'K2_NUL'
/    'PLAN'
/    'ANGLE_IMPO'

/    'ANGLE_IMPO_GAMMA'

/ 'ANGLE_IMPO_BETA_GAMMA'
```

This operand makes it possible to choose the criterion for computation of the bifurcation angle:

- 'SITT_MAX': criterion of the maximum circumferential stress (Maximal Hoop Stress criterion, [R7.02.05 §2.5.2]); this is the default criterion. It is computed from the values of K1 and K2. It is available in 2D and in 3D.
- 'SITT_MAX_DEVER': criterion of the maximum circumferential stress with account taken of KIII [R7.02.05 §2.5.2] and account taken of the twisting rotation [R7.02.13 §2.1]. It is computed from the values of K1, K2 and K3 and is available only in 3D.
- 'K1_MAX', 'K2_NUL': criteria of Amestoy, Bui and Dang-Van [R7.02.05 §2.5.1]. These 2 criteria are available only in 2D. The angle is given to within 10 degrees. Caution: it is observed that the criterion 'K2_NUL' does not work for an angle greater than 60°.
- 'PLAN': defines a zero angle corresponding to a plane propagation.
- 'ANGL_IMPO': makes it possible to choose an angle computed beforehand. In this case, the angle taken is that given in the column BETA of the input table (table given under the keyword TABLE) and the twisting rotation is not activated. For this, the input table of PROPA_FISS must therefore contain a column BETA.
- 'ANGLE_IMPO_GAMMA': makes it possible to choose a twisting angle computed beforehand. In this case, the twisting angle taken is that given in the column GAMMA of the input table (table given under the keyword TABLE) and the branching angle is computed [R7.02.13§2.1]. For this, the input table of PROPA_FISS must therefore contain a column GAMMA. The twisting angle BETA is computed by the operator POST_RUPTURE, in the configuration CRITERE = SITT_MAX_DEVER [U4.82.04](doc:u4.82.04).
- 'ANGLE_IMPO_BETA_GAMMA': makes it possible to choose branching and twisting angles computed beforehand. In this case the branching and twisting angles taken are respectively BETA and GAMMA given in the input table (under the keyword TABLE). For this, the input table must contain a column BETA and a column GAMMA of the same size.

**Note**:

*The possibility of imposing the branching angle BETA and then deducing the twisting angle GAMMA from it is not in accordance with the model. Indeed the computation of GAMMA requires knowledge of the stress intensity factors, which cannot be recovered from the datum of the branching angle BETA alone.*

### 4.1 Operand MODELE

Name of the model on which the crack to be propagated is defined.

### 4.2 Operand TEST_MAIL

> ◊    TEST_MAIL        =    / 'NON'                                        [DEFAUT]

> / 'OUI'

The mesh used for the representation of the level sets (mesh of the model mo) must be sufficiently refined in order to represent the crack well. This can be checked by using the operand TEST_MAIL.

In the case TEST_MAIL='OUI', the same propagation speed is assigned to all the points of the crack fronts defined on the model mo. All the cracks therefore advance by the length given by the operand DA_MAX. If the mesh is sufficiently refined, the crack front after propagation must be homothetic to the front before propagation. If this is not verified, the mesh must be refined.

The operand computes several propagations of the cracks of the model mo (see operands DA_MAX, ITERATIONS and TOLERANCE) and for each propagation it computes the distance between the new front after propagation and the front at the beginning of the propagation (see FISS_ACTUELLE in the operand FISSURE). If the mesh of the model mo or the auxiliary grid griaux used for the representation of the level sets are sufficiently refined, the distance computed must be equal to the imposed advance DA_MAX multiplied by the number of the current iteration. A small tolerance is used for this check (see operand TOLERANCE). If this is not verified, an alert message is issued by PROPA_FISS.

**The solution of the cracked model and the computation of the stress intensity factors are not necessary when this operand is used. The check is therefore very fast (see test cases zzzz255a and zzzz255b).**

### 4.3 Operand ITERATIONS

> # Si TEST_MAIL='OUI'
> ◊    ITERATIONS    =    / iters,                                        [I]

> / 5,                                            [DEFAUT]

This operand is used to give the number of iterations (propagations) computed during the test of the mesh (see operand TEST_MAIL). For each iteration, the crack is propagated and the distance between the propagated crack front and the front at the beginning of the propagation (see FISS_ACTUELLE in the operand FISSURE) is computed and checked. It is strongly recommended to use a number of iterations equal to or greater than 5, because the numerical errors accumulate at each iteration.

### 4.4 Operand TOLERANCE

> # Si TEST_MAIL='OUI'
> ◊ TOLERANCE    =     / toler,                                            [R]

> / 5.0,                                                [DEFAUT]

This operand is used to give the tolerance, expressed as a percentage, used in the test of the mesh (see operand TEST_MAIL). For each iteration of the test (see operand ITERATIONS), the maximum and minimum distances between the propagated front and the front at the beginning of the propagation (see FISS_ACTUELLE in the operand FISSURE) are computed. Theoretically these distances are identical and their value is equal to the imposed advance DA_MAX multiplied by the number of the current iteration. The difference between the maximum and minimum distances and the theoretical distance is therefore computed, and it is checked that the two differences computed are smaller than a limit value. This limit value is computed as a percentage of the length of the smallest edge of the elements of the mesh used for the representation of the level sets. The percentage used is given by the operand TOLERANCE.

### 4.5 Operand ZONE_MAJ

> ◊ ZONE_MAJ        =    / 'TORE'                                        [DEFAUT]

> / 'TOUT'

This operand makes it possible to select the zone used for the update of the level sets. One may choose to carry out the analysis on all the nodes of the model mo by using ZONE_MAJ='TOUT', or to limit the analysis zone to the nodes of the model mo that lie in a torus built around the crack front by using ZONE_MAJ='TORE'.

This latter choice makes it possible to reduce the run time greatly even if the mesh used for the representation of the level sets is not formed of a large number of mesh cells. In the case of propagation in mixed mode, the use of the restriction of the update zone is strongly recommended in order to improve the performance of the analysis and in order to avoid the distortion of the level sets, which can cause a fatal error during the update.

The computation of the radius of the localisation torus and its update at each propagation are done automatically. However, in the case where the size of the elements in the propagation zone is not uniform, the radius computed may be too small and an error message is issued by the operator PROPA_FISS. In this case the user is obliged to give explicitly a value of the radius (see operand RAYON_TORE).

### 4.6 Operand RAYON_TORE

> # Si ZONE_MAJ='TORE'

> ◊ RAYON_TORE    = raytor,                                            [R]

This operand makes it possible to give the value of the radius of the torus used for the restriction of the update zone of the level sets (see operand ZONE_MAJ). The radius of the torus is computed automatically by the operator PROPA_FISS and the user is not obliged to use this operand. However the value computed may be too small in certain cases, for example when the minimum and maximum sizes of the elements in the propagation zone are very different from each other. In all these cases, the user must necessarily give the radius of the torus by following the advice contained in the error messages issued by PROPA_FISS.

It should be noted that the value of the radius cannot change much between two successive propagations (see the documentation R7.02.12 for further details). It is therefore strongly recommended to use the same value at each call to PROPA_FISS by giving the maximum value envisaged. The value of the radius must always be greater than a minimum value that can be estimated by computing the sum of the values of the crack advance (see operand DA_MAX) and of the convergence radius (see operand RAYON). However this value is also linked to the size of the elements in the torus and it is therefore strongly recommended not to use a value smaller than that which was computed automatically by PROPA_FISS. An error is issued by PROPA_FISS if the radius given is not correct with respect to the propagation to be simulated and to the size of the elements of the mesh.

Caution: in the cases of cracks with several crack fronts (for example in the case of a crack of segment type, in 2D, which has 2 crack fronts); the torus must not enclose the other fronts of the crack (even if these other fronts lie “outside the mesh”).

#### 4.7.4 Operand TABLE

> #     Si TEST_MAIL='NON'

> **◊**     TABLE    =    sif,                                                [table]

This operand must be supplied with the table of the stress intensity factors at the front of the crack given by the operand FISS_ACTUELLE. This table comes from the operators CALC_G or POST_K1_K2_K3.

In the case of a multiple crack front (see R7.02.12, paragraph “Multiple crack front”), for which the front of the crack is formed of several pieces, the operator CALC_G/POST_K1_K2_K3 must be called several times, once for each piece (operand NUME_FOND of CALC_G/POST_K1_K2_K3). There are therefore several tables, one table for each piece, which must be combined into a single table that will be given to PROPA_FISS in the operand TABLE. For this, the operator CALC_TABLE may be used. For example, in the case where the crack is formed of two pieces, the combination of the two tables may be carried out in the following way:

**SIF1** = CALC_G(THETA=_F(...

> NUME_FOND= **1** ,),
> …)

**SIF2** = CALC_G(THETA=_F(...

> NUME_FOND= **2** ,),
> …)

SIF = CALC_TABLE(TABLE= **SIF1** ,

ACTION=(_F( **OPERATION='COMB'** ,

TABLE= **SIF2** ,

NOM_PARA=('NUME_ORDRE','NUME_FOND'),),

),

);

SIF is then used in the operator PROPA_FISS. This table is formed by the union of the two tables coming from CALC_G. Any order may be used in the combination of the tables.

#### 4.7.5 Operand NB_POINT_FOND

> # Si TEST_MAIL='NON'
> ◊    NB_POINT_FOND =    nbptfd,                                        [I]

If the operand NB_POINT_FOND has been used in CALC_G for the computation of the stress intensity factors, this operand must necessarily also be used in PROPA_FISS.

**The operator** PROPA_FISS **cannot check whether this operand has been used or not in** CALC_G **or whether the values given are correct. The user must therefore take care to use this operand correctly!**

The operand NB_POINT_FOND accepts a list of integer values. For each piece (see operand TABLE) of the crack given by FISS_ACTUELLE a value of NB_POINT_FOND equal to the value used in CALC_G must be given. If the operand has not been used in CALC_G for a piece, the value zero is used. The order of the values of NB_POINT_FOND in the list is given by the operand NUME_FOND of CALC_G. Examples are given at the end of this document.

### 4.8 Operand RAYON

> **◊**     RAYON    =    ray,                                                    [R]

This operand is used to define the radius of the torus around the crack front in which the residuals will be estimated for the stages of reinitialisation and reorthogonalisation of the level sets. This torus defines the zone in which the level sets must be sufficiently close to signed-distance functions.

**The value of this radius must always be greater than the upper integration radius of the operator** CALC_G **. The operator** PROPA_FISS **cannot check whether this condition is satisfied. The user must therefore take care to use this operand correctly!**

## 5 Operands specific to the method MAILLAGE

### 5.1 Operands MAIL_STRUC

```text
♦    MAIL_STRUC    =    mast,                                            [maillage]
```

This operand makes it possible to define the mesh of the sound structure (*i.e.* without a crack).

### 5.2 Operand ITERATION

```text
♦    ITERATION    =    iter,                                            [I]
```

This operand makes it possible to define the number of the iteration of the propagation analysis. The first iteration (propagation of the initial crack) is iteration 1.

This operand facilitates the carrying out of the analyses: it is thus possible to restart a propagation analysis on a crack that has already been propagated *nb_init* times.

### 5.3 Operand MAIL_TOTAL

```text
♦    MAIL_TOTAL    =    CO('matot2'),                                [TXM]
```

This operand makes it possible to define the name of the total mesh, *i.e.* the concatenation of the mesh of the sound structure (cf. operand MAIL_STRUC) and of the mesh of the propagated crack MAIL_PROPAGE.

It is this total mesh matot2 that must be used in order to define the model and the new crack in DEFI_FISS_XFEM.

### 5.4 Factor keyword FISSURE

This mandatory operand makes it possible to indicate the cracks of the model mo that propagate.

#### 5.4.1 Syntax FISSURE

```text
♦    FISSURE        =    _F(
```

#### 5.4.2 Operand MAIL_ACTUEL

```text
♦    MAIL_ACTUEL    =    max1,                                            [maillage]
```

This operand makes it possible to define the crack that one wishes to propagate by means of its surface mesh max1. The mesh of the crack max1 must comply with certain naming rules, cf. § 5.

#### 5.4.3 Operands FISS_ACTUELLE, TABLE

See paragraphs § 4.7.2 and § 4.7.4.

#### 5.4.4 Operand MAIL_PROPAGE

> ◊    GROUP_MA_FOND    =    /    'FOND'                        [DEFAUT]

```text
/    grmaff                        [TXM]

    ◊    GROUP_MA_FISS    =    /    'FISS'                        [DEFAUT]
```

These operands make it possible to indicate what the mesh-cell groups of the front and of the lips will be called (which will successively be $\text{FOND\_0}$, $\text{FOND\_1}$, etc.). ## 6 Operands specific to the method INITIALISATION

### 6.1 Operands MAIL_STRUC

♦    MAIL_STRUC    =    mast,                                        [maillage]

### 6.2 Operands FORM_FISS

```text
♦    FORM_FISS    =    /    'DEMI_DROITE'
/    'DEMI_PLAN'
/     'ELLIPSE'
```

This operand makes it possible to define the type of the crack: a half-line in 2D or a plane crack with a straight front in 3D, or an elliptical crack in 3D.

### 6.3 Case DEMI_DROITE: operands PFON and DTAN_DTAN

```text
♦    PFON    =    (xf,yf,zf),                                    [l_R]

♦    DTAN    =    (xt,yt,zt),                                    [l_R]
```

These operands make it possible to define the initial crack in 2D by giving the coordinate of the point of the crack front PFON and its propagation direction DTAN.

### 6.4 Case DEMI_PLAN: operands PFON, POINT_ORIG, POINT_EXTR and NB_POINT_FOND

```text
♦    POINT_ORIG    =    (xo,yo,zo),                            [l_R]

♦    POINT_EXTR    =    (xe,ye,ze),                            [l_R]

♦    DTAN            =    (xt,yt,zt),                            [l_R]

♦    NB_POINT_FOND    =    nbfond,                                [I]
```

```text
Ces opérandes permettent de définir la fissure initiale en 3D en donnant les coordonnées des points origine et extrémité du fond de fissure et la direction de propagation de celle-ci DTAN. Le fond de fissure sera discrétisé avec nbfond nœuds.
```

### 6.5 Case ELLIPSE: operands CENTRE, DEMI_GRAND_AXE, DEMI_PETIT_AXE, VECT_X, VECT_Y, ANGLE_ORIG, ANGLE_EXTR and NB_POINT_FOND

```text
Ces opérandes permettent de définir la fissure elliptique initiale en 3D en donnant les coordonnées du centre de l'ellipse, les valeurs des demi-petit axe et demi-grand axe, des vecteurs d'orientation du plan de l'ellipse. Pour plus de détails concernant ces mot-clés, voir U4.82.08.

♦    ANGLE_ORIG        =    ango,                            [R]
♦    ANGLE_EXTR        =    ange,                            [R]

ANGLE_ORIG donne la position (en degrés) du premier point dans le repère (VECT_X, VECT_Y).

ANGLE_EXTR donne la position (en degrés) du dernier point dans le repère (VECT_X, VECT_Y).

NB_POINT_FOND désigne le nombre de point à créer le long du fond de fissure.
```

Several test cases make it possible to validate crack propagation with X-FEM.
