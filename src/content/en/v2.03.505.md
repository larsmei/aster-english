# v2.03.505 SDLS505 - Flaming of an externally pressurized cylindrical casing

**Summary:**

This test represents a stability calculation of a thin cylindrical envelope blocked at its ends subject to external pressure and axial pressure. Critical loads leading to elastic flaming d-Euler are calculated. The geometric rigidity matrix used in solving the problem to the specific values is that due to the initial stresses.

It allows to validate elements Modelling finished:

- COQUE_3D with mesh cells TRIA7 and QUAD9.
- DKT with mesh cells TRIA3 and QUAD4.

The resulting critical load and eigenmode are compared to an analytical reference solution.

Note that in B Modelling, two methods are used to obtain the flash mode: CALC_MODE and CRIT_STAB of the STAT_NON_LINE operator.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.505/Object_1.svg)

The symmetry of the problem allows to model a half cylinder of length $L/2$ , plugged at one of its ends, with symmetry conditions specific to the lower edge.

### 1.2 Material properties

The Material properties component of the plate is:

| $E=2.{10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\nu =0.3$ | Poisson's ratio |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

- Loading:
- uniformly distributed pressure of ${p}_{\mathit{cr}}=1.523{10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}$ on the cylindrical part. This pressure corresponds to the value of the critical load,
- effort distributed on the $N=0.5\times R\cdot {p}_{\mathit{cr}}=3.8075{10}^{4}N/m$ background contour.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Not applicable

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The critical pressure is given in [bib1] or [bib2] by the following expression:

${P}_{\mathit{cr}}=\frac{\mathit{Eh}}{R}\frac{1}{({n}^{2}+\frac{{b}^{2}}{2})}\left[\frac{1}{{(\frac{{n}^{2}}{{b}^{2}}+1)}^{2}}+\frac{{h}^{2}}{12{R}^{2}(1-{\nu}^{2})}{({n}^{2}+{b}^{2})}^{2}\right]$

| with | $b=\frac{\pi R}{L}$ |
| --- | --- |
|  | $n$ represents the number of circumferential modes |

This formula is valid in case $N=0.5R{p}_{\mathit{cr}}$ .

### 3.4 Remarks

Critical pressure is tested without difficulty.

However, it is difficult to test the flash mode because it is a double mode.

The validation of this flash mode shall be done as follows:

1. The mesh coordinates (from 1.e-8) are changed very slightly so that the mode associated with n=8 is the same, at each run, on all machines where the test is performed. (The problem is de-symetricated a little bit).
2. Visually validating the 7 and 8 flashing modes is a shape with 8 lobes (see images below).
3. We check (with a non-regression test) that mode 8 remains the same during code evolutions.

| ![Figure](/figures/v2.03.505/100000000000035600000334FEEF9F081A02AE7E.png) | ![Figure](/figures/v2.03.505/1000000000000356000003346C6D2C778347EEEC.png) |
| --- | --- |

Note:

For the 4 modellings of this test, the fineness of the mesh is insufficient to represent the shape of the mode. This explains why the mode observed is not strictly symmetrical by 45 degrees rotation.

We checked that symmetry was improved with a finer mesh. But to keep a quick test, we keep the coarse mesh.

## 7 Summary of the results

**Modelling** COQUE_3D

The results obtained are satisfactory. Uncertainties on critical pressure do not exceed:

- $\text{10.71\%}$ with mesh cells TRIA7,
- $\text{2.3\%}$ with mesh cells QUAD9.

The resulting modal deformity corresponds well to the expected circumferential mode: $n=8$ for both modellings.

COQUE_3D Modelling with mesh cells QUAD9 is more accurate than COQUE_3D Modelling with mesh cells TRIA7.

**Modelling** DKT

The results obtained are satisfactory for elements plates. The uncertainties on the critical pressure do not exceed $\text{12.5\%}$ any mesh cell (QUAD4, TRIA3).

The resulting modal deformity corresponds well to the expected circumferential mode: $n=8$ for both modellings.

This test tested modellings COQUE_3D and DKT linearly flaming d-Euler of a thin structure subjected to external pressure.
