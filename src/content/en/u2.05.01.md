# u2.05.01 User's guide to the fracture-mechanics operators for the classical approach (non-linear elasticity)

**Summary:**

This document presents the fracture-mechanics operators available in *Code_Aster* within the framework of the classical approach. The classical approach is limited to non-linear elasticity. Advice on use is given.

It is advisable to have read the general methodological guide on fracture mechanics, which lists the various approaches available [U2.05.00](doc:u2.05.00).

## 1 General points

The classical approach is the historical approach for fracture mechanics. It was initially developed within the framework of linear elasticity, then extended to non-linear elasticity.

### 1.1 Linear elastic fracture mechanics in quasi-statics

This paragraph recalls the characteristic parameters in linear elastic fracture mechanics.

#### 1.1.1 Stress intensity factors

The stress intensity factors characterise the singularity of the stresses at the crack tip. Their general expression is of the form: $K=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}\sigma (r)\sqrt{r}$ . Three stress intensity factors are defined, associated with the three opening modes of the crack.

In linear elasticity, the stress intensity factors make it possible to decompose the displacement field $\mathrm{u}$ into a singular part and a regular part [1] [6]:

$u={u}_{R}+{K}_{I}{u}_{S}^{I}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}{u}_{S}^{\mathit{II}}+{K}_{\mathit{III}}{u}_{S}^{\mathit{III}}$ .

#### 1.1.2 Energy release rate

One considers a cracked elastic solid occupying the domain $\Omega$ . Let:

$\mathrm{u}$ the displacement field,

$T$ the temperature field,

$\mathrm{f}$ the field of body forces applied on $\Omega$ ,

$\mathrm{g}$ the field of surface forces applied on a part $S$ of $\partial \Omega$ ,

$U$ the field of imposed displacements on a part ${S}_{d}$ of $\partial \Omega$ .

$\sigma$ the stress tensor,

$\varepsilon$ the strain tensor,

${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}$ the tensor of strains of thermal origin,

$\psi (\varepsilon ,T)$ the free-energy density.

The energy release rate $G$ corresponds to the energetic approach to Griffith fracture [5]. It is defined by the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy at equilibrium $W(u)$ with respect to the domain $\Omega$ :

$G=\frac{-\partial W(u)}{\partial \Omega }$

with: $W(\mathrm{u})=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\psi (\varepsilon (\mathrm{u}),T)d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathrm{f}\mathrm{u}d\Omega -\underset{S}{\int}\mathrm{g}\mathrm{u}d\Gamma$

One recalls that the energy release rate is equivalent to the Rice integral in linear elasticity [4].

In plane linear elasticity, the stress intensity factors are related to the energy release rate by Irwin's formula:

$G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})$     in plane strain

$G=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})$     in plane stress

$G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})+\frac{{K}_{\mathrm{III}}^{2}}{2\mu }$ with $\mu =\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$ , in 3D

### 1.2 Extension to non-linear elasticity

The preceding definitions are rigorous only in linear thermoelasticity, but extensions are possible to non-linear problems. In particular, it is possible to define and to compute the energy release rate in non-linear elasticity, provided that the loading remains radial and monotonic.

## 2 Framework of use of the fracture-mechanics features in Code_Aster

### 2.1 Reminder of the available calculation methods

#### 2.1.1 Theta method

*Calculation of the energy release rate*

The difficulty of the calculation of the energy release rate comes from the derivation with respect to the domain of an integral depending on that same domain. A rigorous method is the theta method, which is a Lagrangian method of derivation of the potential energy [8] [9] [10]. It consists in introducing a field $\theta$ and in considering transformations ${F}^{\eta}:\mathrm{M}\to \mathrm{M}+\eta \theta (\mathrm{M})$ of the reference domain $\Omega$ into a domain ${\Omega}_{\eta}$ which correspond to propagations of the crack. These transformations must not modify the boundaries of the domain other than the crack front.

This method is detailed in [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) and [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03). The use of the method, developed in the operator CALC_G of *Code_Aster*, is described in § 4.1.

*Calculation of the stress intensity factors*

In linear thermoelasticity, one can associate with $G$ a symmetric bilinear form $g(u,v)$ by the polarisation formula. One can then show that this bilinear form defines a scalar product for which the singular functions ${u}_{S}$ are orthogonal to one another and orthogonal to the regular displacement ${u}_{R}$ [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05).

Consequently, one can compute the stress intensity factors from $g(u,v)$ by the theta method:

Finally, in a general manner:

$$
\begin{cases}
   K_I = E g(u, u_S^I) \\
   K_{\mathrm{II}} = E g(u, u_S^{\mathrm{II}})
\end{cases}
\quad \text{(en contraintes planes)}
$$

$$
\begin{cases}
   K_I = \frac{E}{1 - \nu^2} g(u, u_S^I) \\
   K_{\mathrm{II}} = \frac{E}{1 - \nu^2} g(u, u_S^{\mathrm{II}})
\end{cases}
\quad \text{(en déformations planes et en 3D)}
$$

$$
K_{\mathrm{III}} = 2\mu \cdot g(u, u_S^{\mathrm{III}})
\quad \text{(en 3D)}
$$

This calculation is possible in *Code_Aster* with the option `CALC_K_G` of the operator `CALC_G`.

*Local calculation*

In dimension 2, the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ reduces to a point. A single field $\theta$ is sufficient to compute the energy release rate or the stress intensity factors.

#### 2.1.2 Calculation by extrapolation of the displacement field

The method of calculation of the stress intensity factors by extrapolation of the displacement, developed in the operator POST_K1_K2_K3, is based on the asymptotic expansion of the displacement field at the crack front [R7.02.08](doc:r7.02.08).

In 2D, in an elastic, linear, isotropic and homogeneous medium, the stress and displacement fields are known analytically for the opening modes of the crack (characterised by $\mathrm{K1}$ ), the in-plane shear mode ($\mathrm{K2}$ ) and the anti-plane shear mode ($\mathrm{K3}$ ). In the general case in 3D, one can show that the asymptotic behaviour of the displacements and of the stresses is the sum of the solutions corresponding to modes 1 and 2 (in plane strain) and to mode 3 (anti-plane), and of four other particular solutions, which are however more regular than the preceding ones.

In all cases, the singularity is therefore the same and one can write the following relations in the plane normal to the crack front, at a point $M$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{1}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1-{\nu}^{2})}[{U}_{m}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\\ {K}_{2}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1-{\nu}^{2})}[{U}_{n}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\\ {K}_{3}(M)=\underset{r\to 0}{\lim}(\frac{E}{8(1+\nu )}[{U}_{t}]\sqrt{\frac{2\pi }{r}})\end{array}$

**Notes:**

- *One can observe that the signs of* $\mathrm{K2}$ *and* $\mathrm{K3}$ *depend on the orientation of* $\text{t}$ *and* $\text{n}$ *. This is not too troublesome in so far as the fracture or fatigue criteria use only the absolute values of* $\mathrm{K2}$ *and* $\mathrm{K3}$ *.*
- *The method used here is theoretically less accurate than the calculation from the bilinear form* of the energy release rate and of the singular displacements [R7.02.01 and R7.02.05] ( *operator* *CALC_G). It does however make it possible to obtain easily relatively reliable values of the stress intensity factors. Comparing the different calculation methods is always useful in order to estimate the accuracy of the results obtained.*

**Note on the use of** *POST_K1_K2_K3* **with Barsoum elements with contact:**

*Contact is generally not taken into account correctly for the quarter-point nodes (except for the continuous contact formulation). The calculation of* $\mathrm{K1}$ *on a vertex node of a Barsoum element in the presence of contact is therefore wrong. This is not very serious in practice because, when there is contact, one knows that* $\mathrm{K1}$ *must be zero.*

#### 2.2.2 Material characteristics

The calculation of the energy release rate $G$ by the theta method (operator CALC_G option CALC_G) is valid for a homogeneous isotropic material, or for an isotropic bimaterial (crack at the interface of two homogeneous isotropic materials with different characteristics).

The calculation of the stress intensity factors (by the theta method using the bilinear form associated with $G$ in the operator CALC_G option CALC_K_G, or by extrapolation of the displacement field with the operator POST_K1_K2_K3) is valid for a homogeneous isotropic material but not for a bimaterial.

The calculation of $G$ or of the stress intensity factors also remains valid if the material properties (Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, thermal expansion coefficient and possibly elastic limit and hardening modulus) are not homogeneous, but only in the case where the gradient of material properties is orthogonal to the direction of the theta field (propagation direction).

From the software point of view, the operator CALC_G can be used with a function material (operator DEFI_MATERIAU, keyword ELAS_FO) of a command variable among all those which can be assigned to a material field (see [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03): operator AFFE_MATERIAU, keyword AFFE_VARC). However, the operator CALC_G emits an alarm as soon as the material field is assigned with a command variable other than the temperature (TEMP). The operator POST_K1_K2_K3 supports only the command variables TEMP and NEUT1.

#### 2.2.3 Constitutive relation used in fracture-mechanics post-processing

Logically, the constitutive relation used during the fracture-mechanics post-processing is that which was used during the mechanical analysis.

One recalls that the calculation of the energy release rate is possible in the following cases:

- linear thermoelasticity,
- non-linear thermoelasticity (hyperelasticity),
- thermo-elastoplasticity (von Mises criterion with isotropic or kinematic hardening).

The calculation of the stress intensity factors is for its part possible only in linear thermoelasticity under the small-strain assumption.

In these situations, there is therefore no reason to choose a different constitutive relation between the mechanical analysis and the post-processing. In this case, one must therefore not supply COMPORTEMENT under CALC_G. If COMPORTEMENT is not present under CALC_G, then the post-processing will be executed with the constitutive relation of the mechanical analysis.

The factor keyword COMPORTEMENT under CALC_G is used only in the very particular cases in which one wishes to carry out the post-processing with a constitutive law different from that which was used for the mechanical analysis. This keyword must be used with extreme caution.

#### 2.2.4 Loading

Certain loadings are associated with additional terms in the expression for the energy release rate or the stress intensity factors according to the theta method. The loadings which are currently supported for the fracture-mechanics calculation are the following:

- Thermal expansion (transmitted via the command variables);
- Body forces: FORCE_INTERNE, PESANTEUR, ROTATION;
- Surface forces on the crack lips: PRES_REP, FORCE_CONTOUR, FORCE_FACE;
- Initial strain (2D meshed crack with option CALC_G only): PRE_EPSI.

It is not possible to take into account an imposed displacement on the crack lips (DDL_IMPO or FACE_IMPO) or a nodal force on them.

It is important to note that the only loadings which have an influence in a fracture-mechanics calculation with the $\theta$ method are those applied on the elements inside the crown (between ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ for a linear or non-linear thermoelastic constitutive behaviour [R7.02.01 §3.3], between the crack front and ${R}_{\sup}$ for a thermo-elastoplastic relation [R7.02.07](doc:r7.02.07)).

**Note:**

If one carries out an analysis in large rotations and large displacements (keyword DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP' under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT) the only loadings supported are dead loads, typically an imposed force and not a pressure [R7.02.03§2.4].

#### 2.2.5 Initial state

For a meshed crack, it is possible to take into account an initial state (either initial stresses, or initial strains) for the calculation of the energy release rate. Two possibilities are offered to the user:

- define initial strains with the keyword PRE_EPSI in the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA(_F) [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) (see an example of implementation in the test case sslp102); one recalls that recovery in the command CALC_G is automatic but that PRE_EPSI is compatible only for a 2D meshed crack and the option CALC_G.
- recover a stress field coming from a mechanical analysis (evol_noli coming from the command STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)) with the keyword ETAT_INIT.

It is not possible to take into account simultaneously initial stresses and initial strains.

- Taking the initial stress into account is possible in the options CALC_G and CALC_G option K.

#### 2.2.6 Contact / friction

The calculation of the fracture-mechanics quantities (grandeur) in *Code_Aster* is not valid if there is contact with friction between the faces of the crack. Indeed the calculation of the energy release rate does not take dissipative phenomena into account.

On the other hand, if the frictional-contact elements are beyond the crown defined between ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ the calculations of $G$ , $G(s)$ , $\mathrm{K1}$ and $\mathrm{K2}$ are valid.

By contrast, it is possible for the calculation of $G$ and of $G(s)$ only to take into account frictionless contact conditions in order to avoid interpenetration of the crack lips.

#### 2.2.7 Stress intensity factors for a thermo-mechanical problem

The stress intensity factors are computed by evaluating the bilinear form of G with a purely mechanical singular solution (Westergaard asymptotic solution). If one solves a thermo-mechanical problem, one then does not take into account the singularity due to the thermal field. An indicator of the error due to this approximation can be obtained by evaluating the difference between G and G_IRWIN. In practice, one evaluates at every point of the crack front the quantity $\frac{∣G-{G}_{\mathit{irwin}}∣}{∣G∣}$, and one then takes the arithmetic mean of it. If this mean exceeds 50%, one then considers that one is leaving the domain of validity of the approach, and an alarm message is emitted. However, the values of G are correct.

For POST_K1_K2_K3, a similar note is present.

### 2.3 Validity of the calculation of G in the non-linear regime

The essential problem in non-linear situations comes from the difficulty of separating the different energetic contributions. Two very distinct classes of problem must be considered:

- that in which, despite the geometric non-linearities or those of the constitutive behaviour, one can exhibit a potential for the internal and external actions (non-linear elasticity or hyperelasticity),
- that in which such a potential does not exist (thermo-elastoplasticity).

For the first class, one can extend the Griffith criterion by using the potential energy at equilibrium, and compute the energy release rate as in linear thermoelasticity.

For the second class of problem, the essential difficulty comes from the fact that the dissipation is not due solely to the propagation of the crack itself. One can no longer distinguish which part of the released energy serves the propagation and which part is used directly by another dissipative phenomenon (plasticity in this instance).

#### 2.3.1 Non-linear thermoelasticity

**Non-linearity of the constitutive behaviour:** the non-linear elastic constitutive relation is described in [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20). It should be noted that the Hencky-von Mises elastoplastic law (isotropic hardening) in the case of a radial and monotonic loading is equivalent to the non-linear elastic law. The hyperelastic material has a reversible mechanical constitutive behaviour, that is, any loading cycle generates no dissipation. Consequently the constitutive relation of the material derives from the free-energy potential and one knows how to give a meaning to the energy release rate within the framework of Griffith's energetic approach.

**Geometric non-linearity:** The calculations of the energy release rates and of the stress intensity factor are theoretically not valid in large strains. The user is however permitted to carry out these post-processings from an analysis in large transformations but small strains (in STAT_NON_LINE, DEFORMATION=’GREEN_LAGRANGE’). This use of the command CALC_G is automatic: the command automatically detects the formalism used in the mechanical analysis (DEFORMATION = ‘PETIT’ or ‘GREEN_LAGRANGE’).

This use is available only with a LINEAIRE discretisation.

It is up to the user to make sure that such an analysis is well founded by checking that the strains do indeed remain small.

#### 2.3.2 Thermo-elastoplasticity

The domain of validity of the calculation of the classical energy release rate is limited to the linear or non-linear thermoelastic framework. To treat the elastoplastic problem, two solutions may be envisaged:

- reduce it to a non-linear thermoelastic problem with restrictive assumptions,
- use another formulation, such as that of the energetic approach.

##### 2.3.2.1 Equivalence between a non-linear thermoelastic problem and a thermo-elastoplastic problem

The non-linear elastic constitutive relation offers the possibility of treating fracture-mechanics problems by approximating the thermo-elastoplastic constitutive behaviour. In the case of a **radial monotonic loading**, it makes it possible to obtain strains and stresses of the structure similar to those which one would obtain if the material presented isotropic hardening. The use of the indicators of unloading and of loss of radiality makes it possible to make sure of the equivalence of the constitutive laws, cf. § 3.3.

But the conditions of proportional and monotonic loadings, indispensable in order to ensure the consistency of the model with the real material, lead to important restrictions on the field of the problems able to be treated by this method (the thermal part in particular may lead to local unloadings).

## 3 Implementation of a fracture-mechanics study

### 3.1 Mesh

#### 3.1.1 Case of a meshed crack

**Conditions to be respected:** there is no condition to be respected *a priori* on the type of mesh at the crack front. However, the numerical quality of the results coming from the mechanical analysis (displacements and stresses) depends on the quality of the mesh and, consequently, so does the quality of the fracture-mechanics quantities.

The definition of the crack by the operator DEFI_FOND_FISS requires mesh-cell groups relating:

- to the mesh cells of the crack front (line mesh cells in 3D, point mesh cell in 2D),
- to the mesh cells of the crack lips (surface meshes in 3D, line mesh cells in 2D).

**Notes and advice:**

- The calculations of the fracture-mechanics quantities are valid for linear or quadratic elements, but it is strongly advised to use quadratic elements, in particular in 3D. The calculation of these quantities indeed requires the stress and strain fields, which vary strongly in the neighbourhood of the crack front, to be determined with a good approximation. Now, for an identical number of nodes, quadratic elements give better results than linear elements.
- The calculation of the stress intensity factors with the operator POST_K1_K2_K3, or with the operator CALC_G option K, can be carried out only in the case where the crack lips are initially bonded, which corresponds to CONFIG_INIT='COLLEE' for the operator DEFI_FOND_FISS.
- A radiating mesh at the crack front is not mandatory: the radii ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ are not linked to the mesh and the crown may “straddle” several elements. Nevertheless practice shows that a radiating mesh at the crack front gives good numerical results.
- When quadratic meshes are used, it is **strongly advised** to position the mid-side nodes of the quadratic elements touching the crack front at the quarter of the edges (mesh of **Barsoum** type). Thus the dependence in $\sqrt{r}$ of the displacement field is better represented and the quality of the results is improved. One can introduce this type of element directly into an existing quadratic mesh by the keyword MODI_MAILLE (option 'NOEUD_QUART') of the command MODI_MAILLAGE [U4.23.04](doc:u4.23.04). The run time is not changed, but the gain in terms of the quality of the results is considerable.

**Checking of the quality of the mesh:** In order to assess the quality of the mesh it is advised to carry out an elastic analysis and to use the discretisation-error estimators: the Zhu-Zienkiewicz error estimators in 2D elasticity [R4.10.01](doc:r4.10.01), the error estimator by residual [R4.10.02](doc:r4.10.02) and the estimators in Quantity of Interest [R4.10.06](doc:r4.10.06), the available Quantities of Interest being $\mathrm{K1}$ , $\mathrm{K2}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ coming from the method POST_K1_K2_K3.

These estimators are implemented in *Code_Aster* in the command CALC_ERREUR [U4.81.06](doc:u4.81.06). They are activated from the following options: ERZ1_ELEM for $\mathrm{ZZ1}$ , ERZ2_ELEM for $\mathrm{ZZ2}$ and ERME_ELEM for the residual estimator by element. The estimation in Quantity of Interest requires the setting up of a dual problem (see for example the test case zzzz257).

#### 3.1.2 Case of a non-meshed crack

**Conditions to be respected:** the use of the X-FEM method makes it possible to remove certain difficulties linked to the mesh. In particular, a free mesh of the sound structure is sufficient. However, a sufficiently fine mesh remains necessary in the zones of high gradient (around the crack front, for example). It is true that, in linear elasticity, enrichment by the asymptotic functions improves the accuracy of the method: for the same mesh-cell size, the X-FEM elements will therefore be more accurate at the crack front than the classical elements. But this has an influence only very locally. A relatively refined mesh at the crack front is therefore required.

By way of comparison between a classical mesh with a torus of $n$ layers of elements at the crack front and a free X-FEM mesh, one can say that the size of the X-FEM elements at the crack front must be of the order of that of the elements of layer $n/2$ of the torus.

Only the error estimator by residual is available for the X-FEM elements, in 2D only.

**Notes and advice:**

- To fix ideas, for a crack of length $a$ in an infinite medium, the size of the elements at the crack front must be between $a/10$ and $a/20$ in order to obtain an error on the energy release rate of between 1% and 2%.
- In order to obtain a refined mesh at the crack front, two approaches may be envisaged:

1. The introduction of a crack block consists in defining, during the creation of the mesh, a box enclosing the crack. The mesh in this box will be regulated, and the fineness of the mesh must be a parameter of the meshing procedure. The box must be sufficiently wide if the study of crack propagation is envisaged. It is also necessary to create a transition zone between the regulated box and the rest of the structure meshed freely. One can also use the advanced features of meshers such as Blsurf+GHS3D, which make it possible to define size maps locally.
2. The second approach consists in producing a suitable mesh by successive refinements of an initial free mesh judged to be coarse [U2.05.02](doc:u2.05.02). The refinement criterion is the distance to the crack front (operator RAFF_XFEM [U7.03.51](doc:u7.03.51)).

### 3.2 Linear elastic analysis

#### 3.2.1 Case of a meshed crack

The calculation of the various parameters of fracture mechanics is done only in post-processing of the classical mechanical analysis. The implementation of a study is therefore the following:

- Reading of the mesh
- Definition of the model, of the materials, of the loadings
- Mechanical analysis with MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE
- Definition of the characteristics of the crack with DEFI_FOND_FISS [U4.82.01].
- Calculation with the operator CALC_G [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03):

  - option G: calculation of $G$
  - option K: calculation of $K$ and of $G$
- Calculation with the operator POST_K1_K2_K3 [U4.82.05](doc:u4.82.05): calculation of $K$ and of $G$ from the displacement jumps on the lips (extracted directly by the operator from the global displacement field).

#### 3.2.2 Case of a non-meshed crack

In the case of a non-meshed crack, there is a preliminary step of definition and enrichment of the model (X-FEM method, cf. [U2.05.02](doc:u2.05.02) for more details). The implementation of a study is therefore the following:

- Reading of the mesh (without crack)
- Definition of the sound model, of the materials
- Definition of the crack: DEFI_FISS_XFEM
- Creation of the enriched model: MODI_MODELE_XFEM
- Mechanical analysis with MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE
- Calculation with the operator CALC_G_XFEM:

  - option CALC_G_XFEM: calculation of $G$
  - option CALC_K_G_XFEM: calculation of $K$ and of $G$
- Calculation with the operator POST_K1_K2_K3 [U4.82.05]: calculation of $K$ and of $G$ from the displacement jumps on the lips
- Creation of the visualisation mesh and visualisation of the result fields (stresses, displacement): POST_MAIL_XFEM and POST_CHAM_XFEM.

#### 3.2.3 Use of the results

The stress intensity factors and the energy release rates computed can be used to:

- assess the risk of initiation of the defect (comparison with the toughness);
- compute the fatigue propagation speed of the crack (Paris law) [U4.82.04](doc:u4.82.04) for a possible propagation calculation (by remeshing or with PROPA_FISS for a non-meshed crack [U4.82.11](doc:u4.82.11));
- estimate the bifurcation direction of the crack loaded in mixed mode [2], [8], [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03) [U4.82.04](doc:u4.82.04).

### 3.3 Non-linear analysis: indicators of unloading and of loss of radiality

These indicators make it possible to locate the local unloadings and the loss of radiality (field DERA_ELGA and DERA_ELNO computed with CALC_CHAMP.

Care is required in the interpretation of the indicators of unloading and of loss of radiality: the value given at time ${t}_{i}$ corresponds to the diagnosis of what happens between ${t}_{i}$ and ${t}_{i+1}$ . Thus, the value computed at the last time step has no meaning. The unloading indicator is negative in order to indicate a local unloading, and the radiality indicator is equal to 0 for a radial path.

**Note:**

*The interpretation of the indicator of loss of radiality is not easy. In particular one does not know how to define a threshold beyond which the calculation is no longer valid.*

*If one does indeed remain within the domain of validity of the calculation of* $G$ *(radial and monotonic loading), then the results with (G_EPSI) or without recalculation (G) of the stresses are identical. As soon as one leaves this domain of validity, the discrepancy grows. One can therefore check a posteriori that one does indeed remain within the assumptions of the calculation of* $G$ *.*

### 3.4 Calculation of the parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$

The parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$ (also denoted directly $\tilde{J}$) enters into the decomposition of $J$ into an elastic part and a plastic part: $J=J_{el}+J_{pl}$. The parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$ corresponds to a generalisation of the elastic part of $J$, $J_{el}$, in particular for secondary loadings. The parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$ is computed by the post-processing of $J$ in an elastic case of a cracked structure when one applies on the lips the opening stress coming from a sound elastoplastic analysis with the loading of interest.

The calculation of the parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$ therefore calls on the usual fracture-mechanics operators, but the global implementation of a calculation of the parameter $\tilde{J}_{el}$ is not completely trivial.

One proposes the test case sslx105, which makes it possible to illustrate such an implementation:

- sslx105a: 2d-axi configuration: this is the penny-shaped crack in tension. The first calculation having been simplified to an elastic calculation, the reference for $\tilde{J}_{el}$ is analytical. This modelling illustrates how to recover the opening stress (normal to the lips) in a general case in 2D (MODI_REPERE/ANGLE_NAUT).
- sslx105b: 3d- configuration: this is a cracked pipe with a defect of longitudinal orientation, semi-elliptical, breaking the outer surface, subjected to an internal pressure. The symmetries are not exploited, which makes it possible to place oneself in a general configuration of an industrial structure. The normal to the defect is oriented along the Y axis. This modelling illustrates the closure condition of the defect, as well as the recovery of the opening stress in a “simple” case (in which the normal to the lip coincides with an axis of the global frame).
- sslx105c: this is the geometric configuration of modelling B to which a rotation about the axis of the pipe has been applied, so that the normal to the crack no longer coincides with an axis of the global frame (a rotation of 30° has been chosen arbitrarily). The pressure loading is replaced by a thermal gradient applied in the wall, over several time instants. This modelling illustrates the recovery of the opening stress in an arbitrary case as well as the construction of the equivalent loading on the lips when the latter depends on the time instant.

## 4 Recommendations for use

### 4.1 Introduction of the theta field

#### 4.1.1 Conditions to be respected

The field $\theta$ is a vector field, defined on the cracked solid, which represents the transformation of the domain during a crack propagation. This field must satisfy the following conditions:

- The transformation must modify only the position of the crack front and not the boundary of the domain $\partial \Omega$.
The field $\theta$ must therefore be tangent to $\partial \Omega$ (in particular to the crack lips),
that is, denoting by $n$ the normal to $\partial \Omega$ :

$$
\theta \cdot n = 0 \quad \text{sur } \partial \Omega.
$$

- The field $\theta$ must be locally in the plane tangent to the crack lips and, in 3D, normal to the edge to which it belongs. This corresponds to the propagation direction of the crack.
- The field $\theta$ must also be continuous on $\Omega$ .

#### 4.1.2 Advice on the choice of the crowns Rinf and Rsup

In Code_Aster, the choice has been made to define the field $\theta$ in the following way:

- the direction of the field is collinear with the propagation direction of the crack. In 3D, one takes the local direction of the projection of the node considered onto the crack front;
- the norm of $\theta$ is defined from two crowns (or tori in 3D), of radius ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ . Below ${R}_{\inf}$ , the modulus of the theta field is constant and equal to 1, beyond it it is zero and it is linear between the two, cf. .

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.01/Object_127.svg)

**Figure** 4.1.2-1 **: Geometric definition of the theta field**

The construction of the theta field is described precisely in [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01). It is implemented in the command CALC_G.

In 2D and in axisymmetric conditions the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ is limited to a point. The user defines:

- the radii ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ ,

In 3D the user defines:

- the radii ${R}_{\inf}(s)$ and ${R}_{\sup}(s)$ ,
- the topology of the crack front: open or closed according to whether the crack is surface-breaking or not,

**Note:**

- *The displacement and stress fields are singular at the crack front; the accuracy of the calculation is therefore poorer in the neighbourhood of the front. One notes that the form of the theta field* *chosen (* $\theta .m$ *constant between 0 and* ${R}_{\inf}$ *) makes it possible precisely to cancel the contribution of the classical term of* $G(\theta )$ *inside the first crown (term in* ${\int}_{\Omega}\left[\sigma (u):(\nabla u.\nabla \theta )-\Psi (\varepsilon (u))div\theta \right]d\Omega$ *).*
- *Do not forget that the loadings applied beyond* ${R}_{\sup}$ *have a zero contribution in the fracture-mechanics post-processings. This can be useful if one applies an unsupported loading such as* *FORCE_NODALE,* *DDL_IMPO (in 2D) or* *FACE_IMPO (in 3D).*

**Advice on the choice of the radii** ${R}_{\inf}$ **and** ${R}_{\sup}$ **:** the calculation of the fracture-mechanics quantities is theoretically independent of the choice of the integration crown (in the absence of loading on the lips, body force or thermal). Nevertheless it is preferable to respect a few rules:

- never take ${R}_{\inf}$ or too small with respect to the dimensions of the problem, because the singular displacements are poorly computed in the neighbourhood of the crack front (cf. note above);
- the upper radius ${R}_{\sup}$ may be as large as one wishes provided that the crown thus defined is contained in the solid. In 3D, one must not take a radius ${R}_{\sup}$ which is too large, otherwise the direction of the theta field (computed by projection onto the crack front) may be inaccurate;
- the choice of the radii ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ is independent of the topology of the mesh. However, if the mesh is radiating at the crack tip, it is recommended to take integration crowns coincident with the crowns of the mesh (reduction of the oscillations of $G$ along the crack front in 3D);
- in thermo-elastoplasticity, one uses a crack as a notch. One will make sure that the lower radius ${R}_{\inf}$ is indeed greater than the radius of the notch.
- Take several consecutive crowns in order to check [R1,R2], [R2,R3], [R3, R4],...

To fix ideas, for a crack of length *a* in an infinite medium, the size of the elements at the crack front must be less than $a/10$ in order to obtain a reasonable error (of the order of a % on $\mathrm{K1}$ ). For the integration crowns, one then generally takes ${R}_{\inf}$ of the order of 1 to 3 times the size of the elements in the neighbourhood of the front; and ${R}_{\sup}$ of 3 to 7 times the size of the elements.

An automatic determination of ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ is possible (these keywords are optional). If they are not indicated, they are computed automatically from the maximum $h$ of the sizes of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the crack front. It has been chosen to take ${R}_{\sup}=4h$ and ${R}_{\inf}=2h$ . If one chooses the automatically computed value for ${R}_{\inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ , one should however make sure that these values (printed in the .mess file) are consistent with the dimensions of the structure.

#### 4.1.3 Problem of the discretisation in 3D

In 3D, for a current node of the crack front, the propagation direction is defined as being the average of the normals to the segment mesh cells of the crack front to its left and to its right. For the end nodes, the normal is computed from a single mesh cell, and may therefore be less accurate, or even wrong.

**Crack breaking the surface orthogonally to the edges:** the propagation-direction vectors at the origin and at the end of the front are computed automatically by taking the edges of the structure into account. The keywords DTAN_ORIG and DTAN_EXTR (command DEFI_FOND_FISS for the calculation of G on a meshed crack) which make it possible to impose the direction of these vectors therefore do not need to be specified.

**Crack breaking the surface in a non-perpendicular manner:** at the surface-breaking end of the crack front, the field $\theta$ cannot simultaneously be normal to the edge to which it belongs (in the plane tangent to the crack lips) and satisfy the condition $\theta .n=0$ on $\partial \Omega$ .

The recommended solution is to impose as propagation direction at the ends (keywords DTAN_ORIG and DTAN_EXTR) the average of the normal to the crack front at this point ${\theta}_{1}$ and of the tangent to the edge ${\theta}_{2}$ .

### 4.2 Interpolation methods in 3D

#### 4.2.1 General framework

The local energy release rate $G(s)$ is the solution of the variational equation

To solve this equation, one decomposes the scalar field $G(s)$ on a basis which we denote ${({p}_{j}(s))}_{1\le j\le N}$ . Let ${G}_{j}$ be the components of $G(s)$ in this basis:

one gives oneself a basis of test functions for $\theta$ by choosing $P$ independent fields ${\theta}^{i}$ for the trace of the field $\theta$ on the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ : ${(\stackrel{ˉ}{{\theta}^{i}}(s))}_{1\le i\le P}$ .

The $G_j$ are determined by solving the linear system with $P$ and $N$ unknowns:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N} a_{ij} G_j = b_i, \quad i=1, P
$$

with:

$$
\begin{cases}
   a_{ij} = \sum_{k=1}^{M} \theta_k^i \int_{\Gamma_O} p_j(s) q_k(s) \cdot m(s) \, \mathrm{d}s \\
   b_i = G(\theta^i)
\end{cases}
$$

This system has a solution if one chooses $P$ independent fields $\theta^i$ such that $P \geq N$ and $M \geq N$.
It may comprise more equations than unknowns, in which case it is solved in the least-squares sense.

#### 4.2.2 Methods of discretisation of G

In *Code_Aster*, two families of bases have been chosen (cf. [§2.2]):

- the LEGENDRE polynomials ${\gamma}_{j}(s)$ of degree $j$ ($0=j=7$ ),
- The shape functions of node $k$ of $\Gamma_0$ : $\Phi_k(s)$,
with $1 \leq k \leq \text{NNO}$ (number of nodes of $\Gamma_0$).
They are of degree 1 for the linear elements and of degree 2 for the quadratic elements.

These families of bases and the linear systems to be solved are described precisely in the reference documentation [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01). The use of the shape functions is called 'LINEAIRE' in *Code_Aster*.

#### 4.2.3 Notes and advice

- **Choice of the method:** it is difficult to give a preference to one or the other method. In principle the two give equivalent numerical results. Nevertheless the LINEAIRE method (by default) is a little more costly in CPU time than the LEGENDRE method. It is **indispensable to use several methods** and to compare the results, in order to confirm the validity of the model.
- **Choice of the maximum degree of the Legendre polynomials:** this choice depends on the number of nodes on the crack front. If one has a small number of nodes (about ten) it is useless to take a degree greater than 3 (one readily conceives that the results are poor if one tries to find a polynomial of degree 7 passing through 10 points). Beyond about twenty nodes on the crack front one may use degrees up to 7. Experience shows that the choice of a degree equal to 5 gives good results in most cases (cf. note below).
- **Case of closed cracks:** if the crack front is a closed curve, problems of continuity of the solution at the point arbitrarily chosen as the origin of the curvilinear abscissa prohibit the use of Legendre polynomials. If the crack front has been declared “closed” in DEFI_FOND_FISS, one must use the shape functions.
- **Problem of non-respect of the symmetry:** if one models only half of the solid with respect to the crack, one must in principle have a curve $G(s)$ whose tangent slope is zero at the symmetry interface. This is not respected by the two methods. The values of $G(s)$ obtained at the ends of the crack front must always be interpreted with caution, especially if the crack breaks the surface in a non-perpendicular manner (see § 4.1.3).
- **Case of free meshes:** In this case, it is recommended to use the operand NB_POINT_FOND of CALC_G. The choice of a ratio of the order of 5 between the total number of points on the crack front and the number of calculation points seems appropriate. A choice of 20 points on the crack front is often judicious.
- **Performance:** the operand NB_POINT_FOND can also be used in order to reduce the run time of CALC_G.

### 4.3 Advice for the calculations with POST_K1_K2_K3

**Choice of the extrapolation distance:** the extrapolation distance ABSC_CURV_MAXI is the only user parameter. In general, this distance is chosen equal to 3 to 5 times the size of the elements in the neighbourhood of the crack front. In the case of a meshed crack, the parameter ABSC_CURV_MAXI is optional. The default value is then equal to $4h$ where $h$ is the maximum size of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the crack front.

**Case of meshed cracks:** the mesh must preferably be quadratic and comprise enough nodes perpendicularly to the crack front. Furthermore, the results are markedly improved if, in the case where the mesh is composed of quadratic elements, one moves the mid-side nodes (of the edges which touch the crack front) to the quarter of these edges, bringing them closer to the crack front. This is made possible by the keyword MODI_MAILLE (option 'NOEUD_QUART') of the command MODI_MAILLAGE [U4.23.04](doc:u4.23.04).

**Case of non-meshed cracks:** the accuracy of the method is sensitive to the choice of the enrichment zone of the X-FEM method (parameter RAYON_ENRI of DEFI_FISS_XFEM). Ideally, the enrichment radius and the maximum curvilinear abscissa ABSC_CURV_MAXI are of the order of three times the size of the minimum edge of the mesh.

**Performance:** in the X-FEM case, the calculations are fairly consuming in time and in memory if there are many points on the crack front. The use of the keyword NB_POINT_FOND makes it possible to limit the post-processing to a certain number of points equally distributed along the front.

### 4.4 Normalisation, symmetries

#### 4.4.1 2D plane stress and plane strain

In dimension 2 (plane stress and plane strain), the crack front is reduced to a point and the value $G(\theta )$ coming from the command CALC_G is independent of the choice of the field $\theta$ :

$G=G(\theta )\forall \theta \in \Theta$

#### 4.4.2 3D

In dimension 3, the value of $G(\theta )$ for a given field $\theta$ is such that:

$g(\theta )={\int}_{{\Gamma}_{0}}G(s)\theta (s).m(s)\mathrm{ds}$

By default, the direction of the field $\theta$ on the crack front is the normal to the crack front in the plane of the lips. By choosing a field $\theta$ of unit value in the neighbourhood of the crack front, one then has:

$\theta (s).m(s)=1$

and:

$G(\theta )={\int}_{{\Gamma}_{0}}G(s)\mathrm{ds}$

## 5 Conclusions

The points to remember are the following:

- The study of a crack in elasticity (linear or not) can be carried out on a meshed crack as on a non-meshed crack. In both cases the mesh must be sufficiently fine in the neighbourhood of the crack front in order to capture correctly the singularity of the stresses.
- The calculations are possible on a non-planar crack, but the user must take care that it remains sufficiently regular for the calculation assumptions to be valid: one must not have a geometric singularity on the front or on the lips. Typically, the calculation is licit for an axisymmetric crack, but not for a corner.
- In linear elasticity, several operators (CALC_G, POST_K1_K2_K3) and several methods (of extrapolation, of smoothing, ...) are available. It is **indispensable** to compare the results of the different methods and operators in order to make sure of the good quality of the model. It is also recommended to evaluate the sensitivity of the result to the choice of the parameters of the operators (integration radii) and to the refinement of the mesh.
