# v6.04.221 SSNV221 – Hydrostatic test with linear and parabolic DRUCK_PRAGER behavior

**Summary:**

The test case offers a purely hydrostatic loading for the Drücker-Prager law associated with [R7.01.16](doc:r7.01.16). The formulation of this plastic law, often used for soils, is made both on the deviatoric and hydrostatic part; nevertheless, the criterion surface has a singularity for a purely hydrostatic stress state. This analytical test case is used to verify correct hardening in this singularity.

The test is performed on a hardware point with the SIMU_POINT_MAT command. We work with imposed deformations.

One test is performed with linear nutring (modelling A) and another with parabolic nutring (modelling B).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Material properties

  - *Elastics*:

$E=3000\mathrm{MPa}$     Young's modulus

$\nu =0,25$         Poisson's ratio

*DRUCK_PRAGER* *linear(modelization A):*

$\alpha =0,20$ Pressure Dependence Coefficient

${p}_{\mathrm{ultm}}=0,04$ Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation

${\sigma}_{Y}=6\mathrm{MPa}$ Plastic Constraint

$h=100\mathrm{MPa}$ Nutrient module

*DRUCK_PRAGER* *parabolic(modelization B):*

$\alpha =0,20$ Pressure Dependence Coefficient

${p}_{\mathrm{ultm}}=0,04$ Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation

${\sigma}_{Y}=6\mathrm{MPa}$ Plastic Constraint

${\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}=10\mathrm{MPa}$ Ultimate plastic constraint

### 1.2 Loadings and boundary conditions

The load is not monotonous: first, it is charged to the ${\varepsilon}_{v1}$ density deformation, by exceeding the plasticization threshold, then it is discharged to a zero deformation level; then it is charged to the ${\varepsilon}_{v2}$ deformation, thus exceeding the ultimate cumulative plastic deformation ${p}_{\mathrm{ultm}}$, above which it is found to be perfect plasticity; it is still discharged at zero stress (deformation equal to the plastic deformation ${\varepsilon}_{v2}^{p}$) and it is reloaded later until the ${\varepsilon}_{v3}$ deformation. The loading time (see ) is fictitious because the plastic laws are time independent.

## 2 reference solution

The Modelling checks the behaviour of the law with linear hardening.

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The equations that interest us for the analytical calculation are (${I}_{1}=\text{tr}(\sigma )$: stress tensor trace, ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}$: density plastic deformation):

- Plastic constitutive law on the volumetric part:

$$
{I}_{1}=\mathrm{3K}({\varepsilon}_{v}-{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})
$$

- criterion surface, by posing zero stress of Von Bets (${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}=0$ ) :

$$
F(\sigma ,p)=\alpha {I}_{1}-R(p)
$$

- relationship between volume plastic deformation and cumulative plastic deformation (internal variable of the plastic law):

$$
\dot{{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}=3\alpha \dot{p}
$$

- expression of the nutwork

  - Linear:

$$
\begin{array}{}R(p)={\sigma}_{Y}+hp\mathrm{si}p\le {p}_{\mathrm{ult}}\\ R(p)={\sigma}_{Y}+h{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}={\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}\mathrm{si}p>{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}\end{array}
$$

  - parabolic:

$$
\begin{array}{}R(p)={\sigma}_{Y}{(1-(1-\sqrt{\frac{{\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{{\sigma}_{Y}}})\frac{p}{{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}})}^{2}\mathrm{si}p\le {p}_{\mathrm{ult}}\\ R(p)={\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}\mathrm{si}p>{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}\end{array}
$$

It is observed that, as in the linear case, $R(p)={\sigma}_{Y}$ if $p=0$ and one has perfect plasticity if $p>{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}$ .

#### 2.1.1 Deformation at initial elastic limit

This deformation is obtained for ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}=p=0$ .

If $F(\sigma ,p)=0$ is installed (plastic evolution) we have:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{R(p)}{\alpha}=\frac{{\sigma}_{Y}}{\alpha}$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{el}}=\frac{{I}_{1}^{\mathrm{el}}}{3K}$

#### 2.1.2 Ultimate Deformation

The ultimate deformation ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{ult}}$ is called the one obtained for $p={p}_{\mathrm{ult}}$ .

The ${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}$ stress trace and the corresponding ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{pult}}$ plastic deformation are easily found:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}=\frac{R(p)}{\alpha}=\frac{{\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{\alpha}$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{pult}}=3\alpha {p}_{\mathrm{ult}}$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{ult}}=\frac{{I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{3K}+{\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{pult}}$

#### 2.1.3 Deformation between the elastic limit and the ultimate deformation

The cumulative plastic deformation is calculated first.

- By combining the equations , , and with $F(\sigma ,p)=0$ for **linear hardening** we have:

$$
p=\frac{3KA{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v1}}-{\sigma}_{Y}}{9K{\alpha}^{2}+h}
$$

- Combining the equations , , and with $F(\sigma ,p)=0$ for the **parabolic hardening** we arrive at the equation of degrade 2:

$$
{A}_{1}{\stackrel{ˉ}{p}}^{2}+{B}_{1}\stackrel{ˉ}{p}+{C}_{1}=0
\begin{array}{}{A}_{1}={\sigma}_{Y}{(1-\gamma )}^{2}\\ {B}_{1}=9K{\alpha}^{2}{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}-2{\sigma}_{Y}(1-\gamma )\\ {C}_{1}={\sigma}_{Y}-3K\alpha {\varepsilon}_{v1}\\ \gamma =\sqrt{\frac{{\sigma}_{Y}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{{\sigma}_{Y}}}\\ \stackrel{ˉ}{p}=\frac{p}{{p}_{\mathrm{ult}}}\end{array}
$$

The equations are then used and to find ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}$ plastic deformation and ${I}_{1}$ stress trace.

If the material is discharged elastically until zero stress, there is a residual deformation equal to the plastic deformation; however, the compression material must be charged to obtain a total zero deformation. The second branch is also elastic, as the Drücker-Prager material cannot plasticize in hydrostatic compression. In the latter case, the trace of the stresses, negative, is:

$$
{I}_{1}^{c}=-3K{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}
$$

#### 2.1.4 Deformation greater than ultimate deformation

All the quantities of interest are easily found, as the stress trace is known a priori and equal to ${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}$.

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}={\varepsilon}_{v}-\frac{{I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{3K}$

$p=\frac{{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3\alpha }$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The $K$ compressibility module is:

$K=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu )}=2000\mathrm{MPa}$

#### 2.2.1 Deformation at the elastic limit

For both modellings you can easily find:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{el}}=30\mathrm{MPa}$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{el}}=0,005$

#### 2.2.2 Ultimate Deformation

For both modellings you can find:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}=50\mathrm{MPa}$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{pult}}=0,024$

${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{ult}}\approx 0,03233$

#### 2.2.3 Deformation of 0.018 and zero deformation discharge

This ${\varepsilon}_{v1}=0,018$ deformation value is greater than the ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{el}}$ elastic limit and less than ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{ult}}$. First the plastic deformation accumulated with the equations and then the plastic deformation and stress trace is calculated:

- Linear nutring:

${p}_{1}=\frac{3KA{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v1}}-{\sigma}_{Y}}{9K{\alpha}^{2}+h}\approx 0,019$

${\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p}=3\alpha {p}_{1}=0,0114$

${I}_{1}^{1}=3K({\varepsilon}_{v1}-{\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p})\approx 39,51\mathrm{MPa}$

- Parabolic nutring:

${p}_{1}\approx 0.0192$

${\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p}=3\alpha {p}_{1}\approx 0.0115$

${I}_{1}^{1}=3K({\varepsilon}_{v1}-{\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p})\approx 38.956\mathrm{MPa}$

The trace of zero deformation stresses is:

- Linear nutring:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{1c}}=-3K{\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p}\approx -68,49\mathrm{MPa}$

- Parabolic nutring:

${I}_{1}^{\mathrm{1c}}=-3K{\varepsilon}_{v1}^{p}\approx -69,044\mathrm{MPa}$

Indeed, the difference between parabolic and linear cases is very small.

#### 2.2.4 Loading up to deformation of 0.045 and 0.06

The material is charged to the deformation values ${\varepsilon}_{v2}=0,045$ and ${\varepsilon}_{v3}=0,06$ , above ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{\mathrm{ult}}$ .

The results are the same for both modellings.

For ${\varepsilon}_{v2}=0,045$:

${\varepsilon}_{v2}^{p}={\varepsilon}_{v2}-\frac{{I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{3K}\approx 0,03667$

${p}_{2}=\frac{{\varepsilon}_{v2}^{p}}{3\alpha }\approx 0,0611$

Following the elastic discharge (up to zero stress), we find ${\varepsilon}_{v}={\varepsilon}_{v2}^{p}$ , $p={p}_{2}$ .

For ${\varepsilon}_{v3}=0,06$:

${\varepsilon}_{v3}^{p}={\varepsilon}_{v3}-\frac{{I}_{1}^{\mathrm{ult}}}{3K}\approx 0,051667$

${p}_{3}=\frac{{\varepsilon}_{v3}^{p}}{3\alpha }\approx 0,0861$
