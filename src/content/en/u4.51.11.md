# u4.51.11 Non-linear constitutive behaviours

## 1 Purpose

This document describes the non-linear constitutive behaviours introduced by means of the keyword COMPORTEMENT in the non-linear analysis operators: STAT_NON_LINE, DYNA_NON_LINE, SIMU_POINT_MAT, CALCUL

For each constitutive behaviour, the fields of application, the keywords defining the material parameters, the content of the internal variables and the supported modellings are specified.

## 2 Syntax

The syntax of the keyword COMPORTEMENT varies slightly according to the command in which
it is used. Refer to the *Syntax* paragraph of the command under consideration.

### 3.2 Internal variables

The internal variables are described briefly in this document for each constitutive behaviour. The detail of their meaning is provided in the specific reference documents of these constitutive behaviours. The name of the internal variables is nevertheless visible in the "messages" file when STAT_NON_LINE / DYNA_NON_LINE is run.

Selecting the correct internal-variable number (V1, V2, V3, etc.) is impractical in use and becomes very difficult when several constitutive behaviours are mixed. That is why certain commands make it possible to use their named version by means of the keyword NOM_VARI: CALC_CHAMP, CREA_TABLE, IMPR_RESU, POST_CHAMP, POST_ELEM, RECU_FONCTION and TEST_RESU.

In addition, when a result is printed in MED format (IMPR_RESU), a named field (VARI_ELGA_NOMME) is also produced and makes it possible to display these internal variables easily.

**Notes**:

- The internal variable named "plasticity indicator" indicates that plasticity was created during the time step of the analysis and at the current Gauss point, and not over the whole transient;
- The constitutive laws (keyword RELATION) are not the only parameters that create internal variables. The choice of the strain (keyword DEFORMATION='SIMO_MIEHE' for example), but also the keyword POST_ITER and the use of the De Borst algorithm also use internal variables.
- The use of UMAT to program constitutive laws does not make it possible to use the naming of the internal variables.
- The naming of the internal variables is possible with MFront but this does not yet work on restart (POURSUITE).
- Constitutive laws of polycrystalline type do not name their internal variables either.
- Constitutive laws of metallurgical type (META_* ) have a specific naming system, explained in the appendix.

## 4 Keyword COMPORTEMENT

This factor keyword makes it possible to define the constitutive relations.

Most constitutive laws (in particular in plasticity) are written in incremental form, because the history of the material influences its constitutive behaviour; if this is not the case, one is dealing with elastic constitutive behaviours, linear or not. One can have, in the same analysis, certain parts of the structure obeying incremental constitutive behaviours, and other parts obeying various elastic constitutive behaviours.

It is the constitutive behaviour which determines (by means of its catalogue) the type of integration used. For example, the constitutive behaviours CABLE, ELAS_HYPER, ELAS_POUTRE_GR, ELAS_VMIS_LINE, ELAS_VMIS_TRAC, ELAS_VMIS_PUIS are integrated in an elastic (non-linear) manner and not incrementally. As regards the constitutive behaviour ELAS, both types of integration are possible

For the precise meaning of these various relations one should refer to the various Reference documentations as well as to the documentation of DEFI_MATERIAU[U4.43.01].

### 4.1 Modelling of plane stresses by the Borst method

Certain constitutive models have not been developed in plane stress. In that case, one automatically uses the De Borst algorithm [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03), which makes it possible to take the plane-stress assumption into account at the level of the equilibrium algorithm (unlike constitutive models developed explicitly in plane stress, which take this assumption into account at the level of the integration of the constitutive laws). One can therefore also assign any non-linear law to the structural elements DKT, COQUE_3D and TUYAU *). There again, it is necessary to use only the tangent matrix.*

Likewise, for cases using a one-dimensional stress state (POU_D_EM, POU_D_TGM, GRILLE, GRILLE_MEMBRANE, BARRE), in order to be able to use the constitutive behaviours which have not been developed specifically in 1D, one automatically uses a method similar to that of De Borst to integrate in 1D the constitutive behaviours available in 3D [R5.03.09](doc:r5.03.09).

The De Borst method is available neither for the metallurgical constitutive behaviours nor with DEFORMATION = 'SIMO_MIEHE'.

When one uses *MFront*, the De Borst mode is triggered automatically if the law has not been written in plane stress. If *MFront* is used in "prototype" mode (keyword RELATION='MFRONT'), it is up to the user to choose the mode of operation (native plane stress in *MFront* or by the De Borst algorithm).

### 4.2 Local and non-local modelling

In the case of softening constitutive behaviours, the response of a local constitutive model with damage depends on the mesh. To overcome this difficulty, certain models can be used in non-local form. Any model written in non-local form leads to the introduction of an additional material characteristic, the characteristic length. For certain models, it is defined under the factor keyword NON_LOCAL of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU.

The response of a non-local modelling is more independent of the mesh. There are two types of non-local laws, which can be activated in AFFE_MODELE by the keyword MODELISATION:

- '3D_GRAD_VARI', 'D_PLAN_GRAD_VARI' or 'AXIS_GRAD_VARI'. These are non-local laws in which the gradient of the internal variables of the local model appears (see [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01)).
- 'D_PLAN_2DG', 'D_PLAN_DIL' in addition to the model to be regularised (see [R5.04.03](doc:r5.04.03)). This is a model regularised by a microstructural approach in which either the strain field or the volumetric strain appears.

### 4.3 Constitutive laws and command variables

As a reminder, the model may include one or more command variables (temperature, drying, irradiation, metallurgical phase, ...), whose field is assigned to the mesh cells of the mesh via the keyword AFFE_VARC of the command AFFE_MATERIAU (cf. [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03)).

A command variable can affect the material properties, which are then a function of it.

In certain cases, they can in addition generate a strain. This is the case for the following command variables: the temperature 'TEMP', the drying 'SECH' (one then speaks of desiccation-shrinkage strain), the hydration 'HYDR' (endogenous shrinkage strain) and the anelastic strains 'EPSAXX', 'EPSAYY', 'EPSAZZ', 'EPSAXY', 'EPSAXZ', 'EPSAXZ'. These strains are often called "thermal strains" by an abuse of language.

Depending on the way in which the constitutive law was developed, these strains due to the command variables are taken into account by means of a generic mechanism. For other laws, they are taken into account in a specific manner.

At present, the generic mechanism of code_aster covers the following scope:

- computation and taking into account of the thermal strain with an isotropic / anisotropic / transversely isotropic thermal expansion coefficient ALPHA, and differentiable according to the metallurgical phase in the isotropic case;
- computation and taking into account of desiccation shrinkage and endogenous shrinkage in the case where the material parameters controlling them, K_DESSIC and B_ENDOGE, are isotropic;
- taking into account of the anelastic strains.

This generic mechanism is available only for strain measures of small-strain type via the operand DEFORMATION (all except "SIMO_MIEHE", that is "PETIT", "PETIT_REAC", "GDEF_LOG", "GREEN_LAGRANGE" and in principle "GROT_GDEP" – however it is not available for this last kinematics). This means, for example, that a command variable such as the temperature cannot be used at the same time as a "SIMO_MIEHE" or "GROT_GDEP" kinematics with the generic mechanism.

It will be noted that laws of MFront and UMAT type follow the generic mechanism.

### 4.4 Operand RELATION

#### 4.4.1 Elastic models

Unless otherwise indicated, all the models can include a dependence on temperature. In addition, they are all integrated in a purely implicit manner.

##### 4.4.1.1 Constitutive behaviours ELAS, ELAS_ISTR, ELAS_ORTH and ELAS_GLRC

"Linear" elastic constitutive relation, that is to say that the relation between the strains and the stresses considered is linear. Under certain conditions this relation becomes incremental: it then makes it possible to take initial displacements and stresses into account; the constitutive behaviour ELAS is therefore by default non-incremental, except in the following cases:

- if an initial state exists (ETAT_INIT, SIGM_INIT)
- if DEFORMATION=PETIT_REAC
- if the command is CALCUL.

If need be, if these exceptions are not sufficient, one can force an incremental elastic constitutive behaviour by using ELAS_ISOT_INCR. Likewise one can force a hyperelasticity by opting for ELAS_ISOT_ENER.

The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the following keywords:

- ELAS(_FO), as regards isotropic elasticity,
- ELAS_ISTR(_FO), as regards transversely isotropic elasticity,
- ELAS_ORTH(_FO), as regards orthotropic elasticity.
- ELAS_GLRC(_FO), as regards the elasticity of the plate elements DKTG and Q4GG.

The material parameters defined under ELAS are used for a certain number of constitutive behaviours, and also for the computation of the elastic stiffness matrix (PREDICTION='ELASTIQUE' or MATRICE='ELASTIQUE' under the keyword NEWTON cf. [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)).

- Supported modellings: 3D, 2D, CONT_PLAN, DISCRET, INCO_UPG, INCO_UP, POU_* , CONT_1D, CONT_1D(PMF), SHB, CABLE, CABLE_POULIE, COQUE_3D, DKTG, Q4GG.
- Number of internal variables: 1
- Meaning: $\mathit{V1}$ : empty and therefore always equal to zero

##### 4.4.1.2 Constitutive behaviours ELAS_ISOT_ENER

Linear isotropic elastic constitutive relation derived from an energy (hyperelasticity).
The relation between stresses and strains is functional, with no history effect.
The constitutive model is operational in 1D, 2D (including plane stress) and 3D.
The model relies on no internal variable (in practice, there is one, VIDE, which remains zero).

##### 4.4.1.3 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_ISOT_INCR

Incremental isotropic elastic constitutive relation.
This version of elasticity integrates a history effect, without however corresponding to a hypoelastic formulation.
More precisely, one denotes by $\varepsilon^m$ the mechanical strain (the total strain from which one subtracts the strain arising from the command variables), $\sigma$ the stress and $C$ the isotropic Hooke tensor.
The incremental formulation relies on an intermediate strain $a$ and is written as follows:

$$
\begin{split}\left. \begin{array}{l}
\sigma= C : a \\
\dot a = \dot \epsilon^m \\
a_0 = C^{-1}: \sigma_0
\end{array}
\right.\end{split}
$$

It therefore depends on the initial stress and on the initial mechanical strain.
The constitutive model is operational in 1D, 2D (including plane stress) and 3D.
The model relies on no internal variable (in practice, there is one, VIDE, which remains zero).

##### 4.4.1.4 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_HYPER

"Non-linear" hyperelastic constitutive relation, that is to say that the relation between the stresses is the derivative of a hyperelastic potential with respect to the Green strains. The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords ELAS_HYPER. This relation is supported only in HPP and in Green-Lagrange kinematics (DEFORMATION='PETIT' or 'GREEN_LAGRANGE').

- Supported modellings: 3D, D_PLAN
- Example: see test SSNV187
- Reference documentation: R5.03.19

**Note:** this constitutive behaviour cannot take strains of thermal origin into account.

##### 4.4.1.5 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_HYPER_VISC

"Non-linear" visco-hyperelastic constitutive relation. The hyperelastic part is defined by the Signorini strain potential (the same as ELAS_HYPER). The viscous flow is taken into account by adding a Prony series of order n to the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor PK2. The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords ELAS_HYPER_VISC. This relation is supported only in HPP and in Green-Lagrange kinematics (DEFORMATION='PETIT' or 'GREEN_LAGRANGE').

- Supported modellings: 3D, D_PLAN
- Example: see tests SSNV518 and SSNV519
- Reference documentation: R5.03.19

##### 4.4.1.6 Constitutive behaviour HYPER_HILL

Compressible "non-linear" hyperelastic constitutive relation. The law is defined by the Hill strain potential. The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords HYPER_HILL. This relation is supported only in HPP and in Green-Lagrange kinematics (DEFORMATION='PETIT' or 'GREEN_LAGRANGE').

- Supported modellings: 3D, D_PLAN
- Example: see test SSNV520
- Reference documentation: R5.03.19

##### 4.4.1.7 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_VMIS_LINE

"Non-linear" elastic constitutive relation (Hencky's law) of von Mises type with linear isotropic hardening. The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords ECRO_LINE and ELAS (see [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03) for more details of its use in fracture mechanics).

- Supported modellings: 3D, 2D, C_PLAN
- Example: see test SSNP110
- Reference documentation: R5.03.20

**Note:** this constitutive behaviour cannot be used with a non-zero initial stress state. It must therefore not be used on restart.

##### 4.4.1.8 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_VMIS_TRAC

"Non-linear" elastic constitutive relation (Hencky's law) of von Mises type with non-linear isotropic hardening defined by a tensile curve. The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords TRACTION and ELAS (see [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03) for more details of its use in fracture mechanics).

- Supported modellings: 3D, 2D and C_PLAN
- Example: see test SSNV108
- Reference documentation: R5.03.20

**Note:** this constitutive behaviour cannot be used with a non-zero initial stress state. It must therefore not be used on restart.

##### 4.4.1.9 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_VMIS_PUIS

"Non-linear" elastic constitutive relation (Hencky's law) of von Mises type with non-linear isotropic hardening defined by a power function. The parameters are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords ECRO_PUIS and ELAS.

- Supported modellings: 3D, 2D
- Example: see test COMP001I
- Reference documentation: R5.03.20

**Note:** this constitutive behaviour cannot be used with a non-zero initial stress state. It must therefore not be used on restart.

##### 4.4.1.10 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_POUTRE_GR

Elastic constitutive relation for beams in large displacements and large rotations (DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP' is mandatory). The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword ELAS or ELAS_FO (see [R5.03.40](doc:r5.03.40) for more detail).

- Supported modellings: POU_D_T_GD
- Internal variables (of no interest to the user, but necessary for operation): 3
- Example: see test SSNL103

##### 4.4.1.11 Constitutive behaviour CABLE

Elastic constitutive relation adapted to cables (DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP' mandatory): the Young's modulus of the cable can be different in compression and in tension (in particular it can be zero in compression). The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword CABLE (see [R3.08.02](doc:r3.08.02) for more details).

- Supported modellings: CABLE
- Example: see test HSNL100

##### 4.4.1.12 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_MEMBRANE_SV

Saint-Venant–Kirchhoff hyperelastic constitutive relation adapted to membranes (DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP' mandatory, see [R3.08.07](doc:r3.08.07) for more details). The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword ELAS. This relation cannot include a dependence on temperature.

- Supported modellings: MEMBRANE
- Example: see test SSNS115

##### 4.4.1.13 Constitutive behaviour ELAS_MEMBRANE_NH

Neo-Hookean hyperelastic constitutive relation adapted to membranes (DEFORMATION= 'GROT_GDEP' mandatory, see [R3.08.07](doc:r3.08.07) for more details). The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword ELAS. This relation cannot include a dependence on temperature.

- Supported modellings: MEMBRANE
- Example: see test SSNS115

##### 4.4.1.14 Constitutive behaviour CZM_ELAS_MIX

Elastic cohesive constitutive relation, cf. [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11), modelling the elastic opening and sliding of an interface. This law can be used with the interface finite element based on a mixed augmented-Lagrangian formulation (see [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13)) and makes it possible to introduce a cohesive force across the interface in opening and sliding mode. This law can in particular be used to check the data setup of a study before moving on to an interface damage model (cracking). The necessary data are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword CZM_ELAS. One can also choose to apply perfect-adherence conditions in the normal and/or tangential direction (the corresponding stiffnesses are then without effect, perfect adherence corresponding to an infinite stiffness). Finally, one can also adopt a unilateral constitutive behaviour in the normal direction: contact on closure, elasticity on opening. In this last configuration, the model becomes non-linear.

- Supported modellings: all modellings of INTERFACE type (cf. U3.13.14).
- Number of internal variables (for post-processing purposes): 3

V1: normal displacement jump, V2: tangential jump in the first direction, V3: tangential jump in the second direction (zero in 2D).

- Examples: see test SSLV01.

##### 4.4.1.15 Constitutive behaviour INTERF_POU_ELAS

Elastic constitutive relation applying to the soil–pile interaction elements (see [R7.01.49](doc:r7.01.49)). This relation describes the interaction forces and stiffness as a function of the relative displacement between soil and pile at the interface. There is no reference documentation for this law. Its interpretation is trivial from the reference documentation describing the elastoplastic interaction law INTERF_POU_CINE (see [R7.01.50](doc:r7.01.50)).

- Supported modellings: 3D_INTERF_POU
- Examples: see test SSNX103

##### 4.4.2.4 Constitutive behaviour VMIS_ISOT_NL

Von Mises elastoplastic constitutive relation with non-linear isotropic hardening (combination of an affine term, two exponential terms and a power term). The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) under the keywords ECRO_NL(_FO) and ELAS(_FO) (see [R5.03.33](doc:r5.03.33)).

The normalisation is as follows: a piloting increment of $\Delta \tau=\frac{\Delta t}{C}$ causes an increment of cumulated plastic strain of $\Delta \kappa = \Delta \tau \times \text{5\%}$ at a point of the structure.

- Supported local and GRAD_VARI modellings: 3D, D_PLAN, AXIS as well as 1D in local form.

Example: see test SSNV263.

Large strains of GDEF_LOG type are available for this constitutive behaviour.

- Compatible with the modellings GRAD_VARI and GRAD_INCO.

##### 4.4.2.17 Constitutive behaviour DIS_ECRO_TRAC

The constitutive behaviour DIS_ECRO_TRAC is a non-linear constitutive behaviour, making it possible to idealise the constitutive behaviour of a uniaxial device, along the local axis $x$ or in the tangential plane $yz$ of the discrete elements with two nodes (mesh cell SEG2) or of the discrete elements with one node (mesh cell POI1).

The non-linear constitutive behaviour is given by a curve $F=\mathit{fonction}(\Delta U)$ :

- for a SEG2, $\Delta U$ represents the relative displacement of the two nodes in the local frame of the element;
- for a POI1, $\Delta U$ represents the absolute displacement of the node in the local frame of the element;
- for a SEG2 or a POI1, $F$ represents the force expressed in the local frame of the element.

The only necessary datum is the function describing the non-linear constitutive behaviour. This function must meet the following criteria:

- it is a function in the sense of code_aster defined with the operator DEFI_FONCTION;
- the interpolations on the abscissa and ordinate axes are linear;
- the name of the abscissa when the function is defined is DX or DTAN;
- extensions to the left and to the right of the function are excluded;
- the function must be defined by at least three points in the case of isotropic hardening or by exactly three points in the case of kinematic hardening;
- the first point is $(\mathrm{0.0,}0.0)$ and must be supplied in the definition of the function;
- the function must be strictly increasing;
- the derivative of the function must be less than or equal to its derivative at the point $(\mathrm{0.0,0}.0)$ .

##### 4.4.2.25 Constitutive behaviour DIS_BILI_ELAS

The constitutive behaviour DIS_BILI_ELAS is used to model a bilinear elastic constitutive behaviour in translation. The constitutive law was designed to be used with all discrete elements.

The constitutive behaviour is characterised by 2 slopes and by a force which defines the break in slope. For each degree of freedom considered, the constitutive behaviour of the discrete element is either elastic or elastic-bilinear. If in one of the directions the bilinear constitutive behaviour is not defined, the constitutive behaviour in that direction is then elastic and it is the values given in the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM which are taken. The law DIS_BILI_ELAS concerns only the translational degrees of freedom; this therefore implies that the constitutive behaviour is elastic for the rotational degrees of freedom which exist for this discrete element. For each direction, the 3 characteristics (KDEB, KFIN, FPRE) are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword DIS_BILI_ELAS, see also [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17); they are necessarily given in the local frame of the element, so it is necessary in the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM under the factor keyword DISCRET to specify REPERE='LOCAL'. The quantities KDEB and KFIN are functions which depend on the temperature and can be defined in the form of a function, a nappe or a formula. The local frame is defined in the classical way in the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM under the factor keyword ORIENTATION.

There is one internal variable per translational degree of freedom. It can take 3 values:

◦    $V1=0$ , the discrete element has never been loaded in this direction.

◦    $V1=1$ , one is in the case where $\mid F\mid \le \text{FPREC}$

◦    $V1=2$ , one is in the case where $\mid F\mid >\text{FPREC}$

##### 4.4.3.16 Constitutive behaviour VISC_ISOT_PLAS

In the macro-commands [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03) ([MECA_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.04)), [DYNA_NON_LINE](doc:u4.53.01), [SIMU_POINT_MAT](doc:u4.51.12), [CALCUL](doc:u4.51.10) or [TEST_COMPOR](doc:u4.92.06), one uses the following syntax to define the constitutive behaviour VISC_ISOT_PLAS:

```text
⟐ COMPORTEMENT = _F(
    ◇ RELATION = / "VISC_ISOT_PLAS",
    ◇ DEFORMATION = / "PETIT" (par défaut),
                    / "GDEF_LOG",
    ◇ RESI_INTE = float (défaut: 5e-14),
    ◇ ITER_INTE_MAXI = int (défaut: 100),

⟐ : présent par défaut
◇ : optionnel
/ : un seul parmi
```

This is a constitutive relation arising from an isotropic elasto-viscoplastic flow model described in [R5.03.38](doc:r5.03.38), which models the multiscale effects of plasticity as well as those of irradiation. This modelling is mainly adopted to describe the mechanical constitutive behaviour of the steels of reactor vessels (bainitic) in the non-irradiated and irradiated state.

The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01) under the keyword VISC_ISOT_PLAS(_FO). It is recalled that Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio must also be entered under the keyword ELAS(_FO) in [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01).

By default, this constitutive relation is used under the small-strain assumption (keyword DEFORMATION = 'PETIT'). Large strains of type 'GDEF_LOG' are also available for this constitutive behaviour. Any other textual value entered for the optional operand DEFORMATION will cause a fatal error.

Note: in the case where 'GROT_GDEP', 'PETIT_REAC' or 'SIMO_MIEHE' is entered under the keyword DEFORMATION, an error message that is sometimes difficult to understand is displayed. In the case 'GREEN_LAGRANGE', no message is displayed when the run stops with a fatal error.

The constitutive relation VISC_ISOT_PLAS also necessarily requires the definition of a command variable of type 'TEMP' via the keyword AFFE_VARC in [AFFE_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.03) because the temperature is
used explicitly in the law.

In addition, the types of modellings supported for this law are '3D' (default value), 'AXIS', or 'D_PLAN'. This is done via the keyword MODELISATION in [AFFE_MODELE](doc:u4.41.01).

The constitutive relation VISC_ISOT_PLAS also defines between six and eight internal variables<u4.51.11-conventions_notation_variables_internes>, depending on the type of modelling chosen. It is also mentioned that the use of large strains of type 'GDEF_LOG' adds six other additional internal variables<u4.51.11-conventions_notation_variables_internes> corresponding to the stress tensor expressed in logarithmic space and denoted "T".

| V1 | EPSPEQ | Cumulated (visco)plastic strain, also called equivalent (visco)plastic strain (deviatoric), classically affected by the factor $\frac{2}{3}$ inside the square root |
| --- | --- | --- |
| V2 | INDIPLAS | (Visco)plasticity indicator equal to one in the (visco)plastic regime and zero in the elastic regime |
| V3 | ElasticStrainXX | First component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |
| V4 | ElasticStrainYY | Second component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |
| V5 | ElasticStrainZZ | Third component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |
| V6 | ElasticStrainXY | Fourth component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |
| V7 | ElasticStrainXZ | Fifth component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |
| V8 | ElasticStrainYZ | Sixth component of the elastic strain tensor in Voigt notation |

Note: In *Code_Aster*, the notation of symmetric second-order tensors on six vector components differs slightly from the usual Voigt notation. More precisely, the components 'XY' and 'YZ' are exchanged with respect to the Voigt notation usually used in mechanics. In addition, the factor two is not present for the terms 'XY', 'XZ' and 'YZ'.

Finally, the constitutive relation VISC_ISOT_PLAS solves locally (at each Gauss point) a non-linear equation. The optional operands RESI_INTE and ITER_INTE_MAXI are used to test the convergence of this iterative solution algorithm. The keyword RESI_INTE denotes the tolerance granted to the absolute value of the residual. It must be strictly positive and is equal to $5e^{-14}$ by default. The keyword ITER_INTE_MAXI refers to the maximum number of iterations allowed. It must be strictly positive and is equal to $100$ by default.

Note: The default values of RESI_INTE and ITER_INTE_MAXI have been calibrated for the constitutive relation VISC_ISOT_PLAS. They should normally not be modified. In other words, these two optional keywords do not have to be specified during a normal use of VISC_ISOT_PLAS. An alarm message is displayed if a value of RESI_INTE greater than $5 \, 10^{-14}$ is supplied by the user. In that case, the results obtained may be erroneous.

To finish, an example of use of the constitutive relation VISC_ISOT_PLAS is provided in the verification test case COMP004A [V6.07.104](doc:v6.07.104).

##### 4.4.4.1 Constitutive behaviour MONOCRISTAL

This model makes it possible to describe the constitutive behaviour of a single crystal whose constitutive relations are provided via the concept compor.

The number of internal variables depends on the choices made in DEFI_COMPOR:

The first six are the 6 components of the viscoplastic strain: ${E}_{ij}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ :

${E}^{\mathit{vp}}=\sum_{t}(\Delta {E}^{\mathit{vp}})$ with $\Delta {E}^{\mathit{vp}}=\sum_{s}{\mu}_{s}\Delta {\gamma}_{s}$

${V}_{1}={E}_{xx}^{\mathit{vp}}$ , ${V}_{2}={E}_{yy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{3}={E}_{zz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{4}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{5}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{6}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{yz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$

${V}_{7}$ , ${V}_{8}$ , ${V}_{9}$ are the values of ${\alpha}_{1}$ ${\gamma}_{1}$ ${p}_{1}$ for the slip system $s=1$

${V}_{10}$ , ${V}_{11}$ , ${V}_{12}$ correspond to the system $s=2$ , and so on, where:

- ${\alpha}_{s}$ represents the kinematic variable of the system $s$ in the case of phenomenological models, and the dislocation density in a model derived from DD;
- ${\gamma}_{s}$ represents the plastic slip of the system $s$
- ${p}_{1}$ represents the cumulated plastic slip of the system $s$

Taking irradiation into account:

- in the case DD_CC_IRRA, one must add ${n}_{\mathrm{irra}}=12$ internal variables: ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$  to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ contain, for each slip system, the density of dislocations linked to irradiation ${\rho}_{s}^{\mathrm{irr}}$
- in the case DD_CFC_IRRA, one must add ${n}_{\mathrm{irra}}=24$ internal variables: ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$  to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ contain, for each slip system, ${\rho}_{s}^{\mathrm{loops}}$ ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+13}$  to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+24}$ contain, for each slip system, ${\phi }_{s}^{\mathrm{voids}}$

One then stores the resolved shear stresses for each slip system: ${\tau}_{1}$ , … ${\tau}_{{n}_{s}}$

In the case where one takes into account the rotation of the crystal lattice, one must add ${n}_{\mathrm{rota}}=16$ internal variables:

- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+1}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+9}$ are the 9 components of the rotation matrix $Q$ ,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+10}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+12}$ are the 3 components of $\Delta {\omega}^{p}$ ,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+13}$ to ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+15}$ are the 3 components of $\Delta {\omega}^{e}$ ,
- ${V}_{6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+16}$ represents $\Theta$

The antepenultimate internal variable is the cleavage stress: $\underset{s}{\max}(\Sigma .n):n$

The penultimate internal variable contains the global cumulated plastic strain, defined by:

${V}_{p-1}=\sum\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ with $\Delta {E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{\mathit{vp}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(\Delta {\mathrm{E}}^{\mathit{vp}}:\Delta {\mathrm{E}}^{\mathit{vp}})}$

The last internal variable, $\mathit{Vp}$ , ($p=6+{\mathrm{3n}}_{s}+{n}_{\mathrm{rota}}+3$ , ${n}_{s}$ being the total number of slip systems) is a plasticity indicator (cf. Note 1) (threshold exceeded on at least one slip system at the current time step). If it is zero, there has been no increase of internal variables at the current time instant. Otherwise, it contains the number of local Newton iterations (for an implicit solution) which were necessary to obtain convergence.

For more details consult [R5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11).

- Supported modellings: 3D, 2D, C_PLAN (by DE BORST).
- Example: see test SSNV194

##### 4.4.4.2 Constitutive behaviour POLYCRISTAL

This model makes it possible to describe the constitutive behaviour of a polycrystal whose constitutive relations are provided via the concept compor, produced by DEFI_COMPOR.

The number of internal variables is $p=7+\mathrm{6m}+\sum_{g=1,m}({\mathrm{3n}}_{s}(g))+\mathrm{6m}+1$ , $m$ being the number of phases and ${n}_{s}(g)$ being the number of slip systems of the phase $g$ )

- The first six internal variables are the components of the macroscopic viscoplastic strain ${E}^{\mathit{vp}}$ :

${V}_{1}={E}_{xx}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{2}={E}_{yy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{3}={E}_{zz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{4}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xy}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{5}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{xz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ , ${V}_{6}=\sqrt{(2)}{E}_{yz}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ ;

- the seventh is the macroscopic cumulated equivalent viscoplastic strain $P$ :

${V}_{7}=\sum\Delta {E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{\mathrm{vp}}$ with $\Delta {E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{\mathit{vp}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}(\Delta {\mathrm{E}}^{\mathit{vp}}:\Delta {\mathrm{E}}^{\mathit{vp}})}$ ;

- then, for each phase, one finds the 6 components of the viscoplastic strains or of the tensor $\beta$ of the phase: ${\left\lbrace {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),{\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{xy}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{xz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\varepsilon}_{yz}^{\mathrm{vp}}(g)\right\rbrace }_{g=1,m}$ ;
- then, for each phase:

  - for each slip system of the phase, one finds the values of ${\alpha}_{s}$ ${\gamma}_{s}$ ${p}_{s}$ ;
  - in the case where the constitutive behaviour takes irradiation into account (currently MONO_DD_CC_IRRA), one must then add $12$ internal variables: the dislocation densities due to irradiation.
- then, for each phase, one finds the 6 components of the stresses of the phase: ${\left\lbrace {\sigma}_{xx}(g),{\sigma}_{yy}(g),{\sigma}_{zz}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{xy}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{xz}(g),\sqrt{(2)}{\sigma}_{yz}(g)\right\rbrace }_{g=1,m}$ ;
- the last internal variable is a plasticity indicator (cf. Note 1) (threshold exceeded on at least one slip system at the current time step).

For more details consult [R5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11).

- Supported modellings: 3D
- Example: see test SSNV171

##### 4.4.5.6 Constitutive behaviour DIS_GRICRA

The constitutive behaviour DIS_GRICRA makes it possible to model the connections between grids and rods of fuel assemblies. It is based on discrete elements with 2 nodes, with 6 degrees of freedom per node (translation+rotation). The constitutive law on each subsystem (sliding – axial friction, in-plane rotation, and out-of-plane rotation) is of plasticity type with positive hardening in the directions tangential to the discrete element in order to model sliding, and of unilateral elastic type in the direction of the discrete element in order to model contact. The parameters of DIS_GRICRA, characterising contact and friction, are directly rotational stiffnesses and rotational thresholds (of critical-angle type). These parameters are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword DIS_GRICRA. Unlike the other discrete elements, one does not take into account the stiffness characteristics of AFFE_CARA_ELEM. The stiffness matrix of the discrete element must therefore be taken as zero in AFFE_CARA_ELEM. The stiffness comes only from the parameters in DEFI_MATERIAU.

Unilateral contact takes place in the direction $X$ given by the mesh cell SEG2 of the discrete element, and sliding takes place in the direction $Y$ given by the keyword ORIENTATION of AFFE_CARA_ELEM (see [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17) for more details). The tangent matrix is symmetric.

- Supported modellings: DIS_TR
- Number of internal variables: 6
- $\mathrm{V1}$ : cumulated plastic displacement
- $\mathrm{V2}$ : contact/friction indicator (1 if sliding, 0 if no sliding)
- $\mathrm{V3}$ : indicator of separation in rotation
- $\mathrm{V4}$ : plastic angle (sliding)
- $\mathrm{V5}$ : cumulated plastic angle
- $\mathit{V6}$ : storage of the irradiation history (fluence)
- Example: see test SSNL131

##### 4.4.7.1 Constitutive behaviours ROUSSELIER, ROUSS_PR and ROUSS_VISC

**Note:**

*The following three models* *'ROUSSELIER' (elastoplastic model),* *'ROUSS_PR' (elastoplastic model) and* *'ROUSS_VISC' (elastoviscoplastic model) are three different versions of the Rousselier model. This model is an elasto(visco)plastic constitutive relation which makes it possible to account for the growth of cavities and to describe ductile fracture in steels. In the models* *'ROUSS_PR' and* *'ROUSS_VISC', when the porosity reaches a limit porosity, the material is considered fractured. For* *'ROUSSELIER', nothing particular is done because the stress tends naturally towards zero when the porosity tends towards one. The two previous parameters can be entered but have no impact on the model.*

##### 4.4.7.2 Constitutive behaviours GTN and VISC_GTN

The constitutive law entitled 'GTN' or 'VISC_GTN' for its viscoplastic version, named after its authors Gurson, Tvergaard and Needleman, is an elasto-visco-plastic model which accounts for the ductile damage of metals by describing the phases of nucleation, growth and coalescence of cavities.

By nature, this model relies on a large-strain kinematics, restricted to 'GDEF_LOG'.

Besides the local version of the model, a formulation with gradient of cumulated plastic strain (modelling '*_GRAD_VARI') is also available to control the localisation of strains, inherent in damage models. Finally, isochoric plastic strains are generally observed over a large range of the loading; the incompressibility problems which result from them may require the use of mixed finite elements displacement – pressure – swelling (modellings '*_INCO_UPG' in local form or '*_GRAD_INCO' in non-local form).

The data which characterise the material are provided in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), under the keywords ELAS, ECRO_NL, GTN and NON_LOCAL. This model is not developed in plane stresses, the latter not being compatible on the theoretical level with the pronounced gradients which result from the localisation of strains.

The internal variables number 25, to which is added the archiving of the stresses **T** specific to 'GDEF_LOG'. Their meaning is as follows:

- $V1$ : hardening variable
- $\mathrm{V2}$ : total porosity
- $\mathit{V3}$ : plasticity indicator (0 = elastic, 1 = regular plastic, 2 = vertex regime)
- $\mathit{V4}$ to $\mathit{V9}$ : components of the plastic strain
- $V10$ : cumulated plastic strain
- $V11$ : porosity resulting from nucleation
- $V12$ : total damage (combination of nucleation, growth and coalescence)
- $V13$ : damage rate
- $V14$ to $V19$ : components of the stresses **T**
- $V20$ : damage extrapolated for the computation of plasticity, before correction
- $V21$ : Difference between extrapolated and corrected damage, measured in terms of stresses (distance between the thresholds corresponding to each of these damage values)
- $V22$ : Contribution of hardening to the stresses
- $V23$ : Contribution of viscosity to the stresses
- $V24$ : Contribution of non-local interactions to the stresses
- $V25$ : free component available to the user / developer in post-processing

One may refer to the test cases SSNV250 to SSNV257 as well as SSNV265 and SSNV266 for examples of use.

##### 4.4.8.4 Constitutive behaviour ENDO_LOCA_TC

Local quasi-brittle constitutive relation intended for the modelling of the cracking

see [R4.01.47](doc:r4.01.47).

##### 4.4.8.13 Constitutive behaviour GLRC_DAMAGE

This constitutive law replaces an old version, GLRC. It is a global model of a reinforced-concrete plate capable of representing its constitutive behaviour up to failure. Unlike local modellings where each constituent of the material is modelled separately, in global models the constitutive law is written directly in terms of generalised stresses and strains. The phenomena taken into account are the elastoplasticity coupled between membrane and bending effects (as opposed to an elastoplasticity in bending only in GLRC) and damage in bending. The damage coupled membrane/bending is treated by GLRC_DM, which, on the other hand, completely neglects elastoplasticity. The characteristics of the material are defined in DEFI_MATERIAU (U4.43.01) under the keyword GLRC_DAMAGE. For details on the formulation of the model see [R7.01.31](doc:r7.01.31).

**Warning, this constitutive law must not be used in a non-linear analysis.**

This constitutive law is mainly used for the chaining of analyses between Code_Aster and Europlexus.

- Supported modellings: DKTG, Q4GG
- Number of internal variables. 19
- Meaning: $\mathrm{V1}$ to $\mathrm{V3}$ : plastic membrane extension, $\mathrm{V4}$ to $\mathrm{V6}$ : plastic curvatures, V7 : plastic dissipation, $\mathrm{V8}$ to $\mathrm{V9}$ : damage variables for positive and negative bending respectively, $\mathrm{V10}$ : damage dissipation, $\mathrm{V11}$ to $\mathrm{V13}$ : orthotropy angles, $\mathrm{V14}$ to $\mathrm{V19}$ : components of the kinematic hardening tensor (3 for the membrane forces, 3 for the bending moments).

##### 4.4.9.19 Constitutive behaviour NLH_CSRM

The constitutive law NLH_CSRM makes it possible to model the elasto-visco-plastic constitutive behaviour of cohesive geomaterials. It will eventually replace the laws LETK and LKR.

The scope of use of the model NLH_CSRM is for the moment limited to the **isotropic mechanical** constitutive behaviour. The taking into account of **structural anisotropy** and of the dependence of the hardenings on **temperature** — effective in the latest version of the model LKR — will be the subject of forthcoming submissions; likewise the dependence of the criteria on the third invariant of the stress deviator (Lode angle).

The model NLH_CSRM falls within the **thermodynamic framework of generalised standard materials** with two irreversible mechanisms,

- **Plastic mechanism** — **instantaneous** irreversible constitutive behaviour: hardening and/or softening hardenings, contracting or dilating volumetric constitutive behaviour, influence of the mean stress on the brittle character of the response in compression, …;
- **Viscoplastic mechanism** — **delayed** irreversible constitutive behaviour (of Perzyna type): primary, secondary and tertiary creep (failure).

A **coupling between these two mechanisms** makes it possible — among other things — to represent the dependence of the maximum strength of certain geomaterials on the loading rate.

This law has been integrated implicitly via the MFront tool. Its reference documentation bears the key **R7.01.46**. The parameters to be entered in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU number 13: 2 elastic parameters, 11 for the plastic and viscoplastic mechanisms. YoungModulus and PoissonRatio are to be entered in the factor keywords ELAS and NLH_CSRM.

- Supported modellings: '3D', 'AXIS', 'D_PLAN', 'THM', 'THHM', ...
- Integration schemes: implicit 'NEWTON',
- Number of internal variables: 24 in 3D, 18 in 2D
- Example: see test cases SSNV206, WTNV135, COMP012, COMP001.

##### 4.4.9.20 Constitutive behaviour MCC

The constitutive model MCC makes it possible to represent qualitatively the phenomenology of granular soils under monotonic loadings, taking into account the effects of dilatancy/contractancy, of softening/hardening, as well as the critical state. It updates the formulation and the numerical solution method of the law CAM_CLAY [R7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14), while remaining faithful to the elements of phenomenology predicted by the latter, in order, eventually, to replace it. The model MCC is generalised standard. The constitutive equations are solved by using a direct implicit time-integration method in MFront. The reference documentation of the model MCC bears the key [R7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48). The parameters to be entered in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU under the keyword MCC number seven: three parameters define the isotropic non-linear elasticity, three parameters characterise the initial elastic domain, and a last one drives the combined kinematic-isotropic hardening with the volumetric plastic strain. In the keyword ELAS, one enters Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio derived from the initial bulk and shear moduli of the model MCC.

- Supported modellings: '3D', 'AXIS', 'D_PLAN', 'THM', 'THHM'...
- Integration schemes: implicit 'NEWTON'
- Number of internal variables: 12 in 3D, 10 in 2D
- Example: see test cases SSNV160, SSNV202, COMP012, WTNV122

##### 4.4.9.21 Constitutive behaviour CSSM

The constitutive model CSSM ("Critical State Soil Model") is a combination of two models aiming to represent respectively the monotonic and cyclic drained constitutive behaviour of soils. The first component relies on the modified Cam-Clay model, which describes the phenomena of dilatancy or contractancy as well as confinement effects under monotonic and drained loadings. The second component is a multi-mechanism model of Iwan type,
designed to represent the cyclic non-linear constitutive behaviour of soils at low strain levels. The model respects the framework of generalised standard materials. The constitutive equations are solved using a direct implicit time-integration method, implemented in MFront. The reference documentation of the model CSSM bears the key [R7.01.49](doc:r7.01.49). The constitutive model CSSM comprises a total of eleven parameters to be entered in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU: two to describe the reversible (elastic) constitutive behaviour, five for the irreversible constitutive behaviour linked to the modified Cam-Clay component, and three for the irreversible constitutive behaviour linked to the Iwan component. A last parameter weights the contributions of the two components to the shear constitutive behaviour of the model CSSM. In the keyword ELAS, one enters Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio derived from the bulk and shear moduli of the model.

- Supported modellings: '3D', 'AXIS', 'D_PLAN', 'THM', 'THHM'...
- Integration schemes: implicit 'NEWTON'
- Number of internal variables: 71 in 3D, 49 in 2D
- Example: see test cases SSNV160, SSNV205, SSNV207, COMP012, WTNV122

#### 4.4.10 Constitutive behaviours integrated by external software

It is important to note that the use of these *custom* constitutive laws implies a specific validation for the study envisaged, because one places oneself **outside the qualified domain** of **code_aster**.

##### 4.4.10.1 Keyword UMAT

```
♦    NB_VARI    =    nbvar
```

Umat is a format of Fortran routine familiar to users of the Abaqus code, used to integrate their own constitutive laws.

The dynamic library containing the Umat routine must be prepared before the analysis is run. For this, the user has a simple means of compiling this library by using the utility `as_run --make_shared` (*cf.* [U1.04.00](doc:u1.04.00)).

The coupling between Umat and **code_aster** is expressed in the command file in the following way:

- at the level of COMPORTEMENT, the keyword RELATION='UMAT';
- still under COMPORTEMENT, the keyword NB_VARI makes it possible to specify the number of internal variables of the constitutive behaviour, and of course the usual keywords GROUP_MA, DEFORMATION;
- one indicates the path to the library under the keyword LIBRAIRIE and the name of the symbol (name of the routine contained in the library) under the keyword NOM_ROUTINE;
- the plane-stress assumption is taken into account by the [De Borst method](doc:r5.03.03);
- the keywords relating to local integration (RESI_INTE, ITER_INTE_MAXI, ALGO_INTE, PARM_THETA) are not used.

The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword UMAT/UMAT_FO.

The current limitations of the interface between **code_aster** and Umat are:

- output of the energies (for the moment, they are not recovered by **code_aster**);
- likewise no thermo-mechanical coupling for the moment.

For more details on the use of Umat in **code_aster**, one should refer to the dedicated [user's guide](doc:u2.10.01).

- Supported modellings: 3D, AXIS, D_PLAN.
- Example: see the tests [umat001](doc:v1.03.124), [umat002](doc:v1.03.125).

##### 4.4.10.2 Keyword MFRONT

[MFront](https://thelfer.github.io/tfel/web/index.html) is a code generator making it possible to write and integrate constitutive laws easily. It is developed by the CEA Cadarache within the framework of the PLEIADES platform.

The dynamic library containing the MFront routine must be prepared before the analysis is run. For this, the user will call the command [CREA_LIB_MFRONT](doc:u7.03.04).

The coupling between MFront and **code_aster** is expressed in the command file in the following way:

- at the level of COMPORTEMENT, the keyword RELATION='MFRONT';
- still under COMPORTEMENT, the usual keywords GROUP_MA, DEFORMATION (one can in particular use large strains with 'GDEF_LOG' and 'GREEN_LAGRANGE');
- one indicates the constitutive behaviour to be used under the keyword COMPOR_MFRONT, produced by CREA_LIB_MFRONT;
- the plane-stress assumption is taken into account by the [De Borst method](doc:r5.03.03);
- the keywords RESI_INTE, ITER_INTE_MAXI are transmitted to the MFront law;
- the keywords ALGO_INTE, PARM_THETA are not used.
- the option SYME_MATR_TANG makes it possible to say whether the constitutive matrix will be symmetric (by default) or not.

The necessary data of the material field are provided in the operator [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01), under the keyword MFRONT/MFRONT_FO.

If the MFront file allows it, checks can be made on the values of the material parameters. The constitutive behaviour in the event of the bounds being exceeded is driven by the keyword VERI_BORNE, which is equal to 'ARRET' by default. The other possible choices (for a constitutive behaviour in prototype mode) are 'MESSAGE' (printing of the message without interrupting the analysis) and 'SANS' (the error is passed over in silence).

For more information on the use of MFront in **code_aster**, one should refer to the dedicated [user's guide](doc:u2.10.02).

- Supported modellings: 3D, AXIS, D_PLAN.
- Example: see the tests [mfron01](doc:v1.03.126), [mfron02](doc:v1.03.127), [mfron03](doc:v1.03.128), [mfron05](doc:v1.03.130), [mfron06](doc:v1.03.132).

##### 4.5.3.1 Keyword RELATION

The relations KIT_XXXX make it possible to solve simultaneously from two to four equilibrium equations. The equations considered depend on the suffix XXXX with the following rule:

- M denotes the mechanical equilibrium equation,
- T denotes the thermal equilibrium equation,
- H denotes a hydraulic equilibrium equation.
- V denotes the presence of a phase in vapour form (in addition to the liquid)

The associated thermo-hydro-mechanical problems are treated in a fully coupled manner.

A single letter H means that the porous medium is saturated (a single pressure variable $p$ ), for example either with gas, or with liquid, or with a liquid/gas mixture (whose gas pressure is constant).

Two letters H mean that the porous medium is unsaturated (two pressure variables $p$ ), for example a liquid/vapour/gas mixture.

The presence of the two letters HV means that the porous medium is saturated by one component (in practice water), but that this component can be in liquid or vapour form. There is then only one conservation equation for this component, hence a single pressure degree of freedom, but there is a liquid flux and a vapour flux.

##### 4.5.3.2 Keyword RELATION_KIT

For each phenomenon modelled, one must specify in RELATION_KIT:

- the mechanical constitutive model of the skeleton,
- the constitutive behaviour of the liquids/gases,
- the hydraulic law.
- HYDR_UTIL (if the mechanical constitutive behaviour is without damage): Means that no material datum is entered "hard-coded". Concretely, in the saturated case, one will have to define the following 6 curves point by point (by DEFI_FONCTION):
- the saturation as a function of the capillary pressure,
- the derivative of this curve,
- the relative permeability to the liquid as a function of the saturation,
- its derivative.
- the relative permeability to the gas as a function of the saturation,
- its derivative.
- HYDR_VGM (if the mechanical constitutive behaviour is without damage). Here and only for the liquid/gas coupling laws 'LIQU_GAZ', 'LIQU_AD_GAZ', 'LIQU_AD_GAZ' and 'LIQU_VAPE_GAZ_VAPE', the curves of saturation, of relative permeabilities to water and to gas and their derivatives are defined by the Mualem–Van Genuchten model. The user must then enter the parameters of this law $(n,\mathit{Pr},\mathit{Sr})$ . The Mualem–Van Genuchten model is as follows:

${k}_{r}^{w}=\sqrt{{S}_{\mathrm{we}}}{(1-{(1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}}^{1/m})}^{m})}^{2}$ , ${k}_{r}^{\mathrm{gz}}=\sqrt{1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}}}{(1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}}^{1/m})}^{\mathrm{2m}}$

and

${S}_{\mathrm{we}}=\frac{1}{{(1+{(\frac{{P}_{c}}{{P}_{r}})}^{n})}^{m}}$ where ${S}_{\mathrm{we}}=\frac{{S}_{w}-{S}_{\mathrm{wr}}}{1-{S}_{\mathrm{wr}}-{S}_{\mathrm{gr}}}$ and $m=1-\frac{1}{n}$

- HYDR_VGC (if the mechanical constitutive behaviour is without damage). Exactly the same thing as HYDR_VGM except for the law of relative permeability to the gas, which is a cubic law:

${k}_{r}^{\mathrm{gz}}={(1-{S}_{w})}^{3}$

- HYDR_TABBAL (if the mechanical constitutive behaviour is without damage). Only for the liquid/gas coupling laws 'LIQU_VAPE_GAZ', 'LIQU_AD_GAZ', 'LIQU_AD_GAZ_VAPE', 'LIQU_GAZ', 'LIQU_GAZ_ATM'. The user must then enter the parameters of this law $(t(\mathit{HR}),{A}_{0},{S}_{\mathit{BJH}},{\omega}_{\mathit{BJH}},{\left(\frac{\partial \gamma }{\partial {\epsilon}_{s}}\right)|}_{\mu})$ [see [U2.04.05]) as well as, as in the case HYDR_UTIL, the saturation curves, the derivative of the saturation, the relative permeabilities and their derivatives.
- HYDR_ENDO (if one uses 'MAZARS' or 'ENDO_ISOT_BETON') under RELATION_KIT (this keyword makes it possible to enter the saturation curve and its derivative as a function of the capillary pressure as well as the relative permeability and its derivative as a function of the saturation.

This keyword makes it possible to define the assumptions used for the computation of the strains: by default, one considers small displacements and small strains.

#### 4.6.1 Strain model PETIT

The strains used in the constitutive relation are the linearised strains:

${\epsilon}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({u}_{i,j}+{u}_{j,i})$

This means that one remains within the Small Perturbation Hypothesis: small displacements, small rotations, small strains (less than about 5%)

#### 4.6.2 Strain model GREEN_LAGRANGE

This kinematics links the Green-Lagrange strain tensor $\mathrm{E}$ to the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress (PK2) $\mathrm{S}$ :

$\mathrm{E}=\frac{1}{2}({\mathrm{F}}^{T}\mathrm{F}-\mathit{Id})$

$\sigma =\frac{1}{det(\mathrm{F})}(\mathrm{F}\mathrm{S}{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{T}})$

where $\mathrm{F}$ denotes the transformation gradient ( ${F}_{ij}={\delta}_{ij}+{u}_{i,j}$ ) and $\sigma$ the Cauchy stress. One may refer to [:ref:`R5.03.20 <R5.03.20>$].

**Notes:**

- This kinematics is in particular suited to the treatment of the large strains of hyperelastic materials (such as ELAS_HYPER).
- This kinematics is not compatible with plane stresses.

#### 4.6.3 Strain model GROT_GDEP

This kinematic formulation makes it possible to treat large rotations and large displacements, while remaining in small strains in the case of the following modellings:

- to treat large rotations and small strains for all the incremental constitutive laws under COMPORTEMENT provided with the modellings COQUE_3D. It is a total Lagrangian formulation, making it possible to compute the exact configuration for large rotations [R3,07,05].

**Warning:**

*It is strongly inadvisable to use line search for the* *COQUE_3D with the option* *GROT_GDEP (sometimes convergence is impossible and if one converges, the analysis needs more Newton iterations).*

- to treat large displacements and large rotations and small strains for the plate and shell elements: modellings DKT (only in linear elasticity), DKTG (only with the constitutive behaviours GLRC_* ) and SHB.
- to treat large displacements and large rotations and small strains for the modellings POU_D_TGM and POU_D_EM (multifibre beams) (formerly REAC_GEOM). One makes the assumption of an update of the geometry at each iteration and one adds the geometric stiffness to the material stiffness in order to form the tangent stiffness. As regards large rotations, instead of going through a complex "exact" approach as for the POU_D_T_GD and COQUE_3D, one allows moderate rotations (of second order). This type of computation of the strains makes it possible to treat efficiently problems of multifibre beams with non-linear constitutive behaviour, in moderate rotations [R3.08.09](doc:r3.08.09).

#### 4.6.4 Strain model PETIT_REAC

The strain increments used for the incremental constitutive relation are the linearised strains of the displacement increment in the updated geometry. That is, if $X,u,\Delta u$ denote respectively the position, the displacement and the displacement increment computed at a given iteration of a material point [R5.03.24](doc:r5.03.24).

$\Delta {\epsilon}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial \Delta {u}_{i}}{\partial {(X+u)}_{j}}+\frac{\partial \Delta {u}_{j}}{\partial {(X+u)}_{i}})$

Equilibrium is therefore solved on the current geometry but the constitutive behaviour remains written under the small-strain assumption. Consequently, the use of PETIT_REAC is not appropriate to large rotations but it is appropriate to large strains, under certain conditions [10]:

- very small increments,
- very small rotations (which implies a quasi-radial loading)
- elastic strains small compared with the plastic strains,
- isotropic constitutive behaviour.

Outside these assumptions, this approximation can give very poor results. One should therefore check the convergence of the results with respect to the discretisation.

**Warning:**

*It is inadvisable to use this option with the structural elements* *COQUE,* *COQUE_1D and* *POU (an alarm message appears in the .mess file).*

#### 4.6.5 Strain model SIMO_MIEHE

It is an incrementally objective formulation in large strains of the constitutive laws based on a Von Mises criterion with isotropic hardening. The elastic stress–strain relation is hyperelastic. All the information on the transformation gradient $F$ is taken into account, both the rotation and the strains:

${F}_{ij}={\delta}_{ij}+\frac{\partial {u}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}$

This makes it possible to carry out analyses in large plastic strains, with the constitutive relations 'VMIS_ISOT_LINE', 'VMIS_ISOT_TRAC', 'ROUSSELIER' and all the constitutive behaviours, with isotropic hardening only, associated with a material undergoing metallurgical phase changes (relations META_X_IL_XXX_XXX and META_X_INL_XXX_XXX,).

This formulation automatically adds to the chosen constitutive behaviour 6 internal variables, storing at the end the 6 components of the tensor $\frac{1}{2}({I}_{d}-{b}^{e})$ (cf. [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21)).

**Warning:**

*This option is valid only for the modellings* *3D,* *2D,* *INCO_UPG (no plane stress with the* *DE BORST method). For further information on the formulation of large plastic strains according to SIMO and MIEHE, one may refer to [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21).

*In large strains of type 'SIMO_MIEHE', the tangent matrices are not symmetric except in the (hyper)elastic case. Up to version 7.4, one proceeded to a systematic symmetrisation of the matrix. From now on, it is the non-symmetric matrix which is supplied. If he wishes, the user can nevertheless ask for it to be symmetrised under the keyword* *SOLVEUR = _F(SYME = 'OUI'). Warning:* *SYME = 'OUI' is not the default. The solutions will therefore a priori take longer with this new version if one does nothing as regards the command file. On the other hand the non-symmetric tangent matrix will allow better convergence.*

#### 4.6.6 Strain model GDEF_LOG

It is a formulation in large strains using a logarithmic strain measure, arising from an approach due to Miehe, Apel and Lambrecht. It makes it possible to use the following incremental elastoplastic or viscoplastic constitutive laws (cf. [R5.03.24](doc:r5.03.24)):

VMIS_ISOT_*, VMIS_JOHN_COOK, VMIS_CINE_LINE, VMIS_ECMI_*, VMIS_CIN*_CHAB, VMIS_CIN2_MEMO, VISC_CIN*_CHAB, VISC_CIN2_MEMO, LEMAITRE.

This formulation is valid only for the modellings 3D, 2D. It allows an incrementally objective integration of the constitutive laws like the models SIMO_MIEHE and GEDF_HYPO_ELAS. However, like all hypoelastic laws, these constitutive laws are strictly speaking limited to small elastic strains. To save computation time, a specific tensor is stored in 6 additional internal variables. The tensor in question, $T$ , is the stress tensor expressed in logarithmic space. Because it is stored in the internal variables, this implies that the user wishing to use the GDEF_LOG formalism with an initial stress field (ETAT_INIT) should refer to the documents [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) and [V6.03.159](doc:v6.03.159) (test case ssnp159b).

#### 4.6.7 Strain models for MFront

Only the large-strain models GDEF_LOG can be used with MFront constitutive behaviours ( RELATION='MFRONT' ).

The GDEF_LOG models of **code_aster** necessarily rely on a law written in small strains.

### 4.8 Operands RESI_CPLAN_RELA, RESI_CPLAN_MAXI, ITER_CPLAN_MAXI

The DE BORST method makes it possible to add the plane-stress condition to all the models of COMPORTEMENT (for more detail see the documentation [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03)). The plane-stress assumption is checked at convergence.

```text
◊/ RESI_CPLAN_RELA =    /    1.E-6,    [DEFAUT]
```

/    ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rela}}$

> / RESI_CPLAN_MAXI =    /    ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{abso}}$

In certain cases, convergence is reached for the Newton algorithm, but not for the verification of the plane-stress state, which leads to additional iterations, or even an excessive subdivision of the time step. These keywords make it possible to separate the accuracy relating to the integration of the constitutive law from that used to check the plane-stress assumption. For this verification, two criteria are possible:

- either a relative criterion:     $\left|{\sigma}_{zz}\right|<\Vert \sigma \Vert \times {\epsilon}_{\mathit{rela}}$
- or an absolute criterion: $\left|{\sigma}_{zz}\right|<{\epsilon}_{\mathit{abso}}$

```text
◊    ITER_CPLAN_MAXI =     / 10        [DEFAUT]
                           / iter_cplan_maxi
```

This keyword makes it possible to specify the number of iterations left to the DE BORST algorithm within each iteration of the algorithm for solving the equilibrium equations (nested schemes). If this number of iterations is reached, no error is triggered but global convergence cannot be declared and additional iterations for solving the equilibrium equations are activated, even if the equilibrium equations are well solved.

### 4.11 Operands RESI_INTE, ITER_INTE_MAXI

```
◊    RESI_INTE    =    /    resint,
                       /    1.e-6    (par défaut),

◊    ITER_INTE_MAXI    =    /    iteint,
                            /    20    (par défaut),
```

In certain constitutive relations, a non-linear equation or a non-linear system must be solved locally (at each Gauss point). These operands (residual and maximum number of so-called internal iterations) are used to test the convergence of this iterative solution algorithm. They are useless in the case where ALGO_INTE='ANALYTIQUE', 'SPECIFIQUE' or 'SANS_OBJET'. For more details, refer to the reference documentation of each constitutive behaviour.

For example, the optional keyword RESI_INTE can be used for the [coupling with MFront](doc:u2.10.02). In MFront, the implicit and explicit integrators use variables which are strains (or of the same order of magnitude as the strains). The value of RESI_INTE is transmitted to the convergence parameter used by MFront (parameter `epsilon`); it is therefore an *absolute* parameter. In this case, its default value is the same as that defined in the MFront file (directive `@Epsilon` fixed at `1.0e-8` by default). Similarly, the value of the maximum number of iterations in the MFront file (directive `@MaximumNumberOfIterations` or `@IterMax`, although obsolete, fixed at `100` by default) is overloaded only if the keyword ITER_INTE_MAXI is present on the Aster side.

### 4.12 Operand RESI_RADI_RELA

```text
◊    RESI_RADI_RELA =     tolrad
```

Measure of the error $\eta$ due to the discretisation in time, directly related to the rotation of the normal to the loading surface. One computes the angle between ${n}^{-}$ , the normal to the plasticity criterion at the beginning of the time step (time instant t-),
and ${n}^{+}$ , the normal to the plasticity criterion computed at the end of the time
step in the following way: $\eta =\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|\Delta n\right|\right|=\frac{1}{2}\left|\left|{n}^{+}-{n}^{-}\right|\right|=\left|\mathrm{sin}(\frac{\alpha}{2})\right|$ . This provides a measure of the error (also used for the computation of the component ERR_RADI of the option DERA_ELGA of CALC_CHAMP). The time step is subdivided (via DEFI_LIST_INST) if $\eta >\mathrm{tolrad}$ . This criterion is operational for the Von Mises elastoplastic constitutive behaviours with isotropic hardening, linear kinematic hardening and mixed hardening: VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, VMIS_ISOT_PUIS, VMIS_CINE_LINE, VMIS_ECMI_LINE, VMIS_ECMI_TRAC, and for the elasto-visco-plastic constitutive behaviours of Chaboche: VMIS_CIN1_CHAB, VMIS_CIN2_CHAB, VMIS_CIN2_MEMO, VISC_CIN1_CHAB, VISC_CIN2_CHAB, VISC_CIN2_MEMO.

### 4.13 Operand ITER_INTE_PAS

```text
◊    ITER_INTE_PAS =    /    0            [DEFAUT]
                        /    itepas
```

Makes it possible to subdivide locally the time step in order to facilitate the integration of the local constitutive relation (at each integration point). If itepas is equal to $0$ , $1$ or $-1$ there is no subdivision. If itepas is positive, one systematically subdivides the time step locally into itepas small time steps before carrying out the integration of the constitutive relation. If itepas is negative, the subdivision into | itepas| small time steps is carried out only in the event of local non-convergence.

**Warning:** this keyword is possible only with DEFORMATION='PETIT', DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC' and DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP'. It cannot be used in large strains.

### 4.14 Operand ALGO_INTE

```text
◊    ALGO_INTE =    /’ANALYTIQUE’
                    # methodes de resolution d’équations scalaires
                    /’SECANTE’
                    /’DEKKER’
                    /’NEWTON_1D’
                    /’BRENT’
                    # methodes de resolution de systèmes d’équations
                    /’NEWTON’
                    /’NEWTON_RELI’
                    /’NEWTON_PERT’
                    /’RUNGE_KUTTA’
                    # methodes de resolution spécifiques (pas de paramètre)
                    /’SPECIFIQUE’

```

Makes it possible to specify the type of integration scheme for solving the non-linear equation or system of equations formed by the constitutive equations of the constitutive models with internal variables: A default solution method is provided for each constitutive behaviour. However, it is possible to modify the default solution method for a certain number of constitutive behaviours. For example:

- the model VISC_ENDO_LEMA can be integrated either with SECANTE or with BRENT,
- the model VENDOCHAB can be integrated either with NEWTON or with RUNGE_KUTTA.
- the model MONOCRISTAL can be integrated either with NEWTON, or with NEWTON_RELI, or with NEWTON_PERT, or with RUNGE_KUTTA.

### 4.15 Operand TYPE_MATR_TANG

For the relations other than 'RGI_BETON', 'FLUA_PORO_BETON', 'FLUA_ENDO_PORO' and 'RGI_BETON_BA':

```text
◊    TYPE_MATR_TANG=    /’PERTURBATION’,
                        /’VERIFICATION’,
◊    VALE_PERT_RELA =    / 1.E-5,    [DEFAUT]
                        / perturb,[R]
```

This keyword allows the verification of the tangent matrix for a given constitutive behaviour. It is addressed mainly to developers of constitutive laws, and its use must be reserved for models comprising very few elements. In the absence of this keyword, the tangent matrix is computed in the classical way. (These keywords are used together with REAC_ITER=1).

- TYPE_MATR_TANG="PERTURBATION" makes it possible to use the tangent matrix computed by perturbation in place of the tangent matrix computed by the constitutive behaviour. The value of the perturbation is given by perturb. In order for this to work independently of the units, the perturbation is computed in a manner relative to the max norm of the displacement increment on the element: $\delta U=\mathit{perturb}\times max|{U}_{i}|$ . This is possible only for the modellings of 2D and 3D continuous media, in pure mechanics, comprising only displacement degrees of freedom.
- TYPE_MATR_TANG="VERIFICATION" concerns developers who want to verify an elementary tangent matrix (on a small problem: one element is enough: only the last matrices are kept). The matrix by perturbation is stored, as well as the consistent tangent matrix, which makes it possible to compare them. In addition the python module veri_matr_tang makes this comparison easy, as well as the test of symmetry of the matrix. See the tests COMP001, COMP002.

For the relations 'RGI_BETON', 'FLUA_PORO_BETON', 'FLUA_ENDO_PORO' and 'RGI_BETON_BA':

```text
◊    TYPE_MATR_TANG=    /’MATR_ELAS’,                                            [DEFAUT]
                        /’MATR_ENDO’,
```

For these 4 laws, this keyword makes it possible to choose the type of matrix used between the elastic matrix 'MATR_ELAS' and the unloading matrix 'MATR_ENDO'.

### 4.16 Operand POST_ITER

```text
◊     POST_ITER =    /’CRIT_RUPT’,
```

Definition of an action to be carried out in post-processing of the Newton iterations, at each time step.

In the case CRIT_RUPT, it is a fracture criterion in critical stress. If the largest mean principal stress in an element exceeds a given threshold sigc, Young's modulus is divided at the following time step by the coefficient coef. These two coefficients are defined under the keyword CRIT_RUPT of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

This criterion is available for the constitutive laws VISCOCHAB, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC(_LINE), VISC_ISOT_TRAC (_LINE), and validated by the tests SSNV226A, B, C.

### 4.17 Operand POST_INCR

```text
◊     POST_INCR =    /’REST_ECRO’,
```

Definition of an action to be carried out in post-processing of each time step of a thermomechanical analysis.

In the case REST_ECRO, the post-processing consists in modifying the internal variables in order to take into account the phenomenon of restoration of hardening. The cumulated plastic strain is modified by means of the parameters of REST_ECRO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU[U4.43.01].

This criterion is available for the constitutive laws VMIS_ISOT_TRAC(_LINE), VMIS_ECMI_LINE, VMIS_CIN1_CHAB and VMIS_CIN2_CHAB, and for the modellings 3D, AXIS, D_PLAN and C_PLAN.
