## 1 Caughey Absorbing Layer Method: an absorbent layer method

Considering the effectiveness of methods that use absorbent layers with multi-directional attenuation properties, Sembrat et al. introduced an absorption technique based on a simple description of the attenuation process. Physical models are generally very efficient in describing attenuation phenomena; however, these models are not always simple to implement. Given that Caughey damping is already available in most of the codes at the finished elements, Sembrat et al. proposed to use this formulation to describe wave attenuation in a finite absorbent layer. The method is called CALM (Caughey Absorbing Layer Method). This formulation is purely numerical but, as recalled below, interpretation is possible in some cases. This method of constructing the damping matrix is very effective because the operation is very simple and is often available in most calculation codes. Moreover, in a modal approach, the Rayleigh (or Caughey) damping matrix is diagonal in the modal base. This damping is therefore called proportional or conventional. In the case of non-proportional damping, complex modes must be calculated (to decouple the modal equations).
The Rayleigh damping (or Caughey) can also be used to analyze the propagation of a damped elastic wave. For this reason, it may be useful to have a rheological interpretation of such a purely numerical formulation. The absorbent layer is modelled with appropriate damping properties to mitigate the reflections of the waves at the edges of the domain. This method (CALM) can reduce the amplitude of the elastic wave that comes from the elastic medium and, therefore, the reflection at the artificial terminals of the medium. ## 2 Reference problem

The purpose of this study is to determine the monodirectional response of an elastic bar to a compression wave to evaluate the efficiency of the CALM method in absorbing an elastic wave on a domain boundary. The CALM method is therefore evaluated for its ability to model radiative damping, and is also compared to the absorption capacity of absorbent boundaries.

### 2.5 Modelling the CALM layer

The main idea of the CALM method is to model a damping that gradually rises throughout the layer to absorb the waves and without generating too high a contrast that can generate the reflections parasites. To do this, in code_aster one can define material characteristics that are a function of geometry. So one defines damping in two steps:

- The first step is to set a constant Rayleigh damping in the elastic bar. To do this, you will re-appoint a $\xi_0=0.2\%$ damping between $1$ and $10$ Hz
- Then, in the CALM layer, we develop a Rayleigh damping that is $\xi_0\%$ for $x=L_1$ and that is $\xi_{max}=400\%$ for $x=L_1+L_2$ with a parabolic progression dependent on $x$ coordinate

In the geometric configuration of the bar with inclined border (v2.03.201-Geom2), we will also have a dependence on the $y$ coordinate.

## 9 Analysis of results

In this chapter, a critical analysis will be provided on the ability to absorb elastic waves according to the model chosen.
