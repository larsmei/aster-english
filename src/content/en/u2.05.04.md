# u2.05.04 User's guide for the limit-load calculation

**Summary**

The aim of this note is to give the information required for a user to be able to carry out limit-load calculations with *code_aster*.

The first part recalls the main lines of the method and its properties.

The second part presents the various steps required for the implementation in *code_aster*.

Finally, the third part presents a few notes on implementation, based on the *Aster* test case [V6.04.124](doc:v6.04.124) and on the industrial case aimed at determining the limit pressure for a Canopy vessel joint.

## 1 Introduction

This document aims to guide the user in the implementation of a limit-analysis calculation and completes the documentation available on limit analysis, which consists of the:

- reference document: [R7.07.01](doc:r7.07.01);
- validation documents SSNV124A [V6.04.124](doc:v6.04.124) and SSNV146 [V6.04.124](doc:v6.04.124);
- training document: Training section of the site www.code-aster.org

To do this *code_aster* uses:

- a regularised kinematic approach (Norton-Hoff-Friaâ regularisation method, cf. [bib1]_) for the von Mises resistance criterion (adjustment by a regularisation coefficient whose limiting value leads to convergence);
- quasi-incompressible finite elements;
- a non-linear static solution by parametric piloting;
- post-processing in order to obtain an estimate of the upper ${\lambda}_{\lim}^{\sup}$ and lower ${\lambda}_{\lim}^{\inf}$ values which bracket the limit value ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ .

The present document is composed of three parts:

1. the first part, which recalls the main lines of the method and its properties;
2. the second part, which presents the various steps required for the implementation in **code_aster**;
3. the third part, which presents a few notes on implementation, based on the test case [V6.04.124](doc:v6.04.124) and on the industrial case aimed at determining the limit pressure for a Canopy vessel joint.

## 2 The main lines of the method

### 2.1 Presentation

The objectives of limit analysis are:

- the safety analysis with respect to an extreme behaviour (Ultimate Limit State E.L.U.);
- rapid sizing without seeking to describe the whole of the collapse process;
- the energetic characterisation of the collapse and the understanding of the collapse modes;
- the obtaining of simple information on the non-linear evolution of the material of the structure.

Limit analysis is a problem which can be treated in two ways, cf. [bib8]_, [bib9]_, [bib10]_:

- calculation of the “plastic” collapse of elastoplastic structures with a ductile plateau. The loading path and the constitutive model of the material must be described in full;
- calculation of the loss of equilibrium potentiality for a given resistance criterion, for a given loading direction. This is a problem of optimisation (under constraint) of the load parameter $\lambda$ . This approach is called “yield design”.

For standard materials, these two methods give the same result.

“Yield design” or “limit analysis” (the term designating yield design in the case of an elastoplastic material with a normality flow rule) aims to determine directly, in a simplified way and without resorting to the description of the loading path by a costly incremental elastoplastic analysis, the boundary of the plastic-collapse domain (and by deduction the domain of the loads which can be supported) for a structure $\Omega$ , of given geometry and material resistance limits, subjected to a loading given by its direction $\text{F}$ , and of amplitude parametrised by the positive real $\lambda$ . A “permanent” loading ${\text{F}}_{0}$ , such as gravity for example, may possibly be present in addition (without being amplified by $\lambda$ ).

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.04/10000201000002CB00000193241A6BEA61553269.png)

*Ingredients of yield design*

**Note**

One cannot take into account any change of geometry by yield design, as happens in a collapse by buckling, or for a very flexible solid. This constitutes the assumptions of the method: the configuration of the solid is that of its initial geometry, the connections of the structure are assumed given and fixed up to collapse; likewise the loadings, in forces only, are of fixed directions.

Two approaches to yield design are available:

- the static approach, which estimates the limit-load value from inside and which requires the construction of statically admissible stress fields, which is delicate in general by finite elements. It consists in maximising the loading parameter (or parameters) on condition that the (linear) equations of statics remain satisfied and that the stress criterion is not violated;
- the kinematic approach, dual of the previous one, which estimates the limit-load value from outside and which requires a minimisation, by a regularisation method, of the loading parameter (or parameters) under the condition that the power of the external forces remains greater than the resisting power (defined from the resistance criterion) of a non-regular functional, which must consequently be regularised in the general case.

The combined use (the ideal case!) of these two approaches provides bounds on the limit load.

### 2.2 An analytical example [bib7]_

One considers a hyperstatic system with three bars (see u2.05.04-fig-systeme-trois-barres); the bars have an identical resistance criterion, expressed in terms of the normal force (or tension) $N$ : $g(N)=\mid N\mid \le \stackrel{ˉ}{N}$ .

The point $D$ is subjected to a force $\overrightarrow{F}$ of non-zero components $({F}_{x},{F}_{y})$ , amplified by a multiplicative factor $\lambda$ .

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.04/Object_17.svg)

*Three-bar system*

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.04/Object_18.svg)

*Domain of the supportable tensions*

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.04/Object_19.svg)

*Domain of the supportable loadings*

The space of statically admissible solutions (the tensions ${T}_{i}$ in the bars) is defined by the equations:

$$
\left \lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{T}_{1}=\frac{\lambda ({F}_{x}\cos\alpha +{F}_{y}\sin\alpha )-X\sin\alpha }{\sin2\alpha }\\ {T}_{2}=X\\ {T}_{3}=\frac{\lambda ({F}_{y}\sin\alpha -{F}_{x}\cos\alpha )-X\sin\alpha }{\sin2\alpha }\end{array}\right.
$$

For $\lambda$ positive, one observes that it is at ${T}_{1}={T}_{2}=\stackrel{ˉ}{N}$ that the extremum is reached, see u2.05.04-fig-domaine-tensions-supportables. One thus finds that the maximum supportable value of the load factor $\lambda$ (or limit load), obtained by the static approach, is:

$$
\stackrel{ˉ}{\lambda}=\stackrel{ˉ}{N}\frac{(1+2\cos\alpha )\sin\alpha }{{F}_{x}\cos\alpha +{F}_{y}\sin\alpha }
$$

The kinematic approach gives the same result [bib6]_: this is indeed the limit load of this problem.

### 2.3 A few useful properties of the limit-load calculation

In the ideal case, the upper and lower bounds of the limit load must be equal to the limit value. With the numerical approach, there will always be a gap, and it is the lower bound which is the more penalising. It should however be noted that, for structures for which the analysis can be taken sufficiently far (as in the test cases), the upper bound is in practice the one closest to the exact value.

One frequently chooses the elastic limit as the resistance threshold: this is on the side of safety.

A few useful properties of the limit-load calculation are recalled below (see [bib4]_, [bib8]_):

- the limit load is proportional to the value of the resistance limit or threshold ${\sigma}_{y}$ in a homogeneous solid. It does not depend on the history of the loading undergone by the structure beforehand;
- as the resistance criterion is convex (von Mises criterion), the domain of the supportable loadings (hence the boundary of the limit loadings) in the space of the loadings is convex. One can therefore approximate the domain of the admissible loadings by the generalised polyhedron built on the vertices, each corresponding to a direction chosen in the space of the loadings;
- the Dirichlet conditions (an imposed displacement) which are applied on the part ${\Gamma}_{u}$ of the boundary $d\Omega$ of the structure, or an initial anelastic strain – thermal, plastic… – have no effect on the domain of the admissible loads (collapse being the impossibility of satisfying equilibrium, the collapse mode corresponds to a flow velocity-direction);
- the limit load does not depend on the possible presence of a self-equilibrated stress field (residual stresses);
- for a two-dimensional solid, the material and the loading direction being given, a **lower** bound obtained by the static approach in **plane stress** is necessarily lower than the exact limit load obtained in **plane strain**: ${\lambda}_{{C}_{\text{PLAN}}}^{-}\le {\lambda}_{{D}_{\text{PLAN}}}^{\lim}$ ;
- conversely, an **upper** bound obtained by the kinematic approach in **plane strain** is necessarily lower than the exact limit load obtained in **plane stress**: ${\lambda}_{{D}_{\text{PLAN}}}^{+}\ge {\lambda}_{{C}_{\text{PLAN}}}^{\lim}$ . This result therefore supplies an upper bound. If one wishes to treat a problem in plane stress, it is then necessary to carry out the kinematic approach on a three-dimensional modelling of a “slice” of solid;
- the limit loads obtained in plane strain with the Tresca criterion are equal to $\sqrt{3}/2$ times those found with the von Mises criterion;
- for a given geometry and loading direction, if in a given zone of the structure one replaces the material of resistance domain ${G}_{1}$ by a material of resistance domain ${G}_{2}\subset {G}_{1}$ (${g}_{2}(\tau )\le {g}_{1}(\tau )$ ) (for example: Tresca criterion included in that of von Mises), then the support functions (maximum resisting powers) are: ${\pi}_{2}(\varepsilon (v))\le {\pi}_{1}(\varepsilon (v))$ and therefore: ${\lambda}_{2}^{\lim}\le {\lambda}_{1}^{\lim}$ ;
- in particular if one replaces a defect $1$ (hole, crack) present in the structure by the defect $2$ containing the defect $1$ , then one has: ${\lambda}_{2}^{\lim}\le {\lambda}_{1}^{\lim}$ . Likewise, if the structure is heterogeneous, with two zones whose resistance limits are ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{y1}}\le {\sigma}_{\mathrm{y2}}$ , the limit load will be greater than that of the same homogeneous situation for the threshold ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{y1}}$ and lower than that for the threshold ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{y2}}$ ;
- in the presence of a loading direction $f=\lambda (\alpha {f}_{1}+(1-\alpha ){f}_{2})$ combining two directions ${f}_{1}$ and ${f}_{2}$ , $\alpha \in \left[0,1\right]$ , the exact limit load then satisfies: ${\lambda}^{\lim}(\alpha )\ge \stackrel{ˉ}{\lambda}=\frac{{\lambda}_{1}^{\lim}{\lambda}_{2}^{\lim}}{(1-\alpha ){\lambda}_{1}^{\lim}+\alpha {\lambda}_{2}^{\lim}}$ . This result remains valid for approximations from inside of the limit loads.

Whereas general three-dimensional situations are analytically intractable, in 2D plane strain (D_PLAN) and plane stress (C_PLAN) it is possible to construct by hand and to compute solutions for the static approach and the kinematic approach, by means of fields built by blocks, which give bounds on the limit load: this proves useful in corroborating a result obtained by finite elements.

## 3 Implementation of limit analysis

### 3.1 The calculation steps

To carry out a limit analysis, one must:

- use a 2D mesh (plane or axisymmetric) or a 3D mesh compatible with the incompressible finite elements;
- define the model with the incompressible finite elements;
- define the resistance limit of the material ${\sigma}_{y}$ ;
- define the permanent loading ${\text{F}}_{0}$ and the variable loading $\text{F}$ which is piloted by $\lambda$ ;
- define the incompressibility condition;
- carry out a non-linear analysis with the incremental constitutive behaviour and the piloting provided for limit analysis;
- post-process the analysis in order to obtain the upper and lower values of the estimated limit load.

### 3.2 Mesh

For the choice of the mesh and the use of the incompressible elements, one refers to the documentation [r3.06.08](doc:r3.06.08)

**Note:** the use of a three-field UPG element will require *a boundary condition to be imposed on the swelling (GONF); the vertex nodes must therefore be perfectly identified in the mesh:*

- *either from the design and construction stage of the mesh;*
- *or, more simply and a posteriori, by calling the command* *DEFI_GROUP* [u4.22.01](doc:u4.22.01) *with the keyword* *CREA_GROUP_NO, specifying the mesh-cell group of incompressible elements and using the option* *CRIT_NOEUD = 'SOMMET'.*

### 3.3 Model

#### 3.3.1 Modelling options

Incompressible elements must be selected; one therefore refers to the command AFFE_MODELE [U4.41.01].

#### 3.3.2 Incompressibility condition

To express the incompressibility condition for the three-field incompressible UPG elements, one uses the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA [u4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) with the keyword DDL_IMPO in order to impose that the component GONF of the group of vertex nodes of the incompressible elements remains zero.

### 3.4 Material

The material used for limit analysis with the incompressible elements is a material with a von Mises criterion, perfectly plastic elastoplastic.

The data for the material characteristics are supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_LINE of the command DEFI_MATERIAU [u4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

The slope of the tension curve (${E}_{T}$ ) is chosen equal to zero (operand D_SIGM_EPSI); the only datum which needs to be supplied is therefore the elastic limit (that is, the resistance threshold in our case) (operand SY).

### 3.5 Loading

The variable loading $\text{F}$ which is piloted by $\lambda$ must necessarily be of force type (force, pressure, gravity) and be declared in the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01].

If the structure is also subjected to a permanent loading ${\text{F}}_{0}$ , one must remember to recall it during post-processing (see u2.05.04-post-traitement).

### 3.6 List of time instants

The list of time instants is used to control the Norton-Hoff regularisation method, cf. [bib1]_, [bib5]_, by means of a coefficient $m$ , and not the evolution of the loading as in an ordinary analysis:

$$
m=1+{10}^{1-t}
$$

so that, when the time instant becomes sufficiently large, $m$ tends towards 1, and the constitutive behaviour approaches a perfectly rigid-plastic behaviour, see the uniaxial curve u2.05.04-fig-courbe-contrainte.

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.04/100000000000034A0000025385EEA864114B1A9F.jpg)

*Stress–strain curve for different values of the time instant $t$*

In practice, one will choose at the start a list of time instants with constant steps (see Table 2.6-a) before refining the calculation steps in the event of non-convergence.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $m=1+{10}^{1-t}$ | 2.00 | 1.30 | 1.10 | 1.03 | 1.01 |  |

If the user's document for the command POST_ELEM with the keyword CHAR_LIMITE [U4.81.22] recommends, in practice, limiting oneself to time instants lying between 1 and 2 so as not to have analyses which are too long while still making it possible to obtain a sufficiently accurate upper bound of the limit load, we observe that the lower limit load requires at least 2 to 3 additional iterations (time instant greater than 3) in order to converge towards the reference values in the test cases.

### 3.7 Analysis

Modelling with incompressible elements must necessarily use the command STAT_NON_LINE [u4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) and the keyword COMPORTEMENT.

For limit analysis, it is mandatory to call the following operands of the command STAT_NON_LINE:

- In COMPORTEMENT, one chooses RELATION='NORTON_HOFF'. This operand is used to describe the viscous constitutive relation (independent of temperature) in the limit-load calculation of structures, at a von Mises threshold [u4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)
- In PILOTAGE, one chooses TYPE='ANA_LIM'. This piloting mode is specific to the limit-load calculation (NORTON_HOFF law) by the kinematic approach. It must be applied only to the load declared via the factor keyword EXCIT, operand TYPE_CHARGE='FIXE_PILO'.

The limit-load calculation may require many line-search iterations and Newton iterations. It is therefore also strongly advised to use the various calculation options of the command STAT_NON_LINE in order to improve convergence, such as the line search, practice showing that it is sufficient to resort to 2 or 3 iterations.

$$
{u}_{m}(\beta )={\beta}^{-1}{u}_{m}(1);{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}(\beta ))={\beta}^{1-m}{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}(1))
$$

**Note**

If the intensity of the loading is amplified $L\to \mathrm{\beta  }L$ (whereas one does not consider a permanent loading ${L}_{0}=0$ ), the solutions depend on the factor $\mathrm{\beta  }$ according to the following relations:

At convergence for $m\to {1}^{+}$ , the limit loading given by the solution ${u}_{m}(\mathrm{\beta  })$ is indeed the same as that given by ${u}_{m}(1)$ , since ${\lambda}_{\lim}(\beta )={\lambda}_{\lim}(1)/\beta$ .

### 3.8 Post-processing

From the result of the non-linear analysis carried out, the operator POST_ELEM [u4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22) and the keyword CHAR_LIMITE produce a table which gives, for each time instant of the analysis, the estimate of the upper bound CHAR_LIMI_SUP (${\stackrel{ˆ}{\lambda}}_{m}$ ) of the limit load supported by the structure; this sequence is monotonically decreasing when $m\to {1}^{+}$ , that is when $t\to +\infty$ .

Furthermore, in the absence of a permanent loading ${F}_{0}$ (operand CHAR_CSTE='NON', which is the default option), the table also contains the estimate CHAR_LIMI_ESTIM (${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ ) of the lower bound of the limit load. This value (approximation of the gauge of the resistance convex set) is computed only at the Gauss points of the finite elements. Thus the value ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ obtained for each $m$ , lower than ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\lambda}}_{m}$ [bib6]_, can be regarded only as an indication (this sequence is not necessarily monotonic). Together with the value in excess ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\lambda}}_{m}$ , it makes it possible to provide a bracketing of the limit load of the discretised problem.

On the other hand, if a permanent loading ${F}_{0}$ is present (operand CHAR_CSTE='OUI'), such an estimate of the lower bound is no longer available and the table then indicates the power PUIS_CHAR_CSTE of the constant loading in the velocity field solution of the problem.

Visualisation of the displacement field obtained for a value of the coefficient $m\to {1}^{+}$ gives an “idea” of the collapse mode of the structure studied. More details on the post-processing are found in [r7.07.01](doc:r7.07.01).

### 4.3 Influence of the fineness of the meshes

The analysis of the test case with the initial mesh no longer converges after $t=2.45s$ . As for any other non-linear analysis, the mesh was refined in an attempt to improve convergence.

This analysis would above all make it possible to approach the limit load better by a better estimate of the lower value.

With a discretisation twice as fine, that is a mesh of 136 QUAD8 elements (that is 4 elements through the thickness), the analysis was brought to convergence up to $t=2,153s$ .

One also notes that at the same time instants ($t=\mathrm{1 }s$ and $\mathrm{2 }s$ ), one no longer has exactly the same upper and lower values of the estimated limit load as with the initial mesh (u2.05.04-tab-maillage-initial).

The influence of the fineness of the mesh is therefore manifested both in the global convergence and in the accuracy of the values estimated at the saved time instants. One concludes from this that the limit-load calculation by finite elements is sensitive to the meshes. The finer mesh does not necessarily give a limit taken further, because of an earlier stop of the analysis. This is nevertheless not a problem, given that limit-load analysis by the Norton-Hoff law tends towards a singular problem which would pose convergence difficulties. The objective of the analysis is to find a sequence of values of ${\widehat{\lambda}}_{m}$ converging towards a limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ .

### 4.5 Problems presenting symmetries

Let us note in passing an important facility in the implementation. We illustrate this with a 3D variant of the axisymmetric modelling C of the test case ssnv124.

The case thus represents a cylinder of internal radius $a=1\mathit{mm}$ and external radius $b=3\mathit{mm}$ subjected to an internal pressure of $1\mathit{MPa}$ on the internal wall.

### 4.6 Comparison of limit analysis and incremental elastoplastic analysis up to collapse on an example

It is known that in perfect elastoplasticity, the tangent operator has zero eigenvalues from a certain level of force loading: this means that the elastoplastic solid has reached plastic collapse.

It may be of interest to carry out an incremental elastoplastic analysis (with a von Mises criterion) up to collapse, in order to obtain a lower “bound” of the limit load. As the Newton algorithm used to solve the non-linear static equilibrium diverges for this loading level, one must use piloting by arc length [r5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80), or on a displacement variable, serving to control the loading by the strain of the solid undergone before collapse.

Here is an industrial example: it is the calculation of the limit internal pressure of a Canopy vessel joint. The problem is axisymmetric; the part is fixed on its upper boundary. The (flat-rate) resistance limit is set at $100\mathit{MPa}$ ; a von Mises criterion is adopted. Two analyses were carried out:

- a limit-analysis calculation with the method presented in the preceding paragraphs;
- a perfect incremental elastoplastic analysis (without and with hardening), with a von Mises criterion in large transformations (Simo-Miehe, see [r5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21)), in order to check that the change of geometry does not substantially modify the prediction of the limit pressure.

u2.05.04-fig-maillage-deforme shows that the deformed shapes computed are very close between the two methods and make it possible to predict the collapse mode.

u2.05.04-fig-calcul-analyse-limite shows the convergence of the sequences of limit-pressure bounds ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ and ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\lambda}}_{m}$ as a function of the regularisation coefficient of the limit-analysis calculation method, towards the exact limit pressure. The arithmetic mean of the two bounds seems to constitute a good estimate of the limit pressure. For the last value of the regularisation coefficient chosen, the values reported in u2.05.04-tab-pressions-limites were obtained. They are compared with the perfect incremental elastoplastic analysis in large transformations. One observes that the values are very close.

| $18,72\mathit{MPa}$ | $23,84\mathit{MPa}$ | $23,25\mathit{MPa}$ |

*Limit-analysis calculation: convergence of the sequences ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ and ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\lambda}}_{m}$ as a function of the regularisation coefficient, towards the exact limit pressure.*

The perfect incremental elastoplastic analysis in large transformations was carried out with two types of piloting:

- by a radial displacement of a particular point;
- by arc length (this latter piloting having been more efficient in run time);

and the results were compared with those obtained by an analysis in small transformations.

One of the questions which finally arises is the effect of the hardening of the material, and therefore of the choice of the resistance threshold.

This is why an incremental elastoplastic analysis was carried out taking into account the tension curve of the material considered (the principal parameters being: ${\sigma}_{y}=195\mathit{MPa}$ , ${\sigma}_{u}=520\mathit{MPa}$ ).

u2.05.04-fig-calcul-incremental-elastoplastique makes it possible to observe that on this case the two pilotings give an identical solution in large transformations, which is more “flexible” than in small transformations. The collapse pressure found is greater than $56,4\mathit{MPa}$ . Scaled to a value ${\sigma}_{y}=100\mathit{MPa}$ , one would have “found”: $28,9\mathit{MPa}$ .

*This example therefore makes it possible to gauge the conservative effect of the limit-analysis method when the elastic limit* ${\sigma}_{y}$ *has been taken as the resistance threshold. On the other hand, taking the ultimate limit* ${\sigma}_{u}$ *as the threshold seems non-conservative, the structure undergoing substantial changes of geometry as soon as it yields, before collapse, on the path considered for the incremental analysis.*
