# v6.03.120 FORMA21 - mesh thermomechanical adaptive on cracked head

**Summary:**

A ** thermo-elastic calculation is made on a cracked metal head** in flat stress Modelling (for the mechanical part) and luxuried (for the thermal part). In accordance with the "good practices" of the quality of the studies, two distinct meshes are used: linear in thermal and quadratic in mechanical.

The thermal calculation on which the mesh ${P}_{1}$ is freely converged with a spatial error d-indicator map coupling (CALC_ERREUR + ‘ERTH_ELEM)/refining-derefining (MACR_ADAP_MAIL + ‘RAFF_DERA) is performed first (modelization A).

In the second model (B), the two grids are adapted in conjunction with the same process during a chained thermo-mechanical calculation. For the free adaptation of the mechanical mesh, the pure residue indicator ‘ERME_ELEM' was used.

This test case allows testing the non-regression of different map mapping couplings d-errors/refining-defining procedure in thermo-mechanical, and the options of "pre and post-processings" of these calculations.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The mesh is made with elements type TRIA3. The calculation is made in isotropic stationary linear thermal with THER_LINEAIRE lumped operator (PLAN_DIAG Modelling).

Spatial error maps of the indicator are calculated as pure residue (ERTH_ELEM). Before the thermal flow of Gauss points to nodes (FLUX_ELNO) is smoothed, and to post-process the error card (via GIBI), it must be converted from CHAM_ELEM to CHAM_ELEM to nodes to nodes to element. The mean temperature value on GM35 (POST_RELEVE_T) and potential deformation energy (POST_ELEM) is also determined.

The whole is placed in a PYTHON loop allowing the setting up of a free refinement procedure in nb_calc=4 levels (via MACR_ADAP_MAIL option LIBRE=的RAFF_DERA) coupled on the previously exhumed error card. This process is piloted by ERTREL component ERTH_ELEM (relative component of the residual indicator). With CRIT_RAFF_PE=0.2 and CRIT_DERA_PE=0.1 as criteria (we refine 20% of the worst elements and derefinize 10% of the best).

This shows the convergence of temperature and energy values, the increase in their relative errors compared to the errors provided by the indicator (both relative and overall), the variations in the indicator's efficiency indices and its correct verification of the saturation hypothesis.

To illustrate "good practice" tips for the quality of studies, on the mesh geometry, mesh proper and d-type finished elements, the adhoc options of LIRE_MAILLAGE, MACR_ADAP_MAIL and MACR_INFO_MAIL are used.

### 3.4 Remarks

It is necessary to be aware that, as a "simple post-processing" of the thermo-mechanical problem, **l-indicator can unfortunately not provide a more reliable diagnosis in areas where the resolution of the initial problem achoppe** (crack, corners, multi-material, clamped support, shock...). So you have to start a process of adaptation (UNIFORME or LIBRE), with a mesh already a little refined by the user near the areas of discontinuities (materials, geometrics...).

MACR_ADAP_MAIL does not have regularization processes, so a bad initial mesh will produce, even coupled with an indicator, probably a bad adapted mesh!

As in mechanical engineering, **l-chaining "thermal operators/MACR_ADAP_MAIL OPTION ‘LIBRE" allows optimal convergence of the mesh**.

In addition, **"jongler" with the components of the thermal indicator** and boundary conditions, whether "fictive" or not, may be used to orient the construction of a refined or de-refined mesh by zones** (see [§6.3] [R4.10.03](doc:r4.10.03)).

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The mesh thermal (mechanical) is made with elements type TRIA3 (resp.TRIA6). A isotropic stationary linear thermal calculation (via THER_LINEAIRE in PLAN_DIAG Modelling) is carried out and a linear elasticity calculation (via STAT_NON_LINE in C_PLAN Modelling).

Spatial error maps of the indicators are calculated as pure thermal and mechanical residue (ERTH_ELEM and ERME_ELEM). Before this time, the thermal flux and stress field of the Gauss points must be smoothed at nodes (FLUX_ELNO and SIEF_ELNO) and, to post-treat the error card, it must be converted from CHAM_ELEM to element to CHAM_ELEM to nodes to element. The value of the potential deformation energy (POST_ELEM) is also determined.

The whole is placed in a loop PYTHON allowing the setting up of a free refinement procedure in nb_calc=2 levels (via MACR_ADAP_MAIL option LIBRE=的RAFF_DERA) coupled on the previously exhumed error card. This process is piloted:

- ERTREL component of ERTH_ELEM (relative component of the residual indicator) for the thermal mesh,
- NUEST component of ERME_ELEM (relative component of the residual indicator) for the mechanical mesh.

With CRIT_RAFF_PE=0.2 and CRIT_DERA_PE=0.1 (we refine 20% of the worst elements and de-refine 10% of the best).

After each thermal calculation the temperature field of the thermal mesh is projected to the mechanical mesh (via PROJ_CHAMP).

This can be seen in the convergence of energy, the increase in its relative error compared to the errors provided by the indicators (both relative and overall), the variations in the indicators' efficiency indices and their correct verification of the saturation hypothesis.

### 4.4 Remarks

In **thermo-mechanical, different adaptation strategies of mesh** offer to the user:

- n-adapt the mesh only to a thermal criterion,
- idem according to a mechanical criterion,
- First adapt according to a thermal criterion, then according to a mechanical criterion (two separate adjustment loops).
- adapt together according to a thermal and then mechanical criterion (a loop as in this test case),
- adapt according to a thermo-mechanical criterion.

In *Code_Aster* there is no access to explicitly thermomechanical indicators, although mechanical indicators may incidentally have thermal dependence.

Depending on the study needs (rather thermal or rather mechanical, converge a mesh overall, better account for some boundary conditions ...) one can put in place in the code, one of the first four strategies.

**The good practice in a thermo-mechanical calculation is to use the elements** ${P}_{1}$ **heat-lights and the** ${P}_{2}$ **mechanically, this leads to the use of two meshes and interpolation of the linear thermal field solution on the quadratic mechanical mesh (via** **PROJ_CHAMP).**

However, if you want to work with only one mesh, you can easily decline one of the first four strategies via MACR_ADAP_MAIL's MAJ_CHAM option. This allows, while adapting the mesh to a thermal (mechanical) criterion, to update the complementary, mechanical (thermal) field on the new mesh adapted.

The objectives of this test case are multiple, they are:

- familiarise yourself and put into practice the two dual problems: calculation of the map d'indicator d'error and adaptation strategies of mesh. On standard cases, but also on pathological cases and for calculation links,
- detail the various parameters of the operators involved (CALC_ERREUR, MACR_ADAP_MAIL) and related operators that may be particularly interesting for these issues (INFO_MAILLAGE, MACR_INFO_MAIL, PROJ_CHAMP...),
- The aim is to provide "good practice" advice for the quality of studies and the use of tools already available on the subject. The only focus is on the mesh geometry aspects, mesh proper and the d-type of elements finished. The problems of time step, the calibration of numerical parameters and the sensitivity aspects to the data are not discussed here,
- d--to illustrate the tremendous potential and facilities that the ASTER/PYTHON coupling allows in the command file of a study (test, loop, display, calculation, personal macro-command, interactivity...).The official test cases being calibrated to work in batch, some of these aspects have therefore been "commentarized" in the command file.

D-a-point view **computer validation** , this test case allows of course to test the non-regression of different mapping couplings d-errors/process refinement-derefining in thermo-mechanical, but also the options of "pre and post-processings" of these calculations (smoothing of stresses and thermal fluxes to nodes, switching from an error by element to an error at nodes by element).
