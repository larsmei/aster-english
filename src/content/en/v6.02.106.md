# v6.02.106 SSNL106 - Elastoplastic beam in pure traction and bending

**Summary:**

This test validates the elastoplasticity in a straight beam in traction and bending, for perfect elastoplastic behavior or linear nutring as well as options for the post-processing beams.

- Static analysis,
- Elastic behaviour,
- Elastoplastic behaviour,
- 3 sections: rectangular, circular full, circular hollow,
- 1 types of hardening: linear (VMIS_ISOT_LINE and VMIS_ISOT_NL reduced in linear case)

The modellings allows testing of elements POU_D_E (SEULEMENT IN ELASTICITY), POU_D_TGM, POU_D_EM, TUYAU_3M (3 and 4 nodes) COQUE_3D.

The test validates the operation of the integration of these constitutive laws and the resolution algorithm until the complete beam plasification.

One of the modellings allows testing the operation of DYNA_NON_LINE on a quasi-static tensile calculation of a beam modeled in POU_D_TGM.

In addition, G Modelling is used to test the factor keyword ETAT_INIT operation with the STRX_ELGA structure field.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

#### 2.1.1 Pure Flexion - Linear Scoring

analytical solution :

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.106/1000000000000143000000E95CF50480D0B7394D.png)

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.106/100000000000012D000000E6ED2D82D98F367544.png)

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\varphi y$ $\varphi$: curvature

Calculation of the moment by:

> $M(u)={\int}_{s}{\sigma}_{xx}(y).y\mathrm{ds}$
> ${\sigma}_{xx}=E{\varepsilon}_{xx}$ for $0\le ∣y∣\le u$
> ${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{y}+H({\varepsilon}_{xx}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E})$
> for $u<\mid y\mid \le v$

We get:

**for rectangular section:**

$\frac{M}{{M}_{e}}=(1-\frac{H}{E})(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{2}{(\frac{{\varphi}_{e}}{\varphi})}^{2})+\frac{H\varphi }{E}/\varphi$ with ${\varphi}_{e}=\frac{{M}_{e}}{\mathrm{EI}}{M}_{e}=\frac{{I}_{z}\cdot {\sigma}^{y}}{v}$

**for the circular section:**

$M(\mu )=\frac{{R}^{3}{\sigma}^{y}}{E}\left[\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{H}{\mu}+\frac{4}{3}(E-H){(1-{\mu}^{2})}^{3/2}+\frac{E-H}{2\mu }(\mathrm{Arc}\sin\mu -\mu (1-2{\mu}^{2})\sqrt{1-{\mu}^{2}})\right]$

with $\mu =\frac{u}{R}=\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{\mathrm{ER}\varphi }=\frac{{\varphi}_{e}}{\varphi}$

In discharge, after reaching the laden limit load, a limit load of sign to the contrary is obtained.

**for tubular section:**

(Navier-Bernoulli beam hypothesis)

The limit load ($H=0$ ) is equal to:

$\frac{M}{{M}_{e}}=\frac{4}{\pi}$

The complete solution for a thin tube is bib1:

$\frac{M(\mu )}{{M}_{e}}=\frac{\lambda}{\mu}+\frac{2(1-\lambda )}{\pi \mu }(\mathrm{arc}\sin\mu +\mu \sqrt{1-{\mu}^{2}})$ with $\lambda =\frac{{E}_{T}}{E}=\frac{H}{E+H}$

#### 2.1.2 Traction - Linear Scoring

Analytical solution: we have $N=S{\sigma}_{y}(1-\frac{H}{E})+\frac{\mathrm{HS}}{L}.\mathrm{DX}$ immediately.

#### 2.1.3 Pure Traction - Elasticity

analytical solution :

$N=\frac{\mathrm{E.S.}\delta u}{L}$ $\sigma =\frac{\mathrm{E.}\delta u}{L}$ , with $\delta u=7.5E-03$

#### 2.1.4 Pure Flexion - Elasticity

analytical solution :

$\mathrm{Mfz}=\frac{3.0\delta uEI}{{L}^{2}}$ $\sigma =\frac{{M}_{\mathrm{fz}}.h}{I}$ , with $\delta u=7.5E-03$

with $h=R$ for circular section, $h=v$ for rectangular section.

### 11.3 Note

The particular feature of $L$ Modelling is to test the operation of DYNA_NON_LINE in the quasi-static traction calculation of a beam modelled in POU_D_TGM. This type of Modelling has the particularity of making appear zero pivots on the lines of the mass matrix corresponding to the degrees of freedom of the spin. In this case, the initialization of the NEWMARK schema is no longer done by inversion of the mass matrix, which is singular, but by zeroing of the initial acceleration.

## 12 Summary of the results

Modellings multifibre beam solutions provide a solution to less than $2.5\text{\%}$ of the analytical solution, for a very low calculation time, compared to modellings pipe and hull. The only approximation is from the mesh section.

As for the modellings hose and hull, the conclusions are the same, but this time the difference with the analytical solution comes from this solution which is valid for a very thin tubular section beam, without ovalization effect. This ovalization is blocked at the ends, which allows to obtain a solution less than $0.4\text{\%}$ of the analytical solution.
