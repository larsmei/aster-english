# v6.08.104 SSND104 - Validation of the DRUCK_PRAG_N_A behavior

**Summary:**

This test concerns the case of a single mesh cell for both modellings A and B to compare the Drucker-Prager behaviors in the associated and non-associated version. In Modelling A, the idea is to choose a P_ULTM parameter large enough for both laws to be assimilated to perfect plasticity. Therefore, a cross validation of these two behaviors is undertaken. In Modelling B, the material parameters are more common and the goal is to have a validation in NON-REGRESSION.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.104/100000000000029C00000258BC7ED10621CE11D6.png)

**Figure 1.1-a**

The geometry refers to a single square isoparametric finite element, the length of each edge is 1.

### 1.2 Material properties

The massif consists of elasto-plastic material modelled by a Drucker Prager constitutive law, associated or not.

The material elasticities parameters are as follows:

- Young's modulus isotropic elasticity: $E={10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$
- Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0,3$
- Actual constant density: $\rho =2764\mathit{kg}.{m}^{-3}$
- Isotropic thermal expansion coefficient: $\alpha =0$

The nut-work characteristics are then given by:

- Pressure dependency coefficient: $\alpha =0,328$
- Elasticity limit: ${\sigma}_{y}=2.11\times {10}^{6}$
- Ultimate Constraint: ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ULTM}}=1.0\times {10}^{6}$

**For modelling A:**

Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation: ${P}_{\mathrm{ULT}}=2$

**For modelling B:**

Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation: ${P}_{\mathit{ULT}}=1.225\times {10}^{-2}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A unit displacement is imposed on the $y$ axis on the $\mathrm{CD}$ segment, zero on the $y$ axis on the $\mathrm{AB}$ segment and zero on the $x$ segment on the $\mathrm{AD}$ segment.

In this case of test, concerning Modelling A, we simulate a case of perfect plasticity with the two laws of behavior Drucker-Prager in associated and non-associated condition to verify the computer consistency of the results. To do this, just take large values for ${P}_{\mathit{ULT}}$ .

## 4 Summary of the results

This test allows for cross validation between two modellings, the idea was to fix in model A, a P_ULTM parameter large enough for both laws to be assimilated to perfect plasticity, while in model B, the material parameters are more consistent with typical soil data. In this case, the validation is in NON-REGRESSION. This example of non-regression allowed testing the influence of the P_ULTM parameter between the two modellings.
