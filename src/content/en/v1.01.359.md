# v1.01.359 ZZZZ359 – Validation of POST_CZM_FISS / OPTION macro-command = 'TRIAXIALITE'

**Summary**

This test validates the functionality available under keyword OPTION = 'TRIAXIALITE' of POST_CZM_FISS [U4.86.02](doc:u4.86.02) Macro Command. In this case, the macro command provides a sized card with mesh cells number bearing cohesive elements (joint or interface), containing the value of the average triaxiality rate in the elements of the mass directly connected to each of these mesh cells. The objective is then to use this card as a control variable in the AFFE_MATERIAU (AFFE_VARC / CHAM_GD) [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03) operator to vary the parameters of the cohesive law with triaxiality.

This feature is valid on a simple uniaxial traction problem, for which one has a analytical solution: it is a linear elastic bar pressed in traction, at the center of which is arranged a discontinuity governed by cohesive behavior. For this material a zero Poisson's ratio is chosen to maintain a uniaxial state since the modellings of this test is performed in dimension 2 and 3. The two modellings of this test have the following characteristics:

- Modelling A: 3D Modelling for the massif, 3D_INTERFACE Modelling for the cohesive interface, the bar is fully modelled;
- Modelling B: D_PLAN Modelling for the massif, PLAN_JOINT Modelling for the cohesive interface, only half of the bar (symmetry condition) is modelled.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A one-dimensional $\mathrm{2L}=199\mathit{mm}$ length bar is considered, at the centre of which is a discontinuity governed by cohesive behaviour.

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.359/100002010000053800000128C82A270D778CF653.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: One-dimensional tensile bar**

The space variable is noted $\widehat{x}$, and the center of the bar is located by $\widehat{x}=0$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The behaviour of the massif is linear elastic:

- Young's modulus: $E=30000\mathit{MPa}$ *
- Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0$

The cohesive behaviour is described by a softening affine law (CZM_TAC_MIX for interface elements used in Modelling A, and CZM_LIN_REG for joint elements used in Modelling B), with the following parameters:

- Critical constraint: ${\sigma}_{c}=3\mathit{MPa}$
- Breaking energy: ${G}_{c}=0.1N/\mathit{mm}$

The values of the additional numerical parameters are as follows:

- $\text{PENA\_LAGR}=45000$ for Modelling A (CZM_TAC_MIX)
- $\text{PENA\_ADHERENCE}={10}^{-4}$ for Modelling B (CZM_LIN_REG)

*Note:*

When only half the bar (as is the case for model B) is modelled by imposing a symmetry condition, the value of the breaking energy is divided by 2.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The problem is one-dimensional. The bar is pressed in traction, imposing the same $U$ intensity displacement at each end.

The displacement is increased by a ramp from $U=0$ to ${U}_{\mathit{test}}=2L\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}=0.0199\mathit{mm}$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

*Notes:*

> $u$: displacement;
> $\sigma$: constraint;
> $\epsilon$: deformation;
> $\delta =⟦u⟧$: displacement jump through the cohesive discontinuity;
> ${\delta}_{c}=\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}$: displacement jump corresponding to the breakage of the bar (zero stress).

This is a analytical solution. The problem is one-dimensional, all the quantities are scalar and the constraint is constant in space. The problem is symmetrical and can therefore be restricted to the $[0,L]$ interval. The following set of equations is available:

In the bar:

> $\sigma =E\epsilon$                                                                     (eq1)
> $\frac{\mathit{du}}{d\widehat{x}}=\epsilon$                                                                     (eq2)

In terms of discontinuity (cohesive affine law in softening regime):

> $\delta ={\delta}_{c}(1-\frac{\sigma}{{\sigma}_{c}})$                                                             (eq3)

By integrating (eq2) on the $[0,L]$ interval and using (eq1), the following relationship is obtained:

> $u(\widehat{x}=L)-u(\widehat{x}=0)=L\epsilon$ , i.e. $U-\frac{\delta}{2}=L\frac{\sigma}{E}$ (eq4)

${U}_{c}$ and ${U}_{f}$ are noted as the required displacements corresponding to the stress levels ${\sigma}_{c}$ (break-off threshold) and 0 (break-up material), respectively.

> ${U}_{c}=L\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}$ et ${U}_{f}=\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2}$                                                     (eq5)

The values chosen for the material parameters $E$ , ${\sigma}_{c}$ and ${G}_{c}$ , and for the length of the $L$ bar, are assumed to lead to a stable response of the bar (no "snap-backs"). In addition, the hypothesis of a monotonous loading is increased, so ${U}_{c}⩽U⩽{U}_{f}$ is in nonlinear mode. The values of the material and geometric parameters must therefore verify the following inequality:

> $L\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}<\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2}$ , i.e. $\frac{L}{E}<\frac{{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}^{2}}$ (eq6)

When this inequality is verified, the constraint can then be expressed in relation to the displacement imposed:

> $\sigma =\frac{\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2}-U}{\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2{\sigma}_{c}}-\frac{L}{E}},\forall U\in [\frac{L{\sigma}_{c}}{E},\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2}]$                                             (eq7)

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

At the moment corresponding to the ${U}_{\mathit{test}}=0.0199\mathit{mm}$ displacement, the $\sigma$ (space constant) constraint and the $u$ displacement to the point $\widehat{x}={0}^{+}$ (half displacement jump) are tested.

> $\sigma =\frac{\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2}-\mathrm{2L}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}}{\frac{{\delta}_{c}}{2{\sigma}_{c}}-\frac{L}{E}}$ digital application: $\sigma =1.72344975053\mathit{MPa}$
> $u(\widehat{x}={0}^{+})=2{\delta}_{c}(1-\frac{\sigma}{{\sigma}_{c}})$ digital application: $u(\widehat{x}={0}^{+})=0.0141838916607\mathit{mm}$

| **Identification** | **Reference type** | **Reference value** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Under load ${U}_{\mathit{test}}=0.0199\mathit{mm}$ , in total point: $\sigma$ | 'ANALYTIQUE' | $1.72344975053\mathit{MPa}$ |
| Under load ${U}_{\mathit{test}}=0.0199\mathit{mm}$ , in $\widehat{x}={0}^{+}$ : $u$ | 'ANALYTIQUE' | $0.0141838916607\mathit{mm}$ |

## 3 Test principle

The test consists of defining two $\mathit{MAT1}$ and $\mathit{MAT2}$ materials:

- For $\mathit{MAT1}$ , constant cohesive parameters are defined via DEFI_MATERIAU / RUPT_FRAG: $\text{GC}={G}_{c}$ and $\text{SIGM\_C}={G}_{c}$ ;
- For $\mathit{MAT2}$ , it is defined via DEFI_MATERIAU / RUPT_FRAG_FO that are cohesive parameters that are functions of triaxiality (noted $\alpha$ ): $\text{GC}=f(\alpha ).{G}_{c}$ and $\text{SIGM\_C}=f(\alpha ).{G}_{c}$ ;

In uniaxial traction the triaxiality is $\alpha =1/3$ , so we choose to define for $\mathit{MAT2}$ a linear dependence on triaxiality with a slope of $3$: $f(\alpha )=3\alpha$ . In this way: the values of the parameters of the cohesive law in $\mathit{MAT2}$ must remain constant and identical to those chosen in $\mathit{MAT1}$ throughout the load history.

The test shall then be conducted as follows:

1. $\mathit{MAT1}$ material definition;
2. first call to STAT_NON_LINE with $\mathit{MAT1}$ up to a required displacement level below ${U}_{\mathit{test}}$;
3. call POST_CZM_FISS on the mechanical result previously obtained;
4. $\mathit{MAT2}$ material definition by using the triaxiality board obtained with POST_CZM_FISS as the control variable;
5. with the material $\mathit{MAT1}$ , further calculation with STAT_NON_LINE from the mechanical state obtained at point 2 to the loading ${U}_{\mathit{test}}$ ;
6. with the material $\mathit{MAT2}$ , further calculation with STAT_NON_LINE from the mechanical state obtained at point 2 to the loading ${U}_{\mathit{test}}$ .

It is then ensured that the results obtained under the ${U}_{\mathit{test}}$ loading in steps 5 and 6 are in agreement with the analytical solution presented earlier.
