# r7.01.45 Constitutive law RGI_BETON_BA

**Summary:**

The model presented in this document is an enrichment of a model already effective in Code_Aster: RGI_BETON (cf. R7.01.30). It makes it possible to consider reinforcements distributed within a concrete mass without differentiating their mesh. The behaviours of the two materials are evaluated and homogenised in order to deduce from them the response of the reinforced-concrete material.

**Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The model presented in this document is a development of a model already effective in Code_Aster: RGI_BETON. This document therefore follows on from that one [r7.01.30](doc:r7.01.30), since only the development will be described here.

It makes it possible to consider reinforcements distributed within a concrete mass without differentiating their mesh. The behaviours of the two materials are evaluated independently and then homogenised in order to deduce from them the response of the reinforced-concrete material.

## 2 Constitutive law RGI_BETON_BA

### 2.1 Constitutive law of the homogenised reinforced concrete

This law therefore makes it possible to consider the reinforced concrete as a single material and thus to avoid differentiating the mesh of the concrete from that of the reinforcements. The evaluation of its behaviour is obtained by combining the contribution of the reinforcement and of the concrete by a homogenised constitutive law. In the model presented here, taken from the work of , up to five reinforcement densities may be considered. These are defined according to an index *r* , with which are associated a direction $\vec{{V}^{r}}$, a surface density ${\rho}^{r}$, and other material parameters.

The material then being defined as reinforced concrete, the parameters supplied in ELAS in order to approach the solution therefore correspond to those specific to the reinforced concrete. The response of this material being obtained from the contributions of the concrete and of the steel evaluated independently, it is therefore a matter of supplying to the model the parameters specific to the concrete alone. Considering ${E}^{\mathit{be}}$ the modulus of the concrete alone and ${E}^{r}$ the modulus of the reinforcement, the latter may be evaluated by the following formula,

$$
{E}^{\mathit{ELAS}}=(1-\rho^r ){E}^{\mathit{be}}+\rho^r {E}^{r}
$$

The concrete constitutive model with which this development is associated taking damage into account, it is therefore a matter of considering the impact of these reinforcements in undamaged zones and in damaged zones.

These zones being able to coexist within one and the same element, the same is true of the response of the reinforcement. In order to ensure this differentiation, the contribution of the reinforcement is decomposed according to the undamaged or damaged zone considered, respectively ${\sigma}_{ij}^{H}$ and ${\sigma}_{ij}^{R}$ , and they are weighted so as to consider the local damage of the concrete. Thus, in the cracked zone the stress ${\sigma}_{ij}^{R}$ is multiplied by the local damage of the concrete in the principal direction ${D}_{i}^{t}$ , and in the undamaged zone the stress ${\sigma}_{ij}^{H}$ is multiplied by its complement to 1. Finally, the global behaviour of the reinforced concrete is obtained by homogenising these stresses with that of the concrete matrix ${\sigma}_{ij}^{m}$ such that,

$$
{\sigma}_{ij}=\left(1-\sum_{r=1}^{\mathit{Nr}}{\rho}^{r}\right){\sigma}_{ij}^{m}+\left(1-{D}_{ijkl}^{t}\right){\sigma}_{kl}^{H}+{D}_{ijkl}^{t}\bullet {\sigma}_{kl}^{R}
$$

#### 2.1.1 Contribution in the undamaged zone

In this model, the participation of the reinforcement is limited to that of its axial direction. Its contributions ${\sigma}_{i}^{H}$ and ${\sigma}_{i}^{R}$ , respectively in the undamaged zone and in the cracked zone, are evaluated from this stress ${\sigma}^{r}$ . In the undamaged zone, ${\sigma}_{i}^{H}$ is such that,

$$
{\sigma}_{ij}^{H}=\sum_{r=1}^{\mathit{Nr}}{\rho}^{r}{\sigma}^{r}{(\vec{{V}^{r}}\text{⨂}\vec{{V}^{r}})}_{ij}
$$

#### 2.1.2 Contributions in the damaged zone

In the cracked zone, the orientation of the reinforcement may evolve if the normal to the crack is not aligned with it. This is the Dowell effect, also called the Goujon effect. In the model, and within the crack, only the part of the reinforcement is considered. In addition to the evolution of its direction, it is therefore also a matter of taking into account the impact of the damage on its contribution.

First, the new direction of the reinforcement ${\vec{D}}^{r}$ is evaluated. Crossing a crack of the concrete, it therefore lies between its initial direction $\vec{{V}^{r}}$ and the principal direction of the crack ${\vec{V}}^{I}$ . It should be noted that, this document detailing only the development relating to the homogenised reinforcements, the detail of the computation of ${\vec{V}}^{I}$ is not presented here, and may be found in the document of the initial model ([r7.01.36](doc:r7.01.36))

The new direction may then be written such that,

$$
{\vec{D}}^{r}=\cos({\alpha}^{r})\vec{{V}_{r}}+\sin({\alpha}^{r})\cdot \frac{\vec{{g}_{I}^{r}}}{\Vert \vec{{g}_{I}^{r}}\Vert }
$$

With:

- ${\vec{g}}_{I}^{r}$ the displacement between the crack opening ${w}_{I}^{\mathit{pl},t}$ and its projection onto the initial direction of the reinforcement $\vec{{V}^{r}}$ such that,

$$
{\vec{g}}_{I}^{r}={w}_{I}^{\mathit{pl},t}\left(\vec{{V}^{I}}-\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{I}}\right)\cdot {\vec{V}}^{r}\right)
$$

- ${\alpha}^{r}$ the angle separating the initial reinforcement direction $\vec{{V}^{r}}$ from its new direction ${\vec{D}}^{r}$,

$$
{\alpha}^{r}=2\arcsin{\left(\sqrt{\frac{{g}_{I}^{r}}{4{R}^{r}}}\right)}\le\arccos{\left(\vec{{V}^{I}}\cdot{\vec{V}}^{r}\right)}
$$

- ${R}^{r}$ the radius of curvature obtained such that,

$$
{R}^{r}=\frac{\pi {D}_{r}^{\mathit{eq}}{\sigma}^{r}}{4{f}_{c}}
$$

If several reinforcements cross the same crack, the whole of their contributions is considered. These are weighted by the corresponding local damage, then summed and brought back into the local basis by means of the passage matrix $P$. The evaluation of these contributions is initiated by the definition of a first computation matrix $R$, and they are subsequently deduced from its symmetric part such that,

$$
R=\sum_{r}{\rho}^{r}{\sigma}^{r}{P}^{T}[{D}_{I}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{I}}\right){\vec{D}}_{I}^{r}];{P}^{T}[{D}_{\mathit{II}}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{\mathit{II}}}\right){\vec{D}}_{\mathit{II}}^{r}];{P}^{T}[{D}_{\mathit{III}}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{\mathit{III}}}\right){\vec{D}}_{\mathit{III}}^{r}]
R=\sum_{r}{\rho}^{r}{\sigma}^{r}\left(\begin{array}{c}{P}^{T}[{D}_{I}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{I}}\right){\vec{D}}_{I}^{r}]\\ {P}^{T}[{D}_{\mathit{II}}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{\mathit{II}}}\right){\vec{D}}_{\mathit{II}}^{r}]\\ {P}^{T}[{D}_{\mathit{III}}^{t}\left(\vec{{V}^{r}}\cdot \vec{{V}^{\mathit{III}}}\right){\vec{D}}_{\mathit{III}}^{r}]\end{array}\right)
$$

$$
{D}_{ijkl}^{t}\bullet {\sigma}_{kl}^{R}={\left[P\left[\frac{1}{2}\left[R+{R}^{T}\right]\right]{P}^{T}\right]}_{ij}
$$

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.45/1000000000000328000002DBCFCD3CBE00F9BF2D.png)

*Mechanisms ensuring the Dowel effect (a); Case of a more limited effect (b)*

It should be noted that this effect is made possible by the confinement zones developed between the reinforcements and the concrete, as in r7.01.45-dowel_effect (a). If the number of reinforcements is more limited, r7.01.45-dowel_effect (b), the impact of this Dowel effect is greatly reduced.

Finally, it should be noted that in the case of elements of large size the representation of cracking is not often explicit. It is nevertheless possible, for these reinforced elements, and by considering an anchorage length of the steel and the maximum distance between two cracks, to obtain the number of localised cracks.

### 2.2 Constitutive law of the axial contribution of the reinforcement

The contribution of the reinforcement is considered in its axial direction only. The constitutive law which defines it allows plasticity and relaxation to be taken into account. The strain of the reinforcement is decomposed according to its plastic part ${\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}$ , and its elastic and permanent relaxation parts, ${\epsilon}^{r,K}$ and ${\epsilon}^{r,M}$ .

Moreover, it is also possible to consider densities of active reinforcements by considering a value of initial prestress ${\sigma}_{0}^{r}$ such that,

$$
{\sigma}^{r}-{\sigma}_{0}^{r}={E}^{r}({\epsilon}^{r}-{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}-{\epsilon}^{r,M}-{\epsilon}^{r,K})
$$

The plastic strain is evaluated by a criterion function making it possible to take into account the hardening of the reinforcement. Considering the hardening modulus and the elastic limit, respectively ${H}^{r}$ and ${f}_{y}^{r}$ , the criterion function is written,

$$
{f}^{r}={\sigma}^{r}-{H}^{r}{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}-{f}_{y}^{r}\le 0
$$

#### 2.2.1 Relaxation

As seen in the equation , the model makes it possible to consider a prestress. The present model having the purposes of a structural analysis, the relaxation phenomena must therefore be considered. These being strongly affected by the value of the initial tension as well as by the temperature, a relaxation model considering these effects has been implemented by  . In the same way as the creep model implemented for the concrete [cf. Code_Aster document RGI_BETON], this is ensured by various coefficients affecting the instantaneous characteristics of the reinforcements considered. The model is composed of a first elastic part ensuring the instantaneous behaviour, followed by a Kelvin stage ensuring the share of the viscoelastic strains, and of a third level governing the share of permanent strain, ensured by a Maxwell element r7.01.45-shema_rheo.

##### 2.2.1.1 Maxwell stage

The permanent part of the relaxation is evaluated in a manner analogous to that used by/in /[r7.01.36](doc:r7.01.36) for the modelling of the permanent creep strains. It is evaluated as a function of the ratio of the elastic strain ${\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}$ divided by the product of the characteristic Maxwell time ${\tau}^{r,M}$, a material parameter, and the consolidation coefficient $\mathit{Cc}$,

$$
\frac{\partial {\epsilon}^{r,M}}{\partial t}=\frac{{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}}{{\tau}^{M}\mathit{Cc}}
$$

With $\mathit{Cc}$ a function of the viscous strain reached at the current step, and of a coefficient $k$, the relaxation-amplitude coefficient,

$$
\mathit{Cc}=\frac{1}{k}\exp\left(\frac{1}{k}\frac{{\epsilon}^{r,M}}{{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}}\right)
$$

In this equation, $k$ makes it possible to consider the non-linear impact of the temperature ($T$) and of the loading ratio ($M$) on the relaxation phenomenon,

$$
k={k}_{\mathit{ref}}{k}^{T}{k}^{M}
$$

With,

- ${k}_{\mathit{ref}}$ , a parameter expressing the relaxation capacity of the material, obtained by considering the parameters ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{ref}}^{K}$ and ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ref}}$, respectively the reference potential of the relaxation and the loading ratio at which this potential was fixed, such that,

$$
{k}_{\mathit{ref}}=E\frac{{\epsilon}_{\mathit{ref}}^{K}}{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ref}}}
$$

- ${k}^{M}$ , making it possible to consider the non-linear amplification of the relaxation rate with the loading ratio,

$$
{k}^{M}=\frac{{\mu}^{\mathit{cr}}}{{\mu}^{\mathit{cr}}-\mu }
$$

$\mu$ being the loading ratio defined as a function of the elastic limit ${f}_{y}$ such that,

$$
\mu =\frac{\sigma}{{f}_{y}}
$$

and ${\mu}^{\mathit{cr}}$ the critical loading ratio, expressing the non-linear tendency of the material to increase its relaxation rate with the increase of the loading ratio, a function of $\chi$ the non-linear relaxation coefficient (defined such that the tendency is linear for $\chi=1$ and becomes non-linear for $\chi>1$),

$$
{\mu}^{\mathit{cr}}=\frac{2}{3}\frac{\chi }{\chi-1}
$$

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.45/10000000000002640000020A26C1DF27321AB57F.png)

*Non-linear amplification function ${k}^{M}$ taken from*

- ${k}^{T}$ , the coefficient governing the acceleration of the relaxation with the increase of the temperature, and ensuring the non-linear evolution for high loading levels,

$$
{k}^{T}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\exp\left[{A}^{T}{(T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})}^{{n}^{r}}\right]\phantom{1111}\mathit{si}\phantom{1}T>{T}_{\mathit{ref}}\\ \exp\left[-{A}^{T}{({T}_{\mathit{ref}}-T)}^{{n}^{r}}\right]\phantom{11}\mathit{si}\phantom{1}T⩽{T}_{\mathit{ref}}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The non-linear evolution is governed by ${A}^{T}$, the thermal-activation coefficient, a function of the loading ratio,

$$
{A}^{T}={A}_{\mathit{ref}}^{T} \exp{\left(\gamma \cdot {\mu}^{\mathit{TM}}\right)}
$$

With ${A}_{\mathit{ref}}^{T}$ the reference value for the thermal-activation coefficient, $\gamma$ the coupling coefficient for the effect of the stresses on the activation energy, ${\mu}^{\mathit{TM}}$ the loading ratio which may modify the thermal activation. Considering ${\mu}^{\mathit{thr}}$ the minimum threshold of loading ratio for affecting the thermal-activation coefficient, fixed at $0.7$, ${\mu}^{\mathit{TM}}$ is written such that,

$$
{\mu}^{\mathit{TM}}(t+\mathit{dt})=max({\mu}^{\mathit{TM}}(t),\mu (t+\mathit{dt}),{\mu}^{\mathit{thr}})
$$

##### 2.2.1.2 Kelvin stage

Concerning the viscoelastic behaviour, and as developed by , this is ensured by a Kelvin element. In the same way as the permanent strains, these are also affected by the temperature,

$$
\frac{\partial {\epsilon}^{K}}{\partial t}=\frac{1}{{\tau}^{r,K}}\left(\frac{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{el}}}{{\Psi}^{K}}-{\epsilon}^{K}\right)
$$

With ${\tau}^{r,k}$ and ${\Psi}^{k}$ respectively the characteristic time for the Kelvin strains and the rate of this relaxation with respect to the elastic strain, a function of reference values and of ${k}^{T}$, the thermal-activation multiplier coefficient defined in the equation ,

$$
{\tau}^{r,K}=\frac{{\tau}_{\mathit{ref}}^{r,K}}{{k}^{T}}
$$

$$
{\Psi}^{K}=\frac{{\Psi}_{\mathit{ref}}^{K}}{{k}^{T}}
$$

It should be noted that this part of the model has been validated at the scale of the laboratory structure and applied to a large-scale structure .

#### 2.2.2 Steel-concrete bond

In the version of the present model, the assumption of perfect bond is made between the two materials, steel and concrete. In this way, the total axial strain of the reinforcement is evaluated from its elongation, assumed to be determined from the strain matrix of the concrete $\overline{\overline{\epsilon}}$ such that,

$$
{\epsilon}^{r}\approx {\epsilon}_{r}+\frac{1}{2}{\gamma}_{r}^{2}
$$

With ${\epsilon}_{r}$ the axial strain of the reinforcement,

$$
{\epsilon}_{r}={(\vec{{V}^{r}})}^{t}\overline{\overline{\epsilon}}\vec{{V}^{r}}
$$

And ${\gamma}_{r}$ the shear strain

$$
{\gamma}_{r}=\Vert \overline{\overline{\epsilon}}\vec{{V}^{r}}-{\epsilon}_{r}\vec{{V}^{r}}\Vert
$$

It should be noted that taking a slip at this interface into account is possible but is not implemented in this present model. The latter requiring non-local computations, it is not presented here but may be found in .

## 4 General flowchart of the solution

After evaluation of the behaviour of the concrete, the model evaluates the contribution of the reinforcement. This document aiming to explain only this contribution, that of the concrete will not be described here. Its detailed constitutive law is presented in the document [r7.01.30](doc:r7.01.30). The flowchart presented below summarises the principal stages of the solution of the analysis for taking into account reinforcements homogenised with the concrete.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.45/organigramme.png)

*Solution flowchart*

As said previously, the first consideration induced by these homogenised reinforcements is the assumption of perfect bond. By virtue of this fact, the final strain of the concrete is recovered and the stress in the reinforcement is deduced from it. The stress is subsequently subjected to a plasticity criterion. If the latter is active, the reinforcement has plasticised and an iteration is performed in order to re-evaluate the value of its stress, taking into account the plastic strain induced.

Once the stress of the reinforcement has been obtained, it is the damage of the concrete that one will consider. Indeed, if the reinforced element is cracked, one considers the Goujon effect in order to evaluate the resultant in the cracked part as well as its reduction in the undamaged part. Finally, the stress of the reinforced concrete is deduced from it by combining these stresses with those of the concrete by means of the homogenisation law.

## 5 Numerical integration

In this paragraph, the numerical integration of the taking into account of the distributed reinforcements is presented. The model has been integrated according to an implicit scheme.

### 5.1 Numerical integration scheme

The implicit integration scheme consists of a viscoelastic prediction phase which takes creep into account. It is followed by a plastic correction phase in the space of the effective stresses if at least one of the criteria is reached.

The pseudo-vector notation (symmetric tensor) is adopted.

#### 5.1.1 Viscoelastic prediction phase

The following three equations relate the increments of elastic strain and of creep (Maxwell and Kelvin) to the total strain. The scheme is semi-implicit and $\theta$ is equal to $1/2$,

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{tot}}=\Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{r,K}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{r,M}\\ \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,K}\left(\frac{\Delta t}{{\tau}^{r,K}}+\theta \right)+{\epsilon}^{r,K}=\frac{1}{{\Psi}^{K}}\left({\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}+\theta \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}\right)\\ \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,M}=\frac{\Delta t}{{\tau}^{r,M}{\mathit{Cc}}^{r}}\left({\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}+\theta \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}\right)\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

Summarising,

$$
\phantom{1}\iff \overline{\overline{L}}\cdot \Delta \overline{X}=\overline{Y}
$$

With $\overline{\overline{L}}$ established in the principal basis of $d'\epsilon$ and $\overline{X}=\left(\begin{array}{c}d{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}\\ d{\epsilon}^{r,M}\\ d{\epsilon}^{r,K}\end{array}\right)$

Thus, one has,

$$
\phantom{1}\iff \overline{X}={\overline{\overline{L}}}^{-1}\cdot \overline{Y}
$$

$$
{\overline{X}}^{t}={\overline{X}}^{-t}+\Delta {\overline{X}}^{t}
$$

$$
\Delta {\overline{X}}^{t}\to \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}
$$

The stress increment is written,

$$
\Delta {\sigma}^{r}={E}^{r}\cdot \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{el}}
$$

Thus,

$$
{\sigma}^{r}={\sigma}_{0}^{r}+\Delta {\sigma}^{r}
$$

#### 5.1.2 Correction phase

After the viscoelastic prediction phase, an evaluation of the criteria is carried out,

$$
{f}^{r}=\Delta {\sigma}^{r}-{f}_{y}^{r}
$$

With,

$$
{\Delta \sigma }^{r}={\sigma}^{r}-{E}^{r}\cdot \Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}
$$

If the criterion is activated, one carries out a flow considering the correction for the associated creep,

$$
{f}_{y}^{r}-\Delta {\sigma}^{r}=-{f}^{r}
$$

With an associated flow law,

$$
\Delta {\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}=\frac{{f}^{r}}{{E}^{r}{H}^{r}}
$$

The stress variations entail a viscoelastic correction, which obliges one to couple the viscoelastic predictor equations with those of the radial return; the following system is then obtained and solved,

$$
[\overline{Z}]\left(\begin{array}{c}d{\epsilon}_{e}\\ d{\epsilon}_{M}\\ d{\epsilon}_{K}\\ d{\epsilon}^{r,\mathit{pl}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\\ -{f}^{r}\end{array}\right)
$$

With $[\overline{Z}]$ the matrix containing all the coefficients deduced from the conditions of vanishing of the criterion and from the viscoelastic behaviour.

The increments are used in order to obtain the admissible values of the state variables at the end of the step. These may then be used to compute the damage in accordance with the solution algorithm.
