# u2.02.02 User's guide for the TUYAU_* elements

**Summary:**

This document is a user's guide for the TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M modellings.

The TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M finite elements correspond to line elements for straight or curved piping. The kinematics of the TUYAU elements combines both a beam kinematics, which describes the overall motion of the piping line, and a shell kinematics, which provides the description of the swelling, of the ovalisation and of the warping of the cross-section.

These modellings can be used for problems of relatively thick three-dimensional piping, in linear or non-linear mechanical analysis and in small displacements.

## 1 Introduction

The TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M finite elements correspond to line elements for straight or curved piping. They rely on a Timoshenko beam kinematics for the displacements and the rotations of the mean fibre and on a shell kinematics for the strains of the cross-section (ovalisation, warping, swelling). These transverse strains are decomposed into Fourier series. The TUYAU_3M modelling takes into account 3 modes at most, whereas the TUYAU_6M modelling takes into account 6 Fourier modes.

These modellings can be used for problems of relatively thick three-dimensional piping, only in linear or non-linear mechanical analysis and in small displacements. At present, no thermal or acoustic analysis is possible.

This document presents the capabilities of the TUYAU modelling available since version 6 of *Code_Aster*. The capabilities of this type of modelling are presented first; the formulation of the finite elements and their differences with respect to the beam modellings are then briefly recalled. The list of the options available for each of the elements is also given. The document ends with the presentation of a few academic test cases and, finally, with a few recommendations for use.

The straight or curved pipe elements are grouped under the modellings TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M. The computation options are defined in this document. The present capabilities of these pipe elements are the following:

| straight or curved piping lines, |
| --- |
| line element with 3 nodes (SEG3) or with 4 nodes (SEG4), |
| relatively thick pipe: e/R<0.2 where e represents the thickness and R the radius of the cross-section, |
| internal pressure, in-plane and out-of-plane bending, torsion and extension, |
| small displacements, |
| elastoplastic constitutive behaviour in plane stress, or any incremental non-linear constitutive behaviour, |
| the cross-section may deform by: * swelling due to the internal pressure or to the Poisson effect, * ovalisation due to bending, * warping due to combined in-plane and out-of-plane bending. |

Compared with the TUYAU_3M modelling, the TUYAU_6M modelling provides a better approximation of the behaviour of the cross-section in the case where the latter deforms according to a high-order mode, for example in the case of thin tubes where the ratio of the thickness to the radius of the cross-section is $<0.1$ , and in the case of plasticity.

The TUYAU_3M modelling has 21 degrees of freedom per node (6 beam degrees of freedom and 15 shell degrees of freedom), whereas the TUYAU_6M modelling has 39 degrees of freedom per node (6 beam degrees of freedom and 33 shell degrees of freedom).

For the TUYAU_3M modelling, SEG3 and SEG4 mesh cells can be used. For the TUYAU_6M modelling, only SEG3 mesh cells can be used.

## 2 Modelling capabilities

### 2.1 Reminder of the formulation

#### 2.1.1 Geometry of the pipe elements

The methods and the modellings implemented for the pipe elements, which are presented in the reference document [R3.08.06](doc:r3.08.06), are recalled here.

For the pipe elements a mean fibre is defined, straight or curved ($x$  defines the curvilinear coordinate) and a hollow section of circular type. This section must be small with respect to the length of the piping. Figure [Figure 2.1.1-a] illustrates the two different configurations. A local frame $\mathit{oxyz}$ is associated with the mean fibre.

#### 2.1.2 Formulation of the pipe elements

The kinematics of the pipe [Figure] is made up of a shell kinematics which provides the description of the ovalisation, of the swelling and of the warping, and of a beam kinematics which describes the overall motion of the piping line. The displacement $U$ [Figure] of a material point of the pipe is made up of a macroscopic beam part (${U}^{P}$ ) and of an additional local shell part (${U}^{S}$ ): $U={U}^{P}+{U}^{S}$

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/10007694000069D500004C61C84610E4A3C91320.svg)

**Figure** 2.1.2-a: **Decomposition of the displacement into beam and shell fields.**

The formulation of the elements is based on:

- **Beam theory** for the kinematics of the mean fibre. If the full assumption of beam theory is made: the cross-sections associated with the beam displacements (${U}^{P}$ ), which are perpendicular to the reference **mean fibre**  [Figure 2.1.2-b] remain perpendicular to the mean fibre after deformation. The cross-section does not deform. This will be true on average in the TUYAU element. Beam theory is used only to describe the motion of the mean fibre: the mean fibre of the pipe is equivalent to the mean fibre of a beam. This kinematics makes it possible to describe the overall motion of the piping line.
- **Shell theory** to describe the strain of the cross-sections around the mean fibre. Kinematics of the cross-sections: the cross-sections which are perpendicular to the reference **mean surface**  remain straight. The material points located on the normal to the undeformed mean surface remain on a straight line in the deformed configuration. The formulation used is a LOVE-KIRCHHOFF type formulation without transverse shear for the description of the behaviour of the cross-sections. The thickness of the shell remains constant. The mean surface $\omega$ of the pipe, located at mid-thickness, is equivalent to the mean surface of a shell. This shell kinematics provides the description of the swelling, of the ovalisation and of the warping of the cross-section.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/10003B5E000069D500002DAFB80F32B4E77C752C.svg)

**Figure** 2.1.2-b **: Mean fibre and surface in the case of a straight pipe.**

The additional displacements (${U}^{s}$ ) of the surface of the pipe are approximated by a Fourier series up to order $M$ ($M=3$  for the TUYAU_3M modelling and $M=6$ for the TUYAU_6M modelling).

${u}^{s}(x,\Phi )=\sum_{m=2}^{M}{u}_{m}^{i}(x)\cosm\Phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{u}_{m}^{0}(x)\sinm\Phi$

${v}^{s}(x,\Phi )={w}_{1}^{i}(x)\sin\Phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{v}_{m}^{i}(x)\sinm\Phi -{w}_{1}^{0}(x)\cos\Phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{v}_{m}^{0}(x)\cosm\Phi$

${w}^{s}(x,\Phi )={w}^{0}+\sum_{m=2}^{M}{w}_{m}^{i}(x)\cosm\Phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{w}_{m}^{0}(x)\sinm\Phi$

| Where | ${u}^{s}$ : represents the axial displacement of the mean surface in the local frame xPhi zeta |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${v}^{s}$ : represents the circumferential displacement of the mean surface in the local frame xPhi zeta |
|  | ${w}^{s}$ : represents the radial displacement of the mean surface in the local frame xPhi zeta |
|  | ${w}^{0}$ : represents the swelling |

These elements therefore involve locally:

- 6 kinematic variables for the beam formulation: the displacements ${u}^{p}$ , ${v}^{p}$  and ${w}^{p}$ along the reference fibre and the rotations about the local axes,
- 3 kinematic variables for the shell formulation: the additional displacements ${u}^{s}$ , ${v}^{s}$ and ${w}^{s}$ in the frame of the mean surface,
- 4 stresses in the thickness of the pipe denoted $\mathrm{SIXX}$ ($\mathrm{sxx}$ ), $\mathrm{SIYY}$ ($\mathrm{sff}$ ), $\mathrm{SIXY}$ ($\mathrm{sxf}$ ), and $\mathrm{SIXZ}$ ($\mathrm{sxz}$ ). The stress $\mathrm{SIZZ}$ ($\mathrm{szz}$ ) is zero (plane-stress assumption). The transverse shear stresses are zero (Love Kirchhoff assumption),
- 4 strains in the thickness of the pipe denoted $\mathrm{EPXX}$ ($\mathrm{exx}$ ), $\mathrm{EPYY}$ ($\mathrm{eff}$ ), $\mathrm{EPXY}$ ($\mathrm{exf}$ ), and $\mathrm{EPXZ}$ ($\mathrm{sxz}$ ). The strain $\mathrm{EPZZ}$ ($\mathrm{ezz}$ ) is zero for the beam part.

**Important note**:

The beam kinematics is based on the Timoshenko assumption [R3.08.03](doc:r3.08.03). The pipe element is not "exact" at the nodes for concentrated loadings or torsors at the ends; several elements must be used in the mesh in order to obtain correct results.

*Along the mean fibre, these elements are of isoparametric type. It follows that the displacements vary as polynomials of order 2 along* $X$ *for the elements with 3 nodes and of order 3 for 4 nodes.*

### 2.2 Comparison with other elements

#### 2.2.1 The differences between the pipe elements

The pipe elements TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M are line elements:

- TUYAU_3M with three or four nodes.
- TUYAU_6M with three nodes.

These elements differ only in the approximation of the additional SHELL displacement field, which is carried out by a Fourier-series decomposition:

- TUYAU_3M up to order 3.
- TUYAU_6M up to order 6.

Consequently the number of degrees of freedom is different:

- TUYAU_3M 21 per node (6 beam degrees of freedom and 15 shell degrees of freedom)
- TUYAU_6M 39 per node (6 beam degrees of freedom and 33 shell degrees of freedom)

Compared with the TUYAU_3M modelling, the TUYAU_6M modelling provides a better approximation of the behaviour of the cross-section in the case where the latter deforms according to a high-order mode, for example in the case of thin tubes where the ratio of the thickness to the radius of the cross-section is $<0.1$ , and in certain cases in plasticity. In the lightly loaded zones, the TUYAU_3M modelling is recommended.

Finite elements for a straight or curved pipe

A difference in behaviour appears on the elbows present in the structure between the curved and the straight quadratic meshes. The mid-side node of the elements does not lie on the curvature line of the element following, for example, an operation of type CREA_MAILLAGE/LINE_QUAD .

This difference strongly modifies the stiffness characteristics of the element and also the account taken of the internal pressures (end effect); it is therefore advisable to ensure that the mesh follows the curvature of the elbow as closely as possible.

#### 2.2.2 The differences between the pipe elements and the beam elements

Like the TUYAU finite elements, the POUTRE finite elements also belong to the class of line finite elements. In this part the formulations and the loadings applicable to these two classes of elements are compared.

At the level of the formulation:

- **Element** **POUTRE:**

The formulation is based on an exact solution of the equations of the continuous model carried out for each element of the mesh. Several types of beam element are available:

- POU_D_E: the transverse shear is neglected, as is the rotary inertia. This assumption holds for high slendernesses (Euler assumption),
- POU_D_T: the transverse shear and all the inertia terms are taken into account. This assumption is to be used for low slendernesses (Timoshenko assumption).

These elements use mesh cells of type SEG2 with 6 degrees of freedom per node, 3 displacements and three rotations. The formulation of these elements is presented in the reference document [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). The section is constant; the only possible behaviour of the cross-sections is the translation and the rotation for all the points of the section. The section may be of any shape, constant or variable along the length.

- **Element** **TUYAU:**

The formulation combines both a beam formulation based on the Timoshenko assumption and a shell formulation based on the Love_Kirchhoff assumption, making it possible to model the phenomena of swelling, of ovalisation and of warping. The hollow section, of circular shape, is constant over the whole length of the element. The element is not "exact" at the nodes for concentrated loadings or torsors at the ends; several elements must therefore be used in the mesh in order to obtain correct results, in particular in order to represent the curvature.

These elements use mesh cells of type SEG3 or SEG4 with, for the beam kinematics, 6 degrees of freedom per node, 3 displacements and three rotations, and for the shell kinematics, 15 or 33 degrees of freedom of displacement type.

At the level of the **applicable loadings**:

- **Element** **POUTRE:**

The possible loadings are the extension, bending and torsion loadings. The internal-pressure loading for hollow sections does not exist (the section is non-deformable).

- **Element** **TUYAU:**

The TUYAU element admits the classical beam loadings as well as the application of an internal pressure.

## 3 Description of the command set

### 3.1 Assignment of a modelling and spatial discretisation

In this part, the choice and the assignment of one of the two TUYAU modellings are described, together with the degrees of freedom and the associated mesh cells. Most of the information described is taken from the User documentation [U3.11.06](doc:u3.11.06): TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M modellings.

#### 3.1.1 Degrees of freedom

The degrees of freedom are, at each node of the support mesh cell:

- the six displacement components of the mean fibre (three translations and three rotations),
- three degrees of freedom corresponding to modes 0 and 1,
- for each Fourier mode, 6 degrees of freedom ($U$  corresponds to the warping, $V$ and $W$ to the ovalisation: $V$ to the circumferential displacement, $W$ to the radial displacement, $I$ means "in plane" and $O$ means "out of plane").

#### 3.1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness and mass matrices

The support mesh cells of the finite elements, in a displacement formulation, are segments with 3 or 4 nodes.

| Modelling | Mesh cell | Finite element | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TUYAU_3M | SEG3 | METUSEG3 | Line mesh cell |
|  | SEG4 | MET3SEG4 | Line mesh cell |
| TUYAU_6M | SEG3 | MET6SEG3 | Line mesh cell |

The SEG4 mesh cells, which have cubic shape functions, were developed in order to solve a simple beam-bending problem. For this simple example, the exact solution is obtained with a single element on a SEG4 mesh cell.

For more complex problems, experience shows that one can mesh much more coarsely with SEG4 mesh cells. For example, about fifteen SEG3 elements are needed to obtain a correct solution for an elbow in bending, whereas half as many are needed with SEG4 elements.

**Note:**

> One can use the operator *MODI_MAILLAGE* to build *SEG4* mesh cells from *SEG3* mesh cells.

**Note:**

> The TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M elements are **under-integrated** in stiffness. The full integration of the stiffness matrices raised problems in the computation of the internal forces (non-physical oscillations), in particular for the shear forces.
> The TUYAU_3M elements on the SEG4 support mesh cells are also under-integrated in mass. Full integration can cause the appearance of non-physical eigenmodes on the degrees of freedom not associated with a beam kinematics.

#### 3.1.3 Support mesh cell of the loadings

All the loadings applicable to the elements used are treated by direct discretisation on the support mesh cell of the element in a displacement formulation. The pressure and the other line forces, as well as gravity, are examples of loadings applied directly on the element. No special loading mesh cell is therefore necessary.

### 3.2 Elementary characteristics: AFFE_CARA_ELEM

In this part, the characteristic operands of the pipe element are described. The User documentation of the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM is [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01).

| AFFE_CARA_ELEM |  | TUYAU_3M TUYAU_6M | Notes |

The characteristics which it is possible to assign on the TUYAU elements are:

- SECTION: 'CERCLE'

The section is defined by its external radius 'R' and its thickness 'EP', on each mesh cell, since the mesh is represented by the mean fibre of the pipe.

- TUYAU_NCOU : tncouch

This is the number of layers to be used for the integration of the non-linear constitutive relations in the thickness of the straight-pipe elements. In linear elasticity, one to two layers are sufficient; in the non-linear regime it is advisable to use between 3 and 5 layers. The number of Gauss points is equal to twice the number of layers plus one $(2\times \mathrm{tncouch}+1)$ , which means that the CPU time increases rapidly with the number of layers.

- TUYAU_NSEC : tnsec

This is the number of angular sectors to be used for the integration of the non-linear constitutive relations over the circumference of the straight-pipe elements. By default the number of sectors is 16. It is advisable to use 32 sectors in the non-linear regime for accurate results (beware of the increase in CPU time with the number of sectors).

- ORIENTATION('GENE_TUYAU')

Starting from one of the end nodes of the piping line, a continuous line drawn on the pipe is defined. The operands PRECISION and CRITERE make it possible to define the accuracy for the construction of the generatrix and the limit between a straight pipe element and a curved element.

**Note:**

*The direction vector of the line thus defined must not be collinear with the mean fibre of the elbow at the end node considered; by using the keyword* *INFO=2 one can check whether the defined vector is correct.*

#### 3.2.1 Operand MODI_METRIQUE

The operand MODI_METRIQUE makes it possible to define, for the TUYAU elements, the type of integration through the thickness:

- MODI_METRIQUE= 'NON' leads to assimilating, in the integrations, the radius to the mean radius. This is therefore valid for pipes of small thickness (relative to the radius),
- MODI_METRIQUE= 'OUI' implies a full integration, more accurate for thick piping, but which can in certain cases lead to oscillations of the solution.

**Note**

> The option MODI_METRIQUE='OUI' has been deactivated following the observation of incorrect results when it was used. For the moment, only the option MODI_METRIQUE='NON' is allowed.

#### 3.2.2 Generatrix and notion of local frame: keyword ORIENTATION

The generatrix drawn all along the piping makes it possible to define the origin of the angles $\Phi$  [Figure]. This is used:

- to interpret the ovalisation degrees of freedom;

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/100083D0000069D5000034EB783F3758E80B9F94.svg)

**Figure** 3.2.2-a: **Representation of two non-coplanar elbows connected by a straight pipe.**

For an end cross-section of the piping line [Figure], the user defines in AFFE_CARA_ELEM under the keyword ORIENTATION a vector whose projection onto the end cross-section defines a unit origin vector ${z}_{1}$.

The syntax is as follows:

```
AFFE_CARA_ELEM( # ...
    ORIENTATION= _F(
        GROUP_NO= 'EXTREMITE',
        CARA= ’GENE_TUYAU’,
        VALE = (x,y,z),
    )
)
```

where: EXTREMITE is the centre node of the end cross-section.

$(x,y,z)$ contains the 3 components of the vector orienting the generatrix of the pipe, to be projected onto the end cross-section. This vector must be defined at an end node or an end group_no of the pipe. The geometry is then built automatically for all the connected TUYAU elements.

The intersection between the direction of this vector and the mean surface of the elbow determines the trace of the generatrix on this section. ${x}_{1,}{y}_{1,}{z}_{1}$ denotes the right-handed triad associated with this section, where ${x}_{1}$ is the unit vector perpendicular to the cross-section. The intersection between the cross-section and the straight line issuing from the centre of this section and directed by ${z}_{k}$ is the trace of a generatrix represented below. For all the other cross-sections, the triad ${x}_{k},{y}_{k},{z}_{k}$ is obtained either by rotation of the triad ${x}_{k-1},{y}_{k-1},{z}_{k-1}$ in the case of the elbowed parts, or by translation of the triad ${x}_{k-1},{y}_{k-1},{z}_{k-1}$ for the straight parts of the piping.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/1000271E000069D5000060390D6ACE22D73BC89B.svg)

**Figure**  3.2.2-b: **Representation of the reference generatrix.**

The origin of the $\Phi$ common to all the elements is defined with respect to the trace of this generatrix on the cross-section. The angle between the trace of the generatrix and the current position on the cross-section is located by the angle $\Phi$ . The local frame of the straight and elbowed pipe is thus defined by the option ORIENTATION ('GENE_TUYAU') of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM, which makes it possible to define the first vector ${z}_{k}$ at one end.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/10005B0E000069D50000305FC5BBA25B78F39C59.svg)

**Figure** 3.2.2-c: **Local frame of the element** $\mathrm{XYZ}$ **.**

#### 3.2.3 Example of assignment of a characteristic

This example is a piping system comprising two elbows (Hoovgaard problem taken from the test SSLL101C).

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.02/10002178000069D5000030E3486C0C2AE088561B.svg)

- external diameter of the pipe: $0.185m$
- thickness of the pipe: $6.12m$
- radius of curvature of the elbows: $0.922m$

```python
MODELE=AFFE_MODELE(
    MAILLAGE=MAILLAGE,
    AFFE=_F(
        TOUT = 'OUI',
        PHENOMENE = 'MECANIQUE',
        MODELISATION = 'TUYAU_3M',
    ),
)

CARELEM=AFFE_CARA_ELEM(
    MODELE=MODELE,
    POUTRE=_F(
        GROUP_MA = 'TOUT_ELT',
        SECTION = 'CERCLE',
        CARA = ('R', 'EP',),
        VALE = (0.0925, 0.00612,),
    ),
    ORIENTATION=_F(
        GROUP_NO = 'C',
        CARA = 'GENE_TUYAU',
        VALE = (1., 0., 0.,),
    ),
)
```

### 3.3 Materials: DEFI_MATERIAU

The definition of the constitutive behaviour of a material is carried out by means of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01). There is no particular restriction due to the use of the TUYAU elements.

The materials used with all of the modellings may have elastic constitutive behaviours in plane stress whose linear characteristics are constant or a function of the temperature. The non-linear constitutive behaviours in plane stress are available for the pipe modellings. For more information on these non-linearities one may refer to [§ 2.6].

### 3.4 Loadings and boundary conditions: AFFE_CHAR_MECA and AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F

It is recalled that it is not possible to carry out thermal analyses; however, the assignment of a temperature is possible, by means of the operator CREA_CHAMP. (see paragraph [§3.4.4]).

The assignment of the loadings and of the boundary conditions on a mechanical model is carried out by means of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA, if the mechanical loadings and boundary conditions on a system are real values depending on no parameter, or else AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F, if these values are functions of the position or of the loading increment.

The User documentation of AFFE_CHAR_MECA and AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F is [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01).

#### 3.4.5 Shell–pipe, 3D–pipe and pipe–beam connections: keyword LIAISON_ELEM

The aim is to establish the connection between an end node of a pipe element and a group of edge mesh cells of shell elements or of 3D elements. This makes it possible to mesh part of the piping (for example an elbow) with shells or 3D elements and the rest with straight pipes. The formulation of the shell–pipe connection and of the 3D–pipe connection is presented in the reference document [R3.08.06](doc:r3.08.06). This connection makes it possible to transmit the mean warping and ovalisation of the shell or 3D mesh to the corresponding degrees of freedom of the pipe.

The connection:

- **Shell–pipe**: it makes it possible to connect edge elements (SEG2, SEG3) of the shell part to the node of the pipe to be connected. **Note**: *Pipe–beam connections.*

The aim is to establish a connection between an *end node of a pipe element and an end node of a beam element. The pipe formulation includes a beam-type kinematics identical to the kinematics of the beam elements. There is therefore no discontinuity between the beam-type displacements (3 displacements and 3 rotations). The mean fibre of the beam and of the pipe are the same. On the other hand, the kinematics of the beam elements does not include a shell kinematics (the section is non-deformable) as in the case of the pipe elements; there is therefore a discontinuity at the level of the strain of the cross-section.*

*There is no specific pipe–beam connection in Code_Aster; the connection between these two elements is ensured automatically, without any action by the user, by the node common to the pipe element and to the beam element. Nevertheless, certain precautions must be taken: the transition between the pipe and beam elements must be sufficiently far from any "pipe" zones where the strain of the cross-section is significant, that is to say one must connect only when the ovalisation has died out.*

To model T-junctions, preference will be given to modellings with shell or 3D elements and, consequently, to the use of connections of type COQ_TUYAU or 3D_TUYAU respectively. Regarding the shell/pipe connection, preference will be given to applying it in the straight sections rather than in the curved sections (it is recommended to move the connections out into regular, hence straight, zones).

## 4 Solution

### 4.1 Linear analyses: MECA_STATIQUE and the other linear operators

The linear analyses are carried out in small strains. Several linear solution operators are available:

- MECA_STATIQUE: solution of a linear static mechanics problem [U4.51.01](doc:u4.51.01),
- MACRO_ELAS_MULT: computes the linear static responses for different load cases or Fourier modes [U4.51.02](doc:u4.51.02),
- CALC_MODES: computation of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors by subspace methods or inverse iterations [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02),
- MODE_ITER_CYCL: computation of the eigenmodes of a structure with cyclic symmetry [U4.52.05](doc:u4.52.05),
- DYNA_VIBRA: computation of the transient dynamic response to an arbitrary time excitation [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03).

By default, the only fields computed are the displacement fields DEPL and the stress fields SIEF_ELGA. Other fields are available through the operand OPTION (see the options available in paragraph [§5.2] dealing with the use of CALC_CHAMP).

### 4.2 Non-linear analyses: STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE

#### 4.2.1 Available constitutive behaviours and strain assumptions

The following information is taken from the User documentation of the operator STAT_NON_LINE: [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03).

| STAT_NON_LINE DYNA_NON_LINE |  |  | TUYAU_3M TUYAU_6M |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| COMPORTEMENT | RELATION |  |  | all the constitutive behaviours available in C_PLAN |
|  | DEFORMATION | PETIT |  |  |

The incremental constitutive relations (factor keyword COMPORTEMENT) under the assumption of small displacements and small strains (keyword DEFORMATION= ‘PETIT’) are the only non-linear mechanical constitutive relations available for the TUYAU modelling. These constitutive relations relate the strain rates to the stress rates. The non-linear constitutive behaviours supported are those already existing in plane stress defined in the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE. Furthermore, with the De Borst method [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03), all the 2D constitutive behaviours (D_PLAN, AXIS) in small strains can be used.

The RESULTAT concept of STAT_NON_LINE contains stress fields, a displacement field and internal-variable fields at the integration points, always computed at the Gauss points:

- SIEF_ELGA: Stress tensor per element at the integration points in the local frame of the element,
- VARI_ELGA: Internal-variable field per element at the integration points in the local frame of the element,
- DEPL: displacement fields.

Furthermore, a call to the operator CALC_CHAMP makes it possible to access other fields. In particular, one can transfer the stresses and internal variables from the Gauss points to the nodes in order to form the fields SIEF_ELNO and VARI_ELNO (see paragraph [§5.2]).

A VARI_… field can have several types of component. For example, the components of the field VARI_ELNO are, for the TUYAU elements:

$K$ times: $(\mathrm{V1},\mathrm{V2},\dots \mathrm{..}\mathrm{Vn})$

| Where: | K is the total number of integration points $K=(2\times \mathrm{NCOU}+1)\times (2\times \mathrm{NSEC}+1)$ N is the number of internal variables and depends on the constitutive behaviour. |
| --- | --- |

#### 4.2.2 Details on the integration points

For the linear and non-linear analyses, the numerical integration is carried out with a method of:

- Gauss along the mean fibre.

The stiffness matrices are under-integrated, bringing the number of integration points to 2 for the elements on SEG3 mesh cells, and to 3 for the elements on SEG4 mesh cells. Furthermore, the mass matrices of the elements supported by SEG4 are also under-integrated (3 integration points). For a mesh cell whose vertices are 1 and 2 and which is numbered from 1 to 2, the 3 Gauss points are such that the first is close to 1, the second is equidistant from 1 and 2 and the third is closer to 2. One must therefore pay attention to the orientation of the mesh cells when looking at the results at Gauss points 1 and 3. Indeed, if one changes the orientation of the mesh cell and numbers it from 2 to 1, the first Gauss point is closer to 2.

- Simpson through the thickness and over the circumference:
- The integration through the thickness is a Simpson integration with 3 points per layer. The number of integration points per layer is fixed at 3, at the middle of the layer, on the upper skin and on the lower skin of the layer, the two end points being common with the neighbouring layers.
- The integration along the circumference is a Simpson integration by sector, each sector having an angle $2\pi /\mathrm{NSEC}$ . $\Phi$ is the angle between the generatrix and the centre of the sector. The number of integration points per sector is fixed at 3, at the middle of the sector, on the upper part ($\Phi +2\pi /\mathrm{NSEC}$ ) and the lower part ($\Phi -2\pi /\mathit{NSEC}$ ) of the sector, the two end points being common with the neighbouring sectors.

The number of layers and the number of sectors must be defined by the user from the keywords: TUYAU_NCOU, TUYAU_NSEC of the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM.

For example, with 3 layers and 16 sectors, the number of integration points per element is $(2\times \mathrm{NCOU}+1)\times (2\times \mathrm{NSEC}+1)\times \mathrm{NPG}$ which gives 693 integration points. For each Gauss point along the length of the element, the information on the layers is stored and, for each layer, on all the sectors. If one wants information at the Gauss point $\mathrm{NG}$ , on the layer $\mathrm{NC}$ level $\mathrm{NCN}$ ( $\mathrm{NCN}=–1$ if lower, $\mathrm{NCN}=0$ if middle, $\mathrm{NCN}=+1$ if upper), on the sector $\mathrm{NS}$ , level $\mathrm{NSN}$ ( $\mathrm{NSN}=–1$ if lower, $\mathrm{NSN}=0$ if middle, $\mathrm{NSN}=+1$ if upper), then one looks at the required values at the integration point:

$\begin{array}{cc}\mathrm{NP}=& (\mathrm{NG}-1)\times (2\mathrm{NCOU}+1)\times (2\mathrm{NSEC}+1)\\ & +(2\times \mathrm{NC}+\mathrm{NCN}-1)\times (2\mathrm{NSEC}+1)+(2\times \mathrm{NS}+\mathrm{NSN})\end{array}$ .
