# r7.01.01 Constitutive relations BETON_GRANGER and BETON_GRANGER_V for the basic creep of concrete

**Summary:**

This document presents the “Granger” basic-creep model, which is a way of modelling the basic creep of concrete. The writing and the numerical treatment of the model are also detailed there.

In Code_Aster, the model is declined into two constitutive laws: the complete version BETON_GRANGER_V and a simplified version, named BETON_GRANGER, without the effects of hygrometry and of ageing.

## 1 Introduction

Within the framework of studies of the long-term behaviour of concrete structures, a predominant part of the strains measured on the structure concerns the delayed strains that appear in the concrete during its life. They comprise the shrinkages at early age, the desiccation shrinkage, the basic creep and the drying creep.

The model presented here is dedicated to the modelling of the delayed strain associated with basic creep. Basic creep is, in addition to drying creep, the part of the creep of concrete that would be observed during a test without exchange of water with the exterior. Experimentally, concrete in basic creep presents an ageing viscous behaviour. The creep strain observed is proportional to the loading stress, and depends on the temperature and on the hygrometry. The longitudinal strain is accompanied, as in elasticity, by a transverse strain of opposite sign.

The model chosen is inspired by the one proposed by L. Granger [bib1]. It is a model of viscoelastic type that takes into account the effect of ageing and of hygrometry. The effect of temperature is not modelled.

A brief reminder of linear viscoelastic models is first given, and the model proper is then presented, as well as its numerical integration in *code_aster*.

In *code_aster*, two versions are available: BETON_GRANGER_V, the complete model, and BETON_GRANGER, which does not take ageing into account.

## 2 Reminder of the creep behaviour of a viscoelastic material

The experimental creep curve represents the evolution, as a function of time, of the strain of a material subjected to a constant unidimensional stress $\sigma$ . La déformation de fluage :math:`{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}$ is, as opposed to the instantaneous strain, the part of the strain that evolves with time.

If a material has a linear viscoelastic behaviour, the creep strain of a specimen subjected to a constant load $\sigma$ constante est, par définition, proportionnelle à $\sigma$ . The creep strain (1D) from the loading time ${t}_{c}$ may be written:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\sigma J(t,{t}_{c})
$$

The function $J(t,{t}_{c})$ is an increasing function of $\left(t-{t}_{c}\right)$ and zero for $\left(t-{t}_{c}\right)$ negative.

If the material is non-ageing, the creep function at a given time instant depends only on the time $\left(t-{t}_{c}\right)$ elapsed since the loading time instant [bib3]:

$$
J(t,{t}_{c})=f(t-{t}_{c})
$$

The creep strain is written:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\sigma f(t-{t}_{c})
$$

For an ageing viscoelastic material, the experimental creep curve varies for two different loading times. From the modelling point of view, the creep function $J(t,{t}_{c})$ no longer varies only as a function of the time elapsed from the loading time instant; it depends independently on the time $t$ and on the loading time instant ${t}_{c}$ .

### 2.1 Boltzmann superposition principle

Relation () is valid only for a constant loading. For a non-constant loading history, the Boltzmann superposition principle may be applied, by virtue of the linear dependence on the stress. If the loading history $\sigma (t)$ is decomposed into $n$ load increments, one has:

$$
\sigma (t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}H(t-{t}_{\mathit{ci}})\Delta {\sigma}_{i}
$$

Where $H$ is the Heaviside function. The expression of the creep strain may then be written:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}J(t,{t}_{\mathit{ci}})\Delta {\sigma}_{i}
$$

Passing to the limit, for an infinitely small loading increment:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\underset{{t}_{c}=0}{\overset{t}{\int}}J(t,{t}_{c})\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial {t}_{c}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}d{t}_{c}
$$

For a non-ageing linear viscoelastic material one has:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\underset{{t}_{c}=0}{\overset{t}{\int}}f(t-{t}_{c})\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial {t}_{c}}d{t}_{c}=f\ast \frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial t}
$$

where $\text{*}$ represents the convolution product.

## 3 Presentation of the Granger basic-creep model

### 3.1 Experimental properties of the basic creep of concrete under uniaxial loading

Basic-creep tests on specimens bring out the following properties:

- in a stress range lower than about 50% of the failure strength, the basic creep is proportional to the stress;
- the basic creep of a specimen at hygrometry ${h}_{\mathit{ext}}$ is almost proportional to ${h}_{\mathit{ext}}$ . The basic creep of a dry concrete is almost zero and it is maximal for a concrete saturated with water;
- when the temperature $T$ increases, there is an acceleration of the creep;
- basic creep is a strongly ageing phenomenon: the strain depends not only on the time elapsed since the application of the load, but also on the loading time instant; in other words, the rheological behaviour of the material at the loading time instant depends on its “age”. In the case of concrete, the age is the time elapsed since casting.

It is chosen to model the basic creep of concrete with a linear viscoelastic model, to which the dependence of the creep on hygrometry will be added, as well as ageing [bib1].

The effect of temperature, modelled in [bib1], will not be taken into account here.

### 3.2 Modelling of non-ageing creep by a generalised Kelvin model

It can be shown that any non-ageing linear viscoelastic body can be modelled by a series arrangement of Kelvin chains, called a generalised Kelvin model. The creep function can then be put in the form:

$$
J(t,{t}_{c})=f(t-{t}_{c})=\sum_{s=1}^{r}{J}_{s}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)
$$

Where ${\tau}_{s}$ and ${J}_{s}$ are, respectively, the retardation time and the compliance of each Kelvin chain $s$ . These are positive coefficients identified on the experimental creep curves. The choice has been made in Code_Aster to limit oneself to eight chains ($r=8$ ), which in practice is sufficient to reproduce satisfactorily the experimental creep curves of concrete.

It is very difficult to determine both the ${J}_{s}$ and the ${\tau}_{s}$ as soon as the number of Kelvin series exceeds two, because there are too many possible solutions. A choice is therefore generally made a priori on the ${\tau}_{s}$ , and the ${J}_{s}$ are then determined by linear regression. The retardation times are chosen so as to have ${\tau}_{s}={\tau}_{1}\cdot {10}^{s-1}$ .

The generalised Kelvin viscoelastic model is enriched in order to take into account the effect of hygrometry. To this end, an equivalent stress is defined.

Then, the creep function () of the generalised Kelvin model is modified in order to take into account the effect of ageing.

### 3.3 Effect of ageing

Physically, ageing in concrete is associated with hydration at early age and with other phenomena such as polymerisation for older concrete.

The effect of ageing is modelled by multiplying the coefficients ${J}_{s}$ by an ageing function $k\left(a\left({t}_{c}\right)\right)=k\left({t}_{c}\right)$ depending on the age $a$ of the material at the loading time ${t}_{c}$ . The creep function becomes:

$$
J(t,{t}_{c})=k({t}_{c})\sum_{s=1}^{8}{J}_{s}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)
$$

The choice is made to use the same ageing function for all the Kelvin chains. In this way, the contribution of ageing (which depends on ${t}_{c}$ ) has been decoupled, in the creep function (), from the non-ageing contribution (in $t-{t}_{c}$ ).

In code_aster, the ageing function is a user datum. A possible modelling for taking into account the ageing associated with hydration is that of the CEB [bib2]:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}k(a)=\frac{{28}^{0,2}+0,1}{{a}^{0,2}+0,1}& \text{si}a⩽28\text{jours}\\ k(a)=1& \text{si}a>28\text{jours}\end{array}
$$

where $a$ is expressed in days. The non-ageing law is recovered for $k(a)=\mathit{constante}=1$ .

### 3.4 Effect of hygrometry

The equivalent stress $S$ is defined:

$$
S=h\sigma
$$

For a test at constant stress one will have:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=SJ(t,{t}_{c})=(h\sigma )k({t}_{c})\sum_{s=1}^{8}{J}_{s}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)
$$

For a test characterised by a variable stress history, one will have:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\underset{{t}_{0}}{\overset{t}{\int}}J(t,{t}_{c})\frac{\partial S}{\partial {t}_{c}}{\mathit{dt}}_{c}=\sum_{s=1}^{8}\left({J}_{s}\underset{{t}_{0}}{\overset{t}{\int}}k({t}_{c})\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\frac{\partial S}{\partial {t}_{c}}{\mathit{dt}}_{c}\right)
$$

**Note**

*It is the water content* $C$ *(which corresponds to the command variable* *SECH), and not the relative humidity* $h$ *, that is* *obtained from the* code_aster drying analysis [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12). It is the sorption–desorption isotherm that makes it possible to pass from the variable $C$ *to* $h$ *. Let* $C$ *be the desorption isotherm:* $C=C(h)$ *and* $h={C}^{-1}(C)$ *. The curve* $h={C}^{-1}(C)$ *, of an empirical nature, must be supplied by the user.*

### 3.5 3D modelling

The classical assumption consists in supposing the existence of a creep Poisson’s ratio that is constant and equal to the elastic Poisson’s ratio, that is ${\nu}_{f}=0,2$ . Whence, for $S=h\cdot \sigma$ constant:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=J(t,{t}_{c})\cdot [(1+{\nu}_{f})S-{\nu}_{f}\text{tr}(S)I]
$$

and therefore:

$$
{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{fl}}\left(t\right)=J\left(t,{t}_{c}\right)\cdot \left(1+{\nu}_{f}\right)\tilde{S}
$$

où

$\tilde{\epsilon}$ et $\tilde{S}$ dénotent les parties déviatoriques des tenseurs de déformation et de contrainte.


## 4 Code_Aster constitutive relations

Two constitutive relations associated with basic creep are introduced into *Code_Aster*:

- BETON_GRANGER_V
- BETON_GRANGER

The first takes into account all of the effects (hygrometry and ageing); the second does not take into account the phenomenon of ageing. They are available in 2D and 3D modelling and in plane stress.

The various parameters of the model are supplied in DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keywords BETON_GRANGER, V_BETON_GRANGER and ELAS_FO.

- The $2\times 8$ characteristic constants of the creep function of the generalised Kelvin model, ${J}_{s}$ , ${\tau}_{s}$ , are supplied under the keyword BETON_GRANGER, whose use is common to the two constitutive relations BETON_GRANGER and BETON_GRANGER_V.
- If the ageing constitutive relation BETON_GRANGER_V is used, the keyword V_BETON_GRANGER is supplied in addition, the ageing function $k({t}_{c})$ .
- The desorption curve, necessary for the two constitutive relations BETON_GRANGER and BETON_GRANGER_V, is supplied under the keyword ELAS_FO.

## 5 Numerical integration of the model

### 5.1 Discretisation (1D)

Consider first the non-ageing case. The creep strain for the single Kelvin chain $s$ is written as follows (see ):

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}(t)=\underset{{t}_{0}}{\overset{t}{\int}}{J}_{s}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\frac{\partial S}{\partial {t}_{c}}{\mathit{dt}}_{c}
$$

The numerical integration of the model is carried out by approximating $S\left(t\right)$ as a piecewise linear function over the succession of $n$ time increments ${\Delta t}_{i}={t}_{i}-{t}_{i-1}$ with $i=1,\cdots ,n$ . Consequently, $\dot{S}$ is constant on $t\in [:ref:`{t}_{i-1},{t}_{i} <{t}_{i-1},{t}_{i}>$]` and equal to $\frac{{\Delta S}_{i}}{{\Delta t}_{i}}$ . The strain of chain $s$ at time ${t}_{n}$ is then given by the following expression:

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}({t}_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\Delta {S}_{i}}{\Delta {t}_{i}}\underset{{t}_{i-1}}{\overset{{t}_{i}}{\int}}{J}_{s}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{t-{t}_{c}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right){\mathit{dt}}_{c}
$$

One obtains:

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}({t}_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\Delta {S}_{i}}{\Delta {t}_{i}}\right){J}_{s}\Delta {t}_{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\Delta {S}_{i}}{\Delta {t}_{i}}\right){J}_{s}{\tau}_{s}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{{t}_{n}-{t}_{i}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)-\exp\left(-\frac{{t}_{n}-{t}_{i-1}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)
$$

And therefore:

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}({t}_{n})={J}_{s}\underset{{A}_{n}^{0}}{\underset{⏟}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta {S}_{i}}}-\underset{{A}_{n}^{s}}{\underset{⏟}{{J}_{s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta {S}_{i}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta {t}_{i}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{{t}_{n}-{t}_{i}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta {t}_{i}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)}}={J}_{s}{A}_{n}^{0}-{A}_{n}^{s}
$$

At ${t}_{n+1}$ one may likewise write:

$$

$$

That is, with ${\Delta}_{n+1}={t}_{n+1}-{t}_{n}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}({t}_{n+1})={J}_{s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta {S}_{i}-{J}_{s}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta {S}_{i}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta {t}_{i}}\left(\exp\left(-\frac{{t}_{n}-{t}_{i}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\left(\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta {t}_{i}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\\ +{J}_{s}\Delta {S}_{n+1}-{J}_{s}\Delta {S}_{n+1}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta {t}_{n+1}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right)\right)\end{array}

### 5.2 Integration of the constitutive relation

$\Delta \epsilon$ denotes the infinitesimal strain increment such that:

$$
\Delta \epsilon =\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla (\Delta u)+{\nabla}^{T}(\Delta u)\right)
$$

If, in the partition of the strain, the thermal strain and the strains associated with endogenous shrinkage and with desiccation shrinkage are taken into account, then one has:

$$
\Delta \epsilon =\Delta {\epsilon}^{e}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{fl}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{ret}-\mathit{end}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{ret}-\mathit{des}}
$$

With the elastic strain such that:

$$
{\epsilon}^{e}=H\sigma
$$

$H$ is the Hooke elasticity tensor. The thermal strain is written:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}}){I}_{d}
$$

For a given temperature $T$ , with $\alpha$ le coefficient de dilatation thermique et :math:`{T}_{\mathit{ref}}$ the reference temperature. The endogenous and desiccation shrinkage strains:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\mathit{ret}-\mathit{end}}=-\beta \xi {I}_{d}
$$

With $\xi$ l'hydratation, :math:`C$ the water concentration, ${C}_{\mathit{ref}}$ the reference drying and $(\beta ,\kappa )$ material characteristics.

*In the remainder of the document, one will denote* $\Delta {\epsilon}^{A}=\Delta {\epsilon}^{\text{th}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\text{ret}\text{}\text{end}}+\Delta {\epsilon}^{\text{ret}\text{}\text{des}}$ *.*

### 5.3 State variables

The state variables of the two constitutive relations are therefore:

- $\sigma$ : stress tensor,
- $\epsilon$ : strain tensor,
- $T$ : temperature,
- $C$ : water concentration,
- $\xi$ : degree of hydration,
- ${A}_{s}$ : tensors characteristic of the progress of the creep, that is $6\times 9$ variables,
- $a$ : the age of the concrete.

The components of the eight tensors ${A}_{s}$ and $a$ are the internal variables of the constitutive law (BETON_GRANGER as well as BETON_GRANGER_V). The dimension of each tensor ${A}_{s}$ depends on the modelling (four components in 2D and six in 3D). One therefore has:

- in 3D, 55 internal variables:

  - VI7...VI12: components of the tensor ${A}_{2}$
  - …
  - VI43...VI48: components of the tensor ${A}_{8}$

### 5.4 Tangent matrix

> The tangent matrix is expressed by the following formula:

$$
\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}}{\partial \epsilon }+\frac{1}{3}\frac{\partial (\mathit{tr}\sigma )}{\partial \epsilon }{I}_{d}
$$

With:

$$
\frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}}{\partial \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}}{\partial \tilde{\epsilon}}\frac{\partial \tilde{\epsilon}}{\partial \epsilon }
$$

Which requires the evaluation of the following expressions:

$$
\frac{\partial {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}}{\partial {\epsilon}_{kl}}={\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}{\delta}_{kl}
$$

For a Newton iteration, expression () is differentiated:

$$
\frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}}{\partial \epsilon }\left[1+2\mu (1+{\nu}_{f})(hk({t}_{n+1/2}))\sum_{s}\left({J}_{s}1-\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta t}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{{\tau}_{S}}\right)\right)\right)\right]=2\mu {I}_{d}
$$

And expression ():

$$
\frac{\partial (\mathit{tr}\sigma )}{\partial (\mathit{tr}\epsilon )}\left[1+3K(1-2{\nu}_{f})(hk({t}_{n+1/2}))\sum_{s}\left\lbrace {J}_{s}\left(1-\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{\Delta t}\left(1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{{\tau}_{S}}\right)\right)\right)\right\rbrace \right]=3K{I}_{d}
$$

The prediction phase at the time step $[{t}_{n},{t}_{n+1}]$ is now considered. It is noted beforehand that, in 1D, the time derivative of the creep strain is expressed very simply:

$$
{\left(\frac{\partial {\epsilon}_{s}^{\mathit{fl}}}{\partial t}\right)}_{{t}_{n}}=\frac{{A}_{s}^{-}}{{\tau}_{s}}-{J}_{s}k({t}_{c})\frac{\partial S}{\partial t}
$$

The problem is written in rate form at the time instant ${t}_{n}$ :

$$
\frac{\partial \tilde{\sigma}}{\partial t}\left[1+2\mu \sum_{s}{J}_{s}k({t}_{n}){h}^{-}\right]=2\mu \frac{\partial \tilde{\epsilon}}{\partial t}-2\mu (1+{\nu}_{f})\left[\sum_{s}\left(\frac{\tilde{{{A}_{s}}^{-}}}{{\tau}_{s}}-{J}_{s}k({t}_{n}){\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}\frac{\mathit{dh}}{\mathit{dt}}\right)\right]
$$

For the spherical part:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial (\mathit{tr}\sigma )}{\partial t}\left[1+\mathrm{3K}(1-2{\nu}_{f})(\sum_{s}{J}_{s}\cdot k({t}_{\text{c}})\cdot {h}^{-})\right]=\\ =\mathrm{3K}\frac{\partial (\mathit{tr}\epsilon )}{\partial t}-\mathrm{3K}(1-2{\nu}_{f})\left[\sum_{s}\frac{\mathit{tr}{A}_{s}^{-}}{{\tau}_{s}}-{J}_{s}\cdot k({t}_{n})\cdot (\mathit{tr}{\sigma}^{-})\frac{\mathit{dh}}{\mathit{dt}}\right]+\mathrm{3K}(3\beta \frac{d\xi }{\mathit{dt}})+\mathrm{3K}(3\kappa \frac{\mathit{dC}}{\mathit{dt}})\end{array}
$$

Finally:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\Delta (\text{tr}\sigma )\left[1+\mathrm{3K}(1-2{\nu}_{f})(\sum_{s}{J}_{s}\cdot k({t}_{n})\cdot {h}^{-})\right]=\\ =\mathrm{3K}\Delta (\text{tr}\epsilon )-\mathrm{3K}(1-2{\nu}_{f})\left[\sum_{s}\frac{(\mathit{tr}{A}_{s}^{-})}{{\tau}_{s}}\cdot \Delta t-{J}_{s}\cdot k({t}_{n})\cdot (\text{tr}{\sigma}^{-})\Delta h\right]\\ -\mathrm{3K}(3\alpha \Delta T)+\mathrm{3K}(3\beta \Delta \xi )+\mathrm{3K}(3\kappa \Delta C)\end{array}
$$

Creep therefore introduces a specific right-hand-side term during the prediction phase, which is in fact neglected, with no consequence on the results.
