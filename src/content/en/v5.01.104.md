# v5.01.104 SDND104 - Calculation of wear power of a rubbing mass under harmonic seismic excitation

**Summary:**

A frictional contact mass is considered with a rigid plane to which a vibrational movement of the harmonic type is imposed. The friction is modelled by the Coulomb law. The calculation of the mass response is of a transitional nonlinear type. The wear power resulting from the sliding phases between the mass and the rigid plane is calculated. The calculation of the wear power n- being developed in *Aster* only for modal calculations, the analysis is conducted on the modal (triviale) basis of the system. In order to avoid the numerical problems resulting from the nullity of the single rigid body mode of the mass, a very weak spring is introduced, linking the mass to a solid point of the rigid vibrating plane.

The reference solution is a quasi-analytical calculation of the transient response, whose numerical estimates are programmed with Maple.

The only *Aster* model chosen tests the explicit constant step integration algorithms d-Euler (order1), Devogeleare (order 4) and the variable step algorithms ADAPT_ORDRE2 (order 2) and RUNGE_KUTTA (orders 54 and 32) developed in the operator dedicated to vibratory dynamics, for different amplitudes of the harmonic acceleration of seismic excitation of the rigid support plane. According to this amplitude, the speed of the mass response is of the adherent type for all times (stick), successively adherent and slippery (stick-slip), or always slippery with inversion of the slip direction (slip-slip).

It is noted that in the case of sufficiently low excitation amplitude (first speed, permanent adhesion), the wear power is strictly zero.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The system is composed of a simple heavy mass placed on a rigid support subjected to a imposed vibration of seismic type, sinusoidal. The contact, as well as the dry friction are modelled by penalisation. The system therefore has two degrees of freedom translation (horizontal and vertical).

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.104/Object_1.svg)

A very low stiffness spring connects the mass to the support in the three directions. This spring is a calculational artifice, designed to avoid the nullity of the frequency associated with the rigid mode of horizontal translation of the mass. *Aster* results taking into account the presence of this spring are little different from the results that would be obtained without spring.

The *Aster* solution considered is the calculation of the average wear power during a transient phase from $4$ to $11,99\mathit{secondes}$ (from $8\pi /\omega$ to $24\pi /\omega$). The wear energy during this transient period differs somewhat from the average wear energy (asymptotic) over this period (both in the case of stick-slip and brief-slip). Therefore, to compare it precisely with the *Aster* results, an exact calculation of this energy in the $[\mathrm{4s},11,99s]$ time interval is required.

For ${a}_{0}=\mathrm{15m}/{s}^{2}$, the average asymptotic wear power is $15,1146144886\mathit{Watt}$, while the average wear power on the $[\mathrm{4s},11,99s]$ time interval is $15,257521794\mathit{Watt}$. This last value is the reference result.

**Note:**

As an average power calculation, the wear power calculated over an interval is not necessarily increasing with the interval duration. If a time interval is added to which there is adhesion, the average wear power will be lower.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

A element of type DIS_T on an mesh cell POI1 is used to model the system.

The calculation is done on a modal basis. The movements in $Y$ and $Z$ are blocked, so the modal base contains only one mode.

The dynamic calculation functionality on the modal basis of the DYNA_VIBRA operator is used, with the keyword CHOC to model the local nonlinearity.

An obstacle of type PLAN_Z (two parallel planes separated by a game) is used to simulate the slip plane. We choose to take as generator of this plane $\mathit{Oy}$ that is NORM_OBST: $(0.,1.,0.)$. The origin of the obstacle is ORIG_OBST: $(0.,0.,1.)$, its game that gives the half-rangement between the planes is $0.5$.

The relative marker (single-screw loading) is placed and an acceleration load is applied with CALC_CHAR_SEISME.

${3.10}^{-5}s$ time step is used for time integration to limit the calculation time. This time step is much less than

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.104/Object_137.svg)

.

The stiffness tangential friction is taken as large as possible to ensure the stability of the diagram, i.e. ${K}_{T}=900000N/m$. The ${K}_{T}=1000000N/m$ value leads to numerical instability.

The stiffness normal ${K}_{N}$ must be taken equal to $20N/m$ to compensate exactly for the weight of the mass. (the value of the game is $0,50m$). Any other value leads to outliers.

## 5 Summary of the results

The test case validates the calculation of wear power with POST_DYNA_MODA_T after a transient calculation on a modal basis, both on a variable step diagram (ADAPT_ORDRE2, RUNGE_KUTTA54 and RUNGE_KUTTA32) and on constant step diagrams (Euler and Devogeleare). In particular, the tangential microspeeds induced by the contact model by penalization, during adhesion phases, are correctly cancelled.

The influence of the added spring remains below the precision obtained.

The contact tangential stiffness is the limiting element for greater accuracy. The convergence of the results to the reference solution has been verified. The tangential stiffness has been taken as large as possible to ensure stability of the diagram with $\mathit{dt}={10}^{-4}s$.

Tolerances in the test-resu are taken just above the differences found.
