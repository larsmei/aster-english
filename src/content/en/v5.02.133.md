# v5.02.133 SDNL133 – Rotor rotating cracked, subjected to bending effort

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the Modelling of a 1D crack in a rotor. This feature is available via ROTOR_FISS option of the DYNA_VIBRA operator.

The test case involves a slow rotating beam subjected to a constant bending moment.

The solution is compared with a 3D calculation made with STAT_NON_LINE for the extreme closed crack and open crack positions.

## 1 Reference problem

The objective of this test case is to validate the 1D Modelling of a cracked rotor (ROTOR_FISS option of DYNA_VIBRA) which simulates the behaviour of a crack in a shaft line by an equivalent law.

The law is established by 3D calculations made in quasi-static (see model D).

The results obtained by the beam model and the crack element are compared with the 3D calculation for the extreme positions: closed crack and fully cracked at the maximum.

### 1.1 Geometry

For the cracked rotor, a simple cylindrical straight beam of $\mathrm{2L}=4m$ length and $D=0,8m$ diameter is considered. The crack is in the middle of the beam and has a straight bottom. The crack depth is 65%.

![Figure](/figures/v5.02.133/Cadre1.gif)

Figure 1: Géométrie du rotor fissuré

### 1.2 Material properties

The rotor has a density of $\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$.

The Young's modulus is $E=210{10}^{9}N{m}^{-2}$ and the Poisson's ratio is $\nu =0,3$.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

For modellings A, B and C, the beam is clamped on the left and is subjected to bending moment of unit amplitude on $Y$ on its right end. The crack is considered to be running relatively slowly at $5$ revolutions per second ($300\text{rpm}$).

For Modelling D, the boundary conditions imposed are, on the one hand, an clamped support in the sense of the beam theory of one of the ends of the cylinder by means of a 3D-POU connection, and on the other hand, a unilateral contact without friction between the lips of the cracks. The imposed load is a bending moment unit of $({M}_{x},{M}_{y})$ components applied at the free end. The orientation of this moment evolves according to the moment of calculation.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

In the initial state, $t=0$, the crack is closed. It is gradually opened by a linear ramp spread over $0.2s$ leading to the moment on $Y$ from $0$ to $1\mathrm{Nm}$.

## 2 reference solution

The reference solution is a 3D calculation performed with STA_NON_LINE. The post-processing deformed neutral fiber is recovered.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The rotor is modeled by elements Euler beam (POU_D_E).

The crack is modelled by ROTOR_FISS functionality of DYNA_VIBRA. The constitutive law crack is input by a function, itself determined on a bar of unit diameter, independent of rotor geometry (see Modelling D).

DYNA_VIBRA calculates the transient on a modal basis. The latter is not orthogonal but consists of the closed-crack rotor beam modes (up to $250\text{Hz}$) and the first 2 open-crack beam modes.

## 6 Modelling D

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics

As mentioned above, the characterization of the cracked rotor behaviour requires the creation of a three-dimensional model of a two-axis bending cracked beam.

This D Modelling allows capitalizing the mesh procedure of a standard cracked cylinder as well as the python script to identify the laws characterizing the cracked rotor behavior studied in the modellings A, B and C.

Element rotor Young's modulus $E$ , quadratic inertia $I$ (identical in all directions of the section plane) and $2L=4m$ length and diameter $D=1m$ containing in its median section a transverse crack at right depth 65%.

![Figure](/figures/v5.02.133/100002000000011700000076B97873CBC55FF267.png)

Figure 6: éprouvette fissurée unitaire

A non-linear static calculation is performed with *Code_Aster* for 36 load steps by changing the ${\Phi}_{i}$ orientation at the moment with a $10°$ pitch to cover the complete $[0°-360°]$ interval. The post-processing is made by a Python script that operates the result file from *Code_Aster*. From the $X$ and $Y$ rotations of the end of the rotating cylinder imposed for each calculation moment, the Python script allows to calculate the strain energy, the flexibility associated with the cracked cylinder deformations, the local rigidity and its derivative.

The adimensional and derived curves of stiffness depend on the $\Phi$ orientation of the applied forces relative to the angular position of the crack.

## 7 Summary of the results

The test case implements the slow rotation of a cracked beam, clamped at one end and subjected to one bending moment at the other. The 1D Modelling of the cracked rotor programmed in DYNA_VIBRA is thus validated in relation to the static results obtained with the equivalent 3D model.
