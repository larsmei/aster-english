# u2.08.01 Mesh adaptation strategies with HOMARD

**Summary:**

This document aims to describe all the tools of *Code_Aster* related to mesh adaptation. It constitutes a methodological guide to the good practices to be put in place in order to conduct a study of quality as regards the mesh.

Examples of use illustrate the possibilities and the implementation of remeshing strategies.

## 1 Introduction

Mesh adaptation provides the user with assistance in supplying analyses that are as reliable as possible with respect to the discretisation errors due to the finite-element method employed. Several motivations appear for adapting a mesh:

- the mesh is very complicated to produce: one starts from a simple version and one entrusts an automatic process with the task of refining it.
- one wants to make sure of the convergence of the numerical solution: rather than producing by hand meshes that are finer and finer, one lets the software itself look for the places where the mesh ought to be refined in order to increase the accuracy of the result.
- the conditions of the analysis change in the course of its progress: the zones which must be finely meshed move. If one meshes finely everywhere from the outset, the mesh is too large. By adapting progressively, the mesh will be fine only at the necessary places: its size will be reduced and the quality of the solution will be good.

Whatever software is used, the principle of mesh adaptation remains the same. On the starting mesh, one carries out the standard analysis. From an analysis of the numerical solution obtained, one estimates the error which has been committed with respect to the real solution. This estimate is represented by a value of the error indicator in each element of the analysis. From there, the reasoning is simple: the mesh cells where the error indicator is high ought to be smaller; conversely, the mesh cells where the error indicator is low could be larger. With this information, one feeds the adaptation software, which will modify the mesh accordingly. On the new mesh, one will then start the analysis again.

Schematically, an iteration of mesh adaptation appears as in the figure below. The software computes the numerical solution on mesh no. k, then deduces from it the values of the error indicator over the whole mesh. From the knowledge of mesh no. k and of indicator no. k, the mesh-adaptation software, HOMARD for *Code_Aster*, creates the new mesh no. (k+1).

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.01/1000000000000209000000890408F75A65872704.png)

**Figure** 1-a **: Iteration of mesh adaptation**

For a static analysis, this amounts to seeking to improve the solution by a succession of analyses on different meshes. For a transient analysis in time, the analysis is suspended at a given time instant; the mesh is adapted; the analysis resumes on the new mesh.

With *Code_Aster*, this adaptation is carried out by a call to an external program, specialised in this task, HOMARD. The aim of this document is to provide as complete an entry point as possible for the user wishing to implement this kind of technique in his analyses.

The plan of the document is then as follows:

- How HOMARD works;
- A focus on the error indicators;
- A set of pieces of advice on the "good practices" to be implemented (which points deserve attention during use?);
- A few examples illustrate the use of these techniques and the advice given previously (how does one do it in practice?).

## 2 How does HOMARD work?

HOMARD is a software package developed by EDF R&D. A presentation of it can be found on its Web site [bib1] and in its own documentation [bib2].

### 2.1 The scope of use of HOMARD

The command of *Code_Aster* which makes it possible to access these functions is MACR_ADAP_MAIL. It is documented in [bib3].

HOMARD can handle meshes in 2 or 3 dimensions, of degree 1 or degree 2, and comprising the following elements:

- point mesh cells
- segments
- triangles
- quadrangles
- tetrahedra
- hexahedra

These elements may be present simultaneously. For example, HOMARD will know how to adapt a mesh comprising triangles and quadrangles.

The nodes accepted are obviously the nodes which are the vertices of the mesh cells, which corresponds to the classical description "in degree 1". If the mesh cells are described "in degree 2", that is to say with the presence of nodes in the middle of the edges, these complementary nodes are managed. On the other hand, there cannot be coexistence of mesh cells described in degree 1 and of mesh cells described in degree 2. Finally, HOMARD can take into account isolated nodes, which would not belong to any mesh-cell definition: they will come out of the adaptation process unchanged.

A particular case: the pentahedra. At the date of writing of this document, HOMARD can handle meshes comprising pentahedra but only in order to carry out uniform refinement. Free refinement will be available at the end of 2011.

### 2.2 A word on the modes of cutting of the elements

It is interesting here to know the mode of cutting of the mesh cells in HOMARD so as not to be possibly surprised by the new mesh. We shall give only an example in 2D; for 3D, the reader will consult the HOMARD Web site [bib1].

Among the various techniques possible for adapting a mesh, HOMARD uses refinement and unrefinement by cutting of the mesh cells.

The standard cutting is that which applies to any mesh cell at the heart of a zone to be refined: a triangle is cut into four triangles (Figure 2.2-a) and a quadrangle is cut into four quadrangles (Figure 2.2-b). In 3 dimensions, a tetrahedron is cut into eight tetrahedra, a hexahedron into eight hexahedra, a pentahedron into eight pentahedra. Each cutting rests on the mid-points of the edges bordering the mesh cell.

**How does HOMARD manage conformity?**

Let us start from a situation such as that of Figure 2.2-c, representing a two-dimensional mesh formed of quadrangles and of triangles. The error indicator will have requested a refinement on the hatched zones.

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.01/Object_2.svg)

Figure 2.2-c: Example of a refinement request

The strict application of the principle of cutting of the mesh cells designated by the error indicator produces the mesh of Figure 2.2-d:

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.01/Object_3.svg)

Figure 2.2-d: Example of a raw non-conforming refinement

In order to manage conformity, the rule is as follows:

- If an uncut mesh cell finds itself attacked on at least two sides, it is likewise cut into four;
- If a mesh cell finds itself attacked on a single side:
- if it is a triangle, it is cut into two,
- if it is a quadrangle, it is cut into three triangles

Which gives, for our example:

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.01/Object_4.svg)

Figure 2.2-e: Final enforcement of conformity

**Comment concerning the quality of the meshes resulting from the cutting:**

In the course of the standard cutting, the triangles produced are rigorously identical to the starting triangles. There is a perfect homothety and therefore identity of the mesh qualities. For the quadrangles, the situation is not so simple. If the quadrangle is a parallelogram, the quadrangles produced are likewise homothetic. This is therefore the case for a square or a rectangle. The mesh quality then remains the same. On the other hand, as soon as the quadrangle is arbitrary, the 4 children never resemble the parent: the quality changes.

For the cuttings of final enforcement of conformity, it is blatant that they modify the quality of the mesh cells. An equilateral triangle will have two flattened children; a regular tetrahedron will have slender children. Conversely a flattened triangle cut along its long side will have two fairly regular children, and so on. We therefore have favourable situations and others which are less so. If we do nothing, we shall run the risk of seeing the quality of the mesh deteriorate. Let us indeed imagine the case of a zone in which the refinement indications would lead to subdividing a conformity triangle. After a few adaptations, the quality of the mesh would be completely degraded. HOMARD manages these difficulties by a special algorithm which guarantees that the angles do not flatten out over the course of the iterations. We thus limit the modifications of the quality level of the mesh: the quality of the mesh, at any level of adaptation, remains comparable to the quality of the starting mesh.

We refer the reader to [bib2] for a fine description of this analysis of mesh quality, illustrated by typical cases.

### 2.3 The different modes of adaptation

There exist several choices for driving an adaptation through the command MACR_ADAP_MAIL documented in [bib3].

1. In the first place, one finds the modes of adaptation which are driven by a field or the derivative of this field, for example an error indicator (keywords 'RAFFINEMENT', 'DERAFFINEMENT' and 'RAFF_DERA'). This field may be expressed on the nodes or the elements. In other words, the decision to (un)refine a mesh cell is taken as a function of the value of this field computed beforehand on this mesh cell or of the jump of this field from one mesh cell to its neighbours. The choice of the refinement or unrefinement thresholds is made in three forms:

- an absolute threshold value of this field (keyword 'CRIT_RAF_ABS'): one will refine all the mesh cells where the value of the field is greater than this threshold value.
- a relative value of the field (keyword 'CRIT_RAF_REL'): one will refine the mesh cells where the value of the field is greater than x% of the maximum value.
- a fraction of elements (keyword 'CRIT_RAF_PE'): one will refine the x% of mesh cells where the value of the field is the largest.

**Note:**

*When one applies an unrefinement option, one only goes back on previous refinements. This option must be understood as un-refinement. In particular, one will never be able to obtain a mesh coarser than the initial mesh.*

*If the driving field is defined on the nodes, HOMARD will cut in two all the edges whose nodes have field values greater than the criterion requested.*

Depending on the types of problem, one or the other of the strategies is preferable; it is the know-how proper to each discipline which makes it possible to define good practices. One must nevertheless be careful and not request too much refinement when one works with conforming meshes. Indeed, the threshold will trigger the compulsory refinement of the designated mesh cells. But in order to ensure conformity, there will necessarily be additional refinements in order to connect the zones of different levels. In the end, one will always have more mesh cells cut than those which had been requested initially. Experience has shown us that it is more effective for the accuracy of the solution to chain 3 successive refinements, requesting the refinement of 1% of the mesh cells, rather than to carry out only a single refinement requesting 5%.

1. In the second place, one may decide to refine the mesh in geometrical zones defined by boxes (keyword 'RAFF_DERA_ZONE'). All the mesh cells of which one of the nodes is present in one of these boxes will be refined. This makes it possible to carry out refinements *a priori*, without having carried out an analysis. The zones are rectangles or circles in 2D, parallelepipeds or spheres in 3D. It is possible to define several boxes, overlapping or not.

1. Finally, one may activate a uniform adaptation of a mesh (keywords 'RAFFINEMENT_UNIFORME' and 'DERAFFINEMENT_UNIFORME'). In other words, all the mesh cells of the mesh are treated in the same manner, without taking into account a field serving as a criterion for (un)refining.

**Note:**

*The characteristics of the mesh cells are preserved: a mesh cell which is cut transmits to its daughters its properties of orientation or of belonging to subdomains. This is in particular true for the boundary mesh cells which serve to apply the loadings: the group which defines them is restored and it is unnecessary to reorient them*

### 2.4 Other possible options

The various options which may be activated in MACR_ADAP_MAIL:

- HOMARD updates real fields from the old mesh onto the new mesh when they are defined on the nodes (keyword MAJ_CHAM). This makes it possible, in the course of a transient analysis, to restart from the solution previously computed but expressed on the new mesh. In general, it is moreover recommended to update the fields via HOMARD rather than to project them afterwards from one mesh onto the other in the analysis software. Indeed, HOMARD stores the parentage of all the nodes and mesh cells: it will therefore carry out only the strictly necessary computations and only by analytical formulae. At present, one cannot update fields at the Gauss points (such as the internal variables, for example).
- HOMARD possesses mesh-analysis functions: connectivity (the software reports the number of disjoint blocks which form the global mesh), size (computation of the sizes of the various 1D, 2D or 3D subdomains), quality (determination of the quality of the 2D or 3D elements, extreme values and distribution), interpenetration (check of the non-overlapping of elements). These functions can be activated in the course of an adaptation. They can also be used outside any adaptation in order to check a mesh. They help the user to check that his mesh is correct and to locate errors in its production. One can find these functionalities on the quality of the mesh in the command MACR_INFO_MAIL [bib4].
- The user has the possibility of carrying out boundary tracking (keywords MAILLAGE_FRONTIERE and/or FRONTIERE_ANALYTIQUE). If one of the boundaries of the computational domain is curved and a mesh cell touching this boundary is refined, HOMARD will know how to place the new nodes on the "true" boundary. This option makes it possible to "smooth out", progressively with the cutting, the geometrical singularities (see the example of the rotor in paragraph 6).
- When one adapts a mesh of degree 2 by the command MACR_ADAP_MAIL, one produces as output the new mesh in degree 2. Optionally (keyword MAILLAGE_NP1_ANNEXE), one will also produce the same mesh in degree 1. This option is of interest during a thermomechanical analysis in which one recommends a linear mesh in thermal analysis and a quadratic one in mechanics.
- The keywords NIVE_MAX and NIVE_MIN make it possible to control the maximum level of refinement, respectively of unrefinement, of a mesh cell in the course of the adaptation process.
- The keyword DIAM_MIN makes it possible to control the minimum size of a mesh cell in the course of the adaptation process.

## 3 The different criteria of refinement/unrefinement

One saw in paragraph 2.3 that one can locate the zones to be refined:

- either with an error indicator;
- or with a field (or its derivative) which one considers as a measure of the error (temperature, stress, strain, …). This field may be expressed on the nodes or on the mesh cells (at the Gauss points, at the nodes per element or constant per element). If the criterion is expressed at the Gauss points or at the nodes per element, then HOMARD chooses the maximum of the value in the mesh cell considered;
- or by boxes.

For the error indicators, several choices are offered to the user. This is the subject of the following paragraphs.

### 3.1 The error indicators

#### 3.1.1 What is an error indicator?

It is important here to recall the definition of what is meant by error indicator (or error estimator) so that the user is well aware of what one can, or cannot, make this indicator say.

A function e is an a posteriori error indicator if it satisfies the inequality:

$\parallel u-{u}^{h}\parallel \le e(h,{u}^{h},d)$

where u is the displacement field of the exact solution, ${u}^{h}$ the displacement field of the finite-element solution, $\parallel \cdot \parallel$ a norm on the displacement fields, h the size of the elements and d data of the problem. Moreover if $e(h,d,{u}^{h})$ can be localised in the form:

$e(h,{u}^{h},d)={(\sum_{E}{e}_{E}({u}^{h},d{)}^{2})}^{\frac{1}{2}}$

then the quantities ${e}_{E}({u}^{h},d)$ are called local error indicators.

For the two types of error indicator (hence two different functions e) present in *Code_Aster*, the norm $\parallel \cdot \parallel$ is an energy norm.

#### 3.1.2 What must be retained

These error indicators lead to the estimation of a global error. The choice of a global accuracy founded on the energy norm is often delicate to interpret, because there is no direct quantitative link with an error on local mechanical quantities (stresses at a point or on a line, maximum of the stresses, etc.) and the error in displacement. In other words, X% of the error indicator in a mesh cell does not mean that there is X% of error on the stress or the strain.

Used alone, that is to say without the objective of carrying out mesh adaptation, the indicator does not make it possible to quantify the quality of the initial mesh, except possibly to locate the problem zones, such as the geometrical singularities or the strong gradients.

#### 3.1.3 Scope of use

The error indicators used in *Code_Aster* are a posteriori indicators and are therefore computed in post-processing of *Code_Aster* by the command CALC_ERREUR. They are fields constant per element (hence one value per finite element). Their domain of use is delimited by the following constraints (one will refer to the reference documents given in the paragraph for more details):

- the errors taken into account are the errors of spatial discretisation (hence the size of the elements employed); in particular, the errors of temporal discretisation (or pseudo-temporal in the case of non-linear materials) lie outside this scope;
- the physical phenomena are limited to mechanics (linear or non-linear) and to thermal analysis (likewise);
- in mechanics as in thermal analysis, the constitutive behaviour may be linear or non-linear (except for the Zhu-Zienkiewicz error estimator in mechanics, which treats only linear constitutive behaviour), it being known that the theoretical results of the error indicators are obtained in the linear domain (their use in the non-linear domain is therefore not based on theoretical results but on an empirical observation of their interest);
- the elements used may be arbitrary for the use of the error indicators (except for the Zhu-Zienkiewicz error estimator in mechanics, which treats only the 2D elements; the estimator $\mathrm{ZZ2}$ accepts only meshes composed either of triangles or of quadrangles); These elements may be linear or quadratic.

#### 3.1.4 Error estimator in mechanics

There exist two types of estimator for mechanics:

- The Zhu-Zienkiewicz error estimator [bib5], which is computed directly in the operator CALC_ERREUR with the options 'ERZ1_ELEM' for the estimator $\mathrm{ZZ1}$ and 'ERZ2_ELEM' for the estimator $\mathrm{ZZ2}$. This field comprises three components per element $E$:
- 'ERREST': the absolute total error ${e}_{E}$;
- 'NUEST': the relative total error ${e}_{{E}_{\text{REL}}}=100\times \frac{{e}_{E}}{\sqrt{{e}_{E}^{2}+{\mathrm{\parallel }{\sigma}_{h}\mathrm{\parallel }}_{E}^{2}}}$;
- 'SIGCAL': the energy norm of the computed solution ${\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{E}$.
- The residual error estimator [bib6], which is likewise computed in the operator CALC_ERREUR with the options 'ERME_ELEM' for the computation in the element and 'ERME_ELNO' for the computation at the nodes per element. This field comprises eight components per element $E$:
- 'ERREST': the absolute total error ${e}_{E}$;
- 'TERMRE': the absolute error of the residual of equilibrium;
- 'TERMSA': the absolute error of the stress jumps between two mesh cells;
- 'TERMNO': the absolute error of the jumps between the normal stress and the Neumann force;
- 'NUEST': the relative total error;
- 'TERMR2': the relative error of the residual of equilibrium;
- 'TERMS2': the relative error of the stress jumps between two mesh cells;
- 'TERMN2': the relative error of the jumps between the normal stress and the Neumann force;
- 'SIGCAL': the energy norm of the computed solution;

For these two types of error estimator, the user also has access to printouts in the result file comprising the same information at the global level.

**A few pieces of advice for a good use of the option** **ERME_ELEM:**

- In order properly to carry out the computation of the residual indicator (within the theoretical limits of the formula developed in the elliptic framework with a regular boundary …), one must compute it over the whole model (CALC_ERREUR ( TOUT = 'OUI' (default value))). It should be noted that the model is not necessarily defined over the whole geometry.
- One takes into account only loadings of the type: PESANTEUR, ROTATION, FORCE_INTERNE, PRES_REP, FORCE_FACE, FORCE_ARETE. Only the last three may be variable. For the other types of loading, execution stops in fatal error.
- It is advised to use finite elements of order 2 in the case of body forces, otherwise this term is very poorly computed since DIV(SIGMA) is almost zero!
- In order to take into account the error relative to a zero boundary condition of Neumann type, one must impose it as a function via an AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F. Via a constant, it will not be taken into account.
- In 2D, it takes into account the errors on (and between) the isoparametric elements SEG2/3, TRIA3/6/7, QUAD4/8/9. In 3D, likewise with FACE3/4/6/8/9, TETRA4/10, PENTA6/13/15 and HEXA8/20/27. Only the error on the structural elements (shell, plate, beam...) and the PYRAM are not taken into account.
- On the other hand, one must take care not to insert segments between two quadrangles or two triangles (resp. quadrangle or triangle between two hexahedra), otherwise one cannot compute the jump term relative to this neighbourhood.

#### 3.1.5 Error estimator in thermal analysis

There exists a single error indicator in thermal analysis [bib7], which is of residual type. It is computed in the operator CALC_ERREUR, by the options 'ERTH_ELEM' for the computation per element and 'ERTH_ELNO' for the computation per element at the nodes. For the option 'ERTH_ELEM', one must obligatorily compute the flux per element at the nodes 'FLUX_ELNO'.

The estimator supplies the following components:

- ERTABS: total absolute error
- ERTREL: total relative error
- TERMNO: normalisation term for the relative total error
- TERMVO: absolute error of the residual of equilibrium (volume term)
- TERMV2: relative error of the residual of equilibrium (volume term)
- TERMV1: normalisation term for the volume error
- TERMSA: absolute error of the jumps of the heat flux between two mesh cells
- TERMS2: relative error of the jumps of the heat flux between two mesh cells
- TERMS1: normalisation term for the relative error in jump
- TERMFL: absolute error on the boundary conditions of imposed-flux type
- TERMF2: relative error on the boundary conditions of imposed-flux type
- TERMF1: normalisation term on the relative error on the flux
- TERMEC: absolute error on the boundary conditions of exchange type
- TERME2: relative error on the boundary conditions of exchange type
- TERME1: normalisation term on the relative error on the exchanges

**A few pieces of advice for a good use:**

- particular overloading rules concerning the loadings (generation of an alarm <A> in the event of non-compliance);
- computation over the whole mesh associated with the model (generation of an error <F> in the event of non-compliance) between two time steps, contiguous or not (generation of an alarm <A> in the event of non-compliance);
- all the 2D-plane/axisymmetric and 3D elements are treated (except PYRAM: generation of an alarm <A>);
- all the boundary conditions except ECHANGE_PAROI, FLUX_NL and RAYO are taken into account (generation of an alarm <A> in the event of use of ECHANGE_PAROI, FLUX_NL or RAYO);
- the mesh tolerates "rough drafts" but needs to be somewhat "cleaned up" (no SEG/FACE inserted in surfaces/volumes, problem of symmetrisation, of double points: generation of an alarm <A> or of an error <F>).

### 3.2 The error estimators in quantity of interest

#### 3.2.1 Warning

Unlike the classical error indicators above, the error indicators said to be in quantity of interest make it possible to access a **local error** on physical quantities (stress, displacement), and therefore in the unit of this quantity.

Their use is more delicate and doubtless calls for a rapid reading of the documentation [bib9] and a more attentive reading of the recommendations given for the use of CALC_ERREUR.

Moreover, their development being very recent, there is little feedback of experience on their use. What follows is therefore taken entirely from the reference documentation and from a report [bib10].

#### 3.2.2 What do these indicators compute?

These error indicators make it possible to estimate, in a subregion of the mesh, a physical quantity, for example:

A mean of displacement (the component $x$ for example) in this subregion, expressed mathematically by the equation $Q(u)=\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}{u}_{x}d\omega$.

A mean of stresses (for example the component $xx$) $Q(u)=\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\sigma}_{xx}d\omega$.

Once the quantity of interest has been chosen, the indicator estimates the quantity ${\varepsilon}^{Q}=Q(u)-Q({u}^{h})$.

It is here that the difficulty for the user arises, because this quantity of interest must be expressed in terms of a loading **known** in AFFE_CHER_MECA.

For example, for the quantity of interest as a mean of the displacement, it is equivalent to a body loading:

$Q(u)=\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}{u}_{x}d\Omega =\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}fud\Omega$ with $\overrightarrow{f}=(1,0,0)$

By choosing the vector $\overrightarrow{f}$ correctly, one accesses each component of the displacement and possibly linear combinations of the components. This loading is accessible by the keyword 'FORCE_INTERNE' in 'AFFE_CHAR_MECA'.

In the same way, for the quantity of interest as a mean of the stress, it is equivalent to an initial-strain loading:

$Q(u)=\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\sigma}_{xx}d\Omega =\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}A:\sigma (u)d\Omega =\frac{1}{\mid \Omega \mid }\underset{\Omega}{\int}A:K:\varepsilon (u)d\Omega$

One controls the choice of the component of the stress tensor by the choice of the components of the tensor $A$. This loading is accessible by the keyword 'PRE_EPSI' in 'AFFE_CHAR_MECA'.

These two quantities are fairly simple to implement because they are represented by a loading existing in *Code_Aster*. For more complicated quantities (functions of the stress tensor, for example) and possibly non-linear ones, it is very difficult to express them as a function of known loadings. This is the main difficulty to be overcome in order to program new quantities of interest.

#### 3.2.3 Scope of use

These quantities of interest are computed from the classical error indicators presented in § 3.1 and only for mechanics. Their domain of use is therefore identical to that of the mechanical indicator chosen in order to compute them.

One recalls, again, that the quantity of interest chosen depends on the degree of development of the loadings in AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

#### 3.2.4 The different options in CALC_ERREUR

The different options possible in CALC_ERREUR are:

- 'QIZ1_ELEM' (respectively 'QIZ2_ELEM'): error estimator in quantity of interest based on the Zhu-Zienkiewicz method. One must compute beforehand the option 'ERZ1_ELEM' or 'ERZ2_ELEM'.
- 'QIRE_ELEM' (per element) or 'QIRE_ELNO' (per element at the nodes): error estimator in quantity of interest based on the residuals in mechanics. One must compute beforehand the option 'ERME_ELEM'.

**Advice on use:**

The domain of use of the options 'QIZ1_ELEM' and 'QIZ2_ELEM' is the same as for the options 'ERZ1_ELEM' and 'ERZ2_ELEM', and that of the option 'QIRE_ELEM' is the same as that of the option 'ERME_ELEM' in mechanics.

To date, only two quantities of interest are available: mean of a component of the displacement and mean of a component of the stress tensor.

The computation of the quantity of interest passes through the solution of an elastic analysis (command MECA_STATIQUE) whose loading is, on the one hand, the same as that of the main problem on the Dirichlet conditions (hence in imposed displacement), and, on the other hand, that representing the quantity of interest chosen ('FORCE_INTERNE' or 'PRE_EPSI'). We give below an example of a command file.

# SOLUTION OF THE PROBLEM

CHARG=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(

MODELE=MO,

FACE_IMPO=(_F(GROUP_MA='GRMA1',DY=0.0,),

_F(GROUP_MA='GRMA2', DY=0.91333,),),

_F(GROUP_MA='GRMA4', FX=-2.0,),),);

EXCIT=_F(CHARGE=CHARG,),);

RESU=CALC_ERREUR(reuse =RESU,

RESULTAT=RESU,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

OPTION=('SIGM_ELNO','ERME_ELEM',),);

# COMPUTATION OF THE QUANTITY OF INTEREST AS A MEAN OF STRESS ON THE

MESH-CELL GROUP GRMAQI

CHARQ=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(

MODELE=MO,

FACE_IMPO=(_F(GROUP_MA='GRMA1',DY=0.0,),

_F(GROUP_MA='GRMA2',DY=0.91333,),),

FORCE_INTERNE=_F(GROUP_MA='GRMAQI',FX=1.0,),);

RESUQ=MECA_STATIQUE(MODELE=MO,

CHAM_MATER=CM,

EXCIT=_F(CHARGE=CHARQ,),);

RESUQ=CALC_ERREUR(reuse =RESUQ,

RESULTAT=RESUQ,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

OPTION=('SIGM_ELNO','ERME_ELEM',),);

# COMPUTATION OF THE ERROR ESTIMATOR IN QUANTITY OF INTEREST

RESU=CALC_ERREUR(reuse =RESU,

RESU_DUAL=RESUQ,);

### 3.3 Detection of singularities

Another possible field (only in mechanics) which may serve as a criterion for refining the mesh is the use of the option 'SING_ELEM' (field constant per element) or 'SING_ELNO' (fields per element at the nodes) in CALC_ERREUR. This field is computed from the error indicators and it aims to improve the treatment of the singularities in the mesh-adaptation strategies. The reader will find in [bib8] a theoretical description of the methods employed.

In practice the error indicators are high in the singular zones, so that rapidly only the singular zones are refined. This therefore masks the other sensitive zones (zones with a strong gradient) which one would wish to refine. This field, constant per element, comprises two components:

- 'DEGRE', which indicates the level of singularity of a mesh cell. In practice, this component is equal to the degree of interpolation of the finite elements chosen if the finite element is connected to no singularity, and is equal to the order of the singularity if the finite element is connected to a node considered by the method as singular (for example, for an element neighbouring the tip of a crack, this value is equal to 0.5). This value cannot be greater than the degree of interpolation, hence 1 or 2.
- 'RAPPORT', which corresponds to the map of modification of the size of the finite elements in the event of remeshing for a given global error. This component is equal to the ratio between the current size and the new size of the finite element.
- 'TAILLE', which corresponds to the size map of the finite elements in the event of remeshing for a given global error.

**Advice on use:**

The computation of this option requires, beforehand, the computation of an error indicator (it is the absolute component which is used, and this is hard-coded in Code_Aster) and of the total strain energy. In the case where one of these options is not computed, an alarm message is emitted and the option 'SING_ELEM' is not computed.

For the total strain energy, one uses 'ETOT_ELEM' with STAT_NON_LINE or 'EPOT_ELEM' with MECA_STATIQUE.

The user must also fill in the keyword 'PREC_ERR' (a fatal message is emitted in the event of absence), which makes it possible to compute the accuracy wished for on the global error in order to determine the map of size modification. The value of 'PREC_ERR' lies strictly between 0 and 1, and a fatal message is emitted if this condition is not satisfied.

The scope of use is the same (but more reduced) as that of the error indicator chosen, namely:

- For the residual indicator: finite elements of continuous media in 2D (triangles and quadrangles) or 3D (only the tetrahedra) for an elastoplastic constitutive behaviour,
- For the Zhu-Zienkiewicz indicator: finite elements of continuous media in 2D (triangles and quadrangles) for an elastic constitutive behaviour.

Strictly speaking, the computation of the order of the singularity is obtained from the theoretical energy at the crack tip, an equation valid only in elasticity. The use of this option in elastoplasticity must therefore be handled with caution.

## 4 Advice and good practice

### 4.1 On the initial mesh

Independently of any wish to carry out mesh adaptation, we advise checking the initial mesh with the command MACR_INFO_MAIL [bib4]. This command makes it possible to carry out, at little cost, the following checks:

- check the agreement of the mesh with the initial geometry (in mass, in dimension, in area and in volume);
- list the GROUP_MA and the GROUP_NO, for a good modelling of the boundary conditions;
- diagnose possible problems (symmetrisation or connectivity, rough-draft elements, poor taking into account of boundary conditions, interpenetration of mesh cells);
- evaluate the quality of the mesh by the following criterion q (evaluated mesh cell by mesh cell):
- for the triangles: $q=\alpha \times \frac{h}{r}$, where h is the length of the longest side, r the radius of the inscribed circle and a a normalisation coefficient such that q is equal to 1 for an equilateral triangle,
- for the quadrangles: $q=\alpha \times \frac{h\times k}{S}$, where h is the maximum of the lengths of the sides and of the diagonals, k the root-mean-square of the lengths of the sides, S the minimum of the areas of the four internal triangles and a a normalisation coefficient such that q is equal to 1 for a square,
- for the tetrahedra: $q=\alpha \times \frac{h}{r}$, where h is the length of the longest side, r the radius of the inscribed sphere and a a normalisation coefficient such that q is equal to 1 for an equilateral tetrahedron,
- for the hexahedra: $q=\alpha \times (\text{qualité du pire des 24 tétraèdres inscrits})$, where a is a normalisation coefficient such that q is equal to 1 for a cube.

**Advice:** An empirical criterion may be proposed: for example, at least 50% of the mesh cells with a quality < 1.5, at least 90% of the mesh cells with a quality < 2.0, no mesh cells with a quality above 10.

### 4.2 On HOMARD

After cutting of the mesh cells, the mesh-cell groups are updated; on the other hand the node groups are left unchanged. One must make it a rule to impose boundary conditions on mesh-cell groups and not on node groups; one must therefore proscribe (but this is a rule of common sense) the direct use of mesh-cell or node numbers during the assignments, and prefer to it the notion of mesh-cell group.

One recalls that adaptation by HOMARD accepts all types of element, of degree 1 or 2, in a conforming mesh, in zones which are connected or not, of the same dimension or not, except the pyramids and the pentahedra (for uniform refinement). On the other hand, for mesh analysis, HOMARD handles the pentahedra.

### 4.3 On the adaptation strategy

The chaining "thermomechanical operators / MACR_ADAP_MAIL option 'RAFFINEMENT_UNIFORME'" (i.e. without error indicator) makes it possible to make a mesh converge cleanly, automatically and easily. One must however beware of the number of degrees of freedom generated! This constitutes an easy solution, rapid and robust, but rapidly extremely costly (rather to be reserved for evaluating whether there are large discretisation errors, or for small studies).

The chaining "thermomechanical operators / MACR_ADAP_MAIL option 'RAFFINEMENT'" (i.e. with a field driving the adaptation) makes it possible to make the mesh converge in the most optimal manner possible (taking into account the tools available). This method demands more effort than the previous one, but the number of degrees of freedom generated is proportionally much smaller.

The chaining "thermomechanical operators / MACR_ADAP_MAIL" can be carried out effectively in a Python loop (loop of "for" type), possibly with an exit test (loop of "while" type).

The quality of the mesh cells is little affected by the refinement / unrefinement process. Taking into account the choices made in HOMARD®, it may even improve in 3D!

MACR_ADAP_MAIL does not have a regularisation process; hence a poor initial mesh will probably produce a poor adapted mesh!

Linear elements are not recommended in mechanics. Good practice is rather: lumped P1 in thermal analysis (PLAN_DIAG, AXIS_DIAG, 3D_DIAG) and P2 (possibly under-integrated) in mechanics, [bib14].

The choice of the type of finite elements takes precedence over the quality of the mesh cells on which the elements rest (Cf. the example of the beam below).

### 4.4 On the classical error indicators

Choice of the error indicator in mechanics: the user has the choice between $\mathrm{ZZ1}$ (first version of the Zhu-Zienkiewicz indicator), $\mathrm{ZZ2}$ (second version of the Zhu-Zienkiewicz indicator), and the residual indicator. The first two have a fairly reduced domain of application (linear 2D for $\mathrm{ZZ1}$ and $\mathrm{ZZ2}$, a single type of finite element in the whole mesh for $\mathrm{ZZ2}$): for a "standard" use, one will prefer the residual indicator.

The type of indicator and its mode of normalisation may have an effect on the adapted mesh. For example, in mechanics, ${e}_{{E}_{\text{REL}}}=100\times \frac{{e}_{E}}{\sqrt{{e}_{E}^{2}+{\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{E}^{2}}}$. This way of normalising may be dangerous: if there are zones where the stress norm is low, the error will approach 100% in this zone; if there are zones where the stress norm is very high (singularities, for example), the error will be low in this zone. This is obviously not the result sought. *One must therefore use preferentially the absolute indicator, unless one knows precisely what one is doing.*

For indicators of residual type, one can also "juggle" with the components of the thermal indicator and with the boundary conditions, "fictitious" or not, in order to steer the construction of a mesh which is refined or unrefined by zones.

In the event of the presence of singularities, it is advised to select the number of elements on which the refinement bears by a fraction of elements to be refined, 'CRIT_RAFF_PE' (and not by the quantum of elements presenting an error greater than a fraction of the total error, 'CRIT_RAFF_REL'). Indeed, in the case of a singularity, by using 'CRIT_RAFF_REL', after one or two iterations of adaptation, only the elements touching the singularity will be refined. By using 'CRIT_RAFF_PE', other zones will be able to continue to be refined. Finally the criterion 'CRIT_RAFF_ABS' (choice by a fixed error barrier) is to be reserved for the cases in which the user knows the problem envisaged very well.

As a "simple post-processing" of the thermomechanical problem, the indicator unfortunately cannot supply a more reliable diagnosis in the zones where the solution of the initial problem comes to grief. It is therefore preferable to begin an adaptation process with a mesh already somewhat refined "by hand".

In thermomechanics, different mesh-adaptation strategies are offered to the user:

- adapt the mesh according to a thermal criterion only,
- adapt the mesh according to a mechanical criterion only,
- adapt jointly or separately (i.e. with one or two adaptation loops); in short, chain or couple the first two strategies.

Good practice during such a thermomechanical analysis leads one to use two meshes and to interpolate the thermal field ${P}_{1}$ onto the mechanical mesh ${P}_{2}$ (via the operator PROJ_CHAMP). If one wishes to work with only a single mesh, one may apply one of the strategies via the option MAJ_CHAM of MACR_ADAP_MAIL. This makes it possible, while adapting the mesh according to a criterion, to update the complementary field on the new adapted mesh.

In thermal analysis, in order to carry out a mesh adaptation based on the indicator ERTH_ELEM in the course of a transient, one must not forget to start the computation of the following time step with the old EVOL_THER updated onto the new mesh.

### 5.2 Study on the HP rotor

**Context:**

This study was carried out in the AMA department in 2006 within the framework of the Project Lifetime of the Steam Turbines of the nuclear fleet. Very briefly, the point is to locate the zones of maximum stress encountered on the HP rotor (figure 5.2-a) of a turbine and to evaluate numerically the stress level, in order to check whether there exists a risk of crack initiation by stress-corrosion cracking. In order to reach a conclusion on the risk of cracking by SCC, several modellings were carried out, one of them a 2D axisymmetric modelling of the complete rotor with an aspect concerning the quality of the mesh. The reader is referred to the document [bib12] for further information on this study; our objective here is to point out only the mesh aspect.

**Approach of the study:**

This study is concerned with the maximum stresses obtained numerically, in order to compare them with threshold stresses coming from experimental tests. The analysis of the Von Mises stresses is required in the first place, because this type of stress is always an upper bound with respect to the relevant components of the stress tensor. Thus, when the Von Mises stresses are lower than the threshold stresses, one considers that the risk of cracking by SCC is unlikely in the absence of impurities. However, when the Von Mises stresses are higher than the threshold of cracking by SCC, it becomes necessary to analyse more finely the maximum values of the stress tensor, as well as the principal stresses, in order to evaluate the margins with respect to the risk of cracking by SCC.

**Modelling:**

The constitutive behaviour of the material is elastic. The loading is of centrifugal-force type due to the rotation. The analysis is carried out with the command STAT_NON_LINE. The initial mesh is free and very fine in the zones of curvature, in order to obtain stress values that are as reliable as possible. The elements are quadrilaterals of degree two. The mesh comprises 71037 elements and 217198 nodes.

**Result:**

One looks here at the zones where the Von Mises stress is maximum. The zone most heavily loaded in stress is situated near the coupling on the forward-bearing side, in the zone of curvature of the groove between the coupling and the rest of the rotor (zone A). The other zones loaded in stress are situated at the junction of the blade attachments on the rotor (zones B and C, for example).

One must not deprive oneself of these additional analyses, all the more so as setting them up is simple and carrying them out is automated. Next, one notes the interest of carrying out boundary tracking if the geometry presents curved zones.

### 5.5 Thermoplastic example

One considers the following structure of revolution (modelled in axisymmetric):

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.01/Object_39.svg)

Figure 5.5-a: Diagram of the thermoelastic analysis

where the greyed parts are plastic, the rest elastic. The loading is applied in 2 steps:

- the first consists of a purely mechanical loading (pressure on the zone with arrows on the diagram), with a loading phase followed by an unloading phase;
- the second consists of a transient thermal loading (exchange condition on the lower and upper parts of the structure).

**Remeshing strategy and list of time instants:**

The loading is discretised according to a list of time instants; the question then arises: what strategy should one adopt with respect to remeshing? Indeed, depending on the case treated, one can:

- remesh at each computational step: the mesh is then adapted to each computational step individually. One must then project the fields from one mesh onto the other;
- remesh only once, at the end of the analysis, and start the analysis again from the beginning with the new mesh.

The first strategy is to be adopted in the case where the refinement zones evolve a great deal; we shall see an example of it in the following thermal analysis; the second may be adopted in the case where the refinement zones evolve little, as in this mechanical case, where the point is to follow the growth of a plastic zone.

**Mechanical analysis:**

For the mechanical analysis, one therefore adopts the following strategy:

- computation of the whole list of time instants;
- remeshing;
- repetition of (1 & 2) until a satisfactory result is obtained.

It is not so much the implementation in *Code_Aster* which is of interest in this case (it differs from the previous implementations only by the fact that there are several computational time instants) as the results obtained by mesh adaptation on a non-linear case.

In order to judge the contribution of the remeshing, let us look at the radial stresses on the segment separating the two regions (figure), which are compared with a "reference" obtained by 3 uniform remeshings: the gain from the remeshings based on the error indicator is visible.

**Computation of the thermal transient:**

The point here is to compute a thermal transient, two exchange conditions being imposed at the bottom and at the top of the structure. As the zone which will present a strong temperature gradient will move within the structure (advance of a front), the strategy adopted for the remeshing will take this into account: one must update the mesh regularly in the course of the transient. In practice, one subdivides the list of time instants into blocks; inside these blocks of computational time instants the mesh will be the same (and the remeshing takes place at the end of the block). There are therefore 3 nested loops:

- the loop over the N blocks of time instants;
- the loop over the remeshings of the current block;
- the loop (hidden in THER_LINEAIRE) over the time instants of the block.

If one looks at the results at the last time instant computed, in particular the temperature on the post-processing line already used in mechanics (figure), one notes the interest of mesh adaptation. ### 5.6 Plate presenting a localisation band

**Modelling:**

It is a rectangular plate subjected to compression. The constitutive behaviour of the material is softening (negative isotropic hardening, law 'DRUCKER-PRAGER'). The analysis is carried out with the command STAT_NON_LINE. The mesh is composed of QUAD8. Because of the softening constitutive behaviour, one observes a localisation band of the cumulative plastic strain. Inside this band, the constitutive behaviour is plastic and the plasticity indicator is equal to 1; outside this band, the constitutive behaviour remains elastic and the plasticity indicator is equal to 0.

**Adaptation process:**

The adaptation of the mesh is carried out with the plasticity indicator (third internal variable V3 of the law DRUCK_PRAGER). The cutting criterion chosen is a threshold value fixed at 0.5 (keyword 'CRIT_RAF_ABS'). This guarantees that one refines in the localisation zone, which is the user's objective in this study.

```text
MACR_ADAP_MAIL    (

ADAPTATION    = 'RAFFINEMENT',

MAILLAGE_N    =    MAIL_INI,

MAILLAGE_NP1    =    co (‘MAIL_FIN’),

RESULTAT_N    =    resu,
NOM_CHAM    =    ‘VARI_ELGA’,
NOM_CMP    =    ‘V3’,

CRIT_RAFF_ABS        =    0.5,)
```

**Result of the recutting:**

All the mesh cells are cut into four, even in the elastic zone. This result, which may appear surprising at first sight, is due to the mode of cutting adopted by HOMARD and in particular to the rules of enforcement of conformity (cf. § 2.3). The figures and illustrate this result.
