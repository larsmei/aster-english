# r7.01.38 Iwan law for the cyclic behaviour of granular materials

**Summary:**

The constitutive model of Iwan [ 1 ], [ 2 ] is a classical multi-mechanism elastoplastic model for the description of the cyclic deviatoric behaviour of materials and particularly of geomaterials. The hardening is linear kinematic for each mechanism and the flow rule considered is associated. By construction, the model automatically satisfies the Masing rules.

The model allows an easy description of the behaviour via parameters obtained directly from the curves of variation of the secant shear modulus. The user must simply supply the parameters of a hyperbolic function describing the variation of the secant modulus as a function of the shear strain.

It is implemented in the MFront environment, which facilitates the implementation and the maintainability of the model.

## 1 Theoretical formulation

### 1.1 1D view of the behaviour of the Iwan model

A purely 1D description of the Iwan model consists of an arrangement in parallel or in series of Jenkin elements, each element being composed of a spring and of a perfectly plastic frictional slider (Coulomb criterion) in series (Figure 1). The arrangement of the elements and the parameters of the model determine the shape of the cyclic shear response.

### 1.2 3D formulation of the Iwan model

In the 3D description of the behaviour, each element is associated with a yield surface, defined from the deviatoric stress associated with each mechanism. One therefore passes from a 1D description of the shear behaviour to a 3D description of the deviatoric behaviour on the basis of the stress and strain invariants, defined in the following manner:

$$
\textrm{Pression moyenne:}
\text{p}=\frac{\text{t}\text{r}(\text{σ})}{3}
$$

$$
\textrm{Tenseur déviateur de contraintes:}
\mathrm{S}=\sigma -p\mathrm{I}
$$

| Deviatoric stress: $\text{q}={\mathrm{S}}_{\text{V}\text{M}}^{3\text{D}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\text{S}:\text{S}}$ |  |
| --- | --- |

$$
\textrm{Déformation volumique:}
{\epsilon}_{v}=\mathit{tr}\left(\epsilon \right)
$$

| Deviatoric strain: ${\text{q}}_{\text{n}}={\left(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}}\right)}_{\text{V}\text{M}}^{3\text{D}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}}):(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}})}$ |  |
| --- | --- |

For an arrangement of series-parallel type, when the applied stress exceeds the limit stress ${Y}_{n}$ of mechanism $n$ , there is hardening of this mechanism and the associated stress remains equal to the limit stress, i.e. only a kinematic hardening is associated with each mechanism of the model. The model allows great flexibility in the description of the behaviour, in particular through the possibility of direct use of laboratory test data. The error associated with the description of the cyclic behaviour depends directly on the number of yield surfaces and on their arrangement. However, the greater the number of mechanisms, the more numerically expensive the system to be solved, despite the passage from a tensorial system to a scalar system (section 2).

The model is based on the theory of multi-mechanism elastoplasticity. In a series-parallel description, it assumes an additive and independent decomposition of the strains on the hardening mechanisms:

$$
\epsilon =\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\epsilon}_{n}
$$

For each mechanism, the model considers an additive decomposition of the total strain tensor $\text{ε}$ into an elastic part ${\text{ε}}_{\text{n}}^{\text{e}}$ and a plastic part ${\epsilon}_{n}^{p}$ :

$$
{\epsilon}_{n}={\epsilon}_{n}^{e}+{\epsilon}_{n}^{p}
$$

The stress–strain relation is written directly from the fourth-order elasticity tensor, $C$ **.** The elasticity is therefore linear and the model does not make it possible to take into account the effect of confinement in the elastic constants.

$$
\sigma =C{\epsilon}^{e}
$$

The model describing only the deviatoric behaviour, the volumetric response is assumed linear elastic:

$$
{\epsilon}_{v}=\frac{p}{3K}
$$

where $>$ is the bulk modulus.

In what follows, the various ingredients necessary to describe the constitutive law in an elastoplastic framework are described, namely:

- Yield surface $\text{f}$ : defines the zone in the stress space inside which the behaviour is linear elastic ($\text{f}<0$ or $\text{f}=0$ and $d\text{f}<0$ ) and on which the behaviour is plastic ($\text{f}=0$ and $d\text{f}>0$ ).
- Flow law: rule defining the plastic strain increment, in a generic manner ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\Psi$ , where $\dot{\lambda}$ is the plastic multiplier and $\Psi$ the flow law.

#### 1.2.1 Description of the yield surface

The yield surface of each mechanism is written in the following way:

$$
{f}_{n}={q}_{n}-{Y}_{n}
$$

With ${Y}_{n}$ a limit constant from which the mechanism $>$ hardens. This value is chosen from a pure shear test, by associating with each hardening mechanism $>$ a pair of values $\left({\gamma}_{n},{\tau}_{n}\right)$ with $\gamma =2{\epsilon}_{ij}$ , $i\ne j$ . ${Y}_{n}$ is therefore obtained directly from ${\tau}_{n}$ by the following relation:

$$
{Y}_{n}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{\tau}_{n}
$$

The value of ${q}_{n}$ is the deviatoric stress associated with mechanism $n$ , defined in the following manner:

| ${\text{q}}_{\text{n}}={\left(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}}\right)}_{\text{V}\text{M}}^{3\text{D}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}}):(\text{S}-{\text{X}}_{\text{n}})}$ |  |
| --- | --- |

The tensor ${\text{X}}_{\text{n}}$ represents the kinematic hardening of mechanism $\text{n}$ . It is obtained from the expression defined by Prager:

$$
{X}_{n}={C}_{n}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}
$$

With ${C}_{n}$ a parameter of the model determined from a monotonic shear test. Because ${C}_{n}$ is constant, one considers the hardening to be linear.

#### 1.2.2 Description of the flow law

The plastic strain increments of each mechanism $n$ are obtained from the plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{n}$ and from the flow law ${\Psi}_{n}$ :

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}={\dot{\lambda}}_{n}{\Psi}_{n}
$$

The model assuming an associated flow, the flow law is obtained directly from the yield surface:

$$
{\Psi}_{n}=\frac{\partial {f}_{n}}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\left(S-{X}_{n}\right)}{{q}_{n}}
$$

The plastic multiplier associated with each mechanism is obtained from the consistency equation, i.e. ${\dot{f}}_{n}=0$ :

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}=\frac{\frac{\partial {f}_{n}}{\partial \sigma }:{C}_{n}:\dot{\epsilon}}{\frac{\partial {f}_{n}}{\partial \sigma }:{C}_{n}:{\Psi}_{n}-{C}_{n}\frac{\partial {f}_{n}}{\partial {X}_{n}}:{\Psi}_{n}}
$$

With:

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{n}}{{\partial X}_{n}}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\left(S-{X}_{n}\right)}{{q}_{n}}
$$

The computation of the plastic multiplier is set out in section 2.

#### 1.2.3 Computation of the hardening parameters ${C}_{n}$

The determination of the kinematic hardening parameters ${C}_{n}$ is carried out from a stress path in pure shear ($\Delta p=0$ ). Suppose the response known for this stress path from an initial state with zero deviatoric stress and zero plastic strain. In this case:

$$
\dot{\gamma}=\left(\frac{1}{2G}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\frac{1}{{C}_{n}}\right)\dot{\tau}
$$

From $>$ pairs $\left({\gamma}_{n},{\tau}_{n}\right)$ , one can estimate the value of ${C}_{n}$ recursively with the following expression:

$$
\frac{1}{{C}_{n}}=\frac{{\gamma}_{k+1}-{\gamma}_{k}}{{\tau}_{k+1}-{\tau}_{k}}-\frac{1}{2G}-\sum_{m=1}^{k-1}\frac{1}{{C}_{m}}
$$

The computation of the kinematic hardening parameters ${C}_{n}$ is carried out in the @InitLocalVars block of Mfront.

## 2 Numerical integration of the constitutive law

The choice in MFront is directed towards the integration of the systems of differential equations written in the following manner:

$$
\dot{Y}=G\left(Y,t\right)
$$

Where $G$ is an a priori non-linear function and assumed at least continuously differentiable. An implicit integration of theta-method type is chosen for the numerical integration of the non-linear system. In this case the system is written in the following manner:

$$
Y{|}_{t+\Delta t}=Y{|}_{t}+\Delta tG\left(\left\lbrace 1-\theta \right\rbrace Y{|}_{t}+\theta Y{|}_{t+\Delta t},t+\theta \Delta t\right)
$$

Or equivalently:

$$
F\left(\Delta Y\right)=\Delta Y-\Delta tG\left(\left\lbrace 1-\theta \right\rbrace Y{|}_{t}+\theta Y{|}_{t+\Delta t},t+\theta \Delta t\right)=0
$$

The Newton–Raphson method is chosen for the computation of the zero of the function $F\left(\Delta Y\right)$ . That is:

$$
F\left(\Delta Y\right)=F\left(\Delta {Y}^{-}\right)+{\left(\frac{dF}{d\Delta Y}\right)}_{\Delta Y=\Delta {Y}^{-}}\left(\Delta Y+\Delta {Y}^{-}\right)
$$

Where $J=\frac{dF}{d\Delta Y}$ is the Jacobian matrix of the function $F$ . The approximation $\Delta {Y}^{+}$ is obtained by considering $F\left(\Delta {Y}^{+}\right)=0$ , that is:

$$
\Delta {Y}^{+}=\Delta {Y}^{-}-\left({J}^{-1}\left(\Delta {Y}^{-}\right)\right)F\left(\Delta {Y}^{-}\right)
$$

The difficulty of the Newton–Raphson method lies in the computation of the Jacobian matrix, the analytical computation of which is supplied in section 2.4. A purely implicit scheme is chosen for the integration of the equations ($\theta =1$ ), in order to give physically satisfactory results.

Following the approach presented in document [ 4 ], for a linear kinematic hardening it is possible to reduce the tensorial system to a scalar system, thanks to the collinearity between the plastic strain increment ${\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}$ and the difference $S-{X}_{n}$ in the absence of plastic strain, ${S}_{n}^{e}$ . This can be written in the following manner for a multiple kinematic hardening:

$$
{S}_{n}^{e}={S}^{-}+\dot{S}-{X}_{n}^{-}={S}^{-}+2\mu {\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{e}-{X}_{n}^{-}
$$

Therefore:

$$
S-{X}_{n}={S}_{n}^{e}-{\dot{X}}_{n}={S}_{n}^{e}-{C}_{n}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}
$$

The plastic strain increment of mechanism $>$ is therefore written:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}}{{q}_{n}}\left({S}_{n}^{e}-{C}_{n}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}\right)=\frac{3}{2}\frac{{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}}{{q}_{n}^{e}}{S}_{n}^{e}
$$

With ${q}_{n}^{e}$ the norm of ${S}_{n}^{e}$ . By substituting ${\dot{\epsilon}}_{n}^{p}$ into the expression (), one obtains:

$$
S-{X}_{n}={S}_{n}^{e}\left(1-\frac{\frac{3}{2}{C}_{n}\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}{{q}_{n}^{e}}\right)
$$

By injecting the expression of $S-{X}_{n}$ into the condition $f=0$ , one obtains:

$$
\left({q}_{n}^{e}-\frac{3}{2}{C}_{n}\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}\right)-{Y}_{n}=0
$$

Insofar as ${Y}_{n}>0$ and ${q}_{n}^{e}>0$ , this equation admits the following solution for the plastic multiplier:

$$
\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}=\frac{{q}_{n}^{e}-{Y}_{n}}{\frac{3}{2}{C}_{n}}
$$

Therefore, necessarily:

$$
0<\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}<\frac{{q}_{n}^{e}}{\frac{3}{2}{C}_{n}}
$$

The system to be solved is therefore based directly on the plastic multiplier and not on the hardening tensor, which is an auxiliary variable (@AuxiliaryStateVariable) in the MFront routine.

Insofar as the update of the stress tensor in the @ComputeStress block is carried out before the Newton iteration of the solution algorithm, in the MFront routine ${q}_{n}^{e}$ is obtained directly from the updated value of the deviatoric tensor $\mathrm{S}$ , ${q}_{n}$ .

### 2.1 Prediction stage

The prediction stage is managed from the factor keyword PREDICTION of the keyword NEWTON in the non-linear solution operator, the @Predictor block in MFront not needing to be supplied for a solution with **code_aster** .

### 2.2 Correction stage by the Newton algorithm

The vector of unknowns being given by $Y=\left({\epsilon}^{e},{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}\right)$ , the iterations of the Newton algorithm consist in solving the following system:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{f}_{\epsilon}^{e}={\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}-\dot{\epsilon}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}{\Psi}_{n}=0\\ {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}=\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}-\frac{{q}_{n}-{Y}_{n}}{\frac{3}{2}{C}_{n}}\end{array}
$$

Writing the equations in scalar form makes it possible to reduce the size of the system of equations to be solved and in this way to improve the performance of the model.

### 2.3 Update stage

Once the convergence of the local solution has been achieved, the vector of unknowns is updated in the classical manner as follows:

$$
{Y}^{+}={Y}_{\text{j+}1}={Y}_{j}+\delta {Y}_{\text{j+}1}
$$

One also takes the opportunity to update the auxiliary variables ${X}_{n}$ and ${\epsilon}^{p}$ in the @UpdateAuxiliaryStateVars block.

### 2.4 Computation of the terms of the Jacobian matrix

In order to compute the consistent tangent operator (directive @TangentOperator of MFront), one must have the terms of the Jacobian matrix $J$ of the system of equations to be solved. In the present case, the Jacobian matrix is written in the following manner:

$$
J=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}{I}_{4}& \frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}\otimes I\\ \frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}\otimes I& \frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}\end{array}\right]
$$

MFront makes it possible to obtain these terms numerically by the use of the algorithm NewtonRaphson_NumericalJacobian. But the version developed here uses an analytical matrix, which is more efficient. In what follows, one proposes an analytical description of the partial derivatives by considering $\theta =1$ .

The terms to be computed are the following: $\frac{\partial {f}_{{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}}}{\partial {\dot{\lambda}}_{n}}$ (scalar), $\frac{\partial {f}_{{\dot{\lambda}}_{n}}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}$ (second-order tensor), $\frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}$ (second-order tensor) and $\frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}$ (fourth-order tensor). The partial derivatives with respect to the plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{n}$ for each mechanism $n$ are immediate:

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}=1
$$

And:

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}={\Psi}_{n}
$$

It remains to compute the derivatives with respect to the elastic strain ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}$ . One obtains the following expression for $\frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}$ :

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{\epsilon}^{e}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}=\frac{2\mu \dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}{{q}_{n}}\left(M-\Psi \otimes \Psi \right)
$$

With the following expression for the fourth-order tensor $M$ :

$$
M=\frac{3}{2}\left({I}_{4}-\frac{1}{3}I\otimes I\right)
$$

${I}_{4}$ being the fourth-order identity tensor and $\otimes$ the tensor product. The computation of $\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial \dot{{\epsilon}^{e}}}$ for each mechanism $n$ is more delicate, because it depends on whether the mechanism is active or not during the Newton iterations.

If the mechanism is not active (${f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}<0$ ):

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}=0
$$

If the mechanism is active (${f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}>0$ ):

$$
\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}=-\frac{4}{3}\frac{\mu}{{C}_{n}}{\Psi}_{n}:{I}_{4}
$$

On checking the values obtained for the numerical Jacobian (option @CompareToNumericalJacobian), it has been observed that for values of ${f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}$ close to zero (positive or negative), $\frac{\partial {f}_{\dot{{\lambda}_{n}}}}{\partial {\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}}$ is equal to half of the expression obtained analytically. The treatment in this case is carried out by considering a critical value of the value of the yield surface of ${10}^{-6}$ .

## 3 Implementation in code_aster

The number of kinematic hardening surfaces of the model is fixed at twelve. The first eleven surfaces make it possible to cover the range of shear strains between ${10}^{-5}$ and $2,0\times {10}^{-2}$ . One considers a linear elastic behaviour for strains smaller than ${10}^{-5}$ and a maximum shear strain of $2,0\times {10}^{-2}$ . The interpolation values used are the following:
