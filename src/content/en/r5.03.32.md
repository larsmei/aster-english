# r5.03.32 Constitutive law of the ASSE_CORN assembly

**Summary:**

This document describes the non-linear behaviour of the non-linear angle assemblies of pylons modelled by discrete elements DIS_TR. This constitutive law is assigned to the discrete elements by means of the relation ASSE_CORN, called by the operators for the solution of non-linear problems STAT_NON_LINE [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) or DYNA_NON_LINE [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05).

The law represents both the tensile behaviour of the assembly and the moment–rotation relation about the axis of the bolts perpendicular to the assembly. The other loading directions exhibit a linear elastic behaviour described by classical stiffness characteristics.

Two phases, associated with two mechanisms, are distinguished in the constitutive law: the first represents the friction and the slip of the bolts up to the stop, and the second represents the plastification of the assembly up to failure. The plastic-type laws describing each of these phases have the same shape and present a concavity at their junction which makes convergence problematic and requires a particular numerical treatment in the analysis options called by the iterative Newton method.

## 2 Physical model of the unidirectional behaviour of the assembly

The assembly of an angle onto the leg of another angle, or onto a plate (gusset or cover plate), by bolts is shown schematically in [Figure 2-a].

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/10000000000008FE00000387E7A6DCFBFB4A1F96.png)

**Figure 2-a: local frame of the connection; the axis** $x$ **coincides with the axis of the bar** **and the axis** $y$ **coincides with the axis of the bolts**

The unidirectional behaviour of the assembly is modelled for tensile or flexural loading.

The modelling adopted for the unidirectional behaviour under loading of the assembly subjected to a normal force or to a moment about $y$ is represented by [Figure 2-b].

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/100000000000049D000001CE22D88B1300C17E3B.png)

**Figure 2-b: assembly mechanisms in normal force and moment**

Two phases of the behaviour, associated with two mechanisms, are distinguished:

| mechanism 1: | friction and slip up to the stop (onset of shear of the bolts). |
| --- | --- |
| mechanism 2: | plastification of the assembly up to failure by shear of the bolts or tearing-out of the edge distances. |

The friction limit surface (SLF) is the curve corresponding to the onset of slip in the spaces $({N}_{x}-{U}_{x})$ and $({M}_{y}-{\theta}_{y})$ . Friction is described by Coulomb's law.

The ultimate limit surface (SLU) is the curve corresponding to failure of the assembly in the spaces $({N}_{x}-{U}_{x})$  and $({M}_{y}-{\theta}_{y})$ . Failure may be due, depending on the design of the assembly, to shear of the bolts or to tearing-out of the edge distances.

Tests on the same geometry but with different bolt tightening torques show that the tangent stiffness of mechanism 2 at the stop point decreases as the SLF approaches the SLU.

This justifies the physical modelling adopted for the assembly of the two mechanisms [Figure 2-b].

## 3 Constitutive relation of the mechanisms

The behaviour of mechanisms 1 and 2 is similar. It is non-linear, between an initially rigid tangent behaviour and an asymptotic limit behaviour.

It is described by two essential parameters: the non-linearity parameter and the limit-surface parameter.

The stop (mechanism 1) or the failure (mechanism 2) are described by an associated kinematic criterion.

### 3.1 Unidirectional behaviour

It was stated in [§2] that the unidirectional behaviours in normal force and in moment about y are similar [Figure 2-b].

They can be described by the same relation if the dimensionless quantities are used:

1. reduced forces: $n=\frac{{N}_{x}}{\overline{N}}\text{et}m=\frac{{M}_{y}}{\overline{M}}$
2. reduced displacements: ${U}_{r}=\frac{U}{\overline{U}}\text{et}{\theta}_{r}=\frac{\theta}{\overline{\theta}}$

[Figure 3.1-a] represents the unidirectional behaviour in dimensionless form. Analytically, it may be written (this is a choice):

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{}{U}_{r}=h(n)\text{ou}{\theta}_{r}=h(m)\\ \text{avec}h(x)=\frac{1}{\overline{d}}\frac{{x}^{a+1}}{1-{x}^{a}}\\ \overline{d}=\frac{{\overline{n}}^{a+1}}{1-{\overline{n}}^{a}}\end{array}\end{array}$

$a$ is the scalar non-linearity parameter. $\overline{n}$ and $a$ are identified from the unidirectional tests. $\overline{n}$ , which takes the variability of the tests into account, generally takes the value $0.95$ .

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/100015EA00002DFE000014C6890AEB74205214A0.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: constitutive relation of the assembly**

It is observed that $h(\overline{n})=1$ or $h(\overline{m})=1$ , that is: ${U}_{r}=1$ or ${\theta}_{r}=1$ , or again: $U=\overline{U}$ or $\theta =\overline{\theta}$ .

The unidirectional kinematic criterion is therefore satisfied for $n=\overline{n}$ or $m=\overline{m}$ .

### 3.2 Incremental two-dimensional behaviour

The coupling at the limit is defined by the limit surface:

${(\frac{{N}_{x}}{\overline{N}})}^{2}+{(\frac{{M}_{y}}{\overline{M}})}^{2}=1$

The unidirectional behaviour in reduced variables is described by the relation of [§3.1]:

$\begin{array}{c}\underline{d}=h(\underline{f})\end{array}$

| where | $\underline{d}$ is the reduced displacement vector $(\begin{array}{}{U}_{r}\\ {\theta}_{r}\end{array})$ |
| --- | --- |
|  | $\underline{f}$ is the reduced force vector $(\begin{array}{}n\\ m\end{array})$ |

In two-dimensional behaviour, isotropy is expressed by a model with one **scalar** internal variable $p$ such that:

$\begin{array}{c}p=h(\text{feq})\text{en chargement}\end{array}$

where $\text{feq}$ is the equivalent reduced force (scalar).

$\text{feq}$ is defined such that:

$\underline{F}=\text{feq}\ast \underline{\overline{F}}\ast$

| where | $\underline{F}$ is the current loading point $(\begin{array}{}{N}_{x}\\ {M}_{y}\end{array})$ |
| --- | --- |
|  | $\begin{array}{}\underline{\overline{F}}\ast \\ \end{array}$ is the limit loading associated with $\underline{F}$ $(\begin{array}{}{\overline{N}}_{x}^{\text{*}}\\ {\overline{M}}_{y}^{\text{*}}\end{array})$ |

The expression for $\text{feq}$ is deduced from the expression for the limit surface. The fact that $\begin{array}{}\underline{\overline{F}}\ast \\ \end{array}$ belongs to the limit surface is written:

${(\frac{{\overline{N}}_{x}^{\text{*}}}{\overline{N}})}^{2}+{(\frac{{\overline{M}}_{y}^{\text{*}}}{\overline{M}})}^{2}=1$

By the definition of $\text{feq}$ , one may write:

${(\frac{{N}_{x}}{\text{feq}\overline{N}})}^{2}+{(\frac{{M}_{y}}{\text{feq}\overline{M}})}^{2}=1$

that is, in terms of the reduced forces $n$ and $m$ :

${(\frac{n}{\text{feq}})}^{2}+{(\frac{m}{\text{feq}})}^{2}=1$

whence $\begin{array}{c}\text{feq}=\sqrt{{n}^{2}+{m}^{2}}\end{array}$

The loading surface $F$ , homothetic to the limit surface, is then defined by:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{cc}F:& \text{feq}-R(p)=0\\ & \text{où}R(p)={h}^{-1}(p)\end{array}\end{array}$

For a formalism similar to that of plasticity with isotropic hardening [bib2], one obtains the continuous constitutive relation expressed in reduced quantities:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{}\underline{\stackrel{.}{d}}=\stackrel{.}{p}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \underline{f}}=\stackrel{.}{p}\frac{\underline{f}}{\text{feq}}\\ \stackrel{.}{p}=0\text{si}\text{feq}-R(p)<0\\ \stackrel{.}{p}=h'(\text{feq})\stackrel{.}{f}\text{eq}\text{si}\text{feq}-R(p)=0\end{array}\end{array}$

The rigid–plastic constitutive relation without elasticity is finally written:

$\begin{array}{}\underline{\stackrel{.}{D}}=\frac{\stackrel{.}{p}}{\text{feq}}\left[\overline{D}\right]{\left[\overline{F}\right]}^{-1}\underline{F}\\ \text{où}\underline{D}=(\begin{array}{}U\\ \theta \end{array})\mathrm{et}\underline{F}=(\begin{array}{}{N}_{x}\\ {M}_{y}\end{array})\\ \left[\overline{D}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\overline{U}& 0\\ 0& \overline{\theta}\end{array}\right]\mathrm{et}\left[\overline{F}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\overline{N}& 0\\ 0& \overline{M}\end{array}\right]\end{array}$

The incremental constitutive relation in reduced quantities is obtained by integration of the continuous relation between $t$ (variables -) and $\text{t+ dt}$ (variables +).

Under loading, $\mathrm{\Delta \; p}$ satisfies $F$ = 0 at $t$ + $\text{dt}$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{\text{feq}}^{+}=R({p}^{-}+\mathrm{\Delta \; p})\end{array}$                             eq 2.2-1

Introducing the constitutive relation,

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta \underline{d}=\mathrm{\Delta \; p}\frac{{\underline{f}}^{+}}{{\text{feq}}^{+}}\end{array}$                             eq 2.2-2

one deduces the value of $\mathrm{\Delta \; p}$ ,

$\begin{array}{c}\mathrm{\Delta \; p}=\parallel \Delta \underline{d}.\Delta \underline{d}\parallel =\sqrt{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}^{2}+{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}^{2}}\end{array}$

and one computes the value of ${\text{feq}}^{+}$ by [eq 2.2-1]. The constitutive relation [eq 2.2-2] gives the reduced forces:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{}{n}^{+}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}R({p}^{-}+\mathrm{\Delta \; p})\\ {m}^{+}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}R({p}^{-}+\mathrm{\Delta \; p})\end{array}\end{array}$

Under unloading, $\mathrm{\Delta \; p}$ = 0 and one has, by [eq 2.2-2]:

$\Delta \underline{d}=0$

## 4 Implementation in Code_Aster

The constitutive relation ASSE_CORN is assigned to discrete elements of modelling DIS_TR with 2 coincident nodes. This relation is called by the operators for the solution of non-linear problems STAT_NON_LINE [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) or DYNA_NON_LINE [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05).

The local axes of these elements x, y, z are defined as in [Figure 2-a].

The integration of this constitutive relation of the assemblies in the operator STAT_NON_LINE of *Code_Aster*  requires the formulation of the tangent operators ${K}_{o}$ and ${K}_{n}$ [bib3].

1. ${K}_{o}$ is the tangent stiffness at the beginning of the time step, time instant $t$ .
2. ${K}_{n}$ is the tangent stiffness at the end of the time step, time instant $\text{t+dt}$ .

The illustration of the operators ${K}_{o}$ and ${K}_{n}$ is given by [Figure 4-a].

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/10000000000006A20000049CD260E8EAE8F088A3.png)

**Figure 4-a: definition of the operators** ${K}_{o}$ **and** ${K}_{n}$

### 4.1 Formulation in reduced quantities under loading

#### 4.1.1 Operator ${K}_{\mathit{nr}}$

It was seen in [§3.2] that the constitutive relation is written:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{}{\underline{f}}^{+}=\frac{\Delta \underline{d}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}R({p}^{-}+\mathrm{\Delta \; p})\\ \text{avec}\mathrm{\Delta \; p}=\parallel \Delta \underline{d}.\Delta \underline{d}\parallel =\sqrt{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}^{2}+{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}^{2}}\end{array}\end{array}$

The operator ${K}_{\text{nr}}$ is defined by:

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{\text{nr}}=\left[\frac{\partial {f}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{j}}\right]1\le i,j\le 2\end{array}$

It is written:

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{\text{nr}}=\frac{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}\left[\text{Id}\right]-\left\lbrace \Delta \underline{d}\right\rbrace .<\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \; p}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \; d}}_{j}}{>}^{+}}{{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}^{2}}R({p}^{+})+\left\lbrace \Delta \underline{d}\right\rbrace .<\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \; p}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \; d}}_{j}}{>}^{+}\frac{R'({p}^{+})}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}\\ \end{array}$

The calculation then gives:

$\begin{array}{}\text{<}\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \; p}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \; d}}_{j}}{>}^{+}=(\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}};\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}})\\ \left\lbrace \Delta \underline{d}\right\rbrace .<\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \; p}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \; d}}_{j}}{>}^{+}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}^{2}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}& \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}\\ \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; U}}_{r}{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}& \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \; \theta }}_{r}^{2}}{\mathrm{\Delta \; p}}\end{array}\right]\end{array}$

and with $\underline{a=1}$ (the only case treated at present), one has: $h(x)=\frac{1}{\overline{d}}\frac{{x}^{2}}{1-x}$

$\begin{array}{}R(p)={h}^{\text{- 1}}(p)=\frac{1}{2}(-\overline{d}p+\sqrt{{\overline{d}}^{2}{p}^{2}+4\overline{d}p})\\ R'(p)=\frac{1}{h'\left[R(p)\right]}=\frac{\overline{d}{\left[1-R(p)\right]}^{2}}{R(p)\left[2-R(p)\right]}\end{array}$

#### 4.1.2 Operator ${K}_{\text{or}}$

For elastoplastic behaviours, the operator ${K}_{o}$ at $t=0$ is equal to the stiffness of the elastic structure. In the present case, the initial tangent behaviour is rigid. The operator ${K}_{\text{or}}$ is then defined by the limit, as $p$ tends to 0, of the operator ${K}_{\text{nr}}$ . One obtains:

$\begin{array}{}R'(p)\underset{p\to 0}{=}\frac{R(p)}{p}\\ \text{d'où}{K}_{\text{or}}\underset{p\to 0}{=}\frac{R(p)}{p}\left[\text{Id}\right]\end{array}$

Now $R(p)<1\forall p$ and if one assumes that the user gives, for the first loading step, values such that $\mathrm{\Delta \; p}>{10}^{-4}$ , one may in practice retain:

${K}_{{\text{or}}_{t=0}}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{10}^{4}& 0\\ 0& {10}^{4}\end{array}\right]$

> This default value can be modified (see §5).

These remarks are illustrated by [Figure 4.1.2-a].

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/10000000000005B70000043EA22806FAEAC026BA.png)

**Figure 4.1.2-a: operator Ko at t = 0**

At the current time instant $t$ , the operator ${K}_{\text{or}}$ is equal to the operator ${K}_{\text{nr}}$ of the previous step defined in [§4.1.1].

### 4.2 Formulation in reduced quantities under unloading

In order to avoid numerical problems, the (rigid) behaviour under unloading is described by:

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{\text{or}}={K}_{\text{nr}}={K}_{{\text{or}}_{t=0}}\end{array}$

### 4.3 Tangent operators ${K}_{n}$ and ${K}_{o}$

The tangent operator ${K}_{n}$ is written, from the reduced tangent operator computed in §4.1.

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{n}=\left[\frac{\partial {F}_{i}}{\partial {D}_{j}}\right]1\le i,j\le 6\end{array}$

with

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial {F}_{1}}{\partial {D}_{1}}=\frac{\partial {N}_{x}}{\partial U}=\frac{\partial n}{\partial {U}_{r}}\times \frac{\overline{N}}{\overline{U}}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{1}}{\partial {D}_{5}}=\frac{\partial {N}_{x}}{\partial \theta }=\frac{\partial n}{\partial {\theta}_{r}}\times \frac{\overline{N}}{\overline{\theta}}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{5}}{\partial {D}_{1}}=\frac{\partial {M}_{y}}{\partial U}=\frac{\partial m}{\partial {U}_{r}}\times \frac{\overline{M}}{\overline{U}}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{5}}{\partial {D}_{5}}=\frac{\partial {M}_{y}}{\partial \theta }=\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\theta}_{r}}\times \frac{\overline{M}}{\overline{\theta}}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{2}}{\partial {D}_{2}}={K}_{y}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{3}}{\partial {D}_{3}}={K}_{z}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{4}}{\partial {D}_{4}}={\text{KR}}_{x}\\ \frac{\partial {F}_{6}}{\partial {D}_{6}}={\text{KR}}_{z}\end{array}$

The other values are zero.

The tangent operator ${K}_{o}$ , at $t$ = 0, is written:

${K}_{o}=\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}{10}^{4}\frac{\overline{N}}{\overline{U}}& & & & O& \\ & {K}_{y}& & & & \\ & & {K}_{z}& & & \\ & & & {K}_{\text{Rx}}& & \\ O& & & & {10}^{4}\frac{\overline{M}}{\overline{\theta}}& \\ & & & & & {K}_{\text{Rz}}\end{array}\right]$

### 4.4 Numerical treatment of the junction between the mechanisms of the assembly law

During the solution of each loading step by the iterative Newton method, one must compute at each iteration the tangent to the force–displacement equilibrium curve of the constitutive law. The problem is that the junction between the mechanisms of the assembly law, on the constitutive law, presents a positive concavity (cf. [Figure 2-b]) which makes convergence problematic when, during a loading step, one passes from one mechanism to the other.

In the subroutine TE0041, which computes, for each load increment, the elementary tangent stiffness matrix of a discrete finite element with 2 nodes possessing degrees of freedom in translation and in rotation, it proved necessary, in order to converge, to compute a secant stiffness directed from the initial state of zero force and displacement towards the state, at the end of the loading step, consisting of the imposed force and the corresponding displacement on the equilibrium curve of the constitutive law. For that purpose it was necessary, which was unusual at the level of this option, to know the internal iteration number of the numerical procedure computing the loading step, and then to estimate the force imposed on the element at the end of this step.

Indeed, if one denotes by ${F}^{+}$ the force imposed at the level of an element (unknown a priori, since only the assembled forces are known), by ${U}^{+}$ the corresponding displacement on the equilibrium curve, and, for iteration $i$ , the respective values $U(i),F(i),Ks(i)$ of the displacement, of the force and of the secant matrix — standing in for the tangent matrix — computed at the end of the iteration, one knows on entry to the aforementioned subroutine only ${U}^{i}$ , and the values at the beginning of the loading step $F(0)$ and $U(0)$ , because the values at the previous iteration $i-1$ have not been stored. In the expression for the residual computed at the end of iteration $i-1$ : ${F}^{+}-F(i-1)={K}_{s}(i-1).(U(i)-U(i-1))$ , one therefore no longer knows anything but $U(i)$ at iteration $i$ , except in the particular case $i=1$ where one has:

${F}^{+}-F(0)={K}_{s}(0).(U(1)-U(0))$

${F}^{+}$ is there the only value unknown at the outset, and it is deduced from the others. One likewise deduces the displacement ${U}^{+}$ at the end of the step from the equilibrium relation:

$\stackrel{.}{p}.\left[n,m\right]=R(p).\left[{\stackrel{.}{U}}_{r},{\stackrel{.}{\Theta}}_{r}\right]$ , whence the secant stiffness ${K}_{s}(1)={F}^{+}/{U}^{+}$ .

The problem is that in this first iteration, the imposed displacement $U(1)$ is different from the final displacement to be computed ${U}^{+}$ in equilibrium with ${F}^{+}$ , which is henceforth known (to within the equilibrium test of the previous loading step). The force computed at the end of this iteration $F(1)$ must therefore likewise be different from ${F}^{+}$ and such that $F(1)={K}_{s}(1).U(1)$ , so that, starting from the pair $U(1)$ and $F(1)$ , one points with the secant ${K}_{s}(1)$ at the pair ${U}^{+}$ and ${F}^{+}$ . One thus obtains at the beginning of iteration 2 a displacement $U(2)$ very close to ${U}^{+}$ , and one can then compute by the equilibrium relation $F(2)$ , likewise very close to ${F}^{+}$ , as well as the secant stiffness ${K}_{s}(2)=F(2)/U(2)$ .

If one has converged exactly at the previous loading step, 2 internal iterations are sufficient to converge exactly; otherwise a few additional iterations are required in order to satisfy the equilibrium test on the residual.

The so-called “directed secant” method is shown schematically in [Figure 4.3-a], where one has the following correspondences:

$\begin{array}{}{U}^{i}=U(i)\\ {K}_{t}({U}^{i})={K}_{s}(i)\end{array}$

for a constitutive law $\text{LC}({U}^{i})=F(i)$ .

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.32/Object_192.svg)

**Figure 4.3-a: directed secant method**

One therefore now sees why, in the option computed by the aforementioned subroutine, it was necessary to know the internal iteration number $i$ in order to distinguish the particular case $i=1$ .

Variables and parameters of the constitutive law

### 4.5 Variables of the law

The constitutive law comprises 7 internal variables per computation point:

1. $\mathit{V1}$ is the maximum equivalent reduced displacement *p*  reached in mechanism 1,
2. $\mathit{V2}$ is the maximum equivalent reduced displacement *p* reached in mechanism 2,
3. $\mathit{V3}$ is an indicator equal to 1 or 2 according as one is respectively on the limit surface of mechanism 1 or 2, and 0 if one is below this limit surface (after unloading, for example),
4. $\mathit{V4}$ and $\mathit{V5}$ are respectively the maximum force and moment reached in mechanism 2 before unloading,
5. $\mathit{V6}$ and $\mathit{V7}$ are respectively the displacement and the rotation at the origin of mechanism 1, which may be non-zero when, after unloading in mechanism 2, the loading changes sign in order to return to mechanism 1.

### 4.6 Parameters of the law

The parameters of the constitutive law are entered as data under the keyword ASSE_CORN of the command DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01):

- NU_1    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{N}}_{1}$ of mechanism 1,
- MU_1    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{M}}_{1}$ of mechanism 1,
- DXU_1    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{U}}_{1}$ of mechanism 1,
- DRYU_1    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{\theta}}_{1}$ of mechanism 1,
- C_1        : following this keyword one enters the value common to the parameters $\bar{n}$ and $\bar{m}$ of mechanism 1,
- NU_2    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{N}}_{2}$ of mechanism 2,
- MU_2    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{M}}_{2}$ of mechanism 2,
- DXU_2    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{U}}_{2}$ of mechanism 2,
- DRYU_2    : following this keyword one enters the value of the parameter ${\overline{\theta}}_{2}$ of mechanism 2,
- C_2        : following this keyword one enters the value common to the parameters $\bar{n}$ and $\bar{m}$ of mechanism 2,
- KY, KZ, KRX, KRZ    take the values of the linear behaviour characteristics in the local directions *«* respectively,
- RP_0    : following this keyword one enters a possible value of ${K}_{\text{or}}$ ($10^{4}$ by default).

*Note* **:** The parameter $a$ is not accessible to the user. It is fixed at the value $a=1$ .
