# v6.03.140 SSNP140 - Tensile test on an elastoplastic hole plane by the IMPLEX method

**Summary:**

This test case is used to validate the implementation of the IMPLEX resolution method. The test case studied is a tensile test of a flat holed test piece. The solution obtained with the IMPLEX method is very similar to that obtained with the Newton-Raphson method.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The geometry of the test case studied is a rectangular plate with a hole in its centre subject to traction.

For obvious symmetry reasons, only one quarter of the plate is studied.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.140/Object_2.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Problem studied**

The objective is to test the different constitutive laws, so the different modellings use different constitutive laws.

In the case of modellings $A$ , $B$ and $E$ , the material that forms the plate is modelled by an elastoplastic constitutive law. ### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

They are identical for the 5 modellings.

To recreate symmetry conditions, movements are blocked:

- following $y$ on $\mathrm{AB}$ ,
- next $x$ on $\mathrm{DE}$ .

The load is defined by imposing a displacement of $0,3\mathrm{mm}$ along the $y$ axis at the $\mathrm{DC}$ border.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

At the moment $0$ , the system is at equilibrium and does not undergo any prestress.

## 2 Reference solutions

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is calculated with Code_Aster using the "classical" algorithm of Newton-Raphson.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The reference result is the force curve – displacement obtained with the iterative method of Newton-Raphson. The force values at different loading levels are compared.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

Comparison of two solutions obtained with Code_Aster. As the IMPLEX method is by definition approached, one will have a difference to the Newton solution (but which must remain weak).

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

  1. OLIVER, A.E. HUESPE and J.C. CANTE "An Implicit/Explicit scheme to increase compatibility of non-linear material and contact/friction problems", Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, vol. 197, 2008.

## 9 Summary of the results

The results of the IMPLEX simulations are very similar to those obtained with the Newton-Raphson method, with the 4 constitutive laws and the time step automatic management method. As indicated in [1], the only difference observed is the appearance of overstress when plasticity is initiated. This phenomenon disappears during refinement of temporal discretization.
