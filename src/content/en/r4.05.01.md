# r4.05.01 Seismic response by transient analysis

**Summary**

The methods most frequently used for the seismic analysis of structures are the spectral methods and the transient methods.

The transient methods (direct linear or not, by modal synthesis) make it possible to compute the response of structures under the effect of imposed earthquakes: single excitation (identical at each of the anchor points of the structure) or multiple, and to take into account their possible non-linear behaviour.

As regards the spectral methods, one computes the maximum response, for each vibration mode, of each anchor point. The maximum response of the structure as a whole, on average, in the statistical sense, is then determined by combination of the maximum responses of the modes. This type of analysis is set out in the reference documentation [R4.05.03](doc:r4.05.03).

**Contents**

## 1 Seismic behaviour of a structure

The analysis of the seismic behaviour of a structure consists in studying its response to an imposed motion: an acceleration, at its various supports. The imposed acceleration is a time signal $\gamma (t)$ called an accelerogram (cf. [Figure 1.1-a]).

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.01/10000200000003A60000029AC5E16151C961C123.png)

**Figure** **1.1-a:** Accelerogram LBNS

The seismic motion considered in the analysis is either a known real accelerogram read by the operator LIRE_FONCTION [U4.32.02](doc:u4.32.02) or a synthetic accelerogram computed directly in the code, for example with the procedure FORMULE [U4.31.05](doc:u4.31.05).

## 2 Seismic response of a single-degree-of-freedom system

Let there be a simple oscillator constituted of a mass $m$ connected to a fixed point by a spring $k$ and a damper $c$ able to move in a single direction $x$ (cf. [Figure 2-a]). This single-degree-of-freedom oscillator is subjected to a horizontal accelerogram $\gamma (t)$ at its support (point $A$ ).

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.01/100002000000021400000172595D7A071753BA36.png)

**Figure** **2-a**  : simple oscillator subjected to a seismic loading.

The displacements of the oscillator are measured or computed, either in a relative frame attached to the point $A$ : relative displacement ${x}_{r}$ , or in an absolute frame $({R}_{a})$ : absolute displacement ${x}_{a}$ . The absolute displacement ${x}_{a}$ is decomposed into a uniform translational entrainment displacement ${x}_{e}$ and a relative displacement ${x}_{r}$ :

${x}_{a}(t)={x}_{r}(t)+{x}_{e}(t)$                                 eq 2-1

One deduces from this by differentiation the relation between the accelerations:

${\ddot{x}}_{a}(t)={\ddot{x}}_{r}(t)+\gamma (t)$ with $\gamma (t)={\ddot{x}}_{e}(t)$                             eq 2-2

The mass is subjected to a horizontal restoring force which is proportional to the relative displacement: ${F}_{r}=-k.{x}_{r}$ and to a horizontal damping force assumed proportional to the relative velocity: ${F}_{v}=-c.{\dot{x}}_{r}$ .

The equation of motion of the mass is then written: $-k.{x}_{r}-c.{\dot{x}}_{r}=m.{\ddot{x}}_{a}$ .

That is, taking into account equations [eq 2-1] and [eq 2-2]:

$m.{\ddot{x}}_{r}+c.{\dot{x}}_{r}+k.{x}_{r}=-m.\gamma (t)=p(t)$                     eq 2-3

**Note:**

*The study of the seismic response of a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator in the relative frame therefore consists in the study of the response of an oscillator to a force* $p(t)$ of arbitrary form. The solution of the equation of motion [eq 2-3] is then supplied by the Duhamel integral:

> ${x}_{r}(t)=\frac{1}{m.{\omega}_{D}}{\int}_{0}^{t}p(\tau ).{e}^{-\xi .\omega .(t-\tau )}.\sin\left[{\omega}_{D}(t-\tau )\right].d\tau$

*for initial conditions at rest, with:*

> $\begin{array}{}p(t)=-m.\gamma (t)\\ \omega =\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}},\xi =\frac{c}{2.m.\omega }\text{et}{\omega}_{D}=\omega .\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}\end{array}$

## 3 Seismic response of a multi-degree-of-freedom system

### 3.1 Equations of motion in the absolute frame

The equilibrium of a mechanical system consists in writing, whatever the computation instant $t$ considered, that the sum of the internal, inertia and damping forces is equal to the external forces imposed on the said system:

${F}_{\text{iner}}(t)+{F}_{\text{amo}}(t)+{F}_{int}(t)={F}_{\text{ext}}(t)$

In the case of a linear behaviour, if the system is represented by a model of finite elements or of discrete elements, one has (after spatial discretisation):

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{\text{iner}}=M{\ddot{X}}_{\mathrm{ab}}\\ {F}_{int}=K{X}_{\mathrm{ab}}\end{array}$

- ${X}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ is the vector of the nodal displacements of the discretised structure, in the absolute frame;
- $M$ is the mass matrix of the structure;
- $K$ is the stiffness matrix of the structure;
- ${F}_{\text{ext}}={F}_{e}-{F}_{c}$ is the vector of the forces imposed on the structure under study, ${F}_{c}$ that of the possible shock forces (cf. [R5.06.03](doc:r5.06.03)).

One calls "support" the edges or boundaries of the structure on which the imposed (generalised) displacements are described by given functions of time (and of space); two distinct supports are therefore distinguished by distinct functions describing the imposed displacements. To simplify the remainder of the presentation, one considers that the structure is loaded only by displacements imposed at its various supports. Thus, ${F}_{e}=0$ .

With the aim of simplifying the presentation, one generally separates the degrees of freedom into two families, according to their type, cf.[R5.05.05, §2.6.2]:

- the structural degrees of freedom not subjected to an imposed motion - also called "active" degrees of freedom - these are the unknowns of the problem;
- the structural degrees of freedom subjected to an imposed motion - also called "constrained" degrees of freedom - these are the displacement boundary conditions of the problem (Dirichlet boundary conditions: DDL_IMPO).

On the supports of the structure where the displacements ${X}_{s}$ are imposed, one has: ${BX}_{\mathrm{ab}}={X}_{s}$ . $B$ is the transformation matrix from all the degrees of freedom of the structure to the structural degrees of freedom subjected to an imposed motion.

The equilibrium of the system is then written, whatever $v$ belonging to the space of the kinematically admissible displacements, but free at the supports, denoting by ${\lambda}_{s}$ the "LAGRANGE multipliers" of the conditions ${BX}_{\mathrm{ab}}={X}_{s}$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\langle M.{\ddot{X}}_{\mathrm{ab}}+{F}_{\text{amo}}+K.{X}_{\mathrm{ab}}+{B}^{T}.\lambda -{F}_{\text{ext}},v\rangle =0\\ \langle B.{X}_{\mathrm{ab}}-{X}_{s},v\rangle =0\end{array}$

That is:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}M.{\ddot{X}}_{\mathrm{ab}}+{F}_{\text{amo}}+K.{X}_{\mathrm{ab}}={F}_{\text{ext}}-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{s}\\ B.{X}_{\mathrm{ab}}={X}_{s}\end{array}$                         eq 3.1-1

${F}_{s}=-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{s}$ is the vector of the reaction forces exerted by the supports on the structure. One admits that the vector of the damping forces satisfies ${B}^{T}.{F}_{\text{amo}}=0$ .

Taking into account the partition of the degrees of freedom into "active" degrees of freedom and "constrained" degrees of freedom, the displacement vector in the absolute frame is written: ${X}_{\mathrm{ab}}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{a}\\ {x}_{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ .

The operators describing the structure become: $M=\left[\begin{array}{cc}m& {m}_{\text{xs}}\\ {m}_{\text{sx}}& {m}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right]$ , $K=\left[\begin{array}{cc}k& {k}_{\text{xs}}\\ {k}_{\text{sx}}& {k}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right]$ with ${m}_{\text{sx}}={m}_{\text{xs}}^{T}$ and ${k}_{\text{sx}}={k}_{\text{xs}}^{T}$ and the vector of the external forces (here of shock only) applied to the structure is written: ${F}_{\text{ext}}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-{f}_{c}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace$ .

The fundamental equation of dynamics in the absolute frame is then written, taking into account the partition of the degrees of freedom:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}m& {m}_{\text{xs}}\\ {m}_{\text{sx}}& {m}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{x}}_{a}\\ {\ddot{x}}_{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace +{F}_{\text{amo}}+\left[\begin{array}{cc}k& {k}_{\text{xs}}\\ {k}_{\text{sx}}& {k}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{a}\\ {x}_{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-{f}_{c}\\ {f}_{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

That is, considering only the active degrees of freedom:

$m{\ddot{x}}_{a}+{F}_{\text{amo}}+k{x}_{a}=-{f}_{c}-{m}_{\text{xs}}{\ddot{x}}_{s}-{k}_{\text{xs}}{x}_{s}$

This approach requires knowledge of the absolute displacements and velocities associated with the accelerogram $\gamma (t)$ of each support; now the recorders measure either accelerations or velocities. One can recover the displacements by single or double integration with the command CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04). However, the integration and the measurement uncertainties give drifts which it is appropriate to correct: the displacements are therefore less well known than the velocities and accelerations. One will bear in mind the following orders of magnitude of the maximum amplitudes:

- a few tenths of "$g$" for the accelerations;
- a few tens of $\mathit{cm}/s$ for the velocities;
- a few tens of $\mathit{cm}$ for the displacements.

One will also ensure that at the end of the earthquake the velocity and the displacement are realistic, that is to say at most a few tens of $\mathit{cm}$ for the displacement, zero for the velocity. One can also correct an accelerogram (seismic signal in acceleration) so that the displacement signal has no drift: keyword CORR_ACCE of the command CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04).

### 3.2 Equations of motion in the relative frame

#### 3.2.1 Decomposition of the absolute motion

The loadings undergone by a structure during an earthquake are classified into two types in the construction rules (ASME, RCC-M):

- the stresses induced by the relative motion of the structure with respect to its static deformed shape, or **primary stresses** . These loadings are due to the inertial effects of the earthquake;
- the stresses induced by the differential displacements of the anchorages, or **secondary stresses** .

Most often, one therefore decomposes the study of structures with linear behaviour into the study of the static deformed shape due to the motions of the supports (this is the entrainment motion) and into the study of the vibrations induced by the accelerations of the supports about this deformed shape (this is the "relative" motion).

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.01/100002000000034E0000017127F1393F45AE8168.png)

**Figure**  3.2.1 **-a**  : decomposition of the motion.

The absolute displacement of any point $M$ of the structure, not subjected to an imposed displacement, is equal to the sum of the "relative" displacement and of the displacement of this point caused by the entrainment motion of the supports:

${X}_{\mathrm{ab}}(M,t)={X}_{r}(M,t)+{X}_{e}(M,t)$                             eq 3.2.1-1

That is:

- ${X}_{\mathrm{ab}}$ , the vector of the displacements in the absolute frame;
- ${X}_{r}$ , the vector of the relative displacements defined as the vector of the displacements of the structure with respect to the deformed shape which it would have under the static action of the displacements imposed at the supports. ${X}_{r}$ is therefore zero at the support points: $B.{X}_{r}=0$ ;
- ${X}_{e}$ , the vector of the entrainment displacements defined as the displacements of the structure loaded statically by the imposed displacements of the supports:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}B.{X}_{e}={X}_{s}\\ K.{X}_{e}=-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{e}\end{array}$     with    $\lambda ={\lambda}_{r}+{\lambda}_{e}$ $\iff {X}_{e}=\Psi .{X}_{s}$

- $\Psi$  is the matrix of the static modes. The static modes represent, in the absence of external forces, the linear static response of the structure to a unit displacement imposed on each connection degree of freedom (the others being fixed); they satisfy:

$\langle K.{\Psi}_{k},v\rangle =0$ whatever $v$ such that $B.v=0$

In particular, $\langle K.{\Psi}_{k},{\Phi}_{j}\rangle =0$ , whatever the vibratory mode ${\Phi}_{j}$ on fixed supports considered.

#### 3.2.2 Single or multiple excitation

To set out in more detail the approach in relative motion, and more particularly the computation of the entrainment components, for example under earthquake, it is necessary to introduce the notion of single or multiple excitation.

##### 3.2.2.1 Single excitation: "single-support" case

One considers that the imposed entrainment motion is a rigid-body motion. One generally says that **the structure is single-supported** .

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.01/10000200000000FD00000160EC74BD54441D7DA3.png)

**Figure**  3.2.2 **-a**  : "single-support" case.

The **absolute displacement**  of any point $M$ of the structure, not subjected to an imposed displacement, is therefore decomposed into a **relative displacement with respect to a moving frame attached to the support**  where the seismic motion is imposed and into a **rigid entrainment displacement** .

In this case, the static modes correspond to the six rigid-body modes. As the structure is linear elastic, one studies separately the effects of the six components of the seismic motion. For each seismic direction, one writes the entrainment inertia forces induced by the seismic accelerogram simply in the following form:

$P(t)=-M.\Psi .{\ddot{X}}_{s}(t)=-\gamma (t).M.\Delta$

- $\gamma (t)$ is the accelerogram of the seismic motion in one direction;
- $\Delta$ is the rigid-body mode in this direction and unitary on the support considered;
- Seismographs measure only translational signals. Considering that the structure under study is single-supported amounts to assuming that all its supports undergo the same translation. In this case, the components of $\left[\Delta \right]$ are equal to 1 for the degrees of freedom which correspond to displacements in the seismic direction considered and to 0 for the degrees of freedom which correspond to displacements in seismic directions perpendicular to the one considered or to rotations.
- However, given the size of the models, the complete seismic analysis of a superstructure is generally carried out in several stages. The detailed seismic analysis of the superstructure considered then uses as excitations the accelerations computed on its supporting structure. They are composed of the six translational and rotational accelerograms. One therefore computes the three modes corresponding to the imposed translational displacements and the three modes corresponding to the imposed rotational displacements. If the entrainment motion is an imposed rotation $\overrightarrow{\Omega}$ , of axis passing through a point $O$ , the translational displacements at a point $M$ are: $\overrightarrow{{\Delta}_{M}}=\overrightarrow{\mathrm{MO}}\wedge \overrightarrow{\Omega}$ for the degrees of freedom which correspond to translational displacements, and the degrees of freedom which correspond to rotations are equal to $\Omega$ on the axis considered.

##### 3.2.2.2 Multiple excitation: "multi-support" case

One cannot always consider that:

- the entrainment accelerations undergone by all the supports of the structure under study are identical and in phase;
- the supports are undeformable and animated by one and the same rigid-body motion.

In this case, one says that **the structure is multi-supported** . The static modes $\Psi =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Psi \\ \text{Id}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ then correspond to the ${\mathrm{6.nb}}_{\mathit{supports}}$ static modes (case of a beam or plate model) or ${\mathrm{3.nb}}_{\mathit{supports}}$ modes (case of a three-dimensional continuum) where ${\mathit{nb}}_{\mathit{supports}}$ is the number of different accelerograms undergone simultaneously by the structure. They are computed by the operator MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14) with the option DDL_IMPO. They are the solution of the following equation:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Psi {X}_{s}={X}_{e}\\ K{X}_{e}=-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{e}\end{array}$     that is    $\left[\begin{array}{cc}k& {k}_{\text{xs}}\\ {k}_{\text{sx}}& {k}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Psi \\ \text{Id}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\\ {f}_{se}\end{array}\right\rbrace$             eq 3.2.2.2-1

That is, considering only the active degrees of freedom: $k.\Psi +{k}_{\text{xs}}.\text{Id}=0$ .

The inertia forces induced by the multi-support excitation are then written simply:

$P(t)=-\sum_{m=1}^{\text{nb\_supports}}M.{\Psi}_{m}.{\ddot{X}}_{{s}_{m}}(t)$

#### 3.2.3 Modelling of the damping

One considers that the damping dissipated by the structure is of viscous type and that the damping force is proportional to the relative velocity alone of the structure:

${F}_{\text{amo}}=C.{\dot{X}}_{r}$

where $C$ is the damping matrix of the structure.

This amounts to neglecting the effect of the imposed velocity. Indeed, one can write more generally:

${F}_{\text{amo}}=C.{\dot{X}}_{\mathrm{ab}}=C.{\dot{X}}_{r}+C.\Psi .{\dot{X}}_{s}$

In the case of a uniform excitation at the base (single-support case), the damping intervenes only on the relative displacements (the damping forces are effectively zero for a rigid-body motion). In the case of a multiple excitation (multi-support case) where the static solution is no longer a rigid-body motion, considering that the damping force is proportional to the relative velocity of the structure is a simplifying assumption.

#### 3.2.4 Fundamental equation of dynamics

The fundamental equation of dynamics [eq 3.1-1], in the **relative frame** , is then written, taking into account equations [eq 3.2.1-1] and [eq 3.2.2.2-1], and admitting that the damping force is proportional to the relative velocity alone of the structure:

$M.{\ddot{X}}_{r}+C{\dot{X}}_{r}+K.{X}_{r}=-M.\Psi .{\ddot{X}}_{s}+{F}_{\text{ext}}-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{r}$             eq 3.2.4-1

That is, partitioning the degrees of freedom:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}m& {m}_{\text{xs}}\\ {m}_{\text{sx}}& {m}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{x}}_{r}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}c& {c}_{\text{xs}}\\ {c}_{\text{sx}}& {c}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{x}}_{r}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}k& {k}_{\text{xs}}\\ {k}_{\text{sx}}& {k}_{\text{ss}}\end{array}\right].\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{r}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-{f}_{c}\\ {f}_{{s}_{r}}\end{array}\right\rbrace -\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}(m.\Psi +{m}_{\text{xs}}.\text{Id}){\ddot{x}}_{s}\\ ({m}_{\text{sx}}.\Psi +{m}_{\text{ss}}.\text{Id}){\ddot{x}}_{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

with ${c}_{\text{sx}}={c}_{\text{xs}}^{T}$ .

That is, considering only the active degrees of freedom:

$m.{\ddot{x}}_{r}+c.{\dot{x}}_{r}+k.{x}_{r}=-{f}_{c}-(m.\Psi +{m}_{\text{xs}}.\text{Id}){\ddot{x}}_{s}$

The principal advantages of the approach in "relative" motion with respect to that in absolute displacement are the following:

- it is not necessary to integrate the accelerograms $\gamma (t)$ imposed at the supports;
- the relative displacements obtained make it possible to determine directly the primary stresses induced by the earthquake.

### 3.3 Computation of the seismic loading

The seismic loading (cf. [§3.2]) $-M.\Psi$ that is $-(m.\Psi +{m}_{\text{xs}}.\text{Id}){\ddot{x}}_{s}$ on the active degrees of freedom is built by the operator CALC_CHAR_SEISME [U4.63.01](doc:u4.63.01). It can be used directly during a direct transient analysis or a transient analysis by modal synthesis with DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03). By contrast, during a non-linear direct transient analysis with DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01), one must transform it into a concept of load type. This is carried out from the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) in the following way:

```text
char_sei        = CALC_CHAR_SEISME (...)

charge            = AFFE_CHAR_MECA (MODELE=..., VECT_ASSE= char_sei)

dyna_nlin        = DYNA_NON_LINE (
EXCIT=    _F(    CHARGE= con_lim,)
_F(    CHARGE= cham_no,
FONC_MULT= acceler)

...)
```

In the case of a single-supported structure, it is sufficient to indicate the direction of the entrainment motion:

```text
mono_x = CALC_CHAR_SEISME (MATR_ASSE= masse,

DIRECTION(...), MONO_APPUI=’OUI’)
```

In the case of a multi-supported structure, one must beforehand have computed the unit static modes with the operator MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14). One computes as many entrainment loadings as there are supports which undergo a different acceleration.

```text
multi_xi = CALC_CHAR_SEISME (MATR_ASSE= masse, DIRECTION(...),

NOEUD= NOI, MODE_STAT= mode_stat,)
```

### 3.4 Loading of incident-wave type

It is also possible to impose a seismic loading by plane wave by means of the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) and the factor keyword ONDE_PLANE. This corresponds to the loadings usually encountered during computations of dynamic soil-structure interaction by boundary integral equations with the operator CALC_MISS [U7.03.12](doc:u7.03.12).

In harmonic regime, a plane wave in an isotropic elastic medium is characterised by its direction, its angular frequency and its type (wave $P$ for compression waves, waves $\mathit{SV}$ or $\mathit{SH}$ for shear waves). In transient regime, the datum of the angular frequency, corresponding to a harmonic wave stationary in time, must be replaced by the datum of a displacement profile whose propagation over time in the direction of the propagation wave vector $k$ will be taken into account.

More precisely, one characterises:

- a wave $P$ by the function ${u}_{S}(x,t)=f(k.x-{C}_{p}t)k/\parallel k\parallel$
- a wave $S$ by the function ${u}_{S}(x,t)={f}_{S}(k.x-{C}_{s}t)\wedge k/\parallel k\parallel$

with:

- $k/\parallel k\parallel$ , unit vector of direction along the wave vector (one recalls that $\parallel k\parallel =\omega /C$ for a harmonic wave);
- $f$ or { ${f}_{S}$  then represents the profile of the wave given.

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.01/10000200000002D5000001AA7DAFA5C6FBC8B6AD.png)

**Figure**  3.4 **-a**  : incident plane wave.

${H}_{0}$ is the distance from the principal wave front to the origin $O$ , carried by the directing vector of the wave at the initial instant of the analysis, $H$ the distance from the principal wave front to the origin $O$ , at an arbitrary instant.

**Note:**

*This type of load is available in a direct transient analysis, linear* *DYNA_VIBRA or non-linear* *DYNA_NON_LINE.*

The use of this type of loading is detailed in [R4.02.05](doc:r4.02.05), see also [V2.04.120](doc:v2.04.120) and [V2.04.121](doc:v2.04.121).

## 4 Transient seismic response by modal synthesis

### 4.1 Description of the method

The modal recombination method consists in decomposing the relative motion of the structure on the basis of the eigenmodes. As the latter is zero at the supports, one projects the equation of dynamics onto the basis of the blocked eigenmodes (eigenmodes obtained by blocking all the connection degrees of freedom).

${X}_{r}=\Phi .Q$

- $\Phi$ is the matrix of the blocked eigenmodes;
- $Q$ the vector of the generalised unknowns of the relative motion on the basis of the blocked eigenmodes.

The blocked eigenmodes are the solution of:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}(K-{\Omega}_{i}^{2}.M).{\Phi}_{i}=-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{i}\\ B.{\Phi}_{i}=0\end{array}$ where ${\lambda}_{i}$ are the modal reactions at the support points.

The equation of the relative motion projected onto the basis of the blocked dynamic modes is then written (one recalls that for the blocked vibratory modes: ${\Phi}^{T}.K.\Psi =0$ and that one neglects the damping forces associated with the entrainment velocity):

${M}_{G}\ddot{Q}(t)+{C}_{G}\dot{Q}(t)+{K}_{G}Q(t)=-{\Phi}^{T}.M.\Psi .{\ddot{X}}_{s}+{\Phi}^{T}.{F}_{\text{ext}}-{\Phi}^{T}.{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{r}$

where ${M}_{G}$ , ${C}_{G}$ and ${K}_{G}$ are the generalised mass, damping and stiffness matrices. For simplicity, one considers that they are diagonal. The generalised damping matrix ${C}_{G}$ too, because one assumes that Basile's hypothesis is satisfied (the damping matrix is a linear combination of the mass and stiffness matrices).

That is, considering only the active degrees of freedom:

${m}_{G}.\ddot{q}(t)+{c}_{G}.\dot{q}(t)+{k}_{G}.q(t)=-{\Phi}^{T}.{f}_{c}-{\Phi}^{T}.(m.\Psi +{m}_{\text{xs}}.\text{Id}){\ddot{x}}_{s}$

In the absence of shock or other non-linear force, see § 4.3 , and of a given exciting force, one is therefore led to solve a set of decoupled equations (there are as many of them as eigenmodes), whose right-hand side is built directly with the aid of the entrainment acceleration signals alone which are applied on the supports and of the static modes applied to the mass matrix.

**Note:**

*It is possible to compute a modal basis with non-diagonal matrices. It is sufficient to specify this during the construction of the generalised numbering by the keyword* *STOCKAGE=‘PLEIN’ of the command* *NUME_DDL_GENE* [U4.65.03](doc:u4.65.03).

### 4.2 Choice of the modal basis

For the seismic analysis of a linear structure, one should in principle retain all the modes whose natural frequencies are lower than the cut-off frequency (generally of the order of $33\mathit{Hz}$ ). In practice, one often contents oneself with keeping in the modal basis only the modes which contribute significantly to the response. One then keeps only the modes whose unit effective mass in one direction is greater than 1‰ and one also ensures that, for the set of these retained modes, the cumulated unit effective mass in each direction differs little from the total mass of the structure (greater than 90%). The criterion of cumulation of the effective modal masses is reached by chaining the following operators:

- Computation of the total mass of the structure: POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22)

```text
masse_in = POST_ELEM (MASS_INER=_F (TOUT= ‘OUI’))
```

- Computation of the blocked dynamic eigenmodes: they are computed in the operator CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02).

```text
mode = CALC_MODES(...);
```

- Normalisation of the modes with respect to the generalised mass: NORM_MODE [U4.52.11](doc:u4.52.11)

```text
NORM_MODE(MODE= mode, NORME=’MASSE_GENE’, MASSE_INER= masse_in);
```

- Extraction from the modal basis of the modes whose unit effective mass exceeds a certain threshold (1‰ for example) and verification that the extracted modes represent at least 90% of the total mass: EXTR_MODE [U4.52.12](doc:u4.52.12)

```text
EXTR_MODE(
FILTRE_MODE(MODE= mode, CRIT_EXTRE= ‘MASSE_EFFE_UN’, SEUIL=1.e-3)
IMPRESSION(CUMUL=’OUI’);
```

**Notes:**

*The sum of the effective modal masses is in fact equal to the total mass which works on the chosen modal basis. In other words, this total working mass is equal to the total mass minus the mass contributions which are carried by clamped degrees of freedom (which therefore do not work on the modal basis). Thus, for example, on a mass-spring system with 1 degree of freedom with a mass* $\mathit{M1}$ *at the top and another mass* $\mathit{M2}$ *at the level of the basemat, the working mass will be equal to* $\mathit{M1}$ *and the total mass to* $\mathit{M1}+\mathit{M2}$ *. Consequently, the unit effective modal mass for the single mode of the system will be equal to* $\mathit{M1}/(\mathit{M1}+\mathit{M2})$ *. The total cumulation will therefore have the same value and, according to the ratio between* $\mathit{M1}$ *and* $\mathit{M2}$ *, one will therefore not necessarily be able to reach 90% of the total mass* $(\mathit{M1}+\mathit{M2})$ *, even by considering all the modes (one has only a single mode in this example). In practice, the finer and more realistic the finite-element model, the smaller the difference between the working mass and the total mass will be.*

*The macro-command* *CALC_MODES* [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02), option *'BANDE'* with splitting into several sub-bands, makes it possible to chain directly the set of the three preceding commands.*

Caution: certain local responses (in the particular case of localised non-linearities) can be strongly influenced by higher-order modes whose frequency is beyond the cut-off frequency and whose effective modal mass is small (less than 1‰). The keyword VERI_CHOC of the command DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) makes it possible to verify a posteriori that the chosen modal basis is sufficient. If this is not the case, one strongly advises completing it.

### 4.3 Taking non-linear forces into account in the dynamic response of the structure under study by modal synthesis

It is possible to take into account non-linear forces, resulting from shocks, from support buckling, or from anti-seismic supports, cf. [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17), applied at particular nodes, which are evaluated with the aid of the displacements and velocities in the local physical frame reconstituted from the generalised unknowns of the relative motion on the basis of the blocked eigenmodes, to which one adds the entrainment displacements and velocities at the supports, which the user must then supply.

### 4.4 Computation of the dynamic response of the structure under study by modal synthesis

After having computed the basis of the dynamic eigenmodes and built a generalised numbering by NUME_DDL_GENE [U4.65.03](doc:u4.65.03), one then projects the mass, damping and stiffness matrices onto this same basis with the operator PROJ_MATR_BASE [U4.63.12](doc:u4.63.12), the right-hand side vectors with PROJ_VECT_BASE [U4.63.13](doc:u4.63.13).

**Note:**

*The macro-command* *PROJ_BASE* [U4.63.11](doc:u4.63.11) makes it possible to chain directly the set of the three operations.

The matrices and vectors thus projected, one computes the generalised response of the singly or multiply excited system with the aid of the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03).

### 4.5 Taking the neglected modes into account by static correction

The truncation error resulting from taking into account an incomplete modal basis can be corrected by means of a static correction. The static correction can be carried out in two ways:

  - *a priori*  , by constitution of a Ritz basis from the basis of the dynamic eigenmodes and of static modes, whether they are with imposed acceleration (pseudo-mode) or with imposed force;
  - *a posteriori,*  via the pseudo-mode (possible only in single-support).

#### 4.5.1 A priori static correction

The static modes with imposed acceleration (pseudo-mode) or with imposed force are computed beforehand according to:

> MODE_FO=MODE_STATIQUE(     MATR_RIGI=RIGIDITE,
> MATR_MASS=MASSE,

FORCE_NODALE=_F( TOUT = 'OUI', AVEC_CMP = 'DX') )

or

> MODE_CO=MODE_STATIQUE(    MATR_RIGI=RIGIDITE,
> MATR_MASS=MASSE,

> PSEUDO_MODE=_F( NOEUD = ( 'P1', 'P4', ),

AVEC_CMP = ( 'DX', ))

)

> These modes are then adjoined to the dynamic modes in order to constitute a Ritz basis. The adjoined modes may be:

- the static modes with imposed acceleration (pseudo-mode) and the static modes with imposed force:

BAM0=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=(_F(MODE_MECA=mode,),

_F(MODE_INTF=MODE_FO,),

_F(MODE_INTF=MODE_CO,),),

ORTHO='OUI', MATRICE=MASSE',

NUME_REF=N_DDL,);

- or the static modes with imposed force alone:

BAM1=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=(_F(MODE_MECA=mode,),

_F(MODE_INTF=MODE_FO,),

ORTHO='OUI', MATRICE=MASSE',

NUME_REF=N_DDL,);

- or the static modes with imposed acceleration alone:

BAM2=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=(_F(MODE_MECA=mode,),

_F(MODE_INTF=MODE_CO,),

ORTHO='OUI', MATRICE=MASSE',

NUME_REF=N_DDL,);

One insists on the necessity of re-orthogonalising the basis thus obtained, preferably by the mass matrix (the stiffness matrix may also be suitable, but does not make it possible to normalise the eigenvalues).

#### 4.5.2 A posteriori static correction

In this case, once one has returned to the physical basis, one corrects the value of the computed relative displacement (respectively the relative velocity and the relative acceleration) by the contribution of a pseudo-mode. The pseudo-mode is defined by the difference between the static mode associated with the unit loading of imposed uniform acceleration type and the projection onto the computed dynamic modes of the displacement (respectively the relative velocity and the relative acceleration).

One then has:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{X}_{\text{r\_corrigé}}={X}_{r}+\sum_{i}{f}_{i}(t).({\Psi}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\eta}_{j}.{\Phi}_{j})\\ {\dot{X}}_{\text{r\_corrigé}}={\dot{X}}_{r}+\sum_{i}{\dot{f}}_{i}(t).({\Psi}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\dot{\eta}}_{j}.{\Phi}_{j})\\ {\ddot{X}}_{\text{r\_corrigé}}={\ddot{X}}_{r}+\sum_{i}{\ddot{f}}_{i}(t).({\Psi}_{i}-\sum_{j=1}^{p}{\ddot{\eta}}_{j}.{\Phi}_{j})\end{array}$

The multiplicative functions of time ${f}_{i}(t)$ correspond to the imposed accelerogram ${\gamma}_{i}(t)$ in each direction $i$ considered.

The procedure to be followed is the following:

- Computation of the unit loading of imposed-force type (uniform acceleration) in the direction of the earthquake: AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01). One will take care to change the sign of the direction, since the seismic inertia force is of the form $P(t)=-M\Psi .{\ddot{X}}_{s}$

```text
cham_no = AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=modèle, PESANTEUR=(VALE,DIRECTION));
```

- Computation of the linear static response of the structure to the preceding load case: MACRO_ELAS_MULT [U4.51.02](doc:u4.51.02).

```text
mode_cor = MACRO_ELAS_MULT(CHAR_MECA_GLOBAL= con_lim, ...

CAS_CHARGE=_F (NOM_CAS=’xx’, CHAR_MECA= cham_no));
```

One will note that there are as many load cases as seismic directions

- Computation of the first and second derivatives of the accelerogram: CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04).

```text
deri_pre et deri_sec = CALC_FONCTION ( OPTION= DERIVE);
```

- Computation of the generalised response taking into account the neglected modes by static correction:

```text
dyna_mod = DYNA_VIBRA (BASE_CALCUL = "GENE",
                      TYPE_CALCUL= "TRAN",
                      MATR_MASS=... ,
                      MATR_RIGI=...
                      MODE_CORR= mode_cor,
                      EXCIT=_F(CORR_STAT= ‘OUI’,
                             D_FONC_DT= deri_pre,
                             D_FONC_DT2= deri_sec, ..)

...);

*   Retour vers la base physique: la correction statique n'est pas implicitement prise en compte. Il faut préciser CORR_STAT='OUI' dans RECU_FONCTION ou REST_GENE_PHYS pour que la correction statique soit prise en compte.
```

**Note:**

*In the case of a multiply excited structure, taking the neglected modes into account by static correction* a posteriori *is not available. One post-processes the absolute displacement in this case.*

### 4.6 Taking into account the multi-supported character of a structure

One has seen previously (cf. [§3.3]) that in order to compute the seismic loading in the case of a multi-supported structure, one must beforehand have computed the static modes $\Psi$ , which will drive the entrainment inertia forces of the supports, amplified by the entrainment acceleration signals which are applied to them.

If one wishes to be able to recover the quantities (grandeurs) computed in the absolute frame or if one wishes to be able to take into account localised non-linearities (shock, anti-seismic supports...), cf. § 4.3 , one must also specify in DYNA_VIBRA that the structure under study is multiply excited. Indeed, in this latter case, one compares at each instant the vector of the absolute displacements and velocities of each of the shock points considered, in order to determine whether there is a shock and to compute the corresponding shock forces. It is appropriate to ensure that the absolute displacements and velocities of the supports are consistent with the entrainment acceleration signals considered.

The procedure to be followed is the following:

- Computation of the static modes: MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14).

```text
mode_stat = MODE_STATIQUE(DDL_IMPO= (...));
```

- Computation of the generalised response taking into account the entrainment component:

```text
dyna_mod = DYNA_VIBRA (MASS_GENE=... , RIGI_GENE=...

MODE_STAT= mode_stat

EXCIT=_F(MULT_APPUI= ‘OUI’

ACCE= accelero, VITE= vitesse, DEPL= deplace

DIRECTION= (...), NOEUD=NO1

...)

...);
```

### 4.7 Post-processing

The operators REST_GENE_PHYS [U4.63.31](doc:u4.63.31) or RECU_FONCTION [U4.32.03](doc:u4.32.03) can then recover in the physical space the evolutions computed:

- the operator REST_GENE_PHYS recovers globally (the complete field) the displacements, velocities and accelerations;
- the operator RECU_FONCTION recovers locally (time evolution of a degree of freedom) the displacements, velocities and accelerations.

One can recover the relative quantities by specifying (MULT_APPUI= ‘NON’) or the absolute quantities by (MULT_APPUI= ‘OUI’).

One then obtains the entrainment displacements necessary for the computation of the secondary quantities by subtracting the relative displacements from the absolute displacements. This is carried out by the command CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04) option COMB.

From the preceding evolutions, one can also extract the maximum values and $\mathit{RMS}$ and compute the associated oscillator response spectrum. This is carried out by the command CALC_FONCTION options MAX, RMS and SRO.

## 5 Direct transient seismic response

Direct integration can be carried out either with assumptions of linear behaviour: operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) or with assumptions of non-linear behaviour: operator DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01). Apart from the way of taking the seismic loading into account (cf.[§3.3]), the syntaxes of DYNA_NON_LINE and DYNA_VIBRA are identical.

### 5.1 Taking into account a damping equivalent to the modal damping

Generally, the most precise information that one has on the damping comes from vibration tests which make it possible to determine, for a given resonance frequency ${f}_{i}$ , the corresponding resonance width and therefore the reduced damping ${\xi}_{i}$ at this resonance. **It is therefore necessary to be able to take into account, in a direct transient analysis, a damping equivalent to the modal damping** .

From the spectral development of the identity matrix:

$\text{Id}=\sum_{i=1}^{n\text{\_modes}}\frac{{X}_{i}{X}_{i}^{T}K}{{X}_{i}^{T}K{X}_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{n\text{\_modes}}\frac{{X}_{i}{X}_{i}^{T}K}{{M}_{G\text{\_}i}.{\omega}_{i}^{2}}$

one shows:

- that one can expand the damping matrix of the structure $C$ in a series of eigenmodes:

$C=\sum_{i=1}^{n\text{\_modes}}{a}_{i}.(K.{\Phi}_{i}){(K.{\Phi}_{i})}^{T}$

- and that, taking into account the definition of the percentage of critical damping:

${\Phi}_{i}^{T}.C.{\Phi}_{i}=2.{M}_{G\text{\_}i}.{\omega}_{i}.{\xi}_{i}.{a}_{i}=2.\frac{{\xi}_{i}}{{K}_{G\text{\_}i}.{\omega}_{i}}$

It is therefore advised that the user specify (the syntaxes of DYNA_NON_LINE and DYNA_VIBRA are identical) the values of the modal dampings for each natural frequency by means of the factor keyword AMOR_MODAL.

This amounts to imposing a damping force proportional to the relative velocity of the structure:

${F}_{\text{amo}}=C{\dot{X}}_{r}$ with $C=\sum_{i=1}^{n\text{\_modes}}2.\frac{{\xi}_{i}}{{K}_{G\text{\_}i}.{\omega}_{i}}.(K.{\Phi}_{i}){(K.{\Phi}_{i})}^{T}$

### 5.2 Taking into account a multi-support loading with recovery of the relative and absolute fields

By default, the quantities are computed in the relative frame. In DYNA_NON_LINE and DYNA_LINE, one uses a syntax identical to that of DYNA_VIBRA (presence of the keywords MODE_STAT and MULT_APPUI= ‘OUI’) in order to compute them in the absolute frame.

## 6 Soil-structure interaction

The seismic behaviour of a building depends on the characteristics of the soil on which it rests, since it depends on the seismic motion imposed on the soil and on the dynamic behaviour of the building and of its foundations in the soil. Soil-structure interaction most frequently contributes to decreasing the response of the structure under study.

### 6.1 Impedance of a foundation

Let there be a massless rigid shallow foundation, subjected to a harmonic force of angular frequency $\omega$ : $P(t)={P}_{0}.{e}^{\mathrm{iwt}}$ It is therefore animated by a motion $X(t)$ of the same frequency. One calls **impedance of the foundation** , or dynamic stiffness, the complex number $K(\omega )$ , a function of the frequency $\omega$ such that: $K(\omega )=\frac{P(t)}{X(t)}$ .

Several analytical or numerical methods make it possible to compute the impedance of a foundation according to the complexity of the foundation and of the soil on which it rests or in which it is partially embedded. Among the most frequently used, one cites:

- the analytical methods of the WOLF or DELEUZE springs, where one assumes that the basemat is circular, rigid and resting on a homogeneous soil. The foundation must be shallow;
- the numerical method of the CLASSI code, where one assumes that the basemat is of arbitrary shape, rigid and resting on a possibly stratified soil. The foundation must be shallow;
- the numerical method of the MISS3D code, where the basemat may be of arbitrary shape, possibly deformable and resting on a possibly stratified soil.

It is possible to treat the soil-foundation interaction by the **frequency coupling method**  (taking into account the frequency dependence of the impedance matrix) by carrying out a coupled MISS3D / *Code_Aster* computation. This type of computation is not detailed in this reference documentation. One presents here only the most common case, where the soil-foundation interaction is treated by the **soil-spring method**  (one considers that the terms of the impedance matrix are independent of the frequency).

In the case of a rigid shallow foundation, the impedance is computed at the centre of gravity of the surface in contact in a frame attached to the principal axes of inertia of this surface. For each frequency, it is expressed in the form of a matrix of dimension $(6,6)$ . One then adjusts the value of each term as a function of a particular eigenmode of the building under study with fixed base:

- frequency of the first rocking mode ${\omega}_{0}$ for the horizontal stiffnesses ${K}_{x}({\omega}_{0}),{K}_{y}({\omega}_{0})$ and of rotation ${K}_{\mathrm{rx}}({\omega}_{0}),{K}_{\mathrm{ry}}({\omega}_{0})$ ;
- frequency of the first pumping mode ${\omega}_{1}$ for the vertical stiffness ${K}_{z}({\omega}_{1})$ and of torsion ${K}_{\mathrm{rz}}({\omega}_{1})$ .

As the natural frequencies of the building depend on the soil stiffnesses, the computation of the global values of the six soil springs results from an iterative process illustrated in figure [Figure 6.1-a]. The first soil stiffnesses ${K}_{x}({\omega}_{0}),{K}_{y}({\omega}_{0}),{K}_{z}({\omega}_{1}),{K}_{\mathrm{rx}}({\omega}_{0}),{K}_{\mathrm{ry}}({\omega}_{0})$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{rz}}({\omega}_{1})$ are chosen as a function of the first natural frequencies of rocking $({\omega}_{0})$ and of pumping $({\omega}_{1})$ of the structure with fixed base. The soil stiffnesses are then adjusted to the first significant natural frequencies of the structure on springs until correspondence of the frequencies at which the impedance functions are computed with the values of the natural frequencies of the coupled soil-building system.

### 6.2 Taking into account a modal damping computed according to the RCC-G rule

One decomposes the damping due to the soil into a part of material origin and a geometric part: damping due to the reflection of the elastic waves in the soil.

The RCC-G rule consists in summing, for each mode, the dampings of each constitutive substructure of the building considered and the structural and geometric dampings of the soil weighted by their respective rates of potential energy with respect to the total potential energy:

${\eta}_{i}=\frac{\sum_{k}{E}_{\text{ki}}.{\eta}_{k}+\sum_{s}{E}_{\text{si}}.{\eta}_{\text{si}}}{\sum_{k}{E}_{\text{ki}}+\sum_{s}{E}_{\text{si}}}$

with:

- ${\eta}_{i}$ , the mean reduced damping of mode $i$ ;
- ${\eta}_{k}$ , the reduced damping of the $k$ -th element of the structure;
- ${\eta}_{\text{si}}$ , the reduced damping of the soil spring s for mode $i$ ;
- ${E}_{\text{ki}}$ , the potential energy of the $k$ -th element of the structure for mode $i$ ;
- and ${E}_{\text{si}}$ , the potential energy of the soil spring $s$ for mode $i$ .

In the regulations, the modal damping is limited to a maximum value of 0.3.

The part of material origin of the soil damping is computed by weighting the damping of each substructure by the ratio: potential-energy rate over total potential energy. As for the geometric part of the damping, it is computed by distributing the damping values for each direction (three translations and three rotations) weighted by the potential-energy rate in the soil of the direction. The directional damping values are obtained by interpolating, for each natural frequency computed, the directional damping functions coming from a soil-structure interaction code (PARASOL, CLASSI or MISS3D). The ratio of the imaginary part to twice the real part of the impedance matrix: $\frac{\text{Im}(K(\omega ))}{2.\text{Re}(K(\omega ))}$ , supplies the values of this radiative damping.

The procedure to be followed is the following:

- Computation of the potential energy dissipated in the structure under study: POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22)

${E}_{k}$ =POST_ELEM (ENER_POT=_F (TOUT= ‘OUI’));

- Computation of the modal damping by the RCC-G rule: CALC_AMOR_MODAL [U4.52.13](doc:u4.52.13)

l_amor = CALC_AMOR_MODAL (

ENER_SOL =_F (MODE_MECA= base_modale, GROUP_NO_RADIER=... ,

KX= ${K}_{x}({\omega}_{0})$ , KY= ${K}_{y}({\omega}_{0})$ , KZ= ${K}_{z}({\omega}_{1})$ ,

KRX= ${K}_{\mathrm{rx}}({\omega}_{0})$ , KRY= ${K}_{\mathrm{ry}}({\omega}_{0})$ , KRZ: ${K}_{\mathrm{rz}}({\omega}_{1})$ ));

AMOR_INTERNE=_F (GROUP_MA=..., ENER_POT= ${E}_{k}$ , AMOR_REDUIT= ${\eta}_{k}$ )

AMOR_SOL=F (FONC_AMOR_GEO= $\frac{\text{Im}(K(\omega ))}{2.\text{Re}(K(\omega ))}$ )

);

The computation of the contribution of the soil to the potential energy ${E}_{s}$ (factor keyword ENER_SOL) is computed from the soil impedance values determined previously (cf. [§6.1]). It can be computed according to two different methods according to whether one averages the modal forces (keyword RIGI_PARASOL) or the modal displacements at the nodes of the basemat.

The reduced damping of the soil spring ${\eta}_{s}$ (factor keyword AMOR_SOL) is computed from the values of the radiative damping.

### 6.3 Distribution of the soil stiffnesses and damping

If one wishes to study the effect of an earthquake on the possible uplift of the basemat for example, one may be led to model the soil no longer by a single spring at the centre of gravity of the soil-building interface but by a mat of springs. This is possible thanks to the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01) option RIGI_PARASOL.

The procedure consists in computing at each node of the mesh of the basemat the elementary stiffnesses $({k}_{x},{k}_{y},{k}_{z},{\text{kr}}_{x},{\text{kr}}_{y},{\text{kr}}_{z})$ to be applied from the global values of the three translational springs: $\text{kx},\text{ky},\text{kz}$ and of the three rotational springs: $\text{krx},\text{kry},\text{krz}$ coming from a soil-structure interaction code (or computed analytically).

One computes at each node $P$ of the basemat the tributary surface $S(P)$. This surface is obtained by integration over the elements of the basemat supporting the node.

One assumes that the elementary translational stiffnesses are proportional to the surface $S(P)$ and to a distribution function $f(r)$ depending on the distance $r$ from the node $P$ to the centre of gravity of the basemat $O$ :

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{K}_{x}=\sum_{P}{k}_{x}(P)={k}_{x}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathit{OP})\\ {K}_{y}=\sum_{P}{k}_{y}(P)={k}_{y}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathit{OP})\\ {K}_{z}=\sum_{P}{k}_{z}(P)={k}_{z}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathit{OP})\end{array}$

One then deduces ${k}_{x}$ and then ${k}_{x}(P)$ from the computation:

${k}_{x}(P)={k}_{x}.S(P).f(\mathit{OP})={K}_{x}.\frac{S(P).f(\mathit{OP})}{\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathit{OP})}$ .

One likewise deduces ${k}_{y}(P)$ and ${k}_{z}(P)$ .

For the elementary rotational stiffnesses, one distributes what remains after having removed the contributions due to the translations in the same way as the translations:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{K}_{\text{rx}}=\sum_{P}{k}_{\text{rx}}(P)+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{y}(P).{z}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{z}(P).{y}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]={k}_{\text{rx}}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathrm{OP})+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{y}(P).{z}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{z}(P).{y}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]\\ {K}_{\text{ry}}=\sum_{P}{k}_{\text{ry}}(P)+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{x}(P).{z}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{z}(P).{x}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]={k}_{\text{ry}}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathrm{OP})+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{x}(P).{z}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{z}(P).{x}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]\\ {K}_{\text{rz}}=\sum_{P}{k}_{\text{rz}}(P)+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{x}(P).{y}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{y}(P).{x}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]={k}_{\text{rz}}.\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathrm{OP})+\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{x}(P).{y}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{y}(P).{x}_{\mathrm{OP}}^{2}\right]\end{array}$

One then deduces ${k}_{\text{rx}}$ and then ${k}_{\text{rx}}(P)$ from the computation:

$\begin{array}{c}{k}_{\text{rx}}(P)={k}_{\text{rx}}.S(P).f(\mathit{OP})\\ =({K}_{\text{rx}}-\sum_{P}\left[{k}_{y}(P).{z}_{\mathit{OP}}^{2}+{k}_{z}(P).{y}_{\mathit{OP}}^{2}\right]).\frac{S(P).f(\mathit{OP})}{\sum_{P}S(P).f(\mathit{OP})}\end{array}$

One likewise deduces ${k}_{\text{ry}}(P)$ and ${k}_{\text{rz}}(P)$ .

**Note:**

*By default, one considers that the distribution function is constant and unitary, that is to say that each surface is assigned the same weight.*

*One can likewise distribute six global damping values, analytical or computed by a soil-structure interaction code.*
