# r7.01.28 Mohr-Coulomb law

**Summary:**

This document presents the method of solution of the Mohr-Coulomb law in *Code_Aster*.

## 1 Notations

### 1.1 General data

| ${\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}\ge {\sigma}_{3}$ | Principal stresses (in this order) |
| --- | --- |
| $E$ | Young's modulus |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio |
| $K=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu )}$ | Elastic bulk modulus |
| $G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$ | Elastic shear modulus |
| $\phi$ | Angle of internal friction of the material |
| $\psi$ | Dilatancy angle of the material |
| $c$ | Cohesion of the material |
| $s=\sin(\phi )$ |  |
| $t=\sin(\psi )$ |  |
| $p=\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}=\frac{\mathit{trace}\left(\sigma \right)}{3}$ | Mean stress |
| $p<0$ | Sign convention for the stress in compression |
| ${\sigma}^{e}$ | Elastic stress-predictor tensor |
| $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+d{\epsilon}^{p}$ | Tensors of the total strain, of the elastic strain and plastic strain increment |
| $d{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\mathit{trace}\left(d{\epsilon}^{p}\right)$ | Volumetric plastic strain increment |
| $d{e}^{p}=d{\epsilon}^{p}-\frac{d{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}1$ | Deviatoric plastic strain increment |
| $d{e}^{p}=\parallel d{e}^{p}\parallel =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}d{e}^{p}.d{e}^{p}}$ | Norm of the deviatoric plastic strain increment, or equivalent strain increment |

### 1.2 Convention on the tensor notations

The vectors of the strains and of the stresses in the principal basis $({d}_{1},{d}_{2},{d}_{3})$ are denoted:

$$
\epsilon =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{1}\\ {\epsilon}_{2}\\ {\epsilon}_{3}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The elasticity tensor $C$ making it possible to relate $\varepsilon$ and $\sigma$ in the principal basis, such that $\sigma =C.\varepsilon$ , is written:

$$
C=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}K+\frac{4}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G& K+\frac{4}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G& K+\frac{4}{3}G\end{array}\right]
$$

With $K$ the elastic bulk modulus and $G$ the elastic shear modulus. The strains and the stresses are symmetric tensors of order two. One generally exploits this symmetry (six independent components) by representing them by vectors of dimension six resulting from the projection of these tensors into appropriate bases.

The strains and the stresses given as input and produced as output of the solution of the constitutive law are expressed in the orthonormal basis of the symmetric tensors of order two, denoted $\stackrel{ˉ}{b}$ :

$$
\stackrel{ˉ}{b}=\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ \frac{{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{y}+{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\\ \frac{{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{z}+{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\\ \frac{{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{z}+{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{y}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}
$$

Where $\left({e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}\right)$ represent the unit vectors of the global Cartesian orthonormal basis, assumed fixed. The condensed expression of the strain and stress tensors projected into the basis $\stackrel{ˉ}{b}$ is written:

$$
\stackrel{̄}{\epsilon}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ {\epsilon}_{zz}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{xy}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{yz}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{xz}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

This writing brings out a term in $\sqrt{2}$ in front of the shear components. It makes it possible to:

- Express the elasticity tensor of order four, of $81$ components, by a tensor of order two of $36$ components;
- Symmetrise this elasticity tensor.

Indeed, writing ${\sigma}_{ij}={C}_{ijkl}{\epsilon}_{kl}$ , its form projected into the basis $\stackrel{̄}{b}$ becomes ${\stackrel{̄}{\sigma}}_{i}={\stackrel{̄}{C}}_{ij}{\stackrel{̄}{\epsilon}}_{j}$ , where one has the following expression for $\stackrel{̄}{C}$ :

$$
\stackrel{̄}{C}=\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}{C}_{\mathit{xxxx}}& {C}_{\mathit{xxyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{xxzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxyz}}\\ {C}_{\mathit{xxyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{yyyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{yyzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyyz}}\\ {C}_{\mathit{zzxx}}& {C}_{\mathit{zzyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{zzzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzyz}}\end{array}\right]
$$

The condensed form () is not convenient to use because of the necessity of handling the terms in $\sqrt{2}$  during the matrix operations. One prefers another writing, based on the projection into the basis known as the Voigt basis, denoted $\stackrel{̃}{b}$ and having the following expression:

$$
\stackrel{̃}{b}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ {e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{z}\end{array}
$$

The condensed expression of the strain and stress tensors projected into the Voigt basis $\stackrel{̃}{b}$ is written:

$$
\stackrel{̃}{\epsilon}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ {\epsilon}_{zz}\\ {\epsilon}_{xy}\\ {\epsilon}_{yz}\\ {\epsilon}_{xz}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

This writing makes it possible to dispense with the terms in $\sqrt{2}$ in front of the shear components, and is more convenient to use during the numerical solution of the constitutive law.

## 2 Formulation in terms of principal stresses

This formulation is valid only under the assumption of an isotropy of the material [, ]. Indeed, this condition is necessary to guarantee that the radial-return method preserves the principal directions. Its interest lies in the fact that it simplifies the writing of the equations and thereby allows very efficient solution methods (because they are quasi-analytical).

The elastic behaviour is purely linear.

The yield surface is characterised by six planes in the space of the principal stresses $\left({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{2},{\sigma}_{3}\right)$ . Each of these planes is characterised by an equation of the type:

$$
{F}_{13}\left({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{3}\right)={\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}+\left({\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}\right)\sin\varphi -\mathrm{2c}\cos\varphi =0
$$

Where $\phi$ and $c$ are material data and characterise respectively the angle of internal friction and the cohesion of the material.

The law is non-associated and the plastic flow potential ${G}_{13}$ associated with the yield surface ${F}_{13}$ is written in the same way:

$$
{G}_{13}\left({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{3}\right)={\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}+\left({\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}\right)\sin\psi -\mathrm{2c}\cos\psi
$$

Where $\psi$ is a material datum and characterises the dilatancy angle of the material.

When $\psi =\varphi$ , the plastic flow law becomes associated.

A graphical representation of the Mohr-Coulomb yield surface in the space of the principal stresses is found in the figure and in the $\pi$ plane in the figure .

One observes that the six planes intersect two by two along an angular edge, and meet at the apex of the cone characterised by the equation:

$$
p=c\mathrm{cot}\left(\varphi \right)
$$

These edges, six in number, as well as the apex of the cone, form singularities which pose problems of numerical integration, because the derivatives of the yield surface are not defined at these places. The methods making it possible to resolve this difficulty will be discussed further on.

## 3 Local integration of the Mohr-Coulomb law

The plastic strain rate is given by means of the Koiter formula:

$$
d{\epsilon}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\lambda}_{j}\frac{\partial {G}_{j}}{\partial \sigma }=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\lambda}_{j}{{n}_{G}}_{j}
$$

Where $m$ characterises the number of active mechanisms, equal to one, two or six according to the following situations:

- the final stress lies inside the yield surface, the point is regular and $m=1$ ;
- the final stress lies on an edge of the cone, the point is singular and $m=2$ ;
- the final stress lies neither inside the yield surface nor on an edge. It is then projected onto the apex of the cone, the point is singular and $m=6$ ;

The final stress ${\sigma}^{+}$ is computed from an elastic prediction denoted ${\sigma}^{e}$ and from a correction ${\sigma}^{c}=C.d{\epsilon}^{p}$  such that:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}^{e}-d{\sigma}^{c}={\sigma}^{e}-C.d{\epsilon}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\lambda}_{j}C.{{n}_{G}}_{j}
$$

The plastic multipliers $d{\lambda}_{j}$ are computed by injecting equation () into equation (), which gives:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\lambda}_{j}\left[{(C.{{n}_{G}}_{j})}_{1}-{(C.{{n}_{G}}_{j})}_{3}+({(C.{{n}_{G}}_{j})}_{1}+{(C.{{n}_{G}}_{j})}_{3})s\right]={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{3}^{e}+({\sigma}_{1}^{e}+{\sigma}_{3}^{e})s-\mathrm{2c}\cos\phi
$$

In what follows, one details the expressions corresponding to the various situations mentioned above. The solution procedure is recalled in the flow chart of the figure .

### 3.1 Case where a single mechanism is active

One detects that the prediction activates a criterion when:

$$
{F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{3}^{e}+\left({\sigma}_{1}^{e}+{\sigma}_{3}^{e}\right)s-\mathrm{2c}\cos\varphi \ge 0
$$

One has the following expression for the normal:

$$
{n}_{G}=\langle \begin{array}{ccc}t+1& 0& t-1\end{array}\rangle
$$

And therefore:

$$
C.{n}_{G}=2\langle \begin{array}{ccc}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G& \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t& \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\rangle
$$

Which one introduces into equation (), which becomes:

$$
4d\lambda \left[G+(K+\frac{G}{3})ts\right]={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{3}^{e}+({\sigma}_{1}^{e}+{\sigma}_{3}^{e})s-\mathrm{2c}\cos\phi
$$

Whence one deduces the plastic multiplier:

$$
d\lambda =\frac{{\langle {F}_{13}({\sigma}^{e})\rangle }_{+}}{4\left[G+(K+\frac{G}{3})ts\right]}
$$

Where ${\langle \rangle }_{+}$ denotes the positive part of a quantity. One finally obtains:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{+}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{\text{e}}\end{array}\right\rbrace -\frac{{\langle {F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\rangle }_{+}}{2G+2\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

If one decomposes the plastic strain rate into a deviatoric part $d{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}=\mathit{trace}(d{\varepsilon}^{p})$ and a spherical part $d{e}^{p}=d{\epsilon}^{p}-\frac{d{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}1$ , one has:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\mathrm{2t}d\lambda \\ d{e}^{p}=d\lambda \langle \begin{array}{ccc}\frac{t}{3}+1& \frac{-\mathrm{2t}}{3}& \frac{t}{3}-1\end{array}\rangle \end{array}
$$

That is, the following expression of the equivalent strain increment:

$$
d{e}^{p}=d\lambda \sqrt{{t}^{2}+3}
$$

### 3.2 Case where two mechanisms are active

#### 3.2.1 Formulation of the solution

One checks that ${\sigma}^{+}$ obtained from the previous step () always satisfies:

$$
{\sigma}_{1}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{2}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{3}^{+}
$$

If this is not the case, then two mechanisms must be activated in the following way:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cccc}{\sigma}_{2}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{1}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{3}^{+}& \Rightarrow & {F}_{13}\mathit{et}{F}_{23}\mathit{actifs}& \textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{LEFT}\right]}\\ {\sigma}_{1}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{3}^{+}\ge {\sigma}_{2}^{+}& \Rightarrow & {F}_{13}\mathit{et}{F}_{12}\mathit{actifs}& \textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{RIGHT}\right]}\end{array}
$$

The definition of the LEFT and RIGHT mechanisms is purely conventional, and follows the geometrical logic represented in the **figure** .

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.28/Cadre4.gif)

Figure 3.2.1-1: Definition of the LEFT and RIGHT mechanisms

One has the following expressions for the normals:

$$
{n}_{G}^{13}=\langle \begin{array}{ccc}t+1& 0& t-1\end{array}\rangle
{n}_{G}^{12}=\langle \begin{array}{ccc}t+1& t-1& 0\end{array}\rangle
$$

Therefore:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}C.{n}_{G}^{13}=2\langle \begin{array}{ccc}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G& \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t& \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\rangle \\ C.{n}_{G}^{12}=2\langle \begin{array}{ccc}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G& \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G& \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\end{array}\rangle \\ C.{n}_{G}^{23}=2\langle \begin{array}{ccc}\left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t& \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G& \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\rangle \end{array}
$$

One introduces these expressions into equation (). For the LEFT mechanism, one obtains the following system to be solved:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}4d{\lambda}^{13}\left[G+\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]+2d{\lambda}^{23}\left[G\left(1-t-s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]={F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\\ 2d{\lambda}^{13}\left[G\left(1-t-s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]+4d{\lambda}^{23}\left[G+\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]={F}_{23}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\end{array}
$$

For the RIGHT mechanism, one obtains:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}4d{\lambda}^{13}\left[G+\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]+2d{\lambda}^{12}\left[G\left(1+t+s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]={F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\\ 2d{\lambda}^{13}\left[G\left(1+t+s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]+4d{\lambda}^{12}\left[G+\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]={F}_{12}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\end{array}
$$

For the algorithmic implementation, it is more convenient to rewrite () and () as follows:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}Ad{\lambda}^{1}+{B}^{\mathit{side}}d{\lambda}^{2}={F}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\\ {B}^{\mathit{side}}d{\lambda}^{1}+Ad{\lambda}^{2}={F}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)\end{array}
$$

With the following plastic multipliers:

$$
d{\lambda}^{1}=d{\lambda}^{13}
$$

For the yield surfaces:

$$
{F}_{1}={F}_{13}
$$

The expression of $A$ :

$$
A=4\left[G+\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]
$$

And the expression of ${B}^{\mathit{side}}$ :

$$
{B}^{\mathit{side}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}2\left[G\left(1-t-s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{LEFT}\right]}\\ 2\left[G\left(1+t+s\right)+\left(\mathrm{2K}-\frac{G}{3}\right)ts\right]\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{RIGHT}\right]}\end{array}
$$

The solution of the system of equations () exists if and only if its determinant is non-zero, that is:

$$
det=∣\begin{array}{cc}A& {B}^{\mathit{side}}\\ {B}^{\mathit{side}}& A\end{array}∣={A}^{2}-{\left({B}^{\mathit{side}}\right)}^{2}\ne 0
$$

One can show that this never happens for "physical" values of the parameters $K$ , $G$ , $\varphi$ and $\psi$ . One then has as solutions:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\lambda}^{1}=\frac{A{F}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)-{B}^{\mathit{side}}{F}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)}{det}\\ d{\lambda}^{2}=\frac{A{F}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)-{B}^{\mathit{side}}{F}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)}{det}\end{array}
$$

For the LEFT mechanism, one finally obtains:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{+}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{\text{e}}\end{array}\right\rbrace -2d{\lambda}^{13}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace -2d{\lambda}^{23}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

And for the RIGHT mechanism:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{+}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{+}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{2}^{\text{e}}\\ {\sigma}_{3}^{\text{e}}\end{array}\right\rbrace -2d{\lambda}^{13}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace -2d{\lambda}^{12}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

Or again, simplifying the writing:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}^{e}-2d{\lambda}^{1}{v}_{t}^{1}-2d{\lambda}^{2}{v}_{t}^{2}
$$

With the following plastic multipliers:

$$
d{\lambda}^{1}=d{\lambda}^{13}
$$

And the vectors:

$$
{v}_{t}^{1}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

In the same way, the plastic strain rate is written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\mathrm{2t}\left(d{\lambda}^{1}+d{\lambda}^{2}\right)\\ d{e}^{p}=d{\lambda}^{1}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{t}{3}+1& \frac{-\mathrm{2t}}{3}& \frac{t}{3}-1\end{array}\right)+d{\lambda}^{2}\end{array}\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{-\mathrm{2t}}{3}& \frac{t}{3}+1& \frac{t}{3}-1\end{array}\right)\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{LEFT}\right]}\\ \left(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{t}{3}+1& \frac{t}{3}-1& \frac{-\mathrm{2t}}{3}\end{array}\right)\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{RIGHT}\right]}\end{array}
$$

That is, the following expression of the equivalent strain increment:

$$
d{e}^{p}=\sqrt{\left({\left(d{\lambda}^{1}\right)}^{2}+{\left(d{\lambda}^{2}\right)}^{2}\right)\left({t}^{2}+3\right)+(-{t}^{2}+\mathit{sign}\times 6t+3)d{\lambda}^{1}d{\lambda}^{2}}
$$

With $\mathit{sign}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-1\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{LEFT}\right]}\\ +1\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\textcolor[rgb]{1,0,0}{\left[\text{RIGHT}\right]}\end{array}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Choice of the second mechanism

One proposes a simple and purely geometrical criterion in order to determine with certainty whether the second mechanism, should it be activated, lies on the left (LEFT) or on the right (RIGHT). One defines the vector ${t}_{G}$ perpendicular to the flow direction as represented in the figure , in the following manner:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}t-1\\ -2\\ t+1\end{array}\right\rbrace ={t}_{G}\perp {n}_{G}={n}_{G}^{13}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}t+1\\ 0\\ t-1\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The straight line passing through $0$ and parallel to ${n}_{G}$ , represented as a dashed line, characterises the stress states such that $\sigma .{t}_{G}=0$ . One therefore has the following choice:

- If $\sigma .{t}_{G}>0$ and a second mechanism must be active, this mechanism is necessarily situated on the right, that is the RIGHT mechanism;
- If $\sigma .{t}_{G}<0$ and a second mechanism must be active, this mechanism is necessarily situated on the left, that is the LEFT mechanism;

## 4 Expression of the consistent tangent matrix in the principal basis

### 4.1 Case where a single mechanism is active

The consistent tangent matrix $T$ in the principal basis is obtained by differentiating equation (), which gives:

$$
d{\sigma}^{+}=C.d\epsilon -\frac{2d{F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)}{A}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)t\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)t-G\end{array}\right\rbrace =C.d\epsilon -\frac{2d{F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)}{A}{v}_{t}
$$

Knowing that:

$$
d{F}_{13}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)=d{\sigma}_{1}^{e}-d{\sigma}_{3}^{e}+\left(d{\sigma}_{1}^{e}+d{\sigma}_{3}^{e}\right)s=2\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)s+G\\ \left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)s\\ \left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)s-G\end{array}\right\rbrace .d\epsilon =2{v}_{s}.d\epsilon
$$

One obtains:

$$
d{\sigma}^{+}=\underset{T}{\underset{⏟}{\left(C-\frac{4}{A}\underset{D}{\underset{⏟}{{v}_{t}\otimes {v}_{s}}}\right)}}.d\epsilon
$$

Where $A$ is given by equation ().

## 5 Expression of the consistent tangent matrix in the global basis

Paragraph **§**  4  makes it possible to build the consistent tangent matrix in the principal basis, denoted $T$ . It is now appropriate to bring this matrix back into the global (Cartesian) basis, which will be denoted $\stackrel{̄}{T}$ .

*Important note:*

It should be noted that the construction of this consistent tangent matrix is a crucial step both for the robustness and for the performance of the algorithm:

- Firstly, it is perfectly well known that such a matrix allows a quadratic rate of convergence for the Newton process;
- Secondly, this matrix accounts for the rotation of the principal directions during an increment. Without it, the formulation of the Mohr-Coulomb law in terms of principal stresses described in paragraph **§**  2  would not be complete, since the principal stresses, kept fixed during the local integration of the law ( **§**  3 ), could not rotate at the global level of the structure.

In this paragraph, one describes in detail the method making it possible to build $\stackrel{ˉ}{T}$ from $T$ .

### 5.1 Some results on isotropic symmetric tensors of order two

One defines by ${S}^{3}$ the space of symmetric tensors of order two in the vector space of dimension $n=3$ , and the tensors $Y\in {S}^{3}$ and $X\in {S}^{3}$ such that:

$$
Y(X):{S}^{3}\to {S}^{3}
$$

The tensor function $Y\left(X\right)$ is said to be isotropic if:

$$
R.Y(X).{R}^{t}=Y(R.X.{R}^{t})
$$

Whatever the rotation $R$ . The assumption of isotropy implies that $Y$ and $X$ are coaxial, that is to say that they possess the same principal directions ${d}_{\alpha =1,2,3}$ . One writes:

$$
\begin{array}{c}X=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{x}_{\alpha}\underset{{E}_{\alpha}}{\underset{⏟}{\left({d}_{\alpha}\otimes {d}_{\alpha}\right)}}=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{x}_{\alpha}{E}_{\alpha}\\ Y\left(X\right)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{y}_{\alpha}\left({d}_{\alpha}\otimes {d}_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{y}_{\alpha}{E}_{\alpha}\end{array}
$$

Where ${y}_{\alpha}={y}_{\alpha}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},{x}_{3})$ and ${x}_{\alpha}$ represent the eigenvalues of $Y$ and $X$ , respectively.

### 5.2 Derivative of an isotropic tensor function of order two

One assumes that the isotropic tensor function $Y\left(X\right)$ is differentiable with respect to $X$ , and one defines its derivative $D$ such that:

$$
D\left(X\right)\stackrel{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{dY\left(X\right)}{dX}
$$

Applied to equation (), one obtains the following expression:

$$
D(X)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}({E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{y}_{\alpha}}{dX}+{y}_{\alpha}\frac{d{E}_{\alpha}}{dX})=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}({y}_{\alpha}\frac{d{E}_{\alpha}}{dX}+\sum_{\beta =1}^{3}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{x}_{\beta}}{dX})
$$

#### 5.2.1 Two-dimensional case of plane-stress type (C_PLAN)

In dimension two (C_PLAN case), the characteristic equation $det\left(X-{x}_{\alpha}I\right)=0$ gives a quadratic equation in the eigenvalues ${x}_{\alpha =1,2}$ of $X$ of the following type:

$$
{x}_{\alpha}^{2}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}+{I}_{2}=0
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{I}_{1}=\mathit{trace}\left(X\right)={X}_{11}+{X}_{22}\\ {I}_{2}=det\left(X\right)={X}_{11}{X}_{22}-{X}_{12}{X}_{21}\end{array}
$$

The solution of the spectral problem readily gives the following solutions for the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{x}_{1}=\frac{{I}_{1}+\sqrt{{I}_{1}^{2}-4{I}_{2}}}{2}\\ {x}_{2}=\frac{{I}_{1}-\sqrt{{I}_{1}^{2}-4{I}_{2}}}{2}\end{array}
$$

And the eigenvectors, taking into account the multiplicity of the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}{E}_{\alpha}=\frac{X+({x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1})I}{2{x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1}}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\mathrm{\ne }{x}_{2}\\ {E}_{1}=I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}\end{array}
$$

In particular, Carlson and Hoger show that if ${x}_{1}\mathrm{\ne }{x}_{2}$ , one has:

$$
\frac{d{x}_{\alpha}}{dX}={E}_{\alpha}
$$

Using equations (), () and () in (), one obtains the expression of the derivative $D(X)$ , taking the multiplicity of the eigenvalues into account:

$$
D\left(X\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{{y}_{1}-{y}_{2}}{{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}}\left[{I}_{S}-{E}_{1}\otimes {E}_{1}-{E}_{2}\otimes {E}_{2}\right]+\sum_{\alpha =1}^{2}\sum_{\beta =1}^{2}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{\beta}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\\ \left(\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}\right){I}_{S}+\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}\end{array}
$$

With the identity matrix $I$ :

$$
{(I)}_{ijkl}={\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}
$$

The transposition matrix ${({I}_{t})}_{ijkl}={\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}}$ and the symmetrisation matrix ${I}_{S}$ , such that:

$$
{({I}_{S})}_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}(I+{I}_{t})=\frac{1}{2}({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}})
$$

*Note:*

One notes that the term $\frac{{y}_{1}-{y}_{2}}{{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}}\left[{I}_{S}-{E}_{1}\otimes {E}_{1}-{E}_{2}\otimes {E}_{2}\right]$ in the derivative $D(X)$ of the first equation of () expresses the rotation of the principal directions in the plane.

#### 5.2.2 Two-dimensional case of plane-strain type (D_PLAN) and axisymmetric (AXIS)

The out-of-plane direction $\alpha =3$ being fixed, the expression of the derivative $D(X)$ is obtained from the preceding case. Indeed, by isolating the term $\alpha =3$ in equation (), one has the following expression:

$$
D\left(X\right)=\underset{{D}_{\mathrm{2D}}\left(X\right)}{\underset{⏟}{\sum_{\alpha =1}^{2}\left({y}_{\alpha}\frac{d{E}_{\alpha}}{dX}+\sum_{\beta =1}^{2}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{x}_{\beta}}{dX}\right)}}+\underset{{D}_{3}\left(X\right)}{\underset{⏟}{\sum_{\alpha =1}^{2}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{3}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{x}_{3}}{dX}+\sum_{\beta =1}^{3}\frac{\partial {y}_{3}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{3}\otimes \frac{d{x}_{\beta}}{dX}}}
$$

Where ${D}_{\mathrm{2D}}\left(X\right)$ is given by equation (). The complementary term ${D}_{3}\left(X\right)$ is written, taking the multiplicity of the eigenvalues into account, in the following way:

$$
{D}_{3}\left(X\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\sum_{\alpha =1}^{2}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{3}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{3}+\frac{\partial {y}_{3}}{\partial {x}_{\alpha}}{E}_{3}\otimes {E}_{\alpha}\right)+\frac{\partial {y}_{3}}{\partial {x}_{3}}{E}_{3}\otimes {E}_{3}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\\ \frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{3}}{I}_{p}\otimes {E}_{3}+\frac{\partial {y}_{3}}{\partial {x}_{1}}{E}_{3}\otimes {I}_{p}+\frac{\partial {y}_{3}}{\partial {x}_{3}}{E}_{3}\otimes {E}_{3}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}\end{array}
$$

Where ${I}_{p}$ is the matrix of the orthogonal projection of ${I}_{S}$ into the plane $({e}_{x},{e}_{y})$ :

$$
{I}_{p}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}}\right)& \text{si}i,j,k,l\in \lbrace 1,2\rbrace \\ 0& \text{sinon}\end{array}
$$

#### 5.2.3 Three-dimensional case

In dimension three, the characteristic equation $det\left(X-{x}_{\alpha}I\right)=0$ gives a cubic equation in the eigenvalues ${x}_{\alpha =1,2,3}$ of $X$ of the following type:

$$
{x}_{\alpha}^{3}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}^{2}+{I}_{2}{x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{3}=0
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{I}_{1}=\mathit{trace}\left(X\right)\\ {I}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathit{trace}{\left(X\right)}^{2}-\mathit{trace}\left({X}^{2}\right)\right]\\ {I}_{3}=det\left(X\right)\end{array}
$$

The solution of the spectral problem readily gives the following solutions for the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{1}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\\ {x}_{2}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta +2\pi }{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\\ {x}_{3}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta -2\pi }{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\end{array}
$$

Where $Q$ and $\theta$ are given by:

$$
Q=\frac{{I}_{1}^{2}-3{I}_{2}}{9}
$$

With:

$$
R=\frac{-2{I}_{1}^{3}+9{I}_{1}{I}_{2}-27{I}_{3}}{54}
$$

And the eigenvectors, taking the multiplicity of the eigenvalues into account:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{E}_{\alpha}=\frac{{x}_{\alpha}}{2{x}_{\alpha}^{3}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}^{2}+{I}_{3}}\left[{X}^{2}+\left({x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1}\right)X+\frac{{I}_{3}}{{x}_{\alpha}}I\right]& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\ne {x}_{3}\\ {E}_{\beta}=I-{E}_{\alpha}& \mathit{si}{x}_{\alpha}\ne {x}_{\beta}\\ {E}_{1}=I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}={x}_{3}\end{array}
$$

In the second equation of (), ${E}_{\alpha}$ is computed by means of the first equation. Without giving the intermediate steps of the computation, the derivative $D\left(X\right)$ , taking the multiplicity of the eigenvalues into account, is finally written:

$$
D\left(X\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\begin{array}{c}\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}\frac{{y}_{\alpha}}{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}\right)\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}[\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}-\left({x}_{\beta}+{x}_{\gamma}\right){I}_{S}\\ -\left(2{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right){E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{\alpha}-\left({x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)\left({E}_{\beta}\otimes {E}_{\beta}-{E}_{\gamma}\otimes {E}_{\gamma}\right)]\\ +\sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{b=1}^{3}\frac{\partial {y}_{a}}{\partial {x}_{b}}{E}_{a}\otimes {E}_{b}\end{array}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\ne {x}_{3}\\ {s}_{1}\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}-{s}_{2}{I}_{S}-{s}_{3}X\otimes X+{s}_{4}X\otimes I+{s}_{5}I\otimes X-{s}_{6}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{\alpha}\ne {x}_{\beta}={x}_{\gamma}\\ \left(\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}\right){I}_{S}+\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}={x}_{3}\end{array}
$$

Where $\left(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma \right)$ corresponds to a cyclic permutation of $\left(1,2,3\right)$ . $I$ and ${I}_{S}$ are given by equations () and (), respectively. Noting that $X$ is a symmetric tensor, one must take care to apply the symmetric differentiation operator for the evaluation of $\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}$ , which gives the following form:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}{\left(\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}\right)}_{ijkl}& =\frac{d\left({X}_{im}{X}_{\mathit{mj}}\right)}{d{X}_{kl}}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{lm}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{km}}\right){X}_{\mathit{mj}}+\frac{{X}_{im}}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{mk}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{ml}}{\delta}_{\mathit{kj}}\right)\\ & =\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{X}_{\mathit{lj}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{X}_{\mathit{kj}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}{X}_{\mathit{ik}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{kj}}{X}_{\mathit{il}}\right)\end{array}
$$

Finally, the expressions of ${s}_{i=1,6}$ are the following:

#### 5.2.4 Application to the Mohr-Coulomb case

The transposition of the preceding formulae to the numerical implementation deserves a few clarifications. One first has the following correspondences:

- $X={\stackrel{̃}{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{pred}}$ et ${x}_{\alpha}={\epsilon}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ ;
- $Y={\stackrel{̃}{\sigma}}^{+}$ et ${y}_{\alpha}={\sigma}_{\alpha}^{+}$ ;
- ${E}_{\alpha}={\stackrel{̃}{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}\otimes {\stackrel{̃}{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ ;
- ${\left(T\right)}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}$ is the consistent tangent matrix in the principal basis computed in paragraph **§**  4 ;

The notation ${}^{\mathit{pred}}$ indicates that one works with "predicted" quantities given as input by the Newton process, the notation ${}^{+}$ with quantities coming from the local solution of the constitutive law, and the notation $\tilde{\rbrace$ with the Voigt basis. It will be noted that the predicted principal directions ${\tilde{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ are frozen during the local solution, which is consistent with the isotropy assumption adopted (see the explanations of paragraph **§**  5.1 ).

Having all this information at the end of the local solution of the constitutive law, one deduces from it the consistent tangent matrix $\stackrel{ˉ}{T}=\stackrel{ˉ}{D}$ expressed in the projection basis $\stackrel{ˉ}{b}$ defined in paragraph **§**  1.2 **:**

- Equation () in the two-dimensional case in plane stress (C_PLAN);
- Equation () in the two-dimensional case in plane strain (D_PLAN) or axisymmetric (AXIS);
- Equation () in the three-dimensional case (3D);

The second important piece of information concerns the writing convention of the various tensors. Indeed, for the sake of generality, the notation used for the tensors throughout paragraph **§**  5  is the classical notation, bringing out tensors up to order four. This writing is unsuitable for the numerical solution, where one prefers to use condensed notations made possible by the fact that one works with symmetric tensors of order two (stresses and strains always are). One distinguishes two forms of condensed notations corresponding to two projection bases (see **§**  1.2 ):

- The orthonormal basis $\stackrel{ˉ}{b}$ of the symmetric tensors of order two. It is in this basis that the stresses and the strains are given at the input and at the output of the local solution of the behaviour;
- The basis known as the Voigt basis $\tilde{b}$ , much more convenient to use during the local numerical solution of the behaviour because it avoids having to handle coefficients in $\sqrt{2}$ during the matrix operations;
