# v3.06.100 SSLA100 - Infinite cylinder subject to a field of density and superfacial forces

**Summary:**

This test of quasistatic linear mechanics validates the assignment of a load of force field, either over- or in volume.

The structure studied is cylindrical. nodes fields of density and superfacic forces are read in an Ideas file. For volume loading, the field read varies quadraticly according to the distance to the axis; for surface loading, the field read corresponds to an internal pressure.

Three modellings of the same problem are performed:

- 3D modelling;
- 2D axisymmetric modelling;
- 2D Modelling flat deformations;

The reference solution is an analytical.

### 1.2 Material properties

The cylinder consists of a homogeneous material which follows a linear elastic constitutive law:

- $E=10\mathrm{Pa}$ ;
- $\rho =1\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ ;
- $\nu =0.3$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loadings (see [Figure 1.3-a])

The volumetric force considered is radial, it varies quadraticly with the radius: ${F}_{V}=\alpha .{r}^{2}$ with $\alpha =1N/{m}^{3}$ .

The above-mentioned surface force is applied to the inner wall of the cylinder perpendicular to the wall (equivalent to an internal pressure imposed on the cylinder): ${F}_{S}(r={R}_{int})=\mathrm{1N}/{m}^{2}$ .

The boundary conditions allows you to place yourself in the hypothesis of flat deformations on a section of the cylinder: vertical displacements blocked on the upper and lower sections of the cylinder.

**Note:**

*For 3D modelling, eigenmodes are removed by the 2D plane conditions applied to the lower section of the cylinder. This type of boundary conditions allows to obtain a axisymmetric solution on the move, directly comparable with the analytical solution.*

## 6 Summary of the results

The results of *code_Aster* are very close to analytical solution, despite very coarse mesh sizes.

The modellings 3D and 2D plane provide very similar details because they have the same circumferential and radial discretizations. The 2D axisymmetric Modelling is more efficient because it has 2 radial-wise cuts and no circumferential discretization.
