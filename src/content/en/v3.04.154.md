# v3.04.154 SSLV154 – Circular joint in mixed mode

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the calculation of stress intensity factors (SIF s) along the bottom of a 3D crack in mixed mode, in the linear elasticity.

This test involves a cube with a flat central circular crack, subjected to an inclined force relative to the crack plane.

This test contains 2 modellings:

- Modelling A: the crack is mesh (FEM) with mesh radiating at the bottom of the crack;
- Modelling B: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level-sets (X-FEM)
- Modelling C: the crack is mesh (FEM) for incompressible elements (_INCO_UPG);
- Modelling D: the crack is mesh (FEM) with free mesh from Zcracks

For each model, SIF s are evaluated by POST_K1_K2_K3 and CALC_G commands.

The numerical values are compared with the analytical values.

### 1.1 Geometry

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is elastic isotropic with properties of:

$E=200000\mathrm{MPa}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Given the symmetries of structure, crack and loadings, only half of the structure is modelled: half space such as $Y>0$. Therefore symmetry conditions are applied to the $Y=0$ face: on this face, the following $Y$ displacement is blocked.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.154/1000000000000232000001C3C3B3318ECE7181F4.png)

Figure 1.3-1: Condition de symétrie

The structure is subjected to an ${\sigma}_{xx}=1\mathit{MPa}$ constraint. The local $(x,y,z)$ marker is obtained by rotating an $\alpha =\pi /4$ angle around the $\mathit{OY}$ axis.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.154/10000000000002B20000022EE428EC6E1D6BBC34.png)

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.154/100000000000015B000000E8C71967D18D36A085.png)

Figure 1.3-3: Repère local

Thus, we have:

on the upper side:

- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ZZ}}=\sigma {\sin}^{2}(\alpha )$
- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ZX}}=\sigma \cos(\alpha )\sin(\alpha )$

on the lower side:

- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ZZ}}=-\sigma {\sin}^{2}(\alpha )$
- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ZX}}=-\sigma \sin(\alpha )\cos(\alpha )$

on the right side side:

- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{XX}}=\sigma {\cos}^{2}(\alpha )$
- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{XZ}}=\sigma \cos(\alpha )\sin(\alpha )$

on the left side of the vehicle:

- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{XX}}=-\sigma {\cos}^{2}(\alpha )$
- ${\sigma}_{\mathit{XZ}}=\sigma \cos(\alpha )\sin(\alpha )$

In order to block rigid body modes, the point $\mathit{D1}$ $(L/2,0,0)$ is blocked following $X$ and $Z$ and the point $\mathit{D2}$ $(-L/2,0,0)$ is blocked following $Z$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

(1)
The reference solution is an analytical solution from , for a circular $a$ radius crack in an infinite medium, subjected to a uniform $\sigma$ surface force inclined from an $\alpha$ angle to the crack plane, the stress intensity factors for a point $A$ placed on the crack front are:

${K}_{I}=\frac{2}{\pi}\sigma ({\sin}^{2}\alpha )\sqrt{\pi a}$

${K}_{\mathit{II}}=\frac{4}{\pi (2-\nu )}\sigma (\sin\alpha \cos\alpha )\cos\omega \sqrt{\pi a}$

${K}_{\mathit{III}}=\frac{4(1-\nu )}{\pi (2-\nu )}\sigma (\sin\alpha \cos\alpha )\sin\omega \sqrt{\pi a}$

$\omega$ being the angle characterizing the position of the point $A$ on the circular background (see ).

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

The integration crowns of the theta field for the CALC_G command are:

$\text{RINF}=0,1m$ et $\text{RSUP}=0,5m$ .

The LEGENDRE type smoothing and LINEAIRE type smoothing are tested. The LINEAIRE type smoothing is smoothed by a treatment that uses a cap function at the nodes tops of the crack bottom segments. This treatment is activated automatically in the quadratic case, to avoid oscillations of the G(s) values in this case (vopir reference (2)). G is then noted as "chapeau" ( $\widehat{G}$ ) this smoothed component. The same treatment is also applied to the stress intensity factors values obtained by the CALC_G option K.

The ABS_CURV_MAXI parameter of the POST_K1_K2_K3 operator is selected to retain 5 nodes on the extrapolation segment.

To test the value of ${K}_{I}$ for all crack bottom points, the $min$ and $max$ are tested for values along the bottom.

The ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ is tested only at point such as $\omega =0°$ (where ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ is normally maximum).

The ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ is tested only at point such as $\omega =90°$ (where ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ is normally maximum).

Theoretically, the absolute value of ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ should be tested because the sign is arbitrary.

For information, this compares SIF s along the crack bottom with a smoothing of LINEAIRE raw and with the smoothing of "chapeau". It is found that the raw values of the smoothing of LINEAIRE oscillate along the bottom. This is due to the functions of the quadratic elements shapes that are different between the nodes media and nodes peaks and that render the interpretation of G(s) (and therefore Ki(s)) non-physical, as would be a nodal reaction on a quadratic element. The "chapeau" smoothing is a method to bypass this difficulty as proposed in the reference (2).

There is also a larger error on the points at the bottom ends for ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ from CALC_G. This is due to a particular treatment of the points at the ends, which only works well if the solution is constant along the bottom. This explains why the values of ${K}_{I}$ are better. This particular treatment is only performed for the results ($G$ , ${K}_{I}$ ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ ) from the CALC_G OPTION K option, smoothing LINEAIRE.

### 4.4 Remarks

As with Modelling A, CALC_K_G_XFEM gives less accurate results than POST_K1_K2_K3. For curved fronts, the post-processing of SIF is best preferred with POST_K1_K2_K3 to keep an acceptable error in propagation of a crack. The X-FEM results are as accurate as the results with a mesh crack (Modelling A), which further enhances the interest of X-FEM use compared to the FEM method.

A very good agreement is obtained between the K2 and K3 values calculated with POST_K1_K2_K3 and the analytical solution values (see ).

Furthermore, no parasite effect is observed at the edges as is the case in model A. With a mesh crack, shear is observed at the crack-end points difficult to explain (see ).

## 7 Summary of the results

This test case validates the calculation of stress intensity factors of a 3D crack in mixed mode. For the CALC_G command (option K) there are significant errors at the end points if the $K$ is not constant along the crack bottom.

This problem does not appear for the POST_K1_K2_K3 command.
