# r3.07.03 Plate elements: modellings DKT, DST, DKTG and Q4G

**Summary:**

These modellings of plate finite elements are intended for analyses in small strains and small displacements of thin curved or plane structures. They are plane finite elements which do not take into account the geometric curvature of thin structures, unlike shell elements which are curved: parasitic bendings result, which can be reduced by using more elements so as to be able to approximate curved geometries correctly. The formulation is therefore simplified and the number of degrees of freedom reduced. These finite elements are reputed to be among the most accurate for the computation of the displacements and for modal analysis.

For each of these different modellings several finite elements are available, according to the mesh cells:

- the modelling DKT, according to the Love-Kirchhoff bending model, comprises the triangular (DKT) and quadrangular (DKQ) finite elements, which use fields with sub-points, in order for example to integrate the constitutive relation in the layers making up the thickness;
- the modelling DST, with transverse shear energy in elasticity, comprises the triangular (DST) and quadrangular (DSQ) finite elements;
- the modelling DKTG, according to the Love-Kirchhoff bending model, comprises the triangular (DKTG) and quadrangular (DKQG) finite elements, dedicated to “global” constitutive relations, which have only one layer and a single integration point in the thickness;
- the modelling Q4G (also named $\text{Q4}\gamma$ ) with transverse shear energy in elasticity, but with an interpolation other than for the modelling DST, comprises only the quadrangular finite element (Q4G).

**Note:**

In the document [R3.07.09](doc:r3.07.09), one presents the modelling Q4GG, dedicated to thick plates. This modelling comprises the quadrangular finite elements (Q4G) whose theoretical description is given in the present document and the triangular elements (T3G) .

## 1 Introduction

Shell and plate elements are used in particular to model thin structures where the ratios between the dimensions (thickness/characteristic length) are at most $1/10$ . They therefore intervene in particular in fields such as civil engineering, PWR core internals, vibratory analysis, Euler buckling analysis, the analysis of thin structures in elastic multi-layer composite material... One restricts oneself to the framework of small displacements (even if it is possible but little recommended to use the updating ’PETIT_REAC’) and of small strains.

Unlike shell elements, plane plate elements do not make it possible to take into account the geometric curvature of the structure to be represented and induce parasitic bendings. It is therefore necessary to use a large number of these elements so as to approximate the geometry of the structure correctly, and this all the more so as it is curved. On the other hand, one gains in simplicity of formulation and the number of degrees of freedom is reduced. Moreover, the formulations " *Discrete Shear* " (DST, DSQ and Q4g) or " *Discrete Kirchhoff* " (DKT, DKTG and DKQ, DKQG) of the kinematics, with or without transverse distortion respectively, allow good results in terms of displacements and of modal analysis.

The support mesh cells of these finite elements are linear (triangles and quadrangles). The degrees of freedom of these finite elements are the translations and the rotations of the vertex nodes. The characteristics which one assigns to them are: the thickness, the shear coefficient, the offset, ...

The manner in which these elements are implemented in *code_aster*  as well as certain aspects of the use are given at [§5] of the present documentation.

In particular one classifies these formulations:

| modelling | finite elements | Use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DKT | DKT, DKQ | Love-Kirchhoff model, dedicated to linear and non-linear constitutive relations with integration sub-point fields in the thickness, cf. §:ref:4.9 <RefNumPara__34251967> . |
| DKTG | DKTG, DKQG | Love-Kirchhoff model, dedicated to “global” constitutive relations, which have only one layer and a single integration point in the thickness |
| DST | DST, DSQ | model with transverse shear energy in elasticity |
| Q4G | Q4g | model with transverse shear energy in elasticity |

## 2 Formulation

### 2.1 Geometry of the plate elements [1]

For the plate elements one defines a reference surface, or mean surface, which is plane (plane $x,y$ for example) and a thickness $h(x,y)$ . This thickness must be small with respect to the other dimensions (extents, radii of curvature) of the structure to be modelled. [Figure 2.1-a] below illustrates our point.

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.03/100000000000030C0000036C74EFD203D3D81C6E.png)

**Figure 2.1-a**

One attaches to the mean surface $\omega$ a local orthonormal frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$ associated with the tangent plane of the structure, different from the global frame $\mathit{OXYZ}$ . The position of the points of the plate is given by the Cartesian coordinates $(x,y)$ of the mean surface and the elevation $z$ with respect to this surface.

#### 2.1.1 Intrinsic frame

By taking the previous local frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$ with as origin the first vertex of the element and as axis $\mathit{Ox}$ the side joining vertices 1 and 2, one defines the so-called intrinsic frame.

### 2.2 Plate theory

These elements rest on the theory of plates in small displacements and small strains.

#### 2.2.1 Kinematics

The straight sections, which are the sections perpendicular to the mean surface, remain straight; the material points situated on a normal to the undeformed mean surface remain on a straight line in the deformed configuration. It results from this approach that **the displacement fields vary linearly in the thickness of the plate** . If one denotes by $u,v,w$ the displacements of a point $q(x,y,z)$ along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ , one thus has the Hencky-Mindlin kinematics:

$$
(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}(x,y,z)\\ {u}_{y}(x,y,z)\\ {u}_{z}(x,y,z)\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}u(x,y)\\ v(x,y)\\ w(x,y)\end{array})+z(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{y}(x,y)\\ -{\theta}_{x}(x,y)\\ 0\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}u(x,y)\\ v(x,y)\\ w(x,y)\end{array})+z(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}(x,y)\\ {\beta}_{y}(x,y)\\ 0\end{array})
$$

where $u,v,w$ are the displacements of the mean surface and ${\theta}_{x}$ and ${\theta}_{y}$ the rotations of this surface with respect to the two axes $x$ and $y$ respectively. One prefers to introduce the two rotations ${\beta}_{x}(x,y)={\theta}_{y}(x,y),{\beta}_{y}(x,y)=-{\theta}_{x}(x,y)$ .

The three-dimensional strains at any point, with the kinematics introduced previously, are thus given by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}={e}_{xx}+z{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}={e}_{yy}+z{\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{xy}={\gamma}_{xy}=2{e}_{xy}+2z{\kappa}_{xy}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{xz}={\gamma}_{x}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{yz}={\gamma}_{y}\end{array}
$$

where ${e}_{xx},{e}_{yy}$ and ${e}_{xy}$ are the membrane strains of the mean surface, ${\gamma}_{x}$ and ${\gamma}_{y}$ the strains associated with the transverse shears, and ${\kappa}_{xx},{\kappa}_{yy},{\kappa}_{xy}$ the bending strains (or changes of curvature) of the mean surface, which are written:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\\ {e}_{yy}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\\ 2{e}_{xy}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\\ {\kappa}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial x}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial y}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial x}\\ {\gamma}_{x}={\beta}_{x}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}={\beta}_{y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\end{array}
$$

**Note:**

*In plate theories the introduction of* ${\beta}_{x}$ *and* ${\beta}_{y}$ *makes it possible to symmetrise the formulations of the strains and, as we shall see later, the equilibrium equations. In shell theories one rather uses* ${\theta}_{x}$ *and* ${\theta}_{y}$ *and the associated moments* ${M}_{x}$ *and* ${M}_{y}$ *with respect to* $x$ *and* $y$ *.*

#### 2.2.2 Constitutive law

The behaviour of the plates is a 3D behaviour in “plane stress”. **The transverse stress** ${\sigma}_{zz}$ **is zero**  because it is considered negligible with respect to the other components of the stress tensor (plane-stress assumption). The most general constitutive law is then written as follows:

$$
(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\\ {\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array})=\text{C}(e,\alpha )(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{xx}\\ {\varepsilon}_{yy}\\ {\gamma}_{xy}\\ {\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=\text{Ce}+z\text{C}\kappa +\text{C}\gamma
\textrm{avec}
e=(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ 2{e}_{xy}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}),\kappa =(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})\text{et}\gamma =(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\\ {\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array}).
$$

where $\text{C}(e,\alpha )$ is the local tangent stiffness matrix combining plane stresses and transverse distortion and $\alpha$ represents the set of internal variables when the behaviour is non-linear.

For behaviours where the transverse distortions are uncoupled from the membrane and bending strains, $\text{C}(e,\alpha )$ is put in the form:

$$
\text{C}=(\begin{array}{cc}\text{H}& 0\\ 0& {\text{H}}_{\gamma}\end{array})
$$

where $\text{H}(e,\alpha )$ is a $3\times 3$ matrix and ${\text{H}}_{\gamma}(e,\alpha )$ a $2\times 2$ matrix. One will remain within the framework of this assumption.

For a homogeneous isotropic linear elastic behaviour, one thus has:

$$
\text{C}=\frac{E}{1-{v}^{2}}(\begin{array}{ccccc}1& v& 0& 0& 0\\ v& 1& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-v}{2}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{k(1-v)}{2}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& \frac{k(1-v)}{2}\end{array})
$$

where $k$ is the transverse-shear correction factor whose meaning is given in the following paragraph.

**Note:**

*One does not describe the variation of the thickness nor that of the transverse strain* ${e}_{zz}$ *which one can however compute by using the previous plane-stress assumption. Moreover no restriction is made on the type of behaviour that one can represent.*

#### 2.2.3 Taking transverse shear into account [2]

The taking into account of transverse shear depends on correction factors determined a priori by energy equivalences with 3D models, so that the transverse shear stiffness of the plate model is as close as possible to that defined by the theory of three-dimensional elasticity. Two theories including the strain due to the shear force exist and are presented in [2].

##### 2.2.3.1 The so-called Hencky theory

This theory as well as that of Love-Kirchhoff which follows from it immediately rests on the kinematics presented in §2.2.1. The constitutive relation is the usual one and the shear correction factor is equal to $k=1$ .

**Note:**

*When one does not take into account the transverse distortions* ${\gamma}_{x}$ *and* ${\gamma}_{y}$ *in the Hencky theory, the model obtained is that of Love-Kirchhoff (finite elements DKT(G) and DKQ(G)). The two rotations of the mean surface are then linked to the displacements of the mean surface by the following relation:*

$\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}=-\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ {\beta}_{y}=-\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\end{array}$

##### 2.2.3.2 The so-called Reissner theory (DST, DSQ and Q4g)

The second theory, called Reissner’s, is developed from the stresses. The variation of the membrane stresses (${\sigma}_{xx}$ , ${\sigma}_{yy}$ and ${\sigma}_{xy}$ ) is assumed linear in the thickness as in the case of the Hencky theory where this results from the linearity of the variation of the membrane strains with the thickness.

However, whereas one assumes, in the Hencky theory, that the distortion is constant in the thickness and therefore the shear stresses, which violates the boundary conditions ${\sigma}_{xz}={\sigma}_{yz}=0$ on the upper and lower faces of the plate because of the constitutive law stated in §2.2.2., one uses within the framework of the Reissner theory the equilibrium equations in order to deduce from them the variation of the shear stresses in the thickness of the plate, respecting in particular the equilibrium conditions on the upper and lower faces of the plate. The internal energy of the model obtained after solution of the equilibrium equations in 3D, for bending only, with the variation of the plane stresses along $z$ , brings out, for an elastic material, a relation between the stress resultants and the mean rotations and deflection. It is in this relation that the shear correction factor of $k=5/6$ appears instead of $1$ in the relation which links the shear force to the distortion for a homogeneous and isotropic plate. The determination of the shear correction factors for orthotropic plates or laminated plates is described in the appendix.

##### 2.2.3.3 Equivalence of the Hencky-Love-Kirchhoff and Reissner approaches

If one assimilates the slopes of the mean surface ${\beta}_{x},{\beta}_{y}$ to the means of the slopes in the thickness of the plate and the deflection $w$ to the mean deflection, the only difference between the Hencky theory and that of Reissner is the transverse shear correction coefficient in homogeneous isotropic elasticity of $5/6$ instead of $1$ . This difference is due to the fact that the starting assumptions are of a different nature and above all that the variables chosen are not the same. Indeed, the deflection on the mean surface is not equal to the mean of the deflections over the thickness of the plate. It is therefore normal that constitutive relations which involve different variables are not identical.

The fact of having to solve at the finite-element level problems in displacements rather than problems in stresses by interpolation of the displacements leads us to use the equivalent approach in displacements of the Reissner problem formulated in stresses.

##### 2.2.3.4 Notes

Because of the previous equivalence one presents here only the displacement model for all the elements. In fact the elements DKT, DKTG, DKQG and DKQ rest on the Hencky-Love-Kirchhoff theory and the elements DST, DSQ and Q4G rest on the Reissner theory.

The determination of the correction factors rests, within the framework of another theory, that of Mindlin, on equivalences of eigenfrequency associated with the vibration mode by transverse shear. One then obtains $k={\pi}^{2}/12$ , a value very close to $5/6$ for the elements DST, DSQ and Q4G in the isotropic case.

Within the framework of plasticity the problem of the choice of the transverse-shear correction coefficient arises because the equivalent approach in displacements of the Reissner problem formulated in stresses involves the non-linearity of the behaviour. One cannot therefore deduce, as is the case for elastic materials, a value of the transverse-shear correction coefficient. Plasticity (and other non-linear behaviours) is therefore not developed for these elements.

##### 2.2.3.5 Computation of the shear stress and of the transverse distortion in code_aster

The computation of the shear stress is carried out by considering:

- the equilibrium equations in stress and in generalised force:

$$
\begin{array}{}{\partial}_{z}{\tau}_{xz}={\partial}_{x}{\sigma}_{xx}+{\partial}_{y}{\sigma}_{xy}\\ {\partial}_{z}{\tau}_{yz}={\partial}_{y}{\sigma}_{yy}+{\partial}_{x}{\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}
\textrm{;}
\begin{array}{}{\partial}_{x}{M}_{xx}+{\partial}_{y}{M}_{xy}={T}_{x}\\ {\partial}_{y}{M}_{yy}+{\partial}_{x}{M}_{xy}={T}_{y}\end{array}
$$

- the free-edge conditions ${\sigma}_{xz}(-h/2)={\sigma}_{yz}(h/2)=0$ on the upper and lower faces;
- relations linking the plane stresses to the derivatives of the moments:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\partial}_{x}{\sigma}_{xx}=12/{h}^{3}\cdot z\cdot {\partial}_{x}\mathit{Mxx}\\ {\partial}_{x}{\sigma}_{yy}=12/{h}^{3}\cdot z\cdot {\partial}_{y}\mathit{Myy}\\ {\partial}_{x}{\sigma}_{xy}=12/{h}^{3}\cdot z\cdot {\partial}_{y}\mathit{Mxy}\end{array}
$$

That is, after analytical integration with respect to the variable $z$ of ${\sigma}_{xz}(z),{\sigma}_{yz}(z)$ and identification of the coefficients of the primitive:

- the expression of the shear stress:

${\sigma}_{xz}={T}_{x}\cdot 3/2h\ast (1-4{z}^{2}/{h}^{2})$ ${\sigma}_{yz}={T}_{y}\cdot 3/2h\cdot (1-4{z}^{2}/{h}^{2})$

- the expression of the shear strain within the framework of the Reissner theory:

${\gamma}_{x}={\tau}_{xz}\cdot 2(1+\nu )/\mathit{Ek}$ ${\gamma}_{y}={\tau}_{yz}\cdot 2(1+\nu )/\mathrm{Ek}$ with $k$ the shear correction coefficient

- the expression of the shear strain within the framework of the Kirchhoff theory:

${\gamma}_{x}={\tau}_{xz}\cdot 2(1+\nu )/\mathrm{Ek}-{\partial}_{x}w$ ${\gamma}_{y}={\tau}_{yz}\cdot 2(1+\nu )/\mathit{Ek}-{\partial}_{y}w$

This is an approximation which one also finds in [3] (eq 9).

One generalises the expression of the shear stresses by introducing a function $d1\mathit{iel}(z)$ such that:

${\sigma}_{xz}={T}_{x}\cdot d1\mathit{iel}(z)$ ${\sigma}_{yz}={T}_{y}\cdot d1\mathit{iel}(z)$

In the classical cases, one has: $d1\mathit{iel}(z)=3/(2h)\cdot (1-4{z}^{2}/{h}^{2})$ .

In more general cases (in the presence of the offset for example) $d1\mathit{iel}(z)$ must be modified in order to take into account the phenomenon present. In order to approximate the shear stress correctly, one chooses to postulate a general quadratic form for $d1\mathit{iel}(z)=a\cdot {z}^{2}+b\cdot z+c$ such that the following conditions are satisfied:

- $\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}d1\mathit{iel}(z)=1$ shear-force–stress relation
- $d1\mathit{iel}(z=-h/2)=0;d1\mathit{iel}(z=h/2)=0$ free-edge condition

The first condition makes it possible to satisfy automatically the equilibrium relations in linear elasticity whereas the second makes it possible to recover correct results on the unconstrained edges. The extension of these relations to plasticity is not trivial. However, in plasticity, one chooses to keep a linear elastic description of the shear stresses.

The expression of $d1\mathit{iel}(z)$ in the case of the offset is made explicit in the dedicated reference document [R3.07.06].

## 3 Principle of virtual work

### 3.1 Strain work

The general expression of the 3D strain work for a plate is:

$$
{W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}({\varepsilon}_{xx}{\sigma}_{xx}+{\varepsilon}_{yy}{\sigma}_{yy}+{\gamma}_{xy}{\sigma}_{xy}+{\gamma}_{x}{\sigma}_{xz}+{\gamma}_{y}{\sigma}_{yz})\text{dV}
$$

where $S$ is the mean surface and the position in the thickness of the plate varies between $–h/2$ and $+h/2$ .

#### 3.1.1 Expression of the stress resultants

By adopting the kinematics of §2.2.1, one identifies the work of the internal forces:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}({e}_{xx}{N}_{xx}+{e}_{yy}{N}_{yy}+2{e}_{xy}{N}_{xy}+{\kappa}_{xx}{M}_{xx}+{\kappa}_{yy}{M}_{yy}+2{\kappa}_{xy}{M}_{xy}+{\gamma}_{x}{T}_{x}+{\gamma}_{y}{T}_{y})\text{dS}$ where:

$$
N=(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {N}_{xy}\end{array})=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array})\text{dz}
$$

${N}_{xx},{N}_{yy},{N}_{xy}$ are the membrane stress resultants (in $N/m$ );

${M}_{xx},{M}_{yy},{M}_{xy}$ are the bending stress resultants or moments (in $N$ );

${T}_{x},{T}_{y}$ are the shear stress resultants or shear forces (in $N/m$ ).

#### 3.1.2 Stress-resultant–strain relation

The expression of the strain work is also written:

$$
{W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{s}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}[\varepsilon \text{C}(e,\alpha )\varepsilon ]\text{dV}=\underset{s}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}[\text{eCe}+z\text{eC}\kappa +z\kappa \text{Ce}+{z}^{2}\kappa \text{C}\kappa +\gamma \text{C}\gamma ]\text{dV}
$$

where $\text{C}(e,\alpha )$ is the local constitutive matrix.

By using the expression obtained for ${W}_{\text{def}}$ in the previous paragraph one finds the following relation between the stress resultants and the strains:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\text{N}={\text{H}}_{m}\text{e}+{\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\kappa \\ \text{M}={\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\text{e}+{\text{H}}_{f}\kappa \\ \text{T}={\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}
$$

where:

${H}_{\text{ct}}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{G}_{11}& 0\\ 0& {G}_{22}\end{array}\right)$     $\text{e}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ 2{e}_{xy}\end{array}\right),\kappa =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\end{array}\right),\gamma =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array}\right)$

The matrices ${\text{H}}_{m},{\text{H}}_{f}\text{et}{\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ are the stiffness matrices in membrane, bending and transverse shear, respectively. The matrix ${\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}$ is a stiffness matrix of coupling between the membrane and the bending.

For a homogeneous isotropic elastic behaviour of the plate these matrices have the expression:

$$
{\text{H}}_{m}=\frac{\text{Eh}}{1-{v}^{2}}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& v& 0\\ v& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-v}{2}\end{array}\right),{\text{H}}_{f}=\frac{{\text{Eh}}^{3}}{12(1-{v}^{2})}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& v& 0\\ v& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-v}{2}\end{array}\right),{\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}=\frac{\text{kEh}}{2(1+v)}\left(\begin{array}{cc}1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right)
$$

and ${\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}=0$ because there is material symmetry with respect to the plane $z=0$ .

For an orthotropic material, the behaviour is given in the appendix.

#### 3.1.3 Elastic internal energy of the plate

Given the previous remarks, the elastic internal energy of the plate is expressed more usually for this type of geometry in the following manner:

$$
{\Phi}_{int}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}[\text{e}({\text{H}}_{m}\text{e}+{\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\kappa )+\kappa ({\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\text{e}+{\text{H}}_{f}\kappa )+\gamma {\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}\gamma ]\text{dS}
$$

which one can decompose in the following manner:

${\Phi}_{\text{membrane}}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}\text{e}{\text{H}}_{m}\text{e}\text{dS}$     membrane energy

${\Phi}_{\text{flexion}}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}\kappa {\text{H}}_{m}\kappa \text{dS}$     bending energy

${\Phi}_{\text{cisaillement}}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}\gamma {\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}\gamma \text{dS}$     shear energy

${\Phi}_{\text{couplage}}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}(\text{e}{\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\kappa +\kappa {\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}\text{e})\text{dS}$     membrane-bending coupling energy

#### 3.1.4 Notes

The relations linking ${\text{H}}_{m}$ , ${\text{H}}_{f}$ , ${\text{H}}_{\mathit{mf}}$ to $\text{H}$ and ${\text{H}}_{\mathrm{ct}}$ to ${\text{H}}_{\gamma}$ are valid whatever the constitutive law: elastic, with anelastic strains (thermoelasticity, plasticity, ....).

For a plate made up of $N$ orthotropic layers in elasticity, the matrices ${\text{H}}_{m}$ , ${\text{H}}_{f}$ , ${\text{H}}_{\mathrm{mf}}$ and ${\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}$ are written:

$$
{\text{H}}_{m}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{h}_{i}{\text{H}}_{i},{\text{H}}_{\mathit{mf}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{h}_{i}{\eta}_{i}{\text{H}}_{i},{\text{H}}_{f}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{3}({z}_{i+1}^{3}-{z}_{i}^{3}){\text{H}}_{i},{\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{h}_{i}{\text{H}}_{\gamma i}
$$

where: ${h}_{i}={z}_{i+1}-{z}_{i},{\eta}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\left({z}_{i+1}+{z}_{i}\right)$ and ${\text{H}}_{i},{\text{H}}_{\gamma i}$ represent the matrices $\text{H}$ and ${\text{H}}_{\gamma}$ for the layer $i$ .

Homogenisation for multilayer shells can lead to membrane and bending stiffness matrices which are not proportional, of the type:

$$
{\text{H}}_{m}=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}{C}_{1111}& {C}_{1122}& 0\\ {C}_{1122}& {C}_{2222}& 0\\ 0& 0& {C}_{1212}\end{array}\right),{\text{H}}_{f}=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}{D}_{1111}& {D}_{1122}& 0\\ {D}_{1122}& {D}_{2222}& 0\\ 0& 0& {D}_{1212}\end{array}\right),{\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{G}_{11}& 0\\ 0& {G}_{22}\end{array}\right)
$$

for which one cannot recover equivalent values of Young's modulus and of the thickness making it possible to recover the classical expressions of the stiffness, cf. [4].

### 3.2 Work of the external forces and couples

The work of the forces and couples acting on the plate is expressed in the following manner:

$$
{W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{F}}_{v}.\text{U}\text{dV}+\underset{S}{\int}{\text{F}}_{s}.\text{U}\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{F}}_{c}.\text{U}\text{dz}\text{ds}
$$

where ${\text{F}}_{v},{\text{F}}_{s},{\text{F}}_{c}$ are the body, surface and contour forces acting on the plate, respectively. $C$ is the part of the contour of the plate on which the contour forces ${\text{F}}_{c}$ are applied. With the kinematics of §2.2.1, one thus determines:

$\begin{array}{}{W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}u+{f}_{y}v+{f}_{z}w+{c}_{x}{\theta}_{x}+{c}_{y}{\theta}_{y})\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}({\phi }_{x}u+{\phi }_{y}v+{\phi }_{z}w+{\chi }_{x}{\theta}_{x}+{\chi }_{y}{\theta}_{y})\text{ds}\\ =\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}u+{f}_{y}v+{f}_{z}w+{c}_{y}{\beta}_{x}-{c}_{x}{\beta}_{y})\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}({\phi }_{x}u+{\phi }_{y}v+{\phi }_{z}w+{\chi }_{y}{\beta}_{x}-{\chi }_{x}{\beta}_{y})\text{ds}\end{array}$

- where there are present on the plate:

${f}_{x},{f}_{y},{f}_{z}$ : surface forces acting along $x,y$  and $z$

${f}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{F}}_{v}.{\text{e}}_{i}\text{dz}+{\text{F}}_{s}.{\text{e}}_{i}$ where ${\text{e}}_{x}$ and ${\text{e}}_{y}$ are the basis vectors of the tangent plane and ${\text{e}}_{z}$ their normal vector.

${c}_{x},{c}_{y}$ : surface couples acting about the axes $x$ and $y$ .

${c}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}(z{\text{e}}_{z}\wedge {\text{F}}_{v}).{\text{e}}_{i}\text{dz}+(\pm \frac{h}{2}{\text{e}}_{z}\wedge {\text{F}}_{s}).{\text{e}}_{i}$ where ${\text{e}}_{x},{\text{e}}_{y},{\text{e}}_{z}$ are the basis vectors previously defined.

- and where there are present on the contour of the plate:

${\phi }_{x},{\phi }_{y},{\phi }_{z}$ : line forces acting along $x,y$ and $z$

${\varphi}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{F}}_{c}.{\text{e}}_{i}\text{dz}$ where ${\text{e}}_{x},{\text{e}}_{y},{\text{e}}_{z}$ are the basis vectors previously defined.

${\mathrm{\chi }}_{x},{\mathrm{\chi }}_{y}$ : line couples about the axes $x$ and $y$ .

${\mathrm{\chi }}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}(z{\text{e}}_{z}\wedge {\text{F}}_{c}).{\text{e}}_{i}\text{dz}$ where ${\text{e}}_{x},{\text{e}}_{y},{\text{e}}_{z}$ are the basis vectors previously defined.

**Note:**

*The moments with respect to* $z$ *are zero.*

### 3.3 Principle of virtual work

It is written in the following manner: $\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}=\delta {W}_{\text{def}}$ for all kinematically admissible virtual displacements and rotations.

#### 3.3.1 Hencky kinematics

With this kinematics, there result after integration by parts of the strain work the following static equilibrium equations of the plates:

- For the forces: $\begin{array}{}{N}_{xx,x}+{N}_{xy,y}+{f}_{x}=0,\\ {N}_{yy,y}+{N}_{xy,x}+{f}_{y}=0,\\ {T}_{x,x}+{T}_{y,y}+{f}_{z}=0.\end{array}$
- For the couples: $\begin{array}{}{M}_{xx,x}+{M}_{xy,y}-{T}_{x}+{c}_{y}=0,\\ {M}_{yy,y}+{M}_{xy,x}-{T}_{y}-{c}_{x}=0.\end{array}$

as well as the following boundary conditions on the contour $C$ of $S$ :

$\begin{array}{}{N}_{xx}{n}_{x}+{N}_{xy}{n}_{y}={\phi }_{x}\\ {N}_{yy}{n}_{y}+{N}_{xy}{n}_{x}={\phi }_{y}\\ {T}_{x}{n}_{x}+{T}_{y}{n}_{y}={\phi }_{z},\\ {M}_{xx}{n}_{x}+{M}_{xy}{n}_{y}={\chi }_{y}\\ {M}_{yy}{n}_{y}+{M}_{xy}{n}_{x}=-{\chi }_{x}\end{array}$ where $\begin{array}{}u=\bar{u}\\ v=\bar{v}\\ w=\bar{w}\\ {\beta}_{x}={\bar{\theta}}_{y}\\ {\beta}_{y}=-{\bar{\theta}}_{x}\end{array}$

where ${n}_{x}$ and ${n}_{y}$ are the direction cosines of the normal to $C$ directed towards the outside of the plate.and $\overline{u}$ denotes the trace of $u$ on C $.$

#### 3.3.2 Love-Kirchhoff kinematics

One recalls that within the framework of this kinematics, one has the following relation linking the derivative of the deflection to the rotations: $\begin{array}{}{\beta}_{x}=-\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ {\beta}_{y}=-\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\end{array}$ . After a double integration by parts of the strain work, one obtains the following static equilibrium equations:

- For the membrane forces: $\begin{array}{}{N}_{xx,x}+{N}_{xy,y}+{f}_{x}=0,\\ {N}_{yy,y}+{N}_{xy,x}+{f}_{y}=0,\end{array}$
- For the bending and transverse shear forces:

$\begin{array}{}{M}_{xx,xx}+{\mathrm{2M}}_{xy,xy}+{M}_{yy,yy}+{f}_{z}+{c}_{y,x}-{c}_{x,y}=0,\\ {M}_{xx,x}+{M}_{xy,y}-{T}_{x}+{c}_{y}=0,\\ {M}_{yy,y}+{M}_{xy,x}-{T}_{y}-{c}_{x}=0.\end{array}$

as well as the boundary conditions on the contour $C$ and at the corner points $O$ of the contour $C$ of $S$ :

$\begin{array}{}{N}_{xx}{n}_{x}+{N}_{xy}{n}_{y}={\phi }_{x},\\ {N}_{yy}{n}_{y}+{N}_{xy}{n}_{x}={\phi }_{y},\\ {T}_{n}+{M}_{\text{ns},s}={\phi }_{z}-{\chi }_{n,s},\\ {M}_{\text{nn}}={\chi }_{s},\\ {M}_{\text{ns}}(O+)-{M}_{\text{ns}}(O-)\text{=-}[{\chi }_{n}(O+)-{\chi }_{n}(O-)].\end{array}$ where $\begin{array}{}u=\bar{u}\\ v=\bar{v}\\ w=\bar{w}\\ {\beta}_{n}=-{\bar{w}}_{,n}={\bar{\theta}}_{s}\end{array}$

with $\begin{array}{}{T}_{n}={T}_{x}{n}_{x}+{T}_{y}{n}_{y},\\ {M}_{\text{nn}}={M}_{xx}{n}_{x}^{2}+{\mathrm{2M}}_{xy}{n}_{x}{n}_{y}+{M}_{yy}{n}_{y}^{2},\\ {M}_{\text{ns}}\text{=-}{M}_{xx}{n}_{x}{n}_{y}+{M}_{xy}({n}_{x}^{2}-{n}_{y}^{2})+{M}_{yy}{n}_{x}{n}_{y}.\end{array}$ .

## 4 Numerical discretisation of the variational formulation coming from the principle of virtual work

### 4.1 Introduction

By exploiting the constitutive law, the virtual work of the internal forces is written (with ${H}_{\text{mf}}=0$ up to §4.4, which takes nothing away from the generality of the following results, but makes it possible to lighten the notations):

$\delta {W}_{int}=\underset{S}{\int}(\delta {\text{eH}}_{m}\text{e}+\delta \kappa {\text{H}}_{f}\kappa +\delta \gamma {\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}\gamma )\text{dS}$

with: $\text{e}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{,x}\\ {v}_{,y}\\ {u}_{,y}+{v}_{,x}\end{array}\right),\kappa =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x,x}\\ {\beta}_{y,y}\\ {\beta}_{x,y}+{\beta}_{y,x}\end{array}\right),\gamma =\left(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,x}+{\beta}_{x}\\ {w}_{,y}+{\beta}_{y}\end{array}\right).$

It follows that the plate elements are elements with five degrees of freedom per node. These degrees of freedom are the displacements in the plane of the element $u$ and $v$ , out of plane $w$ and the two rotations ${\beta}_{x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}$ .

The DKT, DKTG and DST elements are triangular elements. The DKQ, DKQG, DSQ and $\text{Q4}\gamma$ elements are quadrilateral elements. They are shown below:

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.03/10000000000002AA000001B8613D4A557591B3AA.png)

**Figure 4.1-a: Actual elements**

The reference elements are shown below:

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.03/10000000000003F8000001C4DBC199C1488BAB84.png)

**Figure 4.1-b: Triangle and quadrangle reference elements**

The reduced frame of the element is defined as the frame $(\xi ,\eta )$ of the reference element. The local frame of the element, in its plane $(x,y)$, is defined by the user. The direction $\mathit{X1}$ of this local frame is the projection of a reference direction $\underline{d}$ onto the plane of the element. This reference direction $\underline{d}$ is chosen by the user, who defines it by two nautical angles in the global frame. The normal $\text{N}$ to the plane of the element ($12\wedge 13$  for a triangle numbered 123 and $12\mathrm{\wedge }14$ for a quadrangle numbered 1234) fixes the second direction. The cross product of the two previously defined vectors $\text{Y1}=\text{N}\mathrm{\wedge }\text{X1}$ makes it possible to define the local triad in which the generalised forces representing the stress state will be expressed. The user must take care that the chosen reference axis does not end up parallel to the normal of certain plate elements. By default, the reference direction $\underline{d}$ is the $X$ axis of the global frame in which the mesh is defined.

The essential difference between the DKT, DKQ, DKTG and DKQG elements on the one hand and the DST, DSQ and $\text{Q4}\gamma$ elements on the other comes from the fact that, for the former, the transverse distortion is zero, that is $\gamma =0$ . The difference between $\text{Q4}\gamma$ and the DST and DSQ elements comes from a different choice of interpolation for the representation of the transverse shear.

### 4.2 Discretisation of the displacement field

If the displacement fields are discretised in the usual manner for isoparametric elements, that is:

$u=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}{u}_{i},v=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}{v}_{i},w=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}{w}_{i},{\beta}_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}{\beta}_{xi},{\beta}_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i}{\beta}_{\text{yi}},$

and this discretisation is introduced into the variational formulation of §4.1, a transverse-shear locking results, analysed in [1], which makes the bending solution controlled by the transverse-shear effects, and not by bending, when the thickness of the plate becomes small with respect to its characteristic dimension.

To remedy this drawback, the variational form presented in the introduction is slightly modified so that:

$$
\delta {W}_{int}=\underset{S}{\int}(\delta {\text{eH}}_{m}\text{e}+\delta \kappa {\text{H}}_{f}\kappa +\delta \overline{\gamma}{\text{H}}_{\mathit{ct}}\overline{\gamma})\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}(\delta {\text{eH}}_{m}\text{e}+\delta \kappa {\text{H}}_{f}\kappa +\delta {\text{TH}}_{\mathit{ct}}^{-1}\text{T})\text{dS}
$$

where $\overline{\gamma}$ are substitute strains satisfying $\overline{\gamma}=\gamma ` in a weak sense (integral over the sides of the element) and such that $\text{T}={\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}\overline{\gamma}$ . One thus checks that on the sides $ij$ of the element $\underset{i}{\overset{j}{\int}}({\overline{\gamma}}_{s}-{\gamma}_{s})\text{ds}=0$ with ${\gamma}_{s}={w}_{,s}+{\beta}_{s}$ .

Two approaches are then possible; in the first, that of the element $\text{Q4}\gamma ` , one uses the bilinear discretisation of the displacement fields and the fact that $\overline{\gamma}$ is constant on the sides of the element. The relations on the sides $ij$ then make it possible to express the values of $\overline{\gamma}$ on the sides as a function of the bending degrees of freedom. In the second approach, which is that of the elements of DKT, DKTG and DST type, one uses the weak formulation of the previous paragraph, which makes it possible to link the bending to the shear forces in order to deduce the interpolation of the bending terms.

$$

### 4.3 Discretisation of the strain field

The Jacobian matrix $\text{J}(\xi ,\eta )$ is:

$$
\text{J}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{x}_{,\xi }& {y}_{,\xi }\\ {x}_{,\eta }& {y}_{,\eta }\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\xi }{x}_{i}& \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\xi }{y}_{i}\\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\eta }{x}_{i}& \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\eta }{y}_{i}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11}& {J}_{12}\\ {J}_{21}& {J}_{22}\end{array}\right)
$$

Moreover:

$$
\left(\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\end{array}\right)=j\left(\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi }\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial \eta }\end{array}\right)\text{avec}j=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{j}_{11}& {j}_{12}\\ {j}_{21}& {j}_{22}\end{array}\right)={\text{J}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{J}\left(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{22}& -{J}_{12}\\ -{J}_{21}& {J}_{11}\end{array}\right)\text{où}J=\det\text{J}={J}_{11}{J}_{22}-{J}_{12}{J}_{21}
$$

One recalls that the displacement field is discretised by:

$$
\left(\begin{array}{c}u\\ v\end{array}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}\left(\xi ,\eta \right)\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}^{k}\\ {v}^{k}\end{array}\right)
$$

the term in brackets being present for the elements of DKT, DKTG and DST type, but not for the elements $\text{Q4}\gamma$ .

**Note:**

*In* *code_aster, the Jacobian matrix makes it possible to pass from the reference element to the local frame of the element (and not the global frame) because it is easier to work in this frame.*

#### 4.3.1 Discretisation of the membrane strain field :

$$
\begin{array}{}{e}_{xx}={u}_{,x}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,x}(\xi ,\eta ){u}^{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}({j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }){u}^{k},\\ {e}_{yy}={v}_{,y}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,y}(\xi ,\eta ){v}^{k}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}({j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }){v}^{k},\\ 2{e}_{xy}={u}_{,x}+{v}_{,y}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,y}(\xi ,\eta ){u}^{k}+{N}_{k,x}(\xi ,\eta ){v}^{k}\\ =\sum_{k=1}^{N}({j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }){u}^{k}+({j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }){v}^{k}\end{array}
$$

That is, in matrix form:

$(\begin{array}{}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ 2{e}_{xy}\end{array})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\text{B}}_{\text{mk}}{\text{U}}_{k}$ where ${\text{U}}_{k}=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{k}\\ {v}_{k}\end{array})$ is the membrane displacement field at the node $k$

and:

${B}_{\text{mk}}=(\begin{array}{cc}{j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }& 0\\ 0& {j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }\\ {j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }& {j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }\end{array})$

The transfer matrix from the membrane strains to the displacement field ${U}_{m}=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}\\ {v}_{1}\\ \mathrm{...}\\ {u}_{N}\\ {v}_{N}\end{array})$ in the plane of the element is thus written: ${\text{B}}_{m[3\times \text{2N}]}=\left({\text{B}}_{{}^{\text{m1}}}\mathrm{...}{\text{B}}_{{}^{\text{mN}}}\right)$ .

#### 4.3.2 Discretisation of the transverse distortion

##### 4.3.2.1 For the Q4g finite elements

One discretises linearly the field ${\overline{\gamma}}^{}$, constant per side, so that:

${\overline{\gamma}}^{}=j{\overline{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}$

${\overline{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{\overline{\gamma}}_{\xi}\\ {\overline{\gamma}}_{\eta}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}\frac{1-\eta }{2}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{12}+\frac{1+\eta }{2}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{34}\\ \frac{1-\xi }{2}{\gamma}_{\eta}^{23}+\frac{1-\xi }{2}{\gamma}_{\eta}^{41}\end{array}\right)$ where ${\overline{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}$ is the transverse-distortion field in the reference element.

By then using the relations (cf.  4.2 ): $\begin{array}{c}\underset{-1}{\overset{+1}{\int}}({\overline{\gamma}}_{\xi}-({w}_{,\xi }+{\beta}_{\xi}))d\xi =0;\\ \underset{-1}{\overset{+1}{\int}}({\overline{\gamma}}_{\eta}-({w}_{,\eta }+{\beta}_{\eta}))d\eta =0\end{array}$ for $\begin{array}{c}\xi =\pm 1\\ \eta =\pm 1\end{array}$ ,

one establishes that: $\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({w}_{j}-{w}_{i}+{\beta}_{\xi i}+{\beta}_{\xi j});\\ {\gamma}_{\eta}^{\text{kp}}=\frac{1}{2}({w}_{p}-{w}_{k}+{\beta}_{\eta p}+{\beta}_{\eta k});\end{array}$ for $(ij)\in (12,34)$ and $(\mathit{kp})\in (23,41)$ .

By substituting the two results above into the expression for ${\bar{\gamma}}^{\mathit{loc}}$ , one deduces that:

${\bar{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}=(\begin{array}{c}{\bar{\gamma}}_{\xi}\\ {\bar{\gamma}}_{\eta}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{\text{B}}_{\xi}^{\mathit{ref}}{u}_{\xi}^{\mathit{ref}}\\ {\text{B}}_{\eta}^{\mathit{ref}}{u}_{\eta}^{\mathit{ref}}\end{array})={\text{B}}_{\mathit{ref}}^{}{u}_{\mathit{ref}}^{}$ where ${u}_{\mathit{ref}}^{}=(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\xi 1}\\ {\beta}_{\eta 1}\\ \mathrm{⋮}\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\xi N}\\ {\beta}_{\eta N}\end{array})$ and ${\text{B}}_{\mathit{ref}}^{}=({\text{B}}_{1}^{},\mathrm{...},{\text{B}}_{N}^{})$ with ${\text{B}}_{k}^{}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k,\xi }& {\xi}_{k}{N}_{k,\xi }& 0\\ {N}_{k,\eta }& 0& {\eta}_{k}{N}_{k,\eta }\end{array})$ , $N=4$ , $k\in [1,N]$ , ${\xi}_{k}$ , ${\eta}_{k}$ are defined in  4.2.1 .

The rotations given here in the reference element must now be expressed as a function of the rotations in the local frame.

As $(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{\xi k}\\ {\beta}_{\eta k}\end{array})={J}_{k}(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{\mathit{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathit{yk}}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11k}& {J}_{12k}\\ {J}_{21k}& {J}_{22k}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{\mathit{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathit{yk}}\end{array})$ one deduces that ${\bar{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}={\text{B}}_{\mathit{loc}}^{}{u}_{\mathit{loc}}^{}$ where ${u}_{\mathit{loc}}^{}=(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathit{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathit{y1}}\\ \mathrm{⋮}\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})$ and ${\text{B}}_{\mathit{loc}}=({\text{B}}_{\mathit{loc}1},\mathrm{...},{\text{B}}_{\mathit{loc}N})$ with ${\text{B}}_{\mathit{loc}k}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k,\xi }& {\xi}_{k}{N}_{k,\xi }{J}_{11k}& {\xi}_{k}{N}_{k,\xi }{J}_{12k}\\ {N}_{k,\eta }& {\eta}_{k}{N}_{k,\eta }{J}_{21k}& {\eta}_{k}{N}_{k,\eta }{J}_{22k}\end{array})$ . One will note that the Jacobian matrix ${J}_{k}$ is expressed at each point of the element.

Finally: $\bar{\gamma}=(\begin{array}{c}{\bar{\gamma}}_{x}\\ {\bar{\gamma}}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{j}_{11}& {j}_{12}\\ {j}_{21}& {j}_{22}\end{array}){\bar{\gamma}}^{\mathit{ref}}={\text{B}}_{c}{u}_{\mathit{loc}}$ with ${\text{B}}_{c[2\times \text{3N}]}={\text{jB}}_{\mathit{loc}}$ .

##### 4.3.2.2 For the finite elements of DKT, DST and DKTG type

where ${P}_{\text{xk}}$ , ${P}_{\text{yk}}$ and ${\alpha}_{k}$ are defined in 4.2.2.

that is, again in matrix form:

$\text{T}=\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x,xx}\\ {\beta}_{x,yy}\\ {\beta}_{x,xy}\\ {\beta}_{y,xx}\\ {\beta}_{y,yy}\\ {\beta}_{y,xy}\end{array})=$

$\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\begin{array}{ccc}0& {j}_{11}^{2}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{11}{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}^{2}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }& 0\\ 0& {j}_{21}^{2}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{21}{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{22}^{2}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }& 0\\ 0& {j}_{11}{j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+[{j}_{11}{j}_{22}+{j}_{12}{j}_{21}]{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }& 0\\ 0& 0& {j}_{11}^{2}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{11}{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}^{2}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }\\ 0& 0& {j}_{21}^{2}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{21}{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{22}^{2}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }\\ 0& 0& {j}_{11}{j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi \xi }+[{j}_{11}{j}_{22}+{j}_{12}{j}_{21}]{N}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta \eta }\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{yk}}\end{array})$ $+\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}\sum_{k=1}^{2N}{\alpha}_{k}(\begin{array}{c}{C}_{k}({j}_{11}^{2}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{11}{j}_{12}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}^{2}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\\ {C}_{k}({j}_{21}^{2}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{21}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{22}^{2}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\\ {C}_{k}({j}_{11}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+[{j}_{11}{j}_{22}+{j}_{12}{j}_{21}]{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\\ {S}_{k}({j}_{11}^{2}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{11}{j}_{12}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}^{2}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\\ {S}_{k}({j}_{21}^{2}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+2{j}_{21}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{22}^{2}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\\ {S}_{k}({j}_{11}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }+[{j}_{11}{j}_{22}+{j}_{12}{j}_{21}]{P}_{k,\xi \eta }+{j}_{12}{j}_{22}{P}_{k,\eta \eta })\end{array})=$

$\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\text{P}}_{f\beta k}(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{yk}}\end{array})+\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N}\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}{\text{T}}_{2}(\begin{array}{c}{C}_{k}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }\\ {C}_{k}{P}_{k,\eta \eta }\\ {C}_{k}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }\\ {S}_{k}{P}_{k,\xi \xi }\\ {S}_{k}{P}_{k,\eta \eta }\\ {S}_{k}{P}_{k,\xi \eta }\end{array}){\alpha}_{k}=\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\text{P}}_{f\beta k}{\text{U}}_{f\beta k}+\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}{\text{T}}_{2}\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N}{\text{T}}_{\mathrm{ck}}{\alpha}_{k}=\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}{\text{P}}_{f\beta }{\text{U}}_{f\beta }+\bar{{\text{H}}_{f}}{\text{T}}_{2}{\text{T}}_{\alpha}\alpha$

where ${\text{T}}_{\alpha}=({\text{T}}_{c(N+1)}\mathrm{...}{\text{T}}_{\mathrm{c2N}})$ and ${\text{T}}_{2}=(\begin{array}{cc}{\text{t}}_{2}& 0\\ 0& {\text{t}}_{2}\end{array})$ with ${\text{t}}_{2}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{j}_{11}^{2}& {j}_{12}^{2}& {\mathrm{2j}}_{11}{j}_{12}\\ {j}_{21}^{2}& {j}_{22}^{2}& {\mathrm{2j}}_{21}{j}_{22}\\ {j}_{11}{j}_{21}& {j}_{12}{j}_{22}& {j}_{11}{j}_{22}+{j}_{12}{j}_{21}\end{array})$ .

One then uses the relation $\underset{i}{\overset{j}{\int}}({\bar{\gamma}}_{s}-{\gamma}_{s})\text{ds}=0$ with ${\gamma}_{s}={w}_{,s}+{\beta}_{s}$ for each of the sides ij of the element, which makes it possible to obtain the ${\alpha}_{k}$ since it is also written:

${w}_{j}-{w}_{i}+\frac{{L}_{k}}{2}({C}_{k}{\beta}_{xi}+{S}_{k}{\beta}_{\text{yi}}+{C}_{k}{\beta}_{\text{xj}}+{S}_{k}{\beta}_{\text{yj}})+\frac{2}{3}{L}_{k}{\alpha}_{k}={L}_{k}{\bar{\gamma}}_{\text{sk}}$ where:

${\bar{\gamma}}_{\text{sk}}=(\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{k}& {S}_{k})\bar{\gamma}=(\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{k}& {S}_{k})\end{array}{\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\text{T}=(\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{k}& {S}_{k}){\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\end{array}[{\overline{\text{B}}}_{c\beta }{\text{U}}_{f\beta }+{\overline{\text{B}}}_{c\alpha }\alpha ]\end{array}$

where ${C}_{k}$ , ${S}_{k}$ and ${L}_{k}$ are defined in 4.2.2.

**Note:**

*The terms* ${\bar{\text{B}}}_{c\alpha }$ *and* ${\bar{\text{B}}}_{c\beta }$ *correspond to the integration of the term* ${\bar{\gamma}}_{s}$ *on each of the sides* $ij$ *of the element. The integral is evaluated using two Gauss points of abscissae* $\pm 1/\sqrt{3}$ *and of weight* $1/2$ *in the reference element* $[-1,+1]$ *. Thus the terms* ${\bar{\text{B}}}_{c\alpha }$ *and* ${\bar{\text{B}}}_{c\beta }$ *may be written:*

${\overline{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\alpha }}=\frac{1}{2}\left[{\bar{H}}_{f}{T}_{2}({\text{PG}}_{1}){T}_{\alpha}({\text{PG}}_{1})+{\bar{H}}_{f}{T}_{2}({\text{PG}}_{2}){T}_{\alpha}({\text{PG}}_{2})\right]$ *and*

${\overline{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\beta }}=\frac{1}{2}\left[{\bar{H}}_{f}{P}_{\mathrm{f\beta }}({\text{PG}}_{1})+{\bar{H}}_{f}{P}_{\mathrm{f\beta }}({\text{PG}}_{2})\right]$ *.*

The relation above is also written in matrix form: ${\text{A}}_{\alpha}\alpha ={\text{A}}_{w}{\text{U}}_{\mathrm{f\beta }}$

with: ${\text{A}}_{\alpha}=\frac{2}{3}(\begin{array}{ccc}{L}_{N+1}& 0& 0\\ 0& \ddots & 0\\ 0& 0& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}\end{array})-(\begin{array}{cc}{L}_{N+1}{C}_{N+1}& {L}_{N+1}{S}_{N+1}\\ ⋮& ⋮\\ {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}}\end{array}){\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{\bar{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\alpha }}$

and:

${A}_{w}=-\frac{1}{2}(\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{ccccccccc}-2& {L}_{N+1}{C}_{N+1}& {L}_{N+1}{S}_{N+1}& 2& {L}_{N+1}{C}_{N+1}& {L}_{N+1}{S}_{N+1}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& -2& {L}_{k+1}{C}_{k+1}& {L}_{k+1}{S}_{k+1}& 2& {L}_{k+1}{C}_{k+1}& {L}_{k+1}{S}_{k+1}\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& -2& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}\\ 2& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}}& 0& 0& \cdots & \cdots & 0& 0\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cccc}\cdots & 0& 0& 0\\ \cdots & 0& 0& 0\\ \cdots & 2& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}-1}\\ \cdots & -2& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}}\end{array}\end{array})$

$+(\begin{array}{cc}{L}_{N+1}{C}_{N+1}& {L}_{N+1}{S}_{N+1}\\ ⋮& ⋮\\ {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{C}_{\mathrm{2N}}& {L}_{\mathrm{2N}}{S}_{\mathrm{2N}}\end{array}){\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{\overline{\text{B}}}_{c\beta }$

Thus $\alpha ={\text{A}}_{\beta}{\text{U}}_{\mathrm{f\beta }}\text{avec}{\text{A}}_{\beta}={\text{A}}_{\alpha}^{-1}{\text{A}}_{w}$ , which implies $\text{T}=[{\bar{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\beta }}+{\bar{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\alpha }}{\text{A}}_{\beta}]{\text{U}}_{\mathrm{f\beta }}$ .

**Note:**

*For the DST elements, this expression simplifies somewhat since* ${\bar{\text{B}}}_{\mathrm{c\beta }}=0$ *because of the linearity of the shape functions* ${N}_{k}$ $(k=1,2,3)$ . *This expression is simpler for the DKT, DKTG and DKQ elements since they have no transverse distortion, that is* $\bar{\gamma}$ *= 0, which implies* ${\text{A}}_{\alpha}=(\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& \ddots & 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array})$ *and*

${A}_{w}=-\frac{3}{4}\left(\begin{array}{cccccccccc}-2/{L}_{N+1}& {C}_{N+1}& {S}_{N+1}& 2/{L}_{N+1}& {C}_{N+1}& {S}_{N+1}& 0& 0& 0& \cdots \\ 0& 0& 0& -2/{L}_{k+1}& {C}_{k+1}& {S}_{k+1}& 2/{L}_{k+1}& {C}_{k+1}& {S}_{k+1}& \cdots \\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& -2/{L}_{2N-1}& {C}_{2N-1}& {S}_{2N-1}& \cdots \\ 2/{L}_{2N}& {C}_{2N}& {S}_{2N}& 0& 0& \cdots & \cdots & 0& 0& \cdots \end{array}\begin{array}{cccc}\cdots & 0& 0& 0\\ \cdots & 0& 0& 0\\ \cdots & 2/{L}_{2N-1}& {C}_{2N-1}& {S}_{2N-1}\\ \cdots & -2/{L}_{2N}& {C}_{2N}& {S}_{2N}\end{array}\right)$

*One also notes that for the DKT and DKTG elements the expression of the shear forces is computed from equilibrium and not from the constitutive behaviour (starting from the constitutive behaviour one would find a zero value of the shear forces, which would not make it possible to satisfy equilibrium!). It follows from §3.1.1 that the transverse shear stresses are non-zero through the thickness of the plate whether one is in the DKT or the DST formulation.*

#### 4.3.3 Discretisation of the bending strain field :

##### 4.3.3.1 For the Q4g elements

The relation linking the bending strains to the bending displacement field is written:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{k}_{xx}={\beta}_{x,x}={j}_{11}{\beta}_{x,\xi }+{j}_{12}{\beta}_{x,\eta }={j}_{11}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\xi }{\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{j}_{12}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\eta }{\beta}_{\text{xk}},\\ {\kappa}_{yy}={\beta}_{y,y}={j}_{21}{\beta}_{y,\xi }+{j}_{22}{\beta}_{y,\eta }={j}_{21}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\xi }{\beta}_{\text{yk}}+{j}_{22}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\eta }{\beta}_{\text{yk}},\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}={\beta}_{y,x}+{\beta}_{x,y}={j}_{11}{\beta}_{y,\xi }+{j}_{12}{\beta}_{y,\eta }+{j}_{21}{\beta}_{x,\xi }+{j}_{22}{\beta}_{x,\eta }={j}_{21}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\xi }{\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{j}_{22}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\eta }{\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ +{j}_{11}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\xi }{\beta}_{\text{yk}}+{j}_{12}\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k,\eta }{\beta}_{\text{yk}}.\end{array}

The relation linking the bending strains to the bending displacement field is also written in matrix form:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\text{B}}_{\mathit{f\beta k}}{\text{U}}_{\mathit{f\beta k}}+\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N}{\text{B}}_{f\alpha k}{\text{U}}_{f\alpha k}$ where ${\text{U}}_{\mathit{f\beta k}}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array}\right)$ and ${\text{U}}_{f\alpha k}={\alpha}_{k}$ represent the bending displacement field at node k, so that:

${\text{B}}_{f\beta k}=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}0& {j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }& 0\\ 0& 0& {j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }\\ 0& {j}_{21}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{22}{N}_{k,\eta }& {j}_{11}{N}_{k,\xi }+{j}_{12}{N}_{k,\eta }\end{array}\right)$ and ${\text{B}}_{f\alpha k}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{j}_{11}{P}_{\text{xk},\xi }+{j}_{12}{P}_{\text{xk},\eta }\\ {j}_{21}{P}_{\text{yk},\xi }+{j}_{22}{P}_{\text{yk},\eta }\\ {j}_{11}{P}_{\text{yk},\xi }+{j}_{12}{P}_{\text{yk},\eta }+{j}_{21}{P}_{\text{xk},\xi }+{j}_{22}{P}_{\text{xk},\eta }\end{array}\right)$ .

The transfer matrix from the bending displacement field ${\text{U}}_{f}=({\text{U}}_{f\beta },\alpha )$ with ${\text{U}}_{f\beta }=(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{y1}}\\ ⋮\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})$ and $\alpha =(\begin{array}{c}{\alpha}_{1}\\ ⋮\\ {\alpha}_{N}\end{array})$ to the bending strains is then written:

${\mathrm{B}}_{f[3\times 4N]}=({\mathrm{B}}_{f\beta 1},\cdots ,{\mathrm{B}}_{f\beta N},{\mathrm{B}}_{f\alpha (N+1)},\cdots ,{\mathrm{B}}_{f\alpha 2N})=({\mathrm{B}}_{f\beta [3\times 3N]},{\mathrm{B}}_{f\alpha [3\times N]})$.

### 4.4 Stiffness matrix

The principle of virtual work is written in the following manner: $\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}=\delta {W}_{int}$ that is, again, in elasticity $\delta {\text{U}}^{T}\text{KU}=\text{F}\delta \text{U}$ in matrix form, where $\text{K}$ is the stiffness matrix coming from the assembly, in the global frame, of all the elementary stiffness matrices.

#### 4.4.1 Elementary stiffness matrix for the Q4g elements

$\begin{array}{}\delta {W}_{int}^{e}=\underset{e}{\int}[\delta e({H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}\kappa )+\delta \kappa ({H}_{\text{mf}}e+{H}_{f}\kappa )+\delta \bar{\gamma}{H}_{\text{ct}}\bar{\gamma}]\text{dS}=\\ \underset{e}{\int}(\delta {U}_{m}^{T}{B}_{m}^{T}{H}_{m}{B}_{m}{U}_{m}+\delta {U}_{m}^{T}{B}_{m}^{T}{H}_{\text{mf}}{B}_{f}{U}_{f}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{B}_{f}^{T}{H}_{\text{mf}}{B}_{m}{U}_{m}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{B}_{f}^{T}{H}_{f}{B}_{f}{U}_{f}\\ +\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{B}_{c}^{T}{H}_{\text{ct}}{B}_{c}{U}_{f})\text{dS}=\\ \delta {U}_{m}^{T}(\underset{e}{\int}{B}_{m}^{T}{H}_{m}{B}_{m}\text{dS}){U}_{m}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}(\underset{e}{\int}{B}_{f}^{T}{H}_{f}{B}_{f}\text{dS}){U}_{f}+{\mathrm{dU}}_{m}^{T}(\underset{e}{\int}{B}_{m}^{T}{H}_{\text{mf}}{B}_{f}\text{dS}){U}_{f}\\ +\delta {U}_{f}^{T}(\underset{e}{\int}{B}_{f}^{T}{H}_{\text{mf}}{B}_{m}\text{dS}){U}_{m}\\ +\delta {U}_{f}^{T}(\underset{e}{\int}{B}_{c}^{T}{H}_{\text{ct}}{B}_{c}\text{dS}){U}_{f}=\delta {U}_{m}^{T}{K}_{m}{U}_{m}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{K}_{f}{U}_{f}+\delta {U}_{m}^{T}{K}_{\text{mf}}{U}_{f}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{K}_{\text{fm}}{U}_{m}+\delta {U}_{f}^{T}{K}_{c}{U}_{f}\end{array}$

#### 4.4.4 Assembly of the elementary matrices

The principle of virtual work for the whole set of elements is written:

$$
\delta {W}_{int}=\sum_{e=1}^{\text{nb}\text{elem}}\delta {W}_{int}^{e}=\delta {\text{U}}^{T}\text{KU}
$$

where $\text{U}$ is the set of degrees of freedom of the discretised structure and K comes from the assembly of the elementary matrices.

##### 4.4.4.1 Degrees of freedom

The assembly process of the elementary matrices implies that all the degrees of freedom are expressed in the global frame. In the global frame, the degrees of freedom are the three displacements with respect to the three axes of the global Cartesian frame and the three rotations with respect to these three axes. Transfer matrices from the local frame to the global frame are therefore used for each element. Now, one has seen previously that the degrees of freedom of the plate elements are the two displacements in the plane of the plate, the out-of-plane displacement and two rotations. These rotations not being exactly the rotations with respect to the axes of the plate since ${\beta}_{x}(x,y)={\theta}_{y}(x,y),{\beta}_{y}(x,y)=-{\theta}_{x}(x,y)$, this must be taken into account at the assembly stage in order to bring out the correct degrees of freedom ${\theta}_{xi},{\theta}_{\text{yi}}$ .

##### 4.4.4.2 Fictitious rotations

**General case:**

The rotation with respect to the normal to the plate is considered not to be a degree of freedom. In order to ensure compatibility in passing from the local frame to the global frame, one therefore adds an additional local rotational degree of freedom to the plate, namely the one corresponding to the rotation with respect to the normal to the plane of the element. This implies an expansion of the blocks of dimension $(5,5)$ of the local stiffness matrix into blocks of dimension $(6,6)$ by adding a row and a column corresponding to this rotation. These additional rows and columns are a priori zero. One then passes from the enlarged local stiffness matrix to the global stiffness matrix.

In the previous transformation, one has merely added the rotations with respect to the normals to the plane of the elements without modifying the strain energy. The contribution to the energy brought by these additional degrees of freedom is indeed zero and no stiffness is associated with them.

The global stiffness matrix thus obtained nevertheless presents the risk of being non-invertible.

To avoid this inconvenience, it is accepted practice to assign a small stiffness to these additional degrees of freedom in the enlarged local stiffness matrix. In practice, it is chosen between ${10}^{–6}$ and ${10}^{–3}$ times the smallest diagonal term of the local bending stiffness matrix. The user may choose this multiplicative coefficient COEF_RIGI_DRZ himself in AFFE_CARA_ELEM; by default it is equal to ${10}^{–5}$ .

**Particular case of the DKT elements:**

It is possible to attach a physical meaning to the degree of freedom DRZ, often called “drilling rotation” with reference to a tendency of the plate to twist. In this case, the expression of the kinematics associated with this degree of freedom is:

${\theta}_{Z}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial y})$ .

To this kinematics one adjoins a dual quantity $\tau$ which is equivalent to the torsional moment of the plate in order to obtain :math:`{\theta}_{Z}$ . The difficulty in incorporating this new kinematics into the classical expression of the DKT elements is:

- Adopting a variational framework which makes it possible to introduce a stiffness which is not fictitious but real, linked to the rotation of the plate,
- Introducing a discretisation of ${\theta}_{Z}$ and $\tau$ .

One denotes by:

- ${\nabla}^{Z}U=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial y})$ the differential operator which, given the values of the membrane displacement, makes it possible to compute the “drilling rotation” kinematics.
- $\gamma$ is a strictly positive real number.

The kinematics (${\nabla}^{Z}U-{\theta}_{Z}=0$ ) is enforced by an augmented Lagrangian method.

$\delta {W}_{int}^{e}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{e}{\int}\delta e({H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}k)+\delta \kappa ({H}_{\text{mf}}e+{H}_{f}k)\text{dS}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{e}{\int}(({\nabla}^{Z}U-{\theta}_{Z})-\frac{1}{2\gamma }\tau ){\tau}^{\text{*}}d\Omega$

There is a second condition, which is $\tau =2\gamma ({\nabla}^{Z}U-{\theta}_{Z})$ sufficiently small to guarantee a weak enforcement of the kinematics about DRZ. With this new variational expression, the classical framework of study of the DKT elements is enriched. In particular one may expect a kinematic response different from the classical DKT models, which do not incorporate a physical stiffness about the normal. One does see, nevertheless, that a penalisation of the physical kinematic condition appears ( $\tau$ sufficiently small). This is a weakness of the method at present: its dependence on $\gamma$ .

One now interpolates ${\theta}_{Z}$ at the Gauss points from the nodal values of ${\theta}_{Z}$ of the element by using the linear shape functions $N$ . Likewise one computes the differential operator ${\nabla}^{Z}U$ from the nodal values of $u,v$ of the element by using the linear shape functions $N$ and the incomplete polynomials $P$ . After discretisation, one obtains the following matrix form:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}{K}_{[6N\times 6N]}\phantom{\rule{8em}{0ex}}{L}_{\tau}\\ {L}_{\tau}^{T}\phantom{\rule{8em}{0ex}}\frac{1}{{\gamma}^{-1}}T\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{c}U\\ \tau \end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{F}^{int}\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

Finally, since one does not wish to introduce a degree of freedom on the additional degrees of freedom $\tau$, one proceeds by a static condensation method which finally gives:

$({K}_{[6N\times 6N]}-{L}_{\tau}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\frac{1}{{\gamma}^{-1}}T{L}_{\tau}^{T})U={F}^{int}$

The kinematic framework has been enriched, with an impact on the global stiffness matrix of the system.

### 4.5 Mass matrix

The terms of the mass matrix are obtained after discretisation of the following variational formulation:

$\begin{array}{c}\delta {W}_{\text{mass}}^{\text{ac}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{S}{\int}\rho \ddot{\text{u}}\delta \text{u}\text{dzdS}=\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{m}(\ddot{u}\delta u+\ddot{v}\delta v+\ddot{w}\delta w)+{\rho}_{\text{mf}}(\ddot{u}\delta {\beta}_{x}+\ddot{v}\delta {\beta}_{y}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{x}\delta u+{\ddot{\beta}}_{y}\delta v)\\ +{\rho}_{f}({\ddot{\beta}}_{x}\delta {\beta}_{x}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{y}\delta {\beta}_{y})\text{dS}\end{array}$

with ${\rho}_{m}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \text{dz},{\rho}_{\text{mf}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \text{zdz},\text{et}{\rho}_{f}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho {z}^{2}\text{dz}$ .

**Note:**

*If the plate is homogeneous or symmetric with respect to* $z=0$ *then* ${\rho}_{\text{mf}}=0$ *. In the remainder of the presentation one considers that this is always the case.*

since: $(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,\xi k}\\ {w}_{,\eta k}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11}& {J}_{12}\\ {J}_{21}& {J}_{22}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,\text{xk}}\\ {w}_{,\text{yk}}\end{array})$ .

This is also written:

$w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{yk}}$

where: $\begin{array}{}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{(k-1)N+1}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )\text{=-}{J}_{11}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{21}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )\text{=-}{J}_{12}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{22}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$ .

Neglecting the inertia effects, the mass matrix thus has the following form:

$$
\text{M}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{M}}_{m}& 0& 0\\ 0& {\text{M}}_{m}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\text{M}}_{f}\end{array}\right]
$$

##### 4.5.2.2 Finite elements of DST type

One knows that for these elements one has ${\beta}_{x}={\gamma}_{x}-{w}_{,x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}={\gamma}_{y}-{w}_{,y}$ where the distortion $\gamma$ is constant over the element.

As:

$\begin{array}{}w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+({J}_{11}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )+{J}_{21}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )){w}_{,\text{xk}}+({J}_{12}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )\\ +{J}_{22}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )){w}_{,\text{yk}}\end{array}$

one may also write:

$\begin{array}{}w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{yk}}\\ +({J}_{11}{\bar{\gamma}}_{x}+{J}_{12}{\bar{\gamma}}_{y}){\mathrm{SN}}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )+({J}_{21}{\bar{\gamma}}_{x}+{J}_{22}{\bar{\gamma}}_{y}){\mathrm{SN}}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$

where: $\begin{array}{}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{(k-1)N+1}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )=-{J}_{11}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{21}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )=-{J}_{12}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{22}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$ ,

$\begin{array}{}\sum{N}_{(k-1)N+1}(\xi ,\eta )=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}(\xi ,\eta )\\ \sum{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )\\ \sum{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$

and $(\begin{array}{c}{\bar{\gamma}}_{x}\\ {\bar{\gamma}}_{y}\end{array})={\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}[{\text{B}}_{\mathrm{c\beta }}+{\text{B}}_{c\alpha }{\text{A}}_{\beta}](\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{y1}}\\ ⋮\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})={\text{T}}_{\gamma w}(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{y1}}\\ ⋮\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})$ .

One then obtains the interpolation for $w$ : $w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{''}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{''}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{''}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{yk}}$

where: $\begin{array}{}{N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{''}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{(k-1)N+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{11}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+1)+{J}_{12}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+1))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{21}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+1)+{J}_{22}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+1))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{''}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{(k-1)N+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{11}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+2)+{J}_{12}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+2))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{21}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+2)+{J}_{22}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+2))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{''}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{(k-1)N+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{11}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+3)+{J}_{12}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+3))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+2}(\xi ,\eta )+\\ ({J}_{21}{T}_{\gamma w}(1,(k-1)N+3)+{J}_{22}{T}_{\gamma w}(2,(k-1)N+3))\sum{N}_{(j-1)N+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$

Neglecting the inertia effects, the mass matrix thus has the following form:

$$
\text{M}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{M}}_{m}& 0& 0\\ 0& {\text{M}}_{m}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\text{M}}_{f}\end{array})
$$

##### 4.5.2.3 Elements of Q4g type

One proceeds in the same way as for the elements of DST type but with:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\bar{\gamma}}_{x}\\ {\bar{\gamma}}_{y}\end{array})={\text{B}}_{c}(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{y1}}\\ M\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})$ where ${\text{B}}_{c}$ is the matrix established in §4.3.2.1.

##### 4.5.2.4 Note

In the expression of the elementary mass matrix one neglects the rotational inertia terms $\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{f}({\ddot{\beta}}_{x}\delta {\beta}_{x}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{y}\delta {\beta}_{y})\text{dS}$  because the latter are negligible [5] with respect to the others. Indeed a multiplicative factor of ${h}^{2}/12$ links them to the other terms and they become negligible for a ratio of thickness to characteristic length lower than $1/20$ .

#### 4.5.3 Assembly of the elementary mass matrices

The assembly of the mass matrices follows the same logic as that of the stiffness matrices. The degrees of freedom are the same and one finds again the treatment specific to the rotations normal to the plane of the plate. For modal analyses involving both the computation of the stiffness matrix and that of the mass matrix, one must take a stiffness or a mass on the degree of rotation normal to the plane of the plate 103 to 106 times smaller than the smallest diagonal term of the stiffness or mass matrix for the bending terms. This makes it possible to inhibit the modes which may appear on the additional degree of freedom of rotation about the normal to the plane of the plate. By default, one takes a stiffness or a mass on the degree of rotation normal to the plane of the plate 105 times smaller than the smallest diagonal term of the stiffness or mass matrix for the bending terms

#### 4.5.4 “Lumped” mass matrix

The use of a “lumped” mass matrix has two advantages: it is simpler to implement numerically and it allows a better convergence . However the results are poorer than with the classical scheme (consistent matrix), for which the error is minimal [6] . The lumped mass matrix is in principle recommended only for transient analyses using an explicit time-integration scheme, which is used almost exclusively in fast dynamics. Unlike the analyses with implicit schemes, where at each increment (and at each Newton iteration in the non-linear case) one assembles and inverts a matrix, determined as a linear combination of the stiffness matrix, the mass matrix and the damping matrix, in explicit analyses only the mass matrix is assembled and inverted. Thus, by using a diagonal mass matrix the gain in terms of the CPU time of the solution as well as the gain in terms of the storage of the matrices are enormous compared with the use of a consistent mass matrix.

*Code_Aster*  not being a code specialised in fast dynamics, this advantage of the diagonal matrix is not exploited. The lumped mass-matrix option is therefore only a modelling choice, allowing possible comparisons with other analysis codes.

Two methods are presented here for diagonalising a consistent mass matrix. It will also be shown in what way the choice between the two methods is conditioned by the choice of the consistent matrix between the classical one (§4.5.1) and the improved one (§4.5.2).

The undoubtedly simplest technique for obtaining a lumped matrix is to retain the diagonal value for each degree of freedom as the sum of the entries of the row of the consistent matrix. Moreover, one recalls that the most important property of a lumped mass matrix is that it makes it possible to represent correctly a rigid-body motion. It is satisfied by the “row summation” method, written in equations in the following manner:

${M}_{C}^{\alpha}=\sum_{\beta}{M}^{\alpha \beta }$ .

Unfortunately the summation of the rows does not guarantee that all the terms ${M}_{C}^{\alpha}$ are positive. Negative terms appear in particular when using the improved consistent mass matrix (mentioned in §4.5.2). For this reason and for most of the elements in Code_Aster, another approach has been chosen, due to Hinton (see [6]), in which the diagonal terms corresponding to the directions $x$ , $y$ , $z$ , ${M}_{H}^{\mathrm{\alpha x}}$ , ${M}_{H}^{\mathrm{\alpha y}}$ and ${M}_{H}^{\mathrm{\alpha z}}$ , are computed as:

$$
{M}_{H}^{\mathrm{\alpha x}}=\frac{\underset{V}{\int}\rho \text{dV}}{\sum_{\beta}{M}_{x}^{\text{ββ}}}{M}_{x}^{\text{αα}}
{M}_{H}^{\mathrm{\alpha y}}=\frac{\underset{V}{\int}\rho \text{dV}}{\sum_{\beta}{M}_{y}^{\text{ββ}}}{M}_{y}^{\text{αα}}
$$

where the indices $\alpha$ correspond to the node numbers. Although Hinton's method is generally more robust, it is unsuitable for plate and shell elements, since [eq. 33] does not make it possible to include the inertia terms, the terms ${M}_{H}^{\alpha \cdot }$ defined in [eq. 33] necessarily having the units of mass and never of inertia.

Consequently, for the elements treated here one modifies the computation of the consistent mass matrix used for the computation of the lumped mass matrix. One adopts the classical method described in §4.5.1 for the consistent mass matrix, then the “row summation” approach for the lumping. The affected option of *code_aster* is MASS_MECA_EXPLI and only for the DKT and DKTG elements. For the others the lumped mass matrix is not available.

#### 4.5.5 Modification of the inertia terms

The lumped mass matrix described in §4.5.4 is not very effective for an analysis in explicit dynamics, where the stability time step is strongly penalised by a poor conditioning of the mass matrix. The terms corresponding to the rotations, that is the inertia terms, are the main culprits, since they are much smaller than the translation terms, that is the displacements. For this reason, a method was proposed in [7] for modifying the problematic terms while avoiding degrading the quality of the solution. Although old and not entirely rigorous, the approach proposed in [7] is widely referenced by the literature of the field and has not really been the subject of remarkable improvements.

One focuses on the terms due to bending, ${\theta}_{x}$ , ${\theta}_{y}$ and $w(={u}_{z})$ , the terms corresponding to the membrane being obtained in a classical manner and also applied to the 2D elements. The mass matrix ${\rm M}$ defined in §4.5 becomes:

$$
{{\rm M}}^{\text{αβ}}={m}^{\text{αβ}}(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{h}^{3}}{12}& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})
$$

where $h$ is the thickness of the plate and ${m}^{\text{αβ}}$ is defined as:

$$
{m}^{\text{αβ}}=\underset{S}{\int}{\mathrm{\rho N}}^{\alpha}{N}^{\beta}\text{dS}
$$

One notes that [eq. 34] and [eq. 35] are equivalent to [eq. 2.3-1] for the plate kinematics. In [7] it is proposed to build the lumped matrix from [eq. 2.5-2] by using the Lobatto quadrature, whose variants are the trapezoidal scheme and Simpson's scheme, in which the integration points coincide with the nodes. The construction of the mass matrix is done through a beam finite element, linear and with two nodes, by using the trapezoidal scheme, leading to:

$$
{{\rm M}}_{0}^{\text{pout}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho \text{LA}(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{I}{A}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{I}{A}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 1\end{array})
$$

for which the vector of degrees of freedom is written as ${(\begin{array}{cccc}{\theta}_{1}& {w}_{1}& {\theta}_{2}& {w}_{2}\end{array})}^{T}$ . $A$ , $L$ and $I$ are the area of the section, the length and the moment of inertia of the beam element, respectively. The use of the matrix [eq. 36] seeming too restrictive with respect to the stability condition, one rather proposes in [7]:

$$
{{\rm M}}^{\text{pout}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho \text{LA}(\begin{array}{cccc}\alpha & 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \alpha & 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 1\end{array})
$$

where the parameter $\alpha$ is introduced so that its adjustment can maximise the stability time step. According to [7] its optimal value would be $\alpha =\frac{1}{8}{L}^{2}$ . By applying these results directly by analogy to plates, one replaces the matrix of [eq. 34] by:

$$
{{\rm M}}^{\text{αβ}}={m}^{\text{αβ}}(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{\mathrm{hA}}^{e}}{8}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{\mathrm{hA}}^{e}}{8}& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})
$$

where ${A}^{e}$ is the area of the element considered. In passing from the beam to the plate one has assumed a certain equivalence between the length of the beam element and the area of the plate element, so that ${A}^{e}\approx {L}^{2}$ . One recalls that the approach proposed in [7] is not rigorous from the geometric point of view and that it focuses on maximising the stability step. In the implemented version, one ensures the desired effect of the modification from [eq. 34] to [eq. 37] by using:

$$
{{\rm M}}^{\text{αβ}}={m}^{\text{αβ}}(\begin{array}{ccc}\max(\frac{{h}^{3}}{12},\frac{{\mathrm{hA}}^{e}}{8})& 0& 0\\ 0& \max(\frac{{h}^{3}}{12},\frac{{\mathrm{hA}}^{e}}{8})& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})
$$

because [eq 37] is of interest only for coarse meshes, a priori the more common ones, whereas [eq. 34] becomes favourable for very fine meshes.

### 4.6 Linear buckling

Linear buckling appears as a particular case of the geometrically non-linear problem. It is based on the assumption of a linear dependence of the displacement, strain and stress fields with respect to the load level.

#### 4.6.1 Strain field

Starting from the Kirchhoff assumption, the components of the Green-Lagrange strain tensor are linked to the components of the displacement in the plane of the plate in the following way:

$$
\varepsilon =(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{x}\\ {\varepsilon}_{y}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{,x}\\ {v}_{,y}\\ ({u}_{,y}+{v}_{,x})\end{array})+(\begin{array}{c}\frac{1}{2}{\beta}_{x}^{2}\\ \frac{1}{2}{\beta}_{y}^{2}\\ ({\beta}_{x}{\beta}_{x})\end{array})+z(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x,x}\\ {\beta}_{y,y}\\ ({\beta}_{x,y}+{\beta}_{y,x})\end{array})
$$

Which one may express in the following form:

> $\varepsilon ={e}_{L}+{e}_{\mathit{NL}}+z\kappa$

with

> $\text{<}{e}_{L}\text{>}=\text{<}{u}_{,x}{v}_{,y}({u}_{,y}+{v}_{,x})\text{>}$         linear membrane strains
> $\text{<}{e}_{\mathit{NL}}\text{>}=\text{<}\frac{1}{2}{\beta}_{x}^{2}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\beta}_{y}^{2}\text{}{\beta}_{x}{\beta}_{y}\text{>}$     non-linear membrane strains
> $\text{<}\kappa \text{>}=\text{<}{{\beta}_{x}}_{,x}\text{}{{\beta}_{y}}_{,y}\text{}{{\beta}_{x}}_{,y}{{\beta}_{y}}_{,x}\text{>}$     linear bending strains

#### 4.6.2 Geometric stiffness matrix [KG]

Starting from the second variation of the internal strain energy and from the non-linear strains, one obtains the following matrix $[{K}_{G}]$:

$$
{\int}_{S}\text{<}\delta {w}_{,x}\delta {w}_{,x}\text{>}\left[\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{xx}& {N}_{xy}\\ {N}_{xy}& {N}_{yy}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\delta {w}_{,x}\\ \delta {w}_{,y}\end{array}\right\rbrace {\mathit{dA}}_{e}=\text{<}\delta {u}_{n}^{f}\text{>}[{K}_{G}]\lbrace \delta {u}_{n}^{f}\rbrace
$$

Which one may write in the following form:

> $\text{<}\delta {u}_{n}^{f}\text{>}[{K}_{G}]\lbrace \delta {u}_{n}^{f}\rbrace =\text{<}\delta {u}_{n}^{f}\text{>}{\int}_{S}[{B}_{\mathit{NL}}][N]{[{B}_{\mathit{NL}}]}^{T}\mathit{dS}\lbrace  \delta {u}_{n}^{f}\rbrace$

With

> $[N]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{xx}& {N}_{xy}\\ {N}_{xy}& {N}_{yy}\end{array}\right]$      the normal forces
> $\text{<}{u}_{n}^{f}\text{>}$                 the bending degrees of freedom
> $[{B}_{\mathit{NL}}]$                 the matrix linking the quadratic strains to the degrees of freedom

**Note:**

*Linear buckling is available only for the* *DKT* *and* *DKTG* *elements with* *TRIA3* *and* *QUAD4* *mesh cells* *.*

### 4.9 Numerical integration for plasticity and other non-linear laws

The integration over the surface of the element is completed by an integration through the thickness of the constitutive behaviour since:

${\text{H}}_{m}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\text{H}\text{dz},{\text{H}}_{\text{mf}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\text{H}\text{zdz},{\text{H}}_{f}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\text{H}{z}^{2}\text{dz}$ where $\text{H}$ is the local plastic constitutive matrix (or other non-linear laws).

The initial thickness is divided into $N$ layers of identical thicknesses and there are three integration points per layer (except for the DKTG and DKQG elements, which have only a single layer and one integration point in the layer). The integration points are located on the upper skin of the layer, at the middle of the layer and on the lower skin of the layer. For $N$ layers, the number of integration points is $2N+1$ .

One advises using from 3 to 5 layers through the thickness for a number of integration points equal to 7, 9 and 11 respectively.

For the stiffness, one computes for each layer, in plane stress, the contribution to the membrane, bending and membrane-bending coupling stiffness matrices. These contributions are added and assembled in order to obtain the total tangent stiffness matrix. For each layer, one computes the stress state $\left({\sigma}_{xx},{\sigma}_{yy},{\sigma}_{xy}\right)$ and the set of internal variables, at the middle of the layer and on the upper and lower skins of the layer, from the local plastic constitutive behaviour and from the local strain field $\left({\epsilon}_{xx},{\epsilon}_{yy},{\epsilon}_{xy}\right)$ . The positioning of the integration points allows us to have the most accurate estimates, because they are not extrapolated, on the lower and upper skins of the layer, where one knows that the stresses are likely to be maximal.

### 4.10 Discretisation of the external work

The variational formulation of the external work for the plate elements is written: $\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}\delta u+{f}_{y}\delta v+{f}_{z}\delta w+{m}_{x}\delta {\beta}_{x}+{m}_{y}\delta {\beta}_{y})\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}({f}_{x}\delta u+{f}_{y}\delta v+{f}_{z}\delta w+{m}_{x}\delta {\beta}_{x}+{m}_{y}\delta {\beta}_{y})\text{ds}$

Taking into account a linear discretisation of the displacements, one may write for an element:

$$
\begin{array}{}\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{e}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta ){\mathrm{du}}_{k}+{f}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {v}_{k}+{f}_{z}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {w}_{k}\\ +{m}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{m}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {\beta}_{\text{yk}})\text{dS}\\ +\underset{C}{\int}({\phi }_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {u}_{k}+{\phi }_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {v}_{k}+{\phi }_{z}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {w}_{k}\\ +{\mu}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{\mu}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\delta {\beta}_{\text{yk}})\text{ds}\\ =\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}{f}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}{\phi }_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{ds}⋮& \underset{S}{\int}{f}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}{\phi }_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{ds}⋮\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}{f}_{z}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}{\phi }_{z}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{ds}⋮& \underset{S}{\int}{m}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}{\mu}_{x}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{ds}⋮\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{c}\underset{S}{\int}{m}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}{\mu}_{y}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\text{ds}⋮\end{array})\delta {\text{U}}_{k}^{e}\\ =\sum_{k=1}^{N}{\text{F}}_{k}^{e}\delta {\text{U}}_{k}^{e}={\text{F}}^{e}\delta {\text{U}}^{e}\end{array}
$$

The variational formulation of the work of the external forces for the whole set of elements is then written:

$\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}=\sum_{e=1}^{\text{nb}\text{elem}}\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{e}=\text{F}\delta \text{U}=\delta {\text{U}}^{T}{\text{F}}^{T}$ where $\text{U}$ is the set of degrees of freedom of the discretised structure and $\text{F}$ comes from the assembly of the elementary force vectors.

As for the stiffness matrices, the assembly process of the elementary force vectors implies that all the degrees of freedom are expressed in the global frame. In the global frame, the degrees of freedom are the three displacements with respect to the three axes of the global Cartesian frame and the three rotations with respect to these three axes. Transfer matrices from the local frame to the global frame are therefore used for each element.

**Note:**

*The external forces may also be defined in the user frame. One then uses a transfer matrix from the user frame to the local frame of the element in order to have the expression of these forces in the local frame of the element and to deduce the corresponding local elementary force vector. For the assembly one then passes from the local frame of the element to the global frame.*

### 4.11 Taking thermal loadings into account

#### 4.11.1 Thermoelasticity of plates

The temperature is represented by the following three-field thermal model [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01):

$$
T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})={T}^{m}({x}_{\gamma}).{P}_{1}({x}_{3})+{T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma}).{P}_{2}({x}_{3})+{T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma}).{P}_{3}({x}_{3})
$$

with: ${T}^{m}({x}_{\gamma})$ : the temperature on the mean sheet

${T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma})$ : the temperature on the upper skin

${T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma})$ : the temperature on the lower skin

${P}_{j}({x}_{3})$ : the three Lagrange polynomials through the thickness: $]-h/2,+h/2[$ :

$$
{P}_{1}({x}_{3})=1-{({\mathrm{2x}}_{3}/h)}^{2};{P}_{2}({x}_{3})=\frac{{x}_{3}}{h}(1+{\mathrm{2x}}_{3}/h);{P}_{3}({x}_{3})=-\frac{{x}_{3}}{h}(1-{\mathrm{2x}}_{3}/h)
$$

from the temperature representation above, one obtains:

- the mean temperature through the thickness:

$\bar{T}({x}_{\gamma})=\frac{1}{h}{\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}=\frac{1}{6}({\mathrm{4T}}^{m}({x}_{\gamma})+{T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma})+{T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma}))$ ;

- the mean temperature gradient through the thickness:

$\hat{T}({x}_{\gamma})=\frac{12}{{h}^{2}}{\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}){x}_{3}{\text{dx}}_{3}={T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma})-{T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma})$ ;

Thus the temperature may be written in the following way:

$T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})=\bar{T}({x}_{\gamma})+\hat{T}({x}_{\gamma}).{x}_{3}/h+\tilde{T}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$ telle que:

${\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\tilde{T}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})=0;{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{x}_{3}\tilde{T}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})=0$ .

If the temperature is indeed affine through the thickness one has, $\tilde{T}=0$ .

*Code_Aster*  treats three different thermoelastic situations, in which the thermoelastic characteristics $E$ , $\nu$ , $\alpha$ depend only on the mean temperature $\bar{T}$ through the thickness:

- the case where the material is homogeneous isotropic thermoelastic through the thickness ;
- the case where the plate models an orthotropic grid (reinforcing steels of concrete);
- the case where the behaviour of the plate is deduced from a thermoelastic homogenisation, cf. [4] .

For the plate elements in thermoelasticity, the thermal effects are taken into account by means of generalised forces, in membrane and in bending. Thus, in the case of a homogeneous plate, knowing the expansion coefficient $\alpha$ , the generalised thermal forces are defined from the plane stresses through the thickness by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{N}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}={\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}{C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}{e}_{\eta \zeta }^{\text{ther}}{\text{dx}}_{3}={\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}\alpha {C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}(T-{T}^{\text{réf}}){\delta}_{\eta \zeta }{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {M}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}={\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}{x}_{3}{C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}{e}_{\eta \zeta }^{\text{ther}}{\text{dx}}_{3}={\int}_{-h/2}^{+h/2}\alpha {x}_{3}{C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}(T-{T}^{\text{réf}}){\delta}_{\eta \zeta }{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {V}_{\beta}^{\text{ther}}=0\end{array}
$$

That is, in the homogeneous isotropic thermoelastic case through the thickness:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{N}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}=\alpha .{C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}.h.(\bar{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}){\delta}_{\eta \zeta }=\alpha .\frac{\text{Eh}}{1-\nu }.(\bar{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}){\delta}_{\beta \gamma };\\ {M}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}=\alpha .{C}_{{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }_{}}.\frac{{h}^{2}}{12}\hat{T}=\alpha .\frac{{\text{Eh}}^{2}}{12(1-\nu )}.\hat{T}{\delta}_{\beta \gamma };{V}_{\beta}^{\text{ther}}=0.\end{array}
$$

The stresses of thermal origin subtracted from the usual mechanical stresses are computed at three positions (upper, mean and lower) through the thickness:

$$
{\sigma}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}=\frac{\alpha .E}{1-\nu }(\bar{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}+\hat{T}.{x}_{3}/h){\delta}_{\beta \gamma }
$$

In the case deduced from the thermoelastic homogenisation, cf. [4] , the generalised thermal forces are defined by the general relation, from the membrane “correctors” ${c}^{\beta \gamma }$ , those of bending ${x}^{\beta \gamma }$ , and that of expansion ${\text{u}}^{\text{dil}}$ , as averages over the representative elementary volume (cell $z$ ):

$\begin{array}{}{N}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}={\langle \langle {C}_{{}_{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }}.\alpha .(\bar{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}+\hat{T}({x}_{\gamma}).{z}_{3}/h+\tilde{T}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})){\delta}_{\eta \zeta }\rangle \rangle }_{Z}+{\langle \langle {e}_{ij}({c}^{\beta \gamma }){C}_{ijkl}.{e}_{kl}({u}^{\text{dil}})\rangle \rangle }_{Z};\\ {M}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}={\langle \langle {z}_{3}.{C}_{{}_{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }}.\alpha .(\bar{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}+\hat{T}({x}_{\gamma}).{z}_{3}/h+\tilde{T}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})){\delta}_{\eta \zeta }\rangle \rangle }_{Z}+{\langle \langle {e}_{ij}({x}^{\beta \gamma }){C}_{ijkl}.{e}_{kl}({u}^{\text{dil}})\rangle \rangle }_{Z};\\ {V}_{\beta}^{\text{ther}}=0\end{array}$

In this case, when one restricts oneself to orthotropic situations without bending-membrane coupling, one neglects the role of $\tilde{T}\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)$  on the corrector ${\text{u}}^{\text{dil}}$ , and one therefore finds that the thermal forces which appear on the right-hand side have the expression:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{N}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}=\alpha .{H}_{{}_{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }}^{m}.\left(\overline{T}-{T}^{\text{réf}}\right){\delta}_{\text{hz}};\\ {M}_{\beta \gamma }^{\text{ther}}=\alpha .{H}_{{}_{\beta \gamma \eta \zeta }}^{f}.\widehat{T}{\delta}_{\eta \zeta };{V}_{\beta}^{\text{ther}}=0\end{array}
$$

One cannot however recover the complete three-dimensional stresses: it would be necessary to know the “correctors” within the basic cell which served for the determination of the homogenised constitutive coefficients.

In the thermo-elastoplastic situations, or else for the shells (elements of the COQUE_3D family), it is necessary to evaluate the three-dimensional stresses, including the thermal stresses, at each integration point through the thickness.

**Note:**

*Recovering the complete three-dimensional stresses is not immediate for multilayer (laminated) shells because one must know the stress state layer by layer; in elasticity, the latter is deduced from the strain state and from the constitutive behaviour at the level of each layer.*

The latter is defined by the knowledge of the 3 fields TEMP_SUP, TEMP_MIL and TEMP_INF given on the lower skin, at mid-thickness and on the upper skin of the shell.

The thermomechanical chaining is also possible if one knows from experimental measurements the variation of the temperature field through the thickness of the structure or of certain parts of the structure. In this case one works with a temperature map defined a priori; the temperature field is no longer given by the three values TEMP_INF, TEMP_MIL and TEMP_SUP of the thermal analysis obtained by EVOL_THER. ### 5.3 Analysis in linear elasticity

The stiffness matrix and the mass matrix (respectively the options RIGI_MECA and MASS_MECA) are integrated numerically. One does not check whether the mesh cell is plane or not. The analysis takes into account the fact that the terms corresponding to the plate degrees of freedom are expressed in the local frame of the element. A transfer matrix makes it possible to pass from the local degrees of freedom to the global degrees of freedom.

The elementary computations (CALC_CHAMP) currently available correspond to the options:

- EPSI_ELNO and SIGM_ELNO which provide the strains and the stresses at the nodes in the user frame of the element on the lower skin, at mid-thickness and on the upper skin of the plate, the position being specified by the user. One stores these values in the following way: 6 components of strain or of stress:
- EPXX EPYY EPZZ EPXY EPXZ EPYZ ou SIXX SIYY SIZZ SIXY SIXZ SIYZ
- DEGE_ELNO: which gives the generalised strains per element at the nodes from the displacements in the user frame: EXX, EYY, EXY, KXX, KYY, KXY, GAX, GAY.
- EFGE_ELNO: which gives the generalised forces per element at the nodes from the displacements: NXX, NYY, NXY, MXX, MYY, MXY, QX, QY.
- SIEF_ELGA: which gives the generalised forces per element at the Gauss points from the displacements: NXX, NYY, NXY, MXX, MYY, MXY, QX, QY.
- EPOT_ELEM: which gives the elastic strain energy per element from the displacements.
- ECIN_ELEM: which gives the kinetic energy per element.

Finally one also computes the option FORC_NODA for the computation of the nodal forces for the operator CALC_CHAMP.

### 5.4 Analysis in linear buckling

The option RIGI_MECA_GE being activated in the catalogue of the element, it is possible to carry out a classical Euler buckling analysis after assembly of the elastic and geometric stiffness matrices.

### 5.5 Analysis in plasticity or other non-linear constitutive behaviour

The stiffness matrix is here too integrated numerically. One calls the analysis option STAT_NON_LINE in which one defines, at the level of the non-linear constitutive behaviour, the number of layers to be used for the numerical integration.

For the DKT modellings, all the plane-stress laws available in *Code_Aster*  may be used.

For the DST and Q4G modellings, only linear elasticity is usable.

For the DKTG modelling, the only constitutive laws used are global laws (since there is only one integration point through the thickness), linking the generalised strains to the generalised stresses. These laws are, in version 9.4: GLRC_DM and GLRC_DAMAGE, as well as their coupling with elastoplastic membrane laws (KIT_DDI).

The elementary computations currently available correspond to the options:

- EPSI_ELNO which provides the strains per element at the nodes in the user frame from the displacements, on the lower skin, at mid-thickness and on the upper skin of the plate.
- SIGM_ELNO which makes it possible to obtain the stress field through the thickness per element at the nodes for all the sub-points (all the layers and all the positions: on the lower skin, at the middle and on the upper skin of the layer).
- EFGE_ELNO which makes it possible to obtain the generalised forces per element at the nodes in the user frame.
- VARI_ELNO which computes the field of internal variables and the stresses per element at the nodes for all the layers, in the local frame of the element.

## 6 Conclusion

The plane plate finite elements which we describe here are used in the analyses of thin structures, in small displacements and small strains, whose ratio of thickness to characteristic length is lower than $1/10$ . As these elements are plane, they do not take into account the curvature of the structures, and it is necessary to refine the meshes in the case where the latter would be significant.

They are elements for which the strains and the stresses in the plane of the element vary linearly with the thickness of the plate. Moreover, the distortion associated with the transverse shear is constant through the thickness of the element. Two families of plate finite elements exist: the DKT, DKQ elements (or DKTG, DKQG) for which the transverse distortion is zero and the finite elements with transverse-shear energy DST, DSQ and Q4G (or Q4g) for which it remains constant and non-zero through the thickness. One advises using the second type of elements when the structure to be studied has a ratio of thickness to characteristic length between $1/20$ and $1/10$ and the first ones in the remaining cases. When the transverse distortion is non-zero, the DST, DSQ and Q4G plate elements do not satisfy the 3D equilibrium conditions and the boundary conditions on the vanishing of the transverse shear stresses on the upper and lower faces of the plate, compatible with a transverse distortion constant through the thickness of the plate. It thus follows that, at the level of the elastic constitutive behaviour, a coefficient of $5/6$ for a homogeneous plate corrects the usual relation between the stresses and the transverse distortion so as to ensure the equality between the shear energies of the 3D model and of the plate model with constant distortion. In this case, the deflection $w$ has as interpretation the mean transverse displacement through the thickness of the plate.

The non-linear behaviours in plane stress are available for the DKT and DKQ plate elements only. Indeed the rigorous taking into account of a constant non-zero transverse shear through the thickness and the determination of the associated correction on the shear stiffness with respect to a model satisfying the equilibrium conditions and the boundary conditions are not possible and therefore make the use of the DST, DSQ and Q4G elements rigorously impossible in plasticity.

For the elements of the DKTG family, only global constitutive relations (moment-curvature relations and membrane forces – elongations) are available.

Elements corresponding to the mechanical elements exist in thermal analysis; the thermomechanical chainings are therefore available except, for the moment, for laminated materials.

: Transverse-shear correction factors for orthotropic or laminated plates
The matrix ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ is defined so that the surface density of transverse-shear energy obtained in the case of the three-dimensional distribution of the stresses coming from the solution of the equilibrium is equal to that of the plate model based on the Reissner assumptions, for a behaviour in simple bending. One must thus find ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ such that:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau {\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}\tau =\frac{1}{2}{\text{TH}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\text{T}=\frac{1}{2}\gamma {\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}\gamma
\textrm{avec}
\tau =(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array})` et :math:`\text{T}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau \text{dz}={\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}\gamma
$$

In order to obtain ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ one uses the distribution of $\tau$ along $z$ obtained from the solution of the 3D equilibrium equations without external couples:

${\sigma}_{xz}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}({\sigma}_{xx,x}+{\sigma}_{xy,y})d\zeta ;{s}_{yz}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}({\sigma}_{xy,x}+{\sigma}_{yy,y})d\zeta$ with ${\sigma}_{xz}={\sigma}_{yz}=0$ for $z=\pm h/2$ .

In the case where there is no membrane-bending coupling (symmetry with respect to $z=0$ ), the stresses in the plane of the element ${\sigma}_{xx}$ , ${\sigma}_{yy}$ and ${\sigma}_{xy}$ have the expression, in the case of a pure bending behaviour:

$\sigma =z\text{A}(z)\text{M}$ with $\text{A}(z)=\text{H}(z){\text{H}}_{f}^{-1}$ .

If $\text{H}(z)$ and ${\text{H}}_{f}$ do not depend on $x$ and $y$ one can determine ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ . Indeed:

$\tau (z)={\text{D}}_{1}(z)\text{T}+{\text{D}}_{2}(z)\lambda$ where $\text{T}=(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx,x}+{M}_{xy,y}\\ {M}_{xy,x}+{M}_{yy,y}\end{array})$ and $\lambda =(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx,x}-{M}_{xy,y}\\ {M}_{xy,x}-{M}_{yy,y}\\ {M}_{yy,x}\\ {M}_{xx,y}\end{array})$

as well as:

${\text{D}}_{1}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}\frac{\zeta}{2}(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}+{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}+{A}_{32}\\ {A}_{31}+{A}_{23}& {A}_{22}+{A}_{33}\end{array})d\zeta$ ,

${\text{D}}_{2}\text{=-}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}\frac{\zeta}{2}(\begin{array}{cccc}{A}_{11}-{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}-{A}_{32}& 2{A}_{12}& 2{A}_{31}\\ {A}_{31}-{A}_{23}& {A}_{33}-{A}_{22}& 2{A}_{32}& 2{A}_{21}\end{array})d\zeta$ .

It follows that $\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau {\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}\tau =\frac{1}{2}(\begin{array}{c}\text{T}\\ \lambda \end{array})(\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{11}& {C}_{12}\\ {C}_{12}^{T}& {C}_{22}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}\text{T}\\ \lambda \end{array})$ with: $\begin{array}{c}{\text{C}}_{11}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{D}}_{1}^{T}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}{\text{D}}_{1}\text{dz};\\ {\text{C}}_{12}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{D}}_{1}^{T}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}{\text{D}}_{2}\text{dz};\\ {\text{C}}_{22}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\text{D}}_{2}^{T}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}{\text{D}}_{2}\text{dz}\end{array}$

As moreover $\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau {\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}\tau =\frac{1}{2}{\text{TH}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\text{T}$ one proposes to take ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}={\text{C}}_{11}^{-1}$ in order to satisfy the two equations as well as possible whatever $T$ and $\lambda$ .

$$
{k}_{1}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{11}/{\bar{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{11}
\textrm{,}
{k}_{12}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{12}/{\bar{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{12}` et  :math:`{k}_{2}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{22}/{\bar{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{22}
$$

For a homogeneous plate, isotropic or anisotropic, one thus finds: ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}=\mathrm{kh}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}$ with $k=5/6$ .

- For a multilayer material , one establishes that:

$$
\begin{array}{}{\text{C}}_{11}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{h}_{i}}{4}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{\eta}_{p}{\text{A}}_{p}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\text{A}}_{i}^{T}){\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{\eta}_{p}{\text{A}}_{p}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\text{A}}_{i})+\\ \frac{1}{24}({z}_{i+1}^{3}-{z}_{i}^{3})[{\text{A}}_{i}^{T}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{\eta}_{p}{\text{A}}_{p}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\text{A}}_{i})+(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{\eta}_{p}{\text{A}}_{p}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\text{A}}_{i}^{T}){\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}{\text{A}}_{i}]\\ +\frac{1}{80}({z}_{i+1}^{5}-{z}_{i}^{5}){\text{A}}_{i}^{T}{\text{H}}_{\gamma}^{-1}{\text{A}}_{i}\end{array}
$$

where: ${h}_{i}={z}_{i+1}-{z}_{i},{\eta}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}({z}_{i+1}+{z}_{i})$ and ${\text{A}}_{i}$ represents the matrix $(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}+{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}+{A}_{32}\\ {A}_{31}+{A}_{23}& {A}_{22}+{A}_{33}\end{array})$ for the layer $i$ .

- The validity of the choice ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}={\text{C}}_{11}^{-1}$ may be examined a posteriori when one has an estimate of the solution (displacement and plane-stress fields, in particular). One may then estimate the discrepancy between the two estimates of the energy. A two-step analysis procedure for multilayer plates and shells (with ${\text{H}}_{\text{ct}}$ diagonal and two coefficients ${k}_{1}$ and ${k}_{2}$ ) has moreover been developed by Noor and Burton [8]and [8] .
- In the case of a homogeneous isotropic or anisotropic plate, the equality between the two energies is satisfied in the strict sense since ${\text{D}}_{2}=0$ . The choice made above is then valid and no a posteriori examination is necessary.
