# r3.06.10 Quadrilateral element with one integration point, stabilised by the “Assumed Strain” method

**Summary:**

Despite all the potential of the linear isoparametric quadrilateral element for finite-element analysis, the application of a classical bilinear formulation to define its displacement field leads to poor results. If under-integration of the element makes it possible to improve its performance, it nevertheless gives rise to spurious modes which make the analyses unstable. The present document takes up the main stages of a stabilisation method for the analyses called the “assumed strain method” and explains the way in which it has been implemented in the analysis code *Code_Aster*. Numerous results based on the stabilised element are compared and commented on in order to conclude on the performance of the method.

## 2 Under-integration of the QUAD4

Despite all the potential of the isoparametric quadrilateral element for finite-element analysis, the application of a classical bilinear formulation to define its displacement field leads to poor results. This is explained by various reasons:

- it presents an excessive stiffness (“lock”), when a loading whose plane-bending part is significant.
- the classical bilinear formulation of the displacement field is very sensitive to mesh distortion and presents severe “locking” when it is applied to an incompressible material.

A solution to these numerical locking problems consists in computing the stiffness matrix by means of a reduced integration. The principle of this method is to consider, in the numerical integration scheme, fewer integration points than are usually required to evaluate the exact stiffness matrices of the element. Starting from the isoparametric QUAD4 element [r3.03.02](doc:r3.03.02), the number of integration points as well as the weight and the coordinates of the latter are modified, in order to create the under-integrated element which will be called in this document: QUAS4

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.10/Shape3.png)

| QUAD4 | QUAS4 |
| --- | --- |
| Number of integration points: 4 | Number of integration points: 1 |
| Weight of each point: 1 | Weight of the point: 4 |
| Coordinates: $(x,y)=(\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}},\pm \frac{1}{\sqrt{3}})$ | Coordinates: $(x,y)=(0,0)$ |

### 2.1 Formulation

At the centre, the discretised gradient operator used to compute the stiffness matrix has the following form:

$B={B}_{c}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{b}^{{t}_{x}}& 0\\ 0& {b}^{{t}_{y}}\\ {b}^{{t}_{y}}& {b}^{{t}_{x}}\end{array}\right]$                                     eq 2.1-1

With

$\begin{array}{}{b}_{x}^{t}=N,x(0)=\frac{\partial N}{\partial x}\mid \xi =\eta =0\\ {b}_{y}^{t}=N,y(0)=\frac{\partial N}{\partial y}\mid \xi =\eta =0\end{array}$                         eq 2.1-2

and $N$ represents $({N}_{1},{N}_{2},{N}_{3},{N}_{4})$ , the vector of the shape functions. It is recalled that the vectors $b$ represent the simplest form of the under-integrated discretised gradient operator introduced by Hallquist and which is based on the evaluation of the derivatives of the isoparametric shape functions at the origin of the reference frame $(\xi ,\eta )$

That is:

$\begin{array}{c}{b}_{x}^{t}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2A}}\left[({y}_{2}-{y}_{4}),({y}_{3}-{y}_{1}),({y}_{4}-{y}_{2}),({y}_{1}-{y}_{3})\right]=\text{Constant sur l'élément}\\ {b}_{y}^{t}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2A}}\left[({x}_{4}-{x}_{2}),({x}_{1}-{x}_{3}),({x}_{2}-{x}_{4}),({x}_{3}-{x}_{1})\right]=\text{Constant sur l'élément}\end{array}$             eq 2.1-3

$A=(1/2).((\mathit{x2}-\mathit{x4}).(\mathit{y3}-\mathit{y1})+(\mathit{x3}-\mathit{x1})\mathrm{\ast }(\mathit{y4}-\mathit{y2}))$ : Area of the element

The stiffness matrix is written:

${K}_{e}={K}_{c}=A.{B}_{{c}^{T}}.C.{B}_{c}$                             eq 2.1-4

$C$ being:

- either the elastic constitutive matrix for the analyses in elasticity
- or the tangent matrix for the analyses in plasticity. Note that during such analyses, it is the integration of the constitutive law at the Gauss point (at the centre in our case) which determines the value of the coefficients of $C$ .

Finally, the internal forces are written:

${F}_{int}={K}_{e}.U={B}_{{c}^{T}}.{\sigma}_{c}$                             eq 2.1-5

$U$ : **Vector of the nodal displacements.**

### 2.2 Failure of the analysis in Code_Aster: spurious modes

The analyses launched on test case no. 1 with such an element fail. The stage of the analysis which consists in inverting ${K}_{e}$ in order to determine the nodal displacements cannot be passed. Indeed, at the centre, the stiffness matrix is singular. Displaying the kernel of ${K}_{e}$ , one observes that its dimension is no longer three but five. Two vectors have been added to the kernel and have made the inversion of the stiffness matrix impossible. The appearance of these two additional vectors in the kernel of ${K}_{e}$ is directly linked to the fact that the centre is chosen as the only integration point. In other words, there exist two nodal displacement fields other than the displacement fields corresponding to rigid-body motion which cancel the internal forces. These modes represent the hourglass modes of the QUAD4 [r3.06.10-modes-sablier]. Subsequently, one of the stages of stabilisation will consist in enriching the discretised gradient operator in order to make ${K}_{e}$ invertible.

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.10/Shape29.gif)

*Hourglass modes of the QUAD4*

### 2.3 Graphical interpretation of the problem linked to under-integration

The problem of the under-integration of the QUAD4 is linked to its hourglass nodal displacement modes (bending loading). [r3.06.10-modes] shows that, on such modes, the coordinates of the centre remain unchanged. If this is in agreement with beam theory (i.e. in pure bending, xx = 0 on the neutral fibre), classical finite-element theory does not make it possible to distinguish the deformed and undeformed state of an element in such a case. This is why these modes are also called zero-energy modes, which, at the level of *Code_Aster*, make the analyses impossible to carry out.

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.10/Shape30.gif)

*Modes*

This problem originates in a value of the strain at the centre which is not representative of the strain of the QUAD4. The next chapter will describe the approach which will make it possible to compute the strain of the QUAD4 whatever the displacement modes of these nodes.

## 3 Stabilisation of the QUAD4 with one Gauss point

The stabilisation of the QUAS4 is carried out in two stages:

- Enrich the discretised gradient operator ${B}_{c}$ and thus make it possible to compute the strain energy linked to the hourglass displacement modes (*hourglass modes*);
- Interpolate a strain / stress field making it possible to account for the strain / the stresses over the whole element while integrating the QUAD4 at the centre (*assumed strain stabilization*).

### 3.1 Variational principle of the problem

This is taken from the weak form of the Hu–Washizu variational principle:

> $\delta \pi (\upsilon ,\stackrel{ˉ}{\dot{\varepsilon}},\stackrel{ˉ}{\sigma})={\int}_{{\Omega}_{e}}{\delta \stackrel{ˉ}{\dot{\varepsilon}}}^{T}.\sigma d\Omega +\delta {\int}_{{\Omega}_{e}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{\sigma}}^{T}.(\nabla {}_{s}\text{}\upsilon -\stackrel{ˉ}{\dot{\varepsilon}})d\Omega -\delta {\dot{d}}^{T}.{f}^{\text{ext}}=0$         eq 3.1-1

With

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\dot{\varepsilon}}(x,t)=B(x).\dot{d}(t)$

The “Assumed strain” stabilisation relies on the fact that the assumed stress is chosen orthogonal to the difference between the symmetric part of the velocity gradient and the strain rate. Consequently, this allows one to write:

$\delta {\dot{d}}^{T}.{\int}_{{\Omega}_{e}}{B}^{T}.\sigma d\Omega -\delta {\dot{d}}^{T}.{f}^{\text{ext}}=0$

And therefore that:

${f}^{int}={\int}_{{\Omega}_{e}}{B}^{T}.\sigma (\stackrel{ˉ}{\dot{\varepsilon}})d\Omega$

### 3.2 Enrichment of the discretised gradient operator

Enriching the discretised gradient operator ${B}_{c}$ consists in making it a new operator $B$ by adding to it a third component $\gamma$ which, like ${b}_{x}$ and ${b}_{y}$ , is a vector of ${\mathrm{\Re }}^{4}$ . However, since the initial operator ${B}_{c}$ correctly computes the gradient of the linear displacement fields, the new component $\gamma$ must be orthogonal to the latter. The stages of the computation of this enriched operator are detailed in paragraph [r3.06.10-cas-test-deux] of the attached document entitled: “Bibliographic report”.

**Note:** In this report, the new operator $B$ relates the strain-rate tensor $\dot{\varepsilon}$ and the vector of nodal velocities ${\dot{d}}_{i}$ . This formulation allows one, in the remainder of the report, to reason in terms of displacement increments, and consequently to treat incremental problems, carried out over several time steps. For the elastic analyses (solutions obtained in one time step) the problem is formulated from the nodal displacements: the operator $B$ then relates the strain tensor $\varepsilon$ and the nodal displacements ${u}_{i}$ .

The new operator $B$ is based on the expression of the displacement field of the QUAD4 written by Belytschko and Bachrach:

${u}_{i}=({\Delta}^{T}+x.{b}_{x}^{T}+y.{b}_{y}^{T}+{h}_{\gamma}^{T}){u}_{i}$                         eq 3.2-1

And is written:

With:

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta =1/4\left[t-({t}^{T}.x){b}_{x}-({t}^{T}.y){b}_{y}\right],\gamma =1/4\left[h-({h}^{T}.x){b}_{x}-({h}^{T}.y){b}_{y}\right]\\ {b}_{x}^{t}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2A}}\left[({y}_{2}-{y}_{4}),({y}_{3}-{y}_{1}),({y}_{4}-{y}_{2}),({y}_{1}-{y}_{3})\right]\\ {b}_{y}^{t}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2A}}\left[({x}_{4}-{x}_{2}),({x}_{1}-{x}_{3}),({x}_{2}-{x}_{4}),({x}_{3}-{x}_{1})\right]\\ h=\xi \eta \end{array}$

$\xi ,\eta$ are the reference coordinates. ${u}_{i}$ are the vectors of nodal displacements and $h$ are the values taken by the function $h$ at the four nodes.

${d}^{T}=\left[{u}_{x},{u}_{y}\right]$

Note that $\gamma$ can be expressed directly as a function of the nodal coordinates:

$\gamma =\frac{1}{4}\left[\begin{array}{c}{x}_{2}({y}_{3}-{y}_{4})+{x}_{3}({y}_{4}-{y}_{2})+{x}_{4}({y}_{2}-{y}_{3})\\ {x}_{3}({y}_{1}-{y}_{4})+{x}_{4}({y}_{3}-{y}_{1})+{x}_{1}({y}_{4}-{y}_{3})\\ {x}_{4}({y}_{1}-{y}_{2})+{x}_{1}({y}_{2}-{y}_{4})+{x}_{2}({y}_{4}-{y}_{1})\\ {x}_{1}({y}_{3}-{y}_{2})+{x}_{2}({y}_{1}-{y}_{3})+{x}_{3}({y}_{2}-{y}_{1})\end{array}\right]$                 eq 3.2-3

The form [] of the operator $B$ is equivalent to that of the QUAD4. However this particular writing of the operator makes it possible to distinguish the terms to be integrated at the centre and the stabilisation terms. It is only by acting on the value of these stabilisation terms that the performance of the element can be improved.

### 3.3 Interpolation of the strain field:

The general form of an “assumed strain” field within a QUAD4 is given by Belytschko and Bindeman. It has the following form:

${\epsilon}_{\text{assumed}\text{strain}}=\left[\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{x(\text{centre})}+{q}_{x}{e}_{1}{h}_{,x}+{q}_{y}{e}_{2}{h}_{,y}\\ {\epsilon}_{y(\text{centre})}+{q}_{x}{e}_{2}{h}_{,x}+{q}_{y}{e}_{1}{h}_{,y}\\ {\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{xy(\text{centre})}+{q}_{x}{e}_{3}{h}_{,y}+{q}_{y}{e}_{3}{h}_{,x}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{x(\text{centre})}+{\epsilon}_{x(\text{stab})}\\ {\epsilon}_{y(\text{centre})}+{\epsilon}_{y(\text{stab})}\\ {\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{xy(\text{centre})}+{\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{xy(\text{stab})}\end{array}\right]$         eq 3.3-1

With:

$\begin{array}{c}{q}_{x}=\gamma .{u}_{x}\\ {q}_{y}=\gamma .{u}_{y}\end{array}$

and ${e}_{1},{e}_{2},{e}_{3}$ which vary according to the physical considerations of each author [r3.06.10-interpolation-deformation].

Each triplet of values characterises an element and gives rise to a particular interpolation of the strain:

| **Element** | $\mathit{e1}$ | $\mathit{e2}$ | $\mathit{e3}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **QUAD4** | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| **ASMD** | 1/2 | -1/2 | 1 |
| **ASBQI** | 1 | $-\nu$ | 0 |
| **ASOI** | 1 | -1 | 0 |
| **ASOI(1/2)** | 1/2 | -1/2 | 0 |

The expression of the discretised gradient operator resulting from the assumed strain field (${\varepsilon}_{(\mathit{stab})}$ ) of the element can thus be deduced. This new operator is denoted ${B}_{n}$ .

$\begin{array}{}\text{QUAD4}:{B}_{n}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{h}_{,x}{\gamma}^{t}& 0\\ 0& {h}_{,y}{\gamma}^{t}\\ {h}_{,y}{\gamma}^{t}& {h}_{,x}{\gamma}^{t}\end{array}\right]\\ \text{ASBQI}:{B}_{n}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{h}_{\text{,x}}{\text{γ}}^{t}& -\stackrel{ˉ}{\nu}{\text{h}}_{\text{,y}}{\text{γ}}^{t}\\ -\stackrel{ˉ}{\nu}{\text{h}}_{\text{,x}}{\text{γ}}^{t}& {h}_{\text{,y}}{\text{γ}}^{t}\\ 0& 0\end{array}\right]\\ \text{ASOI}(\text{1/2}):{B}_{n}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{1}{2}{h}_{,x}{\gamma}^{t}& -\frac{1}{2}{h}_{,y}{\gamma}^{t}\\ -\frac{1}{2}{h}_{,x}{\gamma}^{t}& \frac{1}{2}{h}_{,y}{\gamma}^{t}\\ 0& 0\end{array}\right]\end{array}$                     eq 3.3-2

This writing makes it possible to test the various elements easily.

The stiffness matrix is then written in the following way:

${K}_{e}={K}_{c}+{K}^{\text{stab}}$                                 eq 3.3-3

## 4 Integration of the element in Code_Aster

The QUAS4 element has two families of Gauss points. The first family consists of one point located at the centre and whose weight is equal to four. The elementary computations are naturally carried out with the first family. The options computed are identical to those computed by the QUAD4. The stresses and internal variables, as well as the tangent behaviour, are therefore computed at the centre of the element. Within the framework of a post-processing, each element presents constant fields.

The second family is identical to that of the classical QUAD4 and comprises 4 Gauss points. It is used to compute the stabilisation matrix. In order to store the stabilisation forces, 6 components are added to the stress field at the Gauss point.

This element is activated by choosing the modellings 'C_PLAN_SI' or 'D_PLAN_SI' in AFFE_MODELE for QUAD4 mesh cells. Only analyses in small strains are possible. There remains an important limitation: in version 7.2, two types of stabilisation are programmed: ASBQI and ASOI(1/2), but they are not accessible by a keyword in the command file. In order to activate them one must modify the parameter PROJ in the routine NMAS2D.

### 5.3 Comments

It is observed through this series of analyses that the results obtained with QUAD4 elements contain strong stress oscillations. The results coming from the analyses based on the QUAS4 present almost no more oscillations. When they appear, they are very localised. Whether for the QUAD4 or for the QUAS4, the displacements computed at node $A$ are identical. Indeed, the discretised gradient operator of the QUAS4 is deduced from the strain field of the QUAD4 (cf. [eq 3.2-2]). Consequently, whether it is meshed with one or the other of these elements, the structure presents the same stiffness.

Given the severity of the test and in order to assess the quality of the results supplied by the QUAS4, analyses based on quadratic mesh cells such as the QUAD8, the QUAS8 (under-integrated QUAD8 with 4 Gauss points) or else the QUAD8 INCO_UPG, an element treating incompressibility perfectly, have been carried out. The comparison of elements with quadratic interpolation with elements with linear interpolation has little meaning. Such tests were carried out with a view to having a reference solution and they made it possible to pass a purely qualitative judgement on the QUAS4 element.

Comments on the results

Qualitative aspects:

- Despite the richness of the displacement field of the QUAD8 mesh cell, the majority of the results present oscillations.
- Despite the under-integration of the QUAS8, oscillations appear on QUAS8 mesh cells for certain analyses in plasticity.
- Whatever the element used (except for the QUAD8 INCO_UPG), under-integration remains indispensable, particularly for materials whose $\nu$ approaches 0,5.
- These analyses lead to the following observation: the QUAS4 remains stable with respect to oscillations whatever the analysis parameters used.

Quantitative aspect:

It is still observed that the value of the displacement of node A coming from a QUAS4 mesh remains between 3 and 5 times smaller than the reference solution (slow convergence). This value nevertheless remains identical to that computed with a QUAD4 mesh.

The profiles plotted along the notch allow one to assert that the stress values computed with a QUAS4 mesh are of much better quality than those computed with a QUAD4 mesh. Taking the analysis durations of the QUAD4, the QUAS4 mesh cell allows a substantial reduction in the duration of the analysis:

It will be noted in passing that the time savings seem to increase with the number of mesh cells. In plasticity in particular, this time saving depends for the most part on the number of Newton iterations required for the convergence of the analysis within each time step.

## 6 Conclusions

In elasticity as in plasticity, whether for materials whose $\nu$ is equal to $0,3$ or $0,5$ , the new QUAS4 element remains stable and the results always realistic, without any stress oscillations. This, as has been observed, is far from being the case for the QUAD4. The stability of this new element in the face of test cases as severe as those presented in this report is comparable to that of the under-integrated quadratic QUAD8 element.

On the other hand, this element has the convergence of a linear element in terms of number of degrees of freedom. One must therefore mesh with a sufficient fineness to capture the stress gradients of the sought solution. This necessary refinement must be weighed against the time saving induced by under-integration.

On the examples treated, the QUAS4 has allowed a significant saving in analysis time of the order of 20% on average for elastic and elastoplastic constitutive laws. Note that these laws are relatively inexpensive to integrate. Much larger time savings are expected for laws that are more difficult to integrate.
