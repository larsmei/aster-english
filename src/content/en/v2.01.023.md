# v2.01.023 SDLD23 - Random Excitation Mass and Spring System

**Summary:**

This test was part of the dynamic test set planned for VPCS, which officially failed but served as the basis for many *Code_Aster* dynamic test cases.

It has a set of eight point masses and nine springs excited by a random force imposed on one of the masses.

The excitement is of the white noise type. It is given by the spectral density of power of the excitatory force.

The excited mass movement is calculated by a stochastic approach using different frequency discretizations for the response.

The spectral moments of the response are also calculated in post-processing.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Excitation is a seismic force-type movement applied to point $\mathrm{P4}$ in the $\mathrm{dx}$ direction.

We're interested in DSP moving node $\mathrm{P4}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

| Point mass: | $m=10\mathrm{Kg}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Elastic springs: | $k={10}^{5}N/m$ |
| Amortizers: | $c=50N/(m/s)$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The problem is one-dimensional in the direction $x$ (1 degree of freedom per mass).

The excitation is a constant DSP force level 1, between 3 and 13 $\mathrm{Hz}$.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

** Discrete element in translation** **DIS_T**

Modelling respects geometry.

Features of elements:

Nodes $\mathrm{P1}$ to $\mathrm{P8}$: M_T_D_N mass matrices with $m=10\mathrm{Kg}$ .

Spring elements: stiffness matrix of K_T_D_L type with ${K}_{x}={10}^{5}N/m$

Depreciation elements: a A_T_D_L damping matrix with ${c}_{x}=\mathrm{50 }N/m$

boundary conditions :

All degrees of freedom are blocked except the degree of freedom $\mathrm{dx}$ .

The modal damping is calculated by the modal calculation operator, it is reinjected as modal damping in the random dynamic calculation.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes: 10

Number of mesh cells and types : 9 SEG2, 10 POI1

## 4 Summary of the results

The previous tables highlight the importance of the fineness of the frequent discretization of the DSP response for the calculation of spectral moments.

The user can choose the step: the frequency band is then evenly discretized and the peak(s) may be misrepresented: this is the case with 40 steps of $0.25\mathrm{Hz}$, which results in an error of more than $\text{40\%}$ on spectral moments.

The more discretization is refined, the better the result.

To avoid unnecessary refinement away from peaks, a fairly wide default discretization is proposed, supplemented by a refinement of 50 discretization points around each peak.

In the case of this test, which includes only one peak, this default discretization allows to estimate the spectral moments with precision of the order of $\text{2\%}$.
