## 1 Introduction

This document presents a thermo-elasto-(visco)plastic constitutive law with isotropic hardening in large strains which takes into account the effects of the metallurgical transformations. This model may be used for three-dimensional, axisymmetric and plane-strain problems.

This law represents an “assembly” of two models implemented in *Code_Aster* , namely a thermoelastoplastic model with isotropic hardening written in large strains (factor keyword DEFORMATION: ’SIMO_MIEHE’, cf. [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21)) and a small-strain thermo‑elasto-(visco)plastic model with the effects of the metallurgical transformations (factor keyword ’META_P_**_**’ or ’META_V_**_**’ of COMPORTEMENT of the operator STAT_NON_LINE). The first large-strain model has therefore been extended in order to take account of the consequences of the metallurgical transformations on the mechanics.

In order to understand this document, it is practically indispensable to read the reference documents [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21) and [R4.04.02](doc:r4.04.02) which concern, respectively, the large-strain model without metallurgical effects and the small-strain model with metallurgical effects. In order to justify the extension of the model written in large strains to the large-strain model with metallurgical effects, we take up a few theoretical aspects extracted from [bib1] related to the writing of the large-strain model.

## 3 Reminders on the metallurgical model and on the large-strain model

### 3.1 Model with metallurgical transformations

We present here only the consequences of the metallurgical transformations on the mechanical behaviour.

The determination of the mechanical evolution associated with a process involving metallurgical transformations requires beforehand a thermo-metallurgical analysis. This thermo-metallurgical analysis is uncoupled and makes it possible to determine the thermal evolutions and then the metallurgical ones. For the metallurgical constitutive models of steels, one may consult the document [R4.04.01](doc:r4.04.01).

For the study of the metallurgical transformations of steel, there exist five metallurgical phases: ferrite, pearlite, bainite, martensite (phases $\alpha$ ) and austenite (phase $\gamma$ ).

The effects of the metallurgical transformations (in the solid state) are of four types:

- the mechanical characteristics of the material which undergoes the transformations are modified. More precisely, the elastic characteristics (YOUNG's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ ) are little affected whereas the plastic characteristics, such as the yield stress, are strongly so,
- the volumetric expansion or contraction which accompanies the metallurgical transformations results in a (spherical) “transformation” strain which is superimposed on the strain of purely thermal origin. In general, one groups this effect with that due to the modification of the thermal expansion coefficient $\alpha$ ,
- a transformation taking place under stresses can give rise to an irreversible strain, even for stress levels very much lower than the yield stress of the material. This phenomenon is called “transformation plasticity”. The total strain $\varepsilon$ is then written:

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}^{e}+{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}+{\varepsilon}^{p}+{\varepsilon}^{\text{pt}}$

where ${\varepsilon}^{e}$ , ${\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}$ , ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ and ${\varepsilon}^{\text{pt}}$ are, respectively, the elastic, thermal, plastic and transformation-plasticity strains,

- during the metallurgical transformation one may have a phenomenon of recovery of hardening. The hardening of the parent phase is not totally transmitted to the newly created phases. These may then be born with a virgin hardening state or inherit only part, or even all, of the hardening of the parent phase. The cumulative plastic strain *p*  is then no longer characteristic of the hardening state and other hardening variables must be defined for each phase, denoted ${r}_{k}$ , which take account of the recovery. The evolution laws of these hardenings differ from the usual laws so as to allow a total, or partial, “return to zero” of these parameters during the transformations.

The expressions of the various constitutive relations will be found in the document [R4.04.02](doc:r4.04.02).

### 3.2 Model written in large strains

#### 3.2.1 General presentation

This model is an Eulerian thermo-elasto-plastic constitutive law written in large strains which was proposed by Simo and Miehe ([bib2]) and which tends, under the small-strain assumption, towards the model with isotropic hardening and von Mises criterion described in [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02). It makes it possible to treat not only large strains, but also, in an exact manner, large rotations.

The essential characteristics of this law are the following:

- just as in small strains, one assumes the existence of a relaxed configuration, that is locally stress-free, which makes it possible to decompose the total strain into a thermoelastic part and a plastic part,
- the decomposition of this strain into thermoelastic and plastic parts is no longer additive as in small strains (or for the large-strain models written in strain rates with for example a Jaumann derivative) but multiplicative,
- as in small strains, the stresses depend only on the thermoelastic strains,
- in order to write the constitutive law, one uses the Kirchhoff stress tensor $\tau$ which is related to the Cauchy tensor $\sigma$ by the relation $J\sigma =\tau$ where $J$ represents the variation of volume between the initial and current configurations,
- the plastic strains take place at constant volume. The variation of volume is then due only to the thermo-elastic strains,
- this model leads, during its numerical integration, to an incrementally objective model which makes it possible to obtain the exact solution in the presence of large rotations.

#### 3.2.2 Kinematics

We recall here a few basic notions of mechanics in large strains and of the constitutive model.

Consider a solid subjected to large strains. Let ${\Omega}_{0}$ be the domain occupied by the solid before deformation and $\Omega (t)$ the domain occupied at the instant $t$ by the deformed solid. In the initial configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ , the position of any particle of the solid is designated by $X$ (Lagrangian description). After deformation, the position at the instant $t$ of the particle which occupied the position $X$ before deformation is given by the variable $x$ (Eulerian description).

The global motion of the solid is defined, with $u$ the displacement, by:

$x=\stackrel{ˆ}{x}(X,t)=X+u$

In order to define the change of metric in the neighbourhood of a point, one introduces the transformation-gradient tensor $F$ :

$F=\frac{\partial \stackrel{ˆ}{x}}{\partial X}=\text{Id}+{\nabla}_{X}u$

The transformations of the volume element and of the density are:

$dO=\text{Jd}{O}_{o}$ with $J=\detF=\frac{{\rho}_{o}}{\rho}$

where ${\rho}_{o}$ and $\rho$ are respectively the density in the initial and current configurations.

In order now to write the large-strain model, one assumes the existence of a relaxed configuration ${\Omega}^{r}$ , that is locally stress-free, which then makes it possible to decompose the total strain into thermoelastic and plastic parts, this decomposition being multiplicative.

One will denote by $F$ the gradient tensor which takes one from the initial configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ to the current configuration $\Omega (t)$ , by ${F}^{p}$ the gradient tensor which takes one from the configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ to the relaxed configuration ${\Omega}^{r}$ , and by ${F}^{e}$ that which takes one from the configuration ${\Omega}^{r}$ to $\Omega (t)$ . The index $p$ refers to the plastic part, the index $e$ to the thermoelastic part.

![Figure](/figures/r4.04.03/10000FCA00001CD6000013A398ECE64086423371.svg)

**Figure 3.2.2-a: Decomposition of the gradient tensor** $F$ **into an elastic part** ${F}^{e}$ **and a plastic part** ${F}^{p}$

By composition of the motions, one obtains the following multiplicative decomposition:

$F={F}^{e}{F}^{p}$

The thermoelastic strains are measured in the current configuration with the left Eulerian Cauchy-Green tensor ${b}^{e}$ and the plastic strains in the initial configuration by the tensor ${G}^{p}$ (Lagrangian description). These two tensors are defined by:

${b}^{e}={F}^{e}{F}^{\text{eT}}$ , ${G}^{p}=({F}^{\text{pT}}{F}^{p}{)}^{-1}$ whence ${b}^{e}={\text{FG}}^{p}{F}^{T}$

The model presented is written in such a way as to distinguish the isochoric terms from the terms of change of volume. One introduces for this purpose the following two tensors:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{F}={J}^{-1/3}F$ and ${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}={J}^{-2/3}{b}^{e}$ with $J=\detF$

By definition, one has: $\det\stackrel{ˉ}{F}=1$  and $\det\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}=1$ .

In this model, the plastic strains take place at constant volume, so that:

${J}^{p}=\det{F}^{p}=1$ whence $J={J}^{e}=\det{F}^{e}$

The expressions of the constitutive relations will be found in the reference document ([R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21)).

## 4 Extension of the large-strain model

The objective of this section is to justify the extension of the model written in large strains in order to take account of the metallurgical transformations. In particular, in order to take account of transformation plasticity, we cannot add, as in small strains, an additional strain term related to transformation plasticity. In fact, as regards the kinematic decomposition, taking transformation plasticity into account changes nothing. One still has the decomposition $F={F}^{e}{F}^{p}$ where ${F}^{p}$ contains all the information on the “anelastic” strain (hence including that related to transformation plasticity). It is only at the level of the constitutive behaviour that the treatment of transformation plasticity is carried out, in particular.

First, we recall a few theoretical elements which make it possible to write the model without metallurgical effects, then we show the modifications to be made in order to take account of the metallurgical effects and of transformation plasticity in particular.

### 4.1 Thermodynamic aspect

The writing of the large-strain constitutive law comes from the thermodynamic framework with internal variables. The thermodynamic formalism rests on two assumptions. The first is that the free energy depends only on the elastic strains ${b}^{e}$ and on the internal variables related to the hardening of the material (here the cumulative plastic strain associated with the isotropic hardening variable $R$ ). This makes it possible, thanks to the Clausius-Duhem inequality, to obtain the state laws. The second assumption is the principle of maximum dissipation, which corresponds to the datum of a dissipation potential, which then makes it possible to determine the evolution laws of the internal variables.

The free energy is given by:

$\psi =\psi ({b}^{e},p)={\psi}^{e}({b}^{e})+{\psi}^{p}(p)$

One obtains, by the first assumption, the state laws, namely:

$\tau =2{\rho}_{0}\frac{\partial {\psi}^{e}}{\partial {b}^{e}}{b}^{e}$ and $R={\rho}_{0}\frac{\partial {\psi}^{p}}{\partial p}$

There remains, for the dissipation:

$\tau :(-\frac{1}{2}F{\dot{G}}^{p}{F}^{T}{b}^{e-1})-R\dot{p}\ge 0$

By introducing a threshold function such that $f(\tau ,R)\le 0$ , the principle of maximum dissipation (or equivalently the datum of a dissipation pseudo-potential [bib3]) makes it possible to deduce from it, by the normality property, the evolution laws, namely:

$-\frac{1}{2}F{\dot{G}}^{p}{F}^{T}{b}^{e-1}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \tau }$ and $\dot{p}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}$

This is a model of associated plasticity.

### 4.2 Extension

For the recovery of hardening, there are no particular difficulties related to large strains. It is sufficient that the free energy depend, no longer on the cumulative plastic strain, but on the internal hardening variables ${r}_{k}$ associated with the hardening variables ${Z}_{k}.{R}_{k}$ of each of the metallurgical phases.

In order now to take account of the strains due to transformation plasticity, one proposes to add an additional term in the flow rule of the plastic strain ${G}^{p}$ which derives from a dissipation potential $\Omega$ .

One thus obtains, for the state laws:

$\tau =2{\rho}_{0}\frac{\partial {\psi}^{e}}{\partial {b}^{e}}{b}^{e}$ and ${Z}_{k}.{R}_{k}={\rho}_{0}\frac{\partial {\psi}^{p}}{\partial {r}_{k}}$

and for the evolution laws:

$-\frac{1}{2}F{\dot{G}}^{p}{F}^{T}{b}^{e-1}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \tau }+\underset{\text{plasticité de transformation}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{\partial {\Omega}^{\text{pt}}}{\partial \tau }}}$

${\dot{r}}_{k}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial ({Z}_{k}.{R}_{k})}-\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{restauration d'écrouissage}\\ \text{métallurgique et visqueux}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{\partial {\Omega}^{r}}{\partial ({Z}_{k}.{R}_{k})}}}$

$\Omega ={\Omega}^{\text{pt}}(t)+{\Omega}^{r}$

One chooses the potentials ${\Omega}^{\text{pt}}$ and ${O}^{r}$ , related respectively to transformation plasticity and to the recovery of hardening, in such a way as to recover, under the small-strain assumption, the same evolution laws as those of the model with metallurgical effects written in small strains.

## 5 Numerical formulation

For the variational formulation, it is the same as that given in the document [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21) and which refers to the large-strain constitutive law. ### 5.1 Integration of the various constitutive relations

In the case of an incremental behaviour, factor keyword COMPORTEMENT, knowing the stress tensor ${\sigma}^{-}$ , the internal variables ${r}_{k}^{-}$ , the trace divided by three of the elastic strain tensor $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}$ , the displacements ${u}^{-}$ and $\Delta u$ , the temperatures ${T}^{-}$ and $T$ , and the proportions of the various metallurgical phases ${Z}_{k}$ , $\Delta {Z}_{k}$ , one seeks to determine $(\sigma ,{r}_{k},\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}})$ .

The displacements being known, the transformation gradients from ${O}_{0}$ to ${O}^{-}$ , denoted ${F}^{-}$ , and from ${O}^{-}$ to $\Omega (t)$ , denoted $\Delta F$ , are known.

One will set in what follows:

$\mathrm{DA}=\sum_{i=1}^{4}{K}_{i}{F}_{i}^{'}\langle {\mathrm{DZ}}_{i}\rangle$ , $\Delta {G}_{\gamma}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}\langle -\Delta {Z}_{i}\rangle ({\theta}_{i\gamma }{r}_{i}^{-}-{r}_{\gamma}^{-})}{{Z}_{\gamma}}$ and

$\Delta {G}_{i}=\frac{\langle \Delta {Z}_{i}\rangle ({\theta}_{\gamma i}{r}_{\gamma}^{-}-{r}_{i}^{-})}{{Z}_{i}}$ $(i=1,4)$

The **implicit**  discretisation of the law gives:

$F=\Delta {\text{F F}}^{-}$

$J=\detF$

$\stackrel{ˉ}{F}={J}^{-1/3}F$

${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}={J}^{-2/3}{b}^{e}$

$J\sigma =\tau$

$\tilde{\tau}=\mu {\tilde{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}}^{e}$

$\text{tr}\tau =\frac{\mathrm{3K}}{2}({J}^{2}-1)-\frac{\mathrm{9K}}{2}{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}(J+\frac{1}{J})$

${\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}={Z}_{\gamma}\left[{\alpha}_{\gamma}(T-{T}_{\text{ref}})-(1-{Z}_{\gamma}^{r})\Delta {\varepsilon}_{f\gamma }^{T\text{ref}}\right]+(\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i})\left[{\alpha}_{f}(T-{T}_{\text{ref}})+{Z}_{\gamma}^{r}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{f\gamma }^{T\text{ref}}\right]$

$f={\tau}_{\text{eq}}-(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}){R}_{\gamma}-\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{R}_{i}-{\sigma}_{y}$

${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}=\stackrel{ˉ}{F}{G}^{p}{\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}^{T}=\stackrel{ˉ}{F}{G}^{p-}{\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}^{T}-\frac{\Delta p\text{tr}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\tilde{\tau}-\Delta A\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}\tilde{\tau}$

If ${Z}_{\gamma}>0$ then $\Delta {r}_{\gamma}=\Delta p+\Delta {G}_{\gamma}-\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m}}}$ , otherwise ${r}_{\gamma}^{-}=0$ and $\Delta {r}_{\gamma}=0$

If ${Z}_{i}>0$ , $\Delta {r}_{i}=\Delta p+\Delta {G}_{i}-\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m}}}$ , otherwise ${r}_{i}^{-}=0$ and $\Delta {r}_{i}=0$

In the solution of this system, only the deviatoric stress $\tilde{\tau}$ is unknown because the trace of $\tau$ is a function only of $J$ (known).

One introduces ${\tau}^{\text{Tr}}$ , the Kirchhoff tensor which results from an elastic prediction ($\mathrm{Tr}$ : trial):

${\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}=\mu {\tilde{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}}^{\text{eTr}}$

where

${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}}=\stackrel{ˉ}{F}{G}^{p-}{\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}^{T}=\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e-}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}^{T}$ , $\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}=(\Delta J{)}^{-1/3}\Delta F$ and $\Delta J=\det(\Delta F)$

One obtains ${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e-}$ from the stresses ${\tau}^{-}$ by the stress – thermoelastic strain relation and from the trace of the elastic strain tensor.

${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e-}=\frac{{\tilde{\tau}}^{-}}{{\mu}^{-}}+\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e-}}$

One obtains for the Kirchhoff tensor $\tilde{\tau}$ :

$\tilde{\tau}=\mu {\stackrel{ˉ}{\tilde{b}}}^{\text{eTr}}-\mu \Delta p\frac{\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\tilde{\tau}-\mu \Delta \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}\tilde{\tau}$

If $f<0$ , one then has $\Delta p=0$ and:

$\tilde{\tau}=\frac{{\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}}{1+\mu \Delta \text{A tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}}$

otherwise one obtains:

$\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}=\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}$

$\begin{array}{}1+\mu \Delta p\frac{{\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\\ \\ \tilde{\tau}\left[+\mu \Delta \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{b}\text{eTr}\right]={\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}\end{array}$

By computing the equivalent stress, one obtains the following scalar equation in $\Delta p$ :

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}+\mu \Delta p\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}+\mu \Delta A{\tau}_{\text{eq}}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}={\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}$

**Expression of** ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}$ :

**In plasticity** : ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}+{R}^{'}\mathrm{Dp}+D({r}^{-}\text{;T,Z})$

with

${R}^{'}=(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}){R}_{0\gamma }+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{R}_{\mathrm{0i}}$

and $D({r}^{-}\text{;T,Z})=[1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}]{R}_{\gamma}({r}_{\gamma}^{-}+\Delta {G}_{\gamma})+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{R}_{i}({r}_{i}^{-}+\Delta {G}_{i})$

**In viscosity** :

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}+{R}^{'}\mathrm{\Delta p}+D({r}^{-}\text{;T,Z})+(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)){\eta}_{\gamma}(\Delta \text{p/}\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{1/{n}_{\gamma}}+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{\eta}_{i}(\Delta \text{p/}\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{1/{n}_{i}}$

with

$\begin{array}{}D({r}^{-}\text{;T,Z})=[1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}]{R}_{\gamma}({r}_{\gamma}^{-}+{\mathrm{DG}}_{\gamma}-\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m})\\ +\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{R}_{i}({r}_{i}^{-}+\Delta {G}_{i}-\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m})\end{array}$

$\Delta p$ satisfies:

$(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)){\eta}_{\gamma}(\mathrm{\Delta p}/\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{1/{n}_{\gamma}}+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{\eta}_{i}(\mathrm{\Delta p}/\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{1/{n}_{i}}=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}-\mathrm{\mu \Delta p}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}}{1+\mathrm{\mu \Delta A}\text{'tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}}-D({r}^{-}\text{;T,Z})-{\sigma}_{y}-{R}^{'}\mathrm{\Delta p}$

The solution is carried out in *Code_Aster*  by a secant method with a search interval [bib4].

**Note:**

*In the case of a non-linear isotropic hardening, the hardening slopes* ${R}_{\mathrm{0k}}$ *and the hardenings* ${R}_{k}$ *in the expressions of* ${R}^{'}$ *and* $D({r}^{-};T,Z)$ *correspond to the variables* ${r}_{k}$ *taken at the instant* $t$ *, that is* ${r}_{k}={r}_{k}^{-}+\Delta {G}_{k}+\Delta p-\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m}$ *. Now, since one does not know a priori the value of these variables* ${r}_{k}$ *, one solves the equation in* $\Delta p$ *by taking the slopes* ${R}_{\mathrm{0k}}$ *and the hardenings* ${R}_{k}$ *for the quantities* ${r}_{k}^{-}+\Delta {G}_{k}-\Delta t({\text{Cr}}_{\text{moy}}^{-}{)}^{m}$ *. Once the equation in* $\Delta p$ *has been solved, one checks, for each phase, that one is indeed in the correct interval when computing the hardening and the slope. Otherwise, for the phase or phases concerned, one takes the next interval and one solves the equation in* $\mathrm{Dp}$ *again. One continues this process until the correct interval is found for all the phases.*

One then finds for the stress deviator:

$\tilde{\tau}=\frac{1}{1+\mu \Delta A\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}}\left[1-\mu \frac{\Delta p\text{tr}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}}\right]{\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}$

Once the cumulative plastic strain, the stress tensor and the tangent matrix have been computed, one carries out a correction on the trace of the elastic strain tensor ${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}$ in order to take into account plastic incompressibility, which is not preserved with the simplification made to the flow rule [eq 4.3.1]. This correction is carried out by using a relation between the invariants of ${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e}$ and ${\tilde{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}}^{e}$ and by exploiting the plastic incompressibility condition ${J}^{p}=1$ (or equivalently $\det\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}=1$ ). This relation is written:

${x}^{3}-{\stackrel{ˉ}{J}}_{2}^{e}x-(1-{\stackrel{ˉ}{J}}_{3}^{e})=0$

with ${\stackrel{ˉ}{J}}_{2}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}({\stackrel{ˉ}{\tilde{b}}}^{e}{)}_{\text{eq}}^{2}=\frac{({\tau}_{\text{eq}}{)}^{2}}{2(\mu {)}^{2}}$ , ${\stackrel{ˉ}{J}}_{3}^{e}=\det\stackrel{ˉ}{\tilde{{b}^{e}}}=\det\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{\mu}$ and $x=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}$

The solution of this cubic equation makes it possible to obtain $\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}$ and consequently the thermoelastic strain ${\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{e-}$ at the next time step. In the case where this equation admits several solutions, one takes the solution closest to the solution of the previous time step. This is moreover why one stores in an internal variable $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}$ .

**Note** :

*In the case where transformation plasticity is not taken into account, the expressions obtained are the same by taking* $\Delta A=0$ .

*In the case where it is the recovery of hardening which is neglected, one likewise has the same expressions but by taking all the* $\theta$ *equal to 1.*

### 5.2 Expression of the tangent matrix

We give here only the expressions of the tangent matrix (option FULL_MECA during the Newton iterations, option RIGI_MECA_TANG for the first iteration). For the assumptions concerning the metallurgical part, they are the same as those of the document [R4.04.02](doc:r4.04.02). For the large-strain part, the detail of the linearisation of the constitutive law will be found in the appendix of [bib1].

One sets:

$J=\detF$ , ${J}^{-}=\det{F}^{-}$ and $\Delta J=\det\Delta F$

- For the option FULL_MECA, one has:

$\begin{array}{}\stackrel{ˉ}{A}=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta F}=\frac{(\Delta J{)}^{-1/3}}{J}H-\frac{1}{3J\Delta J}(H\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F})\otimes B-\frac{{J}^{-}}{{J}^{2}}\tau \otimes B\\ +\frac{{J}^{-}}{J}\left[\text{KJ}-\frac{3}{2}K{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}(1-{J}^{-2})\right]\text{Id}\otimes B\end{array}$

where $B$ is:

$\begin{array}{}{B}_{11}=\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{33}-\Delta {F}_{23}\Delta {F}_{32}\\ {B}_{22}=\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{33}-\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{31}\\ {B}_{33}=\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{22}-\Delta {F}_{12}\Delta {F}_{21}\\ {B}_{12}=\Delta {F}_{31}\Delta {F}_{23}-\Delta {F}_{33}\Delta {F}_{21}\\ {B}_{21}=\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{32}-\Delta {F}_{33}\Delta {F}_{12}\\ {B}_{13}=\Delta {F}_{21}\Delta {F}_{32}-\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{31}\\ {B}_{31}=\Delta {F}_{12}\Delta {F}_{23}-\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{13}\\ {B}_{23}=\Delta {F}_{31}\Delta {F}_{12}-\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{32}\\ {B}_{32}=\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{21}-\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{23}\end{array}$

and where $H$ and $H\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}$ are given by:

In the elastic case ($f<0$ ):

${H}_{ijkl}=\frac{\mu}{(1+\mu \Delta A\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}})}({\delta}_{\text{ik}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{jp}}+\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{ip}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{pl}}^{e-}{\delta}_{\text{jk}}-\frac{2}{3}{\delta}_{ij}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{kp}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}-2\Delta A{\tilde{\tau}}_{ij}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{kp}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{pl}}^{e-})$

and

$H\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}=\frac{2\mu }{(1+\mu \Delta A\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}})}({\tilde{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}}^{\text{eTr}}-\Delta A\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}\tilde{\tau})$

otherwise, in plastic or viscoplastic loading, one has:

$\begin{array}{}{H}_{ijkl}=\frac{\mu}{a}({\delta}_{\text{ik}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{jp}}+\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{ip}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{pl}}^{e-}{\delta}_{\text{jk}})\\ -2\mu \left[\frac{{\delta}_{ij}}{\mathrm{3a}}+\frac{{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}({\tau}_{\text{eq}}\Delta A+\Delta p){\tilde{\tau}}_{ij}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}+\mu \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}(1+{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta A))}\right]\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{kp}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\\ +\frac{3{\mu}^{2}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta p-{\tau}_{\text{eq}})}{a{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{3}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}+\mu \text{tr}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}}(1+{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta A))}{\tilde{\tau}}_{ij}{\tilde{\tau}}_{\text{kq}}\Delta {\stackrel{ˉ}{F}}_{\text{qp}}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\end{array}$

and

$\begin{array}{}H\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}=\frac{2\mu }{a}{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}}-2\mu \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}\left[\frac{\text{Id}}{\mathrm{3a}}+\frac{{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}({\tau}_{\text{eq}}\Delta A+\Delta p)\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}+\mu \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}(1+{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta A))}\right]\\ +\frac{3{\mu}^{2}\text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta p-{\tau}_{\text{eq}})}{a{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{3}({\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}+\mu \text{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{\text{eTr}}}(1+{\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}\Delta A))}(\tilde{\tau}:{\stackrel{ˉ}{b}}^{\text{eTr}})\tilde{\tau}\end{array}$

with

${\stackrel{ˉ}{R}}^{'}=(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}){R}_{\mathrm{0\gamma }}+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1,4}{Z}_{i}{R}_{\mathrm{0i}}+\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosite}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)){\eta}_{\gamma}(\mathrm{\Delta p}/\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{(1-{n}_{\gamma})/{n}_{\gamma}}/{n}_{\gamma}\mathrm{\Delta t}+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{\eta}_{i}(\mathrm{\Delta p}/\mathrm{\Delta t}{)}^{(1-{n}_{i})/{n}_{i}}/{n}_{i}\mathrm{\Delta t}}}$

$a=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$

- For the option RIGI_MECA_TANG

for the plastic model: these are the same expressions as those given for FULL_MECA but with $\Delta p=0$ and $\Delta A=0$ , all the variables and material coefficients being taken at the instant ${t}^{-}$ . In particular, one will have $\Delta \stackrel{ˉ}{F}=\text{Id}$ .

for the viscous model: one takes only the expressions of FULL_MECA in the elastic case with $\Delta A=0$ , all the variables being taken at the instant ${t}^{-}$ .
