# 1 **v6.05.114** SSNS114 – Degradation of a reinforced concrete plate under various stresses with BETON_REGLE_PR law

**Summary:**

This test validates the constitutive law nonlinear concrete BETON_REGLE_PR (see [R7.01.27](doc:r7.01.27)) for various cyclic loads: traction/compression, alternating bending, shear in the plane and their combinations. Multilayer modelling is used, in which the steels of the rebars are represented by GRILLE_EXCENTRE. The analyses are done in static (STAT_NON_LINE). The results are compared with those of modelling with the ENDO_ISOT_BETON behavior model (see [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04)).

Modelling H tests thermomechanical processes. In this Modelling, it is verified that the two thermal expansion loads (homogeneous temperature in thickness and constant temperature gradient in thickness) lead to the same stress state as two simple mechanical loads: Ox extension and flexion around Oy.

The modellings E to G correspond to the modellings A to C for higher levels of stress.

**Figure** 1.1-a **: geometry of square reinforced concrete plate**

Length: $L=1.0m$; Plate thickness: $e=0.1m$ .

Steel diameter: $0.01m$ . Coating of the lower and upper rebar sheets of the steels according to the $z$ axis : $0.01m$ .

The Modelling is based on a semi-global multilayer plate Modelling. Concrete and reinforcements are modeled separately. For each rebar sheet, one considers a layer that behaves only in the longitudinal direction of the reinforcements. So one will have 4 layers for the rebar.

On the same mesh 5 models representing reinforced concrete plate are defined: 1 DKT model for concrete and 4 GRILLE models for reinforcements (2 in $X$ direction, 2 in $Y$ direction for lower and upper parts). The rate of ironing for each rebar table is $8.0\times {10}^{-4}{m}^{2}/m$.

The position of the frames (lower or higher) is defined by the key word EXCENTREMENT under the key word factor GRILLE in the AFFE_CARA_ELEM operator, which is $\pm 0.04m$ *:* so it is assumed here that the steels in $X$ and $Y$ are in the same position, which is the usual approximation of the multilayer models.

### 1.2 Material properties

Concrete cracking is modelled by constitutive law BETON_REGLE_PR, while steel modeled by GRILLE_ISOT_LINE.

**Beton (model** **BETON_REGLE_PR)**:

Young's modulus: ${E}_{b}=32308.0\mathrm{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio: ${\nu}_{b}=0.20$

Threshold of single traction damage ${\sigma}_{y}^{t}$ : $3.4\mathrm{MPa}$

Softening pent: ${E}_{T}=-0.2{E}_{b}$

Threshold of damage in single compression ${\sigma}_{y}^{c}$ : $32.308\mathit{MPa}$

Deformation at ${\varepsilon}_{c}$ compression threshold: $0.002$

> Exposing the law of compression hardening $n$ : $2$

** Steel**:

Young's modulus : ${E}_{a}=200000.0\mathrm{MPa}$

Linearity limit ${\sigma}_{e}^{\text{acier}}$: $570.0\mathrm{MPa}$

Post-elastic pent ${E}_{\text{écrouis}}^{\text{acier}}$ : $\text{}=0.0015{E}_{a}=300\mathrm{MPa}$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Different modellings *A to H* are considered for different types of characteristic loads and different plate behaviors. In all cases, loads are displacements (or rotations) imposed on the edges of the plate, differently for each model.

The modellings considered are:

**modelling A (§** 3 **)**: traction – compression – pure traction;

**modelization B (§** 4 **)**: pure alternating bending;

**modelization C (§** 5 **)**: tensile coupling - compression and bending;

**modelization D (§** 6 **)**: pure shear and distortion in the plane;

**modelling E (§** 7 **)**: traction – pure compression, high stress;

**modelling F (§** 8 **)**: pure alternating bending, high stress;

**modelling G (§** 9 **)**: tensile/compression coupling and bending, high loads;

**modelization H (§** 10 **)**: thermal loading.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Initially, the displacements and constraints are identically zero.

### 3.5 Remarks

Based on the previous curves, the multilayer model with BETON_REGLE_PR law represents the overall behaviour of reinforced concrete in traction – pure compression in a satisfactory manner in charge. However, in landfill, the BETON_REGLE_PR law follows the same curve as the load in contrast to the ENDO_ISOT_BETON law.

The effect of Poisson is not modelled by BETON_REGLE_PR law, so a zero displacement in the transverse direction DY is obtained.

A symmetry of the response is observed according to the chosen direction of compression-traction load or reverse, depending on the $\mathit{f1}$ or $\mathit{f2}$ load for BETON_REGLE_PR law.

## 11 Summary of the results

These tests, designed to validate constitutive law concrete BETON_REGLE_PR, are also used to show a number of its weaknesses. The different modellingss allow testing:

**A and E**: Tensile/compression behaviour

**Bet F**: the behaviour in cyclic bending

**This G**: behavior that combines membrane and bending phenomena

**D**: shear and distortion behaviour in the plane

**H**:a thermal load.

In most of these situations, the forces and moments predicted by the BETON_REGLE_PR model are consistent with the ENDO_ISOT_BETON model for load. In the case of discharge, the BETON_REGLE_PR model, which is elastic, presents significant errors compared to the ENDO_ISOT_BETON model. Furthermore, the Poisson effect being not modelled by the BETON_REGLE_PR model, one measures significant differences in the displacements perpendicular to the loading direction.

Compared to test case ssns106, the case of bending and shear coupling in the plane (modellings D and M of test case ssns106) is not modelled here, as there are difficulties in convergence with BETON_REGLE_PR law for these cases.
