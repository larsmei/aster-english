# u4.84.47 Macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE

## 1 Purpose

Check the static stability of embankment structures modelled in 2D, such as embankment dams, slopes and the dykes around reservoirs, *etc*.

The macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE makes it possible to compute the factor of safety representing the available stability margin, defined by the ratio between the shear resistance force and the driving force induced by the loadings applied. CALC_STAB_PENTE groups two categories of methods, thus making it possible to equip oneself with the means of verification, and to adapt to the practices of the hydraulic industry in the different regions of the world.

- The first type of analysis (`METHODE_STAB = "SRM"`) is carried out by progressive reduction of the shear-strength parameters (“*strength reduction method*” in English, called SRM) until divergence of the finite-element analysis. This divergence reflects the instant of failure of the global structure. By the definition of an **SRM zone**, it is possible to indicate directly the slope to be analysed, if several exist in the model.
- The second type of analysis (`METHODE_STAB = "LEM"`) is based on the limit-equilibrium theory (LEM). One computes the factor of safety by solution of the balance of the forces and of the moments of the soil slices considered as rigid solids. Under seismic conditions, it is also possible to solve for the critical-acceleration coefficient instead of the FS by activating `ACCE/CALC_KC`. The result is therefore useful in the macro-command `POST_NEWMARK` ([U4.84.45](doc:u4.84.45)) in order to evaluate the irreversible displacement caused by the earthquake. The macro has optimisation algorithms in order to locate the failure surface. 4 LEM methods are available:

  - `"FELLENIUS"` and `"BISHOP"` simplified for the **circular failure** surfaces.
  - `"SPENCER"` and `"MORGENSTERN-PRICE"` for the **non-circular failure** surfaces.

The macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE supplies as output a table containing the factors of safety (FS) tested and the information about the geometry of the failure surface. If requested, the macro-command generates in addition an `evol_noli` concept for visualisation of the failure surface.

## 3 Description of the macro-command

According to the recommendations, the justification of the stability of embankment dams and dykes, the verification of the stability of the slopes are indispensable both at the design stage and during operation of this kind of structure.

This analysis has the objective of preventing the sudden failure of the slope by evaluating quantitatively the risk of failure, i.e. the factor of safety. The stability of the slopes depends at once on the geometry, on the mechanical properties of the embankment, and on the mechanical and hydraulic loadings such as gravity, the hydrostatic pressure and the pore pressure generated by the infiltration of water within the structure.

The macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE makes it possible to compute the factor of safety via two approaches:

1. **SRM method**: the method of progressive reduction of the parameters linked to

the shear strength of the material (named the SRM method).
This method was proposed by Griffiths [1] in 1999.
The SRM method implemented has been validated only on slopes modelled in 2D.
Use on 3D models is also possible but the relevance
of the result must be checked by the user.

2. **LEM method**: a series of methods based on the limit-equilibrium theory (named the LEM method),

including the simplified Bishop, Fellenius, Spencer and Morgenstern-Price procedures [2].
These methods cut the slope into several slices considered as rigid bodies.
Given a potential failure surface, the computation of the factor of safety is done by
solution of the balance of the forces and of the moments of the set of slices.
The factor of safety is defined by the ratio between the resistance force
and the driving force along the failure surface. CALC_STAB_PENTE
is also equipped with optimisation algorithms in order to locate the failure surfaces minimising
the factor of safety.

The **SRM** method takes advantage of the relevance and of the fidelity of the finite-element method
in order to analyse the stability of the slopes without needing to assume in advance
the slip circles.

Indeed, by using the failure criterion of Mohr-Coulomb type, the SRM method defines
the factor of safety as being the ratio between the initial material parameters and
those reduced leading to the divergence of the non-linear analysis under the loadings applied:

$\mathit{FS}=\dfrac{c}{{c}_{i}}=\dfrac{\tan(\varphi )}{\tan({\varphi}_{i})}=\dfrac{\tan(\psi )}{\tan({\psi}_{i})}$

where

- $\psi$ is the dilatancy angle of the material,
- $c$ and $\varphi$ represent respectively the cohesion force and

the friction angle of the embankment.

CALC_STAB_PENTE makes it possible to define the SRM zone by an independent mesh-cell group.

**The advantages of the SRM method are the following:**

- Possibility of applying various types of mechanical loading, such as the

hydrostatic pressure and the inertia force under seismic conditions (pseudo-static).

- Computation of a stress field faithful to reality without the necessity of presupposing

the failure surfaces, which leads to an FS result more relevant than that of the LEM method.

- Natural visualisation of the failure surface characterised by the high plastic strain.

If the user is not sufficiently experienced, the SRM method will generally
take more analysis time and the relevance of the result is not guaranteed
because of a poorly configured FEM analysis.

Unlike the SRM method, the **LEM** method is, for its part, independent of the FEM analysis.
Nevertheless, the LEM method requires one to presuppose the potential failure surfaces and makes an assumption
on the interaction forces between the slices. Given the simplicity and the efficiency of the analysis,
the LEM method has become the most popular in the hydraulic industry and is
recommended by the regulations for the design and verification of embankment structures.

At the output of the macro-command one recovers a table to the left of the equals sign,
whose contents depend on the analysis method chosen:

- **In the case of SRM**: the table contains the factors of safety tested during the iterations,

as well as the values of the maximum total displacement in the SRM zone. This table facilitates both
the recovery of the stability result and the verification of its relevance.
Indeed, the factor of safety is found in the last line of the table.
The convergence of the algorithm is checked by a progressively “flattened” plot of the
factor of safety as a function of the maximum displacement.

- **In the case of LEM**: the table contains the factors of safety of the critical surface in the course of the mesh refinement, as well as the geometrical parameters of the failure surface. With the detailed output mode activated (`INFO_TABLE=2`), the table also contains the statistics of the potential surfaces visited, which make it possible to justify the result and to check the convergence of the optimisation algorithm.

  - For the Bishop and Fellenius procedures, the failure surface is described by the abscissae of the entry and exit points, the radius and the coordinates of the centre of the circle.
  - For the Spencer and Morgenstern-Price procedures, the failure surface is described by the abscissae of the entry and exit points, and the ordinates of the intermediate points describing the multi-linear surface.

The macro-command supplies the option of visualising the slip surface. If requested, a concept of type `evol_noli`
will be produced for this purpose.

## 4 Operands

### 4.1 Operands CHAM_MATER

```text
♦ CHAM_MATER= cham_mater
```

Name of the material field assigned on the model mo.

One must make sure that the chmat has been created using the same mesh as that of the model `modele`.
Otherwise, a fatal error will occur.

### 4.2 Operand METHODE_STAB = 'SRM'

```text
♦     METHODE_STAB=    'SRM'
```

By choosing the SRM method, the slope-stability analysis is based on the non-linear solver
STAT_NON_LINE with a view to seeking iteratively the factor of safety until the analysis diverges.
Consequently, the inputs of this latter command are also required by the macro-command.

#### 4.2.1 Operand MODELE

```text
♦ MODELE= modele
```

Name of the model whose elements are the subject of the mechanical analysis.
With the THM model (see [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11)), the macro-command takes into account the
hydraulic effect during the stability analysis, which favours above all the evaluation
of the stability of dams and dykes.

On the other hand, the models requiring the definition of elementary characteristics via
the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM are not authorised by the macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE.

#### 4.2.2 Operand GROUP_MA/TOUT

```text
♦    /GROUP_MA = grma
     /TOUT = 'OUI'
```

List of the mesh-cell groups defining the zone where the SRM algorithm applies.
If one analyses the stability of the whole model, one sets the operand TOUT = 'OUI'.

In the case where the mesh-cell groups differ from those which are used in
the assignment of the material to the mesh, the macro-command will identify the materials affecting the SRM zone
on the basis of the material field `chmat` as input, and computes the mesh-cell groups of intersection
between the SRM zone and the zone of assignment of the material in question with a view to assigning the degraded material
in the SRM zone by the overloading principle.

#### 4.2.3 Keyword EXCIT

```text
♦    EXCIT = _F()
```

Factor keyword making it possible to describe at each occurrence a load (excitation and boundary condition),
and possibly a multiplying coefficient and/or a type of load.

The configuration of this factor keyword is identical to that of the command STAT_NON_LINE (see [u4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)),
**except that the types of load are limited to 'FIXE_CSTE', 'SUIV' and 'DIDI'**.

The types of load linked to the PILOTAGE functionality have been eliminated in the macro-command
CALC_STAB_PENTE since they are in contradiction with the SRM algorithm.

#### 4.2.4 Keywords INCREMENT, CONVERGENCE and COMPORTEMENT

```text
♦    INCREMENT= _F()
```

Factor keyword defining the time intervals taken in the
incremental method (see STAT_NON_LINE [u4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03))

```text
♦    CONVERGENCE= _F()
```

Factor keyword making it possible to control the convergence criteria
during the non-linear analysis carried out by the command STAT_NON_LINE ([u4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)).

```text
♦    COMPORTEMENT= _F()
```

Factor keyword defining the constitutive relations.
It is a keyword common to several commands.
Its syntaxes are described in the document [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11).

1. The use of the option of automatic cutting of the time steps defined

by the command `DEFI_LIST_INST` with the operand `ECHEC/ACTION = 'DECOUPE'` (in the factor keyword `INCREMENT`)
is strongly recommended. Indeed, the splitting of the increment over several levels makes it possible to avoid the stopping of CALC_STAB_PENTE because of the divergence of the Newtonian algorithm before the formation of a through-going plastified surface marking the true failure of the slope. Likewise, increasing `ITER_GLOG_MAXI` in the keyword `CONVERGENCE` also favours the capture of the exact instant of collapse of the slope.

2. In the factor keyword `COMPORTEMENT` it is possible to define different constitutive

relations on the different parts of the model.
However, similarly to the processing of the material field in the SRM zone,
the program checks that there exists one and only one constitutive behaviour among MOHR_COULOMB and DRUCKER_PRAGER
in the SRM zone. Otherwise, a fatal error will be produced.
Furthermore, the definition of constitutive relations on the mesh cells is not authorised
by the macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE.

#### 4.2.5 Keyword FS

```text
◊     FS= _F()
```

Factor keyword defining the parameters of the SRM algorithm, including the initial
value of the factor of safety, the initial increment and the refinement method.

##### 4.2.5.1 Operand FS_INIT

```text
◊     FS_INIT = f0
```

This operand makes it possible to supply the initial value of the factor of safety.

1. It is the reduction coefficient of the shear-strength properties for the first SRM iteration. Therefore, the closer this initial value is to the real factor of safety, the shorter the analysis will last. This value is often obtained by an empirical method or by previous tests, but can never exceed the real value, otherwise the analysis will diverge at the first SRM iteration and a fatal error will be produced. `f0` must be strictly positive and the default value is 1.0.
2. In the case of the fatal error informing that `f0` is too small, one must not intuitively

increase it and relaunch the SRM analysis. It is preferable first to examine
the configuration of the non-linear analysis, since it is probable that the divergence of the analysis
at the first SRM iteration is due to a poor configuration of the solver STAT_NON_LINE.

##### 4.2.5.2 Operand INCR_INIT

```text
◊     INCR_INIT= p0
```

This operand makes it possible to define the initial increment of the factor of safety.

##### 4.2.5.3 Operand RESI_MAXI

```text
◊     RESI_MAXI= pfin
```

This operand makes it possible to define the maximum residual of the factor of safety.

The factor of safety finally obtained is within `pfin` of its real value.
This operand therefore defines the precision of the stability analysis. Its default value is
fixed at 0.01 according to the practice of the hydraulic profession.
The large gap between p0 and `pfin` will lead to a long analysis duration if one does not impose
the number of refinements via the keyword ITER_RAFF_LINE.

##### 4.2.5.4 Operand ITER_MAXI

```text
◊     ITER_MAXI = iter_max
```

This operand defines the maximum number of SRM iterations.
It serves to avoid an inefficient analysis because of the inappropriate configuration of the keyword FS.
The analysis stops with the fatal error if iter_max is reached without the algorithm having converged.

##### 4.2.5.5 Operand METHODE

```text
◊     METHODE= /'EXPONENTIELLE'
               /'LINEAIRE'
```

This operand defines the law of variation of the increment of the factor of safety. Two options are available:

1. **'EXPONENTIELLE'**: Exponential variation
  The increment of the factor of safety approaches the maximum residual in an exponential manner.

At the nth refinement, the increment is computed by:
  ${p}_{n}={p}_{0}{2}^{-n}$
  until ${p}_{N}<{p}_{\mathit{fin}}$ .

2. **'LINEAIRE'**: Linear variation
  The increment of the factor of safety approaches the maximum residual in a linear manner.

In that case an additional operand ITER_RAFF_LINE is necessary.
At the nth refinement, the increment is computed by:
  ${p}_{n}={p}_{0}-n\dfrac{{p}_{0}-{p}_{\mathit{fin}}}{N}$
  until ${p}_{N}={p}_{\mathit{fin}}$.

The advantage of the exponential method is that the analysis converges more rapidly,
whereas the linear method controls the precision of the result better.
The exponential variation of the increment is suited above all to the analysis with a large INCR_INIT.

The following method would simplify the choice between the two laws of variation of the increment.
Let $N$ be the number of refinements desired; one chooses the linear law if:

$\dfrac{{p}_{0}-{p}_{\mathit{fin}}}{{\log}_{2}({p}_{0})-{\log}_{2}({p}_{\mathit{fin}})}<N$

and one chooses the exponential law otherwise.

##### 4.2.5.6 Operand ITER_RAFF_LINE

```text
◊     ITER_RAFF_LINE= nbpas
```

This operand makes it possible to define the number of refinements of the increment if the linear law of variation is selected.

### 4.3 Operand METHODE_STAB = 'LEM'

```text
♦     METHODE_STAB=    'LEM'
```

By choosing the LEM method, the computation of the factor of safety no
longer relies on the finite-element method; nevertheless it still relies
on the mesh with a view to dividing the slope into slices and to computing
the mass of the slices via the operator POST_ELEM.

#### 4.3.1 Operand CRITERE

```text
◊     CRITERE = /'MOHR_COULOMB'
                /'DRUCK_PRAGER'
```

This operand makes it possible to indicate the failure criterion to be used in order to compute
the tangential resistance force. In all cases one converts the
parameters of the Mohr-Coulomb or Drucker-Prager criterion into the friction angle and the cohesion
in order to inject them into the formulations of the computation of the factor of safety according to
the procedure METHODE_LEM selected.

#### 4.3.2 Operand GROUP_MA

```text
♦     GROUP_MA=    profil_pente
```

This operand defines the group of line mesh cells representing the profile of the slope.
It is mandatory to indicate it in order to:

- Estimate the ordinates of the entry and exit points.
- Check the potential failure surface so that it lies within

the model but never too close to the profile of the slope.

- Estimate the slope rate at the ends of the failure surface in order to determine

the direction of the interaction force between the slices.

The way in which the profile of the slope intervenes in the various algorithms is explained in more detail in [R7.05.02](doc:r7.05.02). The user must make sure that the profile of the slope supplied includes the search bands of the entry and exit points, and that it satisfies the needs mentioned above.

It is advised to choose a profile of the slope which contains the entire slope as well
as a part of the flattened surfaces upstream and downstream.
An example of the profile of the slope is shown in fig1-geom-lem.

#### 4.3.3 Operand NB_TRANCHE

```text
◊ NB_TRANCHE = / 10
               / Nt
```

This operand defines the number of slices dividing the slope.
One must note that the LEM analysis will be all the slower as one increases
the number of slices. However, one must increase the number of slices
if one analyses the stability of stratified structures whose failure surface
is probably non-circular.

#### 4.3.4 Operands X1_MINI, X1_MAXI, X2_MINI and X2_MAXI

```text
♦     X1_MINI =     x1_min
♦     X1_MAXI =     x1_max
♦     X2_MINI =     x2_min
♦     X2_MAXI =     x2_max
```

These operands define the search bands of the abscissae of the entry
and exit points of the potential failure surface.
By convention, `x1_min` and `x2_min` must be less than `x1_max` and `x2_max`
respectively. An example of the definition of X1 and X2 is shown in fig1-geom-lem.

#### 4.3.5 Keyword RAFF_MAIL

```text
RAFF_MAIL = _F(
       ◊ NB_RAFF_MAXI = / 4
                        / N_raff
       ◊ RAFF_CRIT_STAB = / 1e-3
                          / raff_crit_stab
       )
```

Factor keyword defining the parameters linked to the algorithm of refinement of the mesh
on the contour of the slices. The refinement via the macro-command `MACR_ADAP_MAIL` is driven
by the proximity field measuring the distance between the nodes and the contour of the slices.
The complete procedure is illustrated in [R7.05.02](doc:r7.05.02).

The operand `NB_RAFF_MAXI` defines the maximum number of refinements.
This operand makes it possible to limit the number of refinements in order to be able to
consult the FS result more rapidly, at the expense of the precision of the analysis,
if the FS convergence criterion is not satisfied.

The operand `RAFF_CRIT_STAB` defines the criterion of stabilisation of the factor of safety in the course of the mesh refinement. The algorithm stops if the absolute value of the variation of FS is less than raff_crit_stab.

#### 4.3.6 Keyword CHAM_PRES

```text
◇ CHAM_PRES = _F(
        ◆ RESULTAT = evol_noli,
        ◆ / INST = float,
          / NUME_ORDRE = int,
        ◇ PRECISION = float (défaut: 1e-06),
        ◇ ALGO_PRES = / "INVERSE",
                      / "PROJECTION" (par défaut),
    ),
```

Factor keyword making it possible to couple the hydraulic pressure field coming from the THM model with the stability analysis. The field **DEPL** in the evol_noli result entered in the operand `RESULTAT` must comprise the component **PRE1** and possibly **PRE2** for the unsaturated models. In the absence of PRE2, the air pressure is assumed zero. The pressure field corresponding to the time instant indicated by the operand `INST` or `NUME_ORDRE` will intervene in the evaluation of the shear strength along the failure surface.

Two interpolation methods are available in the operand `ALGO_PRES`:

- `INVERSE`: Inverse-distance-weighted interpolation (IDW). The power value is fixed at 2. It is a simple and efficient method. However, the weighting of the distant nodes decreasing rapidly as a function of the distance, only the nodes in the immediate neighbourhood influence the interpolation result. This method is therefore suited rather to a fine mesh.
- `PROJECTION`: The interpolation is done by PROJ_CHAMP [U4.72.05](doc:u4.72.05) by means of the shape functions of the finite elements. This method is more reassuring than the IDW.

#### 4.3.7 Keyword LIGN_PHREA

```text
◇ LIGN_PHREA = _F(
        ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
          / GROUP_MA = grma,
        ◆ INDIC_PIEZO = (int, ),
        ◆ TABLE = table,
        ◇ PRES_MAX = float,
    ),
```

Factor keyword defining the phreatic lines for the whole model or by zone in the form of an aster_table entered in the operand `TABLE`. The operand `INDIC_PIEZO` makes it possible to indicate the two indices of the columns in the table which will define respectively the abscissa and the pressure head. One determines the capillary pressure at the point with the coordinates $(x, y)$ such that:

$P_c(x, y) = \rho_W g \left[y - y_w(x)\right]cos^2A(x)$

where $A(x)$ is the local angle of inclination of the phreatic line with respect to the horizontal.

The air pressure is assumed zero. One observes that the capillary pressure is positive above the phreatic line and proportional to $y$. This will become problematic if one computes the strength due to the matric suction by the simple parameter `PHI_B`, because this additional strength term will tend towards infinity, which is inconsistent with reality. Therefore the operand `PRES_MAX` makes it possible to limit the positive capillary pressure in order to avoid obtaining an unrealistic result.

#### 4.3.8 Keyword COEF_RU

```text
◇ COEF_RU = _F(
        ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
          / GROUP_MA = grma,
        ◇ VALE = float,
    ),
```

Factor keyword defining by zone the pore-pressure coefficient. This parameter $R_u$ is defined by the ratio between the pore-water pressure and the overburden stress applied on the base of the slices:

$R_u = \frac{u}{\rho_s g H_s}$

This simplest approach for considering the hydraulic effect was proposed for the first time by Bishop and Morgenstern in 1960 [3]. However, this ratio $R_u$ may vary in the slope if the real phreatic line is not parallel to the surface of the slope. One must therefore use it with caution. It is advised to measure $R_u$ at several points in the structure and to compute the mean value in order to avoid an erroneous result.

#### 4.3.9 Operand SUCCION

```text
◇ SUCCION = "OUI"
```

The operand `SUCCION` makes it possible to take into account the shear strength due to the matric suction in the unsaturated soil above the phreatic line, with the aim of obtaining a less conservative stability result. There exist two options for computing the strength with matric suction:

**Option 1**

One defines a constant angle $\varphi_b$ expressing the increase of the strength as a function of the suction. One determines the strength such that:

$S = c' + (\sigma_n - p_a)\tan\varphi' + p_c \tan\varphi_b$

**Option 2**

One computes the strength according to the hydraulic constitutive behaviour supplied by the user in `DEFI_MATERIAU/THM_DIFFU`. As proposed by Vanapalli et al[4], the strength is expressed by:

$S = c' + (\sigma_n - p_a)\tan\varphi' + p_c \left(\frac{\theta_w - \theta_r}{\theta_s - \theta_r}\right) \tan\varphi'$

where $\theta_w$, $\theta_s$, $\theta_r$ represent respectively the water saturation, the saturation in the saturated state and the residual saturation. According to the properties supplied in the cham_mater as input, one determines the effective saturation, which is expressed by:

$S_{we} = \frac{\theta_w - \theta_r}{\theta_s - \theta_r}$

either by the parameters of the Van-Genuchten model, or by the function $\theta_{w}(p_c)$ defined via the operand `SATU_PRES` in `DEFI_MATERIAU/THM_DIFFU`. In the second case, $\theta_s$ and $\theta_r$ take the value of the first and of the last element in the list defining the function.

#### 4.3.10 Keyword PHI_B

```text
◇ PHI_B = _F(
            ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
              / GROUP_MA = grma,
            ◆ VALE = float,
        ),
```

Factor keyword defining the value of the matric-suction angle $\varphi_b$ by zone, on condition of having activated `SUCCION`.

#### 4.3.11 Operand INFO_TABLE

```text
◇ INFO_TABLE = / 1 (par défaut),
               / 2,
```

By default one supplies as output the simplified result containing only the factors of safety / critical-acceleration coefficient at each iteration of the mesh refinement, as well as the geometrical parameters of the critical surface. By setting `INFO_TABLE=2`, one adds furthermore in the output table the statistics of these parameters and the intermediate results of the optimisation algorithm. The detailed explanation of each column in the output table is found in table-1 and table-2.

| FS/KC | Factor of safety or critical-acceleration coefficient. |
| X_ENTRE | Abscissa of the left-hand end of the failure surface. |
| Y_ENTRE | Ordinate of the left-hand end of the failure surface. |
| X_SORTIE | Abscissa of the right-hand end of the failure surface. |
| Y_SORTIE | Ordinate of the right-hand end of the failure surface. |
| CENTRE_X | Abscissa of the centre of the failure surface. |
| CENTRE_Y | Ordinate of the centre of the failure surface. |
| RAYON | Radius of the failure surface. |
| NB_SURF_TEST* | Number of potential surfaces tested. |
| EXT_CENTRE_X* | Extremum X of the centres. |

| HIST_CENTRE_X* | History of the abscissa of the centres. |
| HIST_CENTRE_Y* | History of the ordinate of the centres. |
| HIST_FS/KC* | History of the FS/KC. |

*Additional results when `INFO_TABLE=2`.

- For the Morgenstern-Price and Spencer methods (non-circular surface)

| Result | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| NUME_RAFF | Iteration number of the mesh refinement. |
| FS/KC | Factor of safety or critical-acceleration coefficient. |

| HIST_FS/KC* | History of the FS/KC minima during the EFWA iterations. |

*Additional results when `INFO_TABLE=2`.

Owing to the limitations of the optimisation algorithm and to an incorrect search zone, the result of the stability analysis by LEM may be erroneous without a software error. However, the output table makes it possible to check and to justify the result manually. More precisely, it is advised to examine the following points:

1. **Convergence of FS/KC**: Plot `FS/KC` against `NUME_RAFF` in order to check whether the number of refinements is sufficient. In the case of non-convergence because `NUME_RAFF_MAXI` has been reached, increase the maximum number of refinements or adjust the input mesh.
2. **Convergence of the optimisation algorithm**: With `INFO_TABLE = 2`, one easily justifies the configuration of the optimisation algorithm. For the Bishop and Fellenius methods, one checks that the minimum of `HIST_FS/KC` lies right in the middle of the band of radii tested for each combination of the entry and exit points. For the Morgenstern and Spencer methods, one plots `HIST_FS/KC` as a function of `NUME_ITER` so as to check the convergence graphically. In the case of non-convergence, one examines the extrema of the geometrical parameters of the potential surfaces visited and then corrects the configuration of the optimisation algorithm.

#### 4.3.12 Operands PHI_C_EQUI and TOLE_DIST

```text
◇ PHI_C_EQUI = "OUI",
◇ TOLE_DIST = float,
```

The operand `PHI_C_EQUI` makes it possible to activate the computation of the equivalent cohesion and friction angle on the base of the slices which cross several materials:

$c_{eq} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N_c}c_kl_k}{L}, \quad \tan\varphi_{eq} = \frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N_c}\tan\varphi_kl_k}{L}$

where $N_c$ is the number of materials on the base of the slices, $l_k$ is the base length in material k, $L$ is the total length of the base.

The operand `TOLE_DIST` defines the distance tolerance for distinguishing two different materials. If this value is sufficiently high, `PHI_C_EQUI` will become ineffective. In the contrary case, one manages to identify the soil layers of very small thickness, at the expense of the analysis time. By default, this parameter is adaptive and equal to 5% of the base length.

#### 4.3.13 Operand FONC_PRES

```text
◇ FONC_PRES = fonction / formule
```

The operand `FONC_PRES` makes it possible to apply a distributed pressure on the surface of the slope defined in the operand `GROUP_MA` in the form of a pressure function or formula with the coordinates `X` and `Y` as variables. One evaluates the nodal force at the nodes of the 1D elements constituting the slope profile. One thus computes the force and the moment applied on the soil slices by trapezoidal integration.

#### 4.3.14 Keyword ACCE

```text
◇ ACCE = _F(
        ◆ / CALC_KC = "OUI" ,
          / COEF_ACCE = float,
        ◇ DIRECTION = float,
    ),
```

Factor keyword defining the inertia force caused by the earthquake. By the principle of pseudo-static analysis, one models the seismic effect by the inertia force computed from the acceleration coefficient $k_a$ defined via `COEF_ACCE` and the direction vector $\overrightarrow{r}$ defined via `DIRECTION` such that:

$F_{i} = \rho_s g k_a \frac{\overrightarrow{r}}{\Vert \overrightarrow{r} \Vert_2}$

It is also possible to solve for the critical-acceleration coefficient denoted $k_c$ and the associated critical surface, which may then be used as input to the macro-command `POST_NEWMARK` [U4.84.45](doc:u4.84.45) in order to compute the irreversible displacement of the slope at the end of the earthquake. To do this, one simply has to activate the operand `CALC_KC`, which then excludes the two other operands.

#### 4.3.15 Operand METHODE_LEM = 'BISHOP' or 'FELLENIUS'

```text
◊     METHODE_LEM=    /'BISHOP'
                      /'FELLENIUS'
```

The operand METHODE_LEM makes it possible to switch between the different procedures
for the stability analysis by the LEM method. This operand determines at the same time
the objective function of the optimisation algorithm.

When METHODE_LEM = 'BISHOP' or 'FELLENIUS', one makes
the assumption that the failure surface is circular and one computes the factor
of safety by assuming that the interaction forces between the slices are horizontal (BISHOP)
or zero (FELLENIUS). The surface being circular, one seeks the critical surface by visiting
all the combinations of entry and exit points. For each combination, one automatically computes
the radius minimising FS. Two approaches are available for determining the trial bands of the radius:

- **Option 1**: One visits all the radii which form a kinematically

admissible failure surface.

- **Option 2**: One defines manually the trial band by defining the

horizontal straight lines tangential to the failure surfaces.
This search option speeds up the analysis if the experienced user
foresees a reduced zone in which the failure surface probably lies.
One also takes advantage of this option in order to examine the surfaces in the neighbourhood of the one given by the result.

The document [R7.05.02](doc:r7.05.02) explains in more detail the optimisation algorithm
for the **simplified Bishop** and **Fellenius** procedures.

##### 4.3.15.1 Operands NB_POINT_1 and NB_POINT_2

```text
◊ NB_POINT_1 = / 5
               / nb_point_1
◊ NB_POINT_2 = / 5
               / nb_point_2
```

The operands NB_POINT_1 and NB_POINT_2 define the number of intermediate points
between the search bands of the entry and exit points respectively.
For example, one tests all the points x1:

${x}_{1}^{i}={x}_{1min}+({x}_{1max}-{x}_{min})/({N}_{1}-1)\ast i \quad \text{} \forall i=0,1,\mathrm{...},{N}_{1}-1$

If `X1/2_MINI` and `X1/2_MAXI` are equal, `NB_POINT_1/2` will systematically be reset to 1.

##### 4.3.15.2 Operands Y_MINI and Y_MAXI

```text
◊ Y_MINI =  y_min
◊ Y_MAXI  = y_max
```

The operands Y_MINI and Y_MAXI define the minimum and maximum ordinates of the horizontal straight lines
tangential to the failure surfaces tested. These operands will be necessary if one controls
manually the search for the radius (option 2 mentioned in section
4.3.6 ).

An example of the configuration of Y_MINI and Y_MAXI is shown in fig1-geom-lem.

One must make sure that y_min is less than $min\lbrace x{1}_{max},x{2}_{min}\rbrace$.
Indeed, if the entry and exit points lie on the two sides of the tangential straight line,
this combination will be ignored since it will be impossible to create a tangential failure surface
which crosses its tangent line.

##### 4.3.15.3 Operand NB_RAYON

```text
◊ NB_RAYON = / 15
             / nb_r
```

The operand NB_RAYON defines the number of surfaces to be tested for each combination of the entry and exit points. For the Bishop and Fellenius methods, the circles are generated in the manner illustrated in illus-circ-gene. From the two ends, a third point on the circle is determined by drawing the perpendicular straight line passing through the middle of the segment X1-X2. The operand NB_RAYON controls the number of discretisations of the distance between the segment X1-X2 and the intersection of this straight line with the circle:

$d_n = d_{min} + \frac{d_{max} - d_{min}}{N_r - 1}(n-1)$

where the minimum value $d_{min}$ is determined automatically in the following two situations:

1. If there is no intersection between the segment X1-X2 and the slope profile, $d_{min}$ is computed simply as $d_{min} = \frac{d_{max}}{N_r}$.
2. Otherwise, one chooses the circle whose radius is maximum among those which satisfy the following conditions in order to avoid the failure of creation of the mesh-cell groups of the slices:

- The circle is included in the model between X1 and X2.
- The distance between the circle and the slope profile is greater than the minimum size of the mesh cells in the vertical direction.
- All the intermediate points move away from the slope profile by a vertical distance greater than the size of the mesh cell just below the slope at the same abscissa.

*Generation of the circles in the Bishop and Fellenius methods.*

#### 4.3.16 Operand METHODE_LEM = ‘SPENCER’ or ‘MORGENSTERN_PRICE’

```text
◊     METHODE_LEM=  /'SPENCER'
                    /'MOGENSTERN-PRICE'
```

When METHODE_LEM = 'SPENCER' or 'MORGENSTERN_PRICE', one makes the assumption that the failure surface is multi-linear and one computes the factor of safety by solving at the same time the complete balance of the forces and of the moments of the set of slices. The search for the critical surface relies on the enhanced fireworks algorithm (Enhanced Fireworks Algorithm, EFWA), which is part of the swarm-intelligence algorithms (Swarm Intelligence Algorithm), and therefore requires additional parameters in order to configure the EFWA algorithm.

The document [R7.05.02](doc:r7.05.02) explains the EFWA algorithm in detail.

During the evaluation of the FS of the potential failure surfaces generated by the EFWA,
it is possible that the surface is physically illegal and that the condition of
convergence of the fixed-point iterations is not satisfied, which does not prevent the
location of the global critical surface. Therefore in that case one assigns to this
illegal surface a very large FS (with the issuing of an information message) and continues to test
the other failure surfaces.

In order to avoid the EFWA generating this type of illegal surface, it is better to increase
the number of slices so that the surfaces are smooth.
However, one must check at the same time that the mesh is fine enough, which ensures the existence
of mesh cells all of whose nodes are included in a slice of reduced size.

##### 4.3.16.1 Keyword INIT_ALEA

```text
◇ INIT_ALEA = int,
```

If the keyword INIT_ALEA is supplied, one initialises the seed of the random sequences by this value. Two consecutive analyses with the same initialisation then produce the same results. If the keyword is not supplied, then the seed used is displayed in the message file.

##### 4.3.16.2 Keyword ALGO_EFWA

```text
⟐ ALGO_EFWA = _F(
    ◇ ETAT_INIT = table,
    ◇ ITER_MAXI = int (défaut: 50),
    ◆ A = float,
    ◇ A_INIT = float,
    ◇ A_FINAL = float,
    ◇ N = int (défaut: 5),
    ◇ M = int (défaut: 40),
    ◇ MG = int (défaut: 5),
    ◇ SA = float (défaut: 0.04),
    ◇ SB = float (défaut: 0.8),
    ◇ NB_STAB_MAXI = int (défaut: 10),
    ◇ CRIT_STAB = float (défaut: 0.001),
    ◇ MARGE_PENTE = float,
),
```

Factor keyword defining the parameters linked to the EFWA optimisation algorithm.

```text
◊ ETAT_INIT = tabfs
```

This operand takes as input the table at the output of the macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE,
which makes it possible to initialise the EFWA algorithm by adding to the first generation of fireworks
the position of the critical surface coming from the previous analysis.

This operand is useful in the following two circumstances:

1. Since the EFWA is a probabilistic algorithm, the reaching of the global optimum is not mathematically guaranteed at the end of a limited number of iterations. Therefore CALC_STAB_PENTE offers the possibility of recovering the result before the true optimum is reached and of restarting the analysis if necessary.
2. In order to reduce the number of iterations, one may take the result coming from the Bishop or Fellenius method as the initial state. In general, this initial state is already a perfect approximation of the exact solution.

```text
◊ ITER_MAXI = / 1e2
              / iter_max
```

The operand ITER_MAXI defines the maximum number of iterations of the EFWA. At the end of iter_max iterations, the algorithm stops even if the minimum value of FS has not yet been obtained.

```text
◆ A = float,
◇ A_INIT = float,
◇ A_FINAL = float,
◇ N = int (défaut: 5),
◇ M = int (défaut: 40),
◇ MG = int (défaut: 5),
◇ SA = float (défaut: 0.04),
◇ SB = float (défaut: 0.8),
```

The operands A, A_INIT, A_FINAL, N, M, MG, SA, SB define the parameters linked to the generation of the ordinary and Gaussian sparks in the EFWA.

The meaning of these operands is the following:

- The operand `A`: the total amplitude of the explosion of the fireworks. It depends on the characteristic dimension of the slope. For example, let H be the height of the slope; it is reasonable to estimate approximately the value of A as:
  $A=\frac{m}{\mathit{nb}\text{\_}\mathit{tranches}}H$
- The operands `A_INIT` and `A_FINAL`: the initial and final minimum amplitude which is involved in the computation of the lower bound of the explosion amplitude. This minimum amplitude makes it possible to maintain the local search capacity around the optimal solution. By default, the initial and final amplitudes are equal to 100% and 10% of the width of the soil slices. One adopts the non-linear decreasing function:
  $A_{min}(N) = A_{init} - \frac{A_{init} - A_{fina}}{N_{max}}\sqrt{(2N_{max} - N)N}$
- The operand `N`: number of fireworks in each generation.
- The operand `M`: total number of ordinary sparks generated during the explosion of all the fireworks.
- The operand `MG`: number of Gaussian sparks generated during the explosion of the fireworks. The generation of the Gaussian sparks has the purpose of improving the diversity of the sparks distributed in the space of the state variables, which is the main advantage of the EFWA with respect to the other swarm algorithms.
- The operand `SA`: multiplying coefficient determining the lower limit of the number of sparks generated during the explosion of a firework.
- The operand `SB`: multiplying coefficient determining the upper limit of the number of sparks generated during the explosion of a firework.

```text
◇ NB_STAB_MAXI = int (défaut: 10),
◇ CRIT_STAB = float (défaut: 0.001),
```

The operand `NB_STAB_MAXI` defines the number of consecutive iterations giving an FS/KC whose variation is less than the threshold defined via the operand CRIT_STAB before the analysis is considered to have converged.

```text
◇ MARGE_PENTE = float,
```

The operand `MARGE_PENTE` imposes a vertical margin between the failure surfaces tested and the slope profile, in order to ensure the possibility of creating the mesh-cell groups for the soil slices.

It is not always necessary to impose manually a margin between the failure surface and the slope profile, except if one is confronted with the failure of creation of the mesh-cell groups followed by the error messages of the operator `DEFI_GROUP`. Indeed, the program evaluates this margin automatically by analysing the mesh contained in the cham_mater as input. One attempts to choose a margin as small as possible in order to widen the search zone. Furthermore, the potential surfaces being generated recursively, too large a margin will have an impact on the efficiency of the EFWA and even risks causing the failure of generation of the initial surfaces.

Therefore, in the case of failure of `DEFI_GROUP` or of non-convergence, it is advised first to improve the mesh used before imposing a vertical margin manually.

### 4.4 Operand CHAM_DEFO

```text
◊     CHAM_DEFO=CO(chamdef)
```

Name of the `evol_noli` concept containing the field visualising the failure surface.

1. **In the case where METHODE_STAB = 'SRM':**

The result `chamdef` contains the cumulative plastic-strain field (component V1 of the field VARI_NOEU)
to be produced by the macro-command. It is possible to print this field via the command IMPR_RESU
at the end of the execution of the macro-command in order to visualise the slip surface,
which corresponds to the zone where the cumulative plastic strain is large.

1. **In the case where METHODE_STAB = 'LEM':**

The result `chamdef` contains the indicative displacement field (NOEU_DEPL)
visualising the sliding part of the slope. One assigns to the component DX the value 1
if the node is part of the sliding part, and 0 otherwise.

## 5 Example of use

The stability analysis of a dam or of a dyke proceeds in 5 stages:

1. Definition of the model and of the material field.
2. Computation of the pore-pressure field by the model D_PLAN_H (static) or D_PLAN_HH (transient).
3. Definition of the mechanical loadings (hydrostatic pressure and acceleration coefficient).
4. SRM method: introduction of the hydraulic pressure into a new material field via the factor keyword AFFE_VARC. LEM method: introduce the hydraulic pressure field via CHAM_PRES. One has the option of taking the matric suction into account by activating SUCCION.
5. Computation of the stability factor via the macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE.

### 5.1 Example of an analysis with the SRM method

An example of use for the static analysis with the SRM method is shown below.
More details are found in the test case [V1.01.135](doc:v1.01.135).

```text
CMPTOT = AFFE_MATERIAU(
    AFFE=(
        _F(GROUP_MA=("fondation",), MATER=(FOND,)),
        _F(GROUP_MA=("fond_fill",), MATER=(ROCK,)),
        _F(GROUP_MA=("rockfill_down", "rockfill_up"), MATER=(Remblai,)),
        _F(GROUP_MA=("core",), MATER=(CLAY,)),
    ),
    AFFE_VARC=_F(EVOL=REPTOT, NOM_VARC="PTOT", TOUT="OUI"),
    MAILLAGE=MAILME,
)

TABFS1 = CALC_STAB_PENTE(
    MODELE=modME,
    CHAM_MATER=CMPTOT,
    GROUP_MA=("ZONE_SRM"),
    EXCIT=(
        _F(CHARGE=DIRI),
        _F(CHARGE=Gravity, FONC_MULT=multi),
        _F(CHARGE=Presrep, FONC_MULT=multi),
    ),
    INCREMENT=_F(LIST_INST=LTr),
    CONVERGENCE=_F(ITER_GLOB_MAXI=10),
    COMPORTEMENT=(_F(RELATION="MOHR_COULOMB"), _F(RELATION="ELAS", GROUP_MA=("rockfill_down"))),
    FS=_F(FS_INIT=1.84, INCR_INIT=0.04, RESI_MAXI=0.02),
    CHAM_DEFO=CO("chamdef"),
)

IMPR_TABLE(TABLE=TABFS1, UNITE=8)

IMPR_RESU(RESU = _F(RESULTAT = chamdef), UNITE= 2)
```

The transient hydraulic analysis makes it possible to evaluate the stability of a hydraulic embankment
structure under transient loadings, such as flooding for the dykes and the rapid
drawdown of the reservoir. The macro-command CALC_STAB_PENTE is nevertheless
not capable of processing the transient loadings directly, with the exception of the quasi-static loadings
with a multiplying function which serves to improve the convergence of the analysis.

However, the evaluation of the stability of the structure under transient hydraulic loading can
possibly be carried out with CALC_STAB_PENTE by checking, at each transient analysis step,
the static stability of the structure. With this method one will obtain the tables as a result whose
total number is equal to the number of physical time instants. It is therefore possible to plot the variation
of the factor of safety as a function of time.
