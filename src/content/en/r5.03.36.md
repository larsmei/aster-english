# r5.03.36 Constitutive law KICHENIN_NL combining elastoplasticity and non-linear viscoelasticity

**Summary:**

This document describes the KICHENIN_NL constitutive model, intended to describe the mechanical response of polyethylene. It combines an elastoplastic branch with linear kinematic hardening and a non-linear Maxwell viscoelastic branch. A large-strain modelling is available via the GDEF_LOG kinematics.

By way of preamble, let us make clear that this documentation is based on the note [Lorentz, 2022]. It retains from it only the main elements concerning the formulation and the numerical integration of the model. The interested reader is referred to this note and its references for further details where appropriate.

## 1 Scope

### 1.1 Purpose

The constitutive law KICHENIN_NL combines in parallel an elastoplastic model with linear kinematic hardening and a Maxwell viscoelastic model whose dashpot is non-linear, of Norton type. A rheological illustration of it is given below. It is originally dedicated to the description of polyethylene but can no doubt be applied to other materials.

This constitutive model is compatible with a small-perturbation kinematics (PETIT) or a large-strain kinematics (GDEF_LOG).

Finally, this model describes the stress response as an explicit function of the strain and of the internal variables, and not as an evolution law in incremental stress. The stress does not contribute to defining the mechanical state of the system; it is a consequence of it (in particular, the stress does not enter into the definition of an initial state of the system).

### 1.2 Material parameters

The constitutive relation is based on nine parameters, characteristic of the material considered:

- Young's modulus ${E}^{p}$ and Poisson's ratio ${\nu}^{p}$ of the elastoplastic branch, defined under the factor keyword ELAS(E, NU) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU;
- The elastic limit ${\sigma}^{c}$ and the Prager constant $C$ (without the corrective factor 2/3), defined under the factor keyword KICHENIN_NL (SIGC and PRAGER) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU;
- Young's modulus ${E}^{v}$ and Poisson's ratio ${\nu}^{v}$ of the viscoelastic branch, defined under the factor keyword KICHENIN_NL (E_VISC and NU_VISC) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU;
- the viscosity coefficient $\eta$ , Poisson's ratio ${\nu}^{d}$ and the non-linearity exponent $n\le 1$ of the dashpot, defined under the factor keyword KICHENIN_NL (ETA_AMOR, NU_AMOR and N_AMOR) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU.

These parameters may depend on the temperature (factor keywords *_FO). The constitutive law is expressed in mechanical strains, that is to say that it is compatible with the usual shrinkage terms, in particular the thermal strain.

It is important to note that the stiffness defined under the factor keyword ELAS does not constitute the whole of the (instantaneous) stiffness of the material, but only that relating to the elastoplastic branch.

### 1.3 Internal variables

The constitutive law is based on twelve physical internal variables: the components of the plastic strain ${\epsilon}^{p}$ (at the slider of the elastoplastic branch) and of the viscous strain ${\epsilon}^{v}$ (at the dashpot of the viscoelastic branch). They are completed by the accumulated plastic strain and the accumulated viscous strain.

| EPVXX - EPVYZ | V9- V14 | Components of the viscous strain |

## 2 Continuous model

In accordance with the rheological presentation, the stress $\sigma$ is the sum of two contributions: that coming from the elastoplastic branch ${\sigma}^{p}$ and that coming from the viscoelastic branch ${\sigma}^{v}$ :

$$
\sigma ={\sigma}^{p}+{\sigma}^{v}
$$

### 2.1 Elastoplastic branch

This is a classical plasticity model with a von Mises threshold and linear kinematic hardening. The (mechanical) strain $\epsilon$ is decomposed additively into a plastic part ${\epsilon}^{p}$ and an elastic part $\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p}$ , the latter being related to the stress of the elastoplastic branch by the following elastic relation:

$$
{\sigma}^{p}={C}^{p}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})=3{K}^{p}\mathit{sph}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})S+2{\mu}^{p}\mathit{dev}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})\text{};\text{}3{K}^{p}=\frac{{E}^{p}}{1-2{\nu}^{p}}\text{}2{\mu}^{p}=\frac{{E}^{p}}{1+{\nu}^{p}}
$$

where $\mathit{sph}()$ and :math:`` denote respectively the normalised spherical part and the deviator of a second-order tensor, while $S$ is the **normalised** second-order identity tensor. For an arbitrary second-order tensor $a$ , this is expressed by:

$$
S\equiv \frac{\mathit{Id}}{\sqrt{3}};\mathit{sph}(a)\equiv S:a=\frac{\mathit{tr}(a)}{\sqrt{3}};\mathit{dev}(a)\equiv a-(S:a)S
$$

Note that the use of $S$ and $\mathit{sph}()$ rather than $\mathit{Id}$ and $\mathit{tr}()$ makes it possible to avoid the presence of the $3$ and $1/3$ in the various expressions, thanks to their normalised character.

One now introduces the yield function, which involves a back-stress $X=C{\epsilon}^{p}$ :

$$
F(\sigma ,{\epsilon}^{p})={\Vert \sigma -C{\epsilon}^{p}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}-{\sigma}^{c}\text{};\text{}{\Vert a\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}\equiv \sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\mathit{dev}(a):\mathit{dev}(a)}
$$

The evolution of the plastic strain is governed by the flow equation. The flow direction is normal to the yield surface (associated plasticity):

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{\kappa}\frac{N}{{\Vert N\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}}\text{};\text{}N=\mathit{dev}(\sigma -C{\epsilon}^{p})
$$

Finally, the evolution of the plastic multiplier $\dot{\kappa}$ is fixed by the consistency condition:

$$
\dot{\kappa}\ge 0;F(\sigma ,{\epsilon}^{p})\le 0;\dot{\kappa}F(\sigma ,{\epsilon}^{p})=0
$$

### 2.2 Viscoelastic branch

This is a Maxwell model whose dashpot is non-linear, of Norton type. The (mechanical) strain $\epsilon$ is decomposed additively into a viscous part ${\epsilon}^{v}$ and an elastic part $\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v}$ , the latter being related to the stress of the viscoelastic branch by the following elastic relation:

$$
{\sigma}^{v}={C}^{v}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})=3{K}^{v}\mathit{sph}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})S+2{\mu}^{v}\mathit{dev}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})\text{};\text{}3{K}^{v}=\frac{{E}^{v}}{1-2{\nu}^{v}}\text{}2{\mu}^{v}=\frac{{E}^{v}}{1+{\nu}^{v}}
$$

The evolution of the viscous strain is governed by the Norton evolution law:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}=\frac{1}{{\eta}^{\gamma}}{\Vert {\sigma}^{v}\Vert }_{v}^{\gamma -1}V:{\sigma}^{v};\gamma \equiv \frac{1}{n};{\Vert a\Vert }_{V}\equiv \sqrt{a:V:a};V:a\equiv (1+{\nu}^{d})\mathit{dev}(a)+(1-2{\nu}^{d})\mathit{sph}(a)S
$$

where one has introduced $\gamma$ , the inverse exponent of $n$ , in order to lighten the notation, and $V$ a dimensionless isotropic fourth-order tensor, which makes it possible to “misalign” ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}$ with respect to ${\sigma}^{v}$ .

### 2.3 Variables in post-processing

Two variables may be combined in certain formulations in post-processing in order to predict the failure of the material. These are the accumulated plastic strain and the accumulated viscous strain, defined respectively as follows:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}_{\mathit{cum}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}:{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}};{\dot{\epsilon}}_{\mathit{cum}}^{v}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}:{\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}}
$$

For practical reasons, they are computed at the end of the constitutive integration phase and stored among the internal variables.

## 3 Numerical integration

### 3.1 Discretised equations

The integration of the constitutive law consists in computing the internal variables and the stress for a given (mechanical) strain history. Given the additive character of the stress (), one can proceed independently for each branch. The tangent operator of the system will itself also be the sum of the tangent operators specific to each branch.

The discretisation in time of the constitutive equations is based on an implicit Euler scheme (including for the viscoelastic branch), that is to say that the various variables of the problem are expressed at the final time instant of the time step considered. One denotes by ${Q}_{n}$ the value of a quantity $Q$ at the beginning of the time step, $\Delta Q$ its increment during the time step and (simply) $Q$ its value at the end of the time step. The mechanical state at the beginning of the time step $({\epsilon}_{n},{{\epsilon}^{p}}_{n},{{\epsilon}^{v}}_{n})$ is assumed known, as is the strain increment $\Delta \epsilon$ (and therefore also the strain $\epsilon$ ). It is then a matter of computing the increments of the internal variables $\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}$ and $\Delta {\epsilon}^{v}$ as well as the stress at the end of the time step $\sigma$ .

**Note:** the law being written with respect to the true thermodynamic variables, it does not take into account an initial stress state defined by the factor keyword ETAT_INIT. A non-zero initial state must therefore be defined by the strain internal variables.

### 3.2 Elastoplastic branch

The discretisation of the stress–strain relation () is written as follows:

$$
{\sigma}^{p}=3{K}^{p}\mathit{sph}(\epsilon -{{\epsilon}^{p}}_{n}-\Delta {\epsilon}^{p})S+2{\mu}^{p}\mathit{dev}(\epsilon -{{\epsilon}^{p}}_{n}-\Delta {\epsilon}^{p})
$$

According to the flow equation (), one observes that the trace of the plastic strain increment is zero, so that the same holds for the plastic strain at any time instant (isochoric plastic strain). The relation () is simplified and one can bring out in it an elastic stress (or elastic trial stress) denoted ${\sigma}^{e}$ and an elastic (trial) flow direction denoted ${N}^{e}$ , which are known:

$$
{\sigma}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}-2{\mu}^{p}\Delta {\epsilon}^{p};{\sigma}^{e}=3{K}^{p}\mathit{sph}(\epsilon )S+2{\mu}^{p}\mathit{dev}(\epsilon -{{\epsilon}^{p}}_{n});{N}^{e}=\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{e})-C{\epsilon}_{n}^{p}
$$

If the elastic trial remains inside the elastic domain, the plastic strain increment is zero (elastic evolution):

$$
{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}\le {\sigma}^{c}\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}=0\text{et}{\sigma}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}
$$

Otherwise, one must carry out a plastic correction (plastic evolution). The flow equation () and the consistency equation () are then discretised as follows:

$$
{\Delta \epsilon }^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Delta \kappa }{{\sigma}^{c}}\left[{N}^{e}-(2{\mu}^{p}+C)\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}\right];{\Vert {N}^{e}-(2{\mu}^{p}+C)\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}={\sigma}^{c}
$$

After a few algebraic manipulations, one deduces from this:

$$
N={\sigma}^{c}\frac{{N}^{e}}{{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}};\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}=\frac{{N}^{e}-N}{2{\mu}^{p}+C};{\sigma}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}-2{\mu}^{p}\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}
$$

One now denotes by ${H}^{p}$ the tangent operator of the elastoplastic branch, such that $\delta {\sigma}^{p}={H}^{p}:\delta \epsilon$ . In the case of an elastic evolution, one has simply, according to ():

$$
{H}^{p}=3{K}^{p}S\otimes S+2{\mu}^{p}{P}^{\mathit{dev}};{P}^{\mathit{dev}}\equiv {I}_{4}-S\otimes S
$$

where one has introduced ${I}_{4}$ the fourth-order identity tensor and ${P}^{\mathit{dev}}$ the projector onto the space of deviators. And in the case of a plastic evolution, one proceeds by differentiation of the equations in (). One obtains, after a few calculations:

$$
{H}^{p}=3{K}^{p}S\otimes S+\left(\frac{2{\mu}^{p}}{2{\mu}^{p}+C}\right)\left(\frac{{\sigma}^{c}}{{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}}2{\mu}^{p}+C\right){P}^{\mathit{dev}}-3{\mu}^{p}\left(\frac{2{\mu}^{p}}{2{\mu}^{p}+C}\right)\left(\frac{{\sigma}^{c}}{{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}}\right)\left(\frac{{N}^{e}}{{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}}\otimes \frac{{N}^{e}}{{\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert }_{\mathit{eq}}}\right)
$$

The tangent operator is not continuous at the transition between the elastic and plastic regimes. A priori, knowledge of the evolution regime, and therefore the choice of the tangent operator, depends on whether or not the inequality $\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert \le {\sigma}^{c}$ is satisfied. However, in the prediction phase and if the previous step corresponded to a plastic evolution, one may find oneself in the case where $\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert \approx {\sigma}^{c}$ , to within the solution accuracy, while one expects a new time step in the plastic regime. This is why one chooses to “widen” the plastic regime a little for the choice of the tangent operator in prediction: if $\Vert {N}^{e}\Vert >{\sigma}^{c}(1-\text{prec})$ , with prec fixed at ${10}^{-6}$ , then one chooses the plastic tangent operator.

### 3.3 Viscoelastic branch

The time discretisation of the constitutive equations () and () leads respectively to:

$$
{\sigma}^{v}={C}^{v}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})=3{K}^{v}\mathit{sph}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})S+2{\mu}^{v}\mathit{dev}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v})\text{};\text{}3{K}^{v}=\frac{{E}^{v}}{1-2{\nu}^{v}}\text{}2{\mu}^{v}=\frac{{E}^{v}}{1+{\nu}^{v}}
$$

$$
\frac{{\Delta \epsilon }^{v}}{\Delta t}=\frac{1}{{\eta}^{\gamma}}{\Vert {\sigma}^{v}\Vert }_{v}^{\gamma -1}V:{\sigma}^{v}
$$

One introduces the instantaneous stress ${\sigma}^{i}$ , which is a known quantity:

$$
{\sigma}^{i}={C}^{v}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}_{n}^{v});{\sigma}^{v}={\sigma}^{i}-{C}^{v}:\Delta {\epsilon}^{v}
$$

By substituting () into (), one obtains an equation in ${\sigma}^{v}$ :

$$
\left({I}_{4}+\frac{\Delta t}{{\eta}^{\gamma}}{\Vert {\sigma}^{v}\Vert }_{v}^{\gamma -1}{C}^{v}:V\right):{\sigma}^{v}={\sigma}^{i}
$$

The method for solving this tensor equation is based on the isotropic character of the fourth-order tensors which appear in it. One begins by introducing the unknown $x\ge 0$ such that:

$$
{\Vert {\sigma}^{v}\Vert }_{v}=x{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}
$$

By separating the spherical and deviatoric parts of (), one then obtains respectively:

$$
\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{v})=\frac{\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{i})}{1+{b}_{1}{x}^{\gamma -1}};\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{v})=\frac{\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{i})}{1+{b}_{2}{x}^{\gamma -1}}
$$

where one has introduced the coefficients:

$$
{b}_{0}=\frac{\Delta t}{{\eta}^{\gamma}}{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}^{\gamma -1};{b}_{1}=3{K}^{v}(1-2{\nu}^{d}){b}_{0};{b}_{2}=2{\mu}^{v}(1+{\nu}^{d}){b}_{0}
$$

Knowledge of the unknown $x$ thus makes it possible to compute the whole stress tensor ${\sigma}^{v}$ . By substituting the latter by its expression as a function of $x$ into (), one obtains:

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{{a}_{k}}{{(1+{b}_{k}{x}^{\gamma -1})}^{2}}={x}^{2};{a}_{1}=(1-2{\nu}^{d}){\left(\frac{\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)}^{2};{a}_{2}=(1+{\nu}^{d}){\left(\frac{\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)}^{2}
$$

This is a scalar equation in $x$ which admits a unique solution. It is solved by a Newton method. As $n\le 1$ and therefore $\gamma \ge 1$ , one can determine bounds:

$$
\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{{a}_{k}}{{(1+{b}_{k})}^{2}}}\le x\le 1
$$

One carries out the iterations of the Newton method until a bracketing of the solution ${x}_{m}\le x\le {x}_{p}$ is obtained such that:

$$
{x}_{p}-{x}_{m}\le \text{RESI\_INTE\_RELA}
$$

One thus controls the quality of the solution ${\sigma}^{v}$ in relative terms with respect to $\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert$ .

The tangent operator ${H}^{v}$ such that $\delta {\sigma}^{v}={H}^{v}:\delta \epsilon$ is obtained by successively differentiating the preceding equations. Its expression is given directly:

$$
{H}^{v}=\frac{3{K}^{v}}{1+{b}_{1}{x}^{\gamma -1}}S\otimes S+\frac{2{\mu}^{v}}{1+{b}_{2}{x}^{\gamma -1}}{P}^{\mathit{dev}}-{x}^{\gamma -1}h\otimes {B}_{0}-(\gamma -1){x}^{\gamma -1}{b}_{0}h\otimes X
$$

where one has introduced the following intermediate quantities:

| ${A}_{1}=2(1-2{\nu}^{d})\left[\left(\frac{3{K}^{v}\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}S\right)-{\left(\frac{\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)}^{2}\left(\frac{{C}^{v}:V:{\sigma}^{i}}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)\right]$ |  |
| --- | --- |
| ${A}_{2}=2(1+{\nu}^{d})\left[\left(\frac{2{\mu}^{v}\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)-{\left(\frac{\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)}^{2}\left(\frac{{C}^{v}:V:{\sigma}^{i}}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)\right]$ |  |
| ${B}_{0}=\frac{(\gamma -1)\Delta t}{{\eta}^{\gamma}}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}^{\gamma -1}\left(\frac{{C}^{v}:V:{\sigma}^{i}}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right);{B}_{1}=3{K}^{v}(1-2{\nu}^{d}){B}_{0};{B}_{2}=2{\mu}^{v}(1+{\nu}^{d}){B}_{0}$ |  |
| $X={\left[\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{2{a}_{k}(1+{b}_{k}\gamma {x}^{\gamma -1})}{{(1+{b}_{k}{x}^{\gamma -1})}^{3}}\right]}^{-1}\left[\sum_{k=1}^{2}\frac{(1+{b}_{k}{x}^{\gamma -1}){A}_{k}-2{a}_{k}{x}^{\gamma -1}{B}_{k}}{{(1+{b}_{k}{x}^{\gamma -1})}^{3}}\right]$ |  |
| $h=\frac{3{K}^{v}(1-2{\nu}^{d})}{{(1+{b}_{1}{x}^{\gamma -1})}^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathit{sph}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}S\right)+\frac{2{\mu}^{v}(1+{\nu}^{d})}{{(1+{b}_{2}{x}^{\gamma -1})}^{2}}\left(\frac{\mathit{dev}({\sigma}^{i})}{{\Vert {\sigma}^{i}\Vert }_{v}}\right)$ |  |

### 3.4 Variables in post-processing

As mentioned previously, the accumulated plastic strain and the accumulated viscous strain are computed at the end of the constitutive integration phase. After discretisation in time, equation () thus leads to:

$$
{\Delta \epsilon }_{\mathit{cum}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\Delta \epsilon }^{p}:{\Delta \epsilon }^{p}};{\Delta \epsilon }_{\mathit{cum}}^{v}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\Delta \epsilon }^{v}:{\Delta \epsilon }^{v}}
$$
