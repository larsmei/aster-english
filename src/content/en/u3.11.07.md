# u3.11.07 Modelling POU_D_EM

**Summary:**

The POU_D_EM modelling corresponds to the formulation of multi-fibre beam elements (beam of heterogeneous section divided into several fibres).

They can be used for three-dimensional problems in linear and non-linear mechanical analysis.

## 1 Discretisation

### 1.1 Degrees of freedom

For the multi-fibre beam modelling in three dimensions, the discretisation degrees of freedom are, at each node of the support mesh cell, the six displacement components (three translations and three rotations). These nodes are assumed to describe a segment of the mean fibre of the beam.

| **Finite element** | **Degrees of freedom (at each vertex node)** |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POU_D_EM | DX | DY | DZ | DRX | DRY | DRZ |

### 1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness matrices

The support mesh cells of the finite elements, in a displacement formulation, are two-node segments SEG2:

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Finite element** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POU_D_EM | SEG2 | MECA_POU_D_EM |  |

### 1.3 Support mesh cell of the loadings

As for the classical beam elements (POU_D_E), all the loadings applicable to the multi-fibre beam elements are treated by direct discretisation on the support mesh cell of the element in a displacement formulation.

*No loading support mesh cell is therefore necessary for the edge of the beam or bar elements.*

### 1.4 Main characteristics of the modelling

The POU_D_EM modelling is based on the solution of a beam problem for which each defined section is divided into several fibres.

Each fibre then behaves as an Euler beam, that is to say that the sections remain straight and perpendicular to the mean fibre (high-slenderness assumption).

The section may be of any shape.

## 2 Assignment of the characteristics

For this 1D structural element, it is necessary to assign geometric characteristics which are complementary to the mesh data. The definition of these data is carried out with the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM associated with the following factor keywords:

**•** **AFFE_SECT**

Makes it possible to associate a plane mesh of the section with a beam element.

**•** **AFFE_FIBRE**

Makes it possible to associate a section made up of one or more point fibres with a beam element.

**•** **POUTRE**

Makes it possible to associate a geometric torsion characteristic with a beam element.

**•** **ORIENTATION**

Makes it possible to "rotate" the plane mesh of the section about the axis of the beam.

**Notes on the characteristics of the modelling:**

- Within the framework of a multi-fibre type modelling, there are two "levels" of modelling. There is the so-called "longitudinal" modelling, which will be represented by a beam (with geometric support SEG2), and a plane modelling of the section (perpendicular to the SEG2).

The operand **AFFE_SECT** makes it possible to associate a plane mesh of the section with a beam element. The operand **AFFE_FIBRE** makes it possible to associate a section made up of one or more point fibres (defined by their position and area) with a beam element.

- In general, in the plane modelling of the section, several materials coexist. For example, in a reinforced-concrete section, there is both concrete and reinforcement. In that case, the operator **CREA_MAILLAGE** makes it possible to duplicate the finite-element support so that there is only one material per support.
- The operand **POUTRE** is used to assign a geometric torsion characteristic (JX) which cannot be computed from the plane mesh of the section. If one uses the value GENERALE for the keyword SECTION of the operand POUTRE, one must give the characteristics (CARA) A, IY and IZ in addition to JX, because the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM expects at least these four characteristics for a classical beam.

The values (VALE) given to A, IY and IZ are not used by the POU_D_EM element, because they are computed from the plane mesh of the section. On the other hand, a check of the consistency of the information (AIRE and INERTIE) supplied on the one hand by A, IY, IZ and on the other hand by the keywords AFFE_SECT and AFFE_FIBRE is carried out. The error criterion is based on the relative error and is compared either with the default value or with that given by the user via the keywords PREC_AIRE and PREC_INERTIE. (Cf. command AFFE_CARA_ELEM, keywords PREC_AIRE and PREC_INERTIE of the operand POUTRE).

- The operand **ORIENTATION** is used in general to "rotate" the plane mesh of the section about the axis of the beam (CARA 'ANGL_VRIL'). Indeed, by default, the axis $x$ (horizontal) of the plane mesh of the section coincides with the axis $y$ of the beam (see [Figure]).

## 3 Supported loadings

The loadings available are the following:

**•** **'FORCE_POUTRE'**

Makes it possible to apply line forces.

**•** **'PESANTEUR'**

Makes it possible to apply a gravity-type loading.

**•** **'ROTATION'**

Makes it possible to define the rotational speed and the rotation vector.

The application of a thermal-expansion loading is carried out by defining the factor keyword AFFE_VARC under AFFE_MATERIAU [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03).

## 5 Examples of implementation: test cases

Linear statics

- SSLL111A [V3.01.111](doc:v3.01.111): Static response of a reinforced-concrete beam (section in $T$ ) with a linear constitutive behaviour, subjected to three successive load cases: a point force, the self-weight and a temperature rise.

Non-linear statics

- SSNL119A [V6.02.119](doc:v6.02.119): Three-point bending test, static response of a reinforced-concrete beam (rectangular section) with a non-linear La Borderie constitutive behaviour.

Linear dynamics

Non-linear dynamics

- SDNL130A [V5.02.130](doc:v5.02.130): Seismic response of a reinforced-concrete beam (rectangular section) with non-linear constitutive behaviour.
