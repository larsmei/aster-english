# v7.20.100 HSNA100 - Drying of a concrete enclosure wall

**Summary:**

This test case is designed to validate the calculation of concrete drying, developed in operator SECH_NON_LINE. The case studied corresponds to the simulated drying of the Flamanville enclosure. Geometric data and material characteristics are derived from Laurent Granger's thesis "Distorted behaviour of concrete in nuclear power plant enclosures" published by the Central Laboratory of Bridges and Chaussées (1996, pages 185 to 204).

Drying is done by exchange with the outside, on the internal and external walls of the wall, in model axisymmetric $\mathrm{2D}$ . It is carried out over a period of 54 years.

The diffusion coefficient of the drying dependent on temperature, the analysis consists of the chaining of a thermal calculation and a drying calculation. A mechanical calculation of the removal of linear elasticity and Von Misès plasticity is then carried out with an isotropic hardening.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The enclosure is modelled on a height $\text{Lint}=\text{Lext}=1m$

Outside radius of enclosure: $\mathit{Rext}=23,5m$

Internal radius of the enclosure: $\mathrm{Rint}=22,5m$

![Figure](/figures/v7.20.100/Object_1.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

**For thermal calculation:**

Thermal diffusion coefficient: $\lambda =80J/(\mathit{h.cm.}°C)=2.22W/(\mathit{m.}°C)$

Volumetric heat: $\rho {C}_{p}=2.4{10}^{6}J/{m}^{3}/°C$

**For the calculation of drying:**

In the drying equation:

$\frac{\mathrm{dC}}{\mathrm{dt}}-div\left[D(C,T)\mathrm{grad}C\right]=0$

the $D$ scattering coefficient shall be of the form recommended by Granger [bib1], [bib2] for modellings A to C:

$D(C,T)=A\exp(\mathrm{BC})\frac{T}{{T}_{0}}\exp\left[-\frac{{Q}_{S}}{R}(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{{T}_{0}})\right]$

$A=3.8{10}^{-13}{m}^{2}/s$

$B=0.05$

${T}_{0}=0°C$

$\frac{{Q}_{S}}{R}=4700{K}^{-1}$

For modellings D and E using SECH_RFT law, the parameters are given in each modellings.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

**For thermal calculation:**

Thermal exchange is imposed on the inside and outside walls of the wall of the enclosure (groups of mesh cells ${l}_{int}$ and ${l}_{\text{ext}}$).

For the first five years, the outside temperature is $15°C$ on each wall:

${T}_{int}={T}_{\text{ext}}=15°C$

From the fifth year onwards, the internal outside temperature is changed to $35°C$:

${T}_{int}=35°C$ et ${T}_{\text{ext}}=15°C$

The internal wall is considered unventilated and its exchange coefficient is ${h}_{int}=4\frac{W}{\mathit{m².}°C}$ .

The outer wall is considered to be ventilated and its exchange coefficient is ${h}_{\text{ext}}=6\frac{W}{\mathit{m².}°C}$ . (page 136 in [bib1]).

In practice, the drying time scales are much higher than those of the thermal one can be considered to be almost immediate. The calculation is made in linear thermal.

**For the calculation of drying:**

Boundary conditions is expressed in terms of normal moisture flow on the inner and outer walls of the enclosure (groups of mesh cells ${l}_{int}$ and ${l}_{\text{ext}}$). FLUX_NL is used as the AFFE_CHAR_SECH_F operator's option. The normal flow is expressed in general terms, in a drying calculation, based on the initial concentration ${C}_{0}$ , the concentration at 50% ${C}_{50}$ humidity and the external concentration ${C}_{\mathit{ext}}$ , in the form of:

$w=-D\left(C,T\right)\frac{\partial C}{\partial n}=\frac{0.5\beta }{{\left({C}_{0}-{C}_{50}\right)}^{2}}\left[C-\left(2{C}_{0}-{C}_{\mathit{ext}}\right)\right]\left(C-{C}_{\mathit{ext}}\right)$

with

$\beta ={\mathrm{3.41557.10}}^{–6}\frac{l}{\mathit{m².s}}$ (page 181 in [bib1])

The data used for the Flamanville compound are as follows:

${C}_{0}=105.7l/{m}^{3}$ and ${C}_{50}=57.5l/{m}^{3}$ (page 194 in [bib1])

On the inside side:

| 0-5 years *,* | ${C}_{\mathit{ext}}=69.1l/{m}^{3}$ Value for 60% humidity |
| --- | --- |
| *5 to *54 years,* | ${C}_{\mathit{ext}}=51.6l/{m}^{3}$ Value for 45% humidity |

On the outside face:

${C}_{\mathit{ext}}=69.1l/{m}^{3}$

The drying calculation is performed in nonlinear thermal, by linking with the linear thermal calculation.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Initial conditions consist of the initial temperature, which is taken at $15°C$, and the initial water concentration, which is ${C}_{0}=105.7l/{m}^{3}$

## 2 reference solution

This section only applies to modellings A and C using the Granger Law.

For modellings D and E using SECH_RFT, the reference solutions are obtained by calculations using the SECH_NAPPE behavior to which an equivalent python function has been provided.

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is made up of calculations made with a similar model by L. Granger, as part of his thesis [bib1]. The test case concerns the calculation made with the experimental data corresponding to the Flamanville enclosure. The implementation and results are described in it pages 185 to 204.

Drying is done over 54 years, taking into account the change in boundary conditions in the fifth year.

## 10 Summary of the results

For modellings A to C, the accuracy of the results is less than about $\text{5\%}$ for the water concentration compared to the reference solution of which the accuracy is not known.

For modellings D and E, it can be concluded that the SECH_RFT law is equivalent to the SECH_NAPPE law with the python formula provided.
