## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 General case

In this part, the analytical solution is detailed in pure $I$ mode in its $\mathrm{3D}$ form. For $\mathrm{2D}$ plane calculations, the solution is identical, the component of the jump and the stress vector following $\tau$ does not intervene, and it is enough to replace the $S$ surface with the $L$ length in the solution.

For loads in shear mode, elastic l-element does not interfere. Only a test is performed on the tangential constitutive law. For cohesive laws a displacement jump is imposed and the resulting cohesive stress is checked.

### 2.2 In pure $I$ mode

The analytical solution of the system's overall response is presented in the $(n,t,\tau)$ local marker. A collinear load is applied to normal: $U=Un$, the element cohesive opens in pure $I$ mode and the tangential stresses and tangential jumps remain null. Therefore, one turns to a scalar problem. One notes $\sigma =n.\sigma .n$ the only non-zero component of the elastic l-element stress tensor in the local marker. The solution of the global response for cohesive laws is presented:

**CZM_EXP_REG,CZM_EXP_MIX**

The cohesive behavior relationship is given by (see doc [r7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11)):

$\overrightarrow{\sigma}=\left(\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{n}\\ {\sigma}_{t}\\ {\sigma}_{\tau}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{c}\cdot {e}^{-\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\cdot {\delta}_{n}}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

with ${\delta}_{n}$ the normal displacement jump. The elastic law of the volume element gives:

$\sigma =E\varepsilon =F/S$

where $\varepsilon$ is the elastic deformation and where $F$ is the force corresponding to the displacement imposed on the $S$ surface. In the case of a figure where the threshold stress in the element cohesive is not reached, the solution is elastic, the overall response is linear, it is expressed as follows:

$U(F)=\frac{\mathrm{FL}}{\mathrm{SE}}$

When the break threshold is reached, the leap into the cohesive element is no longer zero, the response is no longer linear. The system equilibrium is given by:

$\sigma={\sigma}_{n}$

In addition, in this simple case of loading, the displacement imposed is equal to the sum of the displacement jump and displacement related to the $\varepsilon$ deformation of the elastic element:

$U={\delta}_{n}+L\varepsilon$

The relationship between force and forced displacement is derived from this:

$U(F)=-\frac{{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}\log(\frac{F}{S{\sigma}_{c}})+\frac{\mathrm{FL}}{\mathrm{SE}}$

*Note:

- Following material data it may not be possible to have a reverse of the overall response that is captured with the load control.
- The elements interface control is performed on the percentage of energy dissipated.

**CZM_LIN_REG, CZM_OUV_MIX, CZM_TAC_MIX, CZM_FAT_MIX**

The cohesive behaviour relationship is given by:

$\vec{\sigma}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n}\\ {\sigma}_{t}\\ {\sigma}_{\tau}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{c}\left(1-{\delta}_{n}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}}\right)\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

The same reasoning is adopted with the exponential law, the analytical solution of the global response expresses itself as follows:

$U(F)=\frac{F}{S}(\frac{L}{E}-\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}^{2}})+2\frac{{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}$

**Note**

For CZM_FAT_MIX law the previous global response is only valid if the load is monotonous. In most cases this one is cyclical since this law is intended for fatigue. It is suggested to refer to [r7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11) documentation of cohesive laws for more information.

**CZM_LIN_MIX**

The deformation being uniform in the block, in the same way as before, we can link the $U$ imposed displacement on the face to the displacement jump through the element cohesive by $U=⟦u⟧+Lϵ$, the $ϵ$ deformation being given by $ϵ=\frac{\sigma}{E}$. The constraint being uniform in the block, it is given by the cohesive law like $\sigma ={\sigma}_{c}\left(1-⟦u⟧\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}}\right)$. Combining these expressions, we obtain:

$U=⟦u⟧+L\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}\left(1-⟦u⟧\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}}\right)$

In this test, we validate the correct implementation of the load control in this test, in addition to validating the correct implementation of the cohesive law. Generally, when using load control, the $\Delta u$ displacement increment is linked to the time step $\Delta t$ by the relationship (see [r5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80) documentation):

$f(\Delta u)=\frac{\Delta t}{\text{COEF_MULT}}$

où:

- $f$ is the control function, which depends on the quantity of one seeks to control,
- COEF_MULT is the value specified under the keyword of the same name, under the factor keyword PILOTAGE of the STAT_NON_LINE command.

More particularly for the cohesive CZM_LIN_MIX law, the steering function is:

$f(\Delta u)=\frac{⟦u⟧}{{w}_{c}}$ where ${w}_{c}=\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}$ is the critical displacement jump.

To recapture, the $⟦\Delta u⟧$ displacement jump increment applied to a time step $\Delta t$ is written:

$⟦\Delta u⟧=\frac{\Delta t}{\text{COEF_MULT}}\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}$

For this $\text{COEF_MULT}=10$ test. The reference load to be driven is defined by a required ${U}_{0}=2.5$ displacement. The ETA_PILO parameter giving the load intensity will therefore be given by $\text{ETA_PILO}=\frac{U}{{U}_{0}}$.

**CZM_FROT_MIX**

In the case of elastic adhesion, the

$$
\sigma_n =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{ll}
\frac{U}{1/K_n + L/E} &\text{si } U \leq \frac{c}{\mu}(\frac{1}{K_n}+\frac{L}{E}), \\
\frac{c}{\mu} &\text{sinon}.
\end{array}
\right.
$$

If the grip is perfect,

$$
\sigma_n =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{ll}
\frac{EU}{L} &\text{si } U \leq \frac{cL}{\mu E}, \\
\frac{c}{\mu} &\text{sinon}.
\end{array}
\right.
$$

### 2.3 In $\text{II}$ and $\text{III}$ pure mode

The constitutive law (see doc [r7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11) and [r7.02.13](doc:r7.02.13)) is tested only:

**CZM_EXP_REG**

$\sigma_T = \sigma_c \cdot e^{\frac{\sigma_c}{G_c} \cdot \delta_T}$

$T$ designating $t$ in $\mathrm{II}$ mode and $\tau$ in $\mathrm{III}$ mode, respectively

**CZM_LIN_REG, CZM_OUV_MIX, CZM_TAC_MIX**

${\sigma}_{T}={\sigma}_{c}(1-{\delta}_{T}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}})$ , $T$ designating $t$ in $\mathrm{II}$ mode and $\tau$ in $\mathrm{III}$ mode respectively

**CZM_FROT_MIX**

$$
\sigma_T =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{ll}
K_t\delta_t &\text{si } \delta_t \leq \frac{c}{K_t}, \\
c &\text{sinon}.
\end{array}
\right.
$$

### 22.3 Tested quantities and results

The above-mentioned guidance for Modelling A is also valid for this Modelling. With no more elements interfaces per se in the model, SIGN and SIGTX tests are replaced by X-FEM LAGS_C and LAGS_F1 contact multipliers respectively.

**Mode** $I$

| **quantity tested** | **time step** | **Reference** | **Tolerance** ( $\text{\%}$ ) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DX on node 2 | 4 | 1.71003596 | 0.10 |
| ETA_PILO | 4 | 7.898168291D-01 | 0.10 |
| SIXX on mesh cell 32 | 2 | 6.599934D-01 | 0.10 |
| LAGS_C on node 9 | 9 | 1.099989D-01 | 0.10 |
| LAGS_F1 on node 9 | 8 | 0.D+00 | 0.10 |

The v6.03.118-en-mode-i-pur explains how to obtain these values.

The $t$ instant jump is: $⟦u⟧=\frac{t}{\text{COEF\_MULT}}\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}$ . Thus, $⟦u⟧=0.163636t$ (in $\mathit{mm}$ ).

The displacement standard applied by $U=⟦u⟧+L\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{E}\left(1-⟦u⟧\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}}\right)$ can then be obtained. For $t=4$, $U=1.97\mathit{mm}$ is obtained, from which $\text{DX}=U\cos(\frac{\pi}{6})=1.71\mathit{mm}$ and $\text{ETA\_PILO}=\frac{U}{{U}_{0}}=0.78\mathit{mm}$ are obtained.

From $⟦u⟧$, you get the stress by $\sigma ={\sigma}_{c}\left(1-⟦u⟧\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2{G}_{c}}\right)$. For $t=9$, $\sigma =0.11\mathit{Mpa}$, which, taking into account the load in pure I mode, can be tested by the LAGS_C Lagrange multiplier. For $t=2$, you find $\sigma =0.88\mathit{Mpa}$, and you have ${\sigma}_{xx}=\sigma {\cos}^{2}(\frac{\pi}{6})=0.66\mathit{MPa}$, which is the value tested.
