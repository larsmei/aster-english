# v6.08.119 SSND119 - Validation of DASHPOT Relationship for Discreet elements

**Summary:**

The objective of this test case is to validate the constitutive law DASHPOT available for discreet elements by an analytical test.

## 1 reference solution

The constitutive law DASHPOT binds, at each ${t}_{i}$ calculation moment, the nodal force $F({t}_{i})$ to the ${\Delta}_{x}({t}_{i})$ displacement increment as follows: $F({t}_{i})=K{\Delta}_{x}({t}_{i})$ where $K$ is a stiffness parameter provided by the user.

In this test, the following moving load is required: $U(t)=\sin(\omega t)$ .

The analytical solution for force is thus expressed as follows: $F(t)=K\omega {\Delta}_{t}\cos(t)$ where ${\Delta}_{t}$ is the time-increment.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Geometry and modelling

The model has 4 discreet elements, 2 based on mesh cells POI1 and 2 based on mesh cells SEG2.

For each mesh cells type, the first vector of the local marker is oriented according to the $X$ vector (global) for one mesh cell and according to $Y$ for the other. For POI1, the keyword ORIENTATION is used to impose this, for SEG2, this is imposed by geometry. This allows to verify the correct consideration of the keyword REPERE that can take the GLOBAL or LOCAL values.

### 2.2 Parameters

The parameters are as follows:

- $\omega =2\pi f$ with $f=50\mathit{Hz}$
- $K=(\mathrm{1,0,0})$ , only a longitudinal stiffness (according to the local $x$ vector) is imposed
- The orientation is chosen: (30.30.30.30) to validate unidirectional behavior in a quelcquonque repere;
- Two ${\Delta}_{t}$ values are considered, a low for the first $1E-4$ calculation, leading to low displacement increments and therefore low forces, and a higher for the second $1E-3$ calculation, leading to larger displacement increments and therefore greater forces.

### 2.3 Results

For both calculations and for the 4 elements, the maximum difference between the analytical value and the calculated value shall be tested at each moment of calculation. This maximum shall be close to 0. For each calculation, this value is identical for the 4 elements. Moreover, as can be expected, the lower the time increment the closer one is to the analytical solution.
