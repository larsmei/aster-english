# r3.08.10 Element CABLE_GAINE

**Summary:**

The element CABLE_GAINE presented in this document is intended to model prestressing cables which may rub or slide in their sheaths and which therefore do not entirely follow the displacements of the concrete in which they are embedded. It therefore complements the modelling BARRE combined with the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP, which makes it possible to model prestressing cables fully bonded to the concrete (see [R7.01.02](doc:r7.01.02)).

This element is very strongly inspired by the interface element described in [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13). It models the interface of the cable with its sheath, and the cable itself.

## 1 Variational formulation

### 1.1 Potential energy and minimisation

The potential energy, whose local minimum is sought, is written as the sum of the elastic strain energy (in the cable) and of the cohesive energy (at the sheath/cable interface), minus the work of the external forces:

${E}_{\mathit{pot}}(u)={E}_{\mathit{el}}(u)+{E}_{\mathit{gc}}(u)-{W}_{\mathit{ext}}(u)$

The expression for the elastic energy is the following:

${E}_{\mathit{el}}(u)={\int}_{\Gamma}\Phi (\varepsilon ({u}_{\mathit{cable}}(s)))\mathit{ds}$

where $\Gamma$ is the path (1D) of the cable, $\Phi$ is the elastic energy density and ${u}_{\mathit{cable}}$ is the displacement of the cable.

The displacement of the cable is defined as follows:

${u}_{\mathit{cable}}(s)={u}_{\mathit{gaine}}(s)+g(s)T(s)$

where $T$ represents the tangent to the cable, ${u}_{\mathit{gaine}}$ the displacement of the sheath and $g$ the relative displacement of the cable with respect to the sheath.

One then has (cf. Salençon, “mechanics of continuous media — Volume II: Elasticity — Curvilinear media”):

$\varepsilon ({u}_{\mathit{cable}}(s))=\frac{d({u}_{\mathit{gaine}}+gT)}{\mathit{ds}}\cdot T=\frac{d({u}_{\mathit{gaine}})}{\mathit{ds}}\cdot T+\frac{\mathit{dg}}{\mathit{ds}}$

It is specified that multiplication by the tangent vector to the cable $T$ makes it possible to pass from an “elongation” vector along the three directions of space to a scalar “elongation” along the cable. In fact, in order to obtain this expression, $\frac{dT}{\mathit{ds}}$ is neglected.

The following two quantities are defined:

${\epsilon}_{{u}_{\mathit{gaine}}}({u}_{\mathit{gaine}})=\frac{d({u}_{\mathit{gaine}})}{\mathit{ds}}\cdot T$ and ${\epsilon}_{g}(g)=\frac{\mathit{dg}}{\mathit{ds}}$

The strain of the cable is thus finally written:

$\epsilon ({u}_{\mathit{cable}}(s))={\epsilon}_{{u}_{\mathit{gaine}}}({u}_{\mathit{gaine}})+{\epsilon}_{g}(g)$

The cohesive energy (sheath/cable), for its part, is written:

${E}_{\mathit{gc}}(u)={\int}_{\Gamma}\Pi (g(s))\mathit{ds}$

where $\Pi$ is the cohesive energy density.

In view of the non-differentiability of $\Pi$, a decomposition–coordination method will be used to treat this minimisation. This consists in introducing the slip $\delta$, in setting $\delta (s)=g(s)$ and in reducing the problem to the following constrained minimisation problem:

$\underset{\begin{array}{c}u,\delta \\ g=\delta \end{array}}{min}E(u,\delta )$

with

$E(u,\delta )={\int}_{\Gamma}\Phi (\varepsilon ({u}_{\mathit{cable}}(s)))\mathit{ds}+{\int}_{\Gamma}\Pi (\delta (s))\mathit{ds}-{W}_{{\mathit{ext}}_{\mathit{gaine}}}({u}_{\mathit{gaine}})-{W}_{{\mathit{ext}}_{\mathit{cable}}}(g)$

This will make it possible to treat separately (decomposition) the minimisation of the cohesive energy at the local level (static condensation), while the minimisation of the elastic energy and of the work will be treated at the global level.

### 1.2 Augmented Lagrangian

The constrained minimisation problem is treated by dualisation: the augmented Lagrangian $L$ and the multiplier field $\lambda$ (coordination) are introduced:

$L(u,\delta ,\lambda )=E(u,\delta )+{\int}_{\Gamma}\lambda (s)\cdot (g(s)-\delta (s))\mathit{ds}+\frac{r}{2}{\int}_{\Gamma}(g(s)-\delta (s))\mathrm{²}\mathit{ds}$

with $r$ a penalisation coefficient.

### 1.3 Characterisation of the saddle point

The first-order optimality conditions make it possible to write:

$\forall \delta {u}_{\mathit{gaine}}:{\int}_{\Gamma}\sigma :{\epsilon}_{{u}_{\mathit{gaine}}}(\delta {u}_{\mathit{gaine}}(s))\mathit{ds}={W}_{{\mathit{ext}}_{\mathit{gaine}}}(\delta {u}_{\mathit{gaine}})$ with $\sigma =\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \epsilon }(\epsilon )$             [eq. 1.1]

$\forall \delta g:{\int}_{\Gamma}\sigma :{\epsilon}_{g}(\delta g(s))\mathit{ds}+{\int}_{\Gamma}[\lambda (s)+r(g(s)-\delta (s))]\cdot \delta g(s)\mathit{ds}={W}_{{\mathit{ext}}_{\mathit{cable}}}(\delta g)$     [eq. 1.2]

$\forall \delta \lambda :{\int}_{\Gamma}[g(s)-\delta (s)]\cdot \delta \lambda (s)\mathit{ds}=0$                                             [eq. 1.3]

$\forall \delta \delta :{\int}_{\Gamma}[t(s)-\lambda (s)-r(g(s)-\delta (s))]\cdot \delta \delta (s)\mathit{ds}=0$ with $t\in \partial \Pi (\delta )$             [eq. 1.4]

## 2 Discretisation of the finite element

*Preliminary note:*

*The finite element envisaged models the interface of the cable with its sheath, and the cable itself. It would have been possible to model separately the cable–sheath interface (with an interface finite element) and the cable itself (with a cable finite element); however, as will be seen below, the frictional constitutive law requires knowledge of the tension in the cable, so it is advantageous to have the interface and the cable in the same element. Furthermore, as will be seen below, the modelling proposed is based on quadratic elements, so it is not possible to reuse the existing element* BARRE*.*

The finite element envisaged is lineic (geometric support in the form of segments), unlike the interface elements, which are surfacic or volumic.

It is considered to represent a cable embedded in a volume (of concrete) $\mathrm{3D}$. Consequently, the degrees of freedom are:

- the displacements of the sheath (3 degrees of freedom): ${u}_{\mathit{gaine}}$
- the relative longitudinal displacement of the cable in the sheath $g$
- and the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$.

By analogy with the interface elements, the relative slip $\delta$ is discretised at the Gauss points and then eliminated by static condensation. The interpolation adopted is P2 for the displacements (of the sheath and of the cable) and P1 for the Lagrange multiplier. The supporting geometric elements will therefore be SEG3 mesh cells. The constraint $g=\delta$ is imposed in the weak sense.

The Gauss points chosen are the same as for the under-integrated interface element, namely 2 integration points (unlike the interface elements presented in [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13), the fact that the cable has only longitudinal displacements controlled by the stiffness of the cable does not require the use of an integration with 3 Gauss points. This point was verified during the tests). The following figure makes it possible to visualise all this information.

## 3 Static condensation

The field $\delta$ disappears from the global formulation thanks to the static condensation. At each collocation point ${s}_{g}$, one has (from eq. 1.4):

${t}_{g}={\lambda}_{g}+r({g}_{g}-{\delta}_{g})\in \partial \Pi ({\delta}_{g})$

with ${t}_{g}=t({s}_{g})$ , ${\lambda}_{g}=\lambda ({s}_{g})$ , ${g}_{g}=g({s}_{g})$ and ${\delta}_{g}=\delta ({s}_{g})$.

Once the problem has been discretised, ${g}_{g}$ and ${\lambda}_{g}$ will be obtained by interpolation, with the appropriate shape functions, from the values of $g$ and $\lambda$ discretised at the nodes, which are denoted $\lbrace  G\rbrace$ and $\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace$.

Integration of the constitutive relation (cf. below) makes it possible to compute ${\delta}_{g}$ as a function of $\lbrace  G\rbrace$ and $\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace$, which is denoted $\delta$:

${t}_{g}={\lambda}_{g}+r({g}_{g}-{\delta}_{g})\in \partial \Pi ({\delta}_{g})\mathrm{\iff }{\delta}_{g}=\stackrel{ˆ}{\delta}({g}_{g},{\lambda}_{g})=\delta (\lbrace  G\rbrace  ,\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  )$

## 4 Integration

The friction laws envisaged are perfect bond, frictionless sliding and a Coulomb friction with a threshold making it possible to recover the tension profiles given by the BPEL (or by the ETCC, since the two codes use equivalent formulae). These three cases can be used with the constitutive law CABLE_GAINE_FROT.

The addition of new friction laws adapted to other regulations is possible.

At a given Gauss point, integration of the laws consists in determining ${\delta}_{g}$. To this end, one seeks the point of intersection between the straight line representing the equation ${t}_{g}={\lambda}_{g}+r({g}_{g}-{\delta}_{g})$ (green curve on the figures) and the curve representing the derivative of $\Pi ({\delta}_{g})$ (blue curve on the figures).

**Note:** in the paragraphs which follow, a notation on the displacements is adopted, so as not to burden the notation, rather than a notation on the displacement increments, as is actually the case.

### 4.1 Perfect bond

In the first case, corresponding to perfect bond, one has:

> $\Pi ({\delta}_{g})={I}_{R⁺}({\delta}_{g})+{I}_{R⁻}({\delta}_{g})$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.10/Object_30.png)

Figure 4-1: Graphical interpretation of the integration of the perfect-bond law

> The solution is: ${\delta}_{g}=0$

### 4.2 Frictionless sliding

In the second case, frictionless sliding, one has:

> $\Pi ({\delta}_{g})=0$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.10/Object_31.png)

Figure 4-2: Graphical interpretation of the integration of the perfect-sliding law

> The solution satisfies:
> $0={\lambda}_{g}+r({g}_{g}-{\delta}_{g})$

whence

> ${\delta}_{g}={g}_{g}+\frac{{\lambda}_{g}}{r}$

#### 4.2.1 Sliding with friction (BPEL)

This law makes it possible to take straight friction and curved friction into account. The tensions imposed by the BPEL are recovered by choosing the threshold of the Coulomb law ${\sigma}_{c}$ as follows:

${\sigma}_{c}=-(\varphi +f\frac{\partial \alpha }{\partial s})N$

with the notation of [R7.01.02](doc:r7.01.02) §2.2.2, which is recalled:

- $f$ the coefficient of friction of the cable on the concrete in the curved part, in ${\mathit{rad}}^{-1}$,
- $\varphi$ the coefficient of friction per unit length, in ${m}^{-1}$,
- $\alpha$ the cumulative angular deviation.

and with $N$ the normal force.

Indeed, considering that the cable slides over its whole length during tensioning and denoting by $s$ the curvilinear abscissa along the cable, the equilibrium of the cable is written:

$\frac{\mathit{dN}}{\mathit{ds}}=-(\varphi +f\frac{\partial \alpha }{\partial s})N$

whence, by integration:

$N={N}_{0}\exp(-(\varphi s+f\alpha (s)))$

One then recognises the expression for the tension of the cable in the presence of the straight friction of the BPEL ([R7.01.02](doc:r7.01.02) §2.2.2).

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.10/Object_23.png)

Figure 4-3: Graphical interpretation of the integration of the BPEL friction law

In order to write the solution, there are 3 cases:

    - if $∣({\lambda}_{g}+{\mathit{rg}}_{g})∣\le {\sigma}_{c}$ : ${\delta}_{g}=0$
    - if ${\lambda}_{g}+{\mathit{rg}}_{g}>{\sigma}_{c}$ : ${\delta}_{g}={g}_{g}+\frac{{\lambda}_{g}-{\sigma}_{c}}{r}$
    - if ${\lambda}_{g}+{\mathit{rg}}_{g}<-{\sigma}_{c}$ : ${\delta}_{g}={g}_{g}+\frac{{\lambda}_{g}+{\sigma}_{c}}{r}$

The case in which the normal force $N$ is negative should not normally occur for prestressing-cable problems. However, very slightly negative values may arise. The choice is made to adopt the sliding behaviour in such a case.

**Note 1**: by introducing such a dependence of the slip on the tension in the cable, the contribution of the variation of this tension to the slip $\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial N}$ induces a non-symmetric matrix.

**Note 2:** In order for the curved friction to be taken into account at the element level, it is imperative that the curvature of the cable be transcribed into the elements which constitute it, that is the three nodes of the SEG3 mesh cells must not be aligned.

## 5 Discretised formulation

### 5.1 Notation

It is recalled that the elements CABLE_GAINE have 3 nodes and that only the end nodes have Lagrange degrees of freedom (cf.).

- The shape functions of the displacements are denoted ${n}_{m}$ with $m\in \lbrace  1,2,3\rbrace$.
- The shape functions of the Lagrange multipliers are denoted ${l}_{p}$ with $p\in \lbrace  1,2\rbrace$.

One denotes (the quantities are evaluated at each Gauss point):

- ${s}_{g}$ the curvilinear abscissa
- ${\omega}_{g}$ the weight of the Gauss points
- ${\sigma}_{g}$ the stress tensor
- ${N}_{g}^{3}$ the matrix of the values of the shape functions at the Gauss point $g$ discretising the displacements of the sheath (3 components)

${N}_{g}^{3}=(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}{n}_{1}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{2}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{3}({s}_{g})& 0& 0\\ 0& {n}_{1}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{2}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{3}({s}_{g})& 0\\ 0& 0& {n}_{1}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{2}({s}_{g})& 0& 0& {n}_{3}({s}_{g})\end{array})$

- ${N}_{g}^{1}$ the matrix of the values of the shape functions at the Gauss point $g$ discretising the relative displacements of the cable (1 component):

${N}_{g}^{1}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{n}_{1}({s}_{g})& {n}_{2}({s}_{g})& {n}_{3}({s}_{g})\end{array})$

- ${T}_{g}$ the tangent vector at the Gauss point $g$
- $\nabla {N}_{g}^{1}$ the derivatives of the shape functions discretising the relative displacements of the cable
- $\nabla {N}_{g}^{3}$ the derivatives of the shape functions discretising the displacements of the sheath
- One also sets, in order to simplify the notation: ${B}_{g}={T}_{g}^{T}\nabla {N}_{g}^{3}$
- ${L}_{g}$ the matrix of the values of the shape functions discretising the Lagrange multiplier at the Gauss point ${L}_{g}=(\begin{array}{cc}{l}_{1}({s}_{g})& {l}_{2}({s}_{g})\end{array})$
- $\lbrace  U\rbrace$ the nodal displacements (sheath + cable)
- $\lbrace  {U}_{\mathit{ga}}\rbrace$ the nodal displacements of the sheath
- $\lbrace {G}_{c}\rbrace$ the relative nodal displacement of the cable
- $\lbrace \Lambda \rbrace$ the nodal Lagrange multiplier
- $\lbrace  {F}_{\mathit{ext}}^{\mathit{ga}}\rbrace$ the nodal external-force vector, dual of the displacements of the sheath
- $\lbrace {F}_{\mathit{ext}}^{c}\rbrace$ the nodal external-force vector, dual of the relative nodal displacement of the cable
- $A$ the cross-section of the cable

### 5.2 Discretisation of the optimality conditions

Equations 1.1, 1.2 and 1.3 (eq. 1.4 being treated at the level of the constitutive law), which characterise the saddle point, are discretised in the form:

$\sum{\omega}_{g}({B}_{g}^{T}:{\sigma}_{g})=\lbrace  {F}_{\mathit{ext}}^{\mathit{ga}}\rbrace$

$\sum{\omega}_{g}[{[\nabla {N}_{g}^{1}]}^{T}:{\sigma}_{g}+{[{N}_{g}^{1}]}^{T}\cdot [[{L}_{g}]\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  +r[{N}_{g}^{1}]\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  -r\delta (\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  ,\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  )]]=\lbrace  {F}_{\mathit{ext}}^{c}\rbrace$

$\sum{\omega}_{g}[{[{L}_{g}]}^{T}\cdot [[{N}_{g}^{1}]\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  -\delta (\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  ,\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  )]]=\lbrace  0\rbrace$

### 5.3 Internal forces

The following notation is introduced:

$({f}_{u}^{g})=({B}_{g}^{T}:{\sigma}_{g})$

$({f}_{{g}_{c}}^{g})={[\nabla {N}_{g}^{1}]}^{T}:{\sigma}_{g}+{[{N}_{g}^{1}]}^{T}\cdot [[{L}_{g}]\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  +r[{N}_{g}^{1}]\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  -r\delta (\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  ,\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  )]$

$({f}_{\lambda}^{g})={[{L}_{g}]}^{T}\cdot [[{N}_{g}^{1}]\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  -\delta (\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace  ,\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace  )]$

The contributions to the forces, for $i\in \lbrace  1,2,3\rbrace$ , $m\in \lbrace  1,2,3\rbrace$ and $p\in \lbrace  1,2\rbrace$, are then written:

${({f}_{u})}_{i,m}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}{({f}_{{u}_{\mathit{gaine}}}^{g})}_{i,m}$

${({f}_{{g}_{c}})}_{m}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}{({f}_{{u}_{\mathit{cable}}}^{g})}_{m}$

${({f}_{\lambda})}_{p}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}{({f}_{\lambda}^{g})}_{p}$

where

${({f}_{u}^{g})}_{i,m}$ is the component $(3(m-1)+i)$ of $({f}_{u}^{g})$,

${({f}_{{g}_{c}}^{g})}_{m}$ is the component $m$ of $({f}_{{g}_{c}}^{g})$ and

${({f}_{\lambda}^{g})}_{p}$ is the component $p$ of $({f}_{\lambda}^{g})$

### 5.4 Stiffness matrix

The notation at a Gauss point is recalled:

- the relative displacement of the cable: ${g}_{g}={N}_{g}^{1}\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace$
- the strain of the cable: ${\varepsilon}_{g}={B}_{g}\lbrace  {U}_{\mathit{ga}}\rbrace  +(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})\lbrace  {G}_{c}\rbrace$
- the Lagrange multiplier: ${\lambda}_{g}=({L}_{g})\lbrace  \Lambda \rbrace$

Setting ${\tau}_{g}={\lambda}_{g}+{\mathit{rg}}_{g}$, the two paragraphs which follow give the contributions to the tangent matrix for the bonded and sliding cases and for the frictional case.

#### 5.4.1 Bonded and sliding cases

In the bonded and sliding cases, the tangent matrix is symmetric (minimisation of a saddle point):

${K}_{uu}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}{B}_{g}^{T}\frac{d\sigma }{d\varepsilon }{B}_{g}$

${K}_{u{g}_{c}}={({K}_{{g}_{c}u})}^{T}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}{B}_{g}^{T}\frac{d\sigma }{d\varepsilon }(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})$

${K}_{u\lambda }={({K}_{\lambda u})}^{T}=0$

${K}_{{g}_{c}{g}_{c}}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[{(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\frac{d\sigma }{d\varepsilon }(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})+{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}r({N}_{g}^{1})-{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\mathit{r²}\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}({N}_{g}^{1})]$

${K}_{{g}_{c}\lambda }={({K}_{\lambda {g}_{c}})}^{T}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}({L}_{g})+{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}r\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}({L}_{g})]$

${K}_{\lambda \lambda }={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[-{({L}_{g})}^{T}\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}({L}_{g})]$

It is specified that $\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}$ is obtained from the friction constitutive law.

#### 5.4.2 Frictional case

In the frictional case, there is a dependence of the slip on the tension in the cable. The following expressions are thereby modified:

${K}_{{g}_{c}u}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[{(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\frac{d\sigma }{d\varepsilon }{B}_{g}-r{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial N}A\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }{B}_{g}]$

${K}_{\lambda u}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[-{({L}_{g})}^{T}\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial N}A\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }{B}_{g}]$

${K}_{{g}_{c}{g}_{c}}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[{(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\frac{d\sigma }{d\varepsilon }(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})+{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}r({N}_{g}^{1})-{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}\mathit{r²}\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}({N}_{g}^{1})-{({N}_{g}^{1})}^{T}r\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial N}A\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})]$

${K}_{\lambda {g}_{c}}={\sum}_{g}{\omega}_{g}[{({L}_{g})}^{T}({N}_{g}^{1})+{({L}_{g})}^{T}r\frac{d{\delta}_{g}}{d{\tau}_{g}}({N}_{g}^{1})-{({L}_{g})}^{T}\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial N}A\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }(\nabla {N}_{g}^{1})]$

(The other expressions are unchanged relative to 5.4.1)

It is found that the matrix is no longer symmetric.

## 6 Convergence of the analysis: adjustment of the parameters

Convergence of an analysis with the elements CABLE_GAINE is sometimes difficult to obtain, in view of the different orders of magnitude present. This is why the penalisation coefficient $r$ must be chosen carefully, and a convergence criterion by reference values (keyword RESI_REFE_RELA) is to be preferred. The following two paragraphs give advice on use on these points.

### 6.1 Penalisation coefficient

When the augmented Lagrangian was written, the coefficient $r$, called the penalisation coefficient, was introduced. In order to choose the value of $r$ (PENA_LAGR in the cohesive law CABLE_GAINE_FROT), it being recalled that the converged solution does not depend on it, or in order to explain the construction of the reference force used in the criterion RESI_REFE_RELA (see the following §), it is necessary to carry out a brief dimensional analysis.

For the choice of $r$, it is seen in the preceding equations that it is judicious for it to be of the order of magnitude of $\frac{\lambda}{u}$.

$u$ is the order of magnitude of the expected displacements.

$\lambda$ is the friction force per unit length, i.e. the shear stress (friction) integrated over the perimeter of the cable. If one takes a stress ${\sigma}^{\mathit{ref}}$ typical of that expected in the concrete in the neighbourhood of the cable, $\lambda$ will be of the order $2\pi {r}_{\mathit{cable}}{\sigma}^{\mathit{ref}}$.

In order to obtain the best possible convergence, $r$ must be chosen of the order of magnitude of $2\pi {r}_{\mathit{cable}}\frac{{\sigma}^{\mathit{ref}}}{{u}^{\mathit{ref}}}$.

### 6.2 Convergence criterion by reference values

Use of a convergence criterion by value is often necessary in order to complete the analysis.

To this end the keyword RESI_REFE_RELA must be activated in the factor keyword CONVERGENCE of STAT_NON_LINE or CALC_PRECONT. In the case of the element CABLE_GAINE, this keyword must be accompanied by three others:

- a reference force (keyword EFFORT_REFE): the tension expected in the cable,
- a reference displacement (keyword DEPL_REFE): a typical displacement of the structure,
- a reference stress (keyword SIGM_REFE): the order of magnitude of the stresses expected in the neighbourhood of the cable (the typical stress of the concrete, except in a particular case); this stress makes it possible to construct a reference for $\lambda$ using the cross-section $A$ of the cable in the form: ${\lambda}^{\mathit{ref}}={\sigma}^{\mathit{ref}}\mathrm{\ast }\sqrt{A}$
