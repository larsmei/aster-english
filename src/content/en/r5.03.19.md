# r5.03.19 Hyperelastic constitutive laws

**Summary:**

Hyperelastic constitutive laws adopted for elastomers are described here.
The compressible and incompressible cases are presented.

For the models treating incompressibility, one proposes:

- the hyperelastic model of Signorini type, which constitutes a generalised version of the Mooney-Rivlin laws, implemented natively, defined by RELATION='ELAS_HYPER',
- the visco-hyperelastic model, whose hyperelastic part is of Signorini type, coupled to a viscoelastic branch of generalised Maxwell type, placed in parallel, implemented under MFront, defined by RELATION='ELAS_HYPER_VISC'.

For compressible materials, one proposes:

- the Hill law, implemented under MFront, defined by RELATION='HYPER_HILL'.

These models are chosen in the commands [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03) or [DYNA_NON_LINE](doc:u4.53.01), by means of the keyword RELATION under the keyword [COMPORTEMENT](doc:u4.51.11). These relations are understood under large transformations via the keyword DEFORMATION='GREEN_LAGRANGE'. They are available for the 3D,
3D_SI, C_PLAN and D_PLAN elements.

## 1 Hyperelastic formulation

### 1.1 Kinematics

One considers a solid $\domain$ subjected to large strains. Let $\gradTransfor$ be the transformation-gradient tensor taking the initial configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ to the current deformed configuration $\domainCurr$ . One denotes by $\posiRefe$ the position of a point in $\domainRefe$ and by $\posiCurr$ the position of this same point after strain in $\domainCurr$ . $\disp$ is then the displacement between the two configurations. One therefore has:

$$
\posiCurr = \posiRefe + \disp
$$

The transformation-gradient tensor is written:

$$
\gradTransfor
=
\frac{\partial \posiCurr}{\partial \posiRefe}
=
\tensTwoUnit + \gradVector{\disp}
$$

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.19/Configurations.png)

*Transformation from the initial configuration to the final one*

This tensor is not the best candidate for describing the strain of the structure. In particular, it is not zero for rigid-body motions and it describes the whole transformation: the change of length of the infinitesimal elements but also their orientation. Now a pure rotation motion does not generate stresses and it is therefore preferable to use a strain measure which does not take this rigid rotation into account. Let us consider an infinitesimal length element denoted $\posiIncrRefe$ in the initial configuration and $\posiIncrCurr$ in the final configuration. If the motion is a rigid rotation $\tensTwo{R}$ , one has:

$$
\posiIncrCurr= \tensTwo{R} \posiIncrRefe
$$

The norm of this vector after transformation is therefore equal to

$$
\posiIncrCurr \cdot \posiIncrCurr
=
\tensTwo{R} \posiIncrRefe \cdot \tensTwo{R} \posiIncrRefe
=
\posiIncrRefe \left( \transpose{\tensTwo{R}}   \tensTwo{R}  \right) \posiIncrRefe
$$

As the transformation is purely rigid, one has:

$$
\transpose{\tensTwo{R}} \tensTwo{R}
=
\tensTwoUnit
$$

$\tensTwo{R}$ is therefore an orthogonal tensor. The strain-gradient tensor may be written as the product of an orthogonal tensor and of a positive definite tensor (polar factorisation):

$$
\gradTransfor
=
\tensTwo{R} \tensTwo{U}
$$

The tensor $\tensTwo{U}$ (called the stretch tensor) is therefore a first measure of large strains. On the other hand, it requires the polar factorisation of $\gradTransfor$, which is a costly operation. One therefore prefers to use the right Cauchy-Green strain tensor:

$$
\ECGDroite
=
\transpose{\gradTransfor} {\gradTransfor}
=
\tensTwo{U}^{2}
$$

This tensor is symmetric. The three invariants of the Cauchy-Green tensor $\ECGDroite$ are given by:

$$
\tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
=
\trace \ECGDroite
$$

$$
\tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
=
\frac{1}{2} \left( { \trace^2 \ECGDroite - \trace \ECGDroite^{2} } \right)
$$

$$
\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}
=
\det \ECGDroite
$$

The last invariant $\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}$ describes the change of volume; one can also write it:

$$
\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}
=
{\left( \det \gradTransfor\right)}^{2}
=
{\jacobTransfor}^{2}
$$

### 1.2 Strain potential – compressible case

A hyperelastic model assumes the existence of an internal-energy density potential $\enerInterne$ , a scalar function of the strain measure. If one considers an isotropic constitutive behaviour, the function $\enerInterne$ must be isotropic as well. One shows that if the function $\enerInterne$ depends only on the invariants of the right Cauchy-Green strain tensor $\ECGDroite$ , then it describes an isotropic constitutive behaviour. The potential is therefore a function of these three invariants:

$$
\enerInterne
=
\enerInterne \left ( \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite} , \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}, \tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite} \right)
$$

The second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor is obtained by differentiation of this potential with respect to the strains (see [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20)):

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
2   \frac{\partial \enerInterne }{\partial \ECGDroite}
$$

#### 1.2.1 Signorini law

The most general form of a potential is polynomial. It is written as a function of the invariants of the right Cauchy-Green tensor and of the material parameters $C_{pqr}$ according to Rivlin:

$$
\enerInterne
=
\sum_{(p,q,r)=0}^{\infty}
{ {C}_{pqr}
  {(\tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}-3)}^{p}
  {(\tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}-3)}^{q}
  {(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}-1)}^{r} }
$$

with ${C}_{000}=\tensTwoZero$ .

There exist particular forms of this potential which are used very frequently, with $r=0$ :

|  | $p$ | $q$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2 | 1 |  |
| ${C}_{10}$ | ${C}_{20}$ | ${C}_{01}$ |  |
| Signorini | YES | YES | YES |
| Mooney-Rivlin | YES | NO | YES |
| Neo-Hookean | YES | NO | NO |

#### 1.2.2 Hill law

The Hill law is a hyperelastic constitutive model, specifically developed for compressible elastomers. The potential of this law is expressed in the following way:

$$
\enerInterne
=
 2   \frac{\mu_i}{\alpha_i^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\lambda_1^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_2^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_3^{\alpha_i}-3+
\frac{1}{\beta_i}(J^{-\alpha_i\beta_i}-1)\right)
$$

With $n$ the order of the expansion of the constitutive law;
$\alpha_i, \beta_i, \mu_i$ coefficients of the law;
$\lambda_1, \lambda_2, \lambda_3$ principal stretches.

As regards the coefficients:

- The coefficients $\mu_i$ are related to the initial shear modulus $\mu$ by the relation $\sum_{i=1}^{n} \mu_i = \mu$.
- The coefficients $\beta_i$ are related to the Poisson's ratios $\nu_i$ by the relation $\beta_i = \frac{\nu_i}{1 - 2\nu_i}$.

### 1.3 Strain potential – incompressible case

#### 1.3.1 Principle

Most hyperelastic materials (such as elastomers) are incompressible, that is:

$$
\det \gradTransfor = 1
$$

And therefore the third invariant $\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}$ is equal to:

$$
\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}
=
1
$$

The hyperelastic potential in the incompressible mode is therefore rewritten:

$$
\enerInterne
=
\sum_{(p,q)=0}^{\infty}{C}_{pq}    {(\tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}-3)}^{p}   {(\tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}-3)}^{q}
$$

Unfortunately, such a writing leads to severe numerical problems (except for the case of plane stresses). We shall therefore propose a new writing which makes it possible to solve the case of incompressibility more efficiently and which has the further advantage of also being valid in the compressible mode, with a judicious (and easy) choice of the coefficients.

#### 1.3.2 Modified right Cauchy-Green tensor

One begins by defining a new right Cauchy-Green tensor $\EDil$ (pure deviatoric or isochoric dilatations) such that:

$$
\EDil
=
{\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{2}{3}}   \ECGDroite
$$

Indeed, ${\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{2}{3}} \tensTwoUnit$ denotes the pure volumetric strain. This tensor remains symmetric. Its invariants are:

$$
\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}
=
{\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{2}{3}}   \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
$$

$$
\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}
=
{\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{4}{3}}   \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
$$

$$
\tensTwoInva{3}{\EDil}
=
\tensTwoUnit
$$

Given that:

$$
\jacobTransfor
=
{\left(
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{1} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{2}{2} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{3}{3}
    -
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{2}^{2} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{3}{3}
    -
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{2}{3}^{2} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{1}
    +
    2  \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{2}  \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{3} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{2}{3}
    -
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{3}^{2} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{2}
\right )}^{\frac{1}{2}}
$$

With:

$$
\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}
=
\frac{\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{1}+
      \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{2}+
      \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{3}{3}}
     {\jacobTransfor^{2/3}}
$$

And:

$$
\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}
=
\frac{\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{2}{2} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{3}{3}+
      \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{1} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{3}{3}+
      \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{1}{1} \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{2}{2}}
      {{\jacobTransfor}^{4/3}}
$$

These invariants are also called the **reduced** invariants of $C$ .

#### 1.3.3 Modified strain potential

If one expresses the strain potential by means of the reduced invariants of $\ECGDroite$ , one can decompose the potential into two parts:

$$
\enerInterne =
{\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{iso}}
+
{\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{vol}}
$$

There is the part ${\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{iso}}$ which corresponds to the isochoric strains ($\jacobTransfor=1$ ):

$$
{\enerInterne }^{\mathrm{iso}}
=
{\sum}_{p,q=0}^{\infty}
{C}_{pq}   {\left( \tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}-3 \right)}^{p}  {\left( \tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil} - 3 \right)}^{q}
$$

And the part ${\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ which corresponds to the volumetric strains ($\jacobTransfor\ne 1$ ):

$$
{\enerInterne }^{\mathrm{vol}}
=
\frac{\bulkModulus}{2}  {\left( \jacobTransfor - 1 \right)}^{2}
$$

$\bulkModulus$ is the compressibility coefficient. This formulation makes it possible to take the incompressible and compressible effects into account:

1. In the incompressible case, and within the framework of a finite-element formulation, the parameter $\bulkModulus$ plays the role of a penalisation coefficient of the incompressibility condition;
2. In the compressible case this same coefficient expresses a material property: the hydrostatic compressibility.

If the model characterising the material is of Mooney-Rivlin type ($p=q=1$ ), $\bulkModulus$ may be given by:

$$
\bulkModulus
=
\frac{4({C}_{01}+{C}_{10})(1+\poissonCoef)}{3(1 - 2\poissonCoef)}
$$

In the case of small strains, $\youngModulus = 4  \left({C}_{01}+{C}_{10})(1+\poissonCoef \right)$ represents Young's modulus whereas $\shearModulus=2   \left({C}_{01}+{C}_{10} \right)$ represents the shear modulus.

#### 1.3.4 Second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor

The second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor, representing the stresses measured in the initial configuration, is written:

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
2   \frac{\partial \enerInterne }{\partial \ECGDroite}
$$

The factor 2 makes it possible to recover the usual expression in small strains. One can separate it into two parts:

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
{\stressPKTwo}^{\textrm{iso}}
+
{\stressPKTwo}^{\textrm{vol}}
$$

With:

$$
{\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{iso}}
=
2   \frac{\partial \enerInterne^{\mathrm{iso}}} {\partial \ECGDroite}
$$

And:

$$
{\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{vol}}
=
2   \frac{\partial \enerInterne^{\mathrm{vol}}} {\partial \ECGDroite}
$$

${\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{iso}}$ is the isochoric stress tensor and ${\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{vol}}$ is that of the volumetric or hydrostatic stresses.

#### 1.3.5 Lagrangian elasticity tensor

The elastic stiffness tensor (the “tangent” matrix for the Newton problem) is given by the double differentiation of the potential:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
4   \frac{\partial^{2}\enerInterne}{\partial\ECGDroite \partial\ECGDroite}
=
4   \frac{\partial^{2}{\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial\ECGDroite \partial\ECGDroite}
+
4   \frac{\partial^{2}{\enerInterne}^{\mathrm{vol}}}{\partial\ECGDroite \partial\ECGDroite}
$$

## 2 Viscous formulation

### 2.1 Second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor

The viscosity is modelled by introducing a Prony series on the isochoric part of the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor ${\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{iso}}$. The Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor of the viscoelastic part is written as the finite sum of $N$ tensors $\tensTwo{H}_{i}$

$$
{\stressPKTwo}^\mathrm{visc}
=
\sum_{i=1}^N \tensTwo{H}_{i}
$$

With,

$$
\tensTwo{H}_{i}|_{t+\Delta t}
 =
 \exp \left( -\frac{dt}{\tau_{i}} \right) \left . \tensTwo{H}_{i} \right \vert_{t}
 +
 g_i \tau_{i} \left( 1-\exp \left( -\frac{dt}{\tau_{i}} \right) \right) \frac{\left({\stressPKTwo}^\mathrm{iso} \vert_{t+\Delta t}
 -
 {\stressPKTwo}^\mathrm{iso} \vert_{t}\right)}{dt}
$$

The Prony series depends on two lists of parameters ${g_i}$ and ${\tau_i}$, which present, respectively, the long-term shear relaxation modulus and the corresponding relaxation time.

### 2.2 Lagrangian elasticity tensor

The elastic stiffness tensor (the “tangent” matrix for the Newton problem) is given by the differentiation of the stress tensor:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\partial \tensTwo{H}_{i}}{\partial\ECGDroite}
$$

## 3 Analytical expressions

This section presents the analytical expressions of the visco-hyperelastic and compressible hyperelastic laws.

### 3.1 Visco-hyperelastic law

One recalls that when the shear relaxation modulus is equal to zero, one recovers the equations of the hyperelastic law.

#### 3.1.1 Case of the hyperelastic stresses

We shall detail the analytical expression of the Piola-Kirchhoff stresses for the Signorini hyperelastic potential ($p=2$  and $q=1$ ) in the incompressible case. One therefore has the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor, representing the stresses measured in the initial configuration, which is written:

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial\partial\ECGDroite}
+
2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}^{\mathrm{vol}}}{\partial\partial\ECGDroite}
$$

With the two potentials:

$$
{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}
=
{C}_{10} \left(\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil} - 3 \right)
+
{C}_{01} \left(\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil} - 3 \right)
+
{C}_{20} \left(\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}-3 \right)^{2}

\text{ et }

{\Psi}^{\mathrm{vol}}
=
\frac{\bulkModulus}{2} {\left( \jacobTransfor-1 \right)}^{2}
$$

In order to obtain the stresses, one must differentiate the potential:

$$
\left \lbrace  \begin{array}{ll}
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo^{\mathrm{iso}}}{i}{j}
    =
    2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}
      \frac{\partial\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo}{i}{j}}}
    +
    2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}
      \frac{\partial\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo}{i}{j}}} \\
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo^{\mathrm{vol}}}{i}{j}
    =
    2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}^{\mathrm{vol}}}{\partial \jacobTransfor}
      \frac{\partial \jacobTransfor}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo}{i}{j}}}
\end{array}  \right.
$$

With:

$$
\left \lbrace  \begin{array}{ll}
    \frac{\partial {\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial \tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}
    =
    {C}_{10} + 2  C_{20}   \left( \tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}-3 \right) \\
    \frac{\partial {\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial \tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}
    =
    {C}_{01}
\end{array}  \right.
$$

As well as the derivatives of the reduced invariants (*cf.* for the derivatives of the invariants of a tensor):

$$
\frac{\partial\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}}}
=
\left(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}\right)^{-\frac{1}{3}}
\left(
    {\tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}}
    -
    \frac{1}{3}  {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}} \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
\right)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}}}
=
\left( \tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite} \right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}
\left(
    \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}   \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
    -
    {\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}}
    -
    \frac{2}{3}   {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}}   \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
\right)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial \jacobTransfor}{\partial{\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}}}
=
\frac{1}{2}   \left( \tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite} \right)^{\frac{1}{2}}   {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}}
$$

Here then is the analytical expression of the volumetric stresses:

$$
\tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo^{\mathrm{vol}}}{i}{j}
=
\bulkModulus  \left(\jacobTransfor-1 \right) \jacobTransfor {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}}
$$

And of the isochoric stresses:

$$
\tensTwoCmpCO{\stressPKTwo^{\mathrm{iso}}}{i}{j}
=
2 \left\lbrace
    \left( {C}_{10}+ 2 C_{20} \left( \tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}-3 \right) \right )
    {\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{2}{3}}
    \left(
        \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
        -
        \frac{1}{3} {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}} \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
    \right)
    +
    {C}_{01}
    {\jacobTransfor}^{-\frac{4}{3}}
    \left( \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
           -
           {\tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}}
           -
           \frac{2}{3} {\tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}} \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
    \right)
\right \rbrace
$$

#### 3.1.2 Case of the visco-hyperelastic stresses

By coupling the viscosity with the hyperelasticity, the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor becomes:

$$
{\stressPKTwo}
=
{\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{iso}}
+
{\stressPKTwo}^{\mathrm{vol}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^N \tensTwo{H}_{i}
$$

#### 3.1.3 Case of the elastic matrix of the hyperelastic law

We shall detail the analytical expression of the elastic matrix for the Signorini hyperelastic potential ($p=2$  and $q=1$ ) in the incompressible case. One therefore has:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
4 \frac{\partial^{2}{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
       {\partial C \partial C}
+
4 \frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{vol}}}{\partial C \partial C}
$$

One must therefore differentiate (twice) the potential. For the isochoric part:

$$
\tensFourCmpCO{\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{iso}}}{i}{j}{k}{l}
=
\frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
     {\partial^2\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}
\frac{\partial^2\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}
     {\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
+
\frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}
     {\partial^2\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}
\frac{\partial^2\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}
     {\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
$$

and for the volumetric part

$$
\tensFourCmpCO{\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{vol}}}{i}{j}{k}{l}
=
\frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{vol}}}
     {\partial^2\jacobTransfor}
\frac{\partial^2\jacobTransfor}
     {\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
$$

The material constants are assumed constant. One therefore has:

$$
\frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial^2\tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}} = 2 C_{20}
\text{ et }
\frac{\partial^2{\Psi}^{\mathrm{iso}}}{\partial^2\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}} = 0
$$

One sees that the coefficient $\bulkModulus$ is indeed a penalisation coefficient and that its choice affects the conditioning of the matrix. The derivatives of the reduced invariants:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 \tensTwoInva{1}{\EDil}}
     {\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
=
\left(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}\right)^{-\frac{1}{3}}
\left(
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{i} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{l}{j} \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}-
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{k}{l}
    -
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{l} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
    +
    \frac{1}{3} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{l} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j} \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
\right)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial^2\tensTwoInva{2}{\EDil}}
     {\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
=
- \frac{2}{3} \left(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{l}
    \left(
        \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
        -
        \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j}
        -
        \frac{2}{3} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j} \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
    \right)
    +
    \left(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}\right)^{-\frac{2}{3}}
    \left(
        \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{k}{l} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{j}
        -
        \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{i}{k} \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{j}{l}
        +
        \frac{2}{3} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{i}
                    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{l}{j}
                    \tensTwoInva{2}{\ECGDroite}
        -
        \frac{2}{3} \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}
                    \left(
                        \tensTwoInva{1}{\ECGDroite}
                        \tensTwoCmpCO{{\tensTwoUnit}}{k}{l}
                        -
                        \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}
                    \right)
    \right)
$$

$$
\frac{\partial^2\jacobTransfor}{\partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{i}{j} \partial \tensTwoCmpCO{\ECGDroite}{k}{l}}
=
\frac{1}{4} \left(\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}
\left(
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{l}
    \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{i}{j}
    -
    2 \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{k}{i}
      \tensTwoCmpCO{\inverse{\ECGDroite}}{l}{j}
\right)
$$

#### 3.1.4 Case of the elastic matrix of the visco-hyperelastic law

In the case where the viscosity is taken into account, the analytical expression of the elastic matrix becomes:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{vol}}
+
\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{iso}}
+
\sum_{i=1}^N \frac{\partial \tensTwo{H}_{i}}{\partial \ECGDroite}
=
\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{vol}}
+
\modulusTangent^{\mathrm{iso}}
    \left(
        1
        +
        \sum_{i=1}^N g_i \tau_{i}
            \frac{ \left(1-\exp \left(-\frac{dt} {\tau_{i}} \right) \right)}
            {dt}
    \right)
$$

### 3.2 Compressible hyperelastic law

#### 3.2.1 Case of the compressible hyperelastic stresses

The second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor is written:

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
2   \frac{\partial{\Psi}}{\partial\ECGDroite}
$$

With:

$$
\Psi
=
 2  \frac{\mu_i}{\alpha_i^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\lambda_1^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_2^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_3^{\alpha_i}-3+
\frac{1}{\beta_i}(\jacobTransfor^{-\alpha_i\beta_i}-1)\right)
$$

Assuming that:

$$
f
=
\lambda_1^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_2^{\alpha_i}+
\lambda_3^{\alpha_i}
$$

One therefore has:

$$
\stressPKTwo
=
4   \frac{\mu_i}{\alpha_i^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\partial{f}}{\partial\ECGDroite}-

\frac{\alpha_i}{2}{\jacobTransfor}^{-\alpha_i\beta_i-2} \frac{\partial{\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}}}{\partial\ECGDroite}\right)
$$

#### 3.2.2 Case of the elastic matrix of the compressible hyperelastic law

The elastic matrix for the compressible Hill hyperelastic potential is written:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
\frac{\partial{\stressPKTwo}}{\partial\ECGDroite}
$$

One assumes:

$$
W_J
=
\frac{1}{\beta_i}(J^{-\alpha_i\beta_i}-1)
$$

One therefore has:

$$
\modulusTangent
=
4\frac{\mu_i}{\alpha_i^2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}
\left(
\frac{\partial^2{f}}{\partial^2\ECGDroite}+

\left(
\frac{\partial^2{W_J}}{\partial^2{\jacobTransfor}}
-\frac{1}{\jacobTransfor}\frac{\partial{W_J}}{\partial{\jacobTransfor}}
\right)

\frac{1}{2\jacobTransfor}\frac{\partial{\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}}}{\partial\ECGDroite}

\otimes\frac{\partial{\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}}}{\partial\ECGDroite}+

\frac{1}{\jacobTransfor}\frac{\partial{W_J}}{\partial{\jacobTransfor}}
\frac{\partial^2{\tensTwoInva{3}{\ECGDroite}}}{\partial^2\ECGDroite}

\right)
$$
