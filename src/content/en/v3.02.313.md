# v3.02.313 SSLP313 - Stitched in unlimited plate, subjected to uniform tensile infinity

**Summary:**

This test is based on independent validation of Code_Aster 3 in breakage mechanics.

${K}_{I}$ , ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ and the energy return rate for a straight crack, inclined at an $b$ angle, are calculated in a large plate with uniform traction. The model is two-dimensional in flat stresses. The material is isotropic linear elastic. This $\mathrm{2D}$ reference test allows the separability of ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ to be verified in a mixed mode.

The reference solution, given for a theoretically unlimited domain, is analytical.

In addition to the energy method (CALC_G), the method of calculating stress intensity factors by extrapolation of displacement (POST_K1_K2_K3) is tested. $B$ modelling allows testing this latter method with a recommended mesh type (nodes media per quarter) to obtain a precise solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.313/100002000000061500000800E410EB20C594714E.png)

Any value is assigned to the inclination, $\beta =37\mathit{degrés}$ .

We choose $a=1.E-3m$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is isotropic linear elastic, Young's modulus $E=2.E11\mathrm{Pa}$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.3$ .

The traction curve is defined as:

- the slope is equal to 3.
- the yield strength is equal to $1.88\mathrm{GPa}$ .

The flat stress hypothesis is applied.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

- Arbitrary limits of the mesh domain:

$-{x}_{\max}\le x\le {x}_{\max}$ with ${x}_{\max}=\mathrm{10a}$

$-{y}_{\max}\le y\le {y}_{\max}$ with ${y}_{\max}=\mathrm{20a}$

- boundary conditions :

In order to block only the 3 rigid plane modes.

$\mathrm{UX}=\mathrm{UY}=0$ at the bottom left corner of the complete model.

$\mathrm{UY}=0$ at the bottom right corner of the complete model.

On the lower edge we impose $\mathrm{UY}=0$

- Loading: ${\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{0}$ uniform voltage on the upper edge:

The value of ${\sigma}_{0}$ is $\mathrm{100MPa}$ , in flat constraints.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Airy's stress function.

### 2.2 Baseline

| ${K}_{I}={\sigma}_{0}\sqrt{({\pi}_{0})}{\cos}^{2}\beta$ |
| --- |
| ${K}_{\mathit{II}}={\sigma}_{0}\sqrt{({\pi}_{0})}\sin\beta \cos\beta$ |
| ${G}_{\mathit{ref}}=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})$ (in flat constraints) |

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

Analytical solution exact (Irwin) in unlimited medium.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

  1. MURAKAMI Stress intensity factors manual, box 4.2, page 188. The Society of Materials Science, Japan, Pergamon Press, 1987.

## 5 Summary of the results

With this choice of computational domain limits, we obtain differences in the order of $1\text{\%}$ on the ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ coefficients, and on the $G$ energy return rate.

For the POST_K1_K2_K3 method, the results are further removed from the reference with a standard mesh (from $–1\text{\%}$ to $–30\text{\%}$ d的difference), however, with a Barsium mesh (nodes media on the quarter side), which is recommended for this type of method, the differences are between $–3\text{\%}$ and $+1.2\text{\%}$ , which is relatively accurate.
