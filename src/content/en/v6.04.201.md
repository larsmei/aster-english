# v6.04.201 SSNV201 – Block with slide contact interface with X-FEM

**Summary**

This test case is designed to test and validate the GLISSIERE feature for contact with X-FEM. The main purpose of this test case is to prove that for an interface generated by X-FEM, where contact with option GLISSIERE is activated, there is no detachment of the surfaces in contact, they can only slide in relation to each other.

This test involves a parallelepipedic block through a modeled interface with X-FEM. The block is subject to imposed displacements, which would result in the separation of the structure when the GLISSIERE option is not activated and the slipping of both blocks without release, when this option is activated. Digitally, the activation of this option is done after the contact is established, either initial contact imposed by the user (CONTACT_INIT='OUI'), or natural contact, resulting from the kinematic evolution of the structure.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a straight parallelepiped with a rectangular and healthy base. The dimensions of the bar (see[Figure1.1-1)) are: $\mathrm{LX}=2m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=4m$ and $\mathrm{LZ}=10m$ .

The interface (Figure 1.1-2) is introduced by level functions (normal level set noted $\text{LN}$ directly in the control file using DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator. The interface is defined as a horizontal crack, in the middle of the block following the $\mathrm{OZ}$ direction given by the following level function:

By imposing this kinematic movement at the top of the block, we want to show the action of the GLISSIERE option. In the initial state, the contact is not affected on the lips of the crack (CONTACT_INI='NON') and therefore the two parts of the block will separate following the first loading step ($\mathrm{DZ}=\mathrm{DEPZ}$ ). They come back into the second step when they are brought together ($\mathrm{DZ}=-\mathrm{DEPZ}$ ). Once in contact, the GLISSIERE option activates and prevents the detachment following the normal direction ($\mathrm{OZ}$ ) during the third step, despite the imposed displacement ($\mathrm{DZ}=\mathrm{DEPZ}$ ), the tangential slide at the interface level remains possible ($\mathrm{DY}=\mathrm{DEPY}$ ).

## 2 reference solution

The solution to such a problem is of course obvious.

*Step 1:*

Both parts of the structure are detached: the lower part has zero displacement and the upper part has an overall movement equal to the displacement imposed on the top of the structure.

*Step 2:*

There is no horizontal displacement. Vertically, both parts of the structure are linked and behave as a single compression block. The displacement at the interface is equal to the half of the displacement at the top of the structure and the displacement jump is zero.

*Step 3:*

Horizontally, the lower part has zero displacement and the upper part has an overall movement equal to the displacement imposed on the top of the structure. Vertically, both parts of the structure are bound and behave as a single pull block. The displacement at the interface is equal to the half of the displacement at the top of the structure and the vertical displacement jump is zero.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

3D X-FEM Modelling is considered with contact consideration. The interface is introduced into the healthy mesh by the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The structure is discretized using elements HEXA8 finished. In the three directions of the selected reference system, $2\times 4\times 9$ elements is then used to achieve a total of 72 elements finished (see [])

The interface will be introduced in the middle of the fifth stage of elements following the $\mathrm{OZ}$ direction.

## 4 Summary of the results

The GLISSIERE option was activated as expected, after the contact was established in step 2, i.e. the absence of the $\mathrm{OZ}$ directional detachment in step 3, despite the imposition of a displacement in that direction, with a tensile structure. The $\mathrm{OZ}$ at the interface level is zero at the end of steps 2 and 3 and the upper part of the block slides in the $\mathrm{OY}$ direction with the imposed value.
