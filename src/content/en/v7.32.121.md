# v7.32.121 WTNP121 – Modelling a straight-line compressible liquid (single phase flow) saturated bar

**Summary:**

This test case has a dual objective:

- validate the finite volume diagrams developed for diphasic flow modelling.
- validate the Modelling Saturated hydraulic finite elements (modellings G and H)

The diphasic problem here will be degenerated into a single phase problem known to the analytical solution. This is the one-dimensional Modelling of a water saturated bar subjected to pressure shock.

## 1 Reference problem

The objective of this test case is to compare the solution obtained with the different finished volume diagrams to an analytical solution.

### 1.1 analytical solution

The single-phase instationary and one-dimensional problem can be written in a general form of the type:

$N\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}-{K}_{\mathit{int.}}\Delta P=0$

$P(t=0)={P}_{0}$

$P(t,x=0)=0$

$\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}(t,x=L)=0$

This problem allows an analytical solution obtained by developing in series of Fourier.

$P={\sum}_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{{\mathrm{4P}}_{0}}{(\mathrm{2k}+1)\pi }\exp(\frac{-{K}_{i}}{N}{\omega}_{k}^{2}t)\sin({\omega}_{k}x)$ with ${\omega}_{k}=(k+\frac{1}{2})\frac{\pi}{L}$

This series can be reduced to a finite $K$ of terms, depending on the moment calculated.

The number of terms is determined as follows:

${n}_{x}$ is the number of ${x}_{i}$ points where the solution is evaluated at a time $t$

Installation: ${a}_{k}^{i}=\frac{4}{(\mathrm{2k}+1)\pi }\exp(\frac{-{K}_{int}}{N}{\omega}_{k}^{2}t)\sin({\omega}_{k}{x}_{i})$

So the solution can write: $P({x}_{i})={\sum}_{k=0}^{K}{P}_{0}.{a}_{k}^{i}$

Choose $K$ as: $\frac{1}{{n}_{x}}\sqrt{{\sum}_{i=1}^{\mathit{nx}}{({a}_{k}^{i})}^{2}}<\epsilon$

In practice, we took $\epsilon ={10}^{-10}$ .

### 1.2 Simplification assumptions

The medium is considered to be completely saturated with water and zero gas pressure is imposed on all nodes. The biphasic system then resolves the following problem:

> $\frac{\partial (\varphi {\rho}_{l})}{\partial t}-d({K}_{i}\frac{{\rho}_{l}{k}_{\mathrm{rl}}}{{\mu}_{l}}\nabla {P}_{l})=0$

- Liquid is incompressible: ${\rho}_{l}=\mathit{cst}$
- The matrix is compressible and the porosity evolves proportionally to the liquid pressure: $\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial {P}_{l}}={E}_{m}$

The mass retention equation for the liquid is thus written:

${\rho}_{l}{E}_{m}\frac{\partial {P}_{l}}{\partial t}-d({K}_{i}\frac{{\rho}_{l}{k}_{rl}}{{\mu}_{l}}\nabla {P}_{l})=0$

### 1.3 Geometry

We consider a $\mathrm{1D}$ bar of $\mathrm{5m}$ long. Specifically the mesh domain will make $[\mathrm{0m},5m]\times [\mathrm{0m};0,05m]$ (in the case of triangle Modelling, it is important not to have triangles too "flat", so the choice of the height of the domain is not insignificant).

(0,0,05) (5;0,05)

(0,0) (0,5)

### 1.4 Mesh

This case was tested on two mesh sizes, one composed of 100 quadrangles (modellings A,B and G) and the other of 200 triangles (modellings C and D).

The modellings E, F and H are an extension $\mathrm{3D}$ (section bar $1\times 1$), the mesh consists of 100 hexahedrons.

### 1.6 Boundary conditions and initials

The limit conditions are as follows:

- Neumann conditions to the right of the domain: $\frac{\partial {P}_{l}}{\partial x}(t,x=5,y)=0$
- Dirichlet conditions on the left side of the domain: ${P}_{l}(t,x=0,y)=0$

The initial liquid pressure is ${P}_{l}(t=0,x,y)={10}^{4}\mathit{Pa}$ .

### 1.7 Simulation time and time step

The simulation time is $100s$ and the number of time step is 100.
