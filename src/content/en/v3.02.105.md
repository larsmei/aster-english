# v3.02.105 SSLP105 - Excavation of a circular tunnel in a linear elastic massif

**Summary:**

This test is an example of the implementation of a global methodology for the two-dimensional simulation of the digging and support of a circular gallery in an underground massif with *Code_Aster*.

To validate the approach on the basis of a simple analytical solution, we have to make restrictive assumptions on the geometry of the problem, the behaviour of the materials (linear elastic) and the initial stress field (isotrope). The reference solution is given by the so-called "convergence-containment" method, which is the classic method for this type of 2D Modelling. For more details on the methodologies used, we will refer to the [U2.04.06](doc:u2.04.06) documentation.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

It is a circular cross section tunnel, coated with a concrete ring, which is excavated in a massif of soil. Both materials are assumed to be linear elastic.

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.105/Object_1.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The materials are linear elastic.

#### 1.2.1 Soil

${E}_{s}=4\mathit{GPa}$

${\nu}_{s}=0,3$

#### 1.2.2 Concrete

${E}_{b}=20\mathit{GPa}$

${\nu}_{b}=0,2$

### 1.3 Initial conditions, boundary conditions and loads

The stresses in the massif are initially assumed to be $({\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{zz}={\sigma}_{0})$ isotropic. The method used to simulate excavation and the laying of the support is the "convergence-containment" method, as presented in [bib1] and [bib2], for example.

The basic principle is that the nodal reactions generated at the edge of the future gallery by the initial state of stress are reduced. This operation is referred to as "disposal". When the deconfination has reached the value corresponding to the construction conditions which one wishes to model, the installation of the concrete support on the edge of the gallery is simulated.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

#### 2.1.1 Behavior of the massif

$\lambda$ is the rate of de-confination, which represents the relative position of the tunnel section considered relative to the face. In the method of convergence - confinement, the excavated future terrain is replaced by an equivalent stress tensor, whose intensity is reduced via $\lambda$ to simulate the digging and distance from the face.

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.105/10006466000069F000003ACFEB3BA202A5EEAD9E.svg)

The solution of the problem is therefore similar to that of the infinitely thick tube charged by an internal pressure of $(1-\lambda ){\sigma}_{0}$ intensity and by an external pressure of ${\sigma}_{0}$ intensity (see [bib3] for the detailed calculations).

Radial, orthoradial stresses and radial displacement to the tunnel wall in elastic medium subject to $\lambda$ de-confination are as follows:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{R}=(1-\frac{\lambda \cdot {R}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}){\sigma}^{0}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta}=(1+\frac{\lambda \cdot {R}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}){\sigma}^{0}\\ {U}_{R}=\lambda \frac{{R}^{2}}{r}\cdot \frac{{\sigma}^{0}}{\mathrm{2G}}\end{array}$

$G$ is the shear module given by the following relationship: $G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$ .

#### 2.1.2 Support behaviour

The support will oppose the natural convergence movement of the tunnel and thus apply artificial confinement to the rock.

${K}_{s}$ the stiffness of the support, it is given by the following relationship if it is considered that the support is similar to a thin tube (${\nu}_{b}$ is the Poisson's ratio of the concrete):

${K}_{s}=\frac{{E}_{b}\cdot e}{(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})\cdot R}$

If ${k}_{s}=\frac{{K}_{s}}{2\cdot G}$ represents the relative rigidity of the concrete relative to the solid and ${\lambda}_{d}$ the rate of de-confination at the installation of the support, then the radial and orthoradial stresses and radial wall displacement are given by[bib1]:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{R}=\frac{{k}_{s}}{1+{k}_{s}}(1-{\lambda}_{d}){\sigma}_{0}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta}=\frac{{k}_{s}}{1+{k}_{s}}(1+{\lambda}_{d}){\sigma}_{0}\\ {U}_{R}=\frac{1+{\lambda}_{d}\cdot {k}_{s}}{1+{k}_{s}}\cdot \frac{{\sigma}_{0}}{\mathrm{2G}}\cdot R\end{array}$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The following quantities at the wall at points $A$ and $B$ in the figure in paragraph 1.1. shall be tested at the moment when the de-confination is total:

1. radial stress: ${\sigma}_{yy}$ in $A$ or ${\sigma}_{zz}$ in $B$;
2. orthoradial stress: ${\sigma}_{zz}$ in $A$ or ${\sigma}_{yy}$ in $B$;
3. radial displacement: ${u}_{y}$ in $A$ or ${u}_{z}$ in $B$ .

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

None. Analytical outcome is accurate.

| **Orders** | **Comments** |
| --- | --- |
