# r4.02.03 Elasto-acoustic beam

**Summary:**

One presents a straight elasto-acoustic coupling element which applies to a structural element of Timoshenko beam type. This element makes it possible to carry out, in vibro-acoustics, the modal analysis of a straight pipe containing a pressurised fluid (water, steam…). One may also carry out response analyses to fluid sources (mass flow rate, volume flow rate, pressure) by modal recombination. The boundary conditions applicable to the nodes of these elements are of Dirichlet type: displacement, pressure or potential may be imposed there.

## 2 Introduction

In order to be able to carry out dynamic response analyses of structures filled with fluid to fluids, 3D fluid-structure coupling elements have been developed in *Code_Aster*  ( *cf* [bib2]).

These volume elements have the advantage of allowing a fine description of the structure at particular locations, such as, for example, the junction between a main pipe and an instrumentation branch. On the other hand, their systematic use for the analysis of branched and complex networks would lead to prohibitive modelling costs (production of the mesh) and analysis costs.

For this reason, and in order to facilitate simplified studies of the dynamic behaviour of piping, a straight elasto-acoustic beam element has been developed, making it possible to carry out, at a lower cost in manpower and in analysis, analyses of the overall behaviour of the straight parts of piping at low frequency.

One finds below a presentation of the finite element for piping of elasto-acoustic beam type. The vibratory behaviour of piping networks is conditioned by the flow of the fluid which runs through them.

## 3 The elasto-acoustic beam model

### 3.1 Assumptions

One studies at low frequency the vibrations of an elastic pipe, linear, homogeneous and isotropic, coupled to a compressible fluid.

The effects due to the viscosity and to the flow of the fluid are neglected.

Piping consists of slender bodies. Indeed, their transverse dimensions are very much smaller than their length: $D\ll L$ , and the thicknesses are such that one may neglect the swelling and ovalisation modes of the pipe. One may use a beam model.

At low frequency the acoustic wavelengths associated with the problems studied are large compared with the transverse dimensions and small compared with the longitudinal dimension of the circuit: $\omega .L/c>1$ and $\omega .D/c\ll 1$ . The compressibility acts indeed principally on the longitudinal displacements. Transversely, one considers that the fluid moves as a non-deformable solid, that is to say that it acts as an added mass. The pressure in a cross-section of the pipe then being constant, one says that the acoustic wave is plane.

### 3.2 Functional of the coupled problem

One may write the variational formulation of the problem of fluid-filled piping from the equilibrium and constitutive equations of the fluid and of the pipe as well as from the boundary conditions. From the general functional of the three-dimensional coupled problem ([bib1], [bib2]), one may write the functional applied to the particular case of beams.

The variational formulation of the 3D problem amounts to minimising the functional:

$F(u,p,\Phi )=\frac{1}{2}\lbrace {\int}_{{\Omega}_{s}}\left[\sigma (u):\varepsilon (u)-{\rho}_{s}{\omega}^{2}{u}^{2}\right]\text{dV}$

$+{\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\left[\frac{{P}^{2}}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}(\frac{\mathrm{2P}\Phi }{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{(\text{grad}\Phi )}^{2})\right]\text{dV}\rbrace -{\rho}_{0}{w}^{2}{\int}_{S}\Phiu .n\text{dA}$

with:

$\Omegas$ , the domain of the structure

$\Omegaf$ , the domain of the fluid

$\Sigma$ , the fluid-structure interaction surface.

#### 3.2.1 Contribution of the piping

The beam model used is that of Timoshenko with shear-force strains and rotational inertia of the cross-section. It corresponds to the modelling POU_D_T, whose elementary computations it takes up. One does not take into account the ovalisation effects [bib3].

The terms associated with the piping in the variational formulation are then written:

${\int}_{L}\left[\sigma (u):\varepsilon (u)-{\rho}_{s}{\omega}^{2}{u}^{2}\right]{S}_{s}\text{ds}$

with: $L$ , the mean fibre of the piping and ${S}_{s}$ , the cross-section of the piping at the abscissa $s$ (cf. [Figure 3.2.1-a]).

**Figure 3.2.1-a: geometry of the piping**

#### 3.2.2 Contribution of the fluid

In this paragraph, one is interested only in the fluid part of the functional, that is to say, the coupling term set aside, in the term which is written in 3D:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\left[\frac{{P}^{2}}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{\rho}_{0}{\omega}^{2}(\frac{\mathrm{2P}\Phi }{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{(\text{grad}\Phi )}^{2})\right]\text{dV}$                     eq 3.2.2-1

One assumes that the pressure is decomposed into two terms:

$P(M(s),t)=p(s,t)+\tilde{p}(M(s),t)$

where $p$ is the value of the pressure averaged over a cross-section:

$p(s,t)=\frac{1}{{S}_{f}(s)}{\int}_{{S}_{f}(s)}P(M(s),t)\text{dM}$

and $\tilde{p}$ is a fluctuating-pressure term which corresponds to the contribution of the transverse modes.

According to the assumptions of paragraph [§1], $p$ satisfies the 1-D Helmholtz equation and $\tilde{p}$ the Laplace equation (incompressible). The integral [eq 3.2.2-1] is therefore decomposed into two terms.

##### 3.2.2.1 Term corresponding to the contribution of $\tilde{p}$

In the motions perpendicular to the axis of the pipe, one considers that the fluid intervenes only through its added mass [bib4]; the term linked to $\tilde{p}$ is therefore an inertia term:

${\int}_{L}{\rho}_{0}{\omega}^{2}{({u}_{t})}^{2}{S}_{f}\text{ds}$

${u}_{t}$ being the transverse components of the displacement vector of the structure and ${S}_{f}$ the cross-section of the fluid at the abscissa $s$ .

##### 3.2.2.2 Term corresponding to the contribution of $p$

${\int}_{L}\left[\frac{{P}^{2}}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-\mathrm{\rho 0}{\omega}^{2}(\frac{\mathrm{2P}\Phi }{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{(\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial s})}^{2})\right]{S}_{f}\text{ds}$

#### 3.2.3 Coupling term

##### 3.2.3.1 Current section

According to the references [bib4] and [bib5], one shows that the coupling term $C$ :

$C\text{=-}{\int}_{S}{\rho}_{0}\Phi \mathrm{u}.\mathrm{n}\text{dA}={\int}_{L}-\frac{{\rho}_{0}\Phi }{R}\mathrm{u}.j{S}_{f}\text{ds}+{\int}_{L}{\rho}_{0}\Phi \mathrm{u}.i\frac{{\text{dS}}_{f}}{\text{ds}}\text{ds}$

The equilibrium equations of the structure and the equation of propagation of the plane waves (Helmholtz) in the fluid are therefore coupled at the level of the bent parts and of the straight parts where there is a change of cross-section of the piping. In the case of a pipe of constant cross-section:

$R\to \infty ` and :math:$frac{{text{dS}}_{f}}{text{ds}}=0` hence $C=0$

There is therefore no coupling between the beam motions of the structure and the longitudinal displacements of the fluid in the straight parts of the circuit. In this case, the fluid is characterised only by its added mass linked to the transverse displacements.

##### 3.2.3.2 Pipe end

![Figure](/figures/r4.02.03/100002CE000016880000087A56E305DDC311AEAC.svg)

In the case of a pipe end, one denotes $\Sigmat =\Sigma +{S}_{f}$ , the total interaction surface between the fluid and the piping.

In the case of a closed straight piping of constant cross-section, the coupling term $C$ is then equal to:

$C\text{=-}{\int}_{S+{S}_{f}}{\rho}_{0}\Phiu .n\text{dA}\text{=-}{\int}_{{S}_{f}}{\rho}_{0}\Phiu .n\text{dA}$

This is the end effect.

This term is added to the coupling term of a current section. Thus, a free node which realises a condition of zero flow rate through the cross-section [bib6] realises an acoustic end condition. Indeed, an incident plane wave is totally reflected on the end: the acoustic pressure in the duct obeys the Helmholtz equation with zero normal pressure gradient (the fluid and solid displacements being zero).

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {x}^{2}}+{k}^{2}p=0\\ (\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}{)}_{{S}_{f}}=0\end{array}$

One seeks the solution in the form: $p=A\cos(\omegat -\text{kx})+B\cos(\omegat +\text{kx})$

that is to say in the form of a linear combination of an incident plane acoustic wave and of a reflected wave.

The condition of zero gradient on the end, verified for all time instants, imposes:

$A=B$

The reflected wave is therefore "equal" to the incident wave (reflection coefficient equal to unity).

#### 3.2.4 Functional of the coupled system in the case of piping

In the particular case which we treat, of an unbent piping of constant cross-section, the functional of the coupled problem is therefore written in the following form:

$\begin{array}{cc}F(u,p,\Phi )=& \frac{1}{2}\lbrace {\int}_{L}\sigma (u):\varepsilon (u){S}_{s}\text{ds}-{\omega}^{2}{\int}_{L}\left[{\rho}_{s}{S}_{s}{u}^{2}+{\rho}_{0}{S}_{f}{({u}_{t})}^{2}\right]\text{ds}\\ & +{\int}_{L}\left[\frac{{P}^{2}}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{\rho}_{0}{\omega}^{2}(\frac{\mathrm{2P}\Phi }{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}-{(\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \sigma })}^{2})\right]{S}_{f}\text{ds}\rbrace -{\omega}^{2}{\int}_{{S}_{f}}{\rho}_{0}\Phi u.n\text{dA}\end{array}$

### 3.3 Discretisation by finite elements

The sought solution $(u,p,\Phi )$ minimises the functional $F$ . The finite-element approximation of the complete problem then leads to the symmetric system:

$\left[\begin{array}{ccc}K& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{K}_{f}}{{\rho}_{0}.{c}^{2}}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}u\\ p\\ \Phi \end{array}\right]-{\omega}^{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}M+{M}_{f}& 0& {M}_{\Sigma}\\ 0& 0& \frac{{M}_{\text{fl}}}{{c}^{2}}\\ {M}_{\Sigma}^{T}& \frac{{M}_{\text{fl}}^{T}}{{c}^{2}}& {\rho}_{0}.H\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}u\\ p\\ F\end{array}\right]=0$

$K$ and $M$ being respectively the stiffness and mass matrices of the structure,

${K}_{f}$ , ${M}_{\text{fl}}$ , $H$ being the fluid matrices, respectively obtained from the quadratic forms:

${\int}_{L}{p}^{2}{S}_{f}\text{ds},{\int}_{L}p\Phi {S}_{f}\text{ds},{\int}_{L}{(\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial s})}^{2}{S}_{f}\text{ds}$

${M}_{f}$ being the fluid matrix obtained from the quadratic form: ${\int}_{L}{p}_{0}{S}_{f}{({u}_{t})}^{2}\text{ds}$

${M}_{\Sigma}$ being the coupling matrix obtained from the bilinear form: ${\int}_{{S}_{f}}{p}_{0}\Phiun \text{dA}$ .

By discretising $p$ and $\Phi$ linearly, one therefore has:

$p={p}_{1}\frac{L-x}{L}+{p}_{2}\frac{x}{L}\text{et}\Phi ={\Phi}_{1}\frac{L-x}{L}+{\Phi}_{2}\frac{x}{L}$ , $L$ being the length of the element considered.

In this case, the elementary stiffness matrix of the fluid is written:

${K}_{f}=\frac{{S}_{f}L}{3}\left\lbrace {p}_{1}{p}_{2}\right\rbrace \left[\begin{array}{cc}1& 1/2\\ 1/2& 1\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{p}_{1}\\ {p}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The elementary coupling matrix is written:

${M}_{\Sigma}={r}_{0}{S}_{f}\left\lbrace {\Phi}_{1}{\Phi}_{2}\right\rbrace \left[\begin{array}{cc}-1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}{u}_{1}\\ {u}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The various elementary fluid mass matrices are written:

${M}_{\Sigma}={r}_{0}{S}_{f}\left\lbrace {\Phi}_{1}{\Phi}_{2}\right\rbrace \left[\begin{array}{cc}-1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}{p}_{1}\\ {p}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

${M}_{\Sigma}=-\frac{{S}_{f}}{L}\left\lbrace {\Phi}_{1}{\Phi}_{2}\right\rbrace \left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\Phi}_{1}\\ {\Phi}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

### 3.4 Implementation in Code_Aster

On the principles which we have just described, a vibro-acoustic beam element, of Timoshenko type for the piping part, straight and of constant or variable cross-section (in this case, only the circular cross-sections are allowed), has been implemented in *Code_Aster* . It belongs to the modelling 'FLUI_STRU' of the phenomenon 'MECANIQUE'.

This element possesses 8 degrees of freedom per node: the displacements and rotations of the piping, the pressure and the displacement potential of the fluid (cf. [Figure 3.4-a]). The formulation is written for **local** displacements  in the local frame of the element constituted of the neutral fibre (axis $X$ ) and of the principal axes of inertia (axis $Y$ , axis $Z$ ) of the cross-section. The two scalars $p$ and $\Phi$ (pressure and fluid-displacement potential) are invariant under a change of frame.

On each node of this element, one may impose boundary conditions of Dirichlet type in pressure, fluid-displacement potential and displacements (translation or rotation).

![Figure](/figures/r4.02.03/100007440000211300000FD0E70D3D1FB8764131.svg)

**Figure 3.4-a: fluid-filled beam element**

To date, this element makes it possible only to compute the eigenmodes of a straight fluid-filled pipe and to carry out a harmonic response analysis. The effects of curvature or of an abrupt widening of the cross-section are not taken into account for the moment, but these fluid-structure effects, when one is dealing with fluids of low density such as the steam of an inlet pipe, do not seem to have a determining importance for the computation of the first modes: the correct mechanical representation of the elbow (flexibility coefficient) seems sufficient to compute these frequencies [bib7].

In modal analysis, one may cite the case of a straight fluid-filled pipe with a free end:

![Figure](/figures/r4.02.03/100004B80000184A00000933589F0760FDD7FBEB.svg)

**Figure 3.4-b: fluid-filled beam, clamped–free**

the eigenfrequency of the tension-compression mode of this coupled fluid/structure system is given by the relation:

$\text{tg}(\frac{\omegal }{c})=\sqrt{\frac{{S}_{s}}{{S}_{f}}\frac{E}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}}$

One denotes by:

$E$ : Young's modulus of the solid material

${S}_{S}$ : cross-section of the solid

${S}_{f}$ : cross-section of the fluid

One assumes here that the speed of sound in the fluid is equal to the speed of sound in the solid ${c}_{S}=\sqrt{\frac{E}{{\rho}_{S}}}$ [bib7].

The transient response analysis for this type of finite element $(u,p,\phi )$ is not yet available in *Code_Aster* .
