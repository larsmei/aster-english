# v6.03.167 SSNP167 - Inclusion of two non-uniform pressurized crowns

**Summary:**

This test is used to evaluate Aster's performance in the treatment of contact between two structures with compatibility and geometric incompatibility between master and slave surfaces in the context of the hypothesis of small disturbances.

The interface between the two crowns is the contact surface. Their rigidity, represented by their Young's modulus, plays an important role in assessing the value of deformations and fluctuations in contact pressure. It is also sought to determine the effects of using a higher mesh, with the use of linear or quadratic meshes.

A analytical solution was developed for this problem to validate the calculated numerical results. Validation of this test is based on the values of contact pressure and displacement.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure consists of two concentric circular crowns. The ${R}_{2}$ internal radius of the outer crown is equal to the outer radius of the inner crown (Figure 1.1-1).

The characteristic dimensions of the structure are:

${R}_{1}=1,0m;{R}_{2}=0,6m;{R}_{3}=0,2m$

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.167/Object_7.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Structure Geometry**

### 1.2 Material properties

The Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio of the outer crown material are given by ${E}_{1}$ and ${\nu}_{1}$ (respectively ${E}_{2}$ and ${\nu}_{2}$ for the inner crown).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The following model will be used to apply an un even pressure on the $r={R}_{1}$ edge of the outer crown: $p={\alpha}_{0}+{\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ , $\theta$ being the polar angle describing the position of the point $\theta =\arctan(\frac{Y}{X})$ . For analytical calculation, the problem will be solved in the first part for a $p={\alpha}_{0}$ pressure , then the problem will be solved for a pressure of the $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ form . Then the superposition principle will be applied.

#### 1.3.1 Boundary conditions in the case of uniform pressure

The edges of the crowns are subjected to displacements ($r={R}_{1}$ and $r={R}_{3}$ ) equivalent to applying a pressure $p={\alpha}_{0}$ to the edge of the outer crown ($r={R}_{1}$ ).

$\begin{array}{c}{\xi}_{X}(r)=f(r)\cos(\arctan(\frac{Y}{X}))\\ {\xi}_{Y}(r)=f(r)\sin(\arctan(\frac{Y}{X}))\end{array}$

$f(r)$ function of radial displacement is given according to material properties and $p$ pressure. It is of the ${A}_{i}r+\frac{{B}_{i}}{r};i=1,2$ shape, in each domain. In the case of flat deformations (MODELISATION = 'D_PLAN') one has:

$\begin{array}{c}f({R}_{1})={A}_{1}{R}_{1}+\frac{{B}_{1}}{{R}_{1}}\\ f({R}_{3})={A}_{2}{R}_{3}+\frac{{B}_{2}}{{R}_{3}}\end{array}$                                     éq 1.1

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{(1+{\nu}_{1})(1-2{\nu}_{1})}{{E}_{1}}\frac{-p{R}_{1}^{2}+\lambda {R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}};{B}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}(-p+\lambda )\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{-(1+{\nu}_{2})(1-2{\nu}_{2})}{{E}_{2}}\lambda \frac{{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}};{B}_{2}=\frac{-1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}\lambda \frac{{R}_{2}^{2}{R}_{3}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}}\end{array}$                     éq 1.2

where $\lambda$ is the contact pressure whose analytical expression is:

$\lambda =2p(1-{\nu}_{1})\frac{\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}}{\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}+{R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{1})}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}+\frac{{E}_{1}}{{E}_{2}}\frac{(1+{\nu}_{2})}{(1+{\nu}_{1})}\frac{{R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{2})+{R}_{3}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}}}$                         éq. 1.3

#### 1.3.2 Boundary conditions in the case of variable pressure

The edges of the crowns are subjected to displacements ($r={R}_{1}$ and $r={R}_{3}$ ) equivalent to applying a pressure $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ to the edge of the outer crown ($r={R}_{1}$ ).

$\begin{array}{c}{\xi}_{X}(r,\theta )={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\cos(\theta )-{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\sin(\theta )\\ {\xi}_{Y}(r,\theta )={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\sin(\theta )+{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\cos(\theta )\end{array}$

${u}_{r}(r,\theta )$ function of radial displacement, and ${u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )$ tangential displacement function are given according to material properties, $p$ pressure, geometric characteristics, and square of the ratios between ${f}_{1}={(\frac{{R}_{2}}{{R}_{1}})}^{2};{f}_{2}={(\frac{{R}_{3}}{{R}_{2}})}^{2}$ rays. In the case of flat deformations (MODELISATION = 'D_PLAN') one has:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}({R}_{1,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}{R}_{1}+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{R}_{1}^{3}}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})-{\nu}_{1}(4{B}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}({R}_{1,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{1}{R}_{1}+{\mathrm{6B}}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{R}_{1}^{3}}-2\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})-{\nu}_{1}({\mathrm{4B}}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}-4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})]\sin(2\theta )\\ {u}_{r}({R}_{3,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{2}{R}_{3}+2\frac{{C}_{2}}{{R}_{3}^{3}}+4\frac{{D}_{2}}{{R}_{3}})-{\nu}_{2}(4{B}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3}+4\frac{{D}_{2}}{{R}_{3}})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}({R}_{3,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{2}{R}_{3}+{\mathrm{6B}}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3}+2\frac{{C}_{2}}{{R}_{3}^{3}}-2\frac{{D}_{2}}{{R}_{3}})-{\nu}_{2}({\mathrm{4B}}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3}-4\frac{{D}_{2}}{{R}_{3}})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$             éq 1.4

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+{f}_{1}^{2}+2{f}_{1})}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{B}_{1}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{2}^{2}}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(3{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1})-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+3{f}_{1}^{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{1}={R}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}+3)-\lambda ({\mathrm{3f}}_{1}+1)}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{D}_{1}=-{R}_{2}^{2}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+2)-\lambda (2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{\lambda (2{f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};{B}_{2}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{3}^{2}}\frac{\lambda (3{f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{2}={R}_{3}^{4}\frac{\lambda ({f}_{2}+3)}{6{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};{D}_{2}=-{R}_{3}^{2}\frac{\lambda ({f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2}+2)}{2{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}}\end{array}$         éq 1.5

where $\lambda$ is the contact pressure whose analytical expression is:

$\lambda =\frac{{\mathit{coef}}_{1}}{{\mathit{coef}}_{2}+{\mathit{coef}}_{3}}{\alpha}_{1}$                                 éq 1.6

such that:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{coef}}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)-{\nu}_{1}(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-3{f}_{1}^{3}-15{f}_{1}^{2}-9{f}_{1}-5)-{\nu}_{1}(-2{f}_{1}^{3}-18{f}_{1}^{2}-6{f}_{1}-6)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{3}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{6{E}_{2}{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}}[(-5{f}_{2}^{3}-9{f}_{2}^{2}-15{f}_{2}-3)-{\nu}_{2}(-6{f}_{2}^{3}-6{f}_{2}^{2}-18{f}_{2}-2)]\end{array}$         éq 1.7

## 2 reference solution

We develop a analytical solution for the problem presented above. This solution will be developed with the hypothesis of small deformations considering that the crown materials are isotropic, governed by a linear elastic law without temperature variation.

The solution on the move of the problem has the following generic form:

$u={u}_{r}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{r}}+{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{\theta}}+{u}_{z}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{z}}$

The problem will be solved in the context of the flat deformation hypothesis. Our loading in the form $p={\alpha}_{0}+{\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ will decouple the problem resolution to a part where the pressure is uniform $p={\alpha}_{0}$, and a part where the pressure is variable $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$.

### 2.1 Uniform pressure

Using the symmetries of the problem and the hypothesis of invariance according to z of the plane deformations, the solution of the problem takes the following form:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}={u}_{r}(r)\\ {u}_{\theta}=0\\ {u}_{z}=0\end{array}$                                         éq 2.1

Using the equation of Lamé-Navier:

$\begin{array}{c}(\lambda +\mu )\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\mu \Delta \underline{u}+\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                             éq 2.2

where $\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}$ are the zero density forces here, and the Laplacian formula:

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta \underline{u}=\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\underline{\mathit{rot}}\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})\end{array}$                                 éq 2.3

Eq 2.2 can be written as:

$\begin{array}{c}(\lambda +2\mu )\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\mu \underline{\mathit{rot}}\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})+\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                     éq 2.4

or still using $\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})=\vec{0}\text{et}\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\vec{0}\text{et}\underline{u}={u}_{r}(r).\underline{{e}_{r}}$:

$\begin{array}{c}\nabla .(\underline{u})=\frac{d}{\mathit{dr}}{u}_{r}(r)+\frac{1}{r}{u}_{r}(r)\\ \underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .\underline{u})=\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}(r{u}_{r}(r))].{\underline{e}}_{r}\\ \text{soit encore}(\lambda +2\mu )\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}(r{u}_{r}(r))]=0\end{array}$                         éq 2.5

By integrating the equation, the following form of the field of displacement is obtained for the crowns (outside and inside):

${u}_{r}={C}_{i}r+\frac{{D}_{i}}{r}\text{}{u}_{\theta}=0\text{}{u}_{z}=0$                                     éq 2.6

To determine ${C}_{i}$ and ${D}_{i}$ , we still have to impose the limit conditions in pressure and displacement. To do this, we must first calculate the deformations and then the stresses associated with the displacement field.

Deformations are the symmetrical part of the gradient of displacements.

$\begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\mathit{rr}}={C}_{i}-\frac{{D}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {ϵ}_{\theta \theta }={C}_{i}+\frac{{D}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {ϵ}_{zz}={ϵ}_{r\theta }={ϵ}_{\mathit{rz}}={ϵ}_{\theta z}=0\end{array}$                                     éq 2.7

Applying the Hooke law:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}=\lambda \mathit{tr}(\underline{\underline{ϵ}})\underline{\underline{1}}+2\mu \underline{\underline{ϵ}}$                                         éq 2.8

the following general form is obtained for the constraints:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{E}{1+\nu }(\frac{{C}_{i}}{1-2\nu }-\frac{{D}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}})\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{E}{1+\nu }(\frac{{C}_{i}}{1-2\nu }+\frac{{D}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}})\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=\frac{2\nu {\mathit{EC}}_{i}}{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}={\sigma}_{\theta z}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                                 éq 2.9

By posing:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{i}=\frac{{E}_{i}}{(1+{\nu}_{i})(1-2{\nu}_{i})}{C}_{i}\text{}{B}_{i}=\frac{{E}_{i}}{1+{\nu}_{i}}{D}_{i}\end{array}$                     éq 2.10

non-zero constraints become:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}={A}_{i}-\frac{{B}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }={A}_{i}+\frac{{B}_{i}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=2\nu {A}_{i}\end{array}$                                         éq 2.11

All we have to do is calculate the ${A}_{i}$ and ${B}_{i}$ values for each crown. ${\lambda}_{n}$ is the contact pressure between the two crowns, such as:

$\begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{1rr}}({R}_{2}).(-{\underline{e}}_{r})={\lambda}_{n}{\underline{e}}_{r}\\ {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{2rr}}({R}_{2}).{\underline{e}}_{r}=-{\lambda}_{n}{\underline{e}}_{r}\end{array}$                                     éq 2.12

with boundary conditions:

$\begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{1rr}}({R}_{1}).{\underline{e}}_{r}=-\mathit{p.}{\underline{e}}_{r}\\ {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{2rr}}({R}_{3}).(-{\underline{e}}_{r})=\underline{0}\end{array}$                                      éq 2.13

The condition of continuity on the interface movement between the two crowns in contact gives further:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r;1}(\mathit{R2})={u}_{r;2}(\mathit{R2})\end{array}$                                     éq 2.14

So we have 5 equations for the 5 unknown $\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1},{B}_{1},{A}_{2},{B}_{2,}{\lambda}_{n}\end{array}$ .

The system of the first 4 equations allows us to obtain:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{-p{R}_{1}^{2}+{\lambda}_{n}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}};{B}_{1}=(-p+{\lambda}_{n})\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}\\ {A}_{2}=-{\lambda}_{n}\frac{{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}};{B}_{2}=-{\lambda}_{n}\frac{{R}_{2}^{2}{R}_{3}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}}\end{array}$                              éq 2.15

and the continuity equation on the displacement finally allows to have the contact pressure:

${\lambda}_{n}=\frac{2p{R}_{1}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{1})}{{R}_{1}^{2}+{R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{1})+\frac{{E}_{1}}{{E}_{2}}\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{1+{\nu}_{1}}\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{2}^{2}-{R}_{3}^{2}}({R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{2})+{R}_{3}^{2})}$                 éq 2.16

### 2.2 Variable pressure

Using the z-invariance hypothesis of flat deformations, the solution of the problem takes the following form:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}={u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\\ {u}_{z}=0\end{array}$                                      éq 2.17

Throughout the rest, the parameters specific to each solid will be noted by an i index, with i=1.2.

In the absence of volume forces, a form of the Airy function proposed by Michell [1], in polar coordinates, will be used:

$\begin{array}{c}\chi (r,\theta )={A}_{01}{r}^{2}+{A}_{02}{r}^{2}\log(r)+{A}_{03}\log(r)+{A}_{04}\theta \\ +({A}_{11}{r}^{3}+{A}_{12}\mathit{rlog}(r)+{A}_{13}{r}^{-1})\cos(\theta )+{A}_{14}r\theta \sin(\theta )\\ +({B}_{11}{r}^{3}+{B}_{12}r\log(r)+{B}_{13}{r}^{-1})\sin(\theta )+{B}_{14}r\theta \cos(\theta )\\ +\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}({A}_{\mathit{n1}}{r}^{n+2}+{A}_{\mathit{n2}}{r}^{-n+2}+{A}_{\mathit{n3}}{r}^{n}+{A}_{\mathit{n4}}{r}^{-n})\cos(n\theta )\\ +\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}({B}_{\mathit{n1}}{r}^{n+2}+{B}_{\mathit{n2}}{r}^{-n+2}+{B}_{\mathit{n3}}{r}^{n}+{B}_{\mathit{n4}}{r}^{-n})\sin(n\theta )\end{array}$                     éq 2.18

The terms of the non-zero Cauchy stress tensor are expressed in terms of the function of Airy as follows:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial r}+\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\chi }{\partial {\theta}^{2}}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{\partial}^{2}\chi }{\partial {r}^{2}}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }=\frac{-\partial }{\partial r}(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial \theta })\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })\end{array}$                                     éq 2.19

Our $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ pressure varies in $\cos(2\theta )$, we will only take the portion that varies in $\cos(2\theta )$ in the Airy function. The Airy function will then be written:

${\chi }_{i}(r,\theta )=({A}_{i}{r}^{2}+{B}_{i}{r}^{4}+\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{2}}+{D}_{i})\cos(2\theta )$                             éq 2.20

From there, non-zero constraints can be expressed in the polar marker:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{i}=(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{i}-6\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}}-4\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }^{i}=({\mathrm{2A}}_{i}+{\mathrm{12B}}_{i}{r}^{2}+6\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{i}=2({A}_{i}+{\mathrm{3B}}_{i}{r}^{2}-3\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}}-\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})\sin(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{zz}^{i}=\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })\end{array}$                              éq 2.21

and the terms of the deformation tensor using the Hooke law:

$\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{E}((1+\nu )\underline{\underline{\sigma}}-\nu \mathit{tr}(\underline{\underline{\sigma}})\underline{\underline{I}})$                                 éq 2.22

The non-zero terms of the strain tensor are then expressed in relation to the constants of the problem:

$\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}^{i}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{i}}{{E}_{i}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{i}-6\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}}-4\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})-{\nu}_{i}({\mathrm{12B}}_{i}{r}^{2}-4\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }^{i}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{i}}{{E}_{i}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{i}+{\mathrm{12B}}_{i}{r}^{2}+6\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}})-{\nu}_{i}({\mathrm{12B}}_{i}{r}^{2}-4\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {\epsilon}_{r\theta }^{i}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{i}}{{E}_{i}}[2({A}_{i}+{\mathrm{3B}}_{i}{r}^{2}-3\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{4}}-\frac{{D}_{i}}{{r}^{2}})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$                 éq 2.23

These fields will be used to express the movements in the polar marker.

On a:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}={\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }=r{\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }-{u}_{r}\\ \frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \theta }-\frac{{u}_{\theta}}{r})={\epsilon}_{r\theta }\end{array}$                                 éq 2.24

By integrating these relationships, one can express the movements:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}^{i}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{i}}{{E}_{i}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{i}r+2\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{3}}+4\frac{{D}_{i}}{r})-{\nu}_{i}({\mathrm{4B}}_{i}{r}^{3}+4\frac{{D}_{i}}{r})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}^{i}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{i}}{{E}_{i}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{i}r+{\mathrm{6B}}_{i}{r}^{3}+2\frac{{C}_{i}}{{r}^{3}}-2\frac{{D}_{i}}{r})-{\nu}_{i}({\mathrm{4B}}_{i}{r}^{3}-4\frac{{D}_{i}}{r})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$                 éq 2.25

Now that we have expressed all our fields according to ${A}_{i},{B}_{i},{C}_{i},{D}_{i}(i=1,2)$ constants, we have to calculate them according to geometrical characteristics and loading.

The contact pressure between the two crowns is noted.

The boundary conditions are:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{1}({R}_{1})=-\alpha \cos(2\theta ):\text{pression externe appliquée}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{1}({R}_{1})=0:\text{pression tangentielle nulle sur le bord extérieur de la couronne 1}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}({R}_{2})=\lambda :\text{pression de contact appliquée par la couronne 1 sur la couronne 2}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{1}({R}_{2})=\lambda :\text{pression de contact appliquée par la couronne 2 sur la couronne 1}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{1}({R}_{2})=0:\text{pas de frottement entre les deux couronnes}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{2}({R}_{2})=0:\text{pas de frottement entre les deux couronnes}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}({R}_{3})=0:\text{bord extérieur de la couronne 2 libre}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{2}({R}_{3})=0:\text{bord extérieur de la couronne 2 libre}\end{array}$

So we have 8 equations for the 8 unknowns: ${A}_{1,}{B}_{1,}{C}_{1,}{D}_{1,}{A}_{2,}{B}_{2,}{C}_{2,}{D}_{2}$ .

By posing:

${f}_{1}={(\frac{{R}_{2}}{{R}_{1}})}^{2}\text{et}{f}_{2}={(\frac{{R}_{3}}{{R}_{2}})}^{2}$

The system of 8 equations allows us to have:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+{f}_{1}^{2}+2{f}_{1})}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{B}_{1}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{2}^{2}}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(3{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1})-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+3{f}_{1}^{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{1}={R}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}+3)-\lambda ({\mathrm{3f}}_{1}+1)}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{D}_{1}=-{R}_{2}^{2}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+2)-\lambda (2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{\lambda (2{f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};{B}_{2}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{3}^{2}}\frac{\lambda (3{f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{2}={R}_{3}^{4}\frac{\lambda ({f}_{2}+3)}{6{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}};{D}_{2}=-{R}_{3}^{2}\frac{\lambda ({f}_{2}^{2}+{f}_{2}+2)}{2{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}}\end{array}$             éq 2.26

The contact pressure can be expressed analytically

Using the continuity of radial displacement at the contact interface:

${u}_{r}^{1}({R}_{2})={u}_{r}^{2}({R}_{2})$                                         éq 2.27

The contact pressure can be expressed analytically:

$\lambda =\frac{{\mathit{coef}}_{1}}{{\mathit{coef}}_{2}+{\mathit{coef}}_{3}}{\alpha}_{1}$                                     éq 2.28

such that:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{coef}}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)-{\nu}_{1}(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-3{f}_{1}^{3}-15{f}_{1}^{2}-9{f}_{1}-5)-{\nu}_{1}(-2{f}_{1}^{3}-18{f}_{1}^{2}-6{f}_{1}-6)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{3}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{6{E}_{2}{(1-{f}_{2})}^{3}}[(-5{f}_{2}^{3}-9{f}_{2}^{2}-15{f}_{2}-3)-{\nu}_{2}(-6{f}_{2}^{3}-6{f}_{2}^{2}-18{f}_{2}-2)]\end{array}$         éq 2.29

### 2.3 Test values

The contact pressure at the interface between the two crowns, at the slave surface, and the displacements according to X and Y: ${u}_{x},{u}_{y}$ (and according to Z also for 3D models) are tested, in flat deformations.

The value of the pressure applied to the outer edge of the crown in $r={R}_{1}$ is expressed in the form: $p(\theta )={10}^{7}+{10}^{5}\cos(2\theta )(\mathit{Pa})$ , with $\theta =\arctan(\frac{Y}{X})$ .

The values of the points on the $r={R}_{2}$ radius on the side of the crown 2 at different polar angles will be tested: $\theta \in \lbrace 0,\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{3\pi }{4},\pi ,\frac{5\pi }{4},\frac{3\pi }{2},\frac{7\pi }{4}\rbrace$ for the A,B,C,D models.

For models E,G and H, a min-max test will be performed on three groups of nodes. Each node group contains three nodes with equal X and Y coordinates, which allows testing of the invariance of the result obtained following Z. The reference value used in the test is the minimum analytical value (respectively maximum) of the component tested for each group of node. For model F, only three nodes with equal X and Y coordinates were tested at the centre of the structure, in order to avoid the edge effects due to the non-compatible meshes of the master and slave contact surfaces.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a Modelling of flat deformations (D_PLAN). The master and slave surfaces are in conformity.

Young ${E}_{1}={E}_{2}$ modules and Poisson ${\nu}_{1}={\nu}_{2}$ coefficients are $1.0E9\mathit{Pa}$ and $0.2$ respectively. The pressure applied to the edge of the outer crown is $1.0E7+\mathrm{10E5.cos}(2\theta )(\mathit{Pa})$ , $\theta$ being the polar angle.

The outer crown defines the master surface.

The mesh (Figure 3.2-1) consists of:

- 480 mesh cells type SEG2;

## 11 Summary of the results

This test case allows the continuous contact formulation to be validated in the presence of curved contact surfaces with linear and quadratic grids, in 2D and 3D. It was possible to establish convergence orders for the continuous contact method and to verify a convergence in energy of 1 and in displacement of 2 for linear grids, 2 for energy and 3 for quadratic grids, for sufficiently rich integration schemes.
