# u4.44.01 Operators AFFE_CHAR_MECA, AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C and AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F

## 1 Purpose

Assign loadings and boundary conditions on a mechanical model.

- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA, the assigned values do not depend on any parameter and are defined
by real values.
- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C, the assigned values do not depend on any parameter and are defined
by complex values.
- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F, the assigned values are a function of one or more parameters in
the set {INST, X, Y, Z, XF, YF, ZF}.

## 3 General points

### 3.1 Principles

There are three main categories of operand:

- The operands applying kinematic conditions or Dirichlet loadings, that is
relations between the degrees of freedom. In AFFE_CHAR_MECA, these conditions are applied
by dualisation (double Lagrange multiplier method, see [R3.03.01](doc:r3.03.01));
- The operands applying loadings of “force” type or Neumann loadings, applied
in weak form, which implies the use of a numerical integration scheme. Some
loadings require the presence of boundary elements in the model;
- The operands applying special loadings, including mixed Dirichlet/Neumann loadings.

Most operands are built on the same principle:

- Specification of the place of application of the boundary conditions by the standard keywords
GROUP_NO, GROUP_MA and sometimes SANS_GROUP_NO and SANS_GROUP_MA.
- Specification of the assigned components, which fall into three groups:

  - Standard components of the quantity considered. This is the quantity DEPL_R
(or DEPL_C or DEPL_F), representing the degrees of freedom of the mechanical problem
  - Combined components DNOR and DTAN, which build a combination of the components
of the quantity DEPL_R on the basis of considerations relating to the tangents and the normal;
  - Components in forces, moments or pressure using either the quantity FORC_R
(or FORC_C or FORC_F), or the quantity PRES_R (or PRES_C or PRES_F);
- The assigned components must be of the correct type according to the operator used:

  - Of real type for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA;
  - Of complex type for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C;
  - Of function type (created in particular by one of the operators DEFI_FONCTION,
DEFI_NAPPE or DEFI_CONSTANTE) for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F. This is true with one
exception: the argument of COEF_MULT for the factor keyword LIAISON_DDL in
AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F is necessarily of real type.

For the loadings LIAISON_DDL, LIAISON_MAIL, LIAISON_GROUP and LIAISON_UNIF, the list of
applicable components is restricted to certain degrees of freedom.

**Note**

Use of the keyword GROUP_MA does not presuppose that the loadings will be applied on
the elements; it is also there to make life easier for users. For example, for
Dirichlet loadings, GROUP_MA is used but one recovers the nodes that belong
to the elements thus designated

### 3.2 Assumptions and limitations

In addition to the definition of the assumptions and limitations specific to each loading, there are
general assumptions which are recalled here.

#### 3.2.1 Linearity of the kinematic relations

A kinematic relation makes it possible to write an equation of the type:

With ${U}_{i}$ the list of the $r$ degrees of freedom, ${\alpha}_{i}$ the
coefficients and $\beta$ the right-hand side.

The kinematic relations are **linear** relations, that is:

- They cannot depend *a priori* on the strain or on the motion of the structure:
they remain valid only under the assumption of small perturbations, unless otherwise
stated (see LIAISON_SOLIDE);
- The coefficients ${\alpha}_{i}$ of the linear relation cannot be
functions of time, because the matrix $B$ of the Dirichlet conditions is constant throughout
the transient. On the other hand, these coefficients may be functions of the **initial**
geometry;
- The right-hand side $\beta$ may be a function of time or of the **initial** geometry;

#### 3.2.2 Neumann loadings

It is entirely possible for some Neumann loadings to be non-linear and, in
particular, to depend on the strain of the structure. Such loadings are commonly
called **follower** loadings. Nevertheless, in this case, the problem becoming non-linear, it
is necessary to use a suitable analysis operator such as STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE and to
state that these loadings are indeed considered as follower loadings
(see [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)).

Most Neumann loadings (except FORCE_NODALE) are applied in weak form,
that is one uses a numerical quadrature formula. Moreover, one cannot apply
simultaneously a Neumann loading and a Dirichlet loading on the same node and in the
same direction. As a result, there may be a difference between the theoretical solution and the
finite-element solution.

For example, on an insufficiently meshed structure, it is possible to observe a discrepancy between
the sum of the nodal forces corresponding to the gravity loading and the value of the true weight,
the discrepancy corresponding *roughly* to the number of clamped nodes of the structure.

A refinement of the mesh makes it possible to minimise this difference. One can also arrange
that the finite elements, on which kinematic conditions are imposed, are of a size
small enough for their weight to be negligible compared with that of the whole structure.

Another solution is to duplicate the nodes on which the kinematic condition is imposed and
to write, for example, an LIAISON_DDL between the two nodes or to use discrete elements.

### 3.5 Choice of units

For Neumann loadings, the forces are to be supplied:

- per mesh unit for line forces,
- per mesh unit squared for surface forces,
- and per mesh unit cubed for volume forces,

consistently with the definition of the material properties (Young’s modulus for example).
In the axisymmetric case, the forces to be supplied are reduced to a sector of $1$ radian
(divide the real loading by $2\pi$ ).

### 3.6 Case of large transformations

#### 3.6.1 The problem

When one applies a Dirichlet loading on a structure in large transformations
(large displacements, large rotations), one must take care that this loading remains applicable
when the assumption of small perturbations is no longer satisfied.

This is naturally the case for the following loadings:

- DDL_IMPO but if one uses AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F, one must do so with functions that
represent the **true** imposed displacement (curvilinear in general). If one does not, the
interpolation in time takes one through “false” intermediate states;
- LIAISON_MAIL + TYPE_RACCORD=’MASSIF’;
- LIAISON_UNIF;
- LIAISON_OBLIQUE;
- LIAISON_DDL;
- LIAISON_CHAM_NO;
- CHAMNO_IMPO;
- LIAISON_SOLIDE provided that one applies the assumption of a follower loading.

On the other hand, for the following loadings, the assumption of large transformations is not
applicable and will produce wrong results:

- FACE_IMPO with DNOR=f(t): the “normal” changes under large rotations;
- LIAISON_MAIL + TYPE_RACCORD=’COQUE_MASSIF’ and LIAISON_MAIL + 'MASSIF_COQUE';
- LIAISON_ELEM;
- LIAISON_RBE3.

#### 3.6.2 Follower loadings

For the non-linear operators (STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE), some loadings may
be “follower” loadings, that is their application depends on the displacement and therefore changes at each
Newton iteration. It is then necessary for the user to state this by the operand
TYPE_CHARGE in the factor keyword EXCIT of these commands
(see [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)). Stating that the loading is a follower loading
sometimes adds a contribution to the stiffness matrix
(see for example [R3.03.04](doc:r3.03.04)) and may make it non-symmetric.

For the loading EVOL_CHAR, it is not necessary to state that the loadings are
follower loadings; they are so automatically by default. Stating it simply activates the
additional matrix contribution and therefore acts on the rate of convergence (and not on the
accuracy of the result).

The loading EFFE_FOND in follower mode is an approximation of reality. It is activated because it
depends simultaneously on a loading of type PRES_REP. On the other hand, if, in the zone where
the loading EFFE_FOND is applied, the motions are not negligible (variation of the cross-section such
as ovalisation or a non-negligible rotation which modifies the normal), an error is made.

### 3.7 Designation of the topological entities for assignment of the loadings

In general, when the entities on which values must be assigned are defined:

- On a single node: one uses the operand GROUP_NO which must obviously contain only one node;
- On a list of nodes: one may use the operand GROUP_NO but also the operand GROUP_MA or
TOUT=’OUI’ to assign over the whole mesh;
- On a single mesh cell: one uses the operand GROUP_MA which must obviously contain only one mesh cell;
- On a list of mesh cells: one may use the operand GROUP_MA or TOUT=’OUI’ to assign over the whole mesh;

Some keywords need to define several topological entities (groups of nodes
facing each other for example); in this case, the names may vary slightly
(GROUP_NO_2, GROUP_MA_ESCL, etc.). It is possible in most keywords to exclude
nodes or mesh cells by means of keywords of type SANS_* . This feature avoids
redefining groups in the mesh or in the command [DEFI_GROUP](doc:u4.22.01)s.

### 3.8 Overloading rule and persistence rule

To define the assignment domain as simply as possible, one uses the overloading
rule defined in the document [U1.03.00](doc:u1.03.00), the principles of which are recalled:

- Assignments are made by superposing the effects of the different loadings;
- In the event of conflict, the last loading prevails over the previous ones;

If, for example, the user writes:

```text
FORCE_FACE=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’,FX= 12 ),
PRES_REP=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’,PRES= 13 )
```


And if the normal for ${G1}$ is oriented along $X$ , then everything will happen as if one
had written:

```text
FORCE_FACE=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’, FX= 25)
```


The preceding overloading rule must be completed by another rule in order to specify what
happens when several quantities can be assigned for each occurrence of a loading. Take for
example:

```text
FORCE_INTERNE=(
    _F(TOUT = ’OUI’, FX = 1. ),
    _F(GROUP_MA = ’GM1’, FY = 2. ),
)
```


The overloading rule tells us that the second occurrence of FORCE_INTERNE overloads the first.
But what is the value of FX on a mesh cell belonging to GM1? Has it been erased by the second occurrence?
If only the overloading rule is applied, FX is not defined on GM1.

One therefore uses a second rule, called the persistence rule, which specifies that when applying the
overloading rule to occurrences, one keeps the components that are not overloaded.

Applying the persistence rule to the example, FX keeps the value assigned beforehand.
All the elements of the model therefore have a value for FX and the elements of GM1 have a value
both for FX and for FY

### 3.9 Definition of the reference frames

Most loadings are defined in the **global** reference frame of the mesh, except:

- For structural elements;
- For the keywords DNOR, DTAN and pressure-type loadings. In this case, it is
necessary to define normals and tangents, or possibly to use the command [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04);

## 4 General operands

### 4.1 Keyword MODELE

The keyword MODELE expects a concept produced by the operator [AFFE_MODELE](doc:u4.41.01) in which are defined the
types of finite elements assigned on the mesh. The model is necessarily of type MECANIQUE.

### 4.2 Keywords CHAM_MATER and CARA_ELEM

The keyword CHAM_MATER must be supplied when one gives a function that
depends on the density (parameter RHO).

The keyword CARA_ELEM must be supplied when one gives a function that depends
on the thickness of the shell mesh cell (parameter EP).

### 4.3 Keyword DOUBLE_LAGRANGE

This keyword makes it possible to say whether or not the user wishes to duplicate the Lagrange multipliers
used to define and dualise the boundary conditions in the assembled matrix.

In concrete terms, duplicating the Lagrange multipliers makes it possible to use linear solvers that
do not allow pivoting. Not duplicating the Lagrange multipliers makes it possible to reduce the number of degrees of
freedom of the problem (and therefore the size of the problem to be solved) but its use is limited to the
solvers MUMPS and Petsc.

Note that the two Lagrange multipliers associated with the relation will be such that the first
will be located before all the terms involved in the relation and the second after them, in the
assembled matrix. This choice has the advantage of giving an assembled matrix whose storage requirement is
smaller but has the disadvantage of being able to introduce a singularity into the matrix.
Since the numbering is in any case liable to be modified by the renumberer of the
external solver chosen, this detail therefore remains purely technical.

### 4.4 Keyword VERI_NORM

The keyword VERI_NORM checks the orientation of the normals to the surface mesh cells in 3D (skin mesh cells
TRIA or QUAD) and the line mesh cells in 2D (skin mesh cells SEG). If a normal is not outward,
a fatal error message is issued.

To reorient the mesh cells so as to have outward normals, one must use
the command [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04), keyword ORIE_PEAU.

No check is possible on shells or plates. To check their orientation, one is also referred
to the command [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04), keyword ORIE_NORM_COQUE.

### 4.5 Keyword VERI_AFFE

The keyword VERI_AFFE checks the relevance of the requested assignments according to the types
of finite elements. For example, one checks that a pressure imposed by PRES_REP is not assigned
on volume elements or point elements.

It is possible to deactivate these checks, on large models, if they take too much time.

### 4.6 Keyword INFO

The keyword INFO controls the level of the printouts on the message file.

### 4.7 Keywords ANGL_NAUT/CENTRE/TRAN

ANGL_NAUT makes it possible to define a reference frame by the nautical angles in degrees.

CENTRE gives the coordinates of the centre of rotation (in the global frame).

TRAN gives the components of the translation vector (in the global frame).

### 5.2 Conflicts between the degrees of freedom

Within the same AFFE_CHAR_MECA, including between the different occurrences, one checks that there is
no redundancy between the boundary conditions by application of the overloading and
persistence rules. Indeed, such a situation would lead to a singular matrix during the analysis.

However, if the same boundary condition is specified twice by two **different** calls
to AFFE_CHAR_MECA (for example, with two values of imposed displacement), this leads to a
singular matrix. The kinematic boundary conditions are always imposed on the **nodes**, not on
the mesh cells.

### 5.3 Pairing operations

Pairing is an operation aimed at giving the relation between two meshes.

#### 5.3.1 Node-to-node pairing (compatible meshes)

This type of pairing (used for example in LIAISON_GROUP or LIAISON_COQUE) makes it possible to establish
pairs of nodes two by two. It is done in the same way as in AFFE_CHAR_THER. First,
one establishes the two lists of nodes ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$
to be placed facing each other (i.e. to be paired), for each occurrence of the factor keyword. Redundancies
being eliminated, **the two lists of nodes obtained must have the same length**.

Determination of the pairs of facing nodes is done in several steps:

- For each node ${N1}$ of the first list, one looks for the image node
${N2}=f({N1})$ of the second list. If $f$ is not injective
(a node ${N2}$ is the image of two distinct nodes ${N1}$ and
${N3}$ ), the following error message is issued:
  There is a conflict in the facing of the nodes.
The node ${N2}$ is at the same time the facing node of node ${N1}$ and of node ${N3}$ .
- For each node ${N2}$ of the second list, one looks for the image node
${N1}=g({N2})$ of the first list. If $g$ is not injective (a node
${N1}$ is the image of two distinct nodes ${N2}$ and ${N3}$ ), the following
error message is issued:
  There is a conflict in the facing of the nodes.
The node ${N1}$ is at the same time the facing node of node ${N2}$ and of node ${N3}$ .
- One checks that $g={f}^{-1}$ , that is that the pairs obtained by the first two
steps are the same (one wants a bijection $f$ between the two lists of nodes).
If $f$ is not surjective, the following error message is issued:
  There is a conflict in the facing of the nodes.
The node ${N1}$ is the image of no node by the inverse correspondence.

For a given node $N$, one calls image node $f(N)$ the node of the other list of nodes
which realises the minimum of the distance to $N$. To facilitate pairing, in particular in
the case of particular geometries (where the boundaries ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$
could “almost” be deduced from one another by the composition of a translation and a
rotation), the possibility is offered of making a virtual geometric transformation of the first
node group (translation and rotation) before computing the distances (keywords TRAN, CENTRE
and ANGL_NAUT).

In the pairs of facing nodes, the order of the nodes matters. If for the first
occurrence of the factor keyword, a node $N$ belongs to the first node group and a
node $M$ to the second node group, and if for the second occurrence of the same factor
keyword it is the reverse, one will obtain at the end of pairing the pairs $(N,M)$ and
$(M,N)$. They will not be eliminated during the detection of redundancies; on the other hand,
the matrix obtained will be singular. Thus, one is advised to keep the same logic when
describing the facing edges.

#### 5.3.2 Mesh-cell-to-node pairing (incompatible meshes)

In the remainder of this paragraph, one will speak of the “slave” face (FACE2) and of the “master”
face (FACE1). The “tying” of two faces will be done by writing linear relations between the
degrees of freedom of the two faces. The displacements of the nodes of the slave face will be linked
to the displacements of their projections on the master face. For each node of the slave face,
one will write 2 (in 2D) or 3 (in 3D) linear relations (see the operator PROJ_CHAMP for more
details).

The principle of the relation is to eliminate the slave degrees of freedom by writing them as
linear relations of the master degrees of freedom. There is a certain symmetry in the problem
and one might believe that one can choose at random which will be the master and which will be the slave.

In reality, one must be careful on two particular points:

- The syntax is not symmetric: on the slave side, the user must specify the nodes to
“weld”, whereas on the master side, the user must give mesh cells. Moreover, the master mesh cells are
(for the moment) of a topological dimension to what would be natural. For example, for a
2D mesh, the surfaces to be tied are lines, and one might expect the
master mesh cells to be segments. The code expects surface mesh cells (quadrangles and
triangles).
- It is preferable (from a mechanical point of view) to choose as slave surface the surface
meshed most finely. In the same way as when one welds two sheets, it is better
to multiply the weld points.

Notes

- In 3D, one must not give surface master mesh cells, but the volume mesh cells
adjacent to the face. The mesh cells specified are “candidates” for the search for
facing points. One may give too many; that is not a problem. In the same way, in 2D,
the “master” mesh cells must be surface mesh cells (QUAD, TRIA) and not line mesh cells.
- When one ties a mesh formed of linear elements $({P1})$ onto another
quadratic mesh $({P2})$, it is rather advised to choose as
“slave” face the quadratic face.

### 5.5 Keyword ARETE_IMPO

The keyword ARETE_IMPO makes it possible to impose, on all the nodes of an edge of volume elements,
one or more values of a degree of freedom.

Topological assignment: GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_NO

The kinematic conditions are imposed on the nodes belonging to the mesh cells given by the
keywords TOUT, GROUP_MA while possibly excluding some by means of the keywords SANS_*. The mesh cells
are necessarily segments.

Components: DX, DY, DZ, GRX, PRE1, PRE2, PRES, PHI, TEMP

**Note**

The imposed degrees of freedom are defined in the global frame of definition of the mesh. If
one wants to impose a degree of freedom in another direction, it is possible to use the
keyword DTAN.

DTAN makes it possible to apply a boundary condition in the direction tangent to the edge. One thus modifies
the values of the displacement degrees of freedom DX, DY and DZ.

Example:

```text
ARETE_IMPO= (_F(GROUP_NO= ’LowSide’, DX= 0, DY= 0, DZ= 0),
             _F(GROUP_MA= ’RightSide’,
                SANS_GROUP_NO= ’Corner’,
                DTAN= 10),)
```

The meaning of the second occurrence of ARETE_IMPO is: “for all the nodes of the mesh-cell group
’RightSide’, DTAN= 10 except for those of the node group ’Corner’”. This makes it possible not to
have redundant boundary conditions.

### 5.6 Keyword FACE_IMPO

The keyword FACE_IMPO makes it possible to impose, on all the nodes of a face of surface or
volume elements, one or more values of a degree of freedom.

Topological assignment: GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_NO

The kinematic conditions are imposed on the nodes belonging to the mesh cells given by the
keywords TOUT, GROUP_MA while possibly excluding some by means of the keywords SANS_*. The mesh cells
are necessarily triangles or quadrangles in 3D and segments in 2D.

Components: DX, DY, DZ, DRX, DRY, DRZ, GRX, PRE1, PRE2, PRES, PHI, TEMP

**Note**

The imposed degrees of freedom are defined in the **global** frame of definition of the mesh. If
one wants to impose a degree of freedom in another direction, it is possible to use the
keyword DTAN.

DTAN makes it possible to apply a boundary condition in the direction tangent to the face. One thus modifies
the values of the displacement degrees of freedom DX, DY and DZ. This condition can work
only in 2D, not in 3D.

DNOR makes it possible to apply a boundary condition in the direction normal to the face. One thus modifies
the values of the displacement degrees of freedom DX, DY and DZ. DNOR blocks the displacement along the normal of the element.

**Note**

If the geometry of the selected (skin) mesh cells is not of good quality, the computation of the
normals may be disturbed and this may lead to “rough” results. If the zone concerned
is plane, one can then replace the keyword FACE_IMPO / DNOR (or DTAN) by the keyword
LIAISON_OBLIQUE. One is then sure that the nodes will move strictly in the same direction.

DRNOR makes it possible to apply a boundary condition in the direction normal to the face. One thus modifies
the values of the displacement degrees of freedom DRX, DRY and DRZ. DRNOR blocks the rotation along the normal of the element.

### 5.7 Keyword LIAISON_DDL

The keyword LIAISON_DDL makes it possible to define a linear relation between degrees of freedom of
two or more nodes. The following kinematic condition will be applied:

With ${U}_{i}$ the list of the $r$ degrees of freedom, ${\alpha}_{i}$ the
coefficients and $\beta$ the right-hand side.

Topological assignment: GROUP_NO gives the list of nodes ${N}_{i}$ **ordered**
in the natural way, that is in the order of the list of node groups, and for each node
group, in the order of definition of the group by GROUP_NO,

Warning! The order of the nodes matters (see the examples).

DDL gives the **ordered** list of the $r$ degrees of freedom ${U}_{i}$.

Warning! The order of the degrees of freedom matters (see the examples).

COEF_MULT gives the **ordered** list of the $r$ **real** coefficients ${\alpha}_{i}$.

Warning! The order of the coefficients matters (see the examples).

COEF_MULT_FONC gives the **ordered** list of the $r$ **function** coefficients
${\alpha}_{i}$ ( AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F ). The functions can depend only on
the initial geometry.

Warning! The order of the coefficients matters (see the examples).

COEF_IMPO is the value of the linear relation $\beta$. If it is a function
(AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F), it may depend on **time or on the initial geometry**.

Example 1: one wants to impose a linear relation between the degrees of freedom of one and the same node.
In this particular case, one will repeat after the keyword GROUP_NO the name (of the group) of the node
as many times as there are degrees of freedom in the relation.
To impose ${U}_{x}={U}_{y}$ on the node of name (of group) ${GN}1$, one will write:

```text
LIAISON_DDL =_F(GROUP_NO  =    (’GN1’, ’GN1’),
                DDL       =    (’DX’, ’DY’),
                COEF_MULT =    (1., -1.),
                COEF_IMPO    =    0.,)
```


Example 2: one wants to impose a linear relation between node groups
It is important to note that to one occurrence of the factor keyword LIAISON_DDL there corresponds one and
only one linear relation. If one wants to impose the same relation between two node groups
GRN01 and GRN02 (the same displacement ${U}_{x}$ node to node for example) **one cannot write**:

```text
LIAISON_DDL = _F(GROUP_NO   = (’GRNO1’ , ’GRNO2’),
                 DDL        = (’DX’, ’DX’),
                 COEF_MULT  = (1. , -1.),
                 COEF_IMPO    =    0.,)
```


This writing makes sense only if ${GRNO1}$ and ${GRNO2}$ each contain only
one node. In the case above it will be necessary to write out each linear relation, node by node,
or to use LIAISON_GROUP which makes it possible to condense the writing of the same linear relations between
two groups of facing nodes.

### 5.8 Keyword LIAISON_OBLIQUE

The keyword LIAISON_OBLIQUE makes it possible to apply, to nodes or node groups, the same
displacement value defined component by component in any oblique frame.

Topological assignment: GROUP_NO, the loading is assigned on the nodes.

Components: DX, DY, DZ, DRX, DRY, DRZ, values of the components.

ANGL_NAUT is the list of the three angles, in degrees, which define the oblique frame of application
of the degrees of freedom (the last angles of the list may be omitted if they are zero).
The nautical angles make it possible to pass from the global frame of definition of the coordinates of the
mesh to any oblique frame. By default the angles are identically zero and therefore
the force components are defined in the global frame.

### 5.9 Keyword LIAISON_UNIF

The keyword LIAISON_UNIF makes it possible to impose on all the degrees of freedom of all the nodes supplied **the same value (unknown)**. One will therefore have:

${U}_{1}({N}_{1})={U}_{i}({N}_{k})\\ \text{ pour tous les degrés de liberté } {U}_{i} \text{désignés sur les nœuds } {N}_{k}$

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ GROUP_MA, GROUP_NO, SANS_GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_NO
```


The kinematic conditions are imposed on the nodes ${N}_{k}$ defined by the keyword GROUP_NO or those belonging to the mesh cells given by the keyword GROUP_MA, without the nodes defined under SANS_GROUP_* .

```text
◆ DDL
```


Gives the list of the degrees of freedom ${U}_{i}$ (see § 5.1 for the possible degrees of freedom).

### 5.10 Keyword LIAISON_CHAMNO

```text
LIAISON_CHAMNO = _F(
           ◆ CHAM_NO = cham_no,
           ◆ COEF_IMPO = float,
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_CHAMNO makes it possible to define a linear relation between all the degrees of freedom present in a CHAM_NO concept. This keyword can also be used to impose on the structure (or on a part of it) a given work, for a loading computed beforehand with another AFFE_CHAR_MECA and leading to an assembled vector produced by ASSE_VECTEUR [U4.61.23](doc:u4.61.23).

```text
◆ CHAM_NO
```


Name of the cham_no which serves to define the linear relation. The degrees of freedom that are linked are all those present in the cham_no. The coefficients to be applied to the degrees of freedom are the values of the cham_no for these degrees of freedom.

```text
◆ COEF_IMPO
```


Value of the real coefficient $\beta$ applied to the right-hand side of the linear relation.

**Example 1:**

Suppose that one has a cham_no bearing on two nodes of name ${N01}$ and ${N02}$ respectively carrying the degrees of freedom ’DX’, ’DY’ and ’DZ’ for the node N01 and ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’, ’DRY’ and ’DRZ’ for the node ${N02}$.

Suppose also that the cham_no has the following values for these degrees of freedom:

```text
’DX’        N01        2.
’DY’        N01        1.
’DZ’        N01        3.
’DX’        N02        1.
’DY’        N02        4.
’DZ’        N02        2.
’DRX’       N02        3.
’DRY’       N02        5.
’DRZ’       N02        2.
```


The linear relation that one is going to impose is:

```text
  2.*DX(N01)    +    1.*DY(N01)    +    3.*DZ(N01)
+ 1.*DX(N02)    +    4.*DY(N02)    +    2.*DZ(N02)
+ 3.*DRX(N02)   +    5.*DRY(N02)   +    2.*DRZ(N02) = b
```


**Example 2: equality of components of displacements averaged on two pieces of boundary of a solid**

One wants to impose an equality of a mean-displacement component on a piece of boundary of a solid with another mean-displacement component on another piece of boundary:

with distributed force components $F_{1i}=1$ and $F_{2j}=-1$ on each respective piece of boundary.
To realise this relation condition above, one will use:

- the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA producing the loadings $F_{1i}=1$ and $F_{2j}=-1$ on each mesh-cell group describing the respective piece of boundary, for example at the edge of a 2D domain:

  - FORCE_CONTOUR specifying the respective component i or j of the forces on the mesh-cell group concerned, or
  - PRES_REP if the mean displacement to be imposed is expressed in a frame local to the boundary: normal component PRES_REP =_F(PRES=-1.) or tangential in 2D PRES_REP =_F(CISA_2D =1.)...).
- or on a face of a 3D domain:

  - FORCE_FACE specifying the respective component i or j of the forces on the mesh-cell group concerned,
- then, to obtain the cham_no concept used in LIAISON_CHAMNO, one must compute:

  - with the command CALC_VECT_ELEM the elementary vector (with the OPTION 'CHAR_MECA'),
  - which one will assemble with ASSE_VECTEUR (having beforehand a numbering concept for the degrees of freedom obtained with the command NUME_DDL),
  - the result obtained will be the cham_no concept wanted in LIAISON_CHAMNO and one will specify COEF_IMPO=0.0.

**Example 3: control of the work of a mechanical response on a boundary**

One wants to impose that the work of a mechanical response in a distributed force field applied on a boundary of a solid be equal to a unit value.

One will use an approach similar to the preceding example to produce the wanted cham_no concept with the distributed force loading on the boundary (with FORCE_CONTOUR, not forgetting to define all the components of this force vector). And in LIAISON_CHAMNO one will specify: COEF_IMPO=1.0.

### 5.11 Keyword CHAMNO_IMPO

```text
CHAMNO_IMPO = _F(
           ◆ CHAM_NO = cham_no,
           ◆ COEF_IMPO = float,
)
```


It is in fact a slight adaptation of the keyword LIAISON_CHAMNO (see § 5.10 ). The latter makes it possible to apply as coefficients of the linear relation the content of a cham_no. In the case of the keyword CHAMNO_IMPO, one takes the content of a cham_no as the right-hand side of the linear relation. It is therefore strictly equivalent to a manual procedure in which one recovers the values of the cham_no by hand and then imposes them via DDL_IMPO.

If the field cham_no contains the value 2 for DX, one imposes the relation: DX = COEF_IMPO * 2. The coefficient ${\alpha}$ is fixed at 1.

```text
◆ CHAM_NO
```


Name of the cham_no which serves to define the right-hand sides of the linear relation.

```text
◆ COEF_IMPO = b
```


Multiplier coefficient of the cham_no.

```text
LIAISON_GROUP = _F(
           ◆ / GROUP_MA_1 = grma,
             / GROUP_NO_1 = grno,
           ◆ / GROUP_NO_2 = grno,
             / GROUP_MA_2 = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_NO = grno,
           ◆ DDL_1 = / "DX",
                     / "DY",
                     / "DZ",
                     / "DRX",
                     / "DRY",
                     / "DRZ",
                     ... liste_dof
           ◆ COEF_MULT_1 = float,
           ◆ DDL_2 = / "DX",
                     / "DY",
                     / "DZ",
                     / "DRX",
                     / "DRY",
                     / "DRZ",
                     ... liste_dof
           ◆ COEF_MULT_2 = float,
           ◆ COEF_IMPO = float ou fonction,
           ◇ TRAN = float,
           ◇ ANGL_NAUT = float,
           ◇ CENTRE = float,
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_GROUP makes it possible to define the same linear relation between certain degrees of freedom of pairs of nodes, these pairs of nodes being obtained by placing facing each other two lists of mesh cells or of nodes (see § 5.3.1 ). The total number of relations imposed is equal to the number of pairs of nodes.

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ GROUP_MA_1, GROUP_NO_1:
```


First list of nodes to be put in relation (denoted ${\Gamma}_{1}$ ).

```text
◆ GROUP_MA_2, GROUP_NO_2:
```


Second list of nodes to be put in relation (denoted ${\Gamma}_{2}$ ).

```text
◇ SANS_GROUP_NO
```


This operand makes it possible to remove from the list of pairs of facing nodes all the pairs of which at least one of the nodes belongs to the list of nodes described by this operand. This makes it possible to avoid the accumulation of linear relations on one and the same node over different repetitions of the factor keyword LIAISON_GROUP, which most of the time leads to a singular matrix.

```text
◆ DDL_1
```


Gives the list of the degrees of freedom for the edge ${\Gamma}_{1}$ (see §  5.1 for the possible degrees of freedom). If DDL_1 = ’DNOR’, one links the displacement degrees of freedom along the normal to the surface of the element (see §  3.9.1 ).

```text
◆ DDL_2
```


Gives the list of the degrees of freedom for the edge ${\Gamma}_{2}$ (see §  5.1 for the possible degrees of freedom). If DDL_2 = ’DNOR’, one links the displacement degrees of freedom along the normal to the surface of the element (see §  3.9.1 ).

```text
◆ COEF_MULT_1
```


List of reals exactly sized to the number of degrees of freedom declared in DDL_1 corresponding to the multiplier coefficients of the linear relation.

```text
◆ COEF_MULT_2
```


List of reals exactly sized to the number of degrees of freedom declared in DDL_2 corresponding to the multiplier coefficients of the linear relation.

```text
◆ COEF_IMPO
```


Blocking coefficient of the linear relation.

```text
◇ CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN
```


The operands CENTRE / ANGL_NAUT / TRAN (see § 4.6 ) make it possible to define an approximate virtual transformation (rotation and/or translation) of $\Gamma_1$ into ${\Gamma}_{2}$ in order to ensure the bijectivity of the facing function [§ 5.3.1 ]. The command first carries out the rotation, then the translation.

**Example**:

One wants to impose a cyclic repetition condition (same normal displacement) between face $1$ and face $2$ of the geometry below:

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/100005D8000069BB000028D30554BBD3D878028B.svg)

Suppose that FACE1 (respectively FACE2) is composed of the list of mesh cells lma1 (respectively lma2). Since the relation must be bijective, the two faces necessarily have the same number of nodes ${nbno}$. One wants to write the following linear relations:

$$
\forall {N}_{i}^{1} nœud de la FACE1 de vis-à-vis {N}_{i}^{2}
\forall i = 1...{nbno}
{u.n}({N}_{i}^{1})={u.n}({N}_{i}^{2})
$$

The data of LIAISON_GROUP will be written:

```text
LIAISON_GROUP = _F( GROUP_MA_1   =  lma1,
                    GROUP_MA_2   =  lma2,
                    DDL_1        =  ’DNOR’,
                    DDL_2        =  ’DNOR’,
                    COEF_MULT_1  =  1.,
                    COEF_MULT_2  =  -1.,
                    COEF_IMPO    =  0,
                    CENTRE       =  (X0,Y0,Z0),
                    ANGL_NAUT    =  (:math:`\alpha`,0.,0.),
)
```


In the case where FACE2 is perpendicular to the X axis, the preceding example can also be written:

```text
LIAISON_GROUP=_F ( GROUP_MA_1 = lma1,
                   GROUP_MA_2 = lma2,
                   DDL_1 = ('DX', 'DY'),
                   DDL_2 = 'DY',
                   COEF_MULT_1 = (-sin(:math:`\alpha` ), cos(:math:`\alpha` )),
                   COEF_MULT_2 = -1.,
                   COEF_IMPO = 0
)
```


**Note:**

*The total number of relations imposed by one occurrence does not depend on the number of arguments of DDL_1 or of DDL_2, which serve only to enrich the relation.*

In the following case, one imposes ${nbno}$ relations:

```text
LIAISON_GROUP=_F ( GROUP_MA_1 = lma1,
                   GROUP_MA_2 = lma2,
                   DDL_1 = ('DX', 'DY'),
                   DDL_2 = ('DX', 'DY'),
                   COEF_MULT_1 = (1, 1),
                   COEF_MULT_2 = (-1, -1),
                   COEF_IMPO = 0
)
```


In the following case, one imposes $2\cdot {nbno}$ relations:

```text
LIAISON_GROUP=(_F( GROUP_MA_1 = lma1,
                   GROUP_MA_2 = lma2,
                   DDL_1 = 'DX',
                   DDL_2 = 'DX',
                   COEF_MULT_1 = 1,
                   COEF_MULT_2 = -1.,
                   COEF_IMPO = 0)
               _F ( GROUP_MA_1 = lma1,
                    GROUP_MA_2 = lma2,
                    DDL_1 = 'DY',
                    DDL_2 = 'DY',
                    COEF_MULT_1 = 1,
                    COEF_MULT_2 = -1.,
                    COEF_IMPO = 0
                )
)
```


It must be carefully noted that these last two examples are not equivalent.

#### 5.13.1 Use and examples

The keyword LIAISON_MAIL makes it possible to define linear relations in order to “tie” two “edges” of a structure. The particular feature of this keyword (compared with LIAISON_GROUP for example) is to make it possible to link the displacements of nodes without a constraint on the mesh. The meshes of FACE1 and FACE2 may be incompatible.

**Note (cf. §**  5.3.2 **):**

*In* *3D, one must not give surface master mesh cells, but the* **volume mesh cells adjacent to the face** *to be linked. The mesh cells specified are “candidates” for the search for facing points. One may give too many; that is not a problem.* *In the same way, in 2D, the “master” mesh cells must be* **surface mesh cells (QUAD,* *TRIA) and not line mesh cells**.

**Examples**:

1. a periodicity condition (study of a homogenisation cell)

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/100004AA000069D500002C2206108FD7E96C96A8.svg)

Experience has shown that for periodic homogenisation computations, the results are much more accurate if the two faces have compatible meshes (that is the meshes of FACE1 and FACE2 can be superimposed modulo an isometry).

1. a cyclic repetition condition

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/1000050A000069D5000027FFC826C70D21B58D73.svg)

1. a simple tying condition

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/1000077A000069D500003C5C4418409CD3BF50FD.svg)

In the remainder of this paragraph, one will speak of the “slave” face (FACE2) and of the “master” face (FACE1). The “tying” of the two faces will be done by writing linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the two faces, see § 5.3.2 .

The displacements of the nodes of the slave face will be linked to the displacements of their projections on the master face. For each node of the slave face, one will write two (in 2D) or three (in 3D) linear relations.

#### 5.13.2 A few notes and precautions of use

- If FACE1 and FACE2 are not geometrically coincident but there exists an isometry (rotation and translation) between the two, the user must define this isometry (the one which transforms FACE2 into FACE1), by means of the keywords CENTRE / ANGL_NAUT / TRAN.
- A “classical” use of this feature is for example the tying of a mesh formed of linear elements $({P1})$ onto another quadratic mesh $({P2})$. In this case it is rather advised to choose as “slave” face the quadratic face.
- The keyword LIAISON_MAIL is in principle made to link two surfaces which are *a priori* disjoint. Sometimes this is not the case and a slave node may belong to one of the master mesh cells. The linear relation that the problem seeks to write becomes a tautology ($X{=}X$), which leads to a zero pivot during factorisation. To avoid this problem, one does not write the relations linking a slave node to its master mesh cell if:

◦    This node belongs to the connectivity of the mesh cell;

◦    And if the keywords CENTRE / ANGL_NAUT / TRAN have not been used.

- One must be aware that for each occurrence of LIAISON_MAIL, one links *a priori* **all** the slave nodes to the master mesh cells **even if the projection distances are large** (alarms are however issued in this case). One can change this behaviour by using the keyword DISTANCE_MAX.
- If one writes:

```text
LIAISON_MAIL = ( _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’,
                    GROUP_MA_MAIT =’GM1’),
                 _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’,
                    GROUP_MA_MAIT =’GM2’)
)
```


It would be a mistake to think that the program will sort in $\text{GE}$ the nodes close to $\text{GM1}$ and those close to $\text{GM2}$. In this example, the nodes of $\text{GE}$ will be eliminated twice and one can expect a zero-pivot problem during factorisation.

The user must write:

```text
LIAISON_MAIL = _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’,
                  GROUP_MA_MAIT=(’GM1’,’GM2’)
)
```

```text

```


List of the slave nodes to be put in relation. When one wants to tie only the normal displacements of the faces (cf. keywords DDL_MAIT and DDL_ESCL), one must be able to determine the normal direction of the faces. The normal direction is computed on the slave face. One must therefore in this case use the keyword GROUP_MA_ESCL with mesh cells of “facet” type.



List of the **master mesh cells** to be put in relation.

```text

```


When a slave node is not found (geometrically) in any master mesh cell, the program puts in relation the node and the point (of the boundary) of the nearest mesh cell.

If one wishes a slave node “far” from the master mesh cells not to be concerned by the relation, one must use the operand DISTANCE_MAX. This operand makes it possible to give the maximum distance beyond which one will not make a relation.

There is no default value for DISTANCE_MAX. Which means that by default, the relation will concern all the slave nodes.

```text
◇ DISTANCE_ALARME = d_ala
```


The keyword DISTANCE_ALARME = d_ala makes it possible to be warned if a slave node is at a distance d > d_ala from the nearest master mesh cell.

There is no default value for DISTANCE_ALARME. By default, an alarm is issued if the distance d is greater than 1/10 of the size of the nearest mesh cell (relative criterion).

```text
◇ TYPE_RACCORD
```


This keyword makes it possible to choose the type of the linear relations that one is going to write in order to eliminate the degrees of freedom of the slave nodes.

- If TYPE_RACCORD = ’MASSIF’, the nodes are assumed to carry translation degrees of freedom (DX, DY, DZ). If the user does not specify DDL_MAIT='DNOR', one will write (for example in 2D) two linear relations for each slave node: one to eliminate its 'DX', the other to eliminate its 'DY'.
- If TYPE_RACCORD = ’COQUE’, the nodes are assumed to carry translation degrees of freedom (DX, DY, DZ) and rotation degrees of freedom (DRX, DRY, DRZ). One will write six linear relations to eliminate the six degrees of freedom of each slave node.
- If TYPE_RACCORD = ’MASSIF_COQUE’, the slave nodes are assumed to be of “solid” type (translations: DX, DY, DZ) and the master nodes are assumed to be of “shell” type (three translations and three rotations). The translation degrees of freedom of the slave nodes are eliminated by writing that they are equal to the translations of the facing “master” point. The translations of the master point are computed as if the small segment normal to the shell remained rigid. The figure below shows a “touching” situation in section of a solid and of a shell with the nodes put in relation: the translation kinematics of the point $A$ of the shell is parametrised by the degrees of freedom of the shell $({U},{\Theta})$ and its position on the finite element considered, obtained by the projection coming from the slave node $A'$ of the MASSIF mesh. In this figure, one has arranged that only the nodes of the boundary of the MASSIF mesh are considered by the relation with the value of DISTANCE_MAX.

The translation degrees of freedom of the slave nodes are eliminated by writing that they are equal to the translations of the facing “master” point. The rotation degrees of freedom of the slave nodes are eliminated by writing that they are equal to the rotations of the facing “master” point $(A)$. These rotations of the point $A$ are computed from the translations, parametrised by the degrees of freedom of the master nodes, of two other points $A1$ and $A2$ located at $+h/2\vec{n}$ and $-h/2\vec{n}$, if $\vec{n}$ is a vector normal to the shell and whose length is the thickness $h$ of the shell (see the mandatory keywords in this case EPAIS and CHAM_NORMALE).



List of the degrees of freedom to which one imposes the relations. The existence of the degrees of freedom is not checked (see § 5.1 for the name of the degrees of freedom).

```text
◇ CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN
```


The operands CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN (see § 4.6 ) make it possible to pass from the slave face to the master face. The command first carries out the rotation, then the translation. If these keywords are absent, it is because the geometric transformation is “the identity”, that is the master and slave faces are geometrically coincident. Warning! **The sense of the transformation is slave towards master**.

```text
◆ EPAIS/CHAM_NORMALE
```


These two keywords are mandatory if TYPE_RACCORD = ’COQUE_MASSIF’: EPAIS gives the thickness of the shell at the level of the relation (assumed constant) and CHAM_NORMALE gives a field at the nodes which contains the direction of the normal to the shell on the nodes of the “slave” mesh cells. The field can be obtained by the command:

```text
CHNOR = CREA_CHAMP(TYPE_CHAM = ’NOEU_GEOM_R’,
               OPERATION = ’NORMALE’,
               MODELE = MODEL,
               GROUP_MA = ’GMCOQU’)
```


```text
◇ ELIM_MULT
```


This keyword serves to solve the problem which may arise when one ties several adjacent slave surfaces (that is which have one or more nodes in common). Imagine for example that one writes (in 2D):

```text
LIAISON_MAIL=(_F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’LIGNE_AB’, GROUP_MAIT= ...)
_F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’LIGNE_BC’, GROUP_MAIT= ...))
```


If the user forces ELIM_MULT=’NON’, the program will treat each occurrence of LIAISON_MAIL independently. The node $B$, belonging to LIGNE_AB and LIGNE_BC, will therefore be taken into account in two different relations and it is probable that the computation will stop during factorisation of the matrix with the message “Almost zero pivot” if the linear relations generated by LIAISON_MAIL are redundant.

Most of the time, the default value ELIM_MULT=’OUI’ is the right choice. The only case where the user might use ELIM_MULT=’NON’ is that of the use of the keyword DDL_ESCL=’DNOR’. If the “slave” normals are not collinear, the linear relations generated will not be redundant.

```text
◇ TRANSF_GEOM_MAIT
◇ TRANSF_GEOM_ESCL
```


The 2 keywords TRANSF_GEOM_[MAIT|ESCL] = (fx, fy, fz) make it possible to carry out one (or two) geometric transformations on the coordinates of the nodes of the master and slave meshes before doing the projection. This geometric transformation is temporary (for the duration of the pairing phase): the meshes are not modified.

### 5.14 Keyword LIAISON_PROJ

```text
LIAISON_PROJ = _F(

                   / "DY",
```

Factor keyword which makes it possible to define linear relations between the nodes of one and the same model. #### 5.14.1 Operands

```text

```

VIS_A_VIS: under this factor keyword the groups make it possible to define the master and slave mesh cells:

- GROUP_MA_1 corresponds to the master mesh cells.
- GROUP_MA_2 corresponds to the slave mesh cells.

```text
◆ DDL
```


List of the degrees of freedom of the slave nodes to which one imposes the relations. The existence of the degrees of freedom on the slave and master nodes is checked. All the degrees of freedom are allowed.

```text
◇ TYPE = ‘EXCENTREMENT’
```


Makes it possible, in the case where rotation degrees of freedom exist on the master mesh cells, to impose on the slave nodes displacements which take these rotations into account.

The relations are of the following form:

- For the degrees of freedom of the slave nodes given under DDL.

${DDL}({N}_{{escl}})={\sum}_{i}{{Coeff}}_{i}\ast {DDL}({N}_{{maître}}^{i})$

with i: node of the master mesh cell containing the slave node.

- In the case where TYPE=’EXCENTREMENT’, the relation on the translation degrees of freedom of the slave nodes becomes:

${DDL}({N}_{{escl}})={\sum}_{i}{{Coeff}}_{i}\ast ({DDL}({N}_{{maître}}^{i})+\overrightarrow{\omega ({N}_{{maître}}^{i})}\wedge \overrightarrow{{N}_{{maître}}^{i}{N}_{{escl}}})$

The existence of the degrees of freedom on the master and slave nodes is checked.

### 5.15 Keyword LIAISON_CYCL

```text
LIAISON_CYCL = _F(
           ◇ GROUP_MA_MAIT1 = grma,
           ◇ GROUP_MA_MAIT2 = grma,
           ◆ | GROUP_MA_ESCL = grma,
             | GROUP_NO_ESCL = grno,
           ◇ TRAN = float,
           ◇ ANGL_NAUT = float,
           ◇ CENTRE = float,
           ◇ COEF_MAIT1 = float,
           ◇ COEF_MAIT2 = float,
           ◇ COEF_ESCL = float,
           ◇ & DDL_MAIT = "DNOR" (ou non renseigné),
             & DDL_ESCL = "DNOR" (ou non renseigné),
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_CYCL makes it possible to define the linear relations in order to impose cyclic symmetry conditions taking a phase shift into account. It is mainly intended to be used within the restrictive framework of dynamic analysis with cyclic symmetry. The particular feature of this keyword (in the image of LIAISON_MAIL) is to make it possible to link the displacements of nodes without a constraint on the mesh. The meshes of ${FACEG}$ and ${FACED}$ may be incompatible.

The cyclic repetition condition applied within the framework of dynamics is based on the mesh-duplication method. The operator therefore starts from the postulate that the initial mesh of a sector is duplicated into two identical meshes in the image of the following figure.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/100000000000011B0000007EE200052A9B0E858A.png)

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/100000000000011B0000007E50F4E5274EEAFD50.png)

In the remainder of this paragraph, one will speak of the “slave” face and of the “master” face. The “tying” of the two faces will be done by writing linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the two faces.

The displacements of the nodes of the slave face will be linked to the displacements of their projections on the master face. For each node of the slave face, one will write two (in 2D) or three (in 3D) linear relations.

If ${FACEG}$ and ${FACED}$ are not geometrically coincident but there exists an isometry (rotation + translation) between the two, the user must define this isometry (the one which transforms ${FACEG}$ into ${FACED}$) by means of the keywords CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN.

The expression of the cyclic symmetry condition for a given inter-sector phase shift $\beta$ and considering $G$ as the slave interface is the following:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{c}{q}_{g}^{1}\\ {q}_{g}^{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{cos}\beta & {sin}\beta \\ -{sin}\beta & {cos}\beta \end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{q}_{d}^{1}\\ {q}_{d}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

In order to write the linear relations making it possible to take this condition into account, it is necessary to give **two** occurrences of the factor keyword LIAISON_CYCL:

- The first makes it possible to link the degrees of freedom of face $G$ of mesh 1 with face $D$ of the same mesh and face $D$ of mesh 2. The coefficients (${cos}\beta$ and ${sin}\beta$) must be supplied by the keywords COEF_MAIT1, COEF_MAIT2.
- The second makes it possible to link the degrees of freedom of face $G$ of mesh 2 with face $D$ of the same mesh and face $D$ of mesh 1. The coefficients ($-{sin}\beta$ and ${cos}\beta$) must be supplied by the keywords COEF_MAIT1, COEF_MAIT2.

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ GROUP_NO_ESCL/GROUP_MA_ESCL:
```


These keywords make it possible to define the set of nodes of the slave face. One takes all the nodes specified by the keyword GROUP_NO_ESCL plus all the nodes carried by the mesh cells specified by the keyword GROUP_MA_ESCL.

```text
◇ GROUP_MA_MAIT_1:
```


This keyword makes it possible to define the set of master mesh cells of mesh 1 (or 2) where one will look for the facing nodes of the slave face of mesh 1 or 2.

```text
◇ GROUP_MA_MAIT_2:
```


This keyword makes it possible to define the set of master mesh cells of mesh 1 (or 2) where one will look for the facing nodes of the slave face of mesh 1 or 2.

```text
◇ COEF_MAIT_1/COEF_MAIT_2/COEF_ESCL
```


These keywords make it possible to define the coefficients of the linear relation to be applied; in the case of cyclic symmetry these are the cosines and sines of the inter-sector phase-shift angle considered. These coefficients must therefore be consistent with the definition of the master and slave interfaces. The coefficient COEF_ESCL makes it possible to put a coefficient in front of the slave degrees of freedom. For example:

${COEF}\text{\_}{ESCL}({q}_{g}^{1}){=}\left[{COEF}\text{\_}{MAIT1}{\times}{COEF}\text{\_}{MAIT2}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{q}_{d}^{1}\\ {q}_{d}^{2}\end{array}\right]{=}\left[{cos}\beta {\cdot}{sin}\beta \right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{q}_{d}^{1}\\ {q}_{d}^{2}\end{array}\right]$

```text
◇ DDL_MAIT/DDL_ESCL
```


If one wants to tie only the displacements normal to the faces, one must specify DDL_MAIT= ’DNOR’ and DDL_ESCL=’DNOR’. The normal direction being computed on the slave face, one must give facet mesh cells (see GROUP_MA_ESCL). This normal direction is transformed by the possible rotation of the geometric transformation (see CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/ TRAN) in order to determine the normal direction on the master face.

```text
◇ CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN
```


The operands CENTRE/ANGL_NAUT/TRAN (see § 4.6 ) make it possible to pass from the slave face to the master face. The command first carries out the rotation, then the translation. If these keywords are absent, it is because the geometric transformation is “the identity”, that is the master and slave faces are geometrically coincident. Warning! **The sense of the transformation is slave towards master**.

### 5.16 Keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE

```text
LIAISON_SOLIDE = _F(
           ◆ / GROUP_NO = grno,
             / GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_NO = grno,
           ◇ DIST_MIN = float,
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE makes it possible to model a non-deformable part of a structure. One imposes linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the nodes of this non-deformable part such that the relative displacements between these nodes are zero, and one possibly imposes the displacements at the values resulting from the translation and/or the rotation. These nodes are defined by the mesh-cell groups and the node groups to which they belong. For the restrictions of use, see § 3.2.1 .

In small perturbations, one imposes in 2D $({{nb}}_{{ddl}}{\times}{{nb}}_{{noeud}}–3)$ relations and in 3D $({{nb}}_{{ddl}}{\times}{{nb}}_{{noeud}}–6)$ relations, where ${{nb}}_{{ddl}}$ is the number of degrees of freedom per node and ${{nb}}_{{noeud}}$ is the number of nodes of the list given after LIAISON_SOLIDE. A solid is determined by the position of one of its points and of a frame at that point. Relations are written by taking the vector formula expressing a rigid-body motion in small rotations:

$$
\overrightarrow{u}(M)=\overrightarrow{u}(A)+\overrightarrow{\Omega}(A)\wedge \overrightarrow{{AM}}
$$

where $A$ is an arbitrary node of the solid. For more details, see the document [R3.03.02](doc:r3.03.02).

It is possible to use LIAISON_SOLIDE in large transformations in STAT_NON_LINE (since the problem becomes non-linear) only by imposing that the loading be a follower loading (TYPE_CHARGE=’SUIV’, see [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)).

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_MA, SANS_GROUP_NO
```


The rigid-body kinematic conditions are imposed on the nodes belonging to the mesh cells given by the keywords TOUT, GROUP_MA while possibly excluding some by means of the keywords SANS_*.

```text
◇ DIST_MIN
```


This keyword serves to define a distance (in the units of the mesh) below which one considers that the points of the mesh are coincident. This distance also serves to determine whether points are aligned, that is whether they lie in a cylinder of diameter smaller than ${dmin}$. By default, ${dmin}{=}0.001{\times}{armin}$, where ${armin}$ is the smallest edge of the mesh.

**Notes:**

*If an element has all its nodes in a zone “solidified” by* LIAISON_SOLIDE *, its strain is zero. The stress state is then also zero, as are the generalised forces if it is a structural element.*

*If the list of nodes to be linked contains only aligned nodes carrying no rotation degree of freedom, then, if the system is to be subjected to a rotation, one must add a fictitious non-aligned node to the list. The other solution is to place oneself under the assumption of large rotations and large displacements (TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV'); one then explicitly imposes on the solid relation that the distances be constant.*

The keyword LIAISON_ELEM makes it possible to link the kinematics of the interfaces between two different modellings of a structure, taking into account the shape and the degree of approximation of the finite elements on the interface, and without creating artefacts of localised stresses.
One can thus transmit the generalised forces or the kinematic conditions without generating “parasitic” stresses.
It is advised to place the junction sufficiently far from the zone to be analysed finely, far from a strong geometric singularity (a strong curvature, a corner, a hole…) or from material heterogeneities, or from strong variations of loading, of temperatures...

This solution makes it possible to treat situations of “generalised plane strain” type, without being affected by the
limitations inherent in this approximation, which is not recommended.

Calling “solid part” the piece of structure modelled with 3D isoparametric elements, which may be a particular zone, “zoomed” from a slender structure, this factor keyword makes it possible to model the junction:

- Of a solid part with a shell part;
- Of a solid part with a beam part [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03) or a pipe element [R3.08.06](doc:r3.08.06) or a discrete element;
- Of a shell part with a beam part [R3.03.06](doc:r3.03.06) or a pipe element [R3.08.06](doc:r3.08.06) or a discrete element;

The beam (or shell) part is dedicated to the global idealisation of the structure studied, in its part where the geometry is relatively regular.
To ensure consistency, it is desirable that the constitutive relations of the constituent materials of the beam (or of the shell) correspond to those modelled in the refined zone in the neighbourhood of the junction.

This factor keyword also makes it possible to connect the edge of a 2D structure with a beam or a discrete element.
The aim of this feature is not to account for the length scales between the parts to be connected but to allow a simplification of the modelling by replacing a solid or surface part by a beam part for example. The junction is treated by imposing linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the nodes of the junction of the two parts to be connected, without imposing superfluous relations.
The kinematic relations thus created are defined on the geometry of the mesh, and are not updated in a large-displacement situation.

One may consult the document: “Modellings of particular zones of slender solids”, EDF/DER CR-MMN 98/099, and also the report “3D beam junctions, 3D shell junctions and generalisations” EDF/DER HI-70/97/001/0.

#### 5.17.1 Option ’COQ_3D’

This option makes it possible to connect a 3D solid part with a part modelled with shell or plate elements.
It makes it possible to ensure mechanical continuity between a volume modelling and a surface modelling at the interface.
This kinematic relation does indeed let the shell/plate “breathe” through its thickness at the interface: the pinching strain remains free.
It is based on a variational formulation, unlike the LIAISON_MAIL relations of TYPE_RACCORD MASSIF_COQUE and COQUE_MASSIF.
All the pairs of supports of the finite elements respectively considered {SEG2, SEG3} * {TRIA3, TRIA6, QUA4, QUA8} are supported at the interface, the mesh cells having to be compatible between the modelled parts.
One will note that Code_Aster does not check the consistency at the interface between the thickness of the solid part and the thickness of the shell or plate part.
One can consult an example in the test case [v3.03.124](doc:v3.03.124).


Mesh-cell group corresponding to the boundary of the domain discretised with volume elements to be connected to the shell or plate part.

```text

```


Concept created by the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM, containing the geometric characteristics of the shell and plate elements, in particular the thickness.

#### 5.17.2 Option ’3D_POU’

This option makes it possible to connect a 3D solid part with a part modelled with Euler or Timoshenko beams. A junction between a 3D solid part and a beam part requires six linear relations.
This kinematic relation can also be used to connect a 3D solid part with a discrete element DIS_TR, which makes it possible to control the displacements and the rotations of the interface of the 3D solid part considered, without introducing parasitic stresses (by Poisson effect) or a **variational crime**, that is without exerting localised forces producing a singularity.
The part modelled with beams must have, at the level of the junction, a neutral line perpendicular to the plane of the cross-section connected to the 3D solid part, arriving at its centre of gravity, in order to be able to interpret the generalised forces correctly.
One notes that this relation treats only the six translation and rotation degrees of freedom (Euler or Timoshenko), but not the warping of the POU_D_TG elements.
One can use the command POST_ELEM, keyword MASS_INER [u4.25.01](doc:u4.25.01), to compute the position of the centre of inertia of the cross-section of the **solid** part that is connected.

```text
◇ APPA_TOLE
```


Tolerance used for the various projection algorithms used during pairing.

```text
◇ AIRE_TOLE
```


If the intersection area between the slave mesh cell and the master mesh cell is smaller than AIRE_TOLE then it will not be retained.
This makes it possible to avoid numerical artefacts linked to small variations of intersection.

```text
◇ TYPE_APPA = / "FORCEBRUTE" (par défaut),
              / "PANG",
              / "RAPIDE",
```

### 5.19 Keyword LIAISON_RBE3

```text
LIAISON_RBE3 = _F(
           ◇ GROUP_NO_MAIT = grno,
           ◆ DDL_MAIT = / "DX",
                        / "DY",
                        / "DZ",
                        / "DRX",
                        / "DRY",
                        / "DRZ",
           ◇ GROUP_NO_ESCL = grno,
           ◆ DDL_ESCL = text,
           ◇ COEF_ESCL = float (défaut: 1.0),
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_RBE3 makes it possible to define in a general way linear relations of RBE3 type between the degrees of freedom of a master node and of slave nodes, while ensuring transmission of the force torsor. These are relations making it possible to specify the value of certain degrees of freedom of a master node as being the weighted average of certain displacements and of certain rotations of slave nodes.

The linear relations produced are such that the forces seen by the master node are distributed to the slave nodes in proportion to their distance to the centre of gravity of the slave nodes. Possible additional weightings supplied by the user can be taken into account. For more details, one may refer to the reference documentation [R3.03.08](doc:r3.03.08).

This option makes it possible to connect a 3D solid part with a part modelled with Euler or Timoshenko beams. A junction between a 3D solid part and a beam part requires six linear relations.

Topological assignment:

```text
◇ GROUP_NO_MAIT:
```


Identifies the master node of the linear relation. One must give only one group, and this group must contain only one node.

```text
◇ GROUP_NO_ESCL:
```


Identification of the slave nodes of the linear relation.

```text
◆ DDL_MAIT
```


Identification of the degrees of freedom of the master node involved in the linear relation. One expects a list comprising at most six entries among ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’, ’DRY’, ’DRZ’.

```text
◆ DDL_ESCL
```


Identification of the degrees of freedom of the slave nodes involved in the linear relation. Either the list has a length equal to the number of slave nodes, or there is only one term. In the latter case, one assumes that this same term corresponds to all the DDL_MAIT. Each term of the list must be a combination of the entries ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’, ’DRY’, ’DRZ’, separated by a hyphen ’-’.

```text
◇ COEF_ESCL
```


List of weighting coefficients of the terms of the linear relation for each slave node. The list must:

- Either have the same length as the number of slave nodes;
- Or be of length $1$, in which case this coefficient is used for all the slave nodes

**Example**:

If one wants to create a relation of RBE3 type between:

- The degrees of freedom ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’ of the master node ’NO1’;

And:

- The degrees of freedom ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’ of the slave node ’NO2’ with the weighting coefficient $0.1$;
- The degrees of freedom ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’ of the slave node ’NO3’ with the weighting coefficient $0.2$;
- The degrees of freedom ’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’ of the slave node ’NO4’ with the weighting coefficient $0.3$;

One must write the command:

```text
LIAISON_RBE3 = _F( GROUP_NO_MAIT     = ’NO1’,
                   DDL_MAIT          = (’DX’, ’DY’, ’DZ’, ’DRX’),
                   GROUP_NO_ESCL     = (’NO2’, ’NO3’, ’NO4’),
                   DDL_ESCL          = (’DX-DY-DZ’,
                                        ’DX-DY-DZ-DRX’,
                                        ’DX-DY-DZ-DRX’),
                   COEF_ESCL         =(0.1, 0.2, 0.3),
)
```


x takes the values 2 and 3 for TUYAU_3M and TUYAU_6M, and 4, 5 and 6 only for TUYAU_6M

To carry out these checks, the user would have to give, in addition to the beam node, the name of the mesh cell assigned by the CARA_ELEM which has as end the junction node. In the great majority of cases, this mesh cell is unknown to the user; it is the meshing software which defines its name.

### 6.2 Keyword LIAISON_COQUE

```text
LIAISON_COQUE = _F(
           ◆ | GROUP_MA_1 = grma,
             | GROUP_MA_2 = grma,
             | GROUP_NO_1 = grno,
             | GROUP_NO_2 = grno,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_MA_1 = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_NO_1 = grno,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_MA_2 = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_NO_2 = grno,
)
```


The keyword LIAISON_COQUE makes it possible to represent the junction between shells by means of linear relations. The classical approach assumes that two planes meshed with shells intersect along a straight line which belongs to the mesh of the structure. The pairs of nodes are obtained by placing facing each other two lists of nodes (see § 5.3.1 ). This has the disadvantage of counting twice the volume which is the intersection of the two shells.

The idea is therefore to stop the mesh of a shell perpendicular to a given shell at the level of the upper or lower skin of the latter.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/10001EF0000069D5000077E47DB9171EA88CEC03.svg)

The volume of the shells has been drawn in solid lines and the mean planes of these shells (which come from the mesh) in dotted lines. The horizontal shell stops at ${A}_{1}{A}_{2}$ and the projection of ${A}_{1}{A}_{2}$ on the mean plane of the vertical shell is ${B}_{1}{B}_{2}$ (which has been drawn in solid lines). The link between the two shells is made by rigid-body relations (see § 5.16 ) between the facing nodes of the segments ${A}_{1}{A}_{2}$ and ${B}_{1}{B}_{2}$.

For example for the nodes ${A}_{1}$ and ${B}_{1}$, one will write the formula (valid in small rotations):

$$
U({B}_{1})=U({A}_{1})+\Omega ({A}_{1})\wedge {A}_{1}{B}_{1}
$$

And the equality of the rotations:

$$
\Omega ({B}_{1})=\Omega ({A}_{1})
$$

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ GROUP_MA_1, GROUP_NO_1:
```


First list of nodes to be put in relation. The nodes are given by the keywords GROUP_MA_1, GROUP_NO_1 while possibly excluding some by means of the keywords SANS_* . These nodes represent the trace of the perpendicular shell on the current shell. In our example, these would be the nodes of the segment ${B}_{1}{B}_{2}$ or of the segment ${A}_{1}{A}_{2}$.

```text
◆ GROUP_MA_2, GROUP_NO_2:
```


Second list of nodes to be put in relation. The nodes are given by the keywords GROUP_MA_2, GROUP_NO_2 while possibly excluding some by means of the keywords SANS_* . These nodes belong to the perpendicular shell and face the nodes of the first list. The facing is adjusted by the program according to the criterion of smallest distance. In our example if the first list consists of the nodes of ${A}_{1}{A}_{2}$, the second list consists of the nodes of ${B}_{1}{B}_{2}$.

### 7.10 Keyword ROTATION

```text
ROTATION = _F(
           ◇ GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◇ SANS_GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◆ VITESSE = float,
           ◆ AXE = float,
           ◇ CENTRE = float,
)
```

The factor keyword ROTATION can be used to apply a force field equivalent to the centrifugal force applied to a rotating structure. Let $\omega$ be the rotation speed and $({a}_{r},{b}_{r},{c}_{r})$ the axis of rotation.

$$
\omega =\omega \frac{({a}_{r}i+{b}_{r}j+{c}_{r}k)}{\sqrt{{a}_{r}^{2}+{b}_{r}^{2}+{c}_{r}^{2}}}

\omega =\omega \frac{({a}_{r}i+{b}_{r}j+{c}_{r}k)}{\sqrt{{a}_{r}^{2}+{b}_{r}^{2}+{c}_{r}^{2}}}
$$

The loading which results from it is of the form:

$$
f=\rho (\omega \mathrm{\wedge }\mathit{OM})\mathrm{\wedge }\omega
$$

Where $O$ is the origin of the coordinates and $M$ a current point of the structure, with $\rho$ the density defined as a characteristic of the material (see the operators DEFI_MATERIAU[U4.43.01] and AFFE_MATERIAU[U4.43.03]).

**There can be only one occurrence of this keyword in** AFFE_CHAR_MECA **.**

Topological assignment:

```text
◇ GROUP_MA
```


By default, this field applies to the whole model. It is possible to restrict it to a part of the model by means of the keyword GROUP_MA, which specifies the mesh cells on which the field applies.

```text
◆ VITESSE
```


Rotation speed.

```text
◆ AXE
```


Axis of rotation. For plane modellings, the axis of rotation must be in the direction $Oz$ and for axisymmetric and Fourier modellings, it must be in the direction $Oy$.

```text
◇ CENTRE
```


If the centre of rotation is not the origin (default), one can specify its coordinates (x, y, z). For axisymmetric and Fourier modellings, the centre must be the origin.

One can vary the rotation speed in time by decomposing the rotation in a multiplicative way between spatial loading and evolution in time $\omega (t)={\omega}_{0}f(t)$, then by multiplying the load by a multiplier function (keyword FONC_MULT) in the transient analysis. However, care must be taken: the loading $\rho (\omega \wedge \mathrm{OM})\wedge \omega$ being proportional to the square of the rotation speed, $\omega {(t)}^{2}$, one must assign the square of the evolution in time, $f{(t)}^{2}$, after FONC_MULT.

This prestrain can be used for example to solve the elementary problems determining the elastic correctors in the basic cell (2D, 3D), in periodic homogenisation. The homogenised elasticity coefficients are obtained by computing with the operator POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22) the keyword ENER_POT, that is the potential energy of elastic strain at equilibrium from the correctors. But it can serve for other applications.

### 9.4 Keyword ONDE_PLANE

```text
ONDE_PLANE =_F(
            ◇ GROUP_MA = grma,
            ◆ TYPE_ONDE =  / "S",
                           / "P",
                           / "SV",
                           / "SH",
            ◆ DIRECTION = (kx,ky,kz),
            ◆ FONC_SIGNAL = f,
            ◇ DEPL_IMPO = fd,
            ◇ COOR_SOURCE = (x0, y0, z0),
            # Si: exists("COOR_SOURCE")
                ◇ COOR_REFLECHI = (x1, y1, z1),

```


The factor keyword ONDE_PLANE (only one occurrence is allowed) makes it possible to impose a seismic loading by plane wave, corresponding to the loadings classically encountered during soil-structure interaction analyses by the integral equations (see [R4.05.01](doc:r4.05.01)). In harmonic regime, an elastic plane wave is characterised by its direction, its angular frequency and its type (wave $P$ for the compression waves, waves $S$, $\mathit{SV}$ or $\mathit{SH}$ for the shear waves). In transient regime, the datum of the angular frequency, corresponding to a wave stationary in time, must be replaced by the datum of a displacement profile whose propagation in the course of time in the direction of the wave will be taken into account. More precisely, one characterises:

- A wave $P$ by the function $u(x,t)=f\left(k\cdot x-{C}_{p}t\right)$;
- A wave $S$ by the function $u(x,t)=f\left(k\cdot x-{C}_{s}t\right)\wedge k$;

With:

- $k$ the unit direction vector;
- $f$ the profile of the wave given along the direction $k$;

**There can be only one occurrence of this keyword in** AFFE_CHAR_MECA **.**

Topological assignment:

```text
◇ GROUP_MA
```


The loading is assigned on the mesh cells of absorbing boundaries concerned by the introduction of the incident wave. If nothing is given, by default, all the mesh cells of the modelling ABSO are concerned.

```text
◇ COOR_SOURCE
```


This list of real parameters makes it possible to determine the phase shift in time due to the passage of the wave, by indicating the coordinates of the entry point ${\mathrm{x}}_{0}$ of the plane-wave loading into the structure. If this parameter is not present, then one will supply the operand FONC_SIGNAL with a nappe of time signals.

```text
◇ COOR_REFLECHI
```


This list of real parameters makes it possible to take the reflected wave into account, activated if one gives the coordinates of the exit point ${\mathrm{x}}_{1}$. In this case, the expression of the profile of the wave taking into account the spatial phase shift linked to the passage of the wave $\dot{f}\left(k.(\mathrm{x}-{\mathrm{x}}_{0})-{C}_{m}t\right)$ then becomes $\dot{f}\left(k.(\mathrm{x}-{\mathrm{x}}_{0})-{C}_{m}t\right)+\dot{f}\left(k.(2{\mathrm{x}}_{1}-{\mathrm{x}}_{0}-\mathrm{x})-{C}_{m}t\right)$ with $m=S$ or $P$.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.01/10000201000004FD000006BF7BB4B4564A10FC81.png)

Figure 9.4-1: Diagram of the propagation of an inclined plane wave in a 2D model

To clarify the use of the operands COOR_SOURCE and COOR_REFLECHI one refers to the diagram in the figure. If one considers a plane wave inclined by an angle $\alpha$ to the vertical, one identifies in an obvious way the first point which comes into contact with the domain of study, that is the point ${\mathrm{x}}_{0}$. This point is considered as the source point of the incident wave and its coordinates represent the input datum of the operand COOR_SOURCE. With the same reasoning, if one wishes to add the taking into account of the reflected wave, one can clearly identify the first point of the free surface which generates the reflected wave, that is the point ${\mathrm{x}}_{1}$. This point is considered as the source point of the reflected wave and its coordinates represent the input datum of the operand COOR_REFLECHI.

Notes concerning the use of the operands COOR_SOURCE and COOR_REFLECHI:

- In the case of a 2D modelling (D_PLAN, for example) one works in the XY frame. In this case one will simply have to enter the X and Y coordinates of the source point of the incident wave (COOR_SOURCE) and, possibly, of the reflected wave (COOR_REFLECHI). The third coordinate, even if it is entered, will not be considered;
- In the case of a vertical propagation, the phase shift of the wave is independent of the coordinates X and Y in 3D (X in 2D). Indeed, the wave front is perfectly horizontal and it is phase-shifted exclusively with respect to the vertical level (Z in 3D and Y in 2D). Consequently, one can enter any coordinate X and Y in 3D (X in 2D) for the source points of the incident wave and of the reflected wave. What matters is to enter correctly the coordinate Z in 3D (Y in 2D) in order to compute correctly the phase shift linked to the vertical propagation.

```text
◆ TYPE_ONDE = ’P’/’S’/’SV’/’SH’
```


Type of the wave (compression or shear).

| $P$ | compression wave |
| --- | --- |
| $\mathit{SV}$ | shear wave (only in 3D) |
| $\mathit{SH}$ | shear wave (only in 3D) |
| $S$ | shear wave (only in 2D) |

The directions of the waves $P$, $\mathit{SV}$ and $\mathit{SH}$ are determined from the vector $V$ supplied by DIRECTION. Namely:

- $P$ is collinear with $V$ and normalised to 1,
- $\mathit{SH}$ is the intersection of the horizontal plane and of the plane normal to $V$, and normalised to 1,
- $\mathit{SV}$ is the vector product of $\mathit{SH}$ and of $P$. There is a case of indeterminacy with this rule when the horizontal plane and the normal plane coincide. In this case, if $V`= :math:`Z$ purely vertical, one imposes $\mathit{SH}$ = $Y$, and $\mathit{SV}$ = $X$.

```text
◆ DIRECTION
```


Direction of propagation of the wave.

```text
◆ FONC_SIGNAL
```


Derivative of the profile of the wave $f(t)$ for $t\in [0,+\infty [$. **Warning:** it is the function corresponding to the velocity $v(t)=\dot{u}(t)$ that the user gives in FONC_SIGNAL. If the operand COOR_SOURCE is absent, one gives a nappe of time signals in velocity parametrised by values of propagation coordinates $k.\mathrm{x}$ for the variable $X$.

```text
◇ DEPL_IMPO
```


Profile of the wave $\mathit{fd}(t)$ for $t\in [0,+\infty [$. **Warning:** it is the function corresponding to the integral of the velocity $v(t)=\dot{u}(t)$ that the user **mandatorily** gives in DEPL_IMPO. This **optional** datum is activated only if one has stiffnesses added on the absorbing boundary, activated by the datum of a non-zero value of the operand LONG_CARA of the keyword ELAS of DEFI_MATERIAU. If the operand COOR_SOURCE is absent, one gives a nappe of time signals in displacement parametrised by values of propagation coordinates $k.\mathrm{x}$ for the variable $X$.

Note concerning the use of this load:
It is strongly advised to orient, by means of the keywords ORIE_PEAU_2D or ORIE_PEAU_3D of MODI_MAILLAGE, the skin mesh cells assigned a modelling of absorbing boundary towards the outside of the domain that they bound.

### 9.6 Keyword FLUX_THM_REP

```text
FLUX_THM_REP = _F(
           ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
             / GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◆ | FLUN = float,
             | FLUN_HYDR1 = float,
             | FLUN_HYDR2 = float,
             | FLUN_FRAC = fonction / formule / nappe,

)
```


The factor keyword FLUX_THM_REP makes it possible to apply to a 2D or 3D continuum domain a heat flux and/or a fluid mass supply (hydraulic flux). The hydraulic fluxes (water and vapour) are defined by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\varphi}^{e}={\rho}_{e}\left(\nabla {P}_{e}-{\rho}_{e}g\right)\cdot n\\ {\varphi}^{v}={\rho}_{v}\left(\nabla {P}_{v}-{\rho}_{v}g\right)\cdot n\end{array}
$$

With the density of the water ${\rho}_{e}$, the density of the vapour ${\rho}_{\nu}$, the pressure of the water ${P}_{e}$ (degree of freedom PRE1 ) and the pressure of the vapour ${P}_{v}$ (degree of freedom PRE2 ).

The heat flux is defined by:

$$
{\varphi}_{T}={\lambda}_{T}\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}+{h}_{m}^{e}{\varphi}^{e}+{h}_{m}^{v}{\varphi}^{v}+{h}_{m}^{a}{\varphi}^{a}
$$

With the mass enthalpies of the water ${h}_{m}^{l}$, of the vapour ${h}_{m}^{v}$, of the air ${h}_{m}^{a}$ and the air flux ${\varphi}^{a}$.

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
  / GROUP_MA = grma,
```


The loading is assigned on these mesh cells. They are necessarily boundary mesh cells (segments in 2D and triangles/quadrangles in 3D). This loading can be used only in (thermo)-hydraulics or in pure hydraulics (THM, THH, THHM, HM or HHM).

```text
◆ | FLUN
```


Value of the heat flux.

```text
◆ | FLUN_HYDR1
```


Value of the hydraulic flux associated with the water constituent.

```text
◆ | FLUN_HYDR2
```


Value of the hydraulic flux associated with the vapour constituent.

```text
◆ | FLUN_FRAC
```


Value of the hydraulic flux injected into a cohesive interface for the HM-XFEM models. This loading is imposed exclusively by means of a function of space on the main elements and not on the boundary elements. For more details concerning the use of this keyword, refer to the documents [R7.02.18](doc:r7.02.18) and [U2.05.02](doc:u2.05.02).

### 9.7 Keyword ECHANGE_THM

```text
ECHANGE_THM = _F(
           ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
             / GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◆ COEF_11 = float,
           ◇ COEF_12 = float,
           ◇ COEF_21 = float,
           ◇ COEF_22 = float,
           ◆ PRE1_EXT = float,
           ◇ PRE2_EXT = float,
)
```


The factor keyword ECHANGE_THM makes it possible, for an unsaturated modelling, to apply to a 2D or 3D continuum domain an exchange condition on the external hydraulic pressures. The hydraulic fluxes (water and air) which result from it are defined by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\left({M}_{w}+{M}_{\text{vp}}\right).n=C11({p}_{1}-{p}_{{}_{1}}^{\text{ext}})-C12({p}_{2}-{p}_{{}_{2}}^{\text{ext}})\\ \left({M}_{\text{ad}}+{M}_{\text{as}}\right).n=C21({p}_{1}-{p}_{{}_{1}}^{\text{ext}})-C22({p}_{2}-{p}_{{}_{2}}^{\text{ext}})\end{array}
$$

With the capillary pressure ${p}_{1}$ (degree of freedom PRE1 ) and the gas pressure ${p}_{2}$ (degree of freedom PRE2 ). ${M}_{w}+{M}_{\text{vp}}$ corresponds to the water flux (sum of the liquid-water flux ${M}_{w}$ and of the vapour flux ${M}_{\text{vp}}$) and ${M}_{\text{ad}}+{M}_{\text{as}}$ corresponds to the air flux (sum of the dissolved-air flux ${M}_{\mathit{ad}}$ and of the dry-air flux ${M}_{\text{as}}$).

```text
◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
  / GROUP_MA = grma,
```


The loading is assigned on these mesh cells. They are necessarily boundary mesh cells (segments in 2D and triangles/quadrangles in 3D). This loading can be used only for the unsaturated modellings of type THH2MS, THH2S, HH2MS or HH2S.

> ◆ PRE1_EXT

Value of the external capillary pressure.

```text
◇ PRE2_EXT
```


Value of the external gas pressure (0 by default).

```text
◆ COEF_11
```


exchange coefficient C11 (cf. eq. ) linking the water flux to the capillary pressure.

```text
◇ COEF_12
```


exchange coefficient C12 (cf. eq. ) linking the water flux to the gas pressure (0 by default).

```text
◇ COEF_21
```


exchange coefficient C21 (cf. eq. ) linking the air flux to the capillary pressure (0 by default).

```text
◇ COEF_22
```


exchange coefficient C22 (cf. eq. ) linking the air flux to the gas pressure (0 by default).

### 9.8 Keyword ECHANGE_THM_HR

```text
ECHANGE_THM_HR = _F(
           ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
             / GROUP_MA = grma,
           ◆ HR_EXT = float,
           ◆ ALPHA = float,
           ◆ PVAP_SAT = float,

)
```


The factor keyword ECHANGE_THM_HR makes it possible, for an unsaturated modelling, to apply to a 2D or 3D continuum domain a mixed exchange condition on the vapour density (proportional to the relative humidity). The water flux is then written:

with $\rho_{vp}$ the vapour density.

Knowing that the relative humidity HR is a function of the density via the saturating vapour density $\rho_{vp}^{sat}$:

The flux is then expressed

$$
\left({M}_{w}+{M}_{\text{vp}}\right).n= \alpha.\rho_{vp}^{sat}\left( HR -{HR}^{ext} \right)
$$

One can express this equation as a function of the capillary pressure (Pc which is an unknown of the model) thanks to the Kelvin relation, that is:

$$
HR = exp\left[ \frac{Pc.M_{H2O}}{\rho_{l}.R.T}\right]
$$

with ${M}_{H2O}$ the molar mass of water, R the perfect-gas constant and T the temperature. Ultimately the expression of the flux becomes:

$$
\left({M}_{w}+{M}_{\text{vp}}\right).n= \alpha.\rho_{vp}.\left( exp\left[ \frac{Pc.M_{H2O}}{\rho_{l}.R.T}\right] -{HR}^{ext} \right)
$$

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
  / GROUP_MA = grma,
```


```text
◆ HR_EXT
```


External relative humidity.

```text
◆ ALPHA
```


Exchange coefficient

> ◆ PVAP_SAT

Saturating vapour pressure

### 9.9 Keyword FORCE_SOL

```text
FORCE_SOL = _F(
           ◆ / GROUP_NO_INTERF = grno,
             / SUPER_MAILLE = ma,
           ◆ | UNITE_RESU_MASS = unit,
             | UNITE_RESU_RIGI = unit,
             | UNITE_RESU_AMOR = unit,
           ◇ UNITE_RESU_FORC = unit,
           ◇ NB_PAS_TRONCATURE = int,
           ◇ TYPE = / "BINAIRE",
                    / "ASCII" (par défaut),
)
```


The factor keyword FORCE_SOL makes it possible to take into account the internal force of a soil domain by using the time evolutions of the contributions in stiffness, mass and damping of the soil impedance. The soil impedance extracted at the initial time instant makes it possible to build, by MACR_ELEM_DYNA, a macro-element representing the behaviour of the soil domain which one adds to the structural model. The dynamic interface of the macro-element is described either by a super-mesh-cell of the model containing both the structure and this macro-element, or by a node group if the physical interface coincides with the modal dynamic interface.

One can also take into account, if it exists, the time evolution of the seismic forces, assigned to this same dynamic interface in the form of a logical unit.

This type of load is taken into account in the command DYNA_NON_LINE. An example of use is supplied in the test case MISS03B [V1.10.122](doc:v1.10.122).

**There can be only one occurrence of this keyword in** AFFE_CHAR_MECA **.**

Topological assignment:

```text
◆ / GROUP_NO_INTERF = grno,
  / SUPER_MAILLE = ma,
```


These operands make it possible to describe the dynamic interface of the macro-element representing the behaviour of the soil domain which one adds to the structural model, either by a super-mesh-cell of the model containing both the structure and this macro-element by the keyword SUPER_MAILLE, or by a node group by the keyword GROUP_NO_INTERF if the physical interface coincides with the modal dynamic interface.

```text
◆ | UNITE_RESU_MASS = unit,
  | UNITE_RESU_RIGI = unit,
  | UNITE_RESU_AMOR = unit,
```


These operands make it possible to introduce the time evolutions of the contributions in stiffness, mass and damping of the soil impedance in the form of logical units.

```text
◇ TYPE = / "BINAIRE",
         / "ASCII" (par défaut)
```


This operand makes it possible to introduce the common writing format ('ASCII' or 'BINAIRE') of the time evolutions of the contributions in stiffness, mass and damping of the soil impedance supplied in the form of logical units by the preceding operands UNITE_RESU_* . The value of the operand must be consistent with the value entered in the operand TYPE_FICHIER_TEMPS during the computation of the option TYPE_RESU='FICHIER_TEMPS' by CALC_MISS.

```text
◇ UNITE_RESU_FORC
```


This operand makes it possible to introduce, if it exists and in the form of a logical unit, the time evolution of the seismic forces, assigned to the dynamic interface of the macro-element representing the behaviour of the soil domain which one adds to the structural model.

```text
◇ NB_PAS_TRONCATURE = int,
```


This operand makes it possible to introduce (if it is present) a truncation of the computation of the contribution to the right-hand side of the soil force in DYNA_NON_LINE. This truncation operates on the summation from the displacement fields stored at the preceding time instants. Following tests, one realises that it is often sufficient to truncate the sum over the last 50 to 100 time steps instead of doing the complete sum over all the preceding stored time instants, which is the default option. It is therefore advised to use as a practical value the integer value of the maximum between 50 and one tenth of the total number of stored time steps.
