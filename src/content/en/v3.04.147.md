# v3.04.147 RCCM09 - POST_RCCM Operator: Calculation of the boot factor

**Summary:**

This static linear thermomechanical test validates the calculation of the boot factor with the POST_RCCM command (option AMORCAGE, EVOLUTION results).

The analytical solution is simple, and allows you to test the post-processing in the sense of the RCC_M.

Test case Model A consists of a set of unit tests, where stresses are directly supplied and not calculated, to verify the correct functioning of the initiation factor calculation.

Model B is the complete resolution (modelization, stress calculation and initiation factor estimation) of an Reference problem presented below.

## 1 Reference problem of Modelling B

### 1.1 Geometry

The Reference problem is flat. A rectangular plate, $W$ wide, with an opening crack of $a$ length is considered.

The starting factor related to the geometric singularity in $P$ is investigated, with the structure being subjected to several transient mechanical loads.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.147/definition_probleme_reference_repere_local.png)

*Definition of Reference problem and local reference*

### 1.2 Material properties

The material properties selected are as follows:

1. Young's modulus: $E=2.E+05\mathrm{MPa}$ ;
2. Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0,3$ .

The characteristics of the RCC-Mcorrespond to the $\text{Ni-Cr-Fe}$ alloy (inconel):

1. parameters of the boot law: A_AMORC = 7091 and B_AMORC = -0.1823;
2. Effective stress parameter: R_AMORC = 1.63;
3. distance d to singularity: D_AMORC = $0,046\mathrm{mm}$ .

The law of initiation is expressed as follows:

$$
\Delta \sigma_{\text{eff}}(d) = A{\text{_AMORC}} \cdot (N_a)^{B{\text{_AMORC}}}
$$

with $N_a$ the number of acceptable cycles and taking the following relationship between magnitude of variation of the actual stresses $\Delta \sigma_{\theta \theta}$ and $\Delta \sigma_{\text{eff}}$ efficiency:

$$
\Delta \sigma_{\text{eff}} = \frac{\Delta \sigma_{\theta \theta}}{1 - \dfrac{R}{R{\text{_AMORC}}}}, \text{ avec } R \text{ rapport de charge.}
$$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The structure is subjected to two transient mechanical loads; all loads consist of a linear force ${F}_{y}$ imposed on segments $\mathrm{AB}$ and $\mathrm{CD}$.

Rigid body movements are blocked at point M.

Transitional 1:

> $t=\mathrm{0s}$     $\mid {F}_{y}\mid =0$     ;     $t=\mathrm{1s}$     $\mid {F}_{y}\mid =100{f}^{0}$

Transitional 2:

> $t=\mathrm{0s}$     $\mid {F}_{y}\mid =10{f}^{0}$     ;     $t=\mathrm{1s}$     $\mid {F}_{y}\mid =110{f}^{0}$     ;     $t=\mathrm{2s}$     $\mid {F}_{y}\mid =50{f}^{0}$

où ${f}^{0}=3{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-1}$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Baseline

#### 2.1.1 Calculation of stress intensity factors

The reference solution for a deep opening crack *a* in a $W$ plate in pure $I$ mode ($\sigma$ force following $y$ axis) is as follows:

${K}_{I}=Y\sigma \sqrt{a}$ , with $Y=1.99-0.41\frac{a}{W}+18.7{(\frac{a}{W})}^{2}-38.48{(\frac{a}{W})}^{3}+53.85{(\frac{a}{W})}^{4}$

*Digital application:* for $15\mathit{mm}$ deep crack ($W=72\mathit{mm}$) and ${f}^{0}$ loading, ${K}_{I}=2,874\mathit{MPa.}\sqrt{\mathit{mm}}$ is obtained.

#### 2.1.2 Calculation of the stresses around the crack bottom

The analytical solution for stress on a $r$ radius circle around the crack bottom, obtained for an infinite-media crack, is as follows:

${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{K}_{I}}{4\sqrt{2\pi r}}(3\cos\frac{\theta}{2}+\cos\frac{3\theta }{2})$

*Digital application:* for ${f}^{0}$ loading and $r={R}_{\mathit{AMORC}}=0,046\mathit{mm}$ radius, we obtain:

$\theta =0$ : ${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=53.5\mathit{MPa}$         ;     $\theta =\pi /2$ : ${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=18.9\mathit{MPa}$

The calculation of the $\mathrm{FA}$ boot factor shall be carried out by an iterative process:

1. identification of the transient of non-zero occurrences leading to a maximum of $\Delta \sigma_{\theta \theta}$;
2. calculation of the associated elemental initiation factor using fatigue law;
3. Re-awareness of the number of occurrences of the combination treated.

Two cases are successively studied with the occurrence numbers of the following two transients: $i=j=1$ and $i=\mathrm{1 };j=2$ . ${N}_{kl-\mathrm{mn}}$ is noted the number of cycles eligible for the combination of $l$ -th time step of $k$ -th transient and $n$ -th time step of $m$ -th transient (calculated as part of $\Delta \sigma_{\theta \theta}$ of this combination and the fatigue law).

**1st case:**

$$
FA_1 = \frac{1}{N_{11-22}} + \frac{1}{N_{12-21}} + \frac{1}{N_{22-23}}
$$

**2nd case:**

$$
FA_2 = \frac{1}{N_{11-22}} + \frac{1}{N_{11-12}} + \frac{1}{N_{12-21}} + \frac{1}{N_{22-23}}
$$

*Digital application:* for Modelling A of this test case, it is assumed that $s°=1$.

${FA}_{1}=2,005{.10}^{-10}$ ;        ${FA}_{2}=2,710{.10}^{-10}$

For Modelling B, the value of $s°$ depends on the $\theta$ angle, and can be calculated using the formula specified in the v3.04.147-calculation-contraints-bottom-crack.

$$
\theta = 0^\circ : \quad \sigma^\circ = 53{,}5 \text{ MPa}, \quad FA_1 = 0{,}607 ; \quad FA_2 = 0{,}819
$$

$$
\theta = 90^\circ : \quad \sigma^\circ = 18{,}9 \text{ MPa}, \quad FA_1 = 2{,}01 \cdot 10^{-3} ; \quad FA_2 = 2{,}72 \cdot 10^{-3}
$$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Model A is only intended to validate the calculation functions of the boot factor in the POST_RCCM operator from the directly given constraints.

There is therefore no mesh Modelling or mechanical calculation.

### 3.2 Test values and results

The calculation of the boot factor shall be tested with:

- different cases of min and max stresses producing the same amplitude of variation (100);
- a combination of 2 transients with different stresses;
- a case of negative values;
- a case with a number of occurrences.

The calculated starting factor values are exactly equal to the analytical reference values.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The objective of Modelling B is to validate the entire process of calculating the boot factor, from mechanical problem resolution to post-processing with POST_RCCM.

The calculation is performed on a 2D model with flat deformations, with a material with linear elastic behaviour.

## 5 Summary of the results

This document presents two tests for the calculation of the boot factor in the POST_RCCM operator for EVOLUTION results.

Model B is particularly useful in testing the whole calculation on a flat cracked structure: calculation of the stress in the structure, extraction of these stresses on circular cutting lines with MACR_LIGN_COUPE and calculation of the boot factor with POST_RCCM. The results are satisfactory, but emphasize the strong influence of the stress value (expressed in the local benchmark) on the calculated use factor.

It is therefore recalled that the need to use sufficiently fine meshes (preferably radiating) in the bottom of the crack to correctly capture the singularity of constraints associated with the presence of geometric singularity.
