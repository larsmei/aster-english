# r7.02.11 Cohesive constitutive laws: CZM_xxx_xxx and load piloting

**Summary:**

The cohesive constitutive laws:

- CZM_EXP_REG (Cohesive Zone Model EXPonential REGularised)
- CZM_LIN_REG (Cohesive Zone Model LINear REGularised)
- CZM_OUV_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model OPENing based on a MIXed formulation)
- CZM_EXP_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model EXPonential based on a MIXed formulation)
- CZM_TAC_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model TAlon-Curnier based on a MIXed formulation)
- CZM_TRA_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model TRApezium based on a MIXed formulation)
- CZM_FAT_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model FATigue based on a MIXed formulation)

make it possible to model the opening of a crack while taking into account a cohesive force between its lips. A surface energy makes it possible to take into account the energy cost of the opening of the crack. The latter is represented by joint finite elements for the first two and by interface finite elements for the following ones.

The cohesive law CZM_ELAS_MIX constitutes a simplified version of the previous models, since the response remains elastic, with or without contact, and possibly with normal or tangential adherence. It makes it possible, among other things, to test a data setup before moving on to the more advanced models.

The constitutive law CZM_LAB_MIX (Cohesive Zone Model Liaison Acier-Béton based on a MIXed formulation) furthermore makes it possible to model the sliding of the steel reinforcement relative to the concrete within a structure.

We present here the form of the energy and of the cohesive law which derives from it as a function of the displacement jump, as well as the internal variables of the model. The existence of potential instabilities during crack propagation may require piloting by elastic prediction, of which the elements specific to these laws are presented.

## 1 General remarks on the cohesive laws

In brittle fracture, in order to remove the problem of infinite stresses at the crack tip, one can introduce cohesive forces which impose a stress initiation criterion (see (3)). The forces act between the particles on either side of the separation plane of the crack (see r7.02.11-fig-fissure). Historically, one of the first to propose this type of approach is Barenblatt (1) at the beginning of the nineteen-sixties. Based on this idea, numerous models have appeared in the literature under the name: “Cohesive zone models”, or CZM in English. For a bibliography one may refer to (5).

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.11/10000000000004A1000002B578234E15122F2FAF.jpg)

*Diagram of a crack*

One considers that the opening of the crack costs an energy proportional to its length. One calls it the surface energy, which one denotes $\Psi$. The displacement field at equilibrium $\boldsymbol{u}$ is obtained by minimising the sum of the elastic energy $\Phi$ of the surface energy $\Psi$ and of the work of the external forces ${W}^{ext}$:

The discontinuity surface is discretised in 2D or 3D by joint finite elements (see [R3.06.09](doc:r3.06.09)) or by interface finite elements (in this case the problem reduces to a search for a saddle point, see [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13)). The displacement jump in the element $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ is a linear function
of the nodal displacements. The cohesive force 1 which acts on the lips of the crack is denoted $\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$; it is defined by the derivative of the surface-energy density with respect to the displacement jump.

One calls a cohesive law a relation between $\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ and the displacement jump $\boldsymbol{\delta}$.

The material parameters of the model are:

- the toughness, or critical surface-energy density, denoted $G_c$ (keyword GC), which expresses the energy cost of cracking;
- and the critical stress at fracture, denoted $\sigma_C$ (keyword SIGC). In the cohesive laws CZM_xxx_REG which are presented in the following section, these parameters may depend on a temperature field.

Furthermore, the laws CZM_xxx_REG depend on two dimensionless numerical parameters: PENA_ADHERENCE and PENA_CONTACT. The first controls the regularisation of the surface energy (or penalisation of the adherence), the second the penalisation of the contact.

The laws CZM_xxx_MIX likewise depend on two numerical parameters, PENA_LAGR and RIGI_GLIS. The first is a penalisation coefficient of the Lagrangian and the second determines the sliding stiffness (only for the law CZM_OUV_MIX). The law dedicated to ductile fracture, CZM_TRA_MIX, has a trapezoidal shape. To define this shape one introduces two additional dimensionless parameters. They are positive and strictly less than 1, named COEF_EXTR, as in extrinsic 2, in order to determine the slope at the origin (infinite or not, at choice) and COEF_PLAS, as in plastic, in order to determine the end of the plastic plateau.

We shall give a detailed definition of the parameters in the following sections.

The irreversibility of the cracking is formulated in the same manner for each of them. Furthermore, these laws are based on the joint model, which requires the regularisation of the energy. ### 2.3 Internal variables

The laws CZM_EXP_REG and CZM_LIN_REG each have nine internal variables. From the point of view of the constitutive law, only the first is strictly speaking an internal variable. The others provide indications on the state of the joint at a given time instant.

**Internal variables identical for the two laws:**

- $V1=\kappa$: jump threshold (greatest norm reached)
- $V2$: dissipation indicator $=0$ if linear regime, $=1$ if dissipative regime
- $V5=V4\times G_{c}$: value of the dissipated energy (see r7.02.11-fig-energies_czm_lin_reg)
- $V7=\delta_{n}$: normal jump
- $V8=\delta_{t}$: tangential jump
- $V9=\delta_{\tau}$: tangential jump (zero in 2D)

**Internal variables for CZM_EXP_REG:**

- $V3$: damage indicator $=0$ if sound material ($\kappa=\kappa_{0}$), $=1$ if damaged material ($\kappa>\kappa_{0}$)
- $V6=\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{+}^{2}\frac{\sigma_{c}}{\kappa}\exp(\frac{-\sigma_{c}}{G_{c}}\kappa)$: value of the current residual energy

**Internal variables for CZM_LIN_REG:**

- $V3$: damage indicator $=0$ if sound material ($\kappa=\kappa_{0}$), $=1$ if damaged material ($\kappa_{0}<\kappa<2G_{c}/\sigma_{c}$), $=2$ if broken material ($\kappa \ge 2G_{c}/\sigma_{c}$)
- $V4 = \kappa\frac{\sigma_c}{2G_c}$: percentage of dissipated energy
- $V6=\frac{1}{2}\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\|_{+}^{2}\sigma_{c}(\frac{1}{\kappa}-\frac{\sigma_{c}}{2G_{c}})$: value of the current residual energy (see r7.02.11-fig-energies_czm_lin_reg)

**Note**

One has seen that, for the needs of the regularisation of the energy, the initial threshold $\kappa_{0}$ (threshold for a sound element) is not zero. This therefore implies that the dissipated energy (internal variable $V5$) is not zero for a sound element. This has no physical reality; this internal variable has a meaning only once the initial threshold has been crossed: $\kappa >\kappa_{0}$.

## 3 Definition of the cohesive laws CZM_xxx_MIX

These laws are intended:

- for the modelling of an elastic interface or for the elementary verification of the data setup of a cracking problem with the law CZM_ELAS_MIX;
- for brittle or quasi-brittle fracture (monotonic loading) for CZM_OUV_MIX, CZM_TAC_MIX and CZM_EXP_MIX. The exponential form of the law CZM_EXP_MIX is well suited to representing the fracture of concretes;
- for ductile fracture for CZM_TRA_MIX;
- for fatigue (cyclic loading) for CZM_FAT_MIX;
- for the degradation of a steel–concrete interface for CZM_LAB_MIX.

In this section one presents the surface energy $\Psi$ as well as the stress vector $\vec{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ for each of them, then the numerical parameters and the internal variables. The irreversibility of the cracking is formulated in the same manner for the first four laws and CZM_LAB_MIX (of Talon-Curnier type with vertical unloading (6)). For CZM_FAT_MIX, it is based on the cumulative opening during the cycles.

The mixed augmented-Lagrangian formulation makes it possible to dispense with the regularisation of the surface energy. This makes it possible to model the perfect adherence of the interface in a sound state. For further information on the interface model (* _INTERFACE and * _INTERFACE_S), the reader may refer to the documentation [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13).

In the case where cracking has not evolved 3 (sound material), the residual contact jump is zero and $\psi$, the cohesive energy density, is defined in the following way:

$$
\psi(\delta_n)=
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{ll}
\sigma_{c}\frac{\delta_n^{2}}{2\delta_{e}}& \text{si }0=\delta_{res}\le \delta_n\le \delta_e\neq 0\\
\sigma_{c}\left(\delta_n-\frac{\delta_e}{2}\right)& \text{si }\delta_e<\delta_n<\delta_p\\

{G}_{c}& \text{si }\delta_n\ge \delta_c
\end{array}
\right.
, \quad
\psi(\delta_T)=\frac{1}{2}C\delta_T^{2}T=t \text{ ou }\tau
$$

*Definition of the evolution of the threshold*

As we have seen in the previous section, the irreversibility is controlled by a threshold denoted $\kappa$. Its initial value is fixed at $\delta_e$; indeed, one considers that there is no dissipation of energy in the adhesion phase.

- In the case where $\delta_e=0$ the evolution of the threshold $\kappa$, from one loading increment to the next, is given classically by: ${\kappa}^{+}=\max({\kappa}^{-},\delta_n)$.
- In the case where $\delta_e\neq 0$ the evolution of the threshold $\kappa$, from one loading increment to the next, is given by ${\kappa}^{+}=\max(\max({\kappa}^{-},\delta_e),\delta_n)$.
- Furthermore, the value of $\kappa$ is bounded by $\delta_c$.

  - GLIS_C: Sliding for which the stress at the interface is maximum
  - ALPHA: Shape parameter of the steel–concrete adherence law for a small sliding
  - BETA: Shape parameter of the steel–concrete adherence law for a large sliding
  - PENA_LAGR: Penalisation of the Lagrangian (optional, 100 by default)
  - CINEMATIQUE: Determines the sliding modes authorised by the interface law. It may be a contact condition ('UNILATERAL'), a sliding in the tangent plane ('GLIS_2D') or a sliding in a single direction ('GLIS_1D'). (optional, 'UNILATERAL' by default)
