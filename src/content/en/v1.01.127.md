# v1.01.127 ZZZZ127 - Validation of keyword LIAISON_MAIL

**Summary:**

This test validates the LIAISON_MAIL keyword of the AFFE_CHAR_MECA and AFFE_CHAR_THER commands in different contexts. This keyword generates linear relationships between the degrees of freedom of the nodes of 2 edges that l-on screws. Programming is validated in 2D and 3D by intercomparison with a similar calculation where the degrees of freedom relationships are directly entered by the LIAISON_DDL keyword.

The following shall also be valid:

- geometric transformation (rotation / translation) applied to one of the edges.
- the restriction of DDL taken into the relationships between degrees of freedom.
- keyword LIAISON_CHAMNO of the AFFE_CHAR_THER command (modelization B).
- the creation of linear relationships in the context of distributed parallelism.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution of modellings A, B, D

In each case, a preliminary calculation is performed with the keyword LIAISON_DDL to introduce the linear relationships between degrees of freedom. This calculation is used as a reference to the calculation with the keyword LIAISON_MAIL that generates these linear relationships.

To obtain the desired linear relationships with LIAISON_MAIL, we write:

**Cas1:**

```text
LIAISON_MAIL:(  NOEUD_2: E, MAILLE_1:Q1,
                CENTRE: B,  ANGL_NAUT: 90., TRAN:( -5. 0.) )
```

This means that the 2 degrees of freedom of node $E$ is eliminated by degrees of freedom of point $E'$ when $E$ is rotated 90 degrees around $B$ and then translated into vector (–5.0). $E'$ is therefore in the middle of $\mathit{CD}$. The displacement vector of $E$ is identified (after 90 degrees rotation) to that of $E'$. Therefore, the 2 equations are obtained:

```text
DX(E) = DY(E’) = 0.5 DY(C) + 0.5 DY(D)

DY(E) = - DX(E’) = -0.5 DX(C) - 0.5 DX(D)
```

**Cas2:**

```text
LIAISON_MAIL:(  MAILLE_2: S1,   MAILLE_1:(Q1,Q2),
                DDL_2:’DNOR’,   DDL_1:’DNOR’,
                CENTRE: B,      ANGL_NAUT: 180.,    TRAN:( +5. +10.) )
```

This means that the normal displacement of nodes $B$ and $E$ (nodes of the $\mathit{S1}$ segment) is eliminated by degrees of freedom of $B'$ and $E'$ points when $B$ and $E$ are rotated 180 degrees around $B$ and then translated into $(+5,+10)$ vectors. $B'$ is therefore in the middle of $\mathit{CF}$ and $E'$ in the middle of $\mathit{DC}$. Normal displacement of B is identified (after 180 degrees rotation) to that of $B'$. The same is done for $B'$. The 2 equations are then obtained:

```text
DY(E) = -DY(E') = -0.5 DY(C) - 0.5 DY(D)

DY(B) = -DY(B') = -0.5 DY(C) - 0.5 DY(F)
```

## 6 Modelling D

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics

[V3.03.101](doc:v3.03.101) SSLS101 - Circular plate placed under uniform pressure.

### 6.2 Mesh characteristics

See test case SSLS101.

### 6.3 Tested Features

Three cases of DDL restriction are tested in this model:

**No restrictions:**

DDL=('DX','DY','DZ','DRX','DRY','DRZ',)

As all DDL are taken into account, the results are identical to the SSLS101 test.

**Restriction on DRZ:**

DDL=('DX','DY','DZ','DRX','DRY',)

Taking into account the load and case studied, removing DRZ from the DDL taken into account does not change the result.

The results are identical to the SSLS101 test.

**Restriction on DRX, DRY , DRZ:**

DDL=('DX','DY','DZ',)

This case is a non-regression test.

The equation verification allows to check that the rotation DDL are not taken into account.
