# r3.08.05 A cable–pulley finite element

**Summary:**

Pulleys play an important role in cable structures such as overhead electric lines. It is therefore useful to know how to model them in a manner which is at once realistic and efficient. The finite-element formulation of a length of cable passing over a pulley is presented: expressions of the internal forces and of the stiffness matrix. The pulley may be fixed or supported by a flexible structure. Its position on the cable at equilibrium is not known a priori: this position is that for which the tension of the cable is the same on either side.

## 2 Introduction

Pulleys are used, during the construction of overhead electric lines, for the cable-stringing operation. The cable in the course of stringing [Figure 2-a] is fixed to one of the anchor supports of the section; it rests on pulleys placed at the bottom of the insulators of the alignment supports and it is retained by a force at the level of the second anchor support. By acting on this force — or by moving its point of application — one adjusts the sag of one of the spans, the one which is the most subject to environmental constraints. The pulleys are then removed and the cable is fixed to the insulators. The length of the cable in the various spans is then fixed, and it determines the subsequent behaviour of the line under static loadings (wind, ice overload) and in the dynamic regime (motion due to the Laplace forces created by the short-circuit currents).

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/10000C8E0000219700001B7EA530030775B01396.svg)

**Figure 2-a: Stringing of a cable in a section with two spans**

The cable–pulley finite element presented here makes it possible to model the stringing operation and therefore to compute, in a natural way, the length of cable in the various spans.

The idea of this finite element came to us some time after the conversation [bib1] and we presented its formulation in [bib2].

## 3 Assumptions and definition of a cable–pulley finite element

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/100004F0000013A300000F315698D3FB29B13029.svg)

**Figure 3-a: Length of cable passing over a pulley — real**

Take a length of cable ${N}_{1}{N}_{2}$ passing over the pulley ${N}_{3}$ [Figure 3-a]. This pulley is not necessarily fixed and may, for example and as is the case in the example of [§8], be mounted at the end of a cable.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/1000048A0000114300000F312EB3018E7CA38B97.svg)

**Figure 3-b: Length of cable passing over a pulley — modelled**

The pulley is assumed to be a point [Figure 3-b]. The following assumptions are made in addition:

- The equilibrium position of ${N}_{3}$ is not known, but it necessarily lies on the length ${N}_{1}$ ${N}_{2}$ deformed from its initial position.

In the modelling of lines, the horizontal motions are of small amplitude and this assumption is generally not restrictive;

- The two strands ${N}_{3}$ ${N}_{1}$ and ${N}_{3}$ ${N}_{2}$ are always rectilinear, like cable elements of the 1st order.

It follows from this assumption that:

${l}_{1}={x}_{1}+{u}_{1}-{x}_{3}-{u}_{3}$                                 eq 3-1

${l}_{2}={x}_{2}+{u}_{2}-{x}_{3}-{u}_{3}$

${l}_{1}=\sqrt{{l}_{1}^{T}{l}_{1}}$                                         eq 3-2

${l}_{2}=\sqrt{{l}_{2}^{T}{l}_{2}}$

The total length of the two strands is:

- in the current position, where the temperature is $T$ :

$l={l}_{1}+{l}_{2}$ ;

- in the initial position, designated by the index $i$ , where the tension is ${N}_{i}$ and the temperature ${T}_{i}$ :

${l}_{i}={({l}_{1})}_{i}+{({l}_{2})}_{i}$ ;

- in an unstressed position, designated by the index 0, where the temperature is ${T}_{0}$ :

${l}_{0}={({l}_{1})}_{0}+{({l}_{2})}_{0}.$

- The pulley is frictionless and therefore the tension is the same in the two strands.

It follows from this assumption that the strain $e$ is also the same, and one takes as its value the measure of the relative elongation of the length with respect to the initial state:

$\varepsilon =\frac{l-{l}_{i}}{{l}_{0}}$                                     eq 3-3

$\varepsilon$ must remain small, so that the section $A$ is considered as constant.

One will note that, within the framework of the finite-element method, the line loads do not prevent the tension from being constant from ${N}_{1}$ to ${N}_{2}$ . These forces are in fact concentrated at the nodes ${N}_{1}$ and ${N}_{2}$ and on the **axis**  of the pulley ${N}_{3}$ .

- The constitutive relation is elastic:

$N=\text{EA}\left[e-\alpha (T-{T}_{i})\right]+{N}_{i}$                         eq 3-4

One calls **cable–pulley finite element** a length of cable ${N}_{1}$ ${N}_{2}$ ${N}_{3}$ satisfying the preceding assumptions.

## 4 Internal forces of a cable–pulley finite element

Let us recall the following definition: the internal forces of a structural finite element are the forces which must be exerted at its nodes in order to maintain it in its current deformed configuration.

In the case of a cable–pulley finite element, the internal forces are deduced immediately from statics. One has indeed [Figure 4-a]:

${F}_{1}=\frac{N}{{l}_{1}}{l}_{1}$                                         eq 4-1

${F}_{2}=\frac{N}{{l}_{2}}{l}_{2}$

and, in order to ensure equilibrium:

${F}_{3}=-({F}_{1}+{F}_{2}).$

${F}_{1}$ and ${F}_{2}$ having the same modulus, ${F}_{3}$ is directed along the bisector of the angle $({N}_{1}{N}_{3}{N}_{2})$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/10000C3C000022BA000017E06399B03E3BB4107A.svg)

**Figure 4-a: Internal forces of a cable–pulley element**

${F}_{3}$ is applied to the **axis**  of the pulley.

The system $F$ of the internal forces of the element is therefore:

$F=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{1}\\ {F}_{2}\\ {F}_{3}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ .

## 5 Stiffness matrix

The stiffness matrix $K$ of the element is the Fréchet derivative of $F$ in the direction of the displacement $\deltau$ of the nodes:

$\deltaf =K\deltau$ .

$K$ is computed by the following classical formula, used intensively in [bib3], [bib4] and [bib5]:

$\deltaf =\underset{\varepsilon \to 0}{\lim}\frac{\delta}{\delta \varepsilon }F(u+\varepsilon \deltau )$ .                            eq 5-1

The detail of the computations is given in [§An1] and the final expression of $K$ is the following:

$K=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{11}+\frac{N}{{l}_{1}}{I}_{3}& {K}_{12}& -{K}_{11}-{K}_{12}-\frac{N}{{l}_{1}}{I}_{3}\\ {K}_{12}^{T}& {K}_{22}+\frac{N}{{l}_{2}}{I}_{3}& -{K}_{22}-{K}_{12}^{T}-\frac{N}{{l}_{2}}{I}_{3}\\ -{K}_{11}-{K}_{12}^{T}-\frac{N}{{l}_{1}}{I}_{3}& -{K}_{22}-{K}_{12}-\frac{N}{{l}_{2}}{I}_{3}& {K}_{11}+{K}_{22}+{K}_{12}+{K}_{12}^{T}+(\frac{1}{{l}_{1}}+\frac{1}{{l}_{2}})N{I}_{3}\end{array}\right]$     eq 5-2

$N$ is given by [eq 3-4] and [eq 3-3] ;

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{11}=(\frac{\text{EA}}{{l}_{0}}-\frac{N}{{l}_{1}})\frac{1}{{l}_{1}^{2}}{l}_{1}{l}_{1}^{T};\\ {K}_{12}=\frac{\text{EA}}{{l}_{0}{l}_{1}{l}_{2}}{l}_{1}{l}_{2}^{T};\\ {K}_{22}=(\frac{\text{EA}}{{l}_{0}}-\frac{N}{{l}_{2}})\frac{1}{{l}_{2}^{2}}{l}_{2}{l}_{2}^{T}.\end{array}$

$K$ is **symmetric** , because of the symmetry of ${K}_{11}$ and of ${K}_{22}$ and of the global symmetry by blocks.

But $K$ depends on the displacements of ${N}_{1},{N}_{2}\text{et}{N}_{3}$ through ${l}_{1},{l}_{2}\text{et}N$ : the cable–pulley finite element is therefore a **non-linear** element.

## 6 Mass matrix

This matrix obviously intervenes only in dynamic problems. The cable–pulley element is used in *Code_Aster*  only for the quasi-static problems of cable stringing [§7].

**Note:**

An example of a dynamic problem comprising a cable–pulley is nevertheless presented in [bib2].

*The mass matrix of the element* ${N}_{1}$ ${N}_{2}$ ${N}_{3}$ *is obtained by assembling the “consistent” mass matrices of the two-node cable elements* ${N}_{3}$ ${N}_{1}$ *and* ${N}_{3}$ ${N}_{2}$ [bib6] and by adding the point mass of the pulley.

*It must be noted that, in the course of a dynamic analysis, this mass matrix must be updated because the lengths* ${l}_{1}$ *and* ${l}_{2}$ *vary.*

## 7 Introduction of the cable–pulley element into Code_Aster

The cable–pulley element is supported by a mesh cell SEG3.

In the command AFFE_MODELE, under the factor keyword AFFE, one must define the arguments of the keywords as follows:

| keyword | GROUP_MA | PHENOMENE | MODELISATION |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| argument | mesh-cell group of cable–pulley | 'MECANIQUE' | 'CABLE_POULIE' |

The constituent material must be elastic.

In the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM, the cable–pulleys are treated as cables.

Since the cable–pulley element is non-linear [§5] and, for the moment, it is used only in statics [§6], it is accessible only through the operator STAT_NON_LINE. Under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT, the arguments of the keywords are the following:

| keyword | GROUP_MA | RELATION | DEFORMATION |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| argument | mesh-cell group of cable–pulley | 'ELAS' | 'GROT_GDEP' |

Finally, the gravity force acting on the nodes ${N}_{1}\text{et}{N}_{2}$ of a cable–pulley element [fig 3‑b] is a follower force, because it depends on the length of the strands ${N}_{3}{N}_{1}\text{et}{N}_{3}{N}_{2}$ . For a structure comprising at least one cable–pulley element, one must specify this (follower) load in STAT_NON_LINE under the factor keyword EXCIT.

An example of application is found in the test SSNL100A [V6.02.100](doc:v6.02.100).

**Note:**

*The cable–pulley element does not make it possible to take into account strain due to temperature.*

## 8 Example of application

This example is that of the stringing of a cable with two spans and with an imposed regulating tension.

In [Figure 7-a], $O$ is the anchorage of the cable on the left-hand anchor support. ${P}_{1}$ is a first pulley placed at the foot of the suspension chain ${P}_{1}C$ , fixed at $C$ to the alignment support. ${P}_{2}$ is a second pulley placed on the right-hand anchor support. $O$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ are, for simplicity, situated on a horizontal line.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/10002D9C000030FD00000BFD694B147970A15849.svg)

**Figure 7-a: Equilibrium of a cable with two spans, subjected to a given regulating tension.** ${\text{OP}}_{1}={\text{P}}_{1}{\text{P}}_{2}=100m$ **;** $w=30N/m$ **;** $E´A=5x{10}^{7}N$ **;** ${N}_{p}=\mathrm{5 000}N$

In the initial position, the cable at rest, assumed weightless, is straight: the centre-line of [Figure 7-a]. For the finite-element modelling, this line is subdivided into:

- ten two-node cable elements between $O$ and ${Q}_{1}$ ;
- one cable–pulley element ${Q}_{1}$ ${R}_{1}$ ${P}_{1}$ ;
- nine cable elements between ${R}_{1}$ and ${Q}_{2}$ ;
- one cable–pulley element ${Q}_{2}$ ${R}_{2}$ ${P}_{2}$ .

The cable is subjected simultaneously to gravity and to the regulating tension ${N}_{p}$ exerted at ${R}_{2}$ . The equilibrium position (solid line of [Figure 7-a]) is reached in 11 iterations by the operator STAT_NON_LINE of *Code_Aster* . The sags are $7.955m$ and $7.867m$ , respectively in the left-hand span and in the right-hand span.

For an **inextensible**  cable of a span of $100m$ , of line weight $30N/m$ and subjected to an end tension of $\mathrm{5 000}N$ , the theoretical sag is $7.941m$ [§An2].

By acting on the regulating tension, one can adjust one of the two sags to a value fixed in advance.

## 9 Conclusion

The cable–pulley finite element presented in this note, of a very simple mechanical formulation, has performances comparable to those of an ordinary cable element. It is very convenient and even indispensable for a realistic modelling of overhead electric lines. It should find other applications, notably in Robotics.

**Computation of the stiffness matrix**

It is shown here how the formula [eq 5-1] applies to the computation of the first three rows of the matrix $K$ [eq 5-2], those which relate to the force ${F}_{1}$ . The other rows are obtained either by permutation of the indices, or by summation of two preceding rows.

The first rows are therefore obtained by computing the derivative:

$\underset{e\to 0}{\lim}\frac{d}{\mathrm{de}}{F}_{1}(u+\text{ed}u)$                                 eq An1-1

and by factoring out the vector $\deltau$ .

According to the relations [eq 4-1], [eq 3-4] and [eq 3-3], one has:

${F}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \delta u)=\left\lbrace \text{EA}\left[\frac{{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon du)+{l}_{2}(u+\varepsilon \delta u)-{l}_{i}}{{l}_{0}}-\alpha (T-{T}_{i})\right]+{N}_{i}\right\rbrace \frac{{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \delta u)}{{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \delta u)}$

with, according to [eq 3-1]:

${l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )={x}_{1}+{u}_{1}+\varepsilon \delta {u}_{1}-{x}_{3}-{u}_{3}-\varepsilon \delta {u}_{3}$

and, according to [eq 3-2]:

${l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )=\sqrt{{l}_{1}^{T}(u+\varepsilon \deltau ){l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )}.$

Consequently:

$\underset{\varepsilon \to 0}{\lim}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon }{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )=\delta {u}_{1}-\delta {u}_{3}$

$\underset{\varepsilon \to 0}{\lim}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon }{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )=\frac{1}{{l}_{1}(u)}{l}_{1}^{T}(u)(\delta {u}_{1}-\delta {u}_{3})$

and by permutation of the indices 1 and 2:

$\underset{\varepsilon \to 0}{\lim}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon }{l}_{2}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )=\frac{1}{{l}_{2}(u)}{l}_{2}^{T}(u)(\delta {u}_{2}-\delta {u}_{3})$

Finally:

$\underset{\varepsilon \to 0}{\lim}\frac{d}{d\varepsilon }{l}_{1}(u+\varepsilon \deltau )=\frac{1}{{l}_{1}^{3}(u)}{l}_{1}^{T}(u)(\delta {u}_{1}-\delta {u}_{3})$

By carrying the preceding expressions into [eq An1-1] and by factoring out the vector $\deltau$ , one easily obtains the first three rows of $K$ .

**Equilibrium shape of an inextensible heavy cable subjected to a given tension at the end**

Take a cable [Figure An2-a] one end of which is fixed at the point $O$ and the other end $P$ of which is subjected to the tension ${N}_{p}$ . $O$ and $P$ are on the same horizontal line and a distance $s$ apart. The line weight is $w$ . One seeks the sag $S$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/1000084200001E9800000A566CA8B9528D4E8E66.svg)

**Figure An2-a: Heavy cable in equilibrium**

One finds in [bib7], p 6, the following well-known formulae:

- equilibrium shape of the cable:

$z(x)=\frac{H}{w}\left[\cosh\frac{w}{H}(\frac{s}{2}-x)-\cosh\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}\right];$                 eq An2-1

- tension:

$N(x)=H\cosh\frac{w}{H}(\frac{s}{2}-x).$                         eq An2-2

$H$ is the horizontal tension, constant along the cable since the distributed external force — the weight — is vertical.

$H$ is computed by [eq An2-2] written at $P$ :

$\cosh\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}-\frac{{\mathrm{2N}}_{p}}{\mathrm{ws}}\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}=0.$

$\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}$ is therefore a root of the transcendental equation:

$\coshX=\frac{{\mathrm{2N}}_{p}}{\mathrm{ws}}X.$

This equation has two roots [Figure An2-b] if:

$\frac{{\mathrm{2N}}_{p}}{\mathrm{ws}}>{p}_{0},$

with:

${p}_{0}=\sinh{x}_{0}$

and:

${x}_{0}=\text{cotanh}{x}_{0}{x}_{0}>0.$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.05/1000062E000018B40000124BDD93E2F6E27C84A1.svg)

**Figure An2-b: Computation of** $\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}$

The smaller root, which corresponds to the greater tension of the cable, is the only useful one. The other root corresponds to a considerable sag of the cable, of the order of magnitude of its span.

$\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}$ having been computed, the sag is deduced from [eq An2-1]:

$S=\frac{s}{2}\frac{\mathrm{2H}}{\mathrm{ws}}(\cosh\frac{\mathrm{ws}}{\mathrm{2H}}-1)$ .
