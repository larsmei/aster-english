# u2.08.05 Propagation of uncertainties and computation of fragility curves

**Summary:**

This document gives the elements for the implementation of numerical Monte Carlo simulations via the distribution of analyses with Code_Aster.

The five main ingredients are:

- the creation of the design of experiments: drawing of the random variables of the $\mathit{Ns}$ study cases;
- the putting into Code_Aster format for the launching of $\mathit{Ns}$ mechanical studies (.distr file);
- the Code_Aster analyses;
- the reading of the analysis results and the writing into a single file;
- the statistical post-processing: computation of classical statistical estimators such as the mean, the standard deviation, etc.; the estimation of the parameters of a log-normal fragility curve (POST_DYNA_ALEA); other post-processings ...

## 1 Introduction

The numerical Monte Carlo method consists in carrying out $\mathit{Ns}$ numerical simulations of the mechanical model in order to evaluate the statistical quantities of the analysis results. The 3 main steps of a propagation of uncertainties by Monte Carlo simulation are:

- The modelling of the uncertainties: generation of a sample of $\text{Ns}$ realisations of the random data at the input of the mechanical model (random parameters and stochastic process),
- The propagation of the uncertainties: computation of the $\text{Ns}$ result quantities corresponding to these data,
- The computation of the statistical estimators of the quantities sought: mean, standard deviation, fractiles …; evaluation of seismic fragility curves.

In the context of a mechanical study with Code_Aster, one can call upon the functionalities for the distribution of parametric studies in order to carry out such a probabilistic analysis. Indeed, in the usual case where the $\mathit{Ns}$ draws and mechanical simulations are independent, one can beforehand establish a design of experiments of the study cases to be launched. One then launches the $\mathit{Ns}$ corresponding studies, and the statistical quantities are computed in post-processing of the results.

The procedure and its implementation with *Code_Aster* are described in this document.

## 2 Monte Carlo simulation with Code_Aster

In the context of the Monte Carlo simulation, the modelling of the uncertainties proceeds by the generation of random variables and possibly of stochastic processes. In what follows, one assumes that the user has chosen laws which characterise well the $m$ uncertain parameters. To do this, one may resort to test results, expert judgement or else the principle of maximum entropy (see [R4.03.05](doc:r4.03.05)). Very often, one can assume that the different sources of uncertainty are independent. One can then carry out random draws according to the $m$ marginal laws of the random variables. In certain cases, one can also include more complex (non-scalar) quantities such as random matrices or else stochastic processes.

One also speaks of a design of experiments to designate the drawing method and the sample which results from it. In *Code_Aster,* the Monte Carlo simulation according to a design of experiments is possible with the help of the distributed-analyses option [U2.08.07](doc:u2.08.07).

### 2.1 Carrying out the design of experiments

All the methods whose draws are independent can be implemented via the distribution of the analyses with *Code_Aster*. In particular, one can call upon random draws according to the Latin hypercube method (Latin Hypercube Sampling – LHS) or quasi Monte Carlo (quasi-random sequences such as the Sobol sequence, …) [bib4, bib5].

- Random variables: the uncertainties on the parameters of the finite-element model (material parameters, values of a clearance, of an elastic stop stiffness, of a Young's modulus, etc.).
- Random matrices: in structural dynamics on a modal basis, the random variables may be the values of the generalised mass, stiffness and damping matrices of the finite-element model. In this approach, one draws these random matrices with the help of the operator GENE_MATR_ALEA [U4.36.06](doc:u4.36.06). In this approach, one models both the model uncertainties and the modelling uncertainties by a probabilistic approach known as “non-parametric”.
- Random excitations: the operator GENE_FONC_ALEA makes it possible to generate stationary random signals from the power spectral density. The operator GENE_ACCE_SEISME makes it possible to generate seismic signals which are non-stationary in amplitude and in frequency content. In other cases, one may have available a database containing signals which model a random physical phenomenon. This may be the case in seismic analysis, where a set of accelerograms measured in situ may be supplied by seismologists.

There exist two possibilities for carrying out the design of experiments: 1) either one creates the sample of the parameter sets beforehand according to the desired drawing method and one stores it in a data file; for this one can use a dedicated software such as OpenTURNS or else the dedicated toolboxes of Python and Matlab 2) or one uses one of the operators of *Code_Aster* to generate a realisation of a stochastic process or of a random matrix. In the case of a transient dynamic analysis, one can thus use random time-dependent loadings generated by *Code_Aster*. One can of course use the two options jointly.

**Note**: If, in the context of a seismic analysis, one has available a sample of $\mathit{Ns}$ accelerograms, one can reduce oneself, for the design of experiments, to a discrete random variable taking values from $1$ to $\mathit{Ns}$ . Each integer designates one of the realisations of the random process (that is to say an accelerogram). Drawing by Latin hypercube then consists in running through the integers from $1$ to $\mathit{Ns}$ .

**Warning**: *The commands* GENE_MATR_ALEA *and* GENE_FONC_ALEA *generate the same sequence of random variables within one and the same execution of Code_Aster. Thus, from one execution to another, a strictly identical command file (the same calls to the commands in the same order with the same arguments) will supply exactly the same results. This is due to the fact that the random-variable generator used by Code_Aster is always initialised to the same value. If one wishes to generate different results from one execution to another, then one must use the keyword* *INIT_ALEA. On the other hand,* *GENE_ACCE_SEISME generates independent random seismic signals.*

### 2.3 Recovering the results of the analyses in a file

The results of the analyses distributed with *Code_Aster* are written in output files which one finds in the $\mathit{Ns}$ directories named ${\mathit{CALC}}_{i}$ *,* $i=1,\mathit{Ns}$ (cf. [U2.08.07](doc:u2.08.07)). In order to proceed to the post-processing with a statistics software (such as OpenTURNS or else the dedicated toolboxes of Python and Matlab, R), one must gather the sample of outputs in a single results file. This can be done very easily via a python script.

## 3 Statistical post-processings

### 3.1 Computation of the statistical quantities

In the context of an approach by Monte Carlo simulation, one is interested not only in a deterministic analysis result, but one wishes to evaluate the mean, the median, the standard deviation, the fractile and other statistics describing the output variable. This makes it possible in particular to associate confidence intervals with the analysis results.

After having recovered the sample of the outputs, one can call upon the preferred software (such as OpenTURNS, the dedicated toolboxes of Python and Matlab, R) to carry out these statistical post-processings. Furthermore, log-normal fragility curves can be evaluated with the help of the operator POST_DYNA_ALEA of *Code_Aster*. This is described in more detail in the following paragraph.

### 3.2 Computation of fragility curves

#### 3.2.1 Definitions

Fragility curves give the conditional probability of failure of a structure or of a component as a function of the level of seismic excitation. The modelling and the propagation of the uncertainties described above also allow the determination of the fragility curves. It is sufficient to introduce a failure criterion and to check at each Monte Carlo analysis whether failure is reached or not.

The main ingredients for the establishment of fragility curves by numerical simulation are the following:

- Determination of the seismic excitation to be considered (database of accelerograms),
- Definition of failure criteria,
- Modelling of the uncertainties and propagation by numerical simulation (Monte Carlo)
- Estimation of the parameters (median and logarithmic standard deviation) of the log-normal fragility curve.

The fragility curve of a component can be defined from the notion of “capacity”. The capacity of a component is the value of the parameter representative of the seismic action from which the component fails. The usual approach consists in modelling the capacity by a random variable following a log-normal law, such that $A={A}_{m}\varepsilon$ , where ${A}_{m}$ is the median capacity and $\varepsilon$ denotes a log-normal random variable of unit median and of logarithmic standard deviation $\beta$ . Also, the capacity $A$ of a component (and ${A}_{m}$ and thus its fragility curve) is characterised by two parameters, which are the median (“median capacity”) and the standard deviation $\beta$ .

Thus, the probability of failure for a given acceleration level $a$ can be written [bib3, bib2]:

log-normal law
${P}_{f}(a)=\underset{0}{\overset{a}{\int}}\underset{}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{p(x)}}\text{dx}=\Phi (\frac{\ln(a/{A}_{m})}{\beta})$

where $\Phi (.)$ denotes the cumulative distribution function of a centred reduced Gaussian random variable. The use of the log-normal model has the advantage of requiring a reduced number of simulations compared with a direct computation of the failure probabilities. Indeed, in a direct computation, without an a priori assumption on the law, one must determine probabilities of rare events (the probabilities in the tail of the distribution), which requires a very large number of Monte Carlo simulations. In what follows, we present two methods, estimation by maximum likelihood and linear regression, for evaluating these two parameters.

If the damage or the failure of a structure are characterised by a continuous variable of interest $Y$ , accompanied by a threshold ${Y}_{s}$ , one can express the probability of failure (of the damage level) for an earthquake of level $a$ as

${P}_{f}(a)=P(Y>{Y}_{s}\mid a)$

Classical damage variables $Y$ for a concrete structure are the drift (differential displacement between two storeys of a building) or else the drop in the first natural frequencies. The notations introduced here will be used for the evaluation of a fragility curve by linear regression (§3.2.2).

#### 3.2.2 Evaluation of the parameters by maximum likelihood

In what follows, one considers that the peak acceleration has been chosen to characterise the level of seismic excitation and therefore the capacity. The procedure followed then consists in modelling the result of the numerical experiments by a Bernoulli random variable $X$ . Indeed, for each numerical simulation $i$ , one has two possible outcomes: either one has reached the critical level and one has failure (${x}_{i}=1$ ) or one does not have failure (${x}_{i}=0$ ). Likewise, for each simulation, one can determine the value of the peak acceleration ${a}_{i}$ . The estimation of the parameters of a fragility curve can be done by the maximum-likelihood method. The likelihood function to be maximised for this problem is written:

$L=\underset{i=1}{\overset{N}{\Pi}}({P}_{f\mid a}({a}_{i})){}^{{x}_{i}}{(1-{P}_{f\mid a}({a}_{i}))}^{1-{x}_{i}}$

In this expression, the realisation ${x}_{i}$ of $X$ therefore takes the value 1 if one has failure or 0 if there is no failure for the loading (the peak acceleration) ${a}_{i}$ . These events occur with the probability ${P}_{f\mid a}$ given by expression (1). The estimates of the parameters $\beta$ and ${A}_{m}$ are those which minimise $-\ln(L)$ :

$({\beta}_{C}^{e},{A}_{m}^{e})=\text{arg}\underset{\beta ,{A}_{m}}{\min}\left[-\ln(L)\right]$ .

This step can be implemented in post-processing of the analysis results. In the *Code_Aster* command file, one must, for each simulation, check whether one has failure or not. By way of example, if the failure criterion consists of an allowable stress, one checks whether the maximum stress computed exceeds this stress.

Then one has the results of the simulations written, with the help of CREA_TABLE, in a results table. This table must contain two columns, PARA_NOCI and DEFA, which give respectively the value ${a}_{i}$ (the PGA value or any other indicator) and the value ${x}_{i}$ ($1$ or $0$ ).

TAB1=CREA_TABLE(LISTE=(

_F(PARA='PARA_NOCI',LISTE_R = PGA,),

_F(PARA='DEFA',LISTE_I =xi ,),

),);

**Note**: If one supplies a column DEMANDE instead of DEFA, then this column must contain the values of the seismic demand (variable of interest describing the failure or the damage, for example a stress or a maximum displacement).

TAB2=CREA_TABLE(LISTE=(

_F(PARA='PARA_NOCI',LISTE_R = PGA,),

_F(PARA='DEMANDE',LISTE_I =demande ,),

),);

The keyword SEUIL must then be supplied in POST_DYNA_ALEA in order to determine the sequence of the ${x}_{i}$ .

The operator POST_DYNA_ALEA [U4.84.04](doc:u4.84.04) makes it possible to estimate the parameters of the log-normal fragility curve from the table TAB1 via the keyword FRAGILITE:

TAB_POST=POST_DYNA_ALEA( FRAGILITE=(_F(TABL_RESU=TAB1,

AM_INI =0.3 ,

BETA_INI=0.1 ,

METHODE= 'EMV',

FRACTILE = (0.0,0.05,0.5,0.95,1.0),

NB_TIRAGE =Ns,

),),

TITRE = 'courbe 1',

INFO=2,);

One gives initial values of the parameters ${A}_{m}$ and $\beta$ to be estimated (starting point for the optimisation algorithm) via AM_INI and BETA_INI. If one supplies the keyword FRACTILE, one also determines the fractiles (confidence intervals) of the fragility curve by a resampling method (known as the “bootstrap” method). Resampling consists in drawing new samples from the values of the original sample. These draws are carried out with replacement; see [bib1] for more details. Then, one determines the parameters of the fragility curve for each “bootstrap” sample, which gives a sample of fragility curves. One then determines the fractiles for the sample of fragility curves obtained. In general, one draws as many “bootstrap” samples as one has values in the original sample. It is nevertheless possible to work with a smaller number of draws by supplying NB_TIRAGE (by default the number of draws corresponds to the size of the original sample, here $\mathit{Ns}$ ). In the example above, one therefore determines the envelope curves (fractiles $1.0$ and $0.0$ ) as well as the median $(0.5)$ and the fractiles at $0.05$ and $0.95$ .

#### 3.2.3 Evaluation by linear regression

Regression is a very widespread method for determining the parameters of a log-normal fragility curve. It is practised above all in the United States in order to evaluate the fragility of civil structures such as roads, engineering structures and public buildings.

In this approach, one relates the variable of interest $Y$ (the drift, for example) to the value of the seismic indicator $a$ (for example the PGA) via a relation of the type:

$Y=b{a}^{c}\eta$

In the expression above, $\eta$ is a log-normal random variable which one can define from the centred reduced normal variable $U$ : $\eta =\exp(\beta U)$ , and the variables $b$ and $c$ are the parameters of the model. These latter are obtained by a linear regression with the help of the following expression:

$\ln(Y)=\ln(b)+c\ln(a)+ϵ$

where $\epsilon$ is a centred normal (Gaussian) random variable of standard deviation $\sigma$ such that $ϵ=\sigma U$ . With these notations, the probability of failure is written as a conditional probability, as a function of the seismic level $a$ and for a threshold ${Y}_{s}$ :

${P}_{f}(a)=P(Y>{Y}_{s}\mid a)=1-P(Y<{Y}_{s}\mid a)$ .

Taking into account the log-normal expression of the variable $Y$ , one obtains the analytical expression of the fragility curve:

${P}_{f}(a)=\Phi \left(\frac{\ln(b{a}^{c}/{Y}_{s})}{\sigma}\right)$

With the help of the regression line, one can express the median threshold ${Y}_{s}$ from the median capacity as ${Y}_{s}=b{a}_{s}^{c}\eta$ . If one considers a “best-estimate” critical threshold which one identifies with the median value in the uncertainty procedure, one can write ${A}_{m}={a}_{s}$ . One thus arrives at the expression of the log-normal fragility curve:

${P}_{f}(a)=\Phi \left(\frac{\ln(a/{A}_{m})}{\beta}\right)$

where $\beta =\sigma /c$ with the standard deviation of the regression error $\sigma$ . In practice, the value of the median capacity can be deduced directly from the regression curve and is expressed from the parameters of the latter as

${A}_{m}=\exp\left[\frac{\ln({Y}_{s}/b)}{c}\right]$

It is important to emphasise that this model makes it possible to take into account the heteroscedastic behaviour (the variance of $Y$ depends on the parameters of the model and is not constant).

The operator POST_DYNA_ALEA [U4.84.04](doc:u4.84.04) makes it possible to estimate the parameters of the log-normal fragility curve by regression from the table TAB2 of §3.2.2 via the keyword FRAGILITE:

TAB_POST=POST_DYNA_ALEA( FRAGILITE=(_F(TABL_RESU=TAB2,

METHODE= 'REGRESSION',

SEUIL=0.05,

),),

TITRE = 'courbe 2',

INFO=2,);

Experience shows that the dispersion (variability) of $Y$ increases with the seismic level. On the other hand, unlike the maximum-likelihood method, the logarithmic standard deviation does not depend on the threshold.
