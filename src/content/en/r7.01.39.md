# r7.01.39 Rankine law

**Summary:**

This document presents the method of solution of the Rankine law.

## 1 Introduction

The Rankine law is formulated in terms of principal stresses. This formulation assumes the isotropy of the material (see [1] and [2]). Indeed, this condition is necessary in order to guarantee that the radial-return method preserves the principal directions. Its interest lies in the fact that it simplifies the writing of the equations and thereby allows very efficient solution methods (because they are quasi-analytical).

The elastic constitutive behaviour is purely linear.

The yield surface is characterised by three planes in the space of the principal stresses ${\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}\ge {\sigma}_{3}$ . Each of these planes is characterised by an equation of the type:

$$
{R}_{i=\mathrm{1,2,3}}\left({\sigma}^{+}\right)={\sigma}_{i}^{+}-{\sigma}_{t}=0
$$

Where ${\sigma}_{t}$ is the single material datum and characterises the tensile limit of the material. The law is associated.

**Notations**

| ${\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}\ge {\sigma}_{3}$ | Principal stresses (in this order) |
| --- | --- |
| $E$ | Young's modulus |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio |
| $K=\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu )}$ | Elastic compressibility modulus |
| $G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$ | Elastic shear modulus |
| ${\sigma}_{t}$ | Tensile limit of the material |
| $p=\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}=\frac{\mathit{trace}\left(\sigma \right)}{3}$ | Mean stress |
| $p<0$ | Sign convention for the stress in compression |
| ${\sigma}^{e}$ | Elastic-prediction tensor of the stresses |
| $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{p}$ | Tensors of the total strain, of the elastic strain and plastic-strain increment |
| ${\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\mathit{trace}\left({\epsilon}^{p}\right)$ | Increment of the volumetric plastic strain |
| ${\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}={\epsilon}^{p}-\frac{{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}1$ | Increment of the deviatoric plastic strain |
| ${\epsilon}^{p}=\Vert {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}\Vert =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}:{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}}$ | Norm of the deviatoric plastic-strain increment, or equivalent-strain increment |

## 2 Local integration of the Rankine law

The plastic strain rate is given by means of Koiter's formula:

$$
d{\epsilon}^{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\mu}_{j}\frac{\partial {R}_{j}}{\partial \sigma }=\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\mu}_{j}{n}_{j}
$$

Where $d{\mu}_{i}\ge 0$ are the plastic multipliers associated with the mechanisms $i$ , and:

$$
\frac{\partial {R}_{i}}{\partial {\sigma}_{j}}={n}_{ij}={\delta}_{ij}
$$

And where $m$ characterises the number of active mechanisms, equal to one, two or three according to the following situations:

- the final stress lies inside the yield surface, the point is regular and $m=1$ ;
- the final stress lies on an edge of the cone, the point is singular and $m=2$ ;
- the final stress lies neither inside the yield surface nor on an edge. It is then projected onto the apex of the cone, the point is singular and $m=3$ ;

The final stress ${\sigma}^{+}$ is computed from an elastic prediction denoted ${\sigma}^{e}$ and from a correction $\Delta {\sigma}_{C}=C.\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}$  such that:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}^{e}-\Delta {\sigma}_{C}={\sigma}^{e}-\sum_{j=1}^{m}\Delta {\mu}_{j}C.{n}_{j}
$$

The plastic multipliers $d{\mu}_{j}$ are computed by injecting equation () into equation (), which gives:

$$
\sum_{j=1}^{m}d{\mu}_{j}{\left(C.{n}_{j}\right)}_{i}={\sigma}_{i}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}
$$

In what follows, the expressions corresponding to the various situations mentioned above are detailed.

The volumetric and equivalent plastic strains are written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}={\epsilon}_{1}+{\epsilon}_{2}+{\epsilon}_{3}\\ {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}=\Vert {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}\Vert =\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}:{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}}\end{array}
$$

With ${\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}={\epsilon}^{p}-\frac{{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}1$ .

### 2.1 Case in which a single mechanism is active

The mechanism ${R}_{1}$ is active. Whence:

$$
d\mu \left(K+\frac{4}{3}G\right)={R}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}
$$

With ${\sigma}_{t}$ the tensile limit of the material. Whence:

$$
d\mu =\frac{{⟨{R}_{1}({\sigma}^{e})⟩}_{+}}{K+\frac{4}{3}G}
$$

Where the operator ${⟨\text{}⟩}_{+}$ denotes the positive part of the associated quantity (grandeur).

The plastic strain is written:

$$
\delta {\epsilon}^{p}=\delta \mu \frac{\partial {R}_{1}}{\partial \sigma }=\delta \mu \left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)
$$

And the correction $\Delta {\sigma}_{C}$ is written:

$$
\Delta {\sigma}_{C}=C.\delta {\epsilon}^{p}=\delta \mu C.\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)=\delta \mu \left(\begin{array}{c}K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\end{array}\right)
$$

The volumetric and equivalent plastic strains are written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\delta {\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\delta \mu \\ {\delta \tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}.\frac{1}{9}\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta \mu \\ -\delta \mu \\ -\delta \mu \end{array}\right).\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta \mu \\ -\delta \mu \\ -\delta \mu \end{array}\right)}=\frac{2}{3}\delta \mu \end{array}
$$

### 2.2 Case in which two mechanisms are active

The mechanisms ${R}_{1}$ and ${R}_{2}$ are active. Whence:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\mu}_{1}\underset{A}{\underset{⏟}{\left(K+\frac{4}{3}G\right)}}+d{\mu}_{2}\underset{B}{\underset{⏟}{\left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)}}={R}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}\\ d{\mu}_{1}\left(K-\frac{2}{3}G\right)+d{\mu}_{2}\left(K+\frac{4}{3}G\right)={R}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{2}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}\end{array}
$$

Setting $det$ the determinant of the system, one has:

$$
det={A}^{2}-{B}^{2}=4G\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)>0
$$

The determinant being always strictly zero, there always exists a solution which is written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\mu}_{1}=\frac{{⟨{\mathit{AR}}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)-{\mathit{BR}}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)⟩}_{+}}{det}\\ d{\mu}_{2}=\frac{{⟨{\mathit{AR}}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)-{\mathit{BR}}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)⟩}_{+}}{det}\end{array}
$$

The plastic strain is written:

$$
\delta {\epsilon}^{P}=\delta {\mu}_{1}\frac{\partial {R}_{1}}{\partial \sigma }+\delta {\mu}_{2}\frac{\partial {R}_{2}}{\partial \sigma }=\delta {\mu}_{1}\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)+\delta {\mu}_{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 1\\ 0\end{array}\right)
$$

And the correction $\Delta {\sigma}_{C}$ is written:

$$
\Delta {\sigma}_{C}=C.\delta {\epsilon}^{P}=\delta {\mu}_{1}C.\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)+\delta {\mu}_{2}C.\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 1\\ 0\end{array}\right)=\delta {\mu}_{1}\left(\begin{array}{c}K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\end{array}\right)+\delta {\mu}_{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\end{array}\right)
$$

The volumetric and equivalent plastic strains are written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\delta {\epsilon}_{v}^{P}=\delta {\mu}_{1}+\delta {\mu}_{2}\\ {\delta \tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}.\frac{1}{9}\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}\\ -\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\end{array}\right).\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}\\ -\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\end{array}\right)}=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\delta {\mu}_{1}^{2}+\delta {\mu}_{2}^{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}\delta {\mu}_{2}}\end{array}
$$

### 2.3 Case of the projection onto the apex of the cone

In this case, one has directly:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{p}^{+}={p}^{e}-K\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}^{p}={\sigma}_{t}\\ {\sigma}^{+}={p}^{+}1\end{array}
$$

One obtains directly the volumetric plastic strain:

$$
\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}^{p}=\frac{{p}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}}{K}
$$

The mechanisms ${R}_{1}$ , ${R}_{2}$ and ${R}_{3}$ are active. Whence:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\mu}_{1}A+\left(d{\mu}_{2}+d{\mu}_{3}\right)B={R}_{1}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{1}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}\\ d{\mu}_{2}A+\left(d{\mu}_{1}+d{\mu}_{3}\right)B={R}_{2}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{2}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}\\ d{\mu}_{3}A+\left(d{\mu}_{1}+d{\mu}_{2}\right)B={R}_{3}\left({\sigma}^{e}\right)={\sigma}_{3}^{e}-{\sigma}_{t}\end{array}
$$

One shows that after a few algebraic manipulations, one obtains:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\mu}_{1}=\frac{\left(A+B\right){R}_{1}-B\left({R}_{2}+{R}_{3}\right)}{6\mathit{KG}}\\ d{\mu}_{2}=\frac{\left(A+B\right){R}_{2}-B\left({R}_{1}+{R}_{3}\right)}{6\mathit{KG}}\\ d{\mu}_{3}=\frac{\left(A+B\right){R}_{3}-B\left({R}_{1}+{R}_{2}\right)}{6\mathit{KG}}\end{array}
$$

The equivalent plastic strain is then written:

$$
{\delta e}^{P}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}.\frac{1}{9}\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{3}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{3}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{3}-\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\end{array}\right).\left(\begin{array}{c}2\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{3}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{3}\\ 2\delta {\mu}_{3}-\delta {\mu}_{1}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\end{array}\right)}
{\delta \tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\delta {\mu}_{1}^{2}+\delta {\mu}_{2}^{2}+\delta {\mu}_{3}^{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}\delta {\mu}_{2}-\delta {\mu}_{1}\delta {\mu}_{3}-\delta {\mu}_{2}\delta {\mu}_{3}}
$$

### 2.4 Internal variables of the model

The internal variables of the model are nine in number:

- V1 is the volumetric plastic strain ${\epsilon}_{v}^{p}$ ;
- V2 is the equivalent (deviatoric) plastic strain ${\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}=\Vert {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{p}\Vert$ ;
- V3 is the plasticity indicator;
- V4 to V9 are the six components of the plastic-strain tensor ${\epsilon}^{p}$ .

## 3 Expression of the tangent matrix in the principal basis

### 3.1 Case in which a single mechanism is active

One has:

$$
d{\sigma}^{+}=C.\delta \epsilon -\frac{\delta {\sigma}_{1}}{K+\frac{4}{3}G}\left(\begin{array}{c}K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G\end{array}\right)
$$

Setting $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}A=K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ B=K-\frac{2}{3}G\end{array}$ , one has:

$$
\frac{\delta {\sigma}_{1}}{A}\left(\begin{array}{c}A\\ B\\ B\end{array}\right)=\frac{1}{A}\left(\begin{array}{c}A\\ B\\ B\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\end{array}\right).C.\delta \epsilon =\frac{1}{A}\left(\begin{array}{c}A\\ B\\ B\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\end{array}\right).\delta \epsilon =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\\ B& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}\\ B& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}\end{array}\right].\delta \epsilon
$$

That is, the following expression of the tangent matrix $T$ (see for the elasticity matrix $C$ ):

$$
T=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{+}}{\delta \epsilon }=C-\left[\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\\ B& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}\\ B& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{A}^{2}-{B}^{2}}{A}& \frac{B\left(A-B\right)}{A}\\ 0& \frac{B\left(A-B\right)}{A}& \frac{{A}^{2}-{B}^{2}}{A}\end{array}\right]
$$

### 3.2 Case in which two mechanisms are active

One has:

$$
d{\sigma}^{+}=C.\delta \epsilon -\underset{\delta \Delta {\sigma}_{C}}{\underset{⏟}{\left(\frac{A\delta {\sigma}_{1}-B\delta {\sigma}_{2}}{det}\left(\begin{array}{c}A\\ B\\ B\end{array}\right)+\frac{A\delta {\sigma}_{2}-B\delta {\sigma}_{1}}{det}\left(\begin{array}{c}B\\ A\\ B\end{array}\right)\right)}}
$$

One obtains:

$$
\delta \Delta {\sigma}_{C}=\delta {\sigma}_{1}\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ \frac{B}{A+B}\end{array}\right)+\delta {\sigma}_{2}\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 1\\ \frac{B}{A+B}\end{array}\right)=\left[\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\\ \frac{B}{A+B}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\end{array}\right)+\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 1\\ \frac{B}{A+B}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{ccc}B& A& B\end{array}\right)\right].\delta \epsilon
$$

That is:

$$
\delta \Delta {\sigma}_{C}=\left[\left[\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\\ 0& 0& 0\\ \frac{\mathit{AB}}{A+B}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A+B}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A+B}\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ B& A& B\\ \frac{{B}^{2}}{A+B}& \frac{\mathit{AB}}{A+B}& \frac{{B}^{2}}{A+B}\end{array}\right]\right].\delta \epsilon
\delta \Delta {\sigma}_{C}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\\ B& A& B\\ B& B& \frac{2{B}^{2}}{A+B}\end{array}\right].\delta \epsilon
$$

That is, the following expression of the tangent matrix $T$ :

$$
T=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{+}}{\delta \epsilon }=C-\left[\begin{array}{ccc}A& B& B\\ B& A& B\\ B& B& \frac{2{B}^{2}}{A+B}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{A\left(A+B\right)-2{B}^{2}}{A+B}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{3\mathit{KG}}{K+\frac{G}{3}}\end{array}\right]
$$

### 3.3 Case of the projection onto the apex of the cone

One shows that one has trivially $T=0$ .

## 4 Expression of the tangent matrix in the global basis

Paragraph **§** 3 makes it possible to construct the consistent tangent matrix in the principal basis, denoted $T$ . This matrix must now be brought back into the global (Cartesian) basis, which will be denoted $\bar{T}$ .

### 4.1 Preliminary note

It should be noted that the construction of this consistent tangent matrix is a crucial stage both for the robustness and for the performance of the algorithm:

- Firstly, it is perfectly well known that such a matrix allows a quadratic rate of convergence for the Newton process;
- Secondly, this matrix accounts for the rotation of the principal directions during an increment. Without it, the formulation of the Rankine law in terms of principal stresses described in paragraph **§** 1 would not be complete, since the principal stresses, kept fixed during the local integration of the law ( **§** 2 ), could not rotate at the global level of the structure.

In this paragraph, the method making it possible to construct $\bar{T}$ from $T$ is described in detail. In the appendix (§ 5), the elements of theory necessary for the transformation of the tensor quantities (grandeur) from one basis to the other will be found.

### 4.2 Application to the case of Rankine

The transposition of the formulae of appendix § 5 to the numerical implementation deserves a few clarifications. One first has the following correspondences:

- $X={\widehat{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{pred}}$ and ${x}_{\alpha}={\epsilon}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ ;
- $Y={\widehat{\sigma}}^{+}$ and ${y}_{\alpha}={\sigma}_{\alpha}^{+}$ ;
- ${E}_{\alpha}={\widehat{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}\otimes {\widehat{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ ;
- ${\left(T\right)}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}$ is the consistent tangent matrix in the principal basis computed in paragraph **§** 3 ;

The notation ${}^{\mathit{pred}}$ indicates that one is working with "predicted" quantities (grandeur) given as input by the Newton process, the notation ${}^{+}$ with quantities (grandeur) coming from the local solution of the constitutive law, and the notation $\widehat{\rbrace}$ with the Voigt basis. It will be noted that the predicted principal directions ${\widehat{d}}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{pred}}$ are frozen during the local solution, which is consistent with the isotropy assumption adopted (see the explanations of paragraph **§** 5 ).

Having all this information at the end of the local solution of the constitutive law, one deduces from it the consistent tangent matrix $\bar{T}=\bar{D}$ expressed in the projection basis $\bar{b}$ defined in paragraph **§** 6 **:**

- Equation () in the two-dimensional case in plane stress (C_PLAN);
- Equation () in the two-dimensional case in plane strain (D_PLAN) or axisymmetric (AXIS);
- Equation () in the three-dimensional case (3D);

The second important piece of information concerns the writing convention of the various tensors. Indeed, for the sake of generality, the notation used for the tensors throughout paragraph **§** 4 is the classical notation, bringing in tensors up to order four. This writing is unsuitable for the numerical solution, in which one prefers to use condensed notations made possible by the fact that one is working with symmetric tensors of order two (stresses and strains always are). Two forms of condensed notations are distinguished, corresponding to two projection bases (see **§** 6 ):

- The orthonormal basis $\bar{b}$ of the symmetric tensors of order two. It is in this basis that the stresses and the strains are given at the input and at the output of the local solution of the constitutive behaviour;
- The so-called Voigt basis $\widehat{b}$ , much more convenient to use during the local numerical solution of the constitutive behaviour because it avoids having to handle coefficients in $\sqrt{2}$ during the matrix operations;

### 4.3 Solution scheme of the constitutive law

Inputs:

- Stresses expressed in the global basis ${\overline{\sigma}}^{-}$ ;
- Strain increment expressed in the global basis $\Delta \overline{\epsilon}$ ;

Computations:

- One evaluates the elastic stresses ${\widehat{\sigma}}^{e}$ and the elastic strains ${\widehat{\epsilon}}^{e}$ in the Voigt basis;
- One transforms them into the principal basis, one obtains ${\sigma}^{e}$ and ${\epsilon}^{e}$ ;
- One integrates the constitutive law and one obtains the plastic-strain increment $\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}$ and the stresses in the Voigt basis ${\widehat{\sigma}}^{+}$ ;
- Computation of the tangent matrix in the principal basis:

  - $\left({\widehat{\sigma}}^{+},{\widehat{\epsilon}}^{e}\right)\to {\widehat{E}}_{\alpha}$ then ${T}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{{\partial \sigma }_{\alpha}}{{\partial \epsilon }_{\beta}}$
- Transfer of the tangent matrix into the Voigt basis:

  - $\left({\widehat{\sigma}}^{+},{\widehat{\epsilon}}^{e},{\widehat{E}}_{\alpha},T\right)\to {\widehat{T}}_{ij}=\frac{{\partial \sigma }_{i}}{{\partial \epsilon }_{j}}$
- Transfer of the tangent matrix into the global basis:

  - $\widehat{T}\to \overline{T}$

## 5 Appendix: a few results on isotropic symmetric tensors of order two

### 5.1 Definition of an isotropic symmetric tensor

One defines by ${S}^{3}$ the space of the symmetric tensors of order two in the vector space of dimension $n=3$ , and the tensors $Y\in {S}^{3}$ and $X\in {S}^{3}$ such that:

$$
Y(X):{S}^{3}\to {S}^{3}
$$

The tensor function $Y\left(X\right)$ is said to be isotropic if:

$$
R.Y(X).{R}^{t}=Y(R.X.{R}^{t})
$$

Whatever the rotation $R$ . The isotropy assumption implies that $Y$ and $X$ are coaxial, that is to say that they possess the same principal directions ${d}_{\alpha =1,2,3}$ . One denotes:

$$
\begin{array}{c}X=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{x}_{\alpha}\left({d}_{\alpha}\otimes {d}_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{x}_{\alpha}{E}_{\alpha}\\ Y\left(X\right)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{y}_{\alpha}\left({d}_{\alpha}\otimes {d}_{\alpha}\right)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}{y}_{\alpha}{E}_{\alpha}\end{array}
$$

Where ${y}_{\alpha}={y}_{\alpha}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},{x}_{3})$ and ${x}_{\alpha}$ represent the eigenvalues of $Y$ and $X$ , respectively.

### 5.2 Derivative of an isotropic tensor function of order two

It is assumed that the isotropic tensor function $Y\left(X\right)$ is differentiable with respect to $X$ , and its derivative $D$ is defined such that:

$$
D\left(X\right)\stackrel{\scriptscriptstyle\mathrm{def}}{=}\frac{dY\left(X\right)}{dX}
$$

Applied to equation (), one obtains the following expression:

$$
D(X)=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}({E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{y}_{\alpha}}{dX}+{y}_{\alpha}\frac{d{E}_{\alpha}}{dX})=\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}({y}_{\alpha}\frac{d{E}_{\alpha}}{dX}+\sum_{\beta =1}^{3}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes \frac{d{x}_{\beta}}{dX})
$$

#### 5.2.1 Two-dimensional case of plane-stress type (C_PLAN)

In dimension two (C_PLAN case), the characteristic equation $det\left(X-{x}_{\alpha}I\right)=0$ gives a quadratic equation of the eigenvalues ${x}_{\alpha =1,2}$ of $X$ of the following type:

$$
{x}_{\alpha}^{2}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}+{I}_{2}=0
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{I}_{1}=\mathit{trace}\left(X\right)={X}_{11}+{X}_{22}\\ {I}_{2}=det\left(X\right)={X}_{11}{X}_{22}-{X}_{12}{X}_{21}\end{array}
$$

The solution of the spectral problem readily gives the following solutions for the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{x}_{1}=\frac{{I}_{1}+\sqrt{{I}_{1}^{2}-4{I}_{2}}}{2}\\ {x}_{2}=\frac{{I}_{1}-\sqrt{{I}_{1}^{2}-4{I}_{2}}}{2}\end{array}
$$

And the eigenvectors, taking into account the multiplicity of the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}{E}_{\alpha}=\frac{X+({x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1})I}{2{x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1}}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\mathrm{\ne }{x}_{2}\\ {E}_{1}=I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}\end{array}
$$

In particular, Carlson and Hoger show that if ${x}_{1}\mathrm{\ne }{x}_{2}$ , one has:

$$
\frac{d{x}_{\alpha}}{dX}={E}_{\alpha}
$$

Using equations (), () and () in (), one obtains the expression of the derivative $D(X)$ , taking into account the multiplicity of the eigenvalues:

$$
D\left(X\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{{y}_{1}-{y}_{2}}{{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}}\left[{I}_{S}-{E}_{1}\otimes {E}_{1}-{E}_{2}\otimes {E}_{2}\right]+\sum_{\alpha =1}^{2}\sum_{\beta =1}^{2}\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}{E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{\beta}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\\ \left(\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}\right){I}_{S}+\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}\end{array}
$$

With the identity matrix $I$ :

$$
{(I)}_{ijkl}={\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}
$$

The transposition matrix ${({I}_{t})}_{ijkl}={\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}}$ and the symmetrisation matrix ${I}_{S}$ , such that:

$$
{({I}_{S})}_{ijkl}=\frac{1}{2}(I+{I}_{t})=\frac{1}{2}({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}})
$$

*Note:*

One notes that the term $\frac{{y}_{1}-{y}_{2}}{{x}_{1}-{x}_{2}}\left[{I}_{S}-{E}_{1}\otimes {E}_{1}-{E}_{2}\otimes {E}_{2}\right]$ in the derivative $D(X)$ of the first equation of () expresses the rotation of the principal directions in the plane.

$$

Where ${I}_{p}$ is the matrix of the orthogonal projection of ${I}_{S}$ onto the plane $({e}_{x},{e}_{y})$ :

$$

#### 5.2.3 Three-dimensional case

In dimension three, the characteristic equation $det\left(X-{x}_{\alpha}I\right)=0$ gives a cubic equation of the eigenvalues ${x}_{\alpha =1,2,3}$ of $X$ of the following type:

$$
{x}_{\alpha}^{3}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}^{2}+{I}_{2}{x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{3}=0
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{I}_{1}=\mathit{trace}\left(X\right)\\ {I}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathit{trace}{\left(X\right)}^{2}-\mathit{trace}\left({X}^{2}\right)\right]\\ {I}_{3}=det\left(X\right)\end{array}
$$

The solution of the spectral problem readily gives the following solutions for the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{1}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\\ {x}_{2}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta +2\pi }{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\\ {x}_{3}=-2\sqrt{Q}\cos\left(\frac{\theta -2\pi }{3}\right)+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}\end{array}
$$

Where $Q$ and $\theta$ are given by:

$$
Q=\frac{{I}_{1}^{2}-3{I}_{2}}{9}
$$

With:

$$
R=\frac{-2{I}_{1}^{3}+9{I}_{1}{I}_{2}-27{I}_{3}}{54}
$$

And the eigenvectors, taking into account the multiplicity of the eigenvalues:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{E}_{\alpha}=\frac{{x}_{\alpha}}{2{x}_{\alpha}^{3}-{I}_{1}{x}_{\alpha}^{2}+{I}_{3}}\left[{X}^{2}+\left({x}_{\alpha}-{I}_{1}\right)X+\frac{{I}_{3}}{{x}_{\alpha}}I\right]& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\ne {x}_{3}\\ {E}_{\beta}=I-{E}_{\alpha}& \mathit{si}{x}_{\alpha}\ne {x}_{\beta}\\ {E}_{1}=I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}={x}_{3}\end{array}
$$

In the second equation of (), ${E}_{\alpha}$ is computed with the aid of the first equation. Without giving the intermediate stages of the computation, the derivative $D\left(X\right)$ , taking into account the multiplicity of the eigenvalues, is finally written:

$$
D\left(X\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\begin{array}{c}\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}\frac{{y}_{\alpha}}{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}\right)\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}[\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}-\left({x}_{\beta}+{x}_{\gamma}\right){I}_{S}\\ -\left(2{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right){E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{\alpha}-\left({x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)\left({E}_{\beta}\otimes {E}_{\beta}-{E}_{\gamma}\otimes {E}_{\gamma}\right)]\\ +\sum_{a=1}^{3}\sum_{b=1}^{3}\frac{\partial {y}_{a}}{\partial {x}_{b}}{E}_{a}\otimes {E}_{b}\end{array}& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}\ne {x}_{2}\ne {x}_{3}\\ {s}_{1}\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}-{s}_{2}{I}_{S}-{s}_{3}X\otimes X+{s}_{4}X\otimes I+{s}_{5}I\otimes X-{s}_{6}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{\alpha}\ne {x}_{\beta}={x}_{\gamma}\\ \left(\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{1}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}\right){I}_{S}+\frac{\partial {y}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}I\otimes I& \mathit{si}{x}_{1}={x}_{2}={x}_{3}\end{array}
$$

Where $\left(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma \right)$ corresponds to a cyclic permutation of $\left(1,2,3\right)$ . $I$ and ${I}_{S}$ are given by equations () and (), respectively. Noting that $X$ is a symmetric tensor, care must be taken to apply the symmetric differentiation operator for the evaluation of $\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}$ , which gives the following form:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}{\left(\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}\right)}_{ijkl}& =\frac{d\left({X}_{im}{X}_{\mathit{mj}}\right)}{d{X}_{kl}}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{\delta}_{\mathit{lm}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{\delta}_{\mathit{km}}\right){X}_{\mathit{mj}}+\frac{{X}_{im}}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{mk}}{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{ml}}{\delta}_{\mathit{kj}}\right)\\ & =\frac{1}{2}\left({\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}{X}_{\mathit{lj}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{il}}{X}_{\mathit{kj}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{jl}}{X}_{\mathit{ik}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{kj}}{X}_{\mathit{il}}\right)\end{array}
$$

Finally, the expressions of ${s}_{i=1,6}$ are the following:

$$
{s}_{1}=\frac{{y}_{\alpha}-{y}_{\gamma}}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{2}}+\frac{1}{{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}\right)
{s}_{2}=2{x}_{\gamma}\frac{{y}_{\alpha}-{y}_{\gamma}}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{2}}+\frac{{x}_{\alpha}+{x}_{\gamma}}{{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}\right)
{s}_{3}=2\frac{{y}_{\alpha}-{y}_{\gamma}}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{3}}+\frac{1}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}+\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}\right)
{s}_{4}=2{x}_{\gamma}\frac{{y}_{\alpha}-{y}_{\gamma}}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{3}}+\frac{1}{{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\beta}}\right)+\frac{{x}_{\gamma}}{{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}^{2}}\left(\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}+\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}{\partial {x}_{\alpha}}-\frac{\partial {y}_{\gamma}}{\partial {x}_{\gamma}}\right)
$$

Where $(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma )$ corresponds to a cyclic permutation of $(1,2,3)$ .

*Note:*

One notes the following term:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\sum_{\alpha =1}^{3}\frac{{y}_{\alpha}}{\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}\right)\left({x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)}[\frac{d{X}^{2}}{dX}-\left({x}_{\beta}+{x}_{\gamma}\right){I}_{S}-\left(2{x}_{\alpha}-{x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right){E}_{\alpha}\otimes {E}_{\alpha}\\ -\left({x}_{\beta}-{x}_{\gamma}\right)\left({E}_{\beta}\otimes {E}_{\beta}-{E}_{\gamma}\otimes {E}_{\gamma}\right)]\end{array}
$$

This term appears in the derivative $D(X)$ of the first equation of () and expresses the rotation of the principal directions in three-dimensional space.

## 6 Appendix: convention on the tensor notations

The vectors of the strains and of the stresses in the principal basis $({d}_{1},{d}_{2},{d}_{3})$ are denoted:

$$
\epsilon =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{1}\\ {\epsilon}_{2}\\ {\epsilon}_{3}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The elasticity tensor $C$ making it possible to relate $\epsilon$ and $\sigma$ in the principal basis, such that $\sigma =C.\epsilon$ is written:

$$
C=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}K+\frac{4}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G& K+\frac{4}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ K-\frac{2}{3}G& K-\frac{2}{3}G& K+\frac{4}{3}G\end{array}\right]
$$

With $K$ the elastic compressibility modulus and $G$ the elastic shear modulus. The strains and the stresses are symmetric tensors of order two. This symmetry is generally exploited (six independent components) by representing them by vectors of dimension six resulting from the projection of these tensors onto appropriate bases.

The strains and the stresses given as input and produced as output of the solution of the constitutive law are expressed in the orthonormal basis of the symmetric tensors of order two, denoted $\bar{b}$ :

$$
\overline{b}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ \frac{{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{y}+{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\\ \frac{{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{z}+{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{x}}{\sqrt{2}}\\ \frac{{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{z}+{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{y}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}
$$

Where $\left({e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}\right)$ represent the unit vectors of the global Cartesian orthonormal basis, assumed fixed. The condensed expression of the strain and stress tensors projected onto the basis $\bar{b}$ is written:

$$
\bar{\epsilon}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ {\epsilon}_{zz}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{xy}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{yz}\\ \sqrt{2}{\epsilon}_{xz}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

This writing brings out a term in $\sqrt{2}$ in front of the crossed components. It makes it possible to:

- Express the elasticity tensor of order four of $81$ components by a tensor of order two of $36$ components;
- Symmetrise this elasticity tensor.

Indeed, denoting ${\sigma}_{ij}={C}_{ijkl}{\epsilon}_{kl}$ , its form projected onto the basis $\bar{b}$ becomes ${\overline{\sigma}}_{i}={\overline{C}}_{ij}{\overline{\epsilon}}_{j}$ , where one has the following expression for $\bar{C}$ :

$$
\bar{C}=\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}{C}_{\mathit{xxxx}}& {C}_{\mathit{xxyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{xxzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xxyz}}\\ {C}_{\mathit{xxyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{yyyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{yyzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yyyz}}\\ {C}_{\mathit{zzxx}}& {C}_{\mathit{zzyy}}& {C}_{\mathit{zzzz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzxy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzxz}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{zzyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xyzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xyyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{xzzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{xzyz}}\\ \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzxx}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzyy}}& \sqrt{2}{C}_{\mathit{yzzz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzxy}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzxz}}& 2{C}_{\mathit{yzyz}}\end{array}\right]
$$

The condensed form () is not convenient to use because of the necessity of handling the terms in $\sqrt{2}$ during the matrix operations. Another writing is preferred to it, based on the projection onto the so-called Voigt basis, denoted $\tilde{b}$ and having the following expression:

$$
\widehat{b}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ {e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{z}\\ {e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{z}\end{array}
$$

The condensed expression of the strain and stress tensors projected onto the Voigt basis $\widehat{b}$ is written:

$$
\widehat{\epsilon}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ {\epsilon}_{zz}\\ {\epsilon}_{xy}\\ {\epsilon}_{yz}\\ {\epsilon}_{xz}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

This writing makes it possible to dispense with the terms in $\sqrt{2}$ in front of the crossed components, and is more convenient to use during the numerical solution of the constitutive law.
