# v2.02.117 SDLL117 - Beam subjected to multiple areas of fluid-elastic and random transverse excitation

**Summary**

This validation case is intended to calculate by frequent method (resorption of FLUSTRU software) the linear vibratory response of a tubular beam structure subjected to transverse external flow.

The effects of fluid-elastic coupling (variation of the frequency and damping of the structure) are calculated in relation to the flow rate, and the vibrational response of the beam to turbulent fluid excitation is then calculated.

The fluid excitation is distributed over several zones and can be identical or different in nature over each of these areas. Thus, this test case compares the results obtained with a single excitation zone (the tube is subjected to fluid excitation spread over its entire length), and with two excitation zones (the tube is subjected to two fluid excitations of the same nature, distributed over each half of the tube). Comparisons are made for different velocity profiles and different fluid-elastic coefficients.

For this test case, there is no reference solution strictly speaking. The results obtained with two areas of fluid excitation are compared to the results obtained with a single excitation zone. The objective of the test case is to check the non-regression of the data structures.

### 1.2 Material properties

The values of the physical quantities characteristic of each elements structure are:

A mass coefficient added for the calculation of the equivalent density of the structure to be studied is taken into account. The structure consists of a beam, an internal fluid and an external fluid.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The beam is simply supported with nodes $A$ and $D$ .

A random load is distributed over the excited length ($[\mathrm{AD}]$ line or $[\mathrm{AB}]$ and $[\mathrm{CD}]$ sections) of the beam. Turbulent excitation is thus done by a transverse flow to the tube. Excitations are defined using a speed profile along the beam and a dimensional excitation spectrum. Each area of fluid excitation is implicitly defined by the portion of the tube over which the flow rate is not zero. In the case of multiple excitations, the excitation zones must be disjointed.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Since the calculation is frequent, no initial conditions are imposed.

## 2 reference solution

The results obtained with two excitation zones of the fluid of the same nature, distributed over each half of the tube, are compared with those obtained with a single excitation zone defined over the entire tube.

For fluid-elastic coupling forces, comparisons are made on reduced frequency and damping variations of the structure, depending on the flow speed. Several calculations are made with uniform speed profiles on $[\mathrm{AD}]$ , or on stairs (uniform on $[\mathrm{AB}]$ and uniform on $[\mathrm{CD}]$ ), and different types of network (configurations VISCACHE 1 and CLOTAIRE). Comparisons were made for $1,5m/s$ flow speed for mode 2.

For turbulent excitation, differences displacement types calculated from vibration response spectra were compared, and these comparisons were also made for structures with uniform speed profiles on $[\mathrm{AD}]$ , or in staircases (uniform on $[\mathrm{AB}]$ and uniform on $[\mathrm{CD}]$ ). These comparisons were made at one of the two nodes mid-tube (node 100), where maximum differences were observed.

### 3.3 Calculation steps

The fluid flow speed profile and parameters that take into account fluid-structure coupling are defined by DEFI_FONC_FLUI and DEFI_FLUI_STRU operators.

The modal parameters of our structure are then calculated by taking into account the fluid-elastic forces with CALC_FLUI_SRU.

The definition of multiple excitation zones is carried out by a call to the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB, which allows the link between the interspectral matrix and the shape functions to be established. The excitations are then projected on the modal basis using the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE and the modal response interspectres are calculated by the operator DYNA_SPEC_MODAL.

Finally, the nodes stress autospectres are deduced by a call to REST_SPEC_PHYS.

The spectral response calculation can only be performed for one flow speed at a time, so these last three steps (PROJ_SPEC_BASE, DYNA_SPEC_MODAL, REST_SPEC_PHYS) are performed in a loop, in the control file, where the flow speed list is searched.

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case allows to check the behavior of the different fluid-structure coupling calculation operators, in the case of multiple excitation zones. It is a test case of non-regression of the operators concerned, and in particular, data structures.

In the case of fluid-elastic forces (operators DEFI_FLUI_STRU and CALC_FLUI_STRU), when several excitation zones of the fluid are defined with identical characteristics, results are well obtained that are strictly equivalent to those where there is only one zone.
