# v2.03.114 SDLS114 - Calculation of stress intensity factors of a modal recombinant cracked plate

**Summary**

This test is designed to validate the calculation of the intensity factors of a modal recombinant cracked plate. Modal intensity factors, therefore associated with each specific mode of vibration of the structure, are calculated with the operator CALC_G, option K (in FEM) and CALC_G_XFEM, option CALC_K_G (in X-FEM). The modellings illustrates the possibility of combining modal intensity factors directly in the control file with python instructions via the operator POST_K_TRANS.

This test contains two modellings 2D (modelization A and C) and two modellings 3D (B and D). The reference solution is derived from a direct temporal resolution of the transient problem. Both modellings A and B are for gridded cracks. Both modellings C and D are for non-grided cracks (X-FEM).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method

The reference solution is the one obtained by a direct temporal resolution of the transient problem. The DYNA_VIBRA operator is used to identify the displacement field, with a Newmark time integration scheme.

The evolution of stress intensity factors over time is then calculated by interpolation of displacement jumps (operator POST_K1_K2_K3).

### 2.2 Reference results – Modelling A

For Modelling A, the plate is pressed by force in the $(O,x,y)$ plane and the displacements in the $z$ direction are blocked. ## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a 2D model – flat stresses. The calculation of the evolution of stress intensity factors over time is carried out in several stages:

1. calculation of the first 15 modes of the structure;
2. calculation of the modal stress intensity factors associated with these modes by two methods;
3. resolution of the transient dynamic problem by modal projection;
4. recombination of the modal $K$.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh is composed of quadratic d-elements. It has 2000 nodes and 700 mesh cells and is refined around the crack bottom.

### 3.4 Note

The difference between the modal values calculated by interpolation of displacement jumps or by the theta method is small and consistent with that observed on static problems.

The value of ${K}_{1}(t)$ is calculated from the modal ${K}_{I}$ (K method) and modal-based resolution coefficients directly in the test case by python command lines:

${K}_{I}(t)=\underset{i=1}{\overset{M}{\Sigma}}{\alpha}_{i}(t).{K}_{I}^{i}$

where ${\alpha}_{i}(t)$ coefficients are the modal participation coefficients, extracted from the DYNA_VIBRA operator result, and ${K}_{I}^{i}$ are the modal stress intensity factors.

The accuracy obtained is satisfactory given the number of d的elements retained in the modal base. The accuracy increases rapidly with the number of modes [bib1].

## 7 Summary of the results

This test validates the calculation of modal intensity factors by the CALC_G (option K) and CALC_G_XFEM (option CALC_K_G) operators and illustrates their use for the resolution of a mechanical problem of low frequency dynamic failure by modal recombination.

The ratio of the calculation times of explicit resolution to modal-based resolution is between 10 and 50 depending on the mesh type, and the accuracy of the modal recombination method is fully satisfactory.

The results of the modellings C and D allow the application of the X-FEM method to be extended to calculate the modal stress intensity factors. The next step will be to solve a mechanical problem of the low frequency dynamic breakage by modal recombination
