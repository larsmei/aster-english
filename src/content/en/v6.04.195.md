# v6.04.195 SSNV195 - Multi-crack bar with X-FEM

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to test and validate the X-FEM functionality for multi-fabricated $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$ structures. Overall, the SSNV173 [V6.04.173](doc:v6.04.173) and SSNV182 [V6.04.182](doc:v6.04.182) test essentials are re-established for a multi-frusted structure this time.

This test involves a parallelepipedic bar, with the middle section clamped, having two cracks that cross it completely (we will then speak of interfaces), placed symmetrically on one side and the other of section clamped. The bar is subject to forced displacements, resulting in the total opening of the interfaces and the separation of the structure into three parts when pressed in traction, or the appearance of contact pressure when the pressure is compression.

Two types of interfaces are considered, horizontal (one stage of elements is cut) and inclined (several stages of elements are cut, to test the proper management of the elements undercutting when several cracks are present in the structure.

The case of an $\mathrm{2D}$ plate with two interfaces is also studied.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 3D geometry

The structure is a straight parallelepiped square-based and healthy. The dimensions of the bar (see[Figure1.1-1)) are: $\mathit{LX}=5m$ , $\mathit{LY}=5m$ and $\mathit{LZ}=50m$ . It has no cracks.

The interfaces will be introduced by level functions (level set rated $\text{LN}$ for normal level set) directly into the command file using DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator. Initially, the interfaces will be horizontal, each one in the middle of the lower and upper blocks compared to clamped section (see [Figure1.1-2]). The equations of level functions for the two horizontal interfaces are therefore:

$\text{LN1}=Z-\text{LZ}/4$                 éq 1.1-1

$\text{LN2}=Z-3\cdot \text{LZ}/4$                 éq 1.1-2

No tangential level is required since the TYPE_DISCONTINUITE='INTERFACE' key word is used, which allows a structure to be completely cut into three parts.

### 1.2 2D geometry

The structure is a healthy rectangle. The dimensions of the bar (see [Figure 1.2-1)) are: $\mathit{LX}=5m$ and $\mathit{LY}=50m$ . It has no cracks.

The interfaces will be introduced by level set functions directly in the command file using DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08). By analogy with the $\mathrm{3D}$ case, the interfaces are present in the media of both parts of the plate, separated by the line of clamped support (see Figure 1.2-a). The corresponding equations of the level functions are:

$\text{LN1}=\text{Y}-\text{LY}/4$                 éq 1.2-1

$\text{LN2}=Y-3\cdot \text{LY}/4$                 éq 1.2-3

No tangential level is required since the TYPE_DISCONTINUITE='INTERFACE' key word is used, which allows a structure to be completely cut into three parts.

### 1.3 Material properties

Young's modulus : $E=100\mathit{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.0$

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

The nodes of the mid-bar surface is clamped (see [Figure 1.1-2] and [Figure 1.2-2]) while the movement is imposed on the lower and upper surfaces. The first step is to show the possibility of separating a structure into 3 parts following the introduction of two interfaces (the required displacements will open the interfaces) and in a second step we will prove the inclusion of the contact on the lips of two interfaces (the required displacements will close the interfaces).

## 2 Modelling A: Opening Horizontal Interfaces

### 2.1 Mesh characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.195/10000000000001150000027C9ECC15841AF5C612.png)

Figure 2.1-1: **Le maillage de la modélisation A**

The structure is discretized using elements HEXA8 finished. In the three directions of the selected reference system, $2\times 2\times 10$ elements is then used, with a total of 40 elements finished (see [])

The number of elements stages in the $Z$ direction was chosen to avoid the enrichment of the same element by both interfaces. Indeed, in the implementation of the multi-cracking (see manual) you avoid approaching two cracks at less than 4 elements healthy in order to avoid conflicts when managing enriched degrees of freedom.

Thus, by choosing 10 floors, you can both impose boundary conditions on the extreme mesh cells in the usual way and avoid that the characteristic enrichments of the X-FEM touch each other. The first interface will be introduced in the middle of the third floor while the second in the middle of the eighth.

### 2.2 boundary conditions

In order to open both interfaces and to prevent rigid body movements, nodes from the median surface is embedded:

GROUP_NO=SURFMED : DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = 0

For nodes belonging to the extreme surfaces (lower and upper) displacements are required as follows:

GROUP_NO=SURFINF: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = -DEPZ

GROUP_NO=SURFSUP: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = DEPZ

The value of the displacement is $\mathit{DEPZ}=3.{10}^{-3}m$ . The two extreme blocks of the bar can actually be moved in three directions by simply assigning a non-zero value for the degrees of freedom corresponding to $\mathit{DX}$ or $\mathit{DY}$ .

### 2.3 Analytical resolution

The solution to such a problem is obvious. As explained for the test case SSNV173 [V6.04.173](doc:v6.04.173), the solution is as follows: all the following moves $x$ and $y$ are zero, all the following moves $z$ below the lower level-set are equal to the ${u}_{z}$ displacement at the base of the structure, all the following moves $z$ between the two level-set are zero and all the $z$ moves above the upper level-set are equal to the ${u}_{z}$ displacement at the top of the structure.

### 2.4 Tested quantities and results

The displacement values after the convergence of the iterations of the STAT_NON_LINE operator are tested. The values determined in [§ 2.3] are verified.

## 3 Modelling B: Horizontal Closing Interfaces

### 3.1 Mesh characteristics and interface

The mesh is the same as the Modelling A. The objective is to test the contact management on the interfaces represented with X-FEM, so only the boundary conditions have changed.

### 3.2 boundary conditions

The boundary conditions will allow this time to test the consideration of the friction-free contact on the lips of the interfaces. So we keep the clamped support of the nodes belonging to the median surface but the displacements imposed on the nodes of the two extremes (lower and higher) will close the interfaces. So we will have:

GROUP_NO=SURFMED: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = 0

GROUP_NO=SURFINF: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = DEPZ

GROUP_NO=SURFSUP: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = -DEPZ

### 3.3 Analytical resolution

The interfaces being horizontal and the uniaxial and normal compression state at the interface, there is no possible slippage. The value of the contact pressure is checked and the solution of the problem is that of the same problem without interface, for each block on either side of the clamped surface. As shown for the test case SSNV182 [V6.04.182](doc:v6.04.182) and given the geometry and boundary conditions described in §2.2, the contact pressure is given by:

> $\lambda =E\frac{\text{DEPZ}}{\text{LZ}/2}$ **éq 4.3-1**

With the numerical values previously introduced, $\lambda =-12{.10}^{3}\text{Pa}$ .

### 3.4 Tested quantities and results

The correct calculation process allows *a priori* to validate the correct contact management for multi-cracking. The contact components of the FISS_XFEM SD for each crack are managed correctly at the MODI_MODELE_XFEM and STAT_NON_LINE operators. The contact pressure values for the nodes mid-range components of the elements cut by cracks are tested.

## 4 Modelling C – Bend interfaces

This Modelling is intended to demonstrate the inclusion of frictional contact for inclined interfaces and the proper functioning of the cutting algorithms and post-processing.

A $\theta =30°$ angle of inclination was chosen according to the rotation around the $\mathit{OX}$ axis for both interfaces. Normal at the interfaces thus created is noted $\mathrm{n}$ and the tangential vector is noted $\tau$:

$\mathrm{n}=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ -1/2\\ \sqrt{3}/2\end{array}),\tau =(\begin{array}{c}0\\ \sqrt{3}/2\\ 1/2\end{array})$ **éq 6-1**

The new level functions introducing the two cracks in the command file are:

$\text{LN1}=\text{Z}-\tan\theta \cdot Y-(\text{LZ}/4-\tan\theta \cdot \text{LX}/2)$                 éq 6-2

$\text{LN2}=\text{Z}-\tan\theta \cdot Y-(3\cdot \text{LZ}/4-\tan\theta \cdot \text{LX}/2)$                 éq 6-3

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.195/10000000000000FD00000264F1748E97E04BB81B.png)

### 4.1 Figure 4.1-1: Maillage pourla modélisation CCaractéristiques du maillage

Compared to modellings A and B, the number of elements in the $Z$ direction (bar height) was multiplied by 2 to avoid the contiguousness of the elements enriched by the two inclined interfaces. Therefore, one has a mesh $2\times 2\times 20$ elements HEXA8 (see []).

### 4.2 boundary conditions

The boundary conditions allows for a mixed load: compression at the first interface and traction for the second.

Thus, in addition to the clamped support of the nodes belonging to the median surface (as for the first two modellings), positive displacements are imposed in the $Z$ direction for nodes belonging to the extreme (lower and upper) surfaces.

GROUP_NO=SURFMED: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = 0

GROUP_NO=SURFINF: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = DEPZ

GROUP_NO=SURFSUP: DX = 0, DY = 0 et DZ = DEPZ

### 4.3 Analytical resolution

The analytical solution is for the lower block (interface 1) the contact pressure and the friction semi-multiplier values. For the upper block (interface 2), the analytical solution is for the displacement values of the nodes. The solution for the latter is identical to that of Modelling A and therefore we will only detail the analytical solution for the lower block.

The interfaces are inclined and can be slipped. To avoid this, we force the adhesion by choosing a sufficiently high coefficient of friction of Coulomb. Theoretically, just take:

> $\mu >\tan(\theta )$ **éq 6.3-1**

Thus, the solution of the problem remains the same as that of the same problem without interface. The analytical resolution for this problem is presented in [V604182 - §4.1]. Contact pressure:

$\lambda ={n}_{z}\cdot E\frac{\text{DEPZ}}{\text{LZ}/2}\cdot {n}_{z}$ **éq 6.3-2**

and for friction semi-multipliers:

$\Lambda =(\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{{\tau}_{z}}{{n}_{z}})\tau$ **éq 6.3-3**

With the numerical values previously introduced and considering $\mu =1$, we obtain for the contact pressure $\lambda =-{9.10}^{3}\mathit{Pa}$ and for the friction semi-multiplier according to the direction $\tau$ , $\Lambda \cdot \tau =1/\sqrt{3}$ .

## 6 Summary of results

The objectives of this test were achieved. The aim was to validate the functioning of the X-FEM evolution towards multi-cracking. Two interfaces were successfully considered in several situations: $\mathrm{3D}$ and $\mathrm{2D}$ models, horizontal interfaces, inclined interfaces, contact without or with friction, etc.

All operators who have undergone changes to the multi-cracking process work properly.
