# v6.08.112 SSND112 – Network rotation and large deformations on a single crystal

**Summary:**

On a reduced point hardware problem, traction is performed on a single crystal

Modelling A: This Modelling validates MONOCRISTAL behaviour of type $\mathrm{CFC}$ .in large deformations

Modelling B: This Modelling validates MONOCRISTAL behaviour of type $\mathrm{CFC}$ . in small deformations with consideration of rotation of the crystal network

Modelling C: This Modelling uses MONOCRISTAL behaviour of type $\mathrm{CFC}$ .in small deformations for qualitative comparison with modellings A and B.

Modelling D: This Modelling uses MONOCRISTAL behavior of type $\mathit{CC}$ .in large deformations.

Modelling E: This Modelling validates MONOCRISTAL behaviour of type $\mathrm{CFC}$ .in large deformations, in the same way as Modelling A, but with the $\text{CFC_IRRA}$ behavior

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This is a material point, representative of a homogeneous state of stress and deformation.

It can be simulated by a volume element represented by a single finite element.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

L-element of cubic volume on the $\mathrm{1m}$ side is subjected to a uniform single tensile test, in required deformations.1 HEXA8.

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.112/100002010000010D0000012086D23B2BC458EA83.png)

The load is as follows:

- The $\mathrm{S1}$ face is blocked in the direction $z$
- The $\mathrm{S3}$ face undergoes a displacement of $0,2\mathrm{mm}$ into $0.2s$ and 100 increments.
- The $X$ and $Y$ moves of the original point are null
- A stiffness worth ${\mathrm{1O}}^{4}N/m$ following $Y$ is added to the original point via a discreet element to allow a quasi-free rotation around $Z$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

No stress and deformations. Initial dislocation density: ${\rho}_{0}={10}^{6}{\mathrm{mm}}^{\text{-2}}$

## 2 reference solution

It is based on bib1 and [v6.08.110](doc:v6.08.110). In the small deformation area, the $\sigma$ stress tensor being uniaxial, the split can be calculated for each sliding system, the split solved by: ${\tau}_{s}=\sigma :{\mu}_{s}$ with ${\mu}_{s}$ the orientation tensor defined by: ${({m}_{s})}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({({n}_{s})}_{i}\cdot {({l}_{s})}_{j}+{({l}_{s})}_{i}\cdot {({n}_{s})}_{j})$ , ${n}_{s}$ designating normal at the sliding plane of the $s$ system and ${l}_{s}$ the sliding direction. The evolution of the plastic slip is given for each $s$ system by (see [r5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11)):

$\mathrm{CFC}$ case: For the chosen orientation, i.e. 1-5-9, the initial Schmid factors, linking the stress tensor to the different ${\tau}_{s}$ solved splits, are for the 12 octahedral systems:

$\begin{array}{c}[0.45784855,0.22892428,0.22892428,0.15261618,0.26707832,0.11446214,\\ 0.19840104,0.29760156,0.4960026,0.04578486,0.11446214,0.16024699]\end{array}$

The first active slip system will therefore be number 9 ($\mathrm{A3}$ ), and the second will be number 1 ($\mathrm{B4}$ ).

In large deformations, or taking into account the network rotation, a third sliding system, $\mathrm{C1}$ (12th system in Code_Aster), which is significantly increased in activity, must be shown for non-infinite-immal deformation, while the viscoplastic slip of the $\mathrm{A3}$ system is no longer bib2.
