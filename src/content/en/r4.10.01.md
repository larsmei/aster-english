# r4.10.01 ZHU-ZIENKIEWICZ error estimator

**Summary:**

This document sets out the method of estimation of the discretisation error proposed by ZHU‑ZIENKIEWICZ.

This estimator is based on a continuous smoothing of the computed stresses, making it possible to obtain a better accuracy on the nodal stresses than the standard methods.

Two successive versions of this estimator are described, each corresponding to a different smoothing.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

Research on error estimators for the solutions obtained by finite-element analyses, and their coupling with adaptive meshing procedures, has grown considerably in recent years. The aim is to remedy the possible inadequacy of a modelling by automatically adapting the mesh to the solution sought, according to certain criteria (equal distribution of the discretisation error, minimisation of the number of nodes in order to reach a given accuracy, least cost).

An a posteriori error estimator is presented here within the framework of linear homogeneous elasticity. Historically, this estimator, proposed by ZHU-ZIENKIEWICZ [bib1] in 1987, was widely used because of its ease of implementation in existing analysis codes and of its low cost. Nevertheless, the poor reliability of this estimator for elements of even degree was observed empirically (underestimation of the error) and led the authors to a modification of their method in 1992 [bib2], [bib3], with numerical verification of the asymptotic convergence of the estimator on all types of element.

Nevertheless, the field of application of the 1992 version being for the moment more restricted (see [3.2]), both versions of this estimator have been implemented in *Aster* and are the subject of this note.

## 2 Principle of the method

### 2.1 Equations to be solved

Consider the solution $(u,s)$ of a linear elastic problem satisfying:

- the equilibrium equations:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\text{Lu}=q\text{dans}\Omega \\ {\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{j}={\bar{t}}_{i}\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{t}\end{array}$

with $L={}^{t}\mathrm{BDB}$ the elasticity operator

- the compatibility equations:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\varepsilon =\text{Bu}\\ u=\bar{u}\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{u}\end{array}$

with $\Gamma ={\Gamma}_{u}\cup {\Gamma}_{t}$

- the constitutive law:

$\sigma =D\varepsilon$

The problem discretised by finite elements consists in finding $({u}_{h},{\sigma}_{h})$, the solution of:

${u}_{h}=N{\bar{u}}_{h}$                                                                     eq 2.1-1

satisfying $K{\bar{u}}_{h}=\text{f}$

with $K=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}(\mathrm{BN})D(\mathrm{BN})d\Omega$

$\text{f}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}\mathrm{Nq}d\Omega +\underset{{G}_{t}}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}N\bar{t}\mathrm{dG}$

where:

${\bar{\text{u}}}_{h}$ represents the nodal unknowns of displacement

$N$ the associated shape functions

The stresses are computed from the displacements by the relation:

${\sigma}_{h}={\mathrm{DBu}}_{h}$                                                                     eq 2.1-2

### 2.2 Error estimator and effectivity index

| One denotes | $e=u-{u}_{h}$ | the error on the displacements |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${e}_{s}=s-{s}_{h}$ | the error on the stresses |

The energy norm of the error $e$ is written:

$\parallel \text{e}\parallel ={(\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}\mathrm{eLe}d\Omega )}^{1/2}$

in the case of elasticity

$={(\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}{e}_{\sigma}{D}^{-1}{e}_{\sigma}d\Omega )}^{1/2}$                                                     eq 2.2-1

The global error above is decomposed into a sum of elementary errors, as follows:

${\parallel e\parallel }^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\parallel e\parallel }_{i}^{2}$

| where | $N$ is the total number of elements. |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${\parallel e\parallel }_{i}$ represents the local error indicator on element $i$. |

The aim is to estimate the exact error from equation [eq 2.2-1] formulated in stresses. The basic idea of the method is to construct a new stress field, denoted ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$, from ${\sigma}_{h}$ and such that:

${e}_{\sigma}\approx {e}_{\sigma}^{\text{*}}={\sigma}^{\text{*}}-{\sigma}_{h}$

The error estimator will then be written:

${}^{0}\text{}\parallel e\parallel ={(\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}{e}_{\sigma}^{\text{*}}{D}^{-1}{e}_{\sigma}^{\text{*}}d\Omega )}^{1/2}$

The quality of the estimator is measured by the quantity $\theta$, called the effectivity index of the estimator:

$\theta =\frac{{}^{0}\text{}\parallel e\parallel }{\parallel e\parallel }$

An error estimator is said to be asymptotically exact if $\theta \to 1$ when $\parallel e\parallel \to 0$ (or when $h\to 0$), which means that the estimated error will always converge towards the exact error when the latter decreases.

Obviously, the reliability of ${}^{0}\text{}\parallel e\parallel$ depends on the "quality" of ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$.

The two versions of the ZHU-ZIENKIEWICZ estimator differ at this level (see [§3]).

### 2.3 Construction of an asymptotically exact estimator

The characterisation of such an estimator is provided by the following theorem (see [bib 2]).

**Theorem**

If $\parallel {e}^{\text{*}}\parallel =\parallel {u-u}^{\text{*}}\parallel$ is the error norm of the reconstructed solution, then the error estimator ${}^{0}\text{}\parallel e\parallel$ defined previously is asymptotically exact

if $\frac{\parallel {e}^{\text{*}}\parallel }{\parallel e\parallel }\to 0$ when $\parallel e\parallel \to 0$

This condition is realised if the rate of convergence with $h$ of $\parallel {e}^{\text{*}}\parallel$ is higher than that of $\parallel e\parallel$. Typically, if one assumes that the exact error of the finite-element approximation converges as $\parallel e\parallel =0({h}^{p})$

and the error of the reconstructed solution as

$\parallel {e}^{\text{*}}\parallel =0({h}^{p+\alpha })$ with $\alpha >0$

then a simple calculation gives:

$1-0({h}^{\alpha})\le \theta \le 1+0({h}^{\alpha})$

and therefore $\theta \to 1$ when $h\to 0$

## 3 Construction of the recomputed stress field

### 3.1 1987 version

The solution ${u}_{h}$ resulting from equation [eq 2.1-1] being ${C}_{0}$ on $\Omega$ (because of the choice of ${C}_{0}$ shape functions), it follows that ${\sigma}_{h}$ computed by [eq 2.1-2] is discontinuous at the element interfaces.

In order to obtain acceptable results for the nodal stresses, one generally resorts to an average at the nodes or to a projection method. It is this latter method which is adopted here.

It is assumed that ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$ is interpolated by the same shape functions as ${u}_{h}$, that is:

${\sigma}^{\text{*}}=N{\bar{\sigma}}^{\text{*}}$                                                                         eq 3.1-1

and a global least-squares smoothing of ${\sigma}_{h}$ is performed, which amounts to minimising the functional $J(\tau )=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}(\tau -{\sigma}_{h})(\tau -{\sigma}_{h})d\Omega$ in the space generated by $N$.

By differentiation, ${s}^{}$ must satisfy $\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}N({\sigma}^{\text{*}}-{\sigma}_{h})d\Omega =0$

using equation [eq 3.1-1], one obtains the linear system:

$M\left\lbrace {\bar{\sigma}}^{\text{*}}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace b\right\rbrace$

with $M=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}\mathrm{NN}d\Omega$ and $\left\lbrace b\right\rbrace =\underset{\Omega}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}N{\sigma}_{h}d\Omega$

This global system is to be solved for each of the components of the stress tensor. The matrix $M$ is computed and inverted only once.

### 3.2 1992 version

The constraint on the field ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$ differs from the 1987 version in the following way:

it is assumed that ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$ is polynomial of the same degree as the displacements over the set of elements possessing an internal vertex node $S$ in common.

Let ${S}_{K}=\underset{S\in K}{\cup}K$ denote this set, called a patch.

For each component of ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$, one writes:

${\sigma}^{\text{*}}{\mid}_{{S}_{K}}={\text{Pa}}_{s}$                                                                 eq 3.2-1

| where | $P$ contains the appropriate polynomial terms |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${a}_{s}$ the unknown coefficients of the corresponding monomials |
| Example:![Figure](images/Object_74.svg) |  $\mathrm{P1}P=\left[1,x,y\right]$ ${a}_{s}{}^{t}=\left[{a}_{1},{a}_{2},{a}_{3}\right]$  |

$\mathrm{Q1}P=\left[1,x,y,xy\right]$     ${a}_{s}={}^{t}\text{}\left[{a}_{1},{a}_{2},{a}_{3},{a}_{4}\right]$

![Figure](/figures/r4.10.01/Object_74.svg)

The coefficients of the polynomial ${a}_{s}$ are determined by minimising the functional:

$\begin{array}{cc}F(a)& =\sum_{i=1}^{N}{({\sigma}_{h}({x}_{i},{y}_{i})-{\sigma}^{\text{*}}{\mid}_{{S}_{K}}({x}_{i},{y}_{i}))}^{2}\\ & =\sum_{i=1}^{N}{({\sigma}_{h}({x}_{i},{y}_{i})-P({x}_{i},{y}_{i}){a}_{s})}^{2}\end{array}$

(discrete local least-squares smoothing of ${\sigma}_{h}$)

| where | $({x}_{i},{y}_{i})$ are the coordinates of the GAUSS points on ${S}_{K}$. |
| --- | --- |
|  | $N$ is the total number of GAUSS points on all the elements of the patch |

The solution ${a}_{s}$ satisfies:

$\sum_{i=1}^{N}{}^{t}\text{}P({x}_{i},{y}_{i})P({x}_{i},{y}_{i}){a}_{s}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{}^{t}\text{}P({x}_{i},{y}_{i}){\sigma}_{h}({x}_{i},{y}_{i})$

whence ${a}_{s}={A}^{-1}b$ with $A=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{}^{t}\text{}P({x}_{i},{y}_{i})P({x}_{i},{y}_{i})$

$A$ may be very ill-conditioned (in particular on high-degree elements) and consequently impossible to invert in this form. To remedy this problem, the authors [bib4] have proposed a normalisation of the coordinates on each patch, which amounts to performing the change of variables:

$\begin{array}{c}\bar{x}=-1+2\frac{x-{x}_{\min}}{{x}_{\max}-{x}_{\min}}\\ \bar{y}=-1+2\frac{y-{y}_{\min}}{{y}_{\max}-{y}_{\min}}\end{array}$

where ${x}_{\min},{x}_{\max},{y}_{\min},{y}_{\max}$ represent the minimum and maximum values of $x$ and $y$ on the patch.

This method appreciably improves the conditioning of $A$ and completely removes the previous problem.

Once ${\mathrm{a}}_{\mathrm{s}}$ has been determined, the nodal values are deduced from equation [eq 3.2-1] only at the nodes internal to the patch, except in the case of patches having boundary nodes.

**Note:**

*In the case of finite elements of different type, the choice of* $P$ *in equation [eq 3.2-1] is delicate (problems of validity of* ${a}_{s}$ *if the space is too rich, loss of superconvergence if it is not rich enough). A more thorough study seems indispensable.*

*This is why the estimator* $\mathit{ZZ2}$ *is for the moment limited to meshes comprising only a single element type. This restriction does not exist for* $\mathit{ZZ1}$*.*

The authors have shown numerically [bib3] that, with this choice of ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}$, their estimator was asymptotically exact for elastic materials whose characteristics are independent of the domain and for all types of element, and that the rates of convergence with $h$ of $\parallel {e}^{\text{*}}\parallel$ were improved relative to the previous version (especially for elements of degree 2: see test case SSLV110, Validation manual), whence a better estimation of the error.

An illustration of these rates of convergence will be found in reference [bib 5].

## 4 Implementation in Code_Aster and present limits of use

### 4.1 Implementation in Code_Aster

The two previous estimators are implemented in *Code_Aster* in the post-processing command CALC_ERREUR [U4.81.04](doc:u4.81.04). They are activated from options (ERZ1_ELEM for ZZ1 and ERz2_ELEM for ZZ2) and enrich a data structure RESULTAT.

Furthermore, the computation of the stress field smoothed by one or other of the methods described in [paragraph 3] may be triggered separately from the computation of the error estimate (option SIZ1_NOEU for ZZ1 and SIZ2_NOEU for ZZ2).

The error estimator supplies:

- a field by element comprising 3 components:
- the estimate of the relative error on the element,
- the estimate of the absolute error on the element,
- the energy norm of the computed solution ${\sigma}_{h}$.
- listing outputs comprising the same information at the global level (over the whole structure)

All the fields obtained may be visualised via the command IMPR_RESU.

### 4.2 Limits of use

The theoretical framework is homogeneous linear elasticity.

For ZZ1, the 2D modellings (plane stress and plane strain, axisymmetric) and 3D are permitted, whereas for ZZ1 only the 2D modellings (plane stress and plane strain, axisymmetric) are permitted.

| Element types: | triangles with 3 and 6 nodes, |
| --- | --- |
|  | quadrilaterals with 4, 8 and 9 nodes. |

For ZZ2, the mesh must comprise only a single type of element.
