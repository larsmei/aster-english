# r7.01.23 Cyclic constitutive law of HUJEUX for soils

**Summary:**

The constitutive model known as “Hujeux”, designed at the MSSMat laboratory of the ECP , is one of the cyclic elastoplastic models of soil mechanics (granular geomaterials: sandy clays, normally consolidated or over-consolidated, gravels…) best suited to simulations of geotechnical structures under earthquake loading. It has moreover been used for many years, so that its parameterisation is well mastered.

This multi-mechanism model (spherical — for a consolidation path — and deviatoric) with memory variables is characterised by eight hardening loading surfaces, defined for monotonic paths and for cyclic paths. The mechanisms are defined by fixed planes, which induces an orthotropy of the soil behaviour. Inside these reversibility surfaces, the material is non-linear elastic. The hardening is governed by several variables and the normal flow rule is adopted for the consolidation mechanisms, whereas the flow rule for the deviatoric mechanisms is non-associated, following the Roscoe dilatancy rule. As with other soil constitutive models, the hardening is positive in the pre-peak phase and negative in the post-peak phase, which corresponds to the effect of dilatancy; these effects induce the “liquefaction” behaviour of the soil. The plastic strain tensor results from the accumulation of the contributions of the various active mechanisms. The volumetric plastic strain couples the mechanisms.

The equations of the model are described, then its parameterisation, then its numerical integration according to a general implicit Newton scheme. Users have the possibility of accessing three implicit integration schemes for the Hujeux model: 'SPECIFIQUE', 'SEMI_EXPLICITE' and 'BASCULE_EXPLICITE', the last two being variants of the 'SPECIFIQUE' scheme introduced in order to provide more flexibility for the local integration of the behaviour during dynamic studies.

## 1 Theoretical formulation

### 1.1 Hujeux constitutive relation

The model described here is the model known as Hujeux. The model was developed by the École Centrale Paris (LMSSMat laboratory) in the 1980s in order to represent the rheology of soils for alternating loadings, for example under earthquake. This constitutive relation of soil mechanics is a multi-mechanism model, characterised by twice four hardening loading surfaces: three linked to deviatoric mechanisms and one to a spherical consolidation mechanism, defined for monotonic paths and for cyclic paths. The deviatoric mechanisms are defined on three fixed planes, which induces an orthotropy of the soil behaviour. The spherical mechanisms reproduce the strong non-linearity of geomaterials on a consolidation path.

Each of these planes, indexed by $k$ , is defined by the basis vectors $({{e}_{i}}_{k},{{e}_{j}}_{k})$, taken from the orthonormal basis $({e}_{1},{e}_{2},{e}_{3})$ of three-dimensional space. The indices are denoted:

$$
{i}_{k}=1+\text{mod}(\text{k,}3) \text{ et } {j}_{k}=1+\text{mod}(\text{k+}1,3)
$$

**Note:**

*In a 2D plane-strain situation, the plane* $\text{k=}3$ *corresponds to the plane* $({\mathrm{e}}_{1},{\mathrm{e}}_{2})$ *of the model.*

The model comprises a non-linear elasticity controlled by the Young's modulus depending on the confining pressure.

A three-dimensional limit criterion close to that of Mohr–Coulomb is considered in order to take into account the influence of the effective mean stress on the stiffness of the soil and on the failure characteristics. The concept of critical state is also integrated into this model in order to represent the coupling between the deviatoric stresses and the volume variations and to formulate the isotropic hardening linked to the granular medium. A kinematic hardening is added in order to represent the cyclic behaviour of soils and is coupled to the density hardening of the soil.

The Hujeux model is expressed in effective stresses — defined as the difference between the total stresses and the water pressure in the case of saturated soils — in the case of hydromechanical coupling: *that is to say, one does not take into account the hydrostatic pressure of the fluid which may circulate in the pores, the latter being computed in the THM modellings.*

#### 1.1.1 Definition of the state variables and expression of the free energy

##### 1.1.1.1 State variables

The variables used to describe the state of the material point are the following:

- $\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ : strain tensor,
- $\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}^{p}}$ : plastic strain tensor; one denotes in particular ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}=\text{tr}{\varepsilon}^{p}=\boldsymbol{{\varepsilon}^{p}}.\boldsymbol{I}$ the volumetric plastic strain, $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{I}}$ being the identity tensor,
- ${r}_{k}^{m}$ : the “mobilisation factors” of the deviatoric mechanisms on a monotonic path: this is a hardening in the pressure–shear plane,
- ${r}_{k}^{c}$ : the “mobilisation factors” of the cyclic deviatoric mechanisms: this is a mixed isotropic and kinematic hardening in the pressure–shear plane.
- ${r}_{4}^{m}$ : the mobilisation factor of the consolidation mechanism on a monotonic path: this is a hardening of the spherical consolidation mechanism (scalar),
- ${r}_{4}^{c}$ : the mobilisation factor of the consolidation mechanism on a cyclic path: this is a mixed isotropic and kinematic hardening (scalar),

accompanied by a certain number of discontinuous history variables, described below.

The various mechanisms of elastoplastic evolution involve hardening variables: the mobilisation factors associated with each of the mechanisms and the volumetric plastic strain ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}=\text{tr}({\boldsymbol{\varepsilon^{p}}})$ , common to all the mechanisms and coupling them. The latter modifies the loading surfaces even if they are not active, because of the hardening produced on the other activated loading surfaces: see [§ 1.1.2  and  1.1.3].

One denotes by $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ the (effective) stress tensor, the confining pressure being: $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$. By convention in code_aster, the case of compression corresponds to negative stresses (and strains). For each plastic mechanism in the plane $k$ , one denotes by:

$$
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)}={\boldsymbol{P}}_{(k)}\cdot \boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot {\boldsymbol{P}}_{(k)}
$$

the stresses in the plane $k$ , where ${\boldsymbol{P}}_{(k)}$ denotes the (symmetric) tensor of projection onto the basis plane $({e}_{{i}_{k}},{e}_{{j}_{k}})$ , of normal ${e}_{k}$ :

$$
{\boldsymbol{P}}_{(k)}={e}_{{i}_{k}}\otimes {e}_{{j}_{k}}+{e}_{{j}_{k}}\otimes {e}_{{i}_{k}}
$$

with ${i}_{k}=1+\text{mod}(k,3)$  and ${j}_{k}=1+\text{mod}(k+1,3)$ , that is:

$$
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)}={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{i}_{k}{i}_{k}}.{e}_{{i}_{k}}\otimes {e}_{{i}_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{j}_{k}{j}_{k}}.{e}_{{j}_{k}}\otimes {e}_{{j}_{k}}+{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{{i}_{k}{j}_{k}}.{e}_{{i}_{k}}{\otimes}_{s}{e}_{{j}_{k}}
$$

then the confining pressure in the plane $k$ :

$$
{p}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}})=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)})
$$

One also defines the deviatoric stress tensor ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}$ in the plane $k$ , with the aid of the second-order identity tensor in the plane: ${\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}={\delta}_{{i}_{k}{j}_{k}}{e}_{{i}_{k}}\otimes {e}_{{j}_{k}}$ :

$$
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)}-{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}).{\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}
$$

One does indeed have $\mathrm{tr}({\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)})=0$ . Finally, one denotes the (von Mises) norm of the deviatoric stress tensor ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}$ :

$$
{q}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})={\parallel{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})\parallel}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\alpha \beta }.{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{\alpha \beta }}
$$

Moreover, several memory variables of the irreversible history retain, during certain phases of the evolution, the values of the state variables at the beginning of these phases, and govern the subsequent evolutions, recording the value of this variable at the beginning of the path on which a cyclic mechanism is triggered:

- ${p}_{H}$ : discontinuous scalar memory variable, recording the value of the confining pressure $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ ,
- ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{vH}}^{p}$ : discontinuous scalar memory variable, recording the value of the volumetric plastic strain,
- ${p}_{\text{kH}}$ : discontinuous scalar memory variable, recording the value of the “confining pressure of the plane $k$ ” ${p}_{k}$ ,
- ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}$ : discontinuous tensorial memory variable, recording the value of the stress deviators ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ in each plane $k$ , whose expression is given in [eq 1.1.1-5],
- ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}$ : discontinuous tensorial memory variable, recording the value of the “modified” stress deviators ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}{\text{,r}}_{k}^{c})$ in each deviatoric plane $k$ , whose expression is given in [eq 1.1.2-12].

**Note:**

*The formulation of the Hujeux model by orthogonal planes does not make it possible to represent axisymmetric states correctly; cf.*  *.*

##### 1.1.1.2 Free energy

One denotes the free energy by the sum of an elastic contribution and a hardening contribution:

$$
\begin{array}{}F(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} (\overrightarrow{u}),{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}},{r}_{k}^{K})={F}_{\text{él}}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} (\overrightarrow{u}),{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}})+{H}_{\text{écr}}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{K})\end{array}
$$

for $K=m,c$ ; $\text{k=}1,...,4$ . The thermodynamic dissipation is obtained by difference from the density of strain power, this in an isothermal process:

$$
D=\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\dot{\overrightarrow{u}})-\dot{F}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}(\overrightarrow{u}),{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}},{r}_{k}^{K})=\boldsymbol{\sigma}\cdot{\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}}-{\dot{H}}_{\text{écr}}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{K})
$$

In so far as one does not solve the thermal coupling, one cannot differentiate the density of dissipated power from the rate of hardening energy, corresponding to a stored-energy term. However $\boldsymbol{\sigma} \cdot {\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}}$ is not necessarily positive, unlike $D$ . As will be seen below, the elastic part of the Hujeux law is non-linear: one cannot easily extract the expression of the potential ${F}_{\text{él}}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} (\overrightarrow{u}),{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}})$ .

As the Hujeux constitutive law possesses a particular non-linear elasticity, cf. [§ 1.1.1.3], one cannot give the integrated expression of the free energy.

##### 1.1.1.3 Non-linear elastoplastic law of Hujeux

One admits that the Young's modulus of the soil depends on the confining pressure $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ by a power law, in the domain of compressions $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})<0$ . The non-linear elastoplastic constitutive relation of Hujeux is written:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma} =\frac{\partial}{\partial \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}{F}_{\text{él}}(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} (\overrightarrow{u}),{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}})=\boldsymbol{C}(p).(\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} (\overrightarrow{u})-{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}^{p}})
$$

where the elasticity tensor $\boldsymbol{C}(p)$ is isotropic.

In practice, one cannot express the relation [eq 1.1.1.9] in analytical form, and one identifies, in incremental (hypoelastic) form, the relation between the elastic volumetric strain and the confining pressure:

$$
\dot{p}=\frac{{K}_{0}}{1-n}.\lvert{\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}}\rvert^{n}.\text{tr}(\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}-{\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}})
$$

where one defines two parameters: $n\in [0,1[$ and ${P}_{\text{réf}}$ (non-zero), as well as the initial moduli ${E}_{0}$ and ${\nu}_{0}$ (or, according to the practice in soil mechanics, the positive initial shear modulus ${G}_{0}$ and bulk modulus ${K}_{0}$ measured at the reference confining pressure ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ ) which define the initial elasticity tensor ${\boldsymbol{C}}^{0}$ . The case $n=0$ corresponds to linear elasticity.

The components of the elasticity tensor of the non-linear elastic law are therefore written:

$$
{C}_{ijkl}(p)={C}_{ijkl}^{0}\cdot\lvert{\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}}\rvert^{n}={G}_{0}.({\delta}_{\text{ik}}{\delta}_{\text{jl}}+{\delta}_{\text{jk}}{\delta}_{\text{il}}+\frac{2{\nu}_{0}}{1-2{\nu}_{0}}{\delta}_{ij}{\delta}_{kl}).\lvert{\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}}\rvert^{n}
$$

One will denote hereafter the compressibility coefficient:

$$
K(p)={K}_{0}\cdot{\lvert}{\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}}{\rvert}^{n}
$$

One notes that the elastic law is not differentiable if the confining pressure is zero (and if $n\ne 0$). In practice, this law leads to a greater stiffness of the soil as one goes deeper.

The criteria (or plastic loading surfaces) involve the critical-pressure function, which characterises the strength of the soil, depending on the void ratio in the material (density hardening):

$$
{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})={P}_{\text{c0}}.{e}^{-{\beta}{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}
$$

in which two material parameters intervene: the initial reference critical pressure ${P}_{\mathrm{c0}}$ (negative) and $\beta$ the plastic compressibility of the material (positive). The critical pressure increases in absolute value when the material has undergone a negative volumetric plastic strain (compression).

#### 1.1.2 Deviatoric elastoplastic mechanisms

One considers three deviatoric mechanisms: one in each slip plane $k$ (of normal ${e}_{k}$ ); moreover one identifies a behaviour valid during the local monotonic loading paths, and another behaviour, controlled by a history memory variable, on the cyclic loading paths (from the unloading starting from the state reached at the end of a monotonic path).

##### 1.1.2.1 Deviatoric criteria under monotonic loading

The deviatoric mechanisms in the plane $({{e}_{i}}_{k},{{e}_{j}}_{k})$ for a monotonic path are governed by the criterion:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}{\text{,r}}_{k}^{m})={q}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})+{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}).F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}).({r}_{k}^{m}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{d})\le 0
$$

with ${p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ and ${q}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})$ defined in [eq 1.1.1-5], [eq 1.1.1-6] and [eq 7-2];

and the function which characterises the strength of the soil as a function of the volumetric plastic strain:

$$
F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma}),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})=M.\left(1-{b}_{h}\ln\lvert\frac{{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}\rvert\right) \Rightarrow \frac{\partial F}{\partial {p}_{k}}=-\frac{{b}_{h}M}{{p}_{k}};\frac{\partial F}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}=-{b}_{h}\beta M
$$

in which three other material parameters intervene: $M=\sin{\varphi}_{\mathrm{pp}}$ (${\varphi}_{\mathrm{pp}}$  is the internal friction angle, the positive coefficient ${b}_{h}$ and the parameter ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{d}\in]0,1[$ which characterises the size of the threshold in the initial state); the critical pressure ${P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ is defined by [eq 1.1.1-13].

The criterion [eq 1.1.2-1] is strongly inspired by the original Cam-Clay  model. Nevertheless, the expression was modified by Hujeux , because this model underestimated the deviatoric strains for over-consolidated clays. In order to correct this weakness, he modified the loading surface by introducing a dependence as a function of the deviatoric strains without modifying the Roscoe volumetric dilatancy flow rule , cf. [§ 1.1.2.2]. Compared with Cam-Clay, the Hujeux model makes it possible to model the cycles better.

[fig. 1.1-a] visualises the effect of the critical pressure ${P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ and of the mobilisation factor ${r}_{k}^{m}$ on the threshold positioned with respect to the critical-state line (angle ${\varphi}_{\mathrm{pp}}$ ) and to the dilatancy line (angle $\psi$ , cf. [eq 1.1.2-3]).

##### 1.1.2.2 Flow and hardening laws under monotonic loading for the deviatoric mechanisms

The first part of the plastic strain rates for the deviatoric mechanism is decomposed into a purely deviatoric part, respecting an associated flow law (normal flow), and a spherical part, non-associated, respecting the principles of the Roscoe dilatancy law . This is a “non-generalised standard material”. The flow law is thus written:

$$
{({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}})}_{(k)}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}.\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}}{{\text{2q}}_{k}}-\frac{{\zeta}_{0}.\zeta ({r}_{k}^{m}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{d})}{2}.\left(\sin\psi +\frac{{q}_{k}}{{p}_{k}}\right).{\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}\right)
$$

The tensor ${\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}$ denotes the flow direction. The dilatancy angle $\psi$ and ${\zeta}_{0}$ are material parameters. The function $\zeta (r)$ makes it possible to control the effect of the volume variation during a plastic flow on a deviatoric mechanism (a flow which is then *non-standard* ). Up to a certain level of shear, the volume variation is zero; beyond that it comes into play. The function $\zeta (r)$ has the expression:

$$
\zeta (r)=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}0& \text{si }r\le {r}_{\text{hys}}& \text{domaine pseudo-élastique}\\ {\left(\frac{r-{r}_{\text{hys}}}{{r}_{\text{mob}}-{r}_{\text{hys}}}\right)}^{{x}_{m}}& \text{si }{r}_{\text{hys}}<r\le {r}_{\text{mob}}& \text{domaine hystérétique}\\ 1& \text{si }{\text{r>r}}_{\text{mob}}& \text{domaine plastique}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

where ${r}_{\text{hys}},{r}_{\text{mob}},{x}_{m}$ are new parameters of the deviatoric mechanisms.

One calls *characteristic state*  the situation corresponding to evolutions where ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}=0$ (zero evolution of the *contractancy* ). In the Cam Clay model, cf.  , this zero evolution of the *contractancy* , followed by a phase of *dilatancy*  where ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}={\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p}.\boldsymbol{I}>0$ , produces the stopping of the positive hardening of the material, but this is not the case of the Hujeux model, for which the negative hardening — the strength decreases, cf. [eq 1.1.2-2] — occurs later.

The evolution law followed by the internal hardening variable ${r}_{k}^{m}$ (monotonic mobilisation factor) is governed by the same plastic multipliers ${\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}$ as on ${({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p})}_{(k)}^{m}$ :

$$
{\dot{r}}_{k}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}\cdot{\rho}_{k}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}\frac{{(1-{r}_{k}^{m}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{d})}^{2}}{{a}_{c}+\zeta ({r}_{k}^{m}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{d})\cdot({a}_{m}-{a}_{c})}
$$

where ${a}_{m},{a}_{c}$ are new (strictly positive) parameters of the deviatoric mechanisms. One must always have ${\dot{r}}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ ; moreover, [eq 1.1.2-5] imposes that ${r}_{k}^{m}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{d}\le 1$ . The hardening is positive in the pre-peak phase and negative in the post-peak phase.

The plastic multipliers ${\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}$ , which must be positive, are obtained by solution of the Kühn-Tücker “complementarity” equation, together with the “consistency” condition:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}.{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{\mathrm{m}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}{\text{,r}}_{k}^{m})=0 \text{ et }{\dot{\mathrm{f}}}_{k}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{m})=0={\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{m}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}\cdot{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}+{\mathrm{f}}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{m}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\dot{r}}_{k}^{m}
$$

whence, by combining with [eq  1.1.1-9], in the case where only this monotonic mechanism $k$ is activated:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}\cdot\mathrm{\boldsymbol{I}}+{f}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{m}}^{m}.{\rho}_{k}^{m}}=\frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{m}.\boldsymbol{C}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}.\boldsymbol{C}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{m}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\rho}_{k}^{m}}
$$

and in the general case, one must take into account the contribution of all the active mechanisms to the plastic flow ${\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p}$ , cf. [eq 1.1.5-1]:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{m}=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{m}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}\cdot(\sum_{(\text{K,t})\mathrm{\ne }(\text{m,k})}{\dot{\lambda}}_{t}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(t)}^{K}.\mathrm{I})}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+{\mathrm{f}}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{m}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\rho}_{k}^{m}}=\frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}+(\sum_{(\text{K,t})\mathrm{\ne }(\text{m,k})}{\dot{\lambda}}_{t}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(t)}^{K}).({\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}.\boldsymbol{I})\rangle }_{+}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}^{\mathrm{p}}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\mathrm{r}}_{k}^{\mathrm{m}}}^{\mathrm{m}}.{\rho}_{k}^{m}}
$$

The various terms appearing here are computed in [eq  7-13], [eq  7-14], [eq  7-15]. The expression [eq 1.1.2-8] contributes to the calculation of the stress increment $\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ from which one draws the tangent operator, cf. [§ 2].

##### 1.1.2.3 Deviatoric criteria under cyclic loading

When the cyclic mechanisms come into play, the monotonic mechanisms are “frozen”, that is to say the associated internal variables remain constant. A “child” cyclic deviatoric mechanism is triggered when an unloading occurs on a previously activated “parent” monotonic mechanism, and when one violates the criterion of this cyclic mechanism on the current state at the time instant $t$ . The condition is thus written:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{\mathrm{m}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} (t),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}(t),{r}_{k}^{m}(t))\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(t)<0 \text{ et } {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{\mathrm{c}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} (t),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}(t),{r}_{k}^{c}(t))>0
$$

This may also occur between two cyclic mechanisms, “parent” and “child”, when a change of direction occurs, cf. [fig.  1.1.2-9b <>]:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{\text{c1}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} (t),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}(t),{r}_{k}^{\text{c1}}(t))\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(t)<0 \text{ et } {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{\text{c2}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} (t),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}(t),{r}_{k}^{\text{c2}}(t))>0
$$

A relative-proximity criterion is however introduced in order to leave the “parent” mechanism active, to the detriment of the “child” mechanism.

In the event of a micro-unloading on a stress path, followed by a reloading, the “child” mechanism is deactivated in favour of the “parent” mechanism, in order to recover the value of the hardening modulus of the latter rather than to follow a path of elastic slope, which would be physically questionable; this is treated at the level of the numerical integration, cf. [§ 2.2.3.1].

It may also happen that a monotonic mechanism “succeeds” a cyclic mechanism, when its loading surface reaches that of the monotonic mechanism, cf. [fig. 1.1-b].

The precise description of the sequencing of the monotonic and cyclic mechanisms, with the recording of the discrete memory variables and the possible restorations of the hardening variables, is a constitutive element of the Hujeux model: it is presented in detail in [§ 2.2.3.1].

The loading surface of each cyclic mechanism is a general formulation common to the three deviatoric planes considered. The cyclic deviatoric criteria are written:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})={q}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})+{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ).F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}).({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\le 0
$$

where ${q}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})$ is a variant, for the cyclic mechanisms, of ${q}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )$ :

$$
{q}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})={\parallel{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})\parallel}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}
$$

with:

$$
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})={\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )-{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )\cdot F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})\cdot\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}+\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\right)
$$

The function $F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ is the same as that defined in [eq  1.1.2-2]. The parameter ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}\in]0,1[$ often has the same value as ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{d}$ for the deviatoric criterion on a monotonic path, cf. [§ 1.1.2.1].

The tensor $\boldsymbol{{X}}_{(k)}^{H}$ is a function of the discontinuous memory variables [§ 1.1.1], in the plane of the mechanism considered, necessary for the description of the history of each of the deviatoric mechanisms, defined initially from the monotonic mechanism; it makes it possible to describe the kinematic behaviour of the hardening. The indices $H$ refer to the memory variables modified at each mechanism transition with a change of direction of the loadings. Its expression is:

$$
\boldsymbol{{X}}_{(k)}^{H}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}}{{p}_{\text{kH}}\cdot F({p}_{\text{kH}},{\varepsilon}_{\text{vH}}^{p})}
$$

**Note:**

*As* ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot \boldsymbol{I}=0$ and also ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot{\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}=0$ , *it is indeed a deviatoric tensor.*

*One may reformulate the cyclic criterion* [eq 1.1.2-9b] *by normalising the equivalent stress* ${q}_{k}^{c}$ *by* ${p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ).F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ *, in such a way that one can interpret the criterion geometrically by a circle of radius* ${r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{ela}}^{d}$ *:*

${\tilde{\mathrm{f}}}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{X}_{(k)},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})={\tilde{q}}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})-({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\le 0$ *.*

**Nota:**

*When one reaches the first cyclic loading after a first monotonic path in the given history, one has:*     ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}=\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}}{{p}_{\text{kH}}\cdot F({p}_{\text{kH}},{\varepsilon}_{\text{vH}}^{p})}$ *,* ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}={\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )$ *,therefore:*

${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})=-{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )\cdot F({p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})\cdot\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})$ *at the initialisation of the* *cyclic path.*

##### 1.1.2.4 Flow and hardening laws under cyclic loading

The contribution of these cyclic deviatoric mechanisms to the plastic strain rate has the same form as for the monotonic mechanism [eq  1.1.2-3] :

$$
{({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p})}_{(k)}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}\cdot\left(\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{2{q}_{k}^{c}}-\frac{{\zeta}_{0}.\zeta ({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})}{2}.\left(\sin\psi +\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}.{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{2{p}_{k}{q}_{k}^{c}}\right).{\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}\right)
$$

where ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}$ is defined by [eq 1.1.2] and the function $\zeta (r)$ defined by [eq 1.1.2-4]. The tensor ${\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{\mathrm{c}}$ denotes the flow direction, which is *non-standard* .

The evolution law associated with the hardening variables ${r}_{k}^{c}$ is identical to that stated for the monotonic mobilisation factor ${r}_{k}^{m}$ [eq 1.1.2-5]:

$$
{\dot{r}}_{k}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}.{\rho}_{k}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}\frac{{(1-{r}_{k}^{c}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})}^{2}}{{a}_{c}+\zeta ({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}).({a}_{m}-{a}_{c})}\cdot {\gamma}_{k}^{c}
$$

with:

$$
{\gamma}_{k}^{c}=\frac{{\text{2q}}_{k}^{c}.{\mathrm{\parallel }{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{\parallel }}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}{{\text{2q}}_{k}^{c}\cdot{\mathrm{\parallel }{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{c}}\mathrm{\parallel }}_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\mathrm{H}}^{\mathrm{c}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{\mathrm{c}}}
$$

**Nota:**

*The last factor appearing in the denominator of* [eq 1.1.2-15] *is not noted in the references*  ,  , *but is present in the Gefdyn software; this factor makes it possible for the predictions to be closer to the experimental results. The denominator in* ${\gamma}_{k}^{c}$ *must not in practice be zero (* ${\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}$ *not being identical to* ${{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}_{H}$ *); however, before convergence of the iterations, if this occurs, one simply sets* ${\gamma}_{k}^{c}=1$ *, as in the monotonic case, cf.* [eq 1.1.2-5]. *This choice of* ${\gamma}_{k}^{c}$ *makes it possible to obtain the equality of the plastic moduli* ${\mathrm{f}}_{k,{r}_{k}^{c}}^{c}\cdot {\rho}_{k}^{c}={\mathrm{f}}_{k,{r}_{k}^{m}}^{m}\cdot {\rho}_{k}^{m}$ *, in accordance with the experimental observation.*

*One must always have* ${\dot{r}}_{k}^{c}\ge 0$ , moreover, [eq 1.1.2-15] *imposes that* ${r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}\le 1$ *.*

The plastic multipliers ${\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}$ , which must be positive, are obtained by solution of the Kühn-Tücker complementarity equation, together with the consistency condition:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}.{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})=0 \text{ et }{\dot{\mathrm{f}}}_{k}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{c})=0
$$

whence, by combining with [eq  1.1.1-9], in the case where only this mechanism is activated:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+{\mathrm{f}}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{k}^{c}}=\frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{{\text{k,r}}_{k}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{k}^{c}}
$$

and in the general case, one must take into account the contribution of all the active mechanisms to the plastic flow $\dot{{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}}$ , cf. [eq 1.1.5-1]:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{c}=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\cdot(\sum_{(K,t)\mathrm{\ne }(c,k)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{t}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(t)}^{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{I})}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{r}_{k}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{k}^{c}}=\frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}+(\sum_{(K,t)\mathrm{\ne }(c,k)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{t}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(t)}^{K}).({\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I})\rangle }_{+}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{k,\sigma }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{k,{r}_{k}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{k}^{c}}
$$

The various terms appearing here are computed in [eq  7-16], [eq  7-17], [eq  7-18]. The expression [eq 1.1.2-18] contributes to the calculation of the stress increment $\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ , from which one draws the tangent operator, cf. [§ 2].

#### 1.1.3 Spherical consolidation elastoplastic mechanisms

On consolidation paths (spherical stresses), the various deviatoric mechanisms do not participate. However, geomaterials present a strong non-linearity for this type of loading. One therefore identifies, in the domain of compressions $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )<0$ , a consolidation behaviour valid during the local monotonic loading paths, and another consolidation behaviour, controlled by a history variable on a cyclic loading path (from the unloading starting from a monotonic path). They act on the volumetric behaviour of the material and therefore on the spherical terms of the tensors. These mechanisms are coupled to the deviatoric mechanisms in order to take into account the phenomenon of density hardening linked to the deviatoric behaviour of the material.

##### 1.1.3.1 Consolidation criterion under monotonic loading

The consolidation mechanism for a monotonic path is governed by the criterion:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{4}^{m})= \lvert p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )\rvert+{d}.{P}_{\text{c0}}.{e}^{-\beta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}.({r}_{4}^{m}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{s})\le 0
$$

in which two new material parameters intervene: $d$  (positive), the distance between the critical-state line and the isotropic consolidation line, in the plane $(\ln\lvert p \rvert ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ , and ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{s}$ , cf. [fig. 1.1-c]. The parameter ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{s}\in]0,1[$ characterises the size of the threshold in the initial state.

**Note:**

*One observes in* [eq 1.1.3-1] *that if one passes from a contractive phase* ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}<0$ *to a dilative phase* ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}>0$ *, the hardening passes from a positive state to a negative state (weakening of the threshold): this passage point during the evolution therefore constitutes a “delicate” point for the integration of the Hujeux model in a structural analysis.*

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/Object_183.svg)

*Critical-state line and isotropic consolidation line.*

##### 1.1.3.2 Flow and hardening laws under monotonic loading

For the consolidation mechanism, respecting an associated flow law, one defines a contribution to the plastic strain rates:

$$
{({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p})}_{4}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(4)}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}.\frac{p}{3\lvert  p\rvert }\cdot\boldsymbol{I}=\frac{1}{3}{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}.\text{sgn}(p)\cdot\boldsymbol{I}
$$

where ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}$ denotes the monotonic spherical plastic multiplier, which must be positive. One will denote: $\mathrm{sgn}(p)=\frac{p}{\lvert p \rvert}$ . In practice, ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}\cdot p\le 0$ .

The evolution law followed by the hardening variable ${r}_{4}^{m}$ (mobilisation factor) is driven by the same plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}$ :

$$
{\dot{r}}_{4}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}{\rho}_{4}^{m}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}\frac{{(1-{r}_{4}^{m}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{s})}^{2}}{{c}_{m}}\frac{{P}_{\text{réf}}}{{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}
$$

where ${c}_{m}$ is a (strictly positive) parameter of the monotonic consolidation mechanism. ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ is the reference confining pressure and ${P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ the critical pressure [eq  1.1.1-13]. One must always have ${\dot{r}}_{4}^{m}\ge 0$ , moreover, [eq 1.1.3-3] imposes that ${r}_{4}^{m}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{s}\le 1$ .

The plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}$ is obtained by solution of the Kühn-Tücker complementarity equation, together with the “consistency” condition:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}.{\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{4}^{m})=0 \text{ et } {\dot{f}}_{4}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{4}^{m})=0={\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{m}}^{m}.{\dot{r}}_{4}^{m}
$$

whence, by combining with [eq 1.1.1-9], in the case where only this mechanism is activated (nota: $\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}=3K\cdot \boldsymbol{I}$ , cf. [eq 6-25]):

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m} &=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.\text{sgn}(p)+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{m}}^{m}.{\rho}_{4}^{m}}= \frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{K(p).\text{sgn}(p).{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\sigma }^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.\text{sgn}(p)-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{m}}^{m}.{\rho}_{4}^{m}}\\
&= \frac{K(p).{\langle \text{sgn}(p).\text{tr}\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{K(p)\text{+d}.\beta .{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}).\text{sgn}(p).({r}_{4}^{m}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{s})-\frac{d.{P}_{\text{réf}}}{{c}_{m}}{(1-{r}_{4}^{m}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{s})}^{2}}
\end{aligned}
$$

and in the general case, one must take into account the contribution of all the active mechanisms to the plastic flow ${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ , cf. [eq 1.1.5-1]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}&=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}.\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.\left(\sum_{(\text{K,k})\ne (m,4)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\right)}{{f}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(4)}^{m}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{m}}^{m}.{\rho}_{4}^{m}}\\
&= \frac{{\langle 3K(p).\text{sgn}(p).\text{tr}\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}+\left(\sum_{(\text{K,k})\ne (m,4)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\right).(K(p).\text{sgn}(p)-{f}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m})\rangle }_{+}}{K(p)-{f}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{m}.\text{sgn}(p)-{f}_{4,{r}_{4}^{m}}^{m}.{\rho}_{4}^{m}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The various terms appearing here are computed in [eq  7-19], [eq  7-20], [eq  7-21]. The expression [eq 1.1.3-5] contributes to the calculation of the stress increment $\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ , from which one draws the tangent operator, cf. [§ 2].

##### 1.1.3.3 Consolidation criterion under cyclic loading

When the cyclic mechanism comes into play, the monotonic mechanism is “frozen”. The “child” cyclic consolidation mechanism is triggered when:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{4}^{m})=0 \text{;}{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{m}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} (t),{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}(t),{r}_{4}^{m}(t))\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\left(\frac{p(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}\right)\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}(t)<0
$$

The cyclic consolidation criterion is written in the same form as in the monotonic case [eq  1.1.3-1] :

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{p}_{H},{\varepsilon}_{\text{vH}}^{p},{r}_{4}^{c})=\lvert {p}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{p}_{H},{\varepsilon}_{\text{vH}}^{p})\rvert +{d}.{P}_{\text{c0}}.{e}^{-\beta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}.({r}_{4}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}})\le 0
$$

where ${p}^{c}$ denotes a variant of $p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )$  for the cyclic mechanisms:

$$
{p}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{p}_{H},{\varepsilon}_{\text{vH}}^{p})=\lvert p(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )\rvert+{p}_{H}.{e}^{-\beta ({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}-{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{vH}}^{p})}
$$

where ${p}_{H}$ (memory variable) denotes the value reached by $p$ at the beginning of the path on which the cyclic mechanism is triggered, cf. [fig. 1.1-d]. The parameter ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}}\in]0,1[$ , often having the same value as for the monotonic criterion, characterises the size of the threshold in the initial state.

**Note:**

*The expression* [eq 1.1.3-9] *is that chosen by the ECP [bib3]_ today, whereas Hujeux [bib4]_ had proposed a formulation also involving* ${r}_{4}^{c}$.

##### 1.1.3.4 Flow and hardening laws under cyclic loading

The contribution of the cyclic consolidation mechanism to the plastic strain rate has the same form as for the monotonic mechanism:

$$
{({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p})}_{4}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(4)}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}.\frac{{p}^{c}.p}{3\lvert {p}^{c}\rvert .\lvert p \rvert }\cdot\boldsymbol{I}=\frac{1}{3}{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}.\text{sgn}(p).\text{sgn}({p}^{c})\cdot\boldsymbol{I}
$$

where $\dot{{\lambda}_{4}^{c}}$  denotes the cyclic spherical plastic multiplier, which must be positive, and where ${p}^{c}$ is defined by [eq.  1.1.3-9].

The evolution law followed by the hardening variable ${r}_{4}^{c}$ is driven by the same plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}$ :

$$
{\dot{r}}_{4}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}{\rho}_{4}^{c}={\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}\frac{{(1-{r}_{4}^{c}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}})}^{2}}{{\text{2c}}_{c}}\frac{{P}_{\text{réf}}}{{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}
$$

${c}_{c}$ is a (strictly positive) parameter of the cyclic consolidation mechanism. ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ is the reference confining pressure and ${P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$  the reference critical pressure [eq 1.1.1-13]. One must always have ${\dot{r}}_{4}^{\text{c}}\ge 0$ , moreover, [eq 1.1.3-11] imposes that ${r}_{4}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}}\le 1$ .

**Nota:**

*The factor 2 appearing in the denominator of* [eq 1.1.3-11] *is not noted in the references [bib3]_, [bib4]_, but is present in the Gefdyn software; this factor makes it possible for the predictions to be closer to the experimental results.*

The plastic multiplier ${\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}$ is obtained by solution of the Kühn-Tücker complementarity equation, together with the consistency condition:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}.{\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{p}_{H}{\text{,p}}_{H}^{c},{r}_{4}^{c})=0 \text{ et } {\dot{\mathrm{f}}}_{4}^{c}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{p}_{H}{\text{,p}}_{H}^{c},{r}_{4}^{c})=0
$$

whence, by combining with [eq  1.1.1-9], in the case where only this mechanism is activated:

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\frac{{p}^{c}}{\lvert {p}^{c} \rvert }\frac{p}{\lvert p \rvert }+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{4}^{c}}=\frac{{\langle {\mathrm{f}}_{4,\sigma }^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\sigma }^{c}\frac{{p}^{c}}{3\lvert {p}^{c}\rvert }\frac{p}{\lvert p\rvert }\cdot \boldsymbol{C}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\frac{{p}^{c}}{\lvert {p}^{c}\rvert }\frac{p}{\lvert p\rvert }-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{4}^{c}}
$$

and in the general case, one must take into account the contribution of all the active mechanisms to the plastic flow $\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}$ , cf. [eq 1.1.5-1]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}&=-\frac{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{c}.\dot{\sigma}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}.\left(\sum_{(\text{K,k})\ne (c,4)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\right)}{{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(4)}^{c}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{r}_{4}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{4}^{c}}\\
&=\frac{{\langle 3K(p).\text{sgn}(p).\text{sgn}({p}^{c})\text{tr}\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}+\left(\sum_{(\text{K,k})\ne (c,4)}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\right).(K(p).\text{sgn}(p).\text{sgn}({p}^{c})-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c})\rangle }_{+}}{K(p)-{\mathrm{f}}_{4,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}^{c}\text{sgn}(p).\text{sgn}({p}^{c})-{f}_{4,{r}_{4}^{c}}^{c}.{\rho}_{4}^{c}}
\end{aligned}
$$

The various terms appearing here are computed in [eq  7-22], [eq  7-23], [eq  7-24]. The expression [eq 1.1.3-13] contributes to the calculation of the stress increment $\dot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}$ , from which one draws the tangent operator, cf. [§ 2].

#### 1.1.4 Treatment of tension complementary to the Hujeux law

Even if the usual loading regime of the soil is compression, it is possible to find oneself in tension, either locally (in space and/or in time), or simply during the iterations of solution of the non-linear system of equilibrium equations. It is therefore necessary to define a behaviour in tension. A perfect elastoplastic mechanism is thus proposed, with zero cohesion, in each plane $k$ , as for the deviatoric criteria in compression.

The criterion is established on the confining pressure ${p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)})$ in the plane $k$ :

$$
{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{\text{tr}}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ){\text{=p}}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )-{p}_{0}^{\text{tr}}\le 0
$$

with ${p}_{0}^{\text{tr}}={10}^{-6}.\lvert {P}_{\text{réf}} \rvert$ .

The flow law is associated:

$$
{({\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}^{p})}_{k}^{\text{tr}}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{\mathrm{tr}}{({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\text{rs}})}_{(k)}^{{\text{tr}}_{(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )}}={\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{\text{tr}}.\frac{1}{2}{\boldsymbol{I}}_{k}
$$

The plastic multiplier $\dot{{\lambda}_{k}^{\mathrm{tr}}}$ is obtained by solution of the Kühn-Tücker complementarity equation, together with the consistency condition.

#### 1.1.5 Total elastoplastic evolution

The total elastoplastic evolution rate results from the contributions of all these mechanisms on monotonic and cyclic paths ([eq  1.1.2-3], [eq  1.1.2-14], [eq  1.1.3-2], [eq  1.1.3-10]), without forgetting the mechanisms associated with tension, cf. [§ 1.1.4]:

$$
{\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}}_{\text{rs}}^{p}=\sum_{\text{K=m,c}}\left(\sum_{\text{k=}1}^{3}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}{({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\text{rs}})}_{(k)}^{{K}_{(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )}}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\text{sgn}(p).({\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{m}+{\dot{\lambda}}_{4}^{c}.\text{sgn}({p}^{c}))\boldsymbol{I}_{\text{rs}}+\sum_{\text{k=}1}^{3}{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{\text{tr}}{({\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{\text{rs}})}_{(k)}^{{\text{tr}}_{(\boldsymbol{\sigma})}}
$$

The volumetric plastic strain rate ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}$ (dilatancy law) is deduced directly with the aid of the relation ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}=\text{tr}{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ (it is not an internal state variable independent of ${\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ ). One specifies nevertheless that the internal state variable relating to the volumetric plastic strains does not take into account the part of the plastic strains coming from the plastic mechanisms of tension.

**Note:**

*In a 2D plane-strain or axisymmetric situation, the plane* $\text{k=}3$ *corresponds to the plane* $({e}_{1,}{e}_{2})$ *of the model. By exploiting* [eq 1.1.2-3] *and* [eq 1.1.2-14], *one notes that* ${\dot{\varepsilon}_{23}^{p}}={\dot{\varepsilon}_{31}^{p}}=0$ *. Therefore one will always have:* ${\varepsilon}_{23}^{p}={\varepsilon}_{31}^{p}=0$ *and* ${\sigma}_{23}={\sigma}_{31}=0$ *, even if the whole set of plastic mechanisms may be activated.*

### 1.2 Identification of the characteristic parameters of the material

The characteristic parameters of the material are identified with the aid of several tests and loading paths. One makes here the inventory of the parameters of the model:

- ${E}_{0}$ and ${\nu}_{0}$ : elastic characteristics at the initial confining pressure ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ , to be drawn from the data of the bulk modulus ${K}_{0}$ and of the shear modulus ${G}_{0}$ (${E}_{0}=\frac{9{K}_{0}{G}_{0}}{3{K}_{0}+{G}_{0}}$ , ${\nu}_{0}=\frac{3{K}_{0}-2{G}_{0}}{6{K}_{0}+2{G}_{0}}$ );
- $n\in [0,1[$ and ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$  : non-linear elastic characteristic and initial confining pressure ($n=0$  corresponds to linear elasticity);
- ${P}_{\mathrm{c0}}$ : initial reference critical pressure (negative);
- $\beta$ : coefficient of volumetric plastic compressibility or of the critical-state law, (positive);
- $M=\sin{\varphi }_{\mathrm{pp}}$ : slope of the critical-state line (or perfect plasticity; in the plane $(\ln\lvert p\rvert ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ ), ${\varphi }_{\mathrm{pp}}$ being the internal friction angle;
- $\psi$ : dilatancy angle defining the boundary $q=\lvert p\rvert .\sin\psi$ in the plane $(p,q)$ between the contractive domain and the dilative domain (not to be confused with the angle appearing in the Mohr–Coulomb law);
- ${b}_{h}$ : positive coefficient, influencing the loading function in the plane $(p,q)$ . If ${b}_{h}=0$ , one recovers a threshold of Mohr–Coulomb type, if ${b}_{h}=1$ , one recovers a threshold of Cam-Clay type;
- $d$ : positive coefficient, distance between the critical-state line (perfect plasticity) and the isotropic consolidation line, in the plane $(\ln\lvert p\rvert ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})$ , cf. [fig. 1.1-c];
- ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{d}\in]0,1[$ : characterises the size of the threshold of the monotonic deviatoric mechanisms in the initial state;
- ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{s}\in]0,1[$ : characterises the size of the monotonic consolidation threshold in the initial state;
- ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}\in]0,1[$ : characterises the size of the threshold of the cyclic deviatoric mechanisms in the initial state;
- ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}}\in]0,1[$ : characterises the size of the cyclic consolidation threshold in the initial state;
- ${a}_{m}$ , ${a}_{c}$ : hardening parameters ($>0$ ) of the deviatoric plastic mechanisms;
- ${c}_{m}$ , ${c}_{c}$ : hardening parameters ($>0$ ) of the consolidation mechanisms;
- ${\zeta}_{0}$ : parameter defining the amplitude of the volumetric plastic strain rate;
- ${r}_{\text{hys}}$ : parameter defining the size of the hysteretic domain;
- ${r}_{\text{mob}}$ : parameter defining the size of the mobilised domain;
- ${x}_{m}$ : parameter of the function $\zeta (r)$ , cf. [eq 1.1.2-4].

The values of the initial thresholds for an ordinary soil are often very small: ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{s}$ ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{d}$ and will be taken small, just as ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{\mathrm{dc}}$ and ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sc}}$ , which may by default have the same value as the two preceding ones. By default, one may take ${\varphi}_{\text{pp}}=\psi$ . One will note that the natural equilibrium of a slope imposes that the friction angle ${\varphi }_{\mathrm{pp}}$ be greater than that of the slope.

## 2 Numerical integration of the constitutive relation

### 2.1 Reminder of the problem

One uses the following notations: ${A}^{-},A,\Delta A$ for a quantity evaluated at the known time instant ${t}^{-}{\text{=t}}_{i-1}$ , at the time instant ${t}_{i}{\text{=t}}^{-}+\Delta t$ and its increment $\Delta t$ respectively. The equations are discretised in an implicit manner, that is to say expressed as a function of the unknown variables at the time instant ${t}_{i}{\text{=t}}^{-}+\Delta t$ .

For a given loading increment and a given set of variables (initial displacement field, stress and internal variable), one solves the discretised global system ([eq 2.2.2.2-1] of ) which seeks to satisfy the equilibrium equations.

code_aster uses a Newton method , initiated by a prediction step (Euler trial) which supplies an estimate $\Delta {\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{0}$ of the displacement increment, whence the prediction $'\boldsymbol{U}_{i}^{0}={\boldsymbol{U}}_{i-1}+\Delta {\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{0}$ , followed by correction iterations $n=1,\mathrm{...}$ , which give corrections $\delta {\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{n+1}$ of the displacement increment, whence the update ${\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{\text{n+}1}={\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{n}+\delta {\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{\text{n+}1}$ , which must converge towards the solution ${\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}={\boldsymbol{U}}^{+}$ .

The option RIGI_MECA_TANG computes the tangent operator ${\boldsymbol{K}}_{i-1}$ , for the prediction step (linearisation of the equilibrium equations about the equilibrium at the time instant ${t}_{i-1}$ ), from the relation of stress rates referred to strain rates, with the aid of the assembled strain operator ${}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}$ :

$$
{\boldsymbol{K}}_{i-1}=\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \boldsymbol{U}}{\rvert}_{({\boldsymbol{U}}_{i-1})}=\frac{{d}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}}{d\boldsymbol{U}}{\rvert}_{({\boldsymbol{U}}_{i-1})}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i-1}
$$

**Note:**

*One may decide, in order to save computation time, not to update this matrix, and to take the elastic stiffness matrix, see* [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)  *, keyword Newton, operand PREDICTION, value ‘ELASTIQUE’, rather than ‘ TANGENTE’. But this may increase the number of correction iterations, or even make the convergence delicate, in particular because of the non-linear elastic law constituting the Hujeux law* [eq 1.1.1-10].

The options RAPH_MECA and FULL_MECA compute the stresses ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i}^{n}$ , the internal variables and the nodal forces $\boldsymbol{R}({\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{n})={}^{T}\boldsymbol{Q}.{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i}^{n}$ , at the global iteration $n$.

The option FULL_MECA moreover computes the tangent operator ${\boldsymbol{K}}_{i}^{n}$ , at the global correction iteration $n$ (on request via the command STAT_NON_LINE, factor keyword NEWTON, keyword MATRICE='TANGENTE' and REAC_ITER), from the stresses ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{i}^{n}$ and from the tangent matrix of the local problem discretised in time:

$$
{{\boldsymbol{K}}_{i}^{n}=\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{R}}{\partial \boldsymbol{U}}\rvert }_{({\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{n})}
$$

The solution of these global systems of equilibrium equations gives us increments $\Delta {\boldsymbol{U}}_{i}^{n}$ , therefore strain increments $\Delta {\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}_{i}^{n}$ . One therefore seeks locally (at each Gauss point) the increment of the stresses and of the internal variables corresponding to $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ and which satisfy the constitutive law. The evolution of the stresses and of the internal variables is obtained by solution of a system of differential equations, by local integration, under the initial conditions at the beginning of the time step, described in [§ 2.2].

The multi-mechanism elastoplastic constitutive equations presented in [§ 1] do not make it possible to demonstrate by a simple analysis the uniqueness of the solution of the structural equilibrium problem, discretised in time. The integration scheme must have a certain influence on the computed solutions.

### 2.2 General scheme of local integration

The local integration schemes chosen for the implementation of the Hujeux law in code_aster are fully implicit, formulated on the incremental problem, for reasons of computational accuracy.

They use an elastic prediction then correction iterations. Their aim is to produce, a strain increment $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ being supplied, the value of the stresses and internal variables at the time instant ${t}_{i}{\text{=t}}^{-}+\Delta t$ . [fig. 2.2-a] presents the general organisation common to the local integration algorithms.

**Note:**

*The evolutions of the computed quantities of the Hujeux model through an implicit integration are reputed to be sensitive to perturbations of the data of the integration algorithm (for example according to the choice of the computing platform or of the choice of spatial discretisation…). This is why various provisions have been taken in order to limit this sensitivity: see below the description of the tolerances introduced on the increments. One must note that, because of the multi-mechanism character with memory variables of the Hujeux model, the effective activation of the mechanisms not being a differentiable process, the rate formulation does not constitute the limit of the incremental formulation in time.*

code_aster does not propose an explicit integration for this model, cf. [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14), although this method is used in the literature, cf. . The available implicit integration algorithms are 3 in number and are distinguished according to the following terms:

- ALGO_INTE = 'SPECIFIQUE', this is the 1st implicit integration scheme developed in code_aster. It is the most robust integration scheme, because it carries out for the user the local subdivisions necessary in order to ensure as far as possible a convergence of the integration scheme.
- ALGO_INTE='SEMI_EXPLICITE', this consists in relying on the integration strategy of the 'SPECIFIQUE' scheme but in accepting, during the iterations of the global Newton scheme, Gauss points that have not reached the local convergence criterion imposed by the user. Nevertheless, the global convergence is accepted only when the local integration is ensured for the whole set of Gauss points.
- ALGO_INTE="BASCULE_EXPLICIT", this consists in this case in accepting the local non-convergence of the behaviour if the global Newton has converged. This strategy has been implemented for the purpose of speeding up the computation in dynamics but does not prove worthwhile for highly non-linear modellings.

The process of local subdivision of the time step is available in order to reduce significantly the possible situations of non-convergence, and to save correction iterations. It consists in solving the local behaviour on linear subdivisions of the strain increment $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ proposed by the global solution. The local subdivision can be activated in the non-linear mechanics operator of code_aster (factor keyword COMPORTEMENT and keyword ITER_INTE_PAS, cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11).

#### 2.2.1 Prediction phase

One begins by establishing the elastic prediction ${{\sigma}_{ij}^{+}}_{é}$ (approximate tangent elastic trial – Euler):

$$
{{\sigma}_{ij}^{+}}_{é}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{C}_{\text{ijrs}}({\hat{p}}_{é}).\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}
$$

The tensor ${C}_{\text{ijrs}}({\hat{p}}_{é})$ , defined by [eq  1.1.1-11], is obtained from an expression discretised by an approximate scheme of the confining pressure:

$$
{\hat{p}}_{é}=\frac{1}{2}({p}^{-}{\text{+p}}_{é}^{+}){\text{=p}}^{-}+\frac{1}{2}{K}_{0}.{\lvert\frac{{p}^{-}}{{P}_{\text{réf}}}\rvert}^{n}.\text{tr}\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}
$$

This choice makes it possible to limit the risk of having a confining-pressure predictor producing stresses too far from the expected values, because of the exponential non-linear form of the elastic law [eq  1.1.1-11].

One begins with a first step of “re-prediction”. A re-subdivision of the strain increment $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ — additional to that possibly requested by the user via the keywords of the non-linear mechanics operator of code_aster — is introduced in such a way as to bound the following increments, associated with the prediction ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{é}$ , by a simple homothety:

$$
\Delta {p}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{é})/{p}_{k}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-})\le 5\text{\%}
\Delta {q}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{é})/{q}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-})\le 5\text{\%}
\sum_{i,j}({\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{é}}-{\sigma}_{ij}^{-})/\sum_{i,j}{\sigma}_{ij}^{-}\le 50\text{\%}
$$

The purpose is to avoid possible excursions during the correction iterations towards loading paths that are too remote, which may lead to divergence (by leaving the convergence domain of the Newton algorithm).

##### 2.2.1.1 Potentially activated plastic mechanisms ${M}^{\text{pot}}$

The elastoplastic mechanisms ${M}_{k}^{K}$ , for $\text{k=}1,...,4$ and $K=m,c$ , are activated or deactivated according to the type of loading followed. A family of potentially active mechanisms is thus determined beforehand at the beginning of the current step from the state reached previously, and serves to compute the evolution actually followed. The initial choice of the potentially activated mechanisms at the start of the time step considered results from the converged mechanical state reached at the time instant ${t}^{-}$ : the activation state of the various mechanisms (monotonic or cyclic) at the time instant ${t}^{-}$ and the values of the variables ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}$ and ${\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{-}$ .

One denotes by ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ the set of potentially activated plastic mechanisms cf. [fig. 2.2-b], and by ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$ the set of plastic mechanisms actually activated during the integration, cf. [fig. 2.2-c]. This definition makes it possible to reduce to the strict minimum the size of the system of local non-linear equations to be solved at each step. One uses the elastic prediction [eq  2.2.1-1] in order to establish a re-estimation of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ with the aid of the evolution $\dot{{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}$  for $\text{k=}1,...,4$ and $K=m,c$ , of the thresholds of the mechanisms and thus to define the situations of loading in the plastic regime or of unloading.

One therefore initialises ${M}^{\text{pot}}:={M}^{\text{act}}({t}^{-})$ , and one records the history variables of the “parent” cyclic mechanisms ${\alpha}_{H}^{-}$ , from the situation at the previous time instant ${t}^{-}$ . One computes for all the mechanisms ${M}_{k}^{K}$ two quantities helping the decision:

$$
{c}_{k}^{K}={\mathrm{f}}_{k,\boldsymbol{\sigma} }^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta {\sigma}_{\text{élas}})\cdot{C}({\hat{p}}_{é}).\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \text{ et } {g}_{k}^{K}{\text{=f}}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\text{élas}})
$$

If it is a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ then one also computes ${c}_{k}^{m}$ for the associated monotonic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ . In the event of difficulties, one proceeds to a re-subdivision of the time step $\Delta t$ . If the value of ${c}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ , and if the two loading surfaces of ${M}_{k}^{c}$ and ${M}_{k}^{m}$ are “close”, then ${M}^{\text{pot}}:={M}^{\text{pot}}\cup \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\right\rbrace \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{c}\right\rbrace$ and the hardening properties of ${M}_{k}^{c}$ are reinitialised to ${r}_{\text{éla}}^{c}$ . This proximity is established by computing the difference between the mobilisation factor reached for the monotonic threshold ${r}_{k}^{m}$ and the value of the mobilisation factor in the previous converged state ${r}_{k}^{c}$ ,

Then one reviews all the possible mechanisms ${M}_{k}^{K}$ by testing ${c}_{k}^{K}$ and ${g}_{k}^{K}$ . One thus identifies the situations:

- ${M}_{k}^{m}\in {M}_{\text{pot}}$ . Then, if ${c}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ , ${M}_{k}^{m}$ remains in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ ; otherwise, one stores the corresponding history variables ${\alpha}_{H}^{+}$ and if ${g}_{k}^{c}<0$ for the cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ associated with ${M}_{k}^{m}$ , one deactivates ${M}_{k}^{c}$ and ${M}_{k}^{m}$ ; but if ${c}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ one deactivates ${M}_{k}^{m}$ in favour of ${M}_{k}^{c}$ placed in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
- ${M}_{k}^{m}\notin {M}_{\text{pot}}$ . Then, if ${c}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ and ${g}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ , then ${M}^{\text{pot}}:{\text{=M}}^{\text{pot}}\cup \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\right\rbrace$ , but if ${g}_{k}^{m}<0$ ${M}_{k}^{m}$ remains outside ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .

- ${M}_{k}^{c}\in {M}_{\text{pot}}$ . Then, if ${c}_{k}^{c}\ge 0$ , ${M}_{k}^{c}$ remains in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ ; otherwise, one stores the corresponding history variables ${\alpha}_{H}^{+}$ and if ${g}_{k}^{c}\ge 0$ , one keeps ${M}_{k}^{c}$ in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , but if ${g}_{k}^{c}<0$ , ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
- ${M}_{k}^{c}\notin {M}_{\text{pot}}$ . Then, if ${c}_{k}^{c}\ge 0$ and ${g}_{k}^{c}\ge 0$ , then ${M}^{\text{pot}}:{\text{=M}}^{\text{pot}}\cup \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{c}\right\rbrace$ and one manages the potentiality of micro-unloadings for the deviatoric mechanisms only: one tests the threshold of the “parent” mechanism of ${M}_{k}^{c}$ : if ${g}_{k}^{{c}^{'}}\ge 0$ then the “child” mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is replaced by this “parent” mechanism ${M}_{k}^{{c}^{'}}$ with update of the history variables ${\alpha}_{H}^{+}$ . If ${c}_{k}^{c}<0$ , one stores the corresponding history variables and if ${r}_{k}^{c}\ge {r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}$ one creates a “child” mechanism ${M}_{k}^{{c}^{'}}$ , whose loading surface is tangent to that of the “parent” mechanism, which is placed in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ if its threshold satisfies ${g}_{k}^{{c}^{'}}\ge 0$ , otherwise, ${M}_{k}^{{c}^{'}}$ is deactivated.

Of course, if ${M}_{k}^{K}\in {M}^{\text{pot}}$ for $K=m$ or $c$ , then the “sibling” mechanism for $K=m$ or $c$ is not in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ . If at the end of the analysis of the various situations above, no mechanism remains in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ then the regime will be elastic, see cf. [fig. 2.2-b].

The elastic regime occurs when no mechanism is activated.

One imposes that the prediction ${\tilde{p}}_{{k}_{é}}$ of each mechanism be strictly negative in order to be able to respect the domains of definition of the various derivatives which intervene in the equations below: one thus obtains the stress prediction ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{\mathrm{ijé}}^{+}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{C}_{\text{ijrs}}({\hat{p}}_{é}).\eta .\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}$ where $\hat{{p}_{é}}$ is defined in [eq 2.2.1-2] and with $\eta$ such that ${\tilde{p}}_{{k}_{é}}\le {10}^{-6}.{P}_{\text{réf}}$ . One denotes hereafter ${\tilde{Y}}_{é}=({\tilde{\sigma}}_{\mathrm{ijé}}^{+},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{{p}^{-}},{r}_{k}^{{K}^{-}},0)$ .

One then seeks an explicit trial solution by computing a candidate ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}$ with the aid of the potentially active mechanisms ${M}_{k}^{K}$ , by solving the equations linearised about the state without plastic evolution ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{é}$ :

$$
\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{é}}.{(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}=-{\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\boldsymbol{C}({\hat{p}}_{é})\cdot\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} ,{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p-},{r}_{k}^{K-})
$$

with:

$$
\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{é}}=-\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{k}^{K}}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{é}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({\hat{p}}_{é}).{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}+\frac{\partial {f}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{é}}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+\frac{\partial {f}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}{\mid}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{é}}.{\rho}_{k}^{K-}
$$

the derivatives being given by [eq  7-13] to [eq  7-24]. Whence then the trial solution $\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{0}$ :

$$
\begin{aligned}
&{(\Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma} )}_{0}={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\boldsymbol{C}({\hat{p}}_{é}).(\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} -\sum_{\text{k,K}}{(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-})\\
&{(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}_{0}=\sum_{\text{k,K}}{(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}\\
&{(\Delta {r}_{k}^{K})}_{0}={(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}.{\rho}_{k}^{K-}
\end{aligned}
$$

Moreover, one imposes the condition ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}\ge 0$ ; the mobilisation factors are also bounded on the trial solution: ${({r}_{k}^{m})}_{0}\le 1$ and ${({r}_{k}^{c})}_{0}\le {({r}_{k}^{m})}_{0}$ , in such a way that the trial solution respects the plausibility of the evolution of the internal variables. On the basis of these criteria, one also introduces a restriction on the evolution of the volumetric plastic strain as well, $\lvert{(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}_{0}\rvert\le 10\text{\%}$ .

**Note:**

*The expression of the critical pressure as a function of the volumetric plastic strain involving an exponential* [eq  1.1.1-13], *one prevents the risk of values that are too high, overflowing the convergence domain, by triggering a subdivision of the given strain increment* $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ *if necessary.*

#### 2.2.2 Correction phase: non-linear equations to be solved

This step consists in solving the system of local non-linear equations established on the basis of the potentially activated mechanisms ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ . After convergence, one re-evaluates the set of mechanisms actually activated ${M}^{\text{act}}$ , cf. [§ 2.2.3.1], and if there is a difference with ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , one restarts the local non-linear solution with ${M}^{\text{act}}$ .

##### 2.2.2.1 Newton correction iterations

The Newton correction iterations consist in solving the following equations, for $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ given, see [eq  6-1]:

- the incremental state equation [eq 1.1.1-9], denoted ${\text{LE}}_{ij}$ (6 scalar equations),
- the incremental evolution of the volumetric plastic strain $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}$ [eq  1.1.5-1]: $\text{LEVP}$ ,
- the incremental evolution of the mobilisation factors $\Delta {r}_{k}^{K}$ [eq 1.1.2-5], [eq 1.1.2-15], [eq 1.1.3-3], and [eq 1.1.3-11], denoted $\text{LR.1}$ and $\text{LR.2}$ (1 for the deviatoric mechanisms, 2 for the consolidation mechanisms; as many equations as there are active mechanisms),
- the criteria of the various potentially active mechanisms ${\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}=0$ [eq 1.1.2-1], [eq 1.1.2-9], [eq 1.1.3-1], and [eq 1.1.3-7], denoted $\text{LF.1}$ and $\text{LF.2}$ (1 for the deviatoric mechanisms, 2 for the consolidation mechanisms; as many equations as there are active mechanisms).

They constitute a square system $\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}\left(\Delta \mathrm{\boldsymbol{Y}}\right)=0$ , where the unknowns are $\Delta \boldsymbol{Y}=(\Delta {\sigma}_{ij},\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},\Delta {r}_{k}^{K},\Delta (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}))$ , which couple the equations (15 in number at most and 9 at least). One solves implicitly the system $\boldsymbol{R}(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})=0$ by a Newton method, for $K=m,c$ and $k=1,\mathrm{...},4$ .

At iteration $j$ of the local Newton correction loop, one solves the matrix equation:

$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{R}}{d(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})}{\rvert}_{\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j}}.\delta \Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{\text{j+}1}=-R(\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j})
$$

where the tangent matrix $\frac{d\mathrm{\boldsymbol{R}}}{d\left(\Delta \mathrm{\boldsymbol{Y}}\right)}{\rvert}_{\Delta {\mathrm{\boldsymbol{Y}}}_{j}}$ , non-symmetric, is computed in the manner presented in [§ 6], according to [eq 6-2], with an update of the elasticity tensor with the updated value of the confining pressure $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\Delta {\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{j})$ at the previous iteration, according to [eq 1.1.1-11]:

$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{R}}{d(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})}{\mid}_{\Delta \boldsymbol{Y}}=\left[\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ 0& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& 0\\ \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& 0\end{array}\right]
$$

One has beforehand put the various lines of this system “to scale”, by dividing the state equations ${\text{LE}}_{ij}$ and the thresholds $\mathrm{LF}$ by the initial Young's modulus ${E}_{0}$ , the evolution laws $\mathrm{LR}$ by ${P}_{\text{réf}}/{E}_{0}$ and the stresses as well as the mobilisation factors ${r}_{k}^{K}$ in the unknown vector $\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j}$ are scaled to the strains via the initial Young's modulus ${E}_{0}$ and the pressure ${P}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ . This choice makes it possible to have equations of the same order of magnitude and therefore to ensure a more “uniform” convergence over the whole of the system [eq  2.2.2-3].

The Jacobian matrix presented above is evaluated analytically for all the integration schemes of the model.

**Note:**

*The determinant of the tangent matrix* $\frac{d\boldsymbol{R}}{d(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})}{\rvert}_{\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j}}$ *is a priori positive in the positive-hardening phase. However, it could become negative; no particular treatment is provided.*

Convergence is deemed to be acquired as soon as ($\mathrm{tol}$  is given by the keyword RESI_INTE_RELA of the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT of the command STAT_NON_LINE, cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

$$
\parallel \boldsymbol{R}(\Delta \boldsymbol{{Y}}_{\text{j+}1})\parallel \le \text{tol}
$$

this norm $\parallel .\parallel$ on the residual also using the scaling of the various terms involved.

**Note:**

*One strongly recommends using a tolerance value less than or equal to* ${10}^{\text{-7}}$ *(keyword* RESI_INTE_RELA *), at least in the most “delicate” steps of the loading path.*

In order to avoid possible excursions during the correction iterations towards loading paths that are too remote, which may lead to divergence (by leaving the local convergence domain of the Newton algorithm), the Hujeux integration algorithm known as 'SPECIFIQUE' also imposes a re-subdivision of the strain increment $\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$  if the correction step ends up producing increments of mobilisation factors $\Delta {r}_{k}^{K}/{r}_{k}^{K}\ge 10$ . Moreover, one imposes that the values predicted at this stage of the deviatoric mobilisation factors satisfy: ${r}_{k}^{{m}^{+}}\le 1$ and ${r}_{k}^{{c}^{+}}\le {r}_{k}^{{m}^{+}}$ for the active mechanisms. Moreover, one records the cases where the plastic multipliers are negative. If $-{\eta}_{\text{tolé}}\le {\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}_{k}^{K}/\underset{K=1,..,4}{\text{Max}}({\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}_{k}^{K})\le 0$ , where ${\eta}_{\text{tolé}}$ denotes the value of RESI_INTE_RELA supplied by the user, then one imposes: ${\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}_{k}^{K}=0$ . This makes it possible to limit the “harmful” effects of the mechanisms under “neutral” loading, that is to say $\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}\approx 0$ .

The last task of management of the mechanisms described below intervenes after a failure of the local Newton algorithm (§ [ 2.2.2]) or if criteria, specified below, are violated. Any modification made to the domain of potentially active mechanisms ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ will then lead to restarting, with the new ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , the solution of the non-linear system of equations. This update process is however limited to 5 reconstruction attempts, failing which one declares a non-convergence of the local integration.

- The first cases treated concern, after each iteration of the local Newton algorithm, the risk of overlap of two deviatoric loading surfaces, cyclic and monotonic or cyclic and cyclic, in the stress space. These problems are avoided via proximity tests between the position of the stress state in the deviatoric plane considered and the loading surface considered.

  - In the particular case of two deviatoric loading surfaces, cyclic and monotonic, if the proximity criterion is satisfied, the monotonic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ is added directly to the domain of mechanisms ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ while removing the cyclic deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ considered active until now in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
  - For the cases of proximity of two loading surfaces of cyclic deviatoric mechanisms “parent” and “child”, the criterion bears on the proximity of the stress state in the deviatoric plane considered with respect to the recorded value ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}$ as the point of tangency between the “parent” and “child” loading surfaces. As soon as the proximity criterion is reached, the “child” deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is removed from the potential domain of active mechanisms ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
- After these proximity tests of deviatoric loading surfaces, it is necessary to treat the tensile stress states which very often cause the non-convergence of the local non-linear system to be solved.

  - If a tensile state is detected after failure of the solution method, for every plastic multiplier associated with a deviatoric mechanism considered in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ that is negative after the explicit Euler trial, ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}$ , this deviatoric mechanism will be removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ . This solution for restarting the solution of the local non-linear system imposes no restriction on the value of the stress predictor. This predictor is established by considering a purely elastic strain increment.
  - If all the plastic multipliers are positive, i.e. ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}\ge 0$ , then all the deviatoric mechanisms are removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ : the consolidation mechanisms integrated alone make it possible to prepare a new predictor for the following local integration step after update of ${M}^{\text{act}}$ .
- For the cases different from the two large classes described above (proximity and tension), if the maximum value of the residual vector $\boldsymbol{R}(\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j})$ is carried by an evolution law of a mobilisation factor of a cyclic deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ , (that is the equation $\mathrm{LR.1}$ of [eq  6-1]), then the mechanism in question is removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
- The following case concerns only the cyclic deviatoric mechanisms never previously hardened. These mechanisms are then removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ if there has been a failure during the solution of the system of non-linear equations.
- The last cases bear on the value of the plastic multipliers after the Euler trial, ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}$ .

  - The mechanisms associated with negative values are removed from ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ .
  - If all the plastic multipliers are positive, one removes the mechanism presenting the smallest value for ${(\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}_{0}$ after the Euler trial.

If none of the specific treatments described above has been called upon, the non-convergence of the model at the local scale is assumed and this information is returned to the global integration algorithm of the non-linear equilibrium.

#### 2.2.3 Update phase

After solution, one updates the solution vector:

$$
`\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}=\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{\text{j+}1}=\Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{j}+\delta \Delta {\boldsymbol{Y}}_{\text{j+}1}
$$

which completes the local integration step, and one sets ${M}^{\text{act}}={M}^{\text{pot}}$ to begin with.

##### 2.2.3.1 Plastic mechanisms actually activated ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$

One describes below the management of the actual activation of the plastic mechanisms. After convergence of the local non-linear system of equations, one is led to verify the set of mechanisms actually activated ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$ according to the following procedure, starting from the prediction ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , cf. [fig. 2.2-c]. This decision tree is a constitutive element of the Hujeux model, since it describes the sequencing of the cyclic mechanisms and the recordings of the discrete memory variables and the possible restorations of the hardening variables, cf. also [fig. 1.1] and [fig. 1.1 -c].

**Note:**

*With an explicit integration, cf.* , *one would rather proceed with re-subdivisions (sub-incrementation) in order to manage the mechanism transitions.*

One therefore reviews all the mechanisms, any modification of ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$ leading to restarting a new local non-linear solution, cf. [§  2.2.2.1]:

- case of an active monotonic deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ with a negative plastic multiplier $\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}$ :

  - if ${r}_{k}{\text{=r}}_{\text{éla}}^{d}$ , then this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ is deactivated: ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\rbrace$ ;
  - otherwise (calling into question the estimate made in ${M}_{\text{pot}}$ ):

    - if a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ existed prior to the step of ${M}_{\text{pot}}$ , one keeps the memory variables of this mechanism, one declares it created and one removes the monotonic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ : ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\rbrace$ ,
    - if no cyclic mechanism existed, one stores the memory variables and one creates a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ and one removes ${M}_{k}^{m}$ : ${M}_{\text{act}}:{\text{=M}}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\rbrace$ .
- case of an active monotonic isotropic mechanism ${M}_{4}^{m}$ with a negative plastic multiplier:

  - if ${r}_{4}{\text{=r}}_{\text{éla}}^{s}$ , then this mechanism ${M}_{4}^{m}$ is deactivated: ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{4}^{m}\rbrace$ ,
  - otherwise:

    - if a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{4}^{c}$ existed prior to the step of ${M}_{\text{pot}}$ , one keeps the memory variables of this mechanism, one declares it created and one removes ${M}_{4}^{m}$ : ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{4}^{m}\rbrace$ ,
    - if no cyclic mechanism exists, one stores the memory variables and one creates a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{4}^{c}$ only if $\dot{\overline{\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}}<0$ (one recalls that ${p}_{c}$ depends on ${\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}$ , which itself varies as a function of all the mechanisms), and in this case, one removes ${M}_{4}^{m}$ .
- case of a monotonic deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ created but inactive: if the threshold ${f}_{k}^{m}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then this mechanism becomes active: ${M}_{\text{act}}:{\text{=M}}_{\text{act}}\cup \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\right\rbrace$ , and the hardening variables of the associated cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ take the virgin initial values.
- case of a monotonic isotropic mechanism ${M}_{4}^{m}$ created but inactive: if the threshold ${f}_{4}^{m}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then this mechanism becomes active: ${M}_{\text{act}}:{\text{=M}}_{\text{act}}\cup \left\lbrace {M}_{4}^{m}\right\rbrace$ ; if the threshold ${f}_{4}^{m}({t}^{+})<0$ then one stores the memory variables and one creates a cyclic mechanism and one removes ${M}_{4}^{m}$ only if $\dot{\overline{\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}}<0$ .
- case of a cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ created previously in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ from a monotonic mechanism:

  - if the plastic multiplier is negative then the mechanism is deactivated: ${M}_{\text{act}}:{\text{=M}}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{k}^{c}\rbrace$ ; otherwise, one goes on to:
  - if the threshold of the associated monotonic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{m}$ ${f}_{k}^{m}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then:

    - for a deviatoric mechanism, one passes to monotonic: ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{k}^{c}\rbrace \cup \left\lbrace {M}_{k}^{m}\right\rbrace$ and the hardening variables of the cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ recover the virgin initial values,
    - for an isotropic mechanism: if the directional history variable changes sign between ${t}^{-}$ and ${t}^{+}$ following ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , then one remains cyclic ${M}_{4}^{c}$ ; otherwise one passes to monotonic: ${M}_{\text{act}}:={M}_{\text{act}}\setminus \lbrace {M}_{4}^{c}\rbrace \cup \left\lbrace {M}_{4}^{m}\right\rbrace$ and the hardening variables of the cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ recover the virgin initial values.
  - if it is a deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ declared active at ${t}^{+}$ , and if $\left(\parallel {\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}+\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\parallel+ {r}_{k}^{c}-{r}_{k}^{m}\right)/{r}_{k}^{m}\ge 0$ (computed at ${t}^{+}$ ), then the memory variables of this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ are updated, and the hardening variables of the “parent” monotonic deviatoric mechanism are reinitialised to the virgin state. This prevents the threshold of the cyclic mechanism from crossing the threshold of the monotonic mechanism.
  - if the cyclic mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is declared created but inactive in ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ then:

    - if it is a deviatoric mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ , then:

      - if the threshold ${f}_{k}^{c}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is declared active and:

        - if ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)\text{père}}^{H}\ne 0$ (if a “parent” mechanism exists), then if at ${t}^{-}$ $\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{pèreH}}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{pèreH}}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)\text{père}}^{H}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{{p}_{k}.F({p}_{k}{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}\right)<0$ and the threshold satisfies ${\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{c}({t}^{-})\ge 0$ this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ recovers the memory variables of the “parent” mechanism — which becomes active again — as well as its value of ${r}_{k}^{c}$ , then the “parent” mechanism is reinitialised to the virgin state;
        - if no “parent” mechanism exists and if $\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{filsH}}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{filsH}}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}\cdot\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)\text{fils}}^{H}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{{p}_{k}.F({p}_{k}{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}\right)\ge 0$ at ${t}^{+}$ , then the memory variables at ${t}^{+}$ of this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ are stored in those of a “parent” mechanism whose values one must keep, and the memory variables of this “child” mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ — which becomes active — become: ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)\text{fils}}^{H}={\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)\text{père}}^{H}+{\text{2r}}_{k}^{c}\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{pèreH}}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)\text{pèreH}}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}$ and $\frac{{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}_{\mathrm{filsH}}}{{\parallel{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}_{\mathrm{filsH}}\parallel}_{\mathrm{VM}}^{\mathrm{2D}}}=\frac{-{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}_{\mathrm{pèreH}}}{{\parallel{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}_{\mathrm{pèreH}}\parallel}_{\mathrm{VM}}^{\mathrm{2D}}}$ ;
      - if the memory variables estimated before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ satisfy ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}\ne {\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}({t}^{-})$ and ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}({t}^{-})\ne 0$ , then if at ${t}^{+}$  $\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}\cdot\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{{p}_{k}.F({p}_{k},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}\right)\ge 0$ one equalises the memory variables at ${t}^{+}$ with these estimated values, and moreover if the threshold ${\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{c}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ , then ${M}_{k}^{c}$ is activated and one also equalises the memory variables at ${t}^{-}$ and at ${t}^{+}$ , likewise the values of ${r}_{k}^{c}$ , and otherwise, it is useless to restart a local non-linear solution;
      - if the memory variables estimated before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ satisfy ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}\ne {\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}({t}^{-})$ and ${\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}({t}^{-})=0$ (the “parent” is a monotonic mechanism), then if $\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}\cdot\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}-\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}{{p}_{k}.F({p}_{k},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p})}\right)\ge 0$ at ${t}^{+}$  the memory variables of this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ are reinitialised to the virgin state and ${M}_{k}^{m}$ is activated;
      - if at ${t}^{-}$ ${r}_{k}^{c}\ne {r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}$ , then one records the memory variables of this mechanism ${M}_{k}^{c}$ at the time instant ${t}^{+}$ (a new “parent” is created) and one reinitialises to the virgin state this mechanism ${r}_{k}^{\text{c+}}={r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}}$ , which is created inactive. In this case, it is useless to restart a local non-linear solution.
    - if it is an isotropic mechanism ${M}_{4}^{c}$ and:

      - if ${\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{+}\le {\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{-}$ and ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid =1$ at ${t}^{-}$ (reduction of the compression): if the threshold ${\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then ${M}_{4}^{c}$ is declared active otherwise the memory variables of this isotropic mechanism at ${t}^{+}$ recover the values before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ . Moreover, if ${p}_{H}^{c}=0$ at the step ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , then the associated mechanisms ${M}_{4}^{m}$ and ${M}_{4}^{c}$ are deactivated;
      - if ${\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{+}>{\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{-}$ and ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid =-1$ at ${t}^{-}$ (increase of the compression): if the threshold ${\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then ${M}_{4}^{c}$ is declared active otherwise the memory variables of this isotropic mechanism at ${t}^{+}$ take the values before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ ;
      - if ${\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{+}>{\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{-}$ and ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid =1$ at ${t}^{-}$ (reduction of the compression):

        - if ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid$ changes sign at this step, then the memory variables of this cyclic isotropic mechanism at ${t}^{+}$  take the values obtained before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ ; moreover if there exists no ${p}_{H}^{c}$ at ${t}^{+}$ , then the mechanisms ${M}_{4}^{m}$ and ${M}_{4}^{c}$ are declared inactive, and otherwise, if the threshold ${f}_{4}^{c}({t}^{+})\ge 0$ then ${M}_{4}^{c}$ — declared active at ${t}^{-}$ — remains created at ${t}^{+}$ and the memory variables at ${t}^{-}$ take the values obtained before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ at ${t}^{-}$ ;
        - if ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid$ does not change sign at this step, then one updates the memory variables of this isotropic mechanism (creation of a “child”) and if the threshold ${\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}$ computed with the stresses at ${t}^{+}$ exceeds the initial criterion (${r}_{\text{éla}}^{c}$ ), then ${M}_{4}^{c}$ is declared active at ${t}^{-}$ and the memory variables at ${t}^{-}$ take the values obtained at ${t}^{+}$ ;
      - if ${\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{+}\le {\left(\frac{\mid p\mid }{d\mid {p}_{c}\mid }\right)}^{-}$ and ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid =-1$ at ${t}^{-}$ (increase of the compression):

        - if ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid$ changes sign at this step, then the memory variables of this isotropic mechanism at ${t}^{+}$ of this mechanism take the values obtained during the estimation of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , and if the threshold ${f}_{c}^{4}$ computed with the stresses at ${t}^{+}$ , then the memory variables at ${t}^{-}$ take the values obtained before the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ and ${M}_{4}^{c}$ is declared active at ${t}^{-}$ and remains created at ${t}^{+}$ ;
        - if ${p}_{H}^{c}/\mid {p}_{H}^{c}\mid$ does not change sign since the step of ${M}^{\text{pot}}$ , then one updates the memory variables of this isotropic mechanism (creation of a “child”) and if the threshold ${\mathrm{f}}_{4}^{c}$ computed with the stresses at ${t}^{+}$ exceeds the initial criterion (${r}_{\text{éla}}^{c}$ ), then ${M}_{4}^{c}$ is declared active at ${t}^{-}$ and remains created at ${t}^{+}$ , and the memory variables at ${t}^{-}$ take the values obtained at ${t}^{+}$ .

**Note:**

*In the case where* ${M}^{\text{act}}\ne {M}^{\text{pot}}$ *, one restarts the local Newton algorithm by using not the elastic predictor of the stresses* ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-}+\boldsymbol{C}(\hat{{p}_{é}}).\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}$ , *cf.* [eq 2.2.1-1], *but the result obtained at the end of the previous failed step* ${\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{\text{échoué}}^{+}$ , *cf.* [eq  2.2.3-1], *associated with the values obtained for the internal variables* ${\boldsymbol{\alpha}}^{-}$ *. One thus hopes to avoid untimely looping and, by starting in a manner closer to the solution sought, to accelerate the convergence.*

#### 2.2.4 Calculation of the incremental tangent stiffness matrix

One finally establishes (on request) the local tangent matrix ${\boldsymbol{C}}^{T}$  of the incremental behaviour, at the current global iteration, which relates the variation of total stress to the variation of total strain:

$$
\Delta {\sigma}_{ij}={C}_{\text{ijrs}}^{T}.\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}={C}_{\text{ijrs}}({p}^{+}).(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}-\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}^{p})
$$

For this, one exploits the “consistency” conditions: ${\dot{\mathrm{f}}}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{+},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}},{r}_{k}^{\text{K+}})=0$ for the active mechanisms, monotonic or cyclic $\text{K=m,c}$ , the quantities intervening in these expressions being computed at the current time instant ${t}^{+}{\text{=t}}_{i}$ . By combining with [eq 1.1.1-9], one obtains:

$$
\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}=\frac{{\langle \frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\mid}_{\boldsymbol{Y}^{+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({p}^{+}).\Delta \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \rangle }_{+}}{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({p}^{+}).{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{\text{K+}}-\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}{\mid}_{\boldsymbol{{Y}}^{+}}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{\text{K+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}.{\rho}_{k}^{\text{K+}}}\ge 0
$$

cf. [eq 1.1.2-7], [eq 1.1.2-17], [eq 1.1.3-5], [eq 1.1.3-13], the various derivatives being given by [eq 7-13] to [eq  7-24]. The sign of $\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({\hat{p}}_{é}).\Delta \dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}$ is compared with a reference tolerance (R8PREM).

One deduces from this the expression of the incremental tangent matrix (option FULL_MECA), on the mechanisms of ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$ :

$$
{C}_{\text{ijrs}}^{T}={C}_{\text{ijlt}}({p}^{+})\cdot\left({I}_{\text{rslt}}-\sum_{{M}^{\text{act}}}\frac{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}\cdot{C}_{\text{pqrs}}({p}^{+}).{({\Psi}_{\text{lt}})}_{(k)}^{\text{K+}}}{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\mid}_{{Y}^{+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({p}^{+}).{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{\text{K+}}-\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}.{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{\text{K+}}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}{\mid}_{{\boldsymbol{Y}}^{+}}.{\rho}_{k}^{\text{K+}}}\right)
$$

The incremental tangent matrix is thus established analytically, cf. [§ 6], the various derivatives present in [eq  2.2.2-2] being computed in [§ 6]. The determinant of the incremental tangent matrix $\det({C}_{\text{ijrs}}^{T})$ , in option FULL_MECA, is stored among the internal variables: VARI_33, cf. [§ 3.1].

**Note:**

*The evaluation of the incremental tangent matrix with the aid of a perturbation technique (by finite differences, by way of verification) is not possible because of the multi-mechanism character of the Hujeux model: the effective activation of the mechanisms not being a differentiable process.*

### 2.3 Rate tangent operator: option RIGI_MECA_TANG

For the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, which is used during the global prediction, called at the first iteration of a new load increment $\Delta \hat{\boldsymbol{L}}({t}_{i})$ , the global tangent operator, denoted ${\boldsymbol{K}}_{i-1}$ , is computed from the results known at the time instant ${t}^{-}{\text{=t}}_{i-1}$ .

The global tangent operator is assembled from the contributions of the tangent matrix at each Gauss point, known as “in rates”:

$$
{\dot{\sigma}}_{ij}{\text{=C}}_{\text{ijrs}}^{\text{elp}}.{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{rs}}
$$

As the constitutive law is non-linear elastic [eq  1.1.1-11], one constructs a non-linear elastic tangent operator.

- If in the previous state the stress tensor is not on the boundary of any plastic threshold, the elastic prediction ${{\sigma}_{ij}^{+}}_{é}$ is written according to [eq 2.2.1-1], with the aid of the estimated confining pressure $\hat{{p}_{é}}$ [eq 2.2.1-2].

In this case, one constructs the matrix ${\boldsymbol{K}}_{i-1}$ with the aid of the elasticity tensor computed for the confining pressure $\hat{{p}_{é}}$ . This choice makes it possible to limit the risk of having a matrix producing predictions of stresses too far from the expected values, because of the exponential non-linear form of the elastic law [eq 1.1.1-11].

- If in the previous state the stress tensor is on the boundary of a plastic threshold, one exploits the “consistency” conditions: ${\dot{\mathrm{f}}}_{k}^{K}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}^{-},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p-},{r}_{k}^{K-})=0$ , for the active mechanisms, monotonic or cyclic $\text{K=m,c}$ , the quantities intervening in these expressions being computed at the previous time instant ${t}^{-}{\text{=t}}_{i-1}$ . By combining with [eq 1.1.1-9], one obtains the expression of the plastic multipliers $\dot{{\lambda}_{k}^{K}}$ :

$$
{\dot{\lambda}}_{k}^{K}=\frac{{\langle {\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }\rvert }_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}\cdot\boldsymbol{C}({\hat{p}}_{é})\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}\rangle }_{+}}{{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }\rvert }_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}.C({\hat{p}}_{é}).{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}-{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}\mid }_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}\cdot{I}+\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}.{\rho}_{k}^{K-}}\ge 0
$$

cf. [eq 1.1.2-7], [eq 1.1.2-17], [eq 1.1.3-6], [eq 1.1.3-13], the various derivatives being given in the appendix by [eq 7-13] to [eq  7-24].

One deduces from this the expression of the tangent matrix “in rates”, on the mechanisms of ${M}^{\mathrm{act}}$ :

$$
{C}_{\text{ijrs}}^{\text{elp}}{\text{=C}}_{\text{ijlt}}({\hat{p}}_{é}).({I}_{\text{rslt}}-\sum_{{M}^{\text{act}}}\frac{{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }\rvert }_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-{}_{\text{pq}}}}.{C}_{\text{pqrs}}({\hat{p}}_{é}).{({\Psi}_{\text{lt}})}_{(k)}^{K-}}{\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial \boldsymbol{\sigma} }{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}.C({\hat{p}}_{é}).{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}-\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\Psi}}_{(k)}^{K-}\cdot\boldsymbol{I}+\frac{\partial {\mathrm{f}}_{k}^{K}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}{\rvert}_{{\hat{\boldsymbol{Y}}}^{-}}.{\rho}_{k}^{K-}})
$$

**Note:**

*One must note that the non-associated character of the flow laws*  [eq  1.1.2-3] and [eq  1.1.2-14] *of the deviatoric mechanisms causes the loss of the major symmetries in the tangent matrix. Likewise, these flow laws introduce couplings between the components* ${C}_{\mathrm{iijj}}^{\mathrm{elp}}$ *and the components* ${C}_{\mathrm{klkl}}^{\mathrm{elp}}$ *for* $k\ne l$ *, which is closely linked to the dilatancy.*

**Note:**

*One must also note that, as the elasticity tensor* $\boldsymbol{C}(\hat{{p}_{é}})$ *is isotropic, the loading situations where only the diagonal directions are loaded, then the tensors* ${({\Psi}_{\text{lt}})}_{(k)}^{K}$ *contain only diagonal terms, and therefore the tangent elasticity tensor* ${\boldsymbol{C}}^{\mathrm{elp}}$ *contains no coupling between diagonal terms and off-diagonal terms.*

This makes it possible to visualise easily, with a simple scalar, the active mechanisms. For example, if VARI_34 = 10000010, then the active criteria are: monotonic deviatoric $\mathrm{XZ}$ (${10}^{1}$ ) and cyclic isotropic (${10}^{7}$).

## 6 Appendix 1: analytical calculation of the local-integration tangent matrix

One must solve by a Newton method the following system of local non-linear equations: $\boldsymbol{R}(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})=0$ , written at the end of the increment studied, where $\Delta \boldsymbol{Y}=(\Delta {\sigma}_{ij},\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},\Delta {r}_{k}^{K},\Delta (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}))$ . This is written:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&{\sigma}_{ij}^{+}-{\sigma}_{ij}^{-}-{C}_{\text{ijrs}}({\sigma}^{+})\cdot\left(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{\text{rs}}-\sum_{\text{K=m,c}}\left(\sum_{\text{k=}1}^{3}\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}{({\Psi}_{\text{rs}})}_{(k)}^{{K}_{({\sigma}^{+})}}\right)-\frac{p}{3\mid p\mid }.\left(\Delta {\lambda}_{4}^{m}+\frac{\Delta {\lambda}_{4}^{c}{p}^{c}}{\mid {p}^{c}\mid }\right){I}_{\text{rs}}\right)=0\text{ }({\text{LE}}_{ij})\\
&{\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}}-{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p-}+\sum_{\text{K=m,c}}\left(\sum_{\text{k=}1}^{3}\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}{\zeta}_{0}.\zeta \left({r}_{k}^{K}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dK}}\right).\left(\sin\psi +\frac{{q}_{k}^{\text{K+}}}{{p}_{k}^{+}}\right)\right)-\frac{p}{\mid p\mid }.(\Delta {\lambda}_{4}^{m}+\frac{\Delta {\lambda}_{4}^{c}{p}^{c}}{\mid {p}^{c}\mid })=0\text{ }(\text{LEVP})\\
&{r}_{k}^{\text{K+}}-{r}_{k}^{K-}-\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K}\frac{{(1-{r}_{k}^{\text{K+}}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dK}})}^{2}}{{a}_{c}+\zeta ({r}_{k}^{\text{K+}}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dK}}).({a}_{m}-{a}_{c})}{\gamma}_{k}^{\text{K+}}=0\text{   }K=m,c\text{   }k=1,2,3\text{ }(\text{LR}.1)\\
&{r}_{4}^{\text{K+}}-{r}_{4}^{K-}-\Delta {\lambda}_{4}^{K}\frac{{(1-{r}_{4}^{\text{K+}}-{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sK}})}^{2}}{{c}_{K}}\left(\frac{{P}_{\text{réf}}}{{P}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}})}\right)=0\text{   }K=m,c\text{ }(\text{ LR}.2)\\
&{q}_{k}^{\text{K+}}{\text{+Mp}}_{k}^{+}\left(1-{b}_{h}.\ln\left(\frac{{p}_{k}^{+}}{{P}_{\text{c0}}.{e}^{-\beta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}}}}\right)\right).({r}_{k}^{\text{K+}}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dK}})=0\text{   }K=m,c\text{ }(\text{LF}.1)\\
&\mid {p}^{\text{K+}}\mid \text{+d}.{P}_{\text{c0}}.{e}^{-\beta {\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}}}.\left({r}_{4}^{\text{K+}}{\text{+r}}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{sK}}\right)=0\text{   }K=m,c\text{ }(\text{LF}.2)
\end{aligned}
$$

with: ${\gamma}_{k}^{m}=1$ and ${\gamma}_{k}^{c}=\frac{2{q}_{k}^{c}.{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}{2{q}_{k}^{c}.{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}-{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}.{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}}$ , cf. [eq 1.1.2-5] and [eq 1.1.2-15].

One must therefore establish the calculation of the tangent matrix ${\frac{d\boldsymbol{R}}{d(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})}\mid }_{\Delta \boldsymbol{Y}}$ , which requires the analytical calculation of the following various derivatives with respect to $\boldsymbol{Y}=({\sigma}_{ij},{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p},{r}_{k}^{K},\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})$ :

$$
\frac{d\boldsymbol{R}}{d(\Delta \boldsymbol{Y})}{\mid}_{\Delta \boldsymbol{Y}}=\left[\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial {\text{LE}}_{ij}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.1}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LR}.2}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.1}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\\ \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{rs}}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial {r}_{k}^{K}}& \frac{\partial \text{LF}.2}{\partial (\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{K})}\end{array}\right]

&{\varepsilon}_{v}^{\text{p+}}-{\varepsilon}_{v}^{p-}+\sum_{\text{k=}1}^{3}\Delta {\lambda}_{k}^{m}.{\zeta}_{0}.\zeta ({r}_{k}^{m}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{d}).\left(\sin\psi +\frac{{q}_{k}^{+}}{{p}_{k}^{+}}\right)\\

\end{aligned}
$$

**Calculation of the components** $\frac{\partial \mathrm{LEVP}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}$ **(second-order tensor):**

$$
\begin{aligned}

\end{aligned}
$$

by using the results [eq  7-7], the interventions of the consolidation mechanisms being zero.

**Calculation of the component** $\frac{\partial \text{LEVP}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{v}^{p}}$ **(scalar):**

$$
\begin{aligned}

&\left[{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}.\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}+\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\right)-\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}\cdot{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}}{2{q}_{k}^{c}}{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}^{c}.\left({\boldsymbol{X}}_{(k)}^{H}+\frac{{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}}{{\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)H}^{c}\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}}.({r}_{k}^{c}+{r}_{\text{éla}}^{\text{dc}})\right)\right]

(cf. [eq 1.1.2-3] and [eq 1.1.2-14]).

## 7 Appendix 2: notation of the tensors, of their invariants and expressions of various derivatives

For greater ease one places oneself in the orthonormal basis $({\mathrm{e}}_{1,}{\mathrm{e}}_{2,}{\mathrm{e}}_{3})$ of three-dimensional space. One defines the second-order identity tensor, here using the Walpole–Cowin notation:

$$
\boldsymbol{I}={\delta}_{ij}.{\mathrm{e}}_{i}\otimes {\mathrm{e}}_{j}=\left(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 1\\ 1\\ 0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)
$$

One denotes, for each mechanism in the plane $k$ :

$$
{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )=\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}({\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)})\\
{q}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )={\parallel {\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )\parallel }_{\text{VM}}^{\text{2D}}
$$

with

$$
{\boldsymbol{S}}_{(k)}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} )={\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{(k)}-{p}_{k}(\boldsymbol{\sigma} ).{\boldsymbol{I}}_{(k)}
$$

## 8 Appendix 3: Validity of the multi-mechanism formulation of the Hujeux law

### 8.1 Representation of a stress state by Mohr's circles

The arbitrary stress state of a point M may be represented by a tensor of the type:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma} =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{xy}& {\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}& {\sigma}_{yy}& {\sigma}_{yz}\\ {\sigma}_{xz}& {\sigma}_{yz}& {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array}\right]
$$

This symmetric tensor is diagonalisable, with ${\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}\ge {\sigma}_{3}$ the eigenvalues (or principal stresses) of $\sigma$ such that:

$$
\boldsymbol{\sigma} =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\sigma}_{1}& 0& 0\\ 0& {\sigma}_{2}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\sigma}_{3}\end{array}\right]
$$

The Mohr representation makes it possible to represent the action of this stress state on any facet of normal $n$ rotating about the point M (Figure 1). Indeed, the stress vector acting on this facet is written:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n}={\sigma}_{n}.n\\
\tau ={\sigma}_{n}-\left({\sigma}_{n}.n\right)n
\end{array}\right.
$$

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/1000020100000096000000A33BB5841977FA6094.png)

*Decomposition of the stress vector acting on a facet of normal n*

The Mohr representation makes it possible to represent in one and the same diagram the components $({\sigma}_{n},\tau )$ :

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n}={\sigma}_{n}.n\\ \tau =\sqrt{\tau .\tau }\end{array}\right.
$$

This is illustrated in Figure 2. This representation leads to the very important result which one highlights by the following proposition:

**Proposition 1**

Any stress state at a point M acting on a facet of arbitrary normal rotating about M **coincides exactly**, in the Mohr representation, with the whole of the green zone comprised between the three Mohr circles.

One considers this result sufficiently well known that one need not explain it any further. It is very important because **it defines the domain of physical validity for any stress state**, and one will show that in the Hujeux law, this constraint is not respected in 3D!

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/10000201000001AF00000135821CCECB1824FE63.png)

*Domain of definition of an arbitrary stress state in the Mohr plane*

### 8.2 Plasticity criterion of Mohr–Coulomb type

Before coming to Hujeux, it is appropriate to explain the relation between the Mohr representation and a failure criterion of Mohr–Coulomb type. The expression of the Mohr–Coulomb criterion is the following:

$$
\underbrace{\frac{{\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}}{2}}_{R}-\sin\left(\mathrm{\phi }\right)\underbrace{\frac{{\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}}{2}}_{C}\le 0
$$

with the principal stresses ${\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}\ge {\sigma}_{3}$ .

Its representation in the Mohr plane is illustrated in Figure 3. The meaning of this criterion is to compare the stress state at the point M, represented by the circle of centre $C=\frac{{\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}}{2}$ and of radius $R=\frac{{\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}}{2}$ , with the failure line of angle $\mathrm{\phi }$ . It is appropriate to note two important points:

- The stress state at the point M is represented by the circle of centre $C$ and of radius $R$ in the Mohr plane, and not by a point, because this circle is the locus of the pairs $({\sigma}_{n},\tau )$ for the whole set of planes of normals $n\perp {n}_{2}$ rotating about M;
- The tangency of the circle and of the failure line is obtained thanks to the term $\sin\left(\mathrm{\phi }\right)$ , as one can deduce from a relatively simple geometric analysis.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/10000000000002BC000001A4D9AC2BBFFD7CFAED.jpg)

*Representation of the Mohr–Coulomb criterion in the Mohr plane*

One summarises what has been established by this analysis by the following proposition:

**Proposition 2**

In a criterion of Mohr–Coulomb type, one compares the stress state at the point M represented by a circle of centre $C$ and of radius $R$ in the Mohr plane with a failure line of angle $\mathrm{\phi }$ . The mathematical expression of such a criterion is of the form: $R-\sin\left(\mathrm{\phi }\right)C\le 0$.

### 8.3 Expression of the Hujeux plasticity criterion

The Hujeux plasticity criterion is a criterion of Mohr–Coulomb type. One begins by decomposing the stress tensor by projection onto the three fixed planes of the Cartesian frame, proceeding as follows:

$$
{\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)=\boldsymbol{\sigma} -\boldsymbol{\sigma} .\left(\vec{{e}_{i}}\otimes \vec{{e}_{i}}\right)-\left(\vec{{e}_{i}}\otimes \vec{{e}_{i}}\right).\boldsymbol{\sigma} +{\sigma}_{ii}\vec{{e}_{i}}\otimes \vec{{e}_{i}}
$$

where ${\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)$ is the projection of the tensor $\boldsymbol{\sigma}$ onto the plane of normal ${\vec{e}}_{i\in \lbrace x,y,z\rbrace }$ . Reciprocally, one will have the inverse of the projection operator:

$$
\sigma ={\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}^{-1}\circ {\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)={\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)+{\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} .\vec{{e}_{i}}\right)}_{T}\otimes \vec{{e}_{i}}+\vec{{e}_{i}}\otimes {\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} .\vec{{e}_{i}}\right)}_{T}+{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}_{ii}\vec{{e}_{i}}\otimes \vec{{e}_{i}}
$$

${\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)$ is called the reduced tensor and may be expressed in each plane of the Cartesian frame according to the normal considered:

|  | ${e}_{x}$ | ${e}_{y}$ | ${e}_{z}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${\boldsymbol{p}}_{\vec{{e}_{i}}}\left(\boldsymbol{\sigma} \right)$ | $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\sigma}_{yy}& {\sigma}_{yz}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{zy}}& {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array}\right]$ | $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{zx}}& {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array}\right]$ | $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{xy}\\ {\sigma}_{yx}& {\sigma}_{yy}\end{array}\right]$ |

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/10000201000001480000012BA5A2471AEE137099.png)

*Representation of the reduced stress tensors in each orthotropic plane of the Cartesian frame*

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/100002010000011C000000EF98F4D64367EAC449.png)

*Representation of the reduced stress tensors in the Mohr plane*

The Hujeux criterion is then written as follows:

$R-\sin(\mathrm{\phi })C\le 0$

with:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}R=\sqrt{{\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}-{\sigma}_{yy}}{2}\right)}^{2}+{\sigma}_{xy}^{2}}\\ C=rF\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy}}{2};{{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{p}\right)\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy}}{2}\end{array}\right.
$$
It is clear from proposition 2 that the Hujeux criterion is a criterion of Mohr–Coulomb type. Consequently, it is possible to represent the reduced stress tensor by a Mohr circle in the Mohr plane. The meaning of the criterion is then to compare the circle of centre $C$ and of radius $R$ with the failure line of angle $\mathrm{\phi }$ .

**Notes**:

How is this formulation justified? According to our understanding, it seems that:

- The Hujeux criterion is derived by analogy with the Mohr–Coulomb criterion applied to triaxial loadings;
- The decomposition of the stress tensor, being bijective and invertible, is justified mathematically, and is therefore satisfactory to the mind.

### 8.4 Validity of the Hujeux criterion

In order to show that the decomposition of the stresses used in the Hujeux law is not physically valid, it is sufficient for us to find a counter-example to proposition 1. Now, finding one in 3D is relatively trivial: it is sufficient to consider an arbitrary 3D stress state.

Let for example the following stress state:

$\sigma =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0.6& -0.1& 0.05\\ -0.1& 0.4& 0.1\\ 0.05& 0.1& 0.3\end{array}\right]$ (in MPa)

One obtains the following principal stresses:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{1}=0.6416\\ {\sigma}_{2}=0.4445\\ {\sigma}_{3}=0.2139\end{array}\right.$ (in MPa)

Whence one deduces the Mohr circles.

One then computes the Hujeux circles in the Mohr plane. To do this, one computes for the first circle:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}R=\sqrt{{\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}-{\sigma}_{yy}}{2}\right)}^{2}+{\sigma}_{xy}^{2}}\\ C=\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy}}{2}\end{array}\right.$

And so on for the other two.

Finally, it is interesting to characterise the locus of the real stresses $({\sigma}_{n},\tau )$ in the Mohr plane given by a normal ${n}_{i\in \lbrace x,y,z\rbrace }$ rotating about the point M in the orthotropic plane of normal ${\vec{e}}_{i\in \lbrace x,y,z\rbrace }$ . The expression of the normals is the following:

${n}_{x}=\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ \cos\left(\theta \right)\\ \sin\left(\theta \right)\end{array}\right);\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{n}_{y}=\left(\begin{array}{c}\cos\left(\theta \right)\\ 0\\ \sin\left(\theta \right)\end{array}\right);\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{n}_{z}=\left(\begin{array}{c}\cos\left(\theta \right)\\ \sin\left(\theta \right)\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

One then computes:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n,i}={\sigma}_{n}.{n}_{i}\\ {\tau}_{i}={\sigma}_{n,i}-\left({\sigma}_{n,i}.{n}_{i}\right){n}_{i}\end{array}\right.$

The real stress is written:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n,i}={\sigma}_{n,i}.{n}_{i}\\ {\tau}_{i}=\sqrt{{\tau}_{i}.{\tau}_{i}}\end{array}\right.$

The solutions are presented in Figure 5:

- The Mohr circles are represented by black dashed lines;
- The Hujeux circles are represented by coloured solid lines;
- The locus of the real stresses in the orthotropic planes is represented by coloured dashed lines of the same colour as the corresponding Hujeux circles.

According to proposition 1, it is clear that the Hujeux circles do not correspond to a real stress state about the point M. The corresponding real stress state should rather be the one given in dashed lines, which does indeed satisfy proposition 1. One concludes from this that the Hujeux criterion, which compares the Hujeux circle with a straight line in the Mohr plane, does not represent either the phenomenological action of a real mechanism.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.23/100002010000066A000004A4E3E632C97B7BCE94.png)

*Representation of the stress state at the point M in the Mohr plane: the Mohr circles are represented by black dashed lines; the Hujeux circles by coloured solid lines; the locus of the real stresses in the 3 Cartesian orthotropic planes by coloured dashed lines with the same colour as the corresponding Hujeux circles.*

**Note**

It is interesting to note that one recovers the Hujeux circles by proceeding as follows:

${y}_{x}=\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ -\sin\left(\theta \right)\\ \cos\left(\theta \right)\end{array}\right);\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{t}_{y}=\left(\begin{array}{c}-\sin\left(\theta \right)\\ 0\\ \cos\left(\theta \right)\end{array}\right);\phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}{t}_{z}=\left(\begin{array}{c}-\sin\left(\theta \right)\\ \cos\left(\theta \right)\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

One then computes:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{n,i}={\sigma}_{n,i}.{n}_{i}\\ {\tau}_{i}={\sigma}_{n,i}.{t}_{i}\end{array}\right.$

with ${\sigma}_{n,i}=\sigma .{n}_{i}$

### 8.5 Conclusions

- The stress state used to form the Hujeux criterion is purely theoretical, but **non-existent**. In this sense, it does not express a real mechanism for a three-dimensional stress state;
- On the other hand, for a “plane” stress state, that is to say one contained in a plane perpendicular to a principal stress, the Hujeux circle coincides perfectly with the Mohr circle. This is the case for example of plane-strain or plane-stress problems, for which the Hujeux criterion therefore remains valid;
- The formulation of a valid 3D criterion requires the use of the stress invariants (${I}_{1},{I}_{2},{I}_{3}$  or the principal stresses).

## 9 Appendix 4: Internal sub-stepping strategy of the Hujeux law

The internal sub-stepping programmed in the Hujeux law occurs at a double level:

- at the level of the generic subroutine REDECE, entry point of all the constitutive laws (LdC) in code_aster;
- at the level of the Hujeux law itself.

The routine REDECE manages in a generic manner the internal sub-stepping for all the constitutive laws. The flow chart of the routine is given on the following page. The idea is based on the interception of a return code sent by the LdC:

- IRET = 0 if the integration of the LdC has succeeded
- IRET = 1 if it has failed
- IRET = 2 if it has failed

When the integration of the LdC has failed, and if IRET= 2, one leaves the routine directly. When the integration of the LdC has failed, and if IRET= 1, one attempts an internal sub-stepping as a function of the value of ITER_INTE_PAS:

- ITER_INTE_PAS = -1, 0, 1: one leaves the routine
- ITER_INTE_PAS > 1: one sub-steps by ITER_INTE_PAS over 3 levels
- ITER_INTE_PAS < -1: one sub-steps by –ITER_INTE_PAS over 3 levels

Let us note that the maximum number of sub-steppings is thus:

$\text{NB\_MAX}=\text{ITER\_INTE\_PAS}\times ({2}^{0}+{2}^{1}+{2}^{2})=7\times \text{ITER\_INTE\_PAS}$

Moreover, as in the routine of the Hujeux law, there is also **a sub-stepping by** **ITER_INTE_MAXI** , the maximum number of sub-steppings for the Hujeux law is thus:

$\text{NB\_MAX}=7\times \text{ITER\_INTE\_PAS}\times \text{ITER\_INTE\_MAXI}$

Finally, the consistent tangent matrix recovered is **averaged** over the whole set of sub-stepping steps. This makes sense only if the latter presents sufficient continuity properties from one sub-step to the next, which is generally not the case of multi-mechanism laws.
