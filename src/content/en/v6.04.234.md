# v6.04.234 SSNV234 - Basic ENDO_FISS_EXP Law and PRED_ELAS Modelling Pilotage Validation

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the integration algorithm of the constitutive law ENDO_FISS_EXP with gradient of internal variables and the PRED_ELAS control available for this law. The problem studied is a homogeneous strained stress imposed for which an analytical solution can be obtained.

The different modellings processed are:

- **Modelling A** ($\mathrm{2D}$ ): D_PLAN_GRAD_VARI Modelling is used.
- **Model B** ($\mathrm{3D}$ ): 3D_GRAD_VARI modelling is used.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

According to $\mathrm{2D}$ or $\mathrm{3D}$ Modelling, one considers a square or a cube on the side 2 $\text{mm}$ respectively.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material obeys the constitutive law fragile elastic ENDO_FISS_EXP gradient damage (D_PLAN_GRAD_VARI and 3D_GRAD_VARI). The macroscopic data correspond to:

| $E=30000\text{MPa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\nu =0.2$ | Poisson's ratio |
| ${G}_{f}=0.1{\text{N/mm}}^{2}$ | Cracking energy |
| $p=5$ | Parameter of shape |
| ${f}_{t}=2.986\text{MPa}$ | Tensile limit |
| ${f}_{c}=29.86\text{MPa}$ | Compression limit |
|  |  |
| $D=50\text{mm}$ | Half width of the damage strip |

The choice of these values of *ft* and *fc* in relation to the François criterion in ENDO_FISS_EXP corresponds to a confined tensile limit of s *c* = 3 MPa. Furthermore, the internal parameters of the model that intervene in the analytical solution are equal to:

$k=1.5\times {10}^{-3}\text{MPa}$ ; $m=11.111$ ; $\gamma =9.534\times {10}^{3}$

In the test, they are calculated by the DEFI_MATER_GC macro-command.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The displacements are imposed in all nodes of the structure, so as to match the desired homogeneous deformation. Specifically, the displacement in one node of $X$ coordinates is valid: $u(x)=\epsilon \cdot x$ . First, a uniaxial tensile deformation is imposed and then a compression deformation is discharged until a compression deformation is imposed to verify the effect of the restoration of rigidity in compression.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

None.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

This problem admits an analytical solution. During the tensile phase, the relationship that to the imposed deformation $\varepsilon$ combines the homogeneous damage level $a$ is determined. Here we adopt a uniaxial deformation of the form:

$\epsilon =\epsilon n\otimes n\text{où}∥n∥=1\text{et}\epsilon >0$

This is therefore a problem in confined uniaxial traction. The problem being homogeneous, damage (laden) and deformation are linked by the consistency relationship:

$A'(a)\Gamma (\epsilon )+\omega '(a)=0$ with $\omega (a)=\mathit{ka},A(a)=\frac{{(1-a)}^{2}}{{(1-a)}^{2}+\mathit{ma}(1+\mathit{pa})}$

The G function is based on the form of the criterion that defines the elasticity domain, cf. [R5.03.28](doc:r5.03.28) or, in more detail, [Lorentz, 2016]. In the present case of a confined uniaxial traction / compression, it is simply written:

$\Gamma (\epsilon )=\frac{1}{2}{E}_{c}{\epsilon}^{2}$ où ${E}_{c}=\lambda +2\mu$

This is why we simply deduce the relationship between $\epsilon$ and $a$:

$\epsilon =\sqrt{\frac{-2k}{{E}_{c}A'(a)}}$

For the test case, the deformation will increase to such a level as the level of $a=0.2$ zero damage and it will be tested that the damage is well at its target value.

In a second step, the deformation is released and then compression is imposed to an ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{comp}}=-2{\sigma}^{c}/{E}_{c}$ level. Damage is no longer evolving. The stress-deformation relationship is written:

$\sigma =A(a){E}_{c}\epsilon +\frac{1}{2}(1-A(a)){E}_{c}S'(\epsilon )$ où $S(\epsilon )={\langle -\epsilon \rangle }^{2}\exp(\frac{1}{\gamma \epsilon })$

The discharge is therefore performed linearly with a $A(a){E}_{c}$ splintering rigidity module. Then, in the compression phase, the model gradually restores the rigidity according to the above relationship. The test checks that the stress level is the one corresponding to the analytical relationship. In practice, rather than projecting the stress tensor to the direction of the stress, we ensure that the strain energy obtained via the POST_ELEM command (factor keyword TRAV_EXT component TRAV_ELAS) is well equal to the expected value, i.e.:

$\frac{\mathit{vol}(\Omega )}{2}\sigma :ϵ=\frac{\mathit{vol}(\Omega )}{2}\sigma ϵ$ where $\mathit{vol}(\Omega )$ is the volume of the element (4 mm2 or 8 mm3)

### 2.2 Baseline

In flat deformations, one adopts a direction of $n=(1/\sqrt{5,}2/\sqrt{5})$ solicitation. In 3D, it is equal to $n=(1/\sqrt{14,}2/\sqrt{14,}3/\sqrt{14})$. One attaches as target a damage $a=0.2$; this corresponds to a intensity of $\epsilon =2.86\times {10}^{-4}$ solicitation after the reference solution above.

The load is applied by PRED_ELAS control technique, in which the maximum terminal is fixed to achieve the above $\varepsilon$ deformation level. It will be checked that the corresponding damage reaches 0.2.

In compression, the required deformation level is ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{comp}}=-1.8\times {10}^{-4}$ for a stress of ${\sigma}_{\mathit{comp}}=-4.537\text{MPa}$.

None.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case is made on a single mesh cell, therefore it is the homogeneous damage response that is recovered numerically. The reference solution is obtained by placing itself on the damage threshold. There is a very good agreement between the Modelling and the reference solution. In compression, the rigidity restoration is tested, also satisfactorily. However, the non-local part of the law is not tested here.
