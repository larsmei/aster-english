# u2.03.10 User's guide to the modelling of junctions between reinforced-concrete civil-engineering structural elements

> **Summary.**

The purpose of this document is to give advice for carrying out studies of reinforced-concrete structures comprising walls, slabs and rafts, using the dedicated features (in particular the kinematic relations, a non-linear constitutive model of a junction). It gives information on the meshing methodology, on the implementation of the kinematic relations, and on the parameterisation of the constitutive models.

The non-linear constitutive behaviour, which makes it possible to model a bending junction between a wall and a floor or a raft and is assigned on a discrete element (hinges), of type DIS_TR on SEG2 mesh cells, with two nodes, accessible via the keyword JONC_ENDO_PLAS, see §13 [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17).

## 1 Introduction

To carry out an analysis on a reinforced-concrete structure made up of walls, slabs and rafts, one may adopt, according to the stakes and the computational performance aimed at, a:

- modelling with solid finite elements in the domain occupied by the concrete (formwork geometry), with the reinforcing steels also modelled as solid finite elements: this choice must be reserved for local analyses;
- modelling with solid finite elements in the domain occupied by the concrete (formwork geometry), with the steels of the reinforcement layers modelled by grid membranes, placed on the mid-plane of the reinforcement layers. This situation is that, for example, of a raft or of thick floors;
- modelling with plate finite elements known as “generalised”, which will carry a constitutive law expressed directly in generalised forces (bending and membrane), representing the constitutive behaviour of the reinforced concrete homogenised over the section. This situation is that, for example, of structural assemblies based on walls and floors. This modelling uses the geometry of the mid-planes of the walls, the slabs, the rafts...

It is also necessary to model the reinforced-concrete junctions connecting a wall and a floor or a raft. These junctions have dimensions identical or close to that of the thickness of the structural elements connected: they are therefore essentially three-dimensional in the geometric part of the connection (the “core”). One can distinguish junctions between 2, 3 or 4 structural elements, of cross, T or L shape.

Various codified construction provisions govern the reinforcement of these junctions. According to the design choices, one may consider a good continuity of transmission of the bending moments between the connected structural elements when the reinforcement is continuous through the junction. On the contrary, if the reinforcement is discontinuous, the possible cracking of the concrete modifies the constitutive behaviour of the connection: a perfect built-in fixity is not always representative.

The choices made for the concreting also have an influence: one may have the case of a floor concreted at the same time as the wall, or on the contrary the case of a floor with a precast slab receiving, by concreting, a complementary compression slab. The construction sequence, with shoring present under the precast slabs, also contributes to the loading of the junction (dead weight of the concrete and of the additional loads during the pouring of the floor).

Often the wall extending above the floor, continuing the wall of the lower lift, is not cast and the reinforcement is left waiting.

The kinematics imposed on the boundaries of the junction core by the connected structural elements is nevertheless (to first order) controlled by that suited to the current zones of these structural elements of walls, slabs, rafts… The kinematics of the junction core itself can be represented with sufficient accuracy by simple strains in the transverse plane.

In this respect, wall–floor junctions behave differently from beam–column joints, which often exhibit cross-shaped cracking within the core.

### 2.1 Mesh for a model with plate finite elements

This choice of mesh offers the advantage of simplicity; however it contains an arbitrary element, because one often uses the coordinates of the formwork planes and not the exact position of the mid-surfaces of the walls and of the floors. This has in addition the drawback of counting twice the volume which lies at the intersection of the two planes (thus in the modelling the mass will be counted twice and the spans will be exaggerated).

It is accepted that two planes meshed with surface mesh cells (floor and wall) intersect along a straight line which belongs to the mesh of the structure.

In order to model more precisely the junction between plane structural elements, it is therefore proposed to provide for the mesh of the “core” volume of the junction, in the form of a parallelepiped with transverse dimensions identical to the true thicknesses of the connected plane structural elements.

On this volume a mesh will be produced as a single band of 8-node hexahedral mesh cells HEXA8. The transverse boundary planes between these mesh cells will contain the end nodes of the plane mesh cells of the geometry of the plane structural elements.

### 3.4 Choice of the constitutive law of the materials

The choice of the constitutive law of the materials, either taken individually (concrete and steel), or after homogenisation for the reinforced concrete, determines the keywords to be filled in under DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01). The general survey [U2.03.07](doc:u2.03.07) may be consulted.

#### 3.4.1 Definition of the non-linear constitutive behaviour of a junction

The constitutive behaviour JONC_ENDO_PLAS [R5.03.17] is used to model, on discrete elements (hinges), of type DIS_TR on SEG2 mesh cells, with two nodes, the non-linear constitutive behaviour of an out-of-plane bending connection. The non-linear relation is expressed in the plane of moment $M$ – relative rotation $\theta$ along the local axis $\mathrm{Oz}$. The phenomenology represented comprises: elasticity, then damage weakening the elastic stiffness, and kinematic hardening (irreversible strains). The paths in “positive” rotation and in “negative” rotation are distinguished, for which the degradation can occur in a non-symmetric manner according to the characteristics of the sections.

### 3.5 Definition of the characteristics of the materials

#### 3.5.1 Definition of the parameters of the non-linear constitutive law of a junction

There are eight of them (positive real numbers, all compulsory, cf. [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)):

- KE: elastic rotational stiffness ${K}_{e}>0$;
- KP: plastic hardening slope ${K}_{p}\le {K}_{e}$;
- KDP: tangent stiffness ${K}_{d}^{+}\in \left[{K}_{e},{K}_{p}\right]$ in the damaging phase for positive bending,
- KDM: tangent stiffness ${K}_{d}^{-}\in \left[{K}_{e},{K}_{p}\right]$ in the damaging phase for negative bending,
- RDP: damage-threshold rotation ${\theta}_{d}^{+}>0$ in positive bending,
- RDM: damage-threshold rotation ${\theta}_{d}^{-}<0$ in negative bending,
- MYP: plasticity threshold ${M}_{y}^{+}$ in positive bending,
- MYM: plasticity threshold ${M}_{y}^{-}\le {K}_{e}{\theta}_{d}^{-}$ in negative bending.

### 3.6 Definition of the kinematic relations

In many situations, the design of the mesh of the structure and the assignment of the finite elements mean that kinematic continuity over all the degrees of freedom on the nodes common to the interfaces between structural elements is ensured automatically.

The kinematic constraint equations are written in the local coordinate system of the discrete element: translation and rotation.

#### 3.6.1 Definition of the local coordinate system

It is recalled that Code_Aster uses a global coordinate system: that of the mesh of the whole structure. And every structural element has a local coordinate system, assigned automatically by Code_Aster by default, or else chosen by the user by means of the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM / keyword ORIENTATION, cf. [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01).

#### 3.6.2 Simple perfect connection by relations on the degrees of freedom

The straight line which belongs to the mesh of the structure and which supports the boundary between the plane of the floor and that of the wall, or between those of two walls, is duplicated: it is the support of pairs of nodes facing each other. The mesh must therefore have been built with nodes facing each other.

The connection between the two plates is made by rigid-body relations: translation and rotations.

The keyword LIAISON_COQUE of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) makes it possible to represent the connection between the two plates, by specifying the mesh-cell group or node group of the edge of the first plate, then the mesh-cell group or node group of the edge of the second plate.

#### 3.6.3 Connection with releases of relations on the degrees of freedom

The *Release Tool* of *Salomé_Méca* [SV1.05.01] makes it possible to build easily conditions of release of the connection between the degrees of freedom at the boundary between the plane of the floor and that of the wall, or between those of two walls. It uses a mesh and a *Code_Aster* model. One chooses the mesh-cell group or node group supporting the connection to be released. There are three possible options:

- node–node release;
- node–plane release;
- plane–plane release;

The degrees of freedom are in the global coordinate system of the mesh: DX, DY, DZ, DRX, DRY, DRZ.
