# v6.03.159 SSNP159 – Elastic energy in large plastic deformations of a tensile bar

**Summary:**

This quasi-static mechanical test consists of a simple pull-out of a rectangular ($\mathrm{3D}$) or cylindrical ($\mathrm{2D}$ axisymmetric) cross-section bar. The object is to validate the calculation of elastic energies in three deformation formalisms: 'PETIT', 'GDEF_LOG' and 'SIMO_MIEHE', with the POST_ELEM command.

In addition, the Modelling of this test case B, proposes to test the ETAT_INIT factor keyword proposed by the STAT_NON_LINE command, with GDEF_LOG. The user wishing to impose an initial stress state with the formalismGDEF_LOG will find the necessary information.

The bar is modelled by a volume element (HEXA20, modelingA) or quadrangular QUAD4 (axisymmetric, modelingB).

The solution is analytical.

## 2 reference solution

SIMO_MIEHE analytical solution allows you to define the load to be applied to obtain a Kirchhoff constraint from $\mathrm{1500MPa}$ . This load is then applied to other models.

### 2.1 Generic result to formalisms

For a single-axial traction test following the $x$ direction, the stress tensors of Kirchhoff $\tau$ and Cauchy $\sigma$ are of the following shape:

$\sigma =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\sigma & 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right],\tau =J\sigma =\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\tau & 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$ ,

The gradient tensors of $F$ and $\stackrel{ˉ}{F}$ transformation are expressed:

$F=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}F& 0& 0\\ 0& {F}_{y}& 0\\ 0& 0& {F}_{y}\end{array}\right],\stackrel{ˉ}{F}={J}^{\frac{-1}{3}}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\stackrel{ˉ}{F}& 0& 0\\ 0& \stackrel{ˉ}{{F}_{y}}& 0\\ 0& 0& \stackrel{ˉ}{{F}_{y}}\end{array}\right]$

The movement shall verify:

$F=1+\frac{{u}^{\mathit{meca}}}{{l}_{0}}$

The linear isotropic hardening function is written:

$R(p)=\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}$

### 2.2 Results for Simo_Miehe

The ${G}^{P}$ plastic deformation isochore tensor is of the form:

${G}^{P}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{G}^{P}& 0& 0\\ 0& {G}_{y}^{P}& 0\\ 0& 0& {G}_{y}^{P}\end{array}\right]$ , with ${G}_{y}^{P}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{{G}^{P}}}$ car $det({G}^{P})=1$

The constitutive law (hydrostatic part) is written:

$\mathit{tr}(\tau )=\frac{\mathrm{3K}}{2}({J}^{2}-1)\mathrm{\Rightarrow }{J}^{2}=\frac{2\tau }{\mathrm{3K}}+1$

The plasticity threshold function is written:

$f=0=\tau -R(p)-{\sigma}_{y}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }p=\frac{E-{E}_{T}}{E{E}_{T}}(\tau -{\sigma}_{y})$

The plastic flow law reads:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{F}.\dot{{G}^{P}}.\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{T}}=-3\dot{p}\frac{1}{{\tau}_{\mathit{eq}}}\mathit{dev}(\tau ).(\stackrel{ˉ}{F}.{G}^{P}.\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{T}})$

By taking the first component, we obtain:

$\frac{\dot{{G}^{P}}}{{G}^{P}}=-2\dot{p}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }{G}^{p}={e}^{-\mathrm{2p}}$ car ${G}^{P}(0)=1$

To conclude the problem, the first component of the deviatoric part of the constraint is used:

$\mathit{dev}(\tau )=\mu \mathit{dev}(\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}})\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\tau =\mu (\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{2}}{G}^{P}-\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}_{y}^{2}}{G}_{y}^{P})\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{3}}-\stackrel{ˉ}{F}\frac{\tau}{\mu {G}^{P}}-\frac{1}{{({G}^{P})}^{(3/2)}}=0$ ,

because $\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}=\stackrel{ˉ}{F}.{G}^{P}.\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{T}}$

The elastic energy is then written:

${\psi}_{\mathrm{SM}}^{\mathrm{elas}}=\frac{K}{2}\left[\frac{{J}^{2}-1}{2}-lnJ\right]+\frac{\mu}{2}\left[\mathrm{tr}\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}}-3\right]=\frac{K}{2}\left[\frac{{J}^{2}-1}{2}-lnJ\right]+\frac{\mu}{2}\left[\stackrel{ˉ}{{F}^{2}}{G}^{P}+\frac{2}{\stackrel{ˉ}{F}\sqrt{{G}^{P}}}-3\right]$

For a stress of Kirchhoff of 1500MPa, can then successively determine:

- $J=1,003$
- $\sigma =1495\mathit{MPa}$
- $p=0,2475$
- ${G}^{p}=0,6096$
- $\stackrel{ˉ}{F}=1,289$
- $F=1,290$
- ${u}^{\mathit{meca}}=290\mathit{mm}$
- ${\psi}_{\mathrm{SM}}^{\mathrm{elas}}=5,63\mathrm{MPa}$ to point hardware.

The displacement applied for both modellings and the 3 formalisms will therefore be ${u}^{\mathit{meca}}=290\mathit{mm}$ .

### 2.3 Results for GDEF_LOG

The logarithmic deformation is written:

${E}_{\log}=\frac{1}{2}\ln[C]=\frac{1}{2}\ln[{F}^{T}.F]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}lnF& 0& 0\\ 0& ln{F}_{y}& 0\\ 0& 0& ln{F}_{y}\end{array}\right]$

The upper left quarter of the Lagragian headlamp is deduced, in Voigt's notation:

$P=2\frac{\partial {E}_{\log}}{\partial C}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{2}{{F}^{2}}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{2}{{F}_{y}^{2}}& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{2}{{F}_{y}^{2}}\end{array}\right]$

And because of the expression of the second constraint of Piola-Kirchhoff:

$S=T:P={F}^{-1}.\tau .{F}^{-T}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }T=\tau$

The constitutive law is written:

$T=E(lnF-p)$

Due to the plasticity threshold:

$f=0=T-R(p)-{\sigma}_{y}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }p=\frac{E-{E}_{T}}{E{E}_{T}}(T-{\sigma}_{y})=\frac{ElnF-{\sigma}_{y}}{E+\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}}$ ,

The elastic energy of this formalism is thus written:

${\psi}_{\log}^{\mathit{elas}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{{T}^{2}}{E}$

From the ${u}^{\mathit{meca}}=290\mathit{mm}$ forced displacement, we deduce:

- $F=1,290$
- $lnF=0,255$
- $p=0,2475$
- $T=\mathrm{1500MPa}$
- $\sigma =\mathrm{1495MPa}$
- ${\psi}_{\log}^{\mathit{elas}}=5,625\mathit{MPa}$ to point hardware.

### 2.4 Results in small deformations

In small deformations, the result is classic.

Axial deformation:

${\varepsilon}_{x}=\frac{{u}^{\mathit{meca}}}{{l}_{0}}$

Behaviour:

$\sigma =E({\varepsilon}_{x}-p)$

Threshold function:

$\sigma -R(p)-{\sigma}_{y}=0$

D'où:

$\begin{array}{c}p=\frac{E{\varepsilon}_{x}-{\sigma}_{y}}{E+\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}}\\ \sigma =E({\varepsilon}_{x}-p)\end{array}$

Elastic energy:

${\psi}_{\mathit{HPP}}^{\mathit{elas}}=\frac{{\sigma}^{2}}{2E}$

From the ${u}^{\mathit{meca}}=290\mathit{mm}$ forced displacement, we deduce:

- ${\varepsilon}_{x}=0,029$
- $p=0,281$
- $\sigma =\mathrm{1570MPa}$
- ${\psi}_{\log}^{\mathit{elas}}=6,16\mathit{MPa}$ to point hardware.

### 2.5 Tests performed

For each formalism and each modellings, the values of the imposed displacement, Cauchy $\sigma$ stress, $p$ cumulative plastic deformation and elastic energy are tested.

Attention, the elastic energy tested is the value for the bar (not the material point). For axisymmetric Modelling, therefore, the elastic energy value of the material point must be multiplied by $\frac{\pi {R}^{2}}{2\pi }$.

### 4.4 GDEF_LOG with ETAT_INIT

To impose an initial stress field in large deformations with the formalism GDEF_LOG, the user must give in input the stress tensor defined in the $T$ logarithmic space (not Cauchy $\sigma$). The components of the $\sigma$ logarithmic space, being stored as internal variables, the VARI and DEPL operands of the factor keyword ETAT_INIT command are to be used as shown below (for example, these fields can be obtained by the CREA_CHAMP command).

The field of internal variables (to get $T$) and also the corresponding field of displacement are recovered:

```text
VAR_LOG1=CREA_CHAMP(INFO=2,

TYPE_CHAM='ELGA_VARI_R',

OPERATION='EXTR',

RESULTAT=LOG1,

NOM_CHAM='VARI_ELGA',

INST=1.0,);
```

```text
DEP_LOG1=CREA_CHAMP(INFO=2,

TYPE_CHAM='NOEU_DEPL_R',

OPERATION='EXTR',

RESULTAT=LOG1,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

INST=1.0,);
```

then the initial stress state is given in STAT_NON_LINE:

```text
ETAT_INIT=_F(VARI=VAR_LOG1,

DEPL=DEP_LOG1).
```

## 5 Summary of the results

The results found in terms of elastic energy with *code_aster* are very satisfactory in small deformations and logarithmic deformations, with differences at analytic less than 0.1%, and correct in deformation of SIMO_MIEHE, with an difference at an analytical of about 3%, due to the term $\mathit{tr}(\stackrel{ˉ}{{b}^{e}})$ , on which the fifth decimal plays a significant role.
