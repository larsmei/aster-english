# v1.01.268 ZZZZ268 - Validation of POST_BORDET in 2D and 3D

**Summary:**

This test validates Bordet's two-dimensional and three-dimensional stress and probability calculation, with thermal evolution, on cases in which the mechanical fields are uniform (which allows an analytical calculation of these quantities); this test case is intended to test numerical developments and not to conduct a realistic case study.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of modelling A

A axisymmetric $\mathrm{1mm}$ radius and $\mathrm{10mm}$ height bar is considered to be subject to a single (imposed displacement) traction test. This test is chosen as it allows uniform mechanical fields to be obtained.

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.268/100002000000013500000338742F0B1CCB770930.png)

**Figure 1.1: axisymmetric Bar.**

### 1.2 Modelling Geometry B

Here we consider a $\mathrm{1mm}$ side cube subject to traction.

The front face is blocked in the direction $\text{X}$ , the left face in the direction $\text{Y}$ , the bottom face in the direction $\text{Z}$ and an incremental traction of $0,001\text{mm}$ is applied on the upper face.

### 1.3 Material properties

The material is perfect for elastoplastics with Young's modulus from $300000\text{MPa}$, Poisson's ratio from 0 and $300\text{MPa}$ elastic limit.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

For Modelling A, 5 $0,01\text{mm}$ traction increments are performed, so that the total deformation increment is $1.0\text{E-3}$ and the elastic limit is reached at the first step.

For Modelling B, the front face is blocked according to the $\text{X}$ direction, the left face according to the $\text{Y}$ direction, the bottom face according to the $\text{Z}$ axis and an incremental $0,001\text{mm}$ traction is applied on the upper face.

Temperature varies linearly in the bar from $\text{O°C}$ to $\text{5O°C}$ on the 5 time step.

In order to test the various possibilities offered by the CALC_BORDET macro-command, the parameters of the temperature and the plastic deformation speed are used, where possible. The list of parameters used is presented in the table below.

| **Parameter** | **Type** | **Value** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| M | Scalar | 22 |
| SIG_CRIT | Scalar | 250 |

| DEF_PLAS_REFE | Scalar | 0 |

**Table** 1.1 **: Bordet parameters used**

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

In both cases, due to the uniform nature of the mechanical fields, the analytical calculation can be performed both of Bordet's constraints and of probability. This is done by using the equations of the Bordet model presented in the [R7.02.06](doc:r7.02.06) documentation: Models of Beremin, Bordet and Rice and Tracey.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Both Bordet stress and associated probability are tested for 0-5 times.

For Modelling A, the following reference results are obtained:

| **Instant** | **Bordet's Continuity** | **Probability of Bordet** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | 0.0000 |
| 1 | 0 | 0.0000 |
| 2 | 247.67 | 0.9999 |
| 3 | 252.23 | 0.9995 |
| 4 | 254.1 | 0.9701 |
| 5 | 254.7 | 0.7780 |

Table 2.1: Résultats analytiques pour la modélisation A

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Because of the analytical character, the reference solution is accurate. So we only admit an error less than $0,5\text{\%}$.

## 5 Summary of the results

In both modellings and for all moments, errors are almost zero.

So it can be said that the command correctly calculates the constraints and probabilities of Bordet in $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$: the development is validated.
