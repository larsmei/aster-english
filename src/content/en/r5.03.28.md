# r5.03.28 Gradient damage law ENDO_FISS_EXP

**Summary:**

This document describes the elastic brittle constitutive model ENDO_FISS_EXP, available in non-local modelling with a damage gradient GRAD_VARI. The damage is modelled there in an isotropic manner, which preferentially implies modelling the cracks individually and not by homogenisation: the scale of discretisation must be suited to this choice. From the phenomenological point of view, the damage criterion is suited to the modelling of concretes, distinguishing in particular the states of tension and of compression. In addition, a restoration of stiffness in compression is introduced in order to reflect crack closure. On the other hand, the model is a priori not suited to modelling the damage of concrete in compression. Finally, it should be noted that the response of the model tends towards that of a cohesive law when the characteristic length becomes small compared with the size of the structure. It follows that the question of identifying this characteristic length does not arise provided that it is sufficiently small. And the model can give access to the quantities of interest of cohesive laws, such as, for example, the crack opening.

## 1 Field of application

### 1.1 Purposes

The constitutive law ENDO_FISS_EXP , available in the command STAT_NON_LINE , has the objective of describing the quasi-brittle damage of concrete under mechanical tensile loadings. The damage is described there in an isotropic manner, which positions the model to describe the cracks individually and not in a homogenised manner. From the phenomenological point of view, the model aims more particularly to reflect:

- the contrast between the elastic limits in tension and in compression, thus avoiding premature damage in compression under prestress, for example;
- the specific shape of the damage surface in multiaxial tension, close to a Rankine criterion;
- the impact on the stiffness of crack closure in the compression directions, which also reinforces the robustness of the simulations in the presence of surface-breaking cracks.

Furthermore, the law ENDO_FISS_EXP falls within the framework of non-local models with an internal-variable gradient (modelling *_GRAD_VARI*). The particular form of the law makes it possible to ensure a fundamental property: when the characteristic length of the model tends towards zero (in practice, when it becomes small compared with the characteristic size of the structure studied), the results tend towards those of a cohesive model, well suited to predicting the propagation kinematics and the crack opening. Consequently, except when seeking information at a sub-centimetric scale (which is not advised), the characteristic length does not appear as a parameter to be calibrated but may be fixed directly from the dimensions of the structure studied.

The interested reader will find detailed elements on the formulation of the law and its physical validation in the reference [Lorentz, 2016].

### 1.2 Material parameters

The constitutive law relies on eight internal parameters (including the elastic constants), as will be seen in the presentation of the equations in the following chapter. However, thanks to the consistency with a cohesive law, these internal parameters may be reformulated in terms of quantities more accessible to the engineer. Besides Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio, one will enter the tensile and compressive strengths (the latter being less significant for the problems targeted), the fracture energy and two parameters (one of which is optional) in order to describe the shape of the softening response. Finally, the characteristic length will be fixed on the basis of the dimensions of the structure or of the component studied. The command DEFI_MATER_GC takes charge of the transformation of these engineering parameters into internal characteristics of the law and replaces the command DEFI_MATERIAU.

### 1.3 Internal variables

The degree of damage a, lying between $0$ for a sound material and $1$ for a fully damaged material, constitutes a priori the only internal variable of interest to the user. It is stored in first position (V1) in the table of internal variables stored by the code. By way of post-processing, component V3 corresponds to the impact of this level of damage on the stiffness in tension, namely $A(a)$ . Finally, for information, questions of performance and of numerical robustness lead one also to archive, in the field VARI_ELGA, the state of the current time step in component V2 (0=elastic, 1=damaging, 2=saturated) as well as the level of strain reached at the end of the time step in components V4 to V9 (where the shear components are assigned a factor $\sqrt{(2)}$ ).

### 2.1 Stress–strain relation

The function $A(a)$ which enters into the stress–strain relation measures the impact of the level of damage on the residual stiffness (in tension). Its particular form is guided by consistency with a cohesive law. It plays the same role as the more classical form (1- *a* ) used in a number of models in the literature.

Furthermore, in order to take account of the restoration of stiffness in compression, the elasticity law is no longer linear, even though it remains hyperelastic ( *i.e.*  it derives from an energy) so as to avoid any spurious production or consumption of energy. It is similar to the formulation adopted for the law ENDO_ISOT_BETON [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04); it takes account not only of the sign of the principal strains but also of that of the trace of the strains. From the practical point of view, it is therefore necessary to determine the eigenvalues of the strain tensor.

Finally, in order to improve the robustness of the model and the convergence of the analysis, the jump in stiffness at the tension–compression transition has been regularised, by means of the infinitely differentiable function $S(x)$ . It ensures a progressive transition between a zero value and a quadratic function; the rapidity (or abruptness) of the transition is controlled there by the parameter $\gamma$ . A default value is proposed in the command DEFI_MATER_GC, which stipulates that 90% of the stiffness without regularisation is recovered for a strain (in compression) of the order of $\mathit{fc}/E$ .

### 2.2 Evolution of the damage

The presence of a term in the Laplacian of the damage in the threshold function, as well as a boundary condition on the damage field of zero-normal-gradient type on the boundary, reflects the non-local character of the model. More precisely, this corresponds to the non-local formulation with an internal-variable gradient as introduced in the booklet [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01). From the numerical point of view, it allows an (almost) classical treatment of the constitutive law. However, the choice of a phenomenologically acceptable damage surface leads one to give up the existence of a single energy functional to describe the whole set of equations of the problem. Here, the evolution of the damage and the equilibrium of the structure result from the minimisation of two distinct energy functionals. From the practical point of view, the tangent operator is then no longer symmetric.

## 3 Numerical integration

### 3.1 Discretised equations

The discretisation in time of the constitutive equations relies on an implicit Euler scheme, that is to say that the various variables of the problem are expressed at the final time instant of the time step. The expression of the stress at the end of the time step thus obeys the same relation as that in the table, the strain and the damage likewise being expressed there at the end of the time step. There remains the delicate part, which consists in computing the evolution of the damage.

Integrating the damage-evolution equation is a problem which is admittedly scalar but non-linear. First of all, following the solution procedure proposed in [Lorentz and Godard, 2011] and taken up in the booklet [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01), a term affine in the damage comes to replace the Laplacian term of the damage field in the threshold function $g$ :

$$
\tilde{g}(a)=-A'(a)\Gamma (\epsilon )+\lambda +r(\alpha -a)-k
$$

At the stage of integration of the constitutive law, the quantities $k$ and $\alpha$ are considered known (as is the positive penalty coefficient $r$ ), in the same way as the strains at the end of the time step, because they are expressed as a function of the nodal unknowns of the problem. Likewise, the value of the function $\Gamma (\epsilon )$ is also known, even though its computation also requires the solution of a non-linear equation, to which one will return further on. This is why the algorithmic threshold function above depends only on $a$ . In the end, the problem to be solved consists in finding $a\le 1$ such that:

$$
\Delta a\ge 0\phantom{\rule{3em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{3em}{0ex}}\tilde{g}(a)\le 0\phantom{\rule{3em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{3em}{0ex}}\Delta a\tilde{g}(a)=0
$$

where $\Delta a=a–{a}_{n}$ denotes the damage increment during the time step. And if there exists no solution (less than $1$ ), then $a=1$ (saturated damage).

### 3.2 Solution of the algorithmic evolution equation

Since $A$ is convex, $\Gamma (\epsilon )$ positive and $r$ positive, the algorithmic threshold function is decreasing. There is therefore existence and uniqueness of the solution (important for the robustness of the numerical integration). In practice, one solves the problem in three stages:

- If $\tilde{g}({a}_{n})\le 0$ then $a={a}_{n}$
- If $\tilde{g}(1)\ge 0$ then $a=1$
- Otherwise solve $\tilde{g}(a)=0$ with ${a}_{n}\le a\le 1$ by a Newton method with controlled bounds

### 3.3 Effective computation of the gamma function

According to the table , the computation of $\Gamma (\epsilon )$ refers to that of $\chi (\epsilon )$ , which is defined only implicitly: one must therefore solve a non-linear equation which is known to admit a unique solution, cf. [Lorentz, 2016]. For this purpose, one begins by introducing a few notations and a change of variable in which $x$ is the new unknown:

$$
{\sigma}^{e}=\lambda \mathit{tr}(\epsilon )\mathit{Id}+2\mu \epsilon
$$

The space of the tensor $s$ is generated by:

$$
\mathit{span}(s)=\lbrace {s}_{1},{s}_{2},{s}_{3}\rbrace
$$

With:

$$
{s}_{\beta}=\parallel s+({\beta}_{0}-\frac{1}{3})\mathit{tr}(s)\mathit{Id}\parallel
$$

The equation to be solved in order to determine $x$ and therefore ${\rm X}$ is thus written:

$$
{s}_{\beta}x+\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\exp(2{s}_{i}x)}-{\gamma}_{0}=0
$$

The left-hand side is convex. One estimates an upper bound ${x}_{0}$ of the solution, then one solves the equation by a Newton method starting from ${x}_{0}$ , which leads to a sequence of decreasing values which converge towards the solution of the equation by virtue of the convexity announced. It should be noted that the upper bound is obtained by one or the other of the two following relations, where ${s}_{M}$ denotes the largest of the three eigenvalues of $s$ :

$$
{s}_{\beta}x+\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\exp(2{s}_{i}x)}\ge {s}_{\beta}x\Rightarrow x\le \frac{{\gamma}_{0}}{{s}_{\beta}}
$$

Or:

$$
{s}_{\beta}x+\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{3}\exp(2{s}_{i}x)}\ge \exp({s}_{M}x)\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\Rightarrow \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}x\le \frac{\ln{\gamma}_{0}}{{s}_{M}}
$$

One will take the smaller of the two upper bounds for the sake of performance (or the only one if ${s}_{M}\le 0$ ).

### 3.4 Tangent matrix

Determining the tangent matrix consistent with the chosen integration scheme raises no particular difficulty (differentiation of composite functions and differentiation of implicit functions) except that the eigenvalues of the strain enter into the computation of $\Gamma (\epsilon )$ and into that of the elastic law at the moment of distinguishing tension from compression. We examine here each of these points more precisely, with a view to dispensing with the derivative of the associated eigenvectors which, it is recalled, are not defined when the eigenvalues are not distinct.

For the derivative of $\Gamma (\epsilon )$ , only the term which associates with $x$ the norm of $\exp(xs)$ requires a particular treatment. Now:

$$
\delta \parallel \exp(xs)\parallel =\frac{\delta \exp(xs):\exp(xs)}{\parallel \exp(xs)\parallel }=\parallel \exp(xs)\parallel \delta x+\frac{\exp(xs)}{\parallel \exp(xs)\parallel }:\exp'(xs):(x\delta s)
$$

At this stage, even though the derivative of the exponential (a fourth-order tensor) appears formally in the expression, it is not useful actually to carry out the differentiation. Indeed, the derivative of the exponential is symmetric because it is the derivative of the following isotropic scalar function (cf. [Silhavy, 2000] for the differentiability properties):

$$
f(S)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{e}^{{A}_{i}}
$$

And therefore:

$$
\exp(A):\exp'(A)=\exp'(A):\exp(A)=\exp(2A)
$$

This last result is easily shown by noting that $A$ and $\exp(A)$ commute. In the end, the computation of the tangent matrix will pass through that of the tensor exponential, according to the relation above, without any need to differentiate the eigenvectors. And for the computation of the tensor exponential, one will rely on the method known as "scaling and squaring", see for example [Higham, 2005], which dispenses with the computation of the eigenvectors.

As regards the stress–strain relation, it exhibits a trace term which poses no particular differentiation problem and an infinitely differentiable functional relation between the principal stress ${\sigma}_{i}$  and the principal strain ${\epsilon}_{i}$ . For the latter, one enters the framework of the derivative of tensor functions based on a scalar function of the eigenvalues: the article [Carlson and Hoger, 1986] then supplies the expressions of the (tensor) derivative whether the eigenvalues are distinct or not.
