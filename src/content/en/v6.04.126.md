# v6.04.126 SSNV126 - Anisothermal traction-relaxation test with VENDOCHAB

**Summary:**

The VENDOCHAB model, is based on a formulation proposed by Chaboche. It is a coupled formulation that covers an elasto-viscoplastic law with multiplicative isotropic hardening and isotropic damage kinetics. This law was originally developed to predict the life and cracking of turboreactors' blades and more generally to predict the ruin time of the structures being pressed at high temperatures.

This non-linear quasi-static mechanical test validates the VENDOCHAB $\mathrm{3D}$ model in the case of a test piece subjected to an anisothermal uniaxial tensile test. The stress and deformation states are homogeneous in the test piece. This test also validates the explicit and implicit integration of this model. The equations of this coupled formulation are described in the [R5.03.15](doc:r5.03.15) reference booklet.

Two modellings of the test piece are tested with one single element $\mathrm{3D}$ at 8 nodes (HEXA8), the other with a quadrangle at 8 nodes (QUAD8) at AXIS.

## 1 Reference problem

The mechanical problem is the load at the required displacement (in the case of relaxation) of an anisotherm axisymmetric test piece.

### 1.1 Geometry

The geometry of the cylindrical test piece is defined by:

- radius: r = 3 mm;
- length: l = 60 mm.

The plane (x,z) is a plane of symmetry, so only half of the test piece is modelled.

The test piece is subjected to a uniform temperature field which may vary over time.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 Thermal

The temperature field is homogeneous but not stationary. Its variation is as follows: T is constant and is 1000°C from t=0s to t=200000s (55.55h); then T increases linearly to 1025°C at t=2000000s (555.55h); T is then constant at 1025°C.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.126/100002010000032C00000264C6A9801F7DB6864F.png)

Image 1.3-1: Thermal loading

#### 1.3.2 Mechanical

The symmetry plane (z,x) imposes a blockage of UY in y=0mm. The mechanical load is UY=0.1 mm in z=30mm. The load is not instantaneous due to convergence problems. The choice was to impose the stress or displacement linearly over time so as to reach the final load in 0.1 seconds.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

No stress and deformation.
