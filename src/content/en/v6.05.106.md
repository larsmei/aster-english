# v6.05.106 SSNS106 – Degradation of a reinforced concrete plate under various stresses with global laws GLRC_DM and DHRC

**Summary:**

This test validates the GLRC_DM reinforced concrete plate damage model (see [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32)) and the DHRC steel-concrete internal sliding and damage model (see [R7.01.37](doc:r7.01.37)) for various cyclic loads: traction/compression, alternating bending, shear in the plane, anticlastic bending and their combinations. The analyses are done in static (STAT_NON_LINE). The results are compared with those of multi-layer modelling, in which the steels of the reinforcing sheet are represented by elasticity and concrete by the ENDO_ISOT_BETON behavior model (see [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04)).

This test can usefully serve as a starting point for setting the GLRC_DM model settings in the various loading situations that may occur in practice.

To complete, two modellingss are processed with a kit between GLRC_DM behavior and elastoplastic behavior with isotropic hardening, to represent the appearance of residual deformations as expected in reality.

The Modelling O tests the thermomechanical system. In this Modelling, it is verified that the two thermal expansion loads (homogeneous temperature in thickness and constant temperature gradient in thickness) lead to the same stress state as two simple mechanical loads: extension according to $\mathrm{Ox}$ and bending around $\mathrm{Oy}$.

The modellings H to J then L and M correspond to the modellings A to E for higher levels of stress. This allows the DHRC model to be validated, with a comparison with the GLRC_DM model. The N Modelling corresponds to an anticlastic bending case (corresponding to the shear in the bending plane).

The S Modelling is elastic only and allows to check the behaviour of the element COQUE_SOLIDE, especially in shearing.

## 2 reference solution

The reference solution is obtained by a semi-global Modelling in multi-layer * plate,* where the mesh and the loading are the same as for the modellings with the corresponding GLRC_DM law.

The concrete and the reinforcements are modelled separately. For each rebar sheet, one considers a layer that behaves only in the longitudinal direction of the reinforcements. So we will have 4 layers for the reinforcements.

In addition, several analytical results with the GLRC_DM model were established.

### 2.1 Models

On the same mesh 5 models representing reinforced concrete plate are defined: 1 DKT model for concrete and 4 GRILLE models for reinforcements (2 in $X$ direction, 2 in $Y$ direction for lower and upper parts). The rate of ironing for each rebar table is $8.0\times {10}^{-4}{m}^{2}/m$.

The position of the frames (lower or higher) is defined by the key word EXCENTREMENT under the key word factor GRILLE in the AFFE_CARA_ELEM operator, which is $\pm 0.04m$ *:* so it is assumed here that the steels in $X$ and $Y$ are in the same position, which is the usual approximation of the multilayer models.

The cracking of concrete is modelled by constitutive law ENDO_ISOT_BETON, while it is assumed that steel remains in the elastic domain.

### 3.5 Remarks

Based on the previous curves, the GLRC_DM model represents the overall tensile reinforced concrete behaviour – pure compression in a satisfactory manner. The relative error of the GLRC_DM model compared to the reference solution is permissible. It should be noted that the difference between the GLRC_DM and ENDO_ISOT_BETON model is the most important during the damage phase: the tensile concrete behaviour is then softening and a negative slope is found in the multi-layer reference model, despite the ironing (steel layers and ENDO_ISOT_BETON layers) while one of the assumptions of the GLRC_DM model is not to model the softening of the reinforced concrete.

Based on the isotropic equivalent material hypothesis (see [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32)), the GLRC_DM model slightly overestimates the effect of Poisson.

The symmetry of the response is also checked according to the chosen direction of load in compression-traction or reverse, depending on the load $\mathit{f1}$ or $\mathit{f2}$.

### 10.5 Remarks

The test case performed here aims to test the GLRC_DM and DHRC models under high loads so that the stiffness recovery of the steels can actually appear on the EIB reference. This test case takes over the test case SSNS106A by adding the DHRC reference to compare the GLRC_DM global model with a model that takes into account more energy dissipation via the representation of internal sliding mechanisms at the steel-beton interface. On the Effort-movement curve, it is noted that the DHRC response seems identical to that of GLRC_DM since there is no activation of the slip between the steels and the concrete.

On the other hand, there are large differences between the GLRC_DM and DHRC models and the EIB model curve on the Poisson displacement curve. This is explained by the fact that $\mathrm{DY}$ displacements are calculated in the multilayer calculation (EIB) on concrete layers, but the stress applied is such that concrete reaches its tensile strength limit at around the $0.1s$ moment.

## 19 Summary of the results

These tests, which are designed to validate the GLRC_DM model and the DHRC model, are also used to show a number of these weaknesses. In summary, the roles of the tests are as follows:

**A**: Only test the tensile/compression behaviour under the condition of uniformity (quasi $\mathrm{1D}$).

**B**: Only test the behaviour in cyclic bending under the condition of uniformity (quasi $\mathrm{1D}$). We identify the bending parameters.

**C**: Test behavior matching membrane and bending phenomena under the condition of uniformity (quasi $\mathrm{1D}$).

**D** :Test shear and distortion behaviour in the plane

**E** :Test bending coupling and shearing in the plane.

**F**:Test traction behavior – pure compression – with damaged elastoplastic "kit_dll" behavior (GLRC_DM + Von Bets).

**G**:Test traction behaviour – pure compression – with damaged elastoplastic "kit_ddl" (GLRC_DM + Von Bets).

**H** :Test the traction-compression behavior with significant stresses to evaluate GLRC_DM and DHRC

**I** :Test the bending behavior alternately with significant stresses to evaluate GLRC_DM and DHRC

**J** :Test the traction-flexion behaviour alternately with significant stresses to assess the coupled GLRC_DM and DHRC stress behaviour

**K** : Test compression behavior - traction with ALPHA_C=100 for GLRC_DM

**L**: Test pure shear and distortion behaviour in the plane with high stresses to evaluate GLRC_DM and DHRC

**M**: Test bending and shear coupling behaviour in the plane with high stresses to evaluate GLRC_DM and DHRC

**N**: Test the anticlastic bending behavior with high stresses to evaluate GLRC_DM and DHRC.

**O** :Test a thermomechanical load of expansion.

In most situations of modellings A to E, the displacements, forces and moments predicted by the GLRC_DM model are represented with a modest error (<10%) by reference to a multilayer model, which seems quite satisfactory for a model that is intended to represent the "global" behavior of a structure. The greater error is observed (~25%) in tests on the effect of Poisson in the damaging phase and when damage is activated in coupled bending membranes. The first defect is less important if one is more interested in dissipated energy in the system and less in displacements. The second defect is more embarrassing and shows well that an optimal "global" model should always be set in relation to the main stress that one wishes to model: the parameters of the model will be chosen accordingly. The main contribution to error is probably due to the anisotropy of the reinforced concrete not fully taken into account by the GLRC_DM model (see [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32)).

The modellings H-I-J-L-M-N allows direct comparison between GLRC_DM and DHRC behavior models for a choice of coherent parameters: the value of accurately representing the position of steels in the plate thickness, of enriching the description of dissipation by the degradation of the restraint steel-beton. However, the overall responses of these two models remain fairly similar in all loading situations: the triggering thresholds for damage are concomitant, the generalized force-displacement curves are similar; with the DHRC model the terms resulting from Poisson effect coupling appear more realistic.

Modelling O validates the use of these models in reinforced concrete building studies with thermal expansion loading.

The two modellings F and Gs where the GLRC_DM kit and Von Mises elastoplasticity are evaluated have above all a demonstrative value of the possibilities offered.
