# r7.01.44 CSSM constitutive model

**Summary:**

This document presents the formulation and the numerical integration of the elastoplastic constitutive model CSSM ("Critical State Soil Model").
The CSSM model is a combination of two models intended to represent respectively the monotonic and the drained cyclic behaviour of soils. The first component is based on the modified Cam-Clay model,
which describes the phenomena of dilatancy or contractancy as well as confinement effects under monotonic and drained loadings. The second component is a multi-mechanism model of Iwan type,
designed to represent the non-linear cyclic behaviour of soils at low strain levels.

The CSSM constitutive model comprises a total of eleven parameters: two to describe the reversible (elastic) behaviour, five for the irreversible behaviour linked to the modified Cam-Clay component, and three for the irreversible behaviour linked to the Iwan component.
A last parameter weights the contributions of the two components to the shear behaviour of the CSSM model.

The constitutive equations are solved using a direct implicit time-integration method, implemented in the tool MFront.

## 1 Notations

The notations and sign conventions used are those of continuum mechanics. A bold character represents a tensor of order two and an open-face character a tensor of order four.

For a tensor of order two $\boldsymbol{a}$, its decomposition into deviatoric and volumetric parts, denoted respectively $\boldsymbol{a}_d$ and $a_v$, is written as follows:

$$
\boldsymbol{a}=\boldsymbol{a}_d + \frac{a_v}{3}\boldsymbol{I},\quad\text{avec}\quad a_v = \boldsymbol{I}:\boldsymbol{a},\quad \boldsymbol{I}:\boldsymbol{a}_d=0
$$

where one denotes by $\boldsymbol{I}=\boldsymbol{\delta}$ the identity tensor of order two. One also writes $a_m=\cfrac{a_v}{3}$.

For a tensor of order two $\boldsymbol{a}$ of the same nature as a strain, one denotes its von Mises equivalent norm by
$a_{eq}=\sqrt{\cfrac{2}{3}\boldsymbol{a}_d:\boldsymbol{a}_d}$. For a tensor of order two $\boldsymbol{A}$ of the same nature as a stress,
it is written $A_{eq}=\sqrt{\cfrac{3}{2}\boldsymbol{A}_d:\boldsymbol{A}_d}$.

The notations defined in r7.01.44-table_notations_latines and r7.01.44-table_notations_grecques will be progressively supplemented by other symbols in the course of the presentation of the CSSM model.

## 2 Formulation of the model

The CSSM model is based on the association of constitutive equations similar to the modified Cam-Clay and Iwan models. These are detailed respectively
in the documents [r7.01.38](doc:r7.01.38) and [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48).

A rheological description of the CSSM model consists in superposing these two previous models as two components, the choice being:

- An association in parallel of the deviatoric behaviour.
- An association in series of the volumetric behaviour.

These associations are represented very schematically in r7.01.44-schema_rheo.

### 2.5 Suppression of the negative hardening of the model

The CSSM model inherits the same hardening as that of the modified Cam-Clay model, driven by the variable $\xi$. Now we have shown, following the plasticity
criterion , that this variable produces two hardenings:

- One kinematic: this mechanism is associated with its conjugate force $p_c$ by the state law -4, playing the role of a hydrostatic recall force.

This hardening moves the plasticity surface in the meridional plane of the stresses without modifying its size or its shape.

- The other isotropic: this mechanism is linked to the parametrisation of the criterion through the function $R(\xi)$. This hardening modifies the size of the plasticity surface.

The kinematic hardening alone cannot produce a negative hardening, because of the convexity of the potential from which $p_c$ derives.
On the other hand, a negative isotropic hardening can occur if $R(\xi)$ decreases, which potentially poses problems of numerical stability in a finite-element analysis.
To avoid this situation, it is proposed to replace the function $R(\xi)$, after incremental discretisation

The algorithmic tangent operator $\mathbb{D}$, computed once the constitutive equations have been solved, is obtained by a chain-rule differentiation:

$$

unchanged. In this situation, the response remains elastic up to a pressure of $-\sigma_m=200$ kPa. Thus, the use of the parameter $\eta$ makes it possible
to introduce a stronger initial compaction for low pressure levels, where the volumetric strain $\varepsilon_v$ is higher in compression. This possibility is interesting for modelling the strongly
contractant behaviour of soils of high porosity.

### 5.3 Example of experimental comparison

r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_0, r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_1 and r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_01 confront the predictions of the CSSM model with
experimental data on loose rockfill materials coming from two
triaxial compression tests at the confinements $p_{\mathrm{conf}}=100$ kPa and $p_{\mathrm{conf}}=200$ kPa, as well as from cyclic shear tests.

On the triaxial compression tests,
the CSSM model was calibrated "as well as possible" by adjusting the five parameters  $p_{c0},M,\beta,\omega$ and $C$, assuming
the other parameters of r7.01.44-table_parametres_simules and considering three distinct cases which have already been the subject of previous comparisons:

- $\rho=0\leftrightarrow$ effect of component 2 of the model only on the shear behaviour.
- $\rho=1\leftrightarrow$  effect of component 1 of the model only.
- $\rho=0.1\leftrightarrow$ effect of the two components of the model.

The last case corresponds to considering that the shear stiffness in component 1 $\rho\mu$ ("static" stiffness) is ten times smaller than the total shear stiffness
$\mu$ ("dynamic" stiffness). This ratio of ten is not unreasonable, compared with the ratios observed between the dynamic and static moduli for soil materials.

r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_0 confronts the predictions for the case where $\rho=0$ with the experimental data. It is clear
that component 2 of the model on its own
does not succeed in reproducing in a realistic manner the evolutions of the von Mises equivalent stress
$\sigma_{eq}$ and of the volumetric strain $\varepsilon_v$ observed during the triaxial compression
tests. On the other hand, component 2 offers a fairly precise representation of the normalised secant shear modulus $\mu_{\mathrm{secant}}/\mu$
during shear tests. As for the reduced damping, the prediction progressively overestimates the experimental evolution with the increase of the distortion level $\gamma$.
This prediction is very similar to that of the Iwan model but also of Hujeux [r7.01.23](doc:r7.01.23).

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.44/comparaison_exp_CSSM_rho_0.svg)

*Comparison of the predictions in the case $\rho=0$ with the experimental data.*

r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_1 confronts the predictions for the case where $\rho=1$ with the experimental data.
In this case, component 1 of the model appreciably improves the evolution of the von Mises equivalent stress during the triaxial compression
tests. However, only the volumetric strain for the confinement $p_{\mathrm{conf}}=200$ kPa is correctly reproduced.
For the confinement
$p_{\mathrm{conf}}=100$ kPa, the prediction lacks dilatancy. As regards the shear tests, the predictions
of the normalised secant shear modulus $\mu_{\mathrm{secant}}/\mu$ and of the reduced damping are largely deteriorated compared
with those obtained for the case $\rho=0$.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.44/comparaison_exp_CSSM_rho_1.svg)

*Comparison of the predictions in the case $\rho=1$ with the experimental data.*

r7.01.44-comparaison_rho_01 finally presents the predictions in the case where $\rho=0.1$. This combination of components 1 and 2 of the model makes it possible:

- To improve the observed evolution of the dilatancy for the confinement $p_{\mathrm{conf}}=100$ kPa during the triaxial compression tests, while keeping a

correct representation of the von Mises equivalent stress.
This representation is similar to that obtained with component 1 alone in the case where $\rho=0$.

- To obtain predictions close to those of component 2 for the shear tests when $\rho=1$.

In summary, the combination of the two components of the model makes it possible to take advantage of the best characteristics of each individual component,
thus offering an improved representation for both types of test, monotonic and cyclic.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.44/comparaison_exp_CSSM.svg)

*Comparison of the predictions in the case $\rho=0.1$ with the experimental data.*

> **Note:**
> The previous comparisons validate the relevance of the CSSM model on loadings with shear, with a calibration
of the parameters $p_{c0},M,\beta,\omega$ and $C$ on the triaxial compression tests. Nevertheless, in order to evaluate more globally, and rigorously, the capabilities of the model,
it will be necessary to compare it with isotropic compression tests.
This step will make it possible to estimate more precisely
the parameter $\eta$, here taken close to one in r7.01.44-table_parametres_simules, and to refine the value of $\omega$, which significantly influence the initial compaction, as we have seen on
r7.01.44-compression_isotrope with $\eta\in\lbrace 0,0.99\rbrace$.
