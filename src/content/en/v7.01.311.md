# v7.01.311 HPLA311 - Murakami 11.39. Circular fissure in the center of a sphere subjected to a uniform temperature on the lips

**Summary:**

This test is based on the independent validation of version 3 in the mechanical of the breakage.

This is a static basic steady-state thermal analysis load axisymmetry test calculated by elements on the same mesh of a limited domain.

The behavior is linear isotropic thermoelastic.

It consists of two modellings axisymmetric for which the ratio a/b is varied, a being the radius of the internal circular crack in the horizontal plane xoy and b radius of the sphere. The stress factors K and the energy return rate are calculated by the theta method (operator CALC_G).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v7.01.311/100192AE00005E4200005BAD4E23E5FB1CB39C9E.svg)

a: radius of the internal circular crack in the horizontal plane xoy

b: sphere radius, with b = 2.5.10-3 m.

> The radius a varies according to the model.

### 1.2 Material properties

| Young's modulus | E = 2 1011Pa |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu$ = 0,3 |
| linear expansion coefficient | $\alpha$ = 1,2 10-5°C-1 |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

UX = ur = 0 on the axis of revolution X = r = 0

UY = uz = 0 in the horizontal plane Y = z = 0, outside the lips a ≤ r ≤ b

Lips are supposed to be free of stress (no partial closing of the crack).

Zero temperature on the sphere surface.

Zero reference temperature (temperature at which thermal deformations are considered to be zero).

Tf = - Tf on the lips of the crack, including the crack bottom. The steady-state thermal analysis problem (Dirichlet type) must be solved beforehand by elements finished on the same mesh as that intended for mechanical calculation. Tf = 100 °C is taken.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Analytical calculation by Hankel transform.

### 2.2 Baseline

For reference solution, the rays shall check condition a/b < 0.5.

$\eta =\frac{a}{b}<0,5$

${K}_{I}=\frac{E\alpha {T}_{f}}{1-\nu }\cdot \sqrt{(\frac{a}{\pi})}\cdot {F}_{I}$

${F}_{I}=1-0.6366\eta -0.4053{\eta}^{2}+2.0163{\eta}^{3}-0.6773{\eta}^{4}-3.8523{\eta}^{5}+4.1687{\eta}^{6}+3.2741{\eta}^{7}$

## 5 Summary of the results

- The calculation of K and G in axisymmetry in the presence of a load steady-state thermal analysis gives good results since the difference maximum for G is 1.75% (excluding first crown) for $\nu$ = 0.4.
- The K and G results for $\nu$ = 0.01 (modelization B) are better than for $\nu$ = 0.4 (modelization A).
- The G calculation is slightly less sensitive to the choice of the kroner of integration than the K calculation.
