# u2.02.03 User's guide for the discrete elements

**Summary:**

This document is a user's guide for the modellings of the discrete elements.

The discrete elements are used in the numerical modelling of stiffnesses, dampings or masses localised on a node (`POI1` mesh cell) or between two nodes (`SEG2` mesh cell). Their degrees of freedom are generalised displacements.

They can be used in linear or non-linear mechanics.

## 1 Introduction

The discrete elements are used to model, very simply, masses, springs or dampers.

They are used to assign point properties to nodes of the mesh and to define point masses, stiffnesses and support damping.

These modellings can be used in linear and non-linear mechanics, under the assumption of small displacements.

Three categories of discrete elements are described in this document:

- mass,
- stiffness,
- damper,

In a first part, the data setup of the properties of the discrete elements is presented, with the various options available.
In the second and third parts, the computation and post-processing operators for the discrete elements are given.
The last part gives a list of test cases, examples of use of the discrete elements.

## 2 Assignment of the properties of the discrete elements

### 2.1 Support mesh cells of the discrete elements

The support mesh cells of the discrete elements are `POI1` (mesh cell with one node) or `SEG2` (segment with two nodes).

### 2.4 Elementary characteristics, operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM

In this part, the characteristic operands of the discrete elements are described. See the user documentation of the operator [AFFE_CARA_ELEM](doc:u4.42.01).
These keywords make it possible to assign directly, to entities (mesh cells or nodes) which support elements of type `DIS_T`, `DIS_TR` (DISCRET 3D) or `2D_DIS_T`, `2D_DIS_TR` (DISCRET_2D), matrices of **stiffness**, of **mass** or of **damping**.
The characteristics can be defined by one of the following four keywords and govern the linear elastic constitutive behaviour.

#### 2.4.1 Keyword: DISCRET or DISCRET_2D

The characteristics of the discrete elements are described by the following nomenclature:

**with**

$\begin{cases}M & Masse\\K & Rigidité\\A & Amortissement\end{cases}$

$\begin{cases}T & Translations\\TR & Translations~et~rotations\end{cases}$

$D$ if present, the matrix is diagonal

$\begin{cases}N & support~nœud~ou~POI1\\L & support~SEG2\end{cases}$

The possibilities for assigning the matrices are:

- Stiffness matrices: K_T_D_N, K_TR_D_N, K_T_D_L, K_TR_D_L, K_T_N, K_TR_N, K_T_L, K_TR_L
- Mass matrices: M_T_D_N, M_TR_D_N, M_T_D_L, M_TR_D_L, M_T_N, M_TR_N, M_T_L, M_TR_L
- Damping matrices: A_T_D_N, A_TR_D_N, A_T_D_L, A_TR_D_L, A_T_N, A_TR_N, A_T_L, A_TR_L

The number of values required to define the matrices is a function of the characteristic chosen in [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01).

By default, the characteristics are given in the global frame. The local frame can be defined for a node, a POI1 mesh cell or an SEG2 mesh cell by means of the keyword ORIENTATION of AFFE_CARA_ELEM and by specifying REPERE=’LOCAL’.

- The index $T$ or $\mathit{TR}$ must be compatible with the definition of the models in AFFE_MODELE, DIS_T or DIS_TR
- One can apply mass and stiffness characteristics on the same mesh cell. If one assigns only a mass on a discrete element, one obtains the alarm DISCRETS_27 to indicate that a zero stiffness is considered. The alarm DISCRETS_26 is emitted in the case where one assigns only a stiffness. To suppress these alarms, the various matrices must be supplied.
- The keyword AMOR_HYST= ${\mathit{amor}}_{h}$  makes it possible to build a complex stiffness matrix (modelling of hysteretic damping); the matrix built is: $\left(1+i.{\mathit{amor}}_{h}\right).\mathrm{K}$ where $\mathrm{K}$ is the matrix K_*  whose values are supplied in the same occurrence of the keyword DISCRET. The complex stiffness matrix will be built during a call to CALC_MATR_ELEM [U4.61.01](doc:u4.61.01) with the option AMOR_HYST (see test SDLD313 and [R5.05.04](doc:r5.05.04)).
- The complete definition of the stiffness, mass or damping matrices makes it possible to define eccentric elements. The test case SDNL113 gives the example of eccentric masses ([V5.02.113](doc:v5.02.113)).

## 4 Additional computations and post-processing

### 4.1 Computations per element: operators CALC_CHAMP

The post-processing options available via CALC_CHAMP are:

- SIEF_ELGA
- SIEF_ELNO
- EFGE_ELGA
- EFGE_ELNO

All these fields give the computation of the generalised forces per element at the Gauss points or at the nodes from the displacements in the local frame of the element.
For discrete elements of type POI1, the Gauss point is at the same position as the node. For a discrete element of type SEG2, the Gauss point is in the middle of the segment (test case ZZZZ266A).
There are no post-processing options for the strains, because the constitutive laws are expressed in forces–displacements and not in stresses–strains.

### 4.2 Computations of quantities on all or part of the structure: operator POST_ELEM

The operator POST_ELEM (documentation [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22)) makes it possible to compute quantities on all or part of the structure. The quantities computed correspond to particular computation options of the assigned modelling.

The options available for the discrete elements are:

- MASS_INER: computation of the geometric characteristics (volume, centre of gravity, inertia matrix).
- ENER_POT: computation of the potential energy of deformation at equilibrium from the displacements in linear mechanics of continuous media (2D and 3D) and in linear mechanics for the structural elements.
- ENER_CIN: computation of the kinetic energy from a velocity field or from a displacement field and a frequency (only for the structural elements and the 3D elements).

The options ENER_ELAS and ENER_TOTALE are not yet available for the discrete elements.

### 4.3 Uplift computations: operator POST_DECOLLEMENT

This post-processing operator computes the contact or uplifted surface between the raft and the soil during a soil–structure interaction (ISS) analysis, carried out with the operator DYNA_NON_LINE.
This post-processing can be useful, for example, for a modelling of the soil with elements whose characteristics are supplied by means of RIGI_PARASOL and which are assigned the constitutive behaviour DIS_CHOC.

## 5 Notes

### 5.1 Length of the discrete elements

For the discrete elements whose support mesh cell is a two-node element SEG2, the length of the element does not come into the response of the element, except for the constitutive law DIS_CHOC.
Indeed, for the other constitutive laws, the relation is expressed as a function of the relative displacements of the two nodes.
For the law DIS_CHOC, the length of the element comes into the determination of the state of contact or of separation, by comparing the displacement of the nodes as a function of the length of the element and of the values DIST_1 and DIST_2.

### 5.2 Transmission of the forces

There is no transmission of the moment associated with a force couple. This limitation is illustrated in the case where a displacement DY is imposed between two nodes of a discrete element DIS_TR or DIS_T.
The two nodal reaction forces FY are equal and opposite, but this couple does not generate moments for the case DIS_T and, for the case DIS_TR, if the coupling terms (translation–rotation) are assumed to be zero.

For discrete elements of great length, one may therefore find an unwanted global balance of the moments.
