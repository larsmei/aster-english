# r5.03.33 Plastic and viscoplastic constitutive laws with non-linear isotropic hardening VMIS_ISOT_NL and VISC_ISOT_NL

**Summary:**

This document describes the elastoviscoplastic constitutive model with non-linear isotropic hardening, available in a local version and with a gradient of internal variables (GRAD_VARI or GRAD_INCO). A modelling of large strains is accessible via the kinematics GDEF_LOG. The hardening function accumulates several terms (an affine term, two exponential terms, a power term, a Lüders plateau) capable of covering most of the non-linear responses of a metallic material; each can easily be cancelled, which offers a form of modularity to the constitutive relation (for example: linear hardening, or power-law hardening only). Finally, the behaviour may be independent of time (VMIS_ISOT_NL) or viscoplastic (VISC_ISOT_NL). In the latter case, plasticity evolves according to a Norton model in which the threshold function appears.

## 1 Domain of application

### 1.1 Purpose

The constitutive law VMIS_ISOT_TRAC makes it possible to account for any form of non-linear isotropic hardening via the stress–strain response in a simple tensile test, given in the form of a sampled function. However, identification of this response may prove simpler if one adopts a predefined function depending on a small number of parameters. The user is then also spared the sampling stage, which is sometimes delicate when the function varies rapidly. The performance of the integration of the constitutive law also benefits, notably when the sampling required would have been particularly fine.

This is the whole purpose of the constitutive law VMIS_ISOT_NL (plasticity) or VISC_ISOT_NL (viscoplasticity), dedicated to metallic materials. The hardening function $R(\kappa)$ consists of the assembly of several elementary terms:

$$
R(\kappa)
=
\left \lbrace \begin{array}{cc}
    \widehat{R}(\varepsilon_{L}^{p})& \text{si }\kappa \le \varepsilon_{L}^{p}\\
    \widehat{R}(\kappa)& \text{si }\kappa \ge \varepsilon_{L}^{p}
\end{array}  \right.
$$

where $\kappa$ denotes the hardening variable (which generally coincides with the accumulated plastic strain), $\varepsilon_{L}^{p}$ fixes the limit of the Lüders plateau (zero in the absence of a plateau) and:

$$
\widehat{R}(\kappa)
=
{R}_{0}
+
{R}_{H}\kappa
+
{R}_{1}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{1}\kappa })
+
{R}_{2}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{2}\kappa })
+
{R}_{K}{({p}_{0}+\kappa)}^{{\gamma}_{K}}
$$

Cancellation of the affine term, of each exponential term or of the power term is carried out by setting respectively ${R}_{H}=0$, ${R}_{1}=0$ or ${R}_{2}=0$ and finally ${R}_{K}=0$, which makes it possible to obtain a simpler hardening function where appropriate.

This constitutive model is compatible:

- with a kinematics of small perturbations (HPP) or of large strains (GDEF_LOG);
- with a plastic response independent of the loading rate (VMIS_ISOT_NL) or a viscoplastic response (VISC_ISOT_NL) on the basis of a Norton law;
- with a local formulation or a formulation with a gradient of an internal variable in which the gradient of the hardening variable appears, cf. [Lorentz & Andrieux, 1999] and [Zhang et al., 2018].

Finally, it should be made clear that the elastic part of the model is expressed explicitly, that is the stress is expressed as a function of the strain and of the plastic strain in the current state. There is therefore no incremental evolution of the stress; it does not contribute to defining the mechanical state of the system, it is a consequence of it (in particular, the stress does not enter the definition of an initial state of the system).

Note:

> The practical consequence of this formalism is that an initial stress given in ETAT_INIT for [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03) will be ignored.

### 1.2 Material parameters

The modular expression of the constitutive relation makes it possible to group the various parameters of the model by categories:

- the elastic part is isotropic and depends on Young's modulus $E$ and on Poisson's ratio $\nu$, defined under the factor keyword ELAS (E, NU) of the command [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01);
- the parameters of the isotropic hardening are defined under the factor keyword ECRO_NL (R0, RH, R1, GAMMA_1, R2, GAMMA_2, RK, P0, GAMMA_K, EPSP_LUDERS);
- the parameters of the Norton viscosity law, when it is present, are defined under the factor keyword NORTON (coefficient K and exponent N);
- in a non-local formulation [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01), the corresponding parameters must also be defined under the factor keyword NON_LOCAL (C_GRAD_VARI and COEF_PENA_LAGR).

The parameters may depend on temperature (factor keywords *_FO). The constitutive law is expressed in mechanical strains, that is it is compatible with the usual shrinkage terms, notably the thermal strain.

### 1.3 Internal variables

The constitutive law relies on eight internal variables: on the one hand, the hardening level $\kappa$ and the six components of the plastic strain (which it is therefore not useful to compute in post-processing); and on the other hand, for post-processing purposes only, the state of the current time step is also retained, with the values 0 = elastic, 1 = plastic with regular flow, 2 = plastic with singular flow (it will be seen below what this means).

The internal variables are grouped in the following table:

| EPSEQ | V1 | Hardening variable $\kappa$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INDIPLAS | V2 | State of the current time step (0, 1 or 2) |
| EPSPXX - EPSPYZ | V3 - V8 | Components of the plastic strain |

## 2 Continuous model

### 2.1 Constitutive equations: local plastic model

This is a classical plasticity model with a von Mises threshold and isotropic hardening. The (mechanical) strain $\strain$ is decomposed additively into a plastic part $\strain^{p}$ and an elastic part $\strain^{e}$, the latter being related to the stress by the following elastic relation:

$$
\sigma
=
K
\trace (\strain^{e})\tensTwoUnit
+
2\mu \,\dev(\strain^{e})\quad;\quad 3K=\frac{E}{1-2\nu }\quad;\quad 2\mu =\frac{E}{1+\nu }
$$

where $\trace$ and $\dev$ denote respectively the trace and the deviator of a second-order tensor.

The threshold function is now introduced, the hardening function $R(\kappa)$ having already been introduced in:

$$
F(\stress,\kappa )
=
\stressCmp_{eq}-R(\kappa)\quad;\quad\stressCmp_{eq}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\dev(\stress):\dev(\stress)}
$$

One remains within the framework of plasticity with positive hardening, so that the hardening function $R(\kappa)$ is required to be increasing.

The evolution of the plastic strain is governed by the flow equation. When the threshold function is differentiable with respect to $\stress$, the flow direction is normal to the threshold surface:

$$
\text{Si } \stressCmp_{eq}\ne 0
\text{ alors } {\dot\strain}^{p}=\dot{\kappa} \tensTwo{N}
\text{ avec } \tensTwo{N}=\frac{3}{2}\dev\frac{\sigma}{\stressCmp_{eq}}
$$

In the singular case where $\stressCmp_{eq}=0$, the notion of derivative is generalised by means of notions of convex analysis (subgradient). The flow direction is subject only to the following condition:

$$
\text{Si } \stressCmp_{eq}=0
\text{ alors } \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} \Vert {\dot\strain}^{p} \Vert \le \dot{\kappa}
\text{ et } \trace({\dot\strain}^{p})=0
$$

Finally, the evolution of the hardening variable is fixed by the consistency condition:

$$
\dot{\kappa} \ge 0 \quad ; \quad F(\sigma, \kappa)\le 0 \quad ; \quad \dot{\kappa}F(\sigma, \kappa)=0
$$

### 2.2 Taking viscosity into account

In the presence of viscosity, plastic flow is delayed. More precisely, the rate of evolution of the hardening is no longer fixed by the consistency condition, but is henceforth a function of the intensity of the threshold function according to the Norton law:

$$
\dot{\kappa}
=
{\left(\frac{\langle F(\sigma,\kappa ) \rangle}{K}\right)}^{n}
$$

where the Macaulay brackets $\langle \cdot \rangle$ denote the positive part. Equations to remain unchanged. When $K \to 0$, the elastoplastic model is recovered.

### 2.3 Taking the hardening gradient into account

A non-local formulation with a gradient of an internal variable is appropriate when the hardening gradient $\nabla \kappa$ becomes large. It consists in introducing an additional term ${\Phi}^{\mathit{grad}}$ into the free energy which reflects the interactions between neighbouring material points, see [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01):

$$
{\Phi}^{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla \kappa)
=
\frac{1}{2} \, c \, {\left(\nabla \kappa \right)}^{2}
$$

Using the augmented relaxed formulation presented in [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01), the impact on the constitutive relation takes the form of a modification of the thermodynamic force associated with the hardening variable and therefore, in practice, of a modification of the hardening function:

$$
\tilde{R}(\kappa)
=
R(\kappa) + r\kappa - \varphi  \text{ avec } \varphi = b\lambda + r \, a
$$

where $\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the relaxation, $r>0$ the augmentation coefficient and $a$ the interpretation of the hardening variable at the scale of the structure. These three quantities are data as far as the constitutive relation is concerned. In the end, the hardening function is corrected by an affine term, which therefore adds no complexity relative to the numerical treatment of the model. On the other hand, since the sign and the amplitude of $\varphi$ are not fixed, this explains why the case of a singular flow for which $\stressCmp_{eq}=0$ must be taken into account.

## 3 Numerical integration

As regards the integration of the constitutive law, that is the calculation of the internal variables and of the stresses for a given (mechanical) strain history, there is no longer any need to distinguish whether the formulation is local or with a gradient. Indeed, the impact of the latter is limited to modifying the hardening function according to. One will continue here to denote it by $R(\kappa)$ without distinction.

On the other hand, the plastic and viscoplastic cases will be distinguished, even though it can be shown that the latter likewise reduces to a correction of the threshold function in the discretised equations.

Finally, in the presence of a Lüders plateau, it has been chosen to simplify the solution by reducing it to the situation without a Lüders plateau. Indeed, because of the uniqueness of the solution of the discretised problem, that solution lies either on the plateau or beyond it. One begins by looking for it on the plateau: one is then reduced to the case of perfect plasticity (a particular case of the function $\widehat{R}$). If the solution obtained in this way proves to lie beyond the plateau, it is not taken into account (it is not the solution of the problem) and the solution is repeated, but this time looking for the solution on the part $\widehat{R}(\kappa)$ of the hardening function, that is without concern for the Lüders plateau (by uniqueness, one is then certain that this new solution does indeed lie beyond the plateau). This is why the solution method in the absence of a Lüders plateau is all that is described below.

### 3.1 Discretised equations

The time discretisation of the constitutive equations is based on an implicit Euler scheme (even in viscoplasticity), that is the various variables of the problem are expressed at the final time instant of the time step under consideration. ${Q}_{n}$ denotes the value of a quantity $Q$ at the beginning of the time step, $\Delta Q$ its increment during the time step and (simply) $Q$ its value at the end of the time step. The mechanical state at the beginning of the time step $(\strain_{n},\strain^{p}_{n},{\kappa}_{n})$ is assumed known, as is the strain increment $\Delta \strain$ (and therefore also the strain $\strain$). The aim is then to compute the increments of the internal variables $\Delta \strain^{p}_{n}$ and $\Delta \kappa$, together with the stress at the end of the time step $\stress$.

The discretisation of the stress–strain relation is written as follows:

$$
\stress
=
K \trace(\strain - {\strain^{p}}_{n}-\Delta \strain^{p})\tensTwoUnit
+
2 \, \mu \,\dev(\strain-{\strain^{p}}_{n}-\Delta \strain^{p})
$$

Whatever the flow regime, regular or singular, it is found that the trace of the plastic-strain increment is zero, so that the same is true of the plastic strain at every time instant (isochoric plastic strain). Relation is simplified and an elastic stress (or elastic trial stress), denoted ${\stress}^{e}$ and which is known, can be brought out in it:

$$
\stress
=
{\stress}^{e}
-
2 \, \mu \Delta \strain^{p}
\text{ avec }
{\stress}^{e}
=
K \trace(\strain)\tensTwoUnit
+
2 \, \mu \,\dev( \strain -\strain^{p}_{n} )
$$

In the case of a regular flow, the time discretisation of is written:

$$
\Delta \strain^{p}
=
\frac{3}{2} \, \frac{\Delta \kappa}{\stressCmp_{eq}} \, \dev({\stress}^{e}-2\mu \Delta \strain^{p})
$$

After a few algebraic manipulations, one deduces:

$$
\stressCmp_{eq}
=
\stressCmp_{eq}^{e}
-
3\mu \Delta \kappa
$$

$$
\Delta \strain^{p}
=
\frac{3}{2} \, \Delta \kappa \frac{\dev({\stress}^{e})}{\stressCmp_{eq}^{e}}
$$

$$
\stress
=
\frac{1}{3} \, \trace({\stress}^{e})\tensTwoUnit
+
\left (
    \stressCmp_{eq}^{e}
     -
    3\mu \Delta \kappa
\right )
\frac{\dev({\stress}^{e})}{\stressCmp_{eq}^{e}}
$$

The regular regime is valid only if $\stressCmp_{eq}>0$, that is, from

$$
3 \mu \Delta \kappa > \stressCmp_{eq}^{e}
$$

In the case of a singular flow, $\stressCmp_{eq}=0$. In view of the definition of the von Mises equivalent stress, this is equivalent to $\dev(\stress) = \tensTwoZero$. In particular, the stress is written simply:

$$
\stress
=
\frac{1}{3} \trace({\stress}^{e})\tensTwoUnit \text{ et } \stressCmp_{eq}=0
$$

Furthermore, by substituting $\dev(\stress)=\tensTwoZero$ into, one deduces the plastic-strain increment:

$$
2 \mu \Delta \strain^{p}
=
\dev({\stress}^{e})
$$

And the flow is subject to, that is, after discretisation:

$$
\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}} \Vert {\Delta \strain }^{p}\Vert \le \Delta \kappa
$$

Substituting into, one obtains:

$$
\stressCmp_{eq}^{e} \le 3\mu \Delta \kappa
$$

It may be noted that the conditions and make it possible to discriminate the regular case from the singular case. In particular, the separation value ${\kappa}^{s}$ between the two regimes is introduced:

$$
{\kappa}^{s}
=
{\kappa}_{n}
+
\frac{\stressCmp_{eq}^{e}}{3\mu }
$$

### 3.2 Solution of the discretised problem — plastic case without viscosity

At this stage, all that remains is to determine the increment $\Delta \kappa$ via the consistency condition, which is written after discretisation:

$$
\Delta \kappa \ge 0\quad ; \quad F(\stress,\kappa )\le 0\quad ; \quad\Delta \kappa F(\stress,\kappa )=0
$$

To this end, one begins by considering the hypothesis of an elastic solution, that is $\Delta \kappa =0$ and $\Delta \strain^{p}=\tensTwoZero$. In this case, $\stress={\stress}^{e}$ and this solution is valid according to as long as ${F}^{e} = F({\stress}^{e},{\kappa}_{n}) \le 0$.

Suppose now that ${F}^{e}>0$. The aim is to find $\Delta \kappa > 0$ such that $F(\stress ,\kappa )=0$. Under the hypothesis of a regular flow (${\kappa}_{n}<\kappa <{\kappa}^{s}$), one may substitute into the expression for the threshold function, which is then equal to:

$$
F(\stress,\kappa)
=
\stressCmp_{eq}^{e}
-
3\mu \Delta \kappa
-
R(\kappa)
=
\widehat{M}(\kappa)
$$

Since the hardening function $R$ is increasing, the function $\widehat{M}$ is strictly decreasing. Furthermore, $\widehat{M}({\kappa}_{n})={F}^{e}>0$. There exists a (unique) solution in the regular-flow regime if and only if $\widehat{M}({\kappa}^{s})<0$. A Newton method with controlled bounds then makes it possible to determine it.

In the case of a singular flow (${\kappa}^{s} \le \kappa$), $\stressCmp_{eq}=0$ and the consistency condition is written simply:

$$
F(\stress,\kappa)
=
-R(\kappa)
=
0
$$

It may be noted that $-R$ is a (strictly) decreasing function. Moreover, when $\kappa \to +\infty$, $-R$ is negative. Indeed, in the local case, $R(+\infty) \ge R(0)>0$. And in the non-local case, $r>0$, so that the function $R$ tends to infinity. There therefore exists a (unique) solution in the singular-flow regime if and only if $-R({\kappa}^{s})=\widehat{M}({\kappa}^{s})\ge 0$. Here again, a Newton method makes it possible to determine this solution.

Finally, there exists a unique solution to the problem of integration of the constitutive law. The regime depends on the values $\widehat{M}^{e}=\widehat{M}({\kappa}_{n})$ and $\widehat{M}^{s}=\widehat{M}({\kappa}^{s})$, where $\widehat{M}^{e}\ge \widehat{M}^{s}$:

- if $0 \ge \widehat{M}^{e} \ge \widehat{M}^{s}$: elastic solution;
- if $\widehat{M}^{e} \ge 0 \ge \widehat{M}^{s}$: plastic solution with regular flow;
- if $\widehat{M}^{e} \ge \widehat{M}^{s} \ge 0$: plastic solution with singular flow.

### 3.3 Solution of the discretised problem: viscoplastic case

In the presence of viscosity, the consistency condition is replaced by the Norton evolution law. Once discretised, the latter is written:

$$
\frac{\Delta \kappa}{\Delta t}
=
\left(
    \frac{\langle F(\stress,\kappa) \rangle }{K}
\right)^{n}
$$

And inverting this relation:

$$
{C}_{\Delta t}{\Delta \kappa }^{q}
=
\langle F(\stress ,\kappa ) \rangle \quad ; \quad{C}_{\Delta t} = \frac{K}{{\Delta t}^{1/n}} \quad ; \quad q=\frac{1}{n}
$$

It may also be expressed in an equivalent manner in the form of a Kuhn–Tucker relation:

$$
\Delta \kappa \ge 0\quad ; \quad{F}_{v}(\stress ,\kappa )\le 0\quad ; \quad{F}_{v}(\stress ,\kappa )\,\Delta \kappa =0
$$

where

$$
{F}_{v}(\stress,\kappa)
=
F(\stress,\kappa)
-
{C}_{\Delta t}{\Delta \kappa }^{q}
=
\stressCmp_{eq}
-
{R}_{v}(\kappa)
$$

with

$$
{R}_{v}(\kappa)
=
R(\kappa)
+
{C}_{\Delta t}{(\kappa -{\kappa}_{n})}^{q}
$$

After discretisation, taking viscosity into account reduces to the addition of a (positive and increasing) term in the hardening function.

One might be tempted to apply the same solution method as before but, unfortunately, this new term is not differentiable at ${\kappa}_{n}$ because $q<1$ in general ($n>1$). This is why it is proposed to begin by seeking the solution of the problem without viscosity (in a coarse manner) and then to switch to the solution with viscosity by a Newton method, by means of the change of variable $\Delta \kappa \to {C}_{\Delta t}\,{\Delta \kappa }^{q}$, thus eliminating the problem of non-differentiability (and the steep slopes which are penalising for a Newton method).

### 3.4 Tangent matrix

In the local case, the aim is to determine the fourth-order tensor ${H}_{\stressCmp \strainCmp} = \delta \stress / \delta \strain$. In the non-local case, the second-order tensors ${H}_{\stressCmp \varphi} =\delta \stress /\delta \varphi$ and ${H}_{\kappa \strainCmp}=\delta \kappa /\delta \strain$ must also be constructed, together with the scalar ${H}_{\kappa \varphi }=\delta \kappa /\delta \varphi$.

The tangent operator depends on the flow regime. In the elastic case, it reduces to:

$$
\delta \stress
=
K \, \trace(\delta \strain) \tensTwoUnit
+
2\mu \dev( \delta \strain )
\text{ et }
\delta \kappa =0
$$

In the case of the singular regime, the expression is again very simple:

$$
\delta \stress
=
K \, \trace(\delta \strain) \tensTwoUnit
\text{ et }
\delta \kappa
=
{\left(\frac{\partial {\tilde{R}_{v}}}{\partial \kappa }\right)}^{-1}\delta \varphi
$$

where the hardening function contains, where applicable, the terms linked to the non-local formulation and to viscosity (hence the tilde and the subscript, to recall this explicitly).

The regular-flow regime leads to a more complex tangent operator. Use of a von Mises criterion leads to the following derivatives:

$$
\delta \stressCmp_{eq}
=
3 \mu N:\delta \strain
$$

and

$$
\delta N
=
\frac{1}{\stressCmp_{eq}} \left[2\mu \dev(\delta \strain)-\delta \stressCmp_{eq}N\right]
$$

Then applying derivatives of composite functions, one obtains:

$$
\delta \stress
=
K \trace(\delta \strain )\tensTwoUnit
+
2\mu \left[
    1
    -
    \frac{3\mu \Delta \kappa}{\stressCmp_{eq}}
\right] \, \dev(\delta \strain)
+
\frac{9{\mu}^{2}\Delta \kappa}{\stressCmp_{eq}} \tensTwo{N} \otimes \tensTwo{N} : \delta \strain
-
3\mu \tensTwo{N} \delta \kappa
$$

$$
\delta \kappa
=
\left[
    3\mu
    +
    \frac{\partial {\tilde{R}_{v}}}{\partial \kappa}
\right]^{-1}
\,
\left[
    \delta \stressCmp_{eq}
    +
    \delta \varphi
\right]
$$

In the prediction phase ($\strain =\strain_{n}$), two specific features are to be noted:

- In the presence of viscosity, $\delta \kappa=0$ because the derivative $\frac{\partial {\tilde{R}_{v}}}{\partial \kappa}$ is infinite.
- The tangent operators are not continuous at the transitions between regimes ($\widehat{M}^{e}\approx 0$ and $\widehat{M}^{s}\approx 0$) and in that case one opts for the domain corresponding to the regime of the previous step. On the other hand, if the mechanical state at the beginning of the time step is not in the neighbourhood of a transition, then the regime is fixed as for the integration of the constitutive law.

## 4 Piloting PRED_ELAS

Within the framework of the constitutive relation VMIS_ISOT_NL, the loading piloting PRED_ELAS consists in determining the intensity of the piloted loading $\eta$ such that the increment of the hardening variable $\Delta \kappa$ is equal to a target value $\Delta p$ fixed by the current time step $\Delta t$ and the value of the piloting coefficient COEF_MULT $C$ via $\Delta p = \frac{\Delta t}{C} \times \text{5 \%}$. One may refer to [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80) for more details on the principle of the PRED_ELAS piloting method. It is recalled in particular that the notion of piloting is meaningful only in the absence of a viscous behaviour. The case without viscosity is therefore all that is considered here. Furthermore, only the piloting for the local model (i.e. without taking the plasticity gradient into account) is treated at present.

At the algorithmic scale of the constitutive law, the piloting introduces a strain affine with respect to $\eta$, namely $\strain=\tensTwo{\strain^0}+\eta\,{\strain^1}$ where ${\strain^0}$ and ${\strain^1}$ are (tensor) quantities known at this stage. In view of, one then has:

$$
\stressCmp^e_{eq}(\eta)
=
 3\mu\,\Delta p + R(\kappa_n + \Delta p)
$$

where the right-hand side is a known quantity. Since $\strain$ is affine in $\eta$, the same is true of ${\stress^e}$ according to, so that solving reduces to finding the roots of a second-degree polynomial. The solutions $\eta$ of the piloting equation are thus determined (0, 1 or 2 in number).
