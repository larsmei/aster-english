# u2.07.03 Carrying out a structural-modification study from measured data

**Summary:**

The purpose of this document is the description of the setting up of a structural-modification study from measured data. The method used is based on the substructuring technique. The first substructure (initial structure) is obtained from the eigenmodes identified experimentally and the second substructure (modification introduced) is modelled numerically by finite elements.

## 1 Context

The detection of too high a vibration level on a given piece of equipment often leads to a stoppage of the use of the equipment in question, in order to avoid its failure. Thus, the putting in place of a specific solution to limit this phenomenon must be provided quickly in order to allow the installation to operate properly.

Two strategies may be envisaged. If the problem is generic, the development of a predictive model updated from the measured information is the most reliable solution, but the updating is a tedious step, and does not make it possible to adapt the analysis to the specificity of each structure. If the problem is specific to a particular piece of equipment, one wishes to be able, without the model-updating step, to predict the effects of a modification on the modal characteristics of the structure. The technique presented here falls within this second strategy.

In the majority of cases, one tries to shift the natural frequencies of the structure so that they do not lie in the excitation frequency band of the equipment concerned. The solution commonly used consists in adding a mass or a stiffness at a place in the structure chosen beforehand. The method for estimating the results of a structural modification described in this document was proposed by M. Corus within the framework of his thesis work [1]. It uses jointly the results of experimental measurements carried out on the initial structure and a numerical model of the modification.

One presents the chaining of the different operators. The optimisation of the location and the mechanical characterisation of the modification are left to the user. Two ways of proceeding are presented in this document: in section 3, the method initially developed reproduces exactly the method proposed by M. Corus in his thesis: the structure is described on a modal basis, in generalised coordinates, and the modification on a physical basis. This technique is used in the test cases sdll137a and sdll137b. In section 4, the structure and the modification are described by their modal basis. This technique, simpler to set up, was developed subsequently for carrying out a modification study on piping. This technique is used in the test case sdll137e, on the same example; another major advantage of the proposed implementation is that the numerical models of the structure and of the modification do not need to have coincident nodes for the assembly. For information, the test cases sdll137c and sdll137d are identical to versions a and b, but use the macro-command CALC_ESSAI for carrying out the analysis.

## 2 Theoretical reminder

This structural-modification technique is based on the substructuring method. The first substructure corresponds to the initial structure and the second substructure corresponds to the modification introduced.

The initial structure is modelled from its eigenmodes identified experimentally. Except in a very particular case, the measurement points are not located at the interface between the initial structure and the modification to be introduced. Before carrying out the coupling between these two substructures, it is therefore necessary to go through an intermediate step which consists in condensing the measured information at the interfaces.

The different models which come into play during this analysis are: the "measurement" model, the "support" model and the "modification" model. We shall describe these different models below.

To simplify the presentation, we adopt the following notation:

    - the index $\mathit{mes}$ relates to the measurement points,
    - the index $int$ relates to the interface nodes,
    - the exponent "+" indicates the generalised inverse of a matrix,
    - $L$: localisation matrix (observability matrix),
    - $y$: displacement field,
    - $\Phi$: matrix made up of the expansion-basis vectors defined on the support model,
    - ${\eta}_{\sup}$: generalised coordinates associated with the expansion basis $\Phi$
    - $\psi$: identified modal matrix (eigenmode shapes) on the initial structure,
    - ${\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}$: generalised coordinates associated with $\psi$

### 2.1 The "measurement" model

We call "measurement" model the modal model of the initial structure obtained from the measured information. On this model is defined a mesh made up of the measurement points.

A measured displacement field can be projected onto a basis composed of the identified eigenmodes, namely:

> ${y}_{\mathrm{mes}}=\psi {\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}$ (1)

One restricts oneself to the case of lightly damped structures (for which the damping can be modelled mode by mode), for which the modal model of the initial structure is written:

$\left\lbrace -{\omega}^{2}[\mathrm{Id}]+j\omega [{\beta}_{\mathrm{test}}]+[{\Omega}_{\mathrm{test}}^{2}]\right\rbrace {\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}={f}_{{\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}}$ (2)

Where:

| $[\mathrm{Id}]$ | <string>:60: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | identity matrix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $[{\Omega}_{\mathrm{test}}^{2}]$ | <string>:60: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | spectral matrix (diagonal matrix whose diagonal terms are the natural angular frequencies squared) |
| $[{\beta}_{\mathrm{test}}]$ | <string>:60: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | matrix of the generalised dampings |
| ${f}_{{\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}}$ | <string>:60: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | modal loading |
| $\omega$ | <string>:60: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | excitation angular frequency |

### 2.2 The "support" model

We call "support" model the simplified numerical model of the initial structure.

This model must include the interfaces between the initial structure and the modification. It is used for the expansion and the condensation of the measured information at the interfaces.

The expansion consists in finding a field defined on this support model, whose restriction to the measurement points is as close as possible to the field measured experimentally. This field is obtained by minimising the distance between the measured information and the information estimated on the support model.

The estimated displacement field ${\tilde{y}}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ at the measurement points is given by the following relation:

${\tilde{y}}_{\mathrm{mes}}={L}_{\mathrm{mes}}\Phi {\eta}_{\sup}$ (3)

The estimated field ${\tilde{y}}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ is of the same type as the measured field ${y}_{\mathrm{mes}}$.

The generalised coordinates ${\eta}_{\sup}$, unknown, are obtained by minimising the distance between the measured field and the estimated field. By using the least-squares technique, one obtains:

${\eta}_{\sup}={({L}_{\mathrm{mes}}\Phi )}^{+}{y}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ (4)

One can thus estimate the field $\tilde{y}$ defined at the nodes of the support model, consistent with the field measured experimentally.

$\tilde{y}=\Phi {\eta}_{\sup}=\Phi {({L}_{\mathrm{mes}}\Phi )}^{+}{y}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ (5)

The restriction of this field to the interface degrees of freedom is:

${\tilde{y}}_{int}={L}_{int}\tilde{y}={L}_{int}\Phi {({L}_{\mathrm{mes}}\Phi )}^{+}{y}_{\mathrm{mes}}={T}_{\mathrm{It}}{y}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ (6)

${T}_{\mathrm{it}}$ can be defined as being the passage matrix between the measurement points and the interface between the initial structure and the modification.

By replacing the measured field ${y}_{\mathrm{mes}}$ by its expression (1), one obtains:

${\tilde{y}}_{int}={T}_{\mathrm{It}}{y}_{int}={T}_{\mathrm{It}}\Psi {\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}$ (7)

That is:

${\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}={({T}_{\mathrm{It}}\Psi )}^{+}{\tilde{y}}_{int}$ (8)

If one writes: $B={({T}_{\mathrm{It}}\Psi )}^{+}$, one obtains:

${\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}=B{\tilde{y}}_{int}$ (9)

By multiplying equation (2) on the left by ${B}^{T}$, and by replacing ${\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}$ by its expression (9), the dynamic stiffness ${\hat{Z}}_{\mathrm{test}}$ of the initial structure is given by the following relation:

$\left\lbrace -{\omega}^{2}{B}^{T}B+j\omega {B}^{T}[{\beta}_{\mathrm{test}}]B+{B}^{T}[{\Omega}_{\mathrm{test}}^{2}]B\right\rbrace {\tilde{y}}_{int}={\hat{Z}}_{\mathrm{test}}{\tilde{y}}_{int}={f}_{int}^{I}$ (10)

With: ${f}_{int}^{I}$: force applied at the interface

### 2.3 The "modification" model

We call "modification" model the numerical model of the modification introduced into the initial structure.

If one denotes ${Z}_{x}$ its dynamic stiffness, by partitioning the unknowns into internal degrees of freedom ${y}_{i}$ and external degrees of freedom ${y}_{e}$ (which are composed of interface degrees of freedom), the model of the modification can be rewritten in the following way:

${Z}_{x}q=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{Z}_{ii}& {Z}_{\mathrm{ie}}\\ {Z}_{\mathrm{ei}}& {Z}_{\mathrm{ee}}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{y}_{i}\\ {y}_{e}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{f}_{i}^{M}\\ {f}_{e}^{M}+{f}_{\mathrm{eext}}^{M}\end{array}\right]$ (11)

The index $i$ relates to the internal degrees of freedom, the index $e$ relates to the external degrees of freedom and ${f}_{\mathrm{eext}}^{M}$ denotes the external force applied to the external degrees of freedom.

### 2.4 Coupling of the two substructures

The dynamic stiffness of the coupled model is obtained by considering the continuity of the displacements at the interfaces:

${\tilde{y}}_{int}={y}_{e}$ (12)

and the equilibrium of the forces at the interfaces:

${f}_{int}^{I}+{f}_{e}^{M}=0$ (13)

That is: $\left[\begin{array}{cc}{Z}_{ii}& {Z}_{\mathrm{ie}}\\ {Z}_{\mathrm{ei}}& {Z}_{\mathrm{ee}}+{\hat{Z}}_{\mathrm{test}}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{y}_{i}\\ {y}_{e}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{f}_{i}^{M}\\ {f}_{\mathrm{eext}}^{M}\end{array}\right]$ (14)

The solution of this system makes it possible to compute the behaviour of the modified structure.

Note: in section 4, the compatibility equations at the interface are written on a modal basis, that is to say that equations (12) and (13) are not satisfied exactly: they are projected onto the subspace made up of the basis of the extended modes of the structure:

${\Phi}^{T}{\tilde{y}}_{int}={\Phi}^{T}{y}_{e}$ (15)

${\Phi}^{T}{f}_{int}^{I}+{\Phi}^{T}{f}_{e}^{M}=0$ (16)

Since there are in general more interface degrees of freedom than eigenmodes used, relations (15) and (16) cannot be ensured exactly.

### 2.5 Back-projection of the field onto the measurement points

One can evaluate the field at the points where the measurement was carried out, by back-projection of the field at the interfaces obtained on the coupled model.

By exploiting relations (1), (9) and (12), the field at the measurement points is written:

${y}_{\mathrm{mes}}=\Psi {\eta}_{\mathrm{test}}=\Psi B{\tilde{y}}_{int}=\Psi B{y}_{e}$ (17)

### 2.6 Checking the quality of the expansion basis

The quality of the results depends of course on the uncertainty in the measurement, but also on the capacity of the expansion basis to represent the behaviour of the real structure and the transmission of the information at the interface.

The physical sense of the user comes into the choice of this expansion basis. It is difficult to know a priori the size of the expansion basis making it possible to obtain the best estimate of the coupled behaviour. The quality of the expansion basis can be measured by re-estimating the field at the interface by static expansion on the initial model from the field coming from the coupled model.

This indicator does not make it possible to estimate directly the quality of the results obtained on the coupled model, but it indicates the relevance of the reconstruction of the field at the interface.

## 3 Setting up the structural-modification analysis, example of the test cases sdll137a and sdll137b

The input data of the analysis are:

- the eigenmodes (frequencies, mode shapes, generalised masses) identified on the initial structure,
- the simplified finite-element model of the initial structure ("support" model),
- the finite-element model of the proposed modification ("modification" model).

In this section, the method described reproduces exactly that proposed by M. Corus in his thesis.

To illustrate the presentation, we take as an example the test case sdll137 [V2.02.137](doc:v2.02.137). We present only the main steps of the modelling.

### 3.1 The "measurement" model

The "measurement" model makes it possible to define the location of the measurement points. The nodes of the mesh can be linked together by discrete elements, for example, in order to facilitate the visualisation of the measured information. The mechanical characteristics which one assigns to this model do not influence the results; they allow the model to accommodate the identified eigenmodes.

This is expressed at the Aster level in the following way:

Reading of the sensor mesh (measurement points):
```text
MAILEXP=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=22);

MODLEXP=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILEXP,

AFFE=_F(TOUT='OUI',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='DIS_TR',),);
```

Reading of the identified eigenmodes:

```text
MODMESU=LIRE_RESU(TYPE_RESU='MODE_MECA',

FORMAT='IDEAS',

MODELE=MODLEXP,

UNITE=21,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

MATR_RIGI =KASSEXP,

MATR_MASS =MASSEXP,

FORMAT_IDEAS=_F(NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

NUME_DATASET=55,

RECORD_6=(1,2,3,8,2,6,),

POSI_ORDRE=(7,4,),

POSI_NUME_MODE=(7,4),

POSI_FREQ=(8,1,),

POSI_MASS_GENE=(8,2),

POSI_AMOR_GENE=(8,3),

NOM_CMP=('DX','DY','DZ',

'DRX','DRY','DRZ'),),

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',);
```

One can possibly carry out a selection on the identified modes in order to keep only the eigenmodes most worthwhile to exploit.

```text
MODEIDE=EXTR_MODE(FILTRE_MODE=_F(MODE=MODMESU,

NUME_MODE=(1,2,3,4,5,),),);
```
### 3.2 The "support" model

The "support" model is used for the expansion of the measurement onto the interface degrees of freedom. It must contain the interface nodes between the initial structure and the modification. On this model is defined an expansion basis which makes it possible to pass from the sensor degrees of freedom to the interface degrees of freedom. This expansion basis can be composed of eigenmodes, or of static responses, or of eigenmodes of the support model condensed onto the interface degrees of freedom and the sensor degrees of freedom[1]. The quality of the results depends on the ability of this basis to reproduce the real behaviour of the structure. The geometrical and mechanical characteristics assigned to this model are not necessarily the exact (updated) mechanical characteristics of the real structure, but they must be as close as possible to the physical reality. Indeed, the closer the model is to reality, the better the results of the expansion will be.

Reading of the mesh of the "support" model:

```text
MAILSUP=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=20);

MODLSUP=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILSUP,

AFFE=(_F(GROUP_MA=('POUTRE','VISUAL',),

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='POU_D_E',),),)
```

Definition of the expansion-basis vectors:

```text
MODESUP=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=KASSUP,

MATR_MASS=MASSUP,

OPTION='PLUS_PETITE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=20,

SEUIL_FREQ=1.E-4,),

VERI_MODE=_F(SEUIL=1.E-05,

STOP_ERREUR='OUI',),

);

MODSTSUP=MODE_STATIQUE(MATR_RIGI=KASSUP,

FORCE_NODALE=(_F(GROUP_NO='CAPTEUR',

AVEC_CMP=('DY','DZ',),),),);

BASEMO=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=(_F(MODE_MECA=MODESUP,NMAX_MODE=0,),

_F(MODE_STAT=MODSTSUP,NMAX_MODE=8,),),

NUME_REF=NUMSUP,);
```

Expansion of the measured information onto the support model:

```text
PROJ=PROJ_MESU_MODAL(MODELE_CALCUL=_F(MODELE=MODLSUP,

BASE=BASEMO,),

MODELE_MESURE=_F(MODELE=MODLEXP,

MESURE=MODEIDE,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',),

RESOLUTION=_F(METHODE='SVD',

EPS=1.E-5),);
```

Condensation of the measurement onto the interface degrees of freedom (here the interface degrees of freedom are chosen as being the "external" degrees of freedom of the macro-element):

```text
SSEXP = MACR_ELEM_STAT(DEFINITION=_F(MODELE=MODLSUP,

PROJ_MESU=PROJ,

MODE_MESURE=MODEIDE,

CARA_ELEM =CHCARSUP,

CHAM_MATER=CHMATSUP,),

EXTERIEUR=_F(GROUP_NO = ('EXTERNE',),),

RIGI_MECA=_F(),

MASS_MECA=_F(),);
```

Creation of the «super mesh cell» comprising the interface nodes:

```text
MAILCOND=DEFI_MAILLAGE(DEFI_SUPER_MAILLE=_F(MACR_ELEM_STAT = SSEXP,

SUPER_MAILLE='SUMAIL',),

DEFI_NOEUD=_F( TOUT = 'OUI', INDEX = (1,0,1,8,),),);
```
### 3.3 Coupling of the measurement and of the modification

Before carrying out the coupling of the substructures, one must make sure that the meshes of the different substructures are consistent (meshes expressed in the same coordinate system, …).

The coupling consists in assembling the two models (initial structure condensed at the interface nodes, and modification) into a single model. The principle is identical to that of substructuring.

Reading of the mesh of the modification:

```text
MAILX=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=24);
```

Assembly of the two meshes (super mesh cell + modification):

```text
MAILCPL=ASSE_MAILLAGE(MAILLAGE_1=MAILCOND,

MAILLAGE_2=MAILX,

OPERATION='SOUS_STR',);

MODLCPL=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILCPL,

AFFE=(_F(GROUP_MA=('POUTRE',),

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='POU_D_E',),),

AFFE_SOUS_STRUC=_F(SUPER_MAILLE = 'SUMAIL',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',),);
```
### 3.4 Analysis on the coupled model

After creation and assembly of the elementary matrices (here, the assembled matrices are KASCPL and MASCPL), one can carry out a modal analysis on the coupled model in order to evaluate the effect of the modification on the first natural frequencies of the modified structure.

```text
MODECPL=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=KASCPL,

MATR_MASS=MASCPL,

OPTION='PLUS_PETITE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=2,

SEUIL_FREQ=1.E-4,),

VERI_MODE=_F(SEUIL=1.E-05,

STOP_ERREUR='OUI',),

);
```
### 3.5 Back-projection onto the measurement model

This is a post-processing which consists in going back up to the initial measurement points. This makes it possible to appreciate the effect of the modification introduced with respect to the initial measurement.

```text
MODERETR=DEPL_INTERNE(DEPL_GLOBAL=MODECPL,SUPER_MAILLE='SUMAIL')
```
### 3.6 Checking the quality of the expansion basis

It is always of interest to check the quality of the expansion of the measurement. One can check it by using an indicator which reports two different estimates of the field at the interface of the coupled model.

It consists in comparing the field at the interface by static expansion on the support model from the field coming from the coupled model, and the field at the interface of the coupled model.

In order to be able to make this comparison, one can create a fictitious model containing only the interface. The idea consists in extracting the fields at the interfaces in order to be able to compare them by carrying out a MAC (Modal Assurance Criterion) computation.

Reading of the mesh of the interface:

```text
MAILINT=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=26);

MODLINT=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILINT,

AFFE=_F(GROUP_MA='VISUAL',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='DIS_TR',),);
```

Computation of the static response on the support model:

```text
MODSTINT=MODE_STATIQUE(MATR_RIGI=KASSUP,

FORCE_NODALE=(_F(GROUP_NO='CAPTEUR',

AVEC_CMP=('DY','DZ',),),),);
```

Definition of the basis for the static expansion:

```text
BASEINT=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=(_F(MODE_MECA=MODESUP,NMAX_MODE=0,),

_F(MODE_STAT=MODSTINT,NMAX_MODE=4,),),

NUME_REF=NUMSUP,);
```

Static expansion of the field at the measurement points obtained during the solution at the global level:

```text
PROJMS=PROJ_MESU_MODAL(MODELE_CALCUL=_F(MODELE=MODLSUP,

BASE=BASEINT,),

MODELE_MESURE=_F(MODELE=MODLEXP,

MESURE=MODERETR,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',),

RESOLUTION=_F(METHODE='SVD',

EPS=1.E-5),);

DEPLPR=REST_GENE_PHYS(RESU_GENE=PROJMS,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

NOM_CHAM='DEPL');
```

Field at the interfaces obtained by static expansion:

```text
DEPLINT=PROJ_CHAMP(METHODE='ELEM',

RESULTAT=DEPLPR,

MODELE_1=MODLSUP,

MODELE_2=MODLINT,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

NUME_DDL=NUMINT,);
```

Field at the interfaces obtained during the solution at the global level:

```text
DEPLXINT=PROJ_CHAMP(METHODE='ELEM',

RESULTAT=MODECPL,

MODELE_1=MODLCPL,

MODELE_2=MODLINT,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

NUME_DDL=NUMINT,);
```

The expansion basis is acceptable if the two fields defined at the interfaces are very close. The distance between the two fields can be measured by computing the scalar product between these two vectors by a MAC (Modal Assurance Criterion) computation.

```text
MACINT=MAC_MODES(BASE_1=DEPLINT,

BASE_2=DEPLXINT,

INFO =2,);
```
This MAC matrix must be as close as possible to the identity matrix. A MAC matrix very different from the identity matrix indicates that the expansion basis chosen does not make it possible to reconstruct the information at the interfaces well. In this case, it is advisable to choose another basis for the expansion of the measurement.

One can also evaluate the distance between the two vectors by computing the energy criterion IERI (Interface Regularity Energy Indicator). This criterion brings in the mass matrix or else the stiffness matrix of the structure.

```text
IERIK = MAC_MODES(BASE_1=DEPLINT,

BASE_2=DEPLXINT,

MATR_ASSE=KASMAC,

IERI=’OUI’,

INFO =2,);

IERIM = MAC_MODES(BASE_1=DEPLINT,

BASE_2=DEPLXINT,

MATR_ASSE=MASMAC,

IERI=’OUI’,

INFO =2,);
```

## 4 Setting up the structural-modification analysis, example of the test case sdll137e

The method presented here differs slightly from that proposed in the preceding section. Here, the two structures are described by their modal basis, whereas in section 3 the modification is described by its physical coordinates (finite-element model). The setting up of this method is much simpler, because it relies largely on the substructuring techniques described in the following documentation:

- reference documentation on substructuring R4.06.02
- user's documentation for setting up a dynamic substructuring analysis: U2.07.05.

Another (essential) advantage of this methodology is that the numerical models of the structure and of the modification do not need to have coincident nodes. The assembly of the two substructures is done by using the mesh-projection operators at the interface (techniques identical to what is done for the option LIAISON_MAIL of AFFE_CHAR_MECA and in PROJ_CHAMP).

One does not present here the details of the setting up of an analysis by substructuring, but one presents the specific features of this test case, adapted to the case where the model of the base structure is not updated.

### 4.1 Experimental model of the structure

One creates an experimental model on the mesh on which the measurement was carried out, and by importing the basis of modes identified via a modal-identification software. The reading of the modes is done with LIRE_RESU. The detail of the method is given in section 3, and in the document U2.07.06.

MODMtmp=LIRE_RESU(TYPE_RESU='MODE_MECA',

FORMAT='IDEAS',

MODELE=MODLEXP,

UNITE=21,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

FORMAT_IDEAS=_F(NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

NUME_DATASET=55,

RECORD_6=(1,2,3,8,2,6,),

POSI_ORDRE=(7,4,),

POSI_NUME_MODE=(7,4,),

POSI_MASS_GENE=(8,2,),

POSI_AMOR_GENE=(8,3,),

POSI_FREQ=(8,1,),

NOM_CMP=('DX','DY','DZ','DRX','DRY','DRZ',),),

MATR_A=KASSEXP,

MATR_B=MASSEXP,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',);

### 4.2 Support model

The support model is a finite-element model of the same structure, not updated. This means, for example, that its natural frequencies do not correspond at all to the natural frequencies of the real structure.

MAILSUP=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=20,);

MAILSUP=DEFI_GROUP(reuse=MAILSUP,MAILLAGE=MAILSUP,

CREA_GROUP_NO=_F(NOM='CAPT2',

DIFFE=('CAPTEUR','EXTERNE'),),);

MODLSUP=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILSUP,

AFFE=_F(GROUP_MA=('SIMPLE','VISUAL',),

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='POU_D_E',),);

MATSUP=DEFI_MATERIAU(ELAS=_F(E=2.1E11,

NU=0.3,

RHO=7800.,),);

MATDBL=DEFI_MATERIAU(ELAS=_F(E=4.2E11,

NU=0.3,

RHO=15600.,),);

# 'SIMPLE' : group that does not carry the modification. 'VISUAL' : group

# supporting the modification

CHMATSUP=AFFE_MATERIAU(MAILLAGE=MAILSUP,

MODELE=MODLSUP,

AFFE=(_F(GROUP_MA='SIMPLE',

MATER=MATSUP,),

_F(GROUP_MA='VISUAL',

MATER=MATDBL,),),);

CHCARSUP=AFFE_CARA_ELEM(MODELE=MODLSUP,

POUTRE=_F(GROUP_MA='POUTRE',

SECTION='RECTANGLE',

CARA=('HY','HZ',),

VALE=(9E-3,38E-3,),),

ORIENTATION=_F(GROUP_MA='POUTRE',

CARA='VECT_Y',

VALE=(0.,0.,1.,),),);

CONDLSUP=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=MODLSUP,

DDL_IMPO=(_F(GROUP_NO='FIXE',

LIAISON='ENCASTRE')));

ASSEMBLAGE(MODELE=MODLSUP,

CHAM_MATER=CHMATSUP,

CARA_ELEM=CHCARSUP,

CHARGE=CONDLSUP,

NUME_DDL=CO('NUMSUP'),

MATR_ASSE=(_F(MATRICE=CO('KASSUP'),

OPTION='RIGI_MECA',),

_F(MATRICE=CO('MASSUP'),

OPTION='MASS_MECA',),),);

BAMO=MODE_STATIQUE(MATR_RIGI=KASSUP,

MATR_MASS=MASSUP,

FORCE_NODALE=(_F(GROUP_NO=('CAPT2','EXTERNE'),

AVEC_CMP=('DY','DZ',),),),);

NB: in the method proposed above, one computes the static responses corresponding to the degrees of freedom of the sensors. Provided that the mesh is sufficiently fine, there always exist nodes of the support mesh «close» to the position of the sensors. IF this is not the case, a method is proposed in the test case sdlll137e, using an external python routine (called calc_modestat_capteurs), to compute the static responses corresponding rigorously to the position of the sensors. This method «looks for» the nodes close to the sensor, and computes the corresponding static response by a weighted sum of the responses on these nodes.

### 4.3 Expansion of the experimental modes

The experimental modes identified on the structure and imported onto the model in section 4.1 are interpolated onto the support model via the expansion methods presented in section 2. The expansion basis is composed of the static responses computed previously (the basis BAMO), re-orthonormalised via the following operations:

PROJ_BASE( BASE=BAMO,

STOCKAGE='PLEIN',

MATR_ASSE_GENE=(_F(MATRICE=CO('KPROJ'),

MATR_ASSE=KASSUP,),

_F(MATRICE=CO('MPROJ'),

MATR_ASSE=MASSUP,),),);

nbmod_expans = 5

MODGENE=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=KPROJ,

MATR_MASS=MPROJ,

> OPTION='PLUS_PETITE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=nbmod_expans,),

VERI_MODE=_F(STOP_ERREUR='NON'),

);

MODREPHY=REST_GENE_PHYS(RESU_GENE=MODGENE,NOM_CHAM='DEPL')

The modes of MODREPHY generate the same subspace as BAMO, but its components are classified by increasing natural frequencies. By choosing the parameter nbmod_expans well, one can control the regularity of the extended-mode basis generated. The choice of this parameter is decisive for the quality of the analysis, and several methods are proposed, notably in the thesis of M. Corus, for choosing the best compromise.

The expansion is done in the following two steps:

# extended modal basis for the structure

tmp=PROJ_MESU_MODAL(MODELE_CALCUL=_F(MODELE=MODLSUP,

BASE=MODREPHY,),

MODELE_MESURE=_F(MODELE=MODLEXP,

MESURE=MODMESU,),);

# extended modal basis for the structure

MODEET=REST_GENE_PHYS(RESU_GENE=tmp)

### 4.4 Creation of the macro-element associated with the structure

The extended-mode basis in the following commands is BAMOSTRU. The macro-element is built by giving to MACR_ELEM_DYNA:

- this basis of mode shapes,
- the modal characteristics identified on the measurement model (called here freq, amor and mass)

INTSTRU=DEFI_INTERF_DYNA(NUME_DDL=NUMSUP,

INTERFACE=_F(NOM = 'INTSTRU',

TYPE = 'MNEAL',

GROUP_NO = 'EXTERNE'))

BAMOSTRU=DEFI_BASE_MODALE(RITZ=_F( MODE_MECA = MODEET),

NUME_REF=NUMSUP,

INTERF_DYNA=INTSTRU)

freq = MODMESU.LIST_PARA()['FREQ']

amor = MODMESU.LIST_PARA()['AMOR_REDUIT']

mass = MODMESU.LIST_PARA()['MASS_GENE']

MACELSTR=MACR_ELEM_DYNA( BASE_MODALE=BAMOSTRU,

MODELE_MESURE=_F(FREQ=freq,

MASS_GENE=mass,

AMOR_REDUIT=amor,));

### 4.5 Modification model

The model of the modification is entirely numerical (in general much simpler; the updating step is not necessary). In the test case sdll137e, one proposes to create the macro-element which describes it simply with the macro-command CREA_ELEM_SSD.

MACELMOD = CREA_ELEM_SSD(

MODELE = MODMODI,

CHARGE = CONDMODI,

CHAM_MATER = CHMAMODI,

CARA_ELEM = CARAMODI,

INTERFACE = _F( NOM = 'INTMODI',

TYPE = 'CRAIGB',

GROUP_NO = 'EXTERNE'),

BASE_MODALE = _F( TYPE = 'RITZ',

TYPE_MODE = 'STATIQUE',

),

CALC_FREQ = _F(OPTION='SANS'),);

### 4.6 Assembly of the substructures, and computation of the natural frequencies of the modified structure

The assembly is likewise done simply by using the macro-command ASSE_ELEM_SSD.

ASSE_ELEM_SSD(RESU_ASSE_SSD = _F(

MODELE = CO('MODEGE'),

NUME_DDL_GENE = CO('NUMEGE'),

RIGI_GENE = CO('RIGGEN'),

MASS_GENE = CO('MASGEN'),

),

SOUS_STRUC = (_F(NOM = 'MODIF',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACELMOD,),

_F(NOM = 'STRUCT',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACELSTR,),),

LIAISON=_F( SOUS_STRUC_2='STRUCT',

INTERFACE_2='INTSTRU',

SOUS_STRUC_1='MODIF',

INTERFACE_1='INTMODI',

GROUP_MA_MAIT_2='toto',

OPTION='REDUIT'),

VERIF = _F( STOP_ERREUR = 'OUI',

PRECISION = 1.E-6,

CRITERE = 'RELATIF'),

METHODE = 'ELIMINE',

),

Note: the use of the keyword GROUP_MA_MAIT_2 makes it possible to declare the direction of the master–slave relation for the projections of nodes from one substructure onto the other. It is not necessary to specify the mesh-cell group used, because, with the option REDUIT, the code uses by default the group which was declared in the definition of the interface in DEFI_INTERF_DYNA.

Computation of the natural frequencies of the assembled structure:

RESGEN=CALC_MODES( MATR_RIGI=RIGGEN,

MATR_MASS=MASGEN,

OPTION = 'BANDE',

CALC_FREQ=_F( FREQ = (1.0, 1000.0),),

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F( DIM_SOUS_ESPACE=10,),

VERI_MODE=_F( STOP_ERREUR='NON',

STURM='NON',),

SOLVEUR=_F( METHODE='MUMPS'),

);

FREQ=RECU_TABLE(CO=RESGEN,NOM_PARA='FREQ')

It should be noted that here, unlike the other test cases, no method of checking the quality of the analysis is proposed by back-projection onto the sensors of the structure. One could envisage adding this procedure. The results of the test case are essentially equal to those obtained for sdll137a and b.

## 5 Conclusion

The structural-modification analysis procedure presented here is based on the joint exploitation of the measured data and of the numerical model of the modification. It uses the principle of substructuring for the coupling of the two models.

This technique must not be used as a black box. The user must intervene in the choice of the expansion basis so that it is as relevant as possible. The user must also find the modification best suited to the constraint imposed by the environment in which the initial structure is located.

One can never insist enough on the importance of carrying out the experimental measurement. Indeed, the uncertainties in the experimental data are propagated throughout the analysis and can lead to a significant error in the final results. It is strongly recommended that the person who carries out the measurement be the same as the one who sets up the numerical modelling. That person is the best able to exploit the measured data, and the best placed to carry out the coupling between the measurement and the analysis.
