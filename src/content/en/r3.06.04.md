# r3.06.04 Fourier elements for axisymmetric structures

**Summary**

Fourier elements are intended to compute the response of a structure of axisymmetric geometry subjected to non-axisymmetric loadings decomposed into Fourier series.

This document sets out a general theory of Fourier analysis with coupling of the symmetric and antisymmetric modes in the anisotropic case. The case of isotropic materials, or of materials orthotropic about the axis $\mathit{Oz}$, in which the modes are uncoupled, is studied separately.

Fourier elements can be used in *Code_Aster* from the modelling AXIS_FOURIER. The support mesh cells of these elements are triangles and quadrilaterals of degree 1 and 2.

## 1 Introduction

Fourier analysis is intended to compute the response of structures of axisymmetric geometries subjected to non-axisymmetric loadings. In this case, it is necessary to expand the loadings in Fourier series. Convergence is generally reached for 4 or 5 harmonics, but the rapidity of this convergence depends on the nature of the loading: the more regular the loading, the more rapidly the corresponding series converges. The most unfavourable case is that of a concentrated force, for which practice shows that one must go further (at least 7 harmonics).

In *Code_Aster*, the decomposition of the loading into Fourier series is assumed to have been carried out beforehand by the user. *Code_Aster* makes it possible to compute the responses to this loading, harmonic by harmonic (modelling AXIS_FOURIER), and globally after recombination of the harmonics with one another (operator COMB_FOURIER).

A first chapter will set out the general framework of anisotropy, with emphasis on the uncoupling of the modes in the orthotropic case. The second chapter explains the calculation of the stiffness matrix in the isotropic case.

For the use of Fourier elements in *Code_Aster*, the reader is referred to the user's guide of the Fourier modelling [U2.07.01](doc:u2.07.01).

## 2 Anisotropic Fourier analysis

### 2.1 General theory

All the fields considered (forces, displacements, strains, stresses) are expressed in cylindrical coordinates with the following convention on the order of the components:

| 1 | radial component along $r$ |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | axial component along $z$ |
| 3 | tangential component along $\theta$ |

Example: $({u}_{r},{u}_{z},{u}_{\theta}),({f}_{r},{f}_{z},{f}_{\theta})$

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.04/10000ACC000069BB00007BB781EFBDEDAC3DE5CC.svg)

The mesh is located in the plane $(r,z)$, the rotational symmetry being about the axis $\mathrm{Oz}$. The triad $(r,z,\theta )$ is oriented in the right-handed sense.

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.04/10000B14000069D500005A0565C67827BF00BDBF.svg)

The displacement $\mathrm{u}$ (or the loading $f$) is decomposed according to $\mathrm{u}={\mathrm{u}}^{s}+{\mathrm{u}}^{a}$ where ${\mathrm{u}}^{s}$ (resp. ${u}^{a}$) denotes the symmetric part (resp. antisymmetric part) of the Fourier-series expansion of $u$ with respect to the variable $\theta$.

One obtains:

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{}{u}_{r}^{s}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{u}_{l}^{s}(r,z)\cosl\theta \\ {u}_{z}^{s}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{v}_{l}^{s}(r,z)\cosl\theta \\ {u}_{\theta}^{s}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{w}_{l}^{s}(r,z)(-\sinl\theta )\end{array}\rbrace \text{partie symétrique}{u}^{s}\\ \begin{array}{}{u}_{r}^{a}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{u}_{l}^{a}(r,z)\sinl\theta \\ {u}_{z}^{a}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{v}_{l}^{a}(r,z)\sinl\theta \\ {u}_{\theta}^{a}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}{w}_{l}^{a}(r,z)\cosl\theta \end{array}\rbrace \text{partie antisymétrique}{u}^{a}\end{array}$

Note the choice of the sign $–$ for ${u}_{\theta}^{s}$, which makes it possible to simplify the subsequent calculations. If one denotes by ${U}_{l}^{s}=({u}_{l}^{s},{v}_{l}^{s},{w}_{l}^{s})\text{}(\text{resp}.{U}_{l}^{a})$ the $l-\text{ième}$ symmetric component (resp. antisymmetric component) of the Fourier-series expansion of $u$, one obtains:

$u=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[(\begin{array}{ccc}\cosl\theta & & 0\\ & \cosl\theta & \\ 0& & -\sinl\theta \end{array}){U}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{ccc}\sinl\theta & & 0\\ & \sinl\theta & \\ 0& & \cosl\theta \end{array}){U}_{l}^{a}\right]$         eq 2.1-1

If one denotes by $\varepsilon$ the linearised strain vector, one sees that $\varepsilon$ may be decomposed into a Fourier series as follows:

$\varepsilon =\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}(\begin{array}{cc}\cosl\theta {I}_{4}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& -\sinl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{cc}\sinl\theta {I}_{4}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& \cosl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array}){\theta}_{l}^{a}$             eq 2.1-2

with $\varepsilon =\left\lbrace {\varepsilon}_{r},{\varepsilon}_{z},{\varepsilon}_{q},{\gamma}_{\text{rz}},{\gamma}_{\mathrm{rq}},{\gamma}_{\mathrm{zq}}\right\rbrace$

${\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}={B}_{l}^{s}{U}_{l}^{s}\text{}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}={B}_{l}^{a}{U}_{l}^{a}$

with (see [bib1]):

${B}_{l}^{s}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{\partial}{\partial z}& 0\\ \frac{1}{r}& 0& -\frac{l}{r}\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial z}& \frac{\partial}{\partial r}& 0\\ \frac{l}{r}& 0& \frac{\partial}{\partial r}-\frac{1}{r}\\ 0& \frac{l}{r}& \frac{\partial}{\partial z}\end{array}\right]$

One has ${B}_{l}^{a}={B}_{l}^{s}\text{}\forall l$ (this is due to the choice of the symmetric expansion of $u$ as $(\cos,\cos,–\sin)$ instead of $(\cos,\cos,\sin)$). From now on the indices $a$ and $s$ will be omitted, and ${B}_{l}$ will denote the operator used to compute the strains corresponding to the harmonic $l$.

### 2.2 Coupling and uncoupling of the symmetric and antisymmetric modes

Taking up the preceding notation, one has:

$u=\sum_{l}(\begin{array}{cc}\cosl\theta {I}_{2}& {0}_{2,1}\\ {0}_{1,2}& -\sinl\theta \end{array}){u}_{l}^{s}+\sum_{l}(\begin{array}{cc}\sinl\theta {I}_{2}& {0}_{2,1}\\ {0}_{1,2}& \cosl\theta \end{array}){u}_{l}^{a}$

which is written, introducing the matrices ${M}_{l}^{s}\text{et}{M}_{l}^{a}$:

$\begin{array}{}u=\sum_{l}({M}_{l}^{s}{U}_{l}^{s}+{M}_{l}^{a}{U}_{l}^{a})\\ {u}_{l}={M}_{l}^{s}{U}_{l}^{s}+{M}_{l}^{a}{U}_{l}^{a}\end{array}$

One deduces that: ${\varepsilon}_{l}=M{'}_{l}^{s}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+M{'}_{l}^{a}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}$

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{cc}\text{avec}& M{'}_{l}^{s}=(\begin{array}{cc}\cosl\theta {I}_{4}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& -\sinl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array})\\ & M{'}_{l}^{a}=(\begin{array}{cc}\sinl\theta {I}_{4}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& \cosl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array})\end{array}\\ \end{array}$

**Calculation of the strain energy**

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{cc}{W}_{l}& =\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{s}{\int}{}^{t}{\varepsilon}_{l}D{\varepsilon}_{l}\mathrm{ds}d\theta \text{avec}\mathrm{ds}=\text{rdrdz}\\ & =\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}d\theta \underset{s}{\int}{}^{t}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s\text{}t}M{'}_{l}^{s}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{s}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}\text{ds}+\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}d\theta \underset{s}{\int}{}^{t}{\theta}_{l}^{a\text{}t}M{'}_{l}^{a}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{a}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}\text{ds}\\ & +\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}d\theta \underset{s}{\int}{}^{t}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a\text{}t}M{'}_{l}^{a}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{s}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}\text{ds}+\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}d\theta \underset{s}{\int}{}^{t}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s\text{}t}M{'}_{l}^{s}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{a}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}\text{ds}\end{array}\\ \text{Puisque}M{'}_{l}^{a}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{s}=(\begin{array}{cc}\sinl\theta {I}_{4}& 0\\ 0& \cosl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{1}& {D}_{3}\\ {}^{t}\text{}{D}_{3}& {D}_{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{cc}\cosl\theta {I}_{4}& 0\\ 0& -\sinl\theta {I}_{2}\end{array})\\ \text{}M{'}_{l}^{a}\text{DM}{'}_{l}^{s}=(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{1}\sinl\theta \cosl\theta & -{D}_{3}{(\sinl\theta )}^{2}\\ {}^{t}\text{}{D}_{3}{(\cosl\theta )}^{2}& -{D}_{2}\sinl\theta \cosl\theta \end{array})\end{array}$

and that $\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\sinl\theta \cosl\theta d\theta =0,\text{si}{D}_{3}=0$ there is therefore no term $({}^{t}\text{}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a},{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s})\text{ou}({}^{t}\text{}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s},{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a})$ in $W$. There is then no coupling $({U}^{a},{U}^{s})\text{ou}({U}^{s},{U}^{a})$.

### 2.3 Calculation of the stresses

In the same way as $\varepsilon ,\sigma$ may be decomposed into Fourier series as follows:

$\sigma =\sum_{l}(M{'}_{l}^{s}{\sigma}_{l}^{s}+M{'}_{l}^{a}{\sigma}_{l}^{a})$

From Hooke's law $\sigma =D\varepsilon$, one deduces:

$s=\sum_{l}(\begin{array}{cc}\cosl\theta {D}_{1}& -\sinl\theta {D}_{3}\\ \cosl\theta {D}_{3}^{t}& -\sinl\theta {D}_{2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{cc}\sinl\theta {D}_{1}& \cosl\theta {D}_{3}\\ \sinl\theta {D}_{3}^{t}& \cosl\theta {D}_{2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}$

That is, bringing out the matrices $M{'}_{l}^{s}\text{et}M{'}_{l}^{a}$:

$\begin{array}{cc}\sigma =& \sum_{l}M{'}_{l}^{s}\left[(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{1}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& {D}_{2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{cc}{0}_{4,4}& {D}_{3}\\ -{D}_{3}^{t}& {0}_{2,2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}\right]\\ & +M{'}_{l}^{a}\left[(\begin{array}{cc}{0}_{4,4}& -{D}_{3}\\ {D}_{3}^{t}& {0}_{2,2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{1}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& {D}_{2}\end{array}){\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}\right]\end{array}$

Setting ${D}^{s}(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{1}& {0}_{4,2}\\ {0}_{2,4}& {D}_{2}\end{array})$ and ${D}^{a}=(\begin{array}{cc}{0}_{4,4}& {D}_{3}\\ -{D}_{3}^{t}& {0}_{2,2}\end{array})$, one deduces the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the stress relating to the harmonic $l$:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{l}^{s}={D}^{s}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+{D}^{a}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}={D}^{s}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{s}+{D}^{a}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{a}\\ {\sigma}_{l}^{a}=-{D}^{a}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}+{D}^{s}{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}=-{D}^{a}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{s}+{D}^{s}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{a}\end{array}$                     eq 2.3-1

**Note:**

*In the case of orthotropy with respect to* $\mathrm{Oz}$*, one has* ${D}^{a}=0$ and [eq 2.1-1] reduces to:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{l}^{s}={D}^{s}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{s}\\ {\sigma}_{l}^{a}={D}^{s}{B}_{l}{u}_{l}^{a}\end{array}$

*That is, if the displacements are symmetric (or antisymmetric), the stresses are too.*

## 3 Calculation of the stiffness matrix

### 3.1 General case

Let $\mathrm{u}$ and $\varepsilon$ be two arbitrary kinematically admissible fields. Applying the principle of virtual work to the volume element $v$, one may write:

$\underset{v}{\int}({}^{t}\text{}\delta \varepsilon .\mathrm{s})\text{dv}=\underset{v}{\int}({}^{t}\text{}\delta \mathrm{u}.f)\text{dv}$

After decomposition into a Fourier series and integration with respect to $\theta$, one obtains, for arbitrary kinematically admissible fields ${\varepsilon}_{l}^{s},{\varepsilon}_{l}^{a},{u}_{l}^{s},{u}_{l}^{a}$ and for every harmonic $l$:

$\underset{{s}_{l}}{\int}({}^{t}\text{}\delta {\varepsilon}_{l}^{s}.{\sigma}_{l}^{s}+{}^{t}\text{}\delta {\varepsilon}_{l}^{a}.{\sigma}_{l}^{a}){\text{ds}}_{l}=\underset{{s}_{l}}{\int}({}^{t}\text{}\delta {u}_{l}^{s}.{f}_{l}^{s}+{}^{t}\text{}\delta {u}_{l}^{a}.{f}_{l}^{a}){\text{ds}}_{l}$

That is, using [eq 2.3-1] and setting:

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{l}^{s}=\underset{{s}_{l}}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}{D}^{s}{B}_{l}{\text{ds}}_{l}\\ {K}_{l}^{a}=\underset{{s}_{l}}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}{D}^{s}{B}_{l}{\text{ds}}_{l}={K}_{l}^{s}={K}_{l}\\ {K}_{l}^{\text{as}}=\underset{{s}_{l}}{\int}{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}{D}^{a}{B}_{l}{\text{ds}}_{l}\end{array}$

The following system of equations is obtained:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{l}{u}_{l}^{s}+{K}_{l}^{\text{as}}{u}_{l}^{a}={f}_{l}^{s}\\ {}^{t}\text{}{K}_{l}^{\text{as}}{u}_{l}^{s}+{K}_{l}{u}_{l}^{a}={f}_{l}^{a}\end{array}$                                     eq 3-1

where ${}^{t}\text{}{K}_{l}^{\text{as}}=-{K}_{l}^{\text{as}}$ one sees that if ${D}_{a}\ne 0$, uncoupling of the modes into symmetric and antisymmetric harmonics is no longer possible. On the other hand, if ${D}_{a}=0$ (orthotropy with respect to $\mathrm{Oz}$) then ${K}_{l}^{\text{as}}=0$ and [eq 3-1] reduces to:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{l}{u}_{l}^{s}={f}_{l}^{s}\\ {K}_{l}{u}_{l}^{a}={f}_{l}^{a}\end{array}$

Taking as displacement vectors (resp. force vectors) corresponding to the harmonic $l$ the vectors:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{l}={\left\lbrace {u}_{r}^{s},{u}_{z}^{s},{u}_{\theta}^{s},{u}_{r}^{a},{u}_{z}^{a},{u}_{\theta}^{a}\right\rbrace }_{l}\\ {f}_{l}={\left\lbrace {f}_{r}^{s},{f}_{z}^{s},{f}_{\theta}^{s},{f}_{r}^{a},{f}_{z}^{a},{f}_{\theta}^{a}\right\rbrace }_{l}\end{array}$

One then has:

${K}_{l}^{g}{u}_{l}={f}_{l}\text{avec}{K}_{l}^{g}=(\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{l}& {K}_{l}^{\text{as}}\\ {}^{t}\text{}{K}_{l}^{\text{as}}& {K}_{l}\end{array})$

### 3.2 Calculation of ${K}_{l}^{g}$ in the isotropic case

In this case one therefore has ${K}_{l}^{\text{as}}=0$. The calculation of ${K}_{l}={}_{{s}_{l}}\text{}\int{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}{D}^{s}{B}_{l}{\text{ds}}_{l}$ is detailed below.

In the isotropic case, one has:

$D={D}^{s}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\begin{array}{cccc}\mathrm{D1}& \mathrm{D2}& \mathrm{D2}& 0\\ \mathrm{D2}& \mathrm{D1}& \mathrm{D2}& 0\\ \mathrm{D2}& \mathrm{D2}& \mathrm{D1}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{D3}\end{array}& 0\\ 0& \begin{array}{cc}\mathrm{D3}& 0\\ 0& \mathrm{D3}\end{array}\end{array}\right]$

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{avec}& \mathrm{D1}=\frac{E(1-\nu  )}{(1+\nu  )(1-2\nu  )}\\ & \mathrm{D2}=\frac{E\nu  }{(1+\nu  )(1-2u)}\\ & \mathrm{D3}=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu  )}\end{array}$

One may write:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{r}\\ {\varepsilon}_{z}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta}\\ {\gamma}_{\text{rz}}\\ {\gamma}_{r\theta }\\ {\gamma}_{z\theta }\end{array}\right\rbrace ={B}_{l}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}{u}_{r}^{s}\\ {u}_{z}^{s}\\ {u}_{\theta}^{s}\end{array}\right\rbrace ={B}_{l}^{'}{}^{t}\text{}\left\lbrace \frac{{u}_{r}}{r},\frac{{u}_{z}}{r},\frac{{u}_{\theta}}{r},\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r},\frac{\partial {u}_{z}}{\partial r},\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r},\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial z},\frac{\partial {u}_{z}}{\partial z},\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial z}\right\rbrace$

> ← shape functions → ← derivatives of the shape functions →

$\text{avec}B{'}_{l}=\left[\begin{array}{ccccccccc}0& 0& 0\text{}& 1& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 1& 0\\ 1& 0& -l\text{}& 0& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 1& 0\text{}& 1& 0& 0\\ l& 0& -1\text{}& 0& 0& 1\text{}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& l& 0\text{}& 0& 0& 0\text{}& 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]$

Denoting by ${\left\lbrace {W}_{J}\right\rbrace }_{J=1\text{à n}}$ the shape functions of the element under consideration, one has:

$U=\left[\begin{array}{}\frac{{u}_{r}}{r}\\ \frac{{u}_{z}}{r}\\ \frac{{u}_{\theta}}{r}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{z}}{\partial r}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial z}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{z}}{\partial z}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial z}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}\cdots & \frac{{W}_{J}}{r}& \stackrel{\text{noeud}J}{\stackrel{}{0}}& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& \frac{{W}_{J}}{r}& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& 0& \frac{{W}_{J}}{r}& \cdots \\ \cdots & \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial r}& 0& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial r}& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& 0& \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial r}& \cdots \\ \cdots & \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial z}& 0& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial z}& 0& \cdots \\ \cdots & 0& \underset{\text{bloc}{P}_{J}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{0}}& \frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial z}& \cdots \end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\cdot \\ \cdot \\ \cdot \\ {u}_{r}(J)\\ {u}_{z}(J)\\ {u}_{\theta}(J)\\ \cdot \\ \cdot \\ \cdot \end{array}\right\rbrace$

One denotes $(P)=({P}_{1},\dots ,{P}_{N})$ where $N$ is the number of nodes of the element.

Then ${K}_{l}={\int}_{{s}_{l}}{}^{t}\text{}P{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}^{'}{\mathrm{DB}}_{l}^{'}P{\text{ds}}_{l}$

${K}_{l}$ is symmetric and formed of blocks ${({K}_{l})}^{I,J}3\times 3$:

${({K}_{l})}^{I,J}={\int}_{{s}_{l}}{}^{t}\text{}{P}_{I}{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}^{'}{\mathrm{DB}}_{l}^{'}{P}_{J}{\text{ds}}_{l}$

The calculation of the blocks ${({K}_{l})}^{I,J}$ is set out below:

$\begin{array}{}{\text{tB}}_{l}^{'}{\mathrm{DB}}_{l}^{'}=\left[\begin{array}{ccccccccc}\mathrm{D1}+{1}^{2}\mathrm{D3}& 0& -l(\mathrm{D1}+\mathrm{D3})& \mathrm{D2}& 0& \text{lD}3& 0& \mathrm{D2}& 0\\ \mathrm{D2}& 0& -\text{lD}2& \mathrm{D2}& 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{D1}& 0\\ 0& \text{lD}3& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{D3}\end{array}\right]\\ {}^{t}\text{}{P}_{I}{}^{t}\text{}{B}_{l}^{'}{\mathrm{DB}}_{l}^{'}{P}_{J}=({K}_{ij}^{l})\begin{array}{c}I,J\\ 3\times 3\end{array}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{11}^{I,J}& {K}_{12}^{I,J}& {K}_{13}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{21}^{I,J}& {K}_{22}^{I,J}& {K}_{23}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{31}^{I,J}& {K}_{32}^{I,J}& {K}_{33}^{I,J}\end{array}\right]\text{avec}\\ \lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{11}^{I,J}=(\frac{\mathrm{D1}+{l}^{2}\mathrm{D3}}{{r}^{2}}){W}_{I}{W}_{J}+\mathrm{D1}\frac{\partial {W}_{I}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial r}+\mathrm{D3}\frac{\partial {W}_{I}}{\partial r}\\ {K}_{23}^{I,J}=-\frac{l}{r}\mathrm{D2}\frac{\partial {W}_{I}}{\partial z}{W}_{J}+\frac{l}{r}{\mathrm{D3W}}_{I}\frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial z}\\ {K}_{32}^{I,J}=-\frac{l}{r}{\mathrm{D2W}}_{I}\frac{\partial {W}_{J}}{\partial z}+\frac{l}{r}\mathrm{D3}\frac{\partial {W}_{I}}{\partial z}{W}_{J}\end{array}\end{array}$

The blocks ${K}^{I,J}$ are not symmetric except for $I=J$ (on the diagonal of $K$). One notes in fact that the blocks ${K}^{I,J}$ may be written for every harmonic ($l=0$ included).

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{11}^{I,J}={\mathrm{K0}}_{11}^{I,J}+{l}^{2}\frac{\mathrm{D3}}{{r}^{2}}{W}_{I}{W}_{J}\\ {K}_{22}^{I,J}={\mathrm{K0}}_{22}^{I,J}+{l}^{2}\frac{\mathrm{D3}}{{r}^{2}}{W}_{I}{W}_{J}\\ {K}_{33}^{I,J}={\mathrm{K0}}_{33}^{I,J}+{l}^{2}\frac{\mathrm{D1}}{{r}^{2}}{W}_{I}{W}_{J}\\ {K}_{12}^{I,J}={\mathrm{K0}}_{12}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{21}^{I,J}={\mathrm{K0}}_{21}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{13}^{I,J}=-{\mathrm{lK0}}_{13}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{31}^{I,J}=-{\mathrm{lK0}}_{31}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{23}^{I,J}=-{\mathrm{lK0}}_{23}^{I,J}\\ {K}_{32}^{I,J}=-{\mathrm{lK0}}_{32}^{I,J}\end{array}$

where the blocks ${\mathrm{K0}}^{I,J}$ are independent of the harmonic $l$.

## 4 Loadings

It is assumed that the loading has been decomposed on the same basis as the displacements, namely:

$\mathrm{f}=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\left[(\begin{array}{ccc}\cosl\theta & & 0\\ & \cosl\theta & \\ 0& & -\sinl\theta \end{array}){\mathrm{F}}_{l}^{s}+(\begin{array}{ccc}\sinl\theta & & 0\\ & \sinl\theta & \\ 0& & \cosl\theta \end{array}){\mathrm{F}}_{l}^{a}\right]$

There is no coupling, for a given harmonic, between the symmetric and antisymmetric parts of the loading, because of the orthogonality of the trigonometric functions $\sinl\theta$ and $\cosl\theta$, and this for all types of loading. This means in particular that the equivalent nodal forces are the same for the symmetric and antisymmetric harmonics if the amplitudes ${F}_{l}^{s}\text{et}{F}_{l}^{a}$ are the same.

For the nature of the loadings admissible with the Fourier modelling, the reader is referred to the user's guide [U2.01.07](doc:u2.01.07).

## 5 Conclusion and outlook

At present, it is assumed that the decomposition of the loading has been carried out beforehand by the user, that is that ${\left\lbrace {F}_{l}^{s},{F}_{l}^{a}\right\rbrace }_{l\ge 0}$ is known. This decomposition could be carried out by an operator of *Code_Aster* which would project the loading onto the Fourier modes.

For the moment, only the non-anisotropic case is implemented, that is there is never any coupling of the modes. The extension to anisotropy may constitute a later development.
