# r3.08.02 Modelling of cables

**Summary:**

Cables are flexible structures which can undergo large displacements. Their analysis is therefore non-linear. From the mechanical point of view, a cable can support no moment and is the seat only of a normal force called tension. The expression of the virtual work and its differentiation with respect to the displacements lead to the finite-element modelling: a stiffness matrix depending on the displacement of the nodes and a constant mass matrix. The static and dynamic iterative algorithms are presented. Two examples are given: one, static, is the search for the equilibrium shape of a cable subjected to a given horizontal tension; the other, dynamic, is the comparison between finite-element analyses and the results of short-circuit tests. Finally four annexes deal with: the calculation of the Laplace forces, the evolution of the temperature of a cable subjected to the Joule effect, the force exerted by the wind and the modelling of the stringing operation.

## 2 Introduction

The essential characteristic of cables is that they are **flexible structures** , whose mechanical analysis is **non-linear**  because they are liable to undergo **large displacements** . They undergo them during the **stringing** operation, when one adjusts sags in order to comply with environmental constraints.

Subsequently, the cables may be set in **large-amplitude motions**  under the impulse of the pressure of the **wind** , of the fall of **ice sleeves** , or, in the event of an incident, of the Laplace forces resulting from **short-circuit** currents. They then exert on their supports forces much higher than the static forces. This must be taken into account in the design of the structures.

For the old structures, which may be subjected to increased short-circuit intensities because of the extension of the network, it must be verified that reliability is still ensured.

## 3 Mechanical assumptions

Cables are considered as perfectly flexible wires, which can support no moment, neither bending nor torsional, and are the seat only of a normal tension. This tension plays the role of a generalised stress.

One wishes to calculate the displacement field $u({s}_{o},t)$ at the time instant $t$ with respect to the reference configuration. The latter is a static configuration of the cable subjected, for example, to gravity and to the temperature ${T}_{o}$ ; it is defined by the field of position vectors $x({s}_{o})$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.02/10000AAC000012B500000EFC635998EC46D1535F.svg)

Figure 1: Segment of cable in the reference and displaced configurations

As [bib1], one takes for the strain the Green measure of the relative elongation with respect to the reference configuration [Figure]:

. $g=\frac{{\text{ds}}^{2}-{\text{ds}}_{o}^{2}}{2{\text{ds}}_{o}^{2}}$

$g$ **must remain small** . The right-hand side lends itself to calculation because it contains only squares of elementary lengths. One sees in the [Figure] that:

> $g=\frac{\partial x}{\partial {s}_{o}}.\frac{\partial u}{\partial {s}_{o}}+\frac{1}{2}{(\frac{\partial u}{\partial {s}_{o}})}^{2}$     [1]

The constitutive relation is:

> $N={E}_{a}A\left[g-\alpha (T-{T}_{o})\right]$     [2]

with:

${E}_{a}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}E\text{si}N>0\\ {E}_{c}\text{si}N\le 0\end{array}$

## 4 Application of the Principle of Virtual Work

If one does not take damping into account, the virtual work of the set of forces applied to a segment of cable during the virtual displacement $\delta u$ is:

> $W(u,\delta u)={W}_{int}(u,\delta u)-{W}_{\text{iner}}(\ddot{u},\delta u)-{W}_{\text{ext}}(u,\delta u)$     [3]

distinguishing the works of the internal forces, of the inertia forces and of the external forces. According to [eq]:

> ${W}_{int}(u,\delta u)={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}(N.\delta g)\mathrm{ds}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}\left\lbrace N.\left[\frac{\partial (x+u)}{\partial {s}_{o}}.\frac{\partial \delta u}{\partial {s}_{o}}\right]\right\rbrace \mathrm{ds}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}(N.B\delta u)\text{ds}$     [4]

where:

> $B={\left\lbrace \left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {s}_{o}}1\right](x+u)\right\rbrace }^{T}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {s}_{o}}1\right]$     [5]

denoting by the upper index $T$ the transpose of a matrix.

> ${W}_{\text{iner}}(\ddot{u},\delta u)=-{\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}(\rho A\ddot{u}.\delta u)\mathrm{ds}$     [6]

In all cases, we consider the work ${W}_{\text{ext}}$ as independent of $u$ during a time step, because:

- either it really is, in the case of conservative forces such as gravity;
- or, in the case of the Laplace forces, the force applied to a cable element depends not only on the displacement of that element (classical follower force), but also on the displacements of the set of cables. One then considers that, during a time step, the force is constant and equal to its value at the end of the preceding time step.

## 5 Linearisation

At equilibrium:

> $W(u,\delta u)=0$     [7]

If, to within a tolerance, the preceding equation is not satisfied, one seeks a correction $\Delta u$ of $u$ such that:

$W(u,\delta u)+\text{DW}(u,\delta u).\Delta u=0$

$\text{DW}(u,\delta u).\Delta u$ being the directional derivative of $\text{W}(u,\delta u)$ in the direction $\Delta u$ [bib] and [bib]. According to [eq], one obviously has:

$\text{DN}.\Delta u={E}_{a}A\mathrm{Dg}.\Delta u={E}_{a}AB.\Delta u$

According to [eq]:

$DB.\Delta u={(\left[\frac{d}{{\text{ds}}_{o}}1\right]\Delta u)}^{T}\left[\frac{d}{{\text{ds}}_{o}}1\right]$ .

Therefore:

> $\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{DW}}_{int}(u,\delta u).\Delta u={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}\left\lbrace {(B\delta u)}^{T}{E}_{a}AB\Delta u\right\rbrace \text{ds}\\ +{\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}\left\lbrace {(\left[\frac{d}{{\text{ds}}_{o}}1\right]\delta u)}^{T}N\left[\frac{d}{{\text{ds}}_{o}}1\right]\Delta u\right\rbrace \text{ds}\end{array}$     [8]

According to [eq]:

> ${\mathrm{DW}}_{\text{iner}}(\ddot{u},\delta u).\Delta u=-{\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}(\delta {u}^{T}\rho A\Delta \ddot{u})\text{ds}$     [9]

## 6 Numerical implementation by finite elements

One denotes by the lower index $h$ the matrices discretised into finite elements. If $v$ is a vector defined on the cable (position, displacement, acceleration, …) one has at the current point of a finite element of nodes $i,j,\dots$ :

$v=\left[L\right]{v}_{e}$

${v}_{e}$ being the vector made up of the components of $v$ at the nodes. Likewise:

${\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {s}_{o}}1\right]}_{h}v=\left[L'\right]{v}_{e}.$

According to [eq]:

${B}_{h}={(x+u)}_{e}^{T}L{'}^{T}.L'$

The internal forces ${F}_{int}^{e}$ of a structural finite element $e$ are the forces which must be exerted at its nodes in order to hold it in its current deformed configuration. According to the theorem of virtual work for continuous media, the work of these point forces is equal to the work of the stresses in the element, that is to ${W}_{int}$ , for every virtual displacement field. One therefore has, according to [eq]:

${F}_{int}^{e}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}N{B}_{h}^{T}\text{ds}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}NL{'}^{T}L'\mathrm{ds}{(x+u)}_{e}$ .

On the other hand, one replaces the inertia forces distributed in the element by point forces at the nodes ${F}_{\text{iner}}^{e}$ such that their work is equal to that of the real inertia forces for every virtual displacement field. According to [eq], one therefore has:

${F}_{\text{iner}}^{e}=-{\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}{L}^{T}\rho AL\mathrm{ds}.\ddot{{u}_{e}}$

Likewise, the distributed external forces are replaced by concentrated nodal forces ${F}_{\text{ext}}^{e}$ equivalent in the sense of virtual work. The differential of the virtual work of the internal forces of a cable finite element is written, according to [eq]:

, ${D}_{h}{W}_{int}(u,\delta u).\Delta u={(\delta {u}_{e})}^{T}({K}_{M}+{K}_{G})\Delta {u}_{e}$

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{K}_{M}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}{B}_{h}^{T}{E}_{a}A{B}_{h}\text{ds}\\ {K}_{G}={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}L{'}^{T}NL'\text{ds}\end{array}$

${K}_{M}$ and ${K}_{G}$ are called the material and geometric stiffness matrices of the element. $({K}_{M}+{K}_{G})\Delta {u}_{e}$ is the principal part of the variation $\Delta {F}_{int}$ of the internal forces at the nodes due to the displacement correction $\Delta {u}_{e}$ . The differential of the virtual work of the inertia forces is deduced from [eq]:

${D}_{h}{W}_{\text{iner}}(\ddot{u},\delta u).\Delta u=-{(\delta {u}_{e})}^{T}M\Delta {\ddot{u}}_{e}$

with:

$M={\int}_{{s}_{1}}^{{s}_{2}}{L}^{T}\rho AL\text{ds}$

$M$ is the mass matrix of the element. $-M\Delta {\ddot{u}}_{e}$ is the variation $\Delta {F}_{\text{iner}}$ of the inertia forces at the nodes due to the acceleration correction $\Delta {\ddot{u}}_{e}$ .

## 7 Particular case of the two-node cable elements

These elements are of the 1st degree: they are therefore straight in the reference position and remain straight in the deformed position.

No moment is applied at their ends and they are the seat only of a uniaxial stress. They are therefore **bar elements** .

In other words: modelling a cable by two-node elements amounts to assimilating it to a **chain**  whose **links**  (the cable elements) would be **perfectly articulated**  with one another.

By contrast, the cable elements having more than two nodes generally exhibit a curvature which varies with the strain. One therefore cannot treat them as bar elements.

## 9 Static problem

This problem is that of the search for the equilibrium of a structure of cables in an arbitrary position and subjected to a system of given forces.

### 9.1 Iterative algorithm

The equilibrium equation, discretised form of [eq] and [eq] without the inertia term, which must be satisfied at each node, is:

> ${F}_{int}={F}_{\text{ext}}$     [10]

Suppose that one has just calculated the displacement field of the cables, ${u}^{n}({s}_{o})$ , at iteration $n$ :

- if this field makes it possible to satisfy, to within a tolerance, [eq 9.1-1], one considers that the line:

$x({s}_{o})+{u}^{n}({s}_{o})$

is the equilibrium shape of the cables;

- otherwise, one calculates displacement corrections $\Delta {u}^{n+1}$ by the linearised system:

$\left[{K}_{M}^{n}+{K}_{G}^{n}\right]\Delta {u}^{n+1}={F}_{\text{ext}}-{F}_{int}^{n}$

One uses for this the quasi-static non-linear algorithm described in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01), and which corresponds to the command STAT_NON_LINE. The displacement at iteration $(n+1)$ is:

${u}^{n+1}={u}^{n}+\Delta {u}^{n+1}$

One checks whether [eq] is satisfied by the field ${u}^{n+1}$ and so on.

### 9.2 Example

One wishes to calculate the equilibrium shape of a heavy cable [fig 9.2-a] one end $A$ of which is fixed and the other, $B$ level with $A$ , is subjected to a given horizontal force.

This problem is treated in [bib], where it is considered as highly non-linear.

## 10 Dynamic problem

This problem is that of the calculation of the evolution of a structure of cables.

### 10.1 Iterative algorithm of time integration

The complete discretised form of [eq] and [eq], which must be satisfied at each node and at each time instant, is:

> ${F}_{int}(t)-{F}_{\text{iner}}(t)={F}_{\text{ext}}(t)$     [11]

The time-integration algorithm is of Newmark type [bib] and [bib]. Suppose that the state of the cable (fields $u,\dot{u}\mathrm{et}\ddot{u}$ at the nodes) is known at the time instant $t$ and that one has just calculated an approximate value of these fields at the nth iteration of the time instant $t+\Delta t$ .

- If these values satisfy [eq], to within a tolerance, one takes them as the values of the fields at the time instant $t+\Delta t$ .
- Otherwise, one seeks the displacement correction $\Delta {u}^{n+1}$ , to which correspond, according to the Newmark algorithm, the corrections of velocity and of acceleration:

$\Delta {\dot{u}}^{n+1}=\frac{\gamma}{\beta \Delta t}\Delta {u}^{n+1}$

and

$\Delta {\ddot{u}}^{n+1}=\frac{1}{\beta \Delta {t}^{2}}\Delta {u}^{n+1}$

such that:

$\left[{K}_{M}^{n}+{K}_{G}^{n}+\frac{1}{\beta \Delta {t}^{2}}M\right]\Delta {u}^{n+1}={F}_{\text{ext}}(t+\Delta t)-{F}_{int}^{n}(t+\Delta t)+{F}_{\text{iner}}^{n}(t+\Delta t)$

In the analysis of the motion of the cables, the Newmark algorithm may be unstable. This is why we use the so-called HHT algorithm, defined in [bib5, bib7], in which the two Newmark parameters are functions of a third parameter $\alpha$ :

$\begin{array}{c}\gamma =\frac{1}{2}-\alpha \\ \beta =\frac{{(1-\alpha )}^{2}}{4}\\ \alpha \le 0\end{array}$

For $\alpha =0$ , the algorithm is that of Newmark, called the "trapezoidal rule". But for $\alpha$ slightly negative, in practice: $\alpha \ge -0.3$ , numerical damping appears which stabilises the calculation.

The determination of the initial acceleration and the initialisation of the fields at the beginning of a new time step are indicated in [bib].

## 11 Conclusion

The modelling of the cables presented above is efficient (reasonable number of iterations per time step or in order to reach a static equilibrium) and accurate: it is suited to the analysis of long cables. For short cables, by contrast, the flexural stiffness is not negligible, especially at the anchorages, and the modelling must be done by beam elements in large displacements and large rotations [R5.03.40](doc:r5.03.40).

: Calculation of the Laplace forces between conductors
Every conductor carrying a current creates a magnetic field in its neighbourhood. This magnetic field, acting on the current conveyed by another conductor, induces on the latter a force called the Laplace force.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.02/1000000000000182000000E4E83A96A032DE10B1.png)

Figure 5: Arrangement of two neighbouring conductors

Take a conductor $(1)$ carrying the current ${i}_{1}(t)$ [Figure A1-a], located in the neighbourhood of the conductor $(2)$ carrying the current ${i}_{2}(t)$ . At the point P of the conductor $(1)$ , where the unit tangent oriented in the direction of the current is ${e}_{1}$ , the linear Laplace force induced by the conductor $(2)$ is:

$f(P)={10}^{-7}{i}_{1}(t){i}_{2}(t){e}_{1}\times {\int}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}^{}{e}_{2}\times \frac{r}{{r}^{3}}{\mathrm{ds}}_{2}$

One is interested only in the forces due to the very intense short-circuit currents, the Laplace forces in normal operation being negligible.

$f(P)$ can obviously be put in the form of the product of a function of time by a function of space.

**A1.1** **Time function of the Laplace forces**

This function $g(t)$ , is, to within a factor, the product of the intensities in the conductors $(1)$ and $(2)$ :

> $g(t)=2.{10}^{-7}{i}_{1}(t){i}_{2}(t)$     [12]

where:

> ${i}_{j}(t)=\sqrt{2}{I}_{\text{ej}}\left[\cos(\omega t+{\varphi}_{j})-{e}^{-t/\tau }\cos{\varphi}_{j}\right]$     [13]

with:

| ${I}_{\text{ej}}$ : | r.m.s. intensity of the current j; |
| --- | --- |
| $\omega$ : | angular frequency of the current ($\omega =100\pi$ for a current of 50 Hz); |
| ${\varphi}_{j}$ : | phase depending on the time instant at which the short circuit occurs; |
| $\tau$ : | time constant of the short-circuited line depending on its electrical characteristics (inductance, capacitance and resistance). |

Very often, one replaces the complete function $g(t)$ [eq] and [eq] by its mean — which one calls the continuous part — neglecting the terms $\cos(\omega t+\mathrm{...})$ and $\cos(2\omega t+\mathrm{...})$ . Taking these terms into account would require a very small time step and the corresponding forces, at $50\mathrm{Hz}$ and $\mathrm{100 }\mathrm{Hz}$ , have practically no effect on the cables whose oscillation frequency is of the order of one hertz.

Therefore:

${g}_{\text{continue}}(t)=2{I}_{\mathrm{e1}}{I}_{\mathrm{e2}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\cos({\varphi}_{1}-{\varphi}_{2})+{e}^{-(\frac{t}{{\tau}_{1}}+\frac{t}{{\tau}_{2}})}\cos{\varphi}_{1}\cos{\varphi}_{2}\right]$

**A1.2** **Space function**

This function is:

$h(P)=\frac{1}{2}{e}_{1}\times \int{e}_{2}\times \frac{r}{{r}^{3}}{\mathrm{ds}}_{2}$

The integral is calculated analytically when one divides the conductor ${\Gamma}_{2}$ into straight elements. Along such an element ${M}_{1}{M}_{2}$ [Figure A1.2-a], one has an effect:

$\begin{array}{}{r}^{3}={({y}^{2}+{r}_{m}^{2})}^{}\\ {e}_{2}\times r={e}_{2}\times {r}_{m}\\ {\mathrm{ds}}_{2}=\text{dy}\end{array}$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.02/10000000000001A10000012DA56BE206C07472A8.png)

Figure 6: Laplace force induced by a straight element of conductor

As:

${\int}_{{y}_{1}}^{{y}_{2}}\frac{\text{dy}}{{({y}^{2}+{r}_{m}^{2})}^{}}=\frac{1}{{r}_{m}^{2}}{\left[\frac{y}{{({y}^{2}+{r}_{m}^{2})}^{}}\right]}_{{y}_{1}}^{{y}_{2}}$

one has:

$\frac{1}{2}{e}_{1}\times {\int}_{{M}_{1}}^{{M}_{2}}{e}_{2}\times \frac{r}{{r}^{3}}\mathrm{dy}=\frac{1}{2{r}_{m}^{2}}{e}_{1}\times {e}_{2}\times {r}_{m}{\left[\frac{y}{{({y}^{2}+{r}_{m}^{2})}^{}}\right]}_{{y}_{1}}^{{y}_{2}}$

The bracket of the right-hand side is also equal to:

$\sin{\alpha}_{2}-\sin{\alpha}_{1}$ .

**A1.3** **Implementation in** *Code_Aster*

The space function $h(P)$ defined previously is calculated by an elementary routine which evaluates, for each of the elements of the conductor $(1)$ , the contribution of all the elements of the conductor $(2)$ which act on it.

This contribution is evaluated at the Gauss points (1 only for the elements with 2 nodes) of the element of the conductor, $(1)$ by the keyword INTE_ELEC of the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

The elementary routine has 2 input parameters:

- the loading map of the element of the conductor $(1)$ comprising the list of the mesh cells of the conductor $(2)$ acting on it;
- the name of the geometry, variable in the course of time, which makes it possible at each time instant to evaluate the quantities ${r}_{m},\sin{\alpha}_{1},\sin{\alpha}_{2}$ .

The time function $g(t)$ is calculated by the operator DEFI_FONC_ELEC which produces a concept of type fonction.

: Calculation of the force exerted by the wind
**A3.1** **Formulation**

One admits that a wind of velocity $V$ exerts in the neighbourhood of the point $P$ of a cable [Figure] a linear aerodynamic force $f$ having the following characteristics:

- $f$ has the direction and the sense of the component ${V}_{n}$ of the wind velocity in the normal plane of the cable.
- $f$ has a modulus proportional to the square of that of ${V}_{n}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.02/1000000000000146000000D92E924E1B9BD49B1B.png)

Figure 7: wind velocity in the neighbourhood of a cable

The regulations for the calculation of lines define the force of a wind by the pressure $p$ which it exerts on a plane surface normal to its direction. For a cable, placed normally to the direction of the wind, these regulations prescribe taking for the linear force: ${f}_{\frac{\pi}{2}}=p\mathrm{\varnothing }$ ,

$\varnothing$ being the diameter of the cable. This amounts to considering that the cable presents to the wind a plane surface equal to its projected frontal area. One thus obtains an overestimate of the force because the cable, being cylindrical, presents a lower resistance to the air than a plane surface.

If the velocity $V$ of the wind makes an angle $\theta$ with the cable, its component in the plane perpendicular to the cable has for modulus: $\parallel {V}_{n}\parallel =\parallel V\parallel .\mid \sin\theta \mid$ .

Therefore, the linear force is: ${f}_{\theta}=p\mathrm{\varnothing }{\sin}^{2}\theta$ .

Of course, the linear force exerted by the wind depends on the position of the cable: it is a "follower" force.

**A3.2** **Use in** *Code_Aster*

: Modelling of the stringing of the cables
A cable in the course of stringing in a section (several spans between poles) [Figure] is fixed to one of the anchor supports. It rests on pulleys placed at the bottom of the alignment insulators and it is retained by a force at the level of the second anchor support.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.02/10000000000001B200000162A1FA24FBAD8C93B8.png)

Figure 8: Stringing of a cable in a section with two spans

By acting on this force — or by moving its point of application — one adjusts the sag of one of the spans, the one which is the most subject to environmental constraints. Then one removes the pulleys and one fixes the cable to the insulators: the length of the cable in the various spans is then fixed. It is on this configuration that one possibly mounts additional components: the spacers, the downleads onto equipment, the point masses, … in order to give the section its definitive shape.

This modelling is described in the reference documentation of the CABLE_POULIE elements [R3.08.05](doc:r3.08.05).
