# v6.03.162 SSNP162 – 2D and 3D seals for JOINT_MECA_RUPT and JOINT_MECA_FROT laws

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the elements hydro-mechanical seals for constitutive laws dams JOINT_MECA_RUPT and JOINT_MECA_FROT.

PLAN_JOINT pure mechanical PLAN_JOINT in $\mathrm{2D}$ (mesh cell QUAD4,QUAD8) and 3D_JOINT in $\mathrm{3D}$ (mesh cell HEXA8, HEXA20 or PENTA6,PENTA15) are tested with both laws in the presence of pressure imposed. Then, for the same quadratic elements the modellings hydromechanical coupled PLAN_JOINT_HYME and C3D_JOINT_HYME, with the pressure profile obtained in the calculation.

The elements on which these modellings are based are the standard elements cohesive joints.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 General case

In this part, the analytical solution is detailed in pure $I$ mode in its $\mathrm{3D}$ form. For $\mathrm{2D}$ plane calculations, the solution is identical. The component of the jump and the stress vector following $\tau$ does not intervene, and it is enough to replace the $S$ surface with the $L$ length in the solution.

For loads in shear mode, elastic l-element does not interfere.

The joint is initially loaded in $I$ mode in a round-trip mode, which allows testing the profile of the displacement force curve at different times for this type of load. In the second part, the load is sequentially loaded in $I$ mode and then in $\mathit{II}$ mode (or $\mathit{III}$ according to the model). Then the load is changed in $I$ mode and the discharge in $\mathit{II}$ mode. The coupling between the two loading modes is requested (for example, the tangential slope is checked for normal loading). The displacement force curve profile for the tangential force is also tested.

### 2.2 In pure $I$ mode

The analytical solution of the system's overall response is presented in the local $(n,t,\tau )$ marker. A collinear load is applied to normal: $U=Un$ , the cohesive element opens in pure $I$ mode and the tangential stresses and tangential jumps remain zero. Therefore, one turns to a scalar problem. One notes $\sigma =n.\sigma .n$ the only non-zero component of the elastic element stress tensor in the local marker:

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.162/1000000000000211000001D61843A8DE8569DDEE.jpg)

*Return charge in $I$ mode JOINT_MECA_RUPT law*

- **JOINT_MECA_RUPT**

The solution of the global response of the joint+cube system is presented (see document [r7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)).

Equal constraints give:

$\left\{ \begin{array}{c}\sigma ={K}_{N}{\delta}_{n}\\ \sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{cube}}=E(U-{\delta}_{n})/{L}_{\mathit{cube}}\end{array}\right.$ in the elastic zone

$\left\{ \begin{array}{c}\sigma ={\sigma}_{max}-{K}_{N}/{P}_{\mathit{rup}}({\delta}_{n}-{\sigma}_{max}/{K}_{N})\\ \sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{cube}}=E(U-{\delta}_{n})/{L}_{\mathit{cube}}\end{array}\right.$ in the softening area

Where ${L}_{\mathrm{cube}}=1$ is the length of the cube edge in our case. We note the maximum opening of the joint up to the complete break by ${U}_{max}={\sigma}_{max}(1+{P}_{\mathit{rup}})/{K}_{N}$ and the maximum elastic opening by ${U}_{\mathit{el}}={\sigma}_{max}({K}_{N}{L}_{\mathit{cube}}+E)/{K}_{N}E$.

The solution is given by the linear function in pieces:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\sigma =-{K}_{N}E/({K}_{N}{L}_{\mathit{cube}}+E/{P}_{\mathit{cont}})U& \text{si}& U<0\\ \sigma ={K}_{N}E/({K}_{N}{L}_{\mathit{cube}}+E)U& \text{si}& U<{U}_{\mathit{el}}\\ \sigma ={K}_{N}E/(-{K}_{N}{L}_{\mathit{cube}}+E\mathrm{\ast }{P}_{\mathit{rup}})({U}_{max}-U)& \text{si}& U<{U}_{max}\\ \sigma =0& \text{si}& U>{U}_{max}\end{array}
$$

The tested force is therefore given by $F=\sigma S$ where $S$ corresponds to the surface on which the load is applied.

For hydromechanical modellings (modellings G, H, I) the force value is given by: $F=(\sigma -{p}_{\mathit{fluide}})S$ where ${p}_{\mathit{fluide}}$ is the fluid pressure that contributes to the joint opening.

- **JOINT_MECA_FROT**

The solution of the overall response of the more cube joint system is presented (see document [r7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)).

Equal constraints give:

$\left\{  \begin{array}{c}\sigma ={K}_{N}{\delta}_{n}\\ \sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{cube}}=E(U-{\delta}_{n})/{L}_{\mathit{cube}}\end{array}\right.$ in the elastic zone

$\left\{  \begin{array}{c}\sigma =c/\mu \\ \sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{cube}}=E(U-{\delta}_{n})/{L}_{\mathit{cube}}\end{array}\right.$ in the plastic area

Where ${L}_{\mathrm{cube}}=1$ is the length of the edge of the cube in our case. As the maximum stress in $I$ mode for Mohr-Coulomb law is given by ${\sigma}_{max}=c/\mu$, the maximum elastic opening (elastic traction threshold) is equal to ${U}_{\mathit{el}}={\sigma}_{max}({K}_{N}{L}_{\mathit{cube}}+E)/{K}_{N}E$. Up to this displacement value we check the value of $\sigma ={\sigma}_{max}=c/\mu$, then we impose double displacement is checked that the stress value is not evolving. Then in the compression field the solution is always elastic $\sigma ={K}_{N}{\delta}_{n}$.

The tested force is given by $F=\sigma S$ where $S$ corresponds to the surface on which the load is applied.

For hydromechanical modellings (modellings J, K, L) the force value is given by: $F=(\sigma -{p}_{\mathit{fluide}})S$ where ${p}_{\mathit{fluide}}$ is the fluid pressure that contributes to the joint opening.

### 2.3 In $\mathit{II}$ and $\mathit{III}$ pure mode

The system written in the local $(n,t,\tau )$ marker. A load perpendicular to normal is applied directly to the joint: $U={U}_{t}\tau$. L-element is a cohesive one that opens in $\mathit{II}$ mode. If the normal load does not change, it is a scalar problem. ${\sigma}_{n}=n.\sigma .n$ , ${\sigma}_{\tau}=\tau .\sigma .n$ are noted as the constraints in the local mark.

The $I$ mode load is maintained and the $\mathit{II}$ mode (or $\mathit{III}$ mode depending on the Modelling) is used. The solution varies according to constitutive law:

- **JOINT_MECA_RUPT**

When the joint is partially opened (${\delta}_{n}>0$ ) the tangential slope evolves according to this normal opening like: ${K}_{T}^{\mathit{evol}}={K}_{T}\times (1-{\delta}_{n}/{L}_{\mathit{CT}})$ (see document [r7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25))

where ${L}_{\mathit{CT}}={\sigma}_{max}(1+{P}_{\mathit{rup}})/{K}_{N}\tan(\alpha \pi /4)$ is the critical length of tangential damage. The solution is given by: ${\sigma}_{\tau}={K}_{T}^{\mathit{evol}}{U}_{t}$

- **JOINT_MECA_FROT**

The force-moving curve for the joint, which is normally loaded, is tested. The joint is partially closed (${\delta}_{n}>0$). The solution is the simple sliding of Mohr-Coulomb. ${\sigma}_{\tau}=-\mu {\sigma}_{n}+c$ .
This curve is tested at different times (see v6.03.162-fig-curve-force-deplacement):
