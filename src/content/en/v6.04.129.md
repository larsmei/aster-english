# v6.04.129 SSNV129 - Contact of 2 plates in single support, one of which is pressure-controlled

**Summary:**

A rectangular plate is subjected to a uniform pressure force and is compressed on an identical plate where it undergoes contact forces.

This test consists of two modellings (elements linear QUAD4 Modelling DKT - elements quadratic QUAD9 Modelling COQUE_3D). Reference calculations were performed without contact.

The contact *Code_Aster* results are obtained in non-regression and analyzed against the contactless results.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Reference Bibliographical

The reference solution is based on the results of ROARK-S's Formulas for Stress and Strain, which can also be found in the aide-memoire on the resistance of materials by Jean Goulet and Jean-Pierre Boutin, page 192, combined with W. D. Pilkey's Formulas for Stress, Strains and Structural Matrix, page 969.

### 2.2 Contactless reference analytical results

The results obtained were calculated on a single-supplement plate subjected to vertical pressure (no contact).

- Calculation of deflection at the centre of the plate:

$\maxz=-\frac{\alpha p{l}^{4}}{E{e}^{3}}$

- Calculation of the stress at the centre of the plate following the width of the lower skin plate:

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.129/Object_3.svg)

${\sigma}_{yy}=+\frac{\beta p{l}^{2}}{{e}^{2}}$

- $p$ is the pressure applied to the plate,
- $E$ le Young's modulus,
- $L$ , length,
- $l$ , width,
- $e$ , thickness,
- $\alpha ,\beta$ being two coefficients obtained from the $a/b$ ratio.

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}\alpha =0.1110\\ \beta =0.6102\end{array}$

Either:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}\maxz=-0.69375\mathrm{cm}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}=1.5255{10}^{10}N/{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}\end{array}$

## 8 Summary of the results

Very good results are seen in the presence of contact for both modellings.

The modellings A, D and E (DKT) have very satisfactory results compared to the analytical results (–1% of the error with or without contact). When a pressure plate is replaced with two plates, one of which is attached to the other, the multiplication of the rigidity by two results in a split by two of the maximum deflection and the stress at the centre of the plates.

Modelling B, C (COQUE_3D) gives a similar behavior to that encountered for modellings A, D and E in DKT when replacing a pressure plate with two contact plates. A double-difference of the maximum deflection and the stress at the centre of the plates is observed. Furthermore, the value of the deflection obtained is rather satisfactory (~7% d) compared to the analytical results. This difference can be explained by the consideration of transverse shear for COQUE_3D for a plate which is all in all relatively thick since $l/e=1/10$ .
