# v6.04.127 SSNV127 - Cylinder in a contact and friction bore

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a quasi-static analysis of a mechanical problem with contact and friction. A cylinder is compressed in a cylindrical bore of slightly larger diameter by a concentrated force applied to its axis.

This test, called "Klang problem", is widely used in the literature to validate friction contact modellings and has been used by P. Alart and A. Curnier [bib1] to validate their contact and friction finished elements.

Three modellings 2D are proposed (D and H with linear elements and F with quadratic elements):

The D Modelling test the algorithm of the "PENALISATION" method with master-slave pairing, the penalization is about contact and friction.

Modellings F and H test the algorithm of the CONTINUE method with elements quadratic (SEG3) and linear (SEG2) contact, respectively.

The results are compared with an analytical solution given by Klang [bib2].

Instead of reproducing this relatively complicated analytical solution we will use the values of pressures obtained from it [bib1].

Position of reference points on the contact surface: one point every three degrees d-angle from the bottom, up to 60°.

### 1.2 Material properties

**Cylinder and bore:**

| Young's modulus: | $E=2.1{10}^{11}N/{m}^{2}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio: | $\nu =0.3$ |  |
| Friction coefficient: | $\mu =0.4$ |  |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The massif containing the bore is supposed to be infinite, its movements will be blocked (following x and y) on a concentric circle with the bore.

The cylinder is subjected to a Q distributed force according to the $(z)$ thickness: $Q=–1875{10}^{3}N/m$ .

This force is applied in one increment.

## 4 Modelling F

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The symmetry of the problem allows only half ($X\ge 0$) to be modeled.

60 elements finished SEG3 are regularly arranged on the initial contact surface (3 degrees d) for each.

The cylinder and circular volume surrounding the bore are gridded with elements QUA8 and TRIA6.

## 6 Summary of the results

The results are of good quality if it is considered that they are compared to an analytical solution and that the meshes used are not particularly fine.

The best solution is given by F (continuous algorithm) Modelling, which uses quadratic elements with quadratic projection (without using this projection, the results are false). Next comes H Modelling, which, like the previous one, uses the continuous algorithm but with linear elements and with master slave pairing. Finally, D Modelling, which uses the penalization algorithm, gives acceptable results. It should be noted that convergence with this algorithm is much slower and difficult.

If the extent of the contact surface is examined using the CONT_NOEU data structure printing (see results file), it is found to be given very precisely (60°). With regard to the adhering surface, the algorithms refer an acceptable value of about 30° instead of 26.2° analytically.
