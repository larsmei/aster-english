# r3.08.07 MEMBRANE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements

**Summary:**

This document describes the formulation and the implementation in *Code_Aster* of the MEMBRANE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements. The MEMBRANE elements make it possible to model a linear behaviour of an arbitrary membrane or a non-linear behaviour of isotropic membranes. The GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements are finite elements more specifically intended for the representation of steel reinforcement in a solid mass (for civil-engineering applications of the reinforced-concrete type). The main characteristics of these elements are the following:

- membrane elements, with no torsional stiffness;
- no rotational degrees of freedom, but, in return, no possibility of offset;
- surface geometric support (triangle, quadrangle; linear or quadratic, and biquadratic only for the MEMBRANE elements);

The GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements make it possible to model a non-linear behaviour of the reinforcing bars.

## 1 Introduction

The MEMBRANE modelling makes it possible to represent the mechanical behaviour of a membrane that may be anisotropic in the linear regime and only isotropic in the non-linear regime. It makes it possible to model structural elements whose bending stiffness is negligible.

The GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements make it possible to represent the possibly non-linear behaviour of reinforcing bars in a reinforced-concrete structure. The main constraint is that the reinforcing bars must be periodically distributed over a surface, and all oriented in the same direction. Note, however, that crossed reinforcing bars can be modelled by superposing two GRILLE_MEMBRANE modellings (see below).

Elements of type GRILLE_MEMBRANE complement the possibilities for modelling reinforcement in *Code_Aster*, in addition to the GRILLE_EXCENTRE modelling. The differences between the GRILLE_MEMBRANE and GRILLE_EXCENTRE modellings are presented below.

Recall that the GRILLE_EXCENTRE modelling is based on a DKT shell kinematics with a single layer through the thickness [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03). This DKT foundation implies the presence of rotational degrees of freedom at the nodes of the GRILLE_EXCENTRE elements: although it allows the notion of offset, it is unnecessary when offset is not required (in that case it burdens the model unnecessarily, because not only do the rotational degrees of freedom lengthen the vector of unknowns, but a non-negligible number of these degrees of freedom must moreover be fixed by a double Lagrange multiplier). The GRILLE_MEMBRANE modelling is a modelling based on a “surface” kinematics; it requires no degrees of freedom other than the usual displacements (on the other hand, of course, this modelling does not allow the notion of offset to be used).

The GRILLE_EXCENTRE modelling, based on DKT, requires geometric support elements of linear triangle or quadrangle type; the GRILLE_MEMBRANE modelling is developed from surface geometric supports, triangle or quadrangle, linear or quadratic.

For both types of modelling, however, only one reinforcement direction is available per finite element. This makes it possible to model any type of reinforcement with several directions, by superposing one element per direction; the computational cost generated by these duplications is low: no duplication of the degrees of freedom (hence a constant matrix-inversion cost), duplication of the elementary calculations (but they remain simple, few in number in 3D — surface elements as against volume elements — and the elementary calculations for structures with a large number of degrees of freedom have a low cost compared with the inversion cost).

## 2 Linear formulation of the MEMBRANE elements

For a membrane element, the strain energy can be written in the form:

$$
\Phi =\frac{1}{2}\int\sigma :\epsilon \text{ds}
$$

with $\sigma$ la contrainte membranaire et $\epsilon$ the membrane strain.

The only difficulty is to obtain an expression of the type $\epsilon =\mathit{BU}$ where $U$ denotes the nodal values of the displacement.

For this, a little differential geometry must be used. Denoting by $a$ the natural basis (non-orthogonal; only the third vector, normal to the surface, is normalised) of the reinforcement plane and by $g$ the contravariant metric associated with this basis (cf. [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04).for more details). One starts from the expression of the contravariant derivative:

| :math:nabla u=frac{partial {u}^{i}}{partial {xi}_{j}}={u}^{i}{ | }_{j}{a}_{i}otimes {a}^{j}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

with $\left\lbrace {\xi}_{j}\right\rbrace$ an admissible parametrisation of the surface and ${a}_{i}=\frac{\partial x}{\partial {\xi}_{i}}$ . Using the metric tensor $g$ , one then has:

| :math:nabla u=frac{partial {u}^{i}}{partial {xi}_{j}}={u}^{i}{ | }_{j}{g}^{mathit{jk}}{a}_{i}otimes {a}_{k}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

The reinforcement direction is then defined by the unit vector ${e}_{1}$, which is completed, for ease of presentation, into an orthonormal basis $\left\lbrace {e}_{i}\right\rbrace$ . $R$ denotes the change-of-basis operator between this basis and the natural basis such that:

$$
{a}_{i}={R}_{i}^{p}{e}_{p}
$$

Greek indices denote the indices taking values only in $(1,2)$ , and one obtains:

$$
{\varepsilon}_{\alpha \beta }={(\nabla u)}_{\alpha \beta }=(\frac{\partial u}{\partial {\xi}^{j}}\cdot {a}^{i}){R}_{i}^{\alpha}{R}_{k}^{\beta}{g}^{\text{jk}}
$$

By definition of $R$ : ${R}_{3}^{1}=0$ and by definition of $g$ : ${g}^{13}={g}^{23}=0$ . One therefore obtains:

$$
{\varepsilon}_{\alpha \beta }={(\nabla u)}_{\alpha \beta }=(\frac{\partial u}{\partial {\xi}^{\delta}}\cdot {a}^{\gamma}){R}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}{R}_{\theta}^{\beta}{g}^{\delta \theta }
$$

If $\hat{B}$ now denotes the derivative of the shape functions at the Gauss point under consideration, one has:

$$
{\epsilon}_{\alpha \beta }={R}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}{R}_{\theta}^{\beta}{g}^{\delta \theta }{\widehat{B}}_{\delta ,n}{\left({a}^{\gamma}\right)}_{i}{U}_{i,n}
$$

With $n$ the node index. Hence the sought $B$:

$$
{B}_{i,n}={R}_{\gamma}^{\alpha}{R}_{\theta}^{\beta}{g}^{\delta \theta }{\widehat{B}}_{\delta ,n}{\left({a}^{\gamma}\right)}_{i}
$$

From $B$ , one then has all the classical expressions for the strain:

$$
\varepsilon =\mathit{BU}
$$

for the nodal forces:

$$
F=\int{B}^{T}\sigma
$$

and for the tangent matrix:

$$
K=\int{B}^{T}\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }B
$$

## 3 Non-linear formulation of the MEMBRANE elements

The whole of the theoretical part, as well as the development of the membrane finite element, is based on the work of Anh LE VAN, published in his book *Coques et membranes, Fondements de l’approche non linéaire*  [:ref:`1]  < [1]>` .

In this document, the following conventions will be observed, unless otherwise stated:

- Latin letters ∈ {1,2,3}
- Greek letters ∈ {1,2}
- the Einstein summation convention is used
- components referring to the initial configuration are written in upper case
- components referring to the deformed configuration are written in lower case
- tensors of order greater than or equal to 1 are written in bold
- a subscript 0 will be used to indicate that one is on the initial configuration
- the braces $\lbrace \mathrm{❑}\rbrace$ denote a column vector
- the brackets $⟨\mathrm{❑}⟩$ denote a row vector
- the square brackets $[\mathrm{❑}]$ denote a matrix with more than one row and more than one column

The study of membranes relies on many elements of differential geometry; the most important principles for the present study are therefore set out below. Differential geometry refers to the application of differential calculus to geometry; it arises in particular in problems involving the notion of curvature. It will be useful in taking account of the curved geometry of the membranes.

### 3.1 Differential geometry

One works in the three-dimensional Euclidean space Ԑ equipped with the usual scalar product $\left(\text{a},\text{b}\right)\mapsto \text{a}.\text{b}$ , the Euclidean norm $\Vert .\Vert$ and an orthonormal global frame $\text{ }(\text{O};{\text{e}}_{1},{\text{e}}_{2},{\text{e}}_{3})$ .

The first element to be made explicit is the local basis (or covariant natural basis); this basis is a priori neither orthogonal nor normalised. Let ${\text{S}}_{0}$ be a surface, ${\text{P}}_{0}$ a point belonging to this surface and $({\text{ξ}}^{1},{\text{ξ}}^{2})$ a coordinate system of this surface. The covariant natural basis ${(\text{A}}_{1},{\text{A}}_{2},{\text{A}}_{3})$ is defined by (denoting by $\text{}\times \text{}$ the vector product) :

$$
{\text{A}}_{\text{α}}=\frac{\partial {\text{P}}_{0}}{\partial {\text{ξ}}^{\text{α}}}
$$

One then writes, in the deformed configuration, denoting by $\text{S}$ the surface and by $\text{P}$ the position of the point ${\text{P}}_{0}$ after deformation :

$$
{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}=\frac{\partial \text{P}}{\partial {\text{ξ}}^{\text{α}}}
$$

The first fundamental form can now be defined :

$$
{\text{A}}_{\text{α}\text{β}}\equiv {\text{A}}_{\text{α}}{.\text{A}}_{\text{β}}
$$

the matrix can then be constructed :

$$
\left[\text{A}..\right]\equiv \left[\begin{array}{cc}{\text{A}}_{11}& {\text{A}}_{12}\\ {\text{A}}_{21}& {\text{A}}_{22}\end{array}\right]
$$

this matrix is symmetric and invertible (because ${\text{A}}_{1}$ and ${\text{A}}_{2}$ are linearly independent). Its inverse is denoted:

$$
\left[\text{A}\ddot{}\right]\equiv {\left[\text{A}..\right]}^{-1}\equiv \left[\begin{array}{cc}{\text{A}}^{11}& {\text{A}}^{12}\\ {\text{A}}^{21}& {\text{A}}^{22}\end{array}\right]
$$

the dual basis $({\text{A}}^{1},{\text{A}}^{2})$ of the basis ${(\text{A}}_{1},{\text{A}}_{2})$ is then defined by:

$$
{\text{A}}^{\text{α}}\equiv {\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{A}}_{\text{β}}
$$

This makes it possible to define the metric tensor :

$$
\text{A}\equiv {\text{A}}_{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{A}}^{\text{α}}\otimes {\text{A}}^{\text{β}}
$$

In shell or membrane theory, it is the tensor $\text{A}$ that takes the place of the identity tensor $\text{I}$ in 3D.

One writes: $\Vert {\text{A}}_{1}\times {\text{A}}_{2}\Vert =\sqrt{\text{A}}$ where $>$ , one calls $\text{A}$ the Jacobian of the transformation.

Finally, the curvature tensor is defined :

$$
\text{B}={\text{B}}_{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{A}}^{\text{α}}\otimes {\text{A}}^{\text{β}}=\left({\text{N}}_{,\text{α}}.{\text{A}}_{\text{β}}\right).{\text{A}}^{\text{α}}\otimes {\text{A}}^{\text{β}}
$$

$\text{B}$ is symmetric by definition; as its name indicates, it is used to measure the curvature of a surface at a point.

### 3.2 Assumptions

In order to arrive at the equations characterising the behaviour of membranes, several assumptions are necessary. The aim here is not to re-derive how these assumptions are reached; they are therefore given directly without going into the details, which are given in  [:ref:`1]  < [1]>` .

The assumptions are :

- The displacements of the membrane are represented by the displacements of the mid-surface.
- The Cosserat kinematic hypothesis is used :

  - Every point defined along a fibre remains, after deformation, along that same fibre.
  - There is no change of shape of the fibre through the thickness.
- The thickness H of the membrane is very small; mathematically this is expressed by the conditions $\text{H}|\text{tr}\text{B}|\ll 1$ and ${\text{H}}^{2}\left(\det\text{B}\right)\ll 1$ .
- A state of plane stress is considered $\left[\text{σ}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{σ}}^{11}& {\text{σ}}^{12}& 0\\ {\text{σ}}^{21}& {\text{σ}}^{22}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$ .
- The mass density is constant through the thickness.
- The stresses are constant through the thickness.
- The second-order bending moment is neglected.

### 3.3 Equations governing the motion of membranes

#### 3.3.1 Principle of virtual power

The tool used to set up the equations of the membranes is the principle of virtual power (PVP), whose expression in Lagrangian variables is written: $>$ , $>$ virtual velocity field $>$ ,

$$
-{\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}{\text{Π}}^{\text{T}}:{\text{grad}}_{{\text{Q}}_{0}}{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}.\text{f}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\partial \text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}\text{T}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{A}}_{0}={\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}
$$

With :

- $\text{Π}$     : the first Piola-Kirchhoff tensor
- $\text{T}=\text{Π}.\text{n}$     : the nominal stress vector (on the boundary)
- ${\text{ρ}}_{0}$     : the mass density in the reference state
- $\text{f}$     : the body forces

Time derivatives are denoted by the notation « $\dot{\text{ }}$ ». ${\text{Ω}}_{0}$ represents the initial volume and ${\text{S}}_{0}$ will denote the initial mid-surface. The boundary ${\partial \text{Ω}}_{0}$ comprises the upper and lower faces and the edge of the membrane. $>$ will denote the thickness.

The following expressions are recognised in the PVP:

- The virtual power of the acceleration quantities

$$
{\text{P}}^{\text{*}}\left({\text{ρ}}_{0}\ddot{\text{U}}\right)={\int}_{{{\text{Ω}}_{0}}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}\text{ }
$$

using the assumptions of small thickness and of mass density constant through the thickness, one obtains:

$$
{\text{P}}^{\text{*}}\left({\text{ρ}}_{0}\ddot{\text{U}}\right)={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}
$$

- The virtual power of the internal forces

$$
{\text{P}}_{int}^{\text{*}}=-{\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}{\text{Π}}^{\text{T}}:{\text{grad}}_{{\text{Q}}_{0}}{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}
$$

in order to write the stresses more simply, the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor will be used, $\text{Σ}={\text{Σ}}^{ij}{\text{A}}_{i}\otimes {\text{A}}_{j}$ . Since the components of the Cauchy stresses ${\text{σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}$ are constant through the thickness and the thickness H is very small, it can be stated that the components of the second Piola-Kirchhoff stresses ${\text{Σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}$ are also constant through the thickness. Assuming in addition a state of plane stress, one arrives at:

$$
\left[\text{Σ}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{Σ}}^{11}& {\text{Σ}}^{12}& 0\\ {\text{Σ}}^{21}& {\text{Σ}}^{22}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]
$$

By virtue of these three assumptions one can deduce that:

$$
{\text{P}}_{int}^{\text{*}}=-{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}{\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}.{\text{U}}_{,\text{β}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}
$$

- The virtual power of the external forces

$$
{\text{P}}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{*}}={\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}^{\text{ }}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}.\text{f}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}+{\int}_{\partial {\text{Ω}}_{0}}^{\text{ }}\text{T}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{A}}_{0}
$$

which can be rewritten in the form:

$$
{\text{P}}_{\text{ext}}^{\text{*}}={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}\text{p}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}\text{c}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+\text{}{\int}_{\partial {\text{S}}_{0}} \left(\text{F}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}+\text{C}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\right)\text{d}{\text{s}}_{0}
$$

where $\text{p}$ (resp. $F$ ) is the surface force on ${\text{S}}_{0}$ (resp. the line force on the edge ${\partial \text{S}}_{0}$ ) and $\text{c}$ (resp. $\text{C}$ ) is the surface couple on ${\text{S}}_{0}$ (resp. the line couple on the edge ${\partial \text{S}}_{0}$ ). One decomposes $\text{p}$ and $\text{F}$ in the current natural basis: $>$ and $>$ .

The PVP can finally be written in the form : $>$ , $>$ , $>$

$$
-{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{N}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}.{\text{U}}_{,\text{β}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{p}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{c}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+\text{}{\int}_{\partial {\text{S}}_{0}}} \left(\text{F}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}+\text{C}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\right)\text{d}{\text{s}}_{0}={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}} {\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}}
$$

#### 3.3.2 Local equation of motion and boundary conditions

The PVP can now be used to obtain the local equations of membrane dynamics and the static boundary conditions. These equations will not be used to code the finite element, but the static boundary conditions are necessary in order to understand the behaviour of membranes.

The first step is to carry out integrations by parts using the divergence theorem in order to eliminate the derivatives ${\text{U}}_{,\text{β}}^{\text{*}}$ and to bring out ${\text{U}}^{\text{*}}$ . One obtains:

$$
{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}} \frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{A}}}{\left({\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}\sqrt{\text{A}}\right)}_{,\text{β}}{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}-{\int}_{\partial {\text{S}}_{0}} {\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{υ}}_{0}^{\text{ }}.{\text{A}}_{\text{β}}{\text{ds}}_{0}+{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{p}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{dS}}_{0}
+{\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{c}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+{\int}_{\partial {\text{S}}_{0}} \left(\text{F}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}+\text{C}.{\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}\right)\text{d}{\text{s}}_{0}={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}} {\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}}}
$$

With ${\text{υ}}_{0}$ the outward unit normal to ${\partial \text{S}}_{0}$ lying in the plane tangent to ${\text{S}}_{0}$ .

Using the fact that the virtual fields ${\text{U}}^{\text{*}}$ and ${\text{a}}_{3}^{\text{*}}$ are independent and arbitrary, one obtains:

- Necessary conditions on the external loading:

  - The surface couple must be zero: $\text{c}=0$ .
  - The line force on the edge of the membrane must have no transverse component along ${\text{a}}_{3}$ : ${\text{F}}^{3}=0$ .
  - The line couple on the edge must be zero: $\text{C}=0$ .
- Local equation of dynamics: $\forall t$ , $\forall \text{ }{\text{P}}_{0}\in {\text{S}}_{0}$

$$
{\text{a}}_{\text{α},\text{β}}{\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}+{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\text{A}}}{({\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}\sqrt{\text{A}})}_{,\text{β}}\text{ }+\text{ }\text{p}={\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}\ddot{\text{U}}
$$

  - Boundary condition: $>$ , $\forall \text{ }{\text{P}}_{0}\in {\partial \text{S}}_{0}$

$$
{\text{a}}_{\text{α}}{\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{υ}}_{0}{\text{A}}_{\text{α}}=\text{F}\text{⟺}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{F}}^{\text{α}}={\text{N}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}({\text{υ}}_{0}{\text{A}}_{\text{β}})\\ {\text{F}}^{3}=0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

One observes that the necessary conditions on the external loading concern the vanishing of certain terms. This could be anticipated, knowing that a membrane has no bending stiffness. Indeed, applying a couple to a membrane would produce a rigid-body motion. Moreover, a pressure applied to a flat membrane will also produce a rigid-body motion as long as the latter is not deformed and has not acquired a so-called “geometric” stiffness. To remedy this problem, an artificial “geometric” stiffness must be created at the beginning of the analysis by imposing a prestress which can be removed later.

#### 3.3.3 Constitutive laws

In order to relate the stresses in the material to its displacements, constitutive laws must be used. In the case of membranes, constitutive laws known as “hyperelastic” are used.

Only two hyperelastic constitutive laws, among all those that exist, will be considered: the Saint Venant-Kirchhoff law and the neo-Hookean law. They will be characterised by their volumetric strain-energy density *w*  as a function of the Green-Lagrange strain tensor $\text{E}$ (such that $\text{E}={\text{E}}_{ij}{\text{A}}^{i}\otimes {\text{A}}^{j}$ with ${\text{E}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\text{a}}_{ij}-{\text{A}}_{ij}\right)$ ) or of the dilation tensor $\text{C}$ (such that $\text{C}={\text{C}}_{ij}{\text{A}}^{i}\otimes {\text{A}}^{j}$ with $>$ ).

The second Piola-Kirchhoff stress, $\text{Σ}$ , is then related to $\text{E}$ or $\text{C}$ by:

$$
\text{Σ}=\text{Sym}\frac{\partial \text{w}}{\partial \text{E}}=2\text{Sym}\frac{\partial \text{w}}{\partial \text{C}}
$$

with

$$
\text{Sym}\frac{\partial \text{w}}{\partial \text{E}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial \text{w}}{\partial \text{E}}+{\left(\frac{\partial \text{w}}{\partial \text{E}}\right)}^{\text{T}}\right)
$$

##### 3.3.3.1 Saint Venant-Kirchhoff law

The standard hyperelastic material is characterised by the Saint Venant-Kirchhoff law. Its volumetric strain-energy density is expressed by:

$$
\text{w}\left(\text{E}\right)={\text{Σ}}_{0}:\text{E}+\frac{1}{2}\text{E}:\text{D}:\text{E}
$$

with $D$ a fourth-order tensor called the elasticity tensor.

##### 3.3.3.2 Neo-Hookean law

The neo-Hookean law is another classical hyperelastic constitutive law; it is used in particular for the study of incompressible materials. This law gives better results when the body undergoes large strains. Its volumetric strain-energy density is expressed by:

$$
\text{w}\left(\text{C}\right)=\frac{\text{μ}}{2}\left(\text{tr}\text{C}-3\right)-\text{μ}.\ln\text{J}+\frac{\text{λ}}{2}(\ln\text{J})\mathrm{²}
$$

$\text{μ}$ and $\text{λ}$ are the Lamé coefficients; they are defined by:

$$
\text{λ}=\frac{\text{Eν}}{\left(1+\text{ν}\right)\left(1-2\text{ν}\right)}
$$

with $\text{E}$ (resp. $\text{ν}$ ) Young’s modulus (resp. Poisson’s ratio).

### 3.4 Finite-element discretisation

The equations used to solve the behaviour of membranes were presented in the previous paragraphs. They are reformulated so that they can be coded in code_aster.

#### 3.4.1 Interpolation of the geometry

${\text{P}}_{0}$ or $\text{X}$ will be used indifferently for the initial position of a current particle lying on the initial mid-surface $>$ . The coordinates of the point $\text{X}$ in a fixed orthonormal frame $(\text{O}\text{ };\text{ }{\text{e}}_{1},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{2},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{3})$ are denoted $({\text{X}}_{1},\text{ }{\text{X}}_{2},\text{ }{\text{X}}_{3})$ .

The reference element of a finite element *e*  of the surface ${\text{S}}_{0}$ is denoted ${\text{e}}_{\text{ξ}}$ . The coordinates of the reference element are denoted ( ${\text{ξ}}^{1},\text{ }{\text{ξ}}^{2})$ . The geometry of the element $e$ is represented by the interpolation:

$$
{\text{e}}_{\text{ξ}}\text{ }\to \text{e}
\left({\text{ξ}}^{1},\text{ }{\text{ξ}}^{2}\right)\mapsto \text{X}\equiv {\text{P}}_{0}\text{ }\text{ }\text{a}\text{v}\text{e}\text{c}\text{ }\forall i\in \left\lbrace 1,2,3\right\rbrace ,\text{ }{\text{ }\text{ }\text{X}}_{i}=⟨\text{N}\left({\text{ξ}}^{1},\text{ }{\text{ξ}}^{2}\right)⟩{\left\lbrace {\text{X}}_{i}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
$$

where

- The row vector $⟨N⟩$ is the geometric interpolation function of the element $e$ : $⟨\text{N}⟩=⟨{\text{N}}_{1},{\text{N}}_{2},\dots ,{\text{N}}_{\text{n}\text{n}\text{e}}⟩\text{ }$ with $\mathrm{nne}$ the number of nodes of the element.
- The column vector ${\left\lbrace {\text{X}}_{i}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}$ contains the $\text{nne}$ coordinates ${\text{X}}_{i}$ of the nodes of the element $\text{e}$ .

At a point $\text{X}\equiv {\text{P}}_{0}$ of the element $e$ , the vectors ${\text{A}}_{\text{α}}=\frac{\partial {\text{P}}_{0}}{\partial {\text{ξ}}^{\text{α}}}$ of the natural basis are computed by

$$
\forall \text{α}\in \left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace ,\text{ }\forall i\in \left\lbrace 1,2,3\right\rbrace ,\text{ }{\left({\text{A}}_{\text{α}}\right)}_{i}=⟨{\text{N}}_{,\text{α}}⟩{\left\lbrace {\text{X}}_{i}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
$$

The area element $>$ is defined by $\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}=\Vert \frac{\partial \text{X}}{\partial {\text{ξ}}^{1}}\times \frac{\partial \text{X}}{\partial {\text{ξ}}^{2}}\Vert \text{d}{\text{ξ}}^{1}\text{d}{\text{ξ}}^{2}=\Vert {\text{A}}_{1}\times {\text{A}}_{2}\Vert \text{d}{\text{ξ}}^{1}\text{d}{\text{ξ}}^{2}$

The reasoning is strictly identical for the edge vectors; only the notation is changed. The geometry of the element $\text{e}'\text{}$ is represented by the interpolation:

$$
{\text{e}'}_{\text{ξ}}\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\to \text{e}'
\text{ξ}\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\mapsto \text{X}\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\text{a}\text{v}\text{e}\text{c}\text{ }\forall i\in \left\lbrace 1,2,3\right\rbrace ,\text{ }{\text{X}}_{i}=⟨\text{N}'\left(\text{ξ}\right)⟩{\left\lbrace {\text{X}}_{i}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}'}
$$

#### 3.4.2 Interpolation of the displacement field

The displacement vector $\text{U}=\text{U}\left({\text{ξ}}^{1},\text{ }{\text{ξ}}^{2},\text{t}\right)$ of the mid-surface is decomposed in the fixed orthonormal basis $({\text{e}}_{1},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{2},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{3})$ :

$$
\text{U}={\text{U}}^{i}{\text{e}}_{i}
$$

Since the basis $({\text{e}}_{1},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{2},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{3})$ is orthonormal, the covariant and contravariant components in this basis coincide. Consequently, the upper or lower position of the indices $i$ in the previous relation is of no importance.

One works with isoparametric finite elements; in a finite element $e$ of the surface ${\text{S}}_{0}$ , each component ${\text{U}}^{i}$ of the displacement is therefore interpolated by:

$$
\forall i\in \left\lbrace 1,2,3\right\rbrace ,\text{ }{\text{ }\text{ }\text{U}}_{i}=⟨\text{N}\left({\text{ξ}}^{1},\text{ }{\text{ξ}}^{2}\right)⟩{\left\lbrace {\text{U}}_{i}\left(\text{t}\right)\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}={\text{N}}_{\text{a}}{\text{U}}_{\text{a}}^{i\text{e}}\text{ }\text{ }(\text{sommation sur a}\in \left[1,\text{n}\text{n}\text{e}\right])
$$

where

- The interpolation functions ${\text{N}}_{\text{a}}$ are the same as previously
- The column vector ${\left\lbrace {\text{U}}_{i}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}$ contains the $\text{nne}$ displacement components ${\text{U}}_{i}(\text{t})$ of the nodes of the element $\text{e}$ .

One writes :

| :math:leftlbrace begin{array}{c}{U}_{1}\ {U}_{2}\ {U}_{3}end{array}rightrbrace =left[begin{array}{c}{N}_{1}\ text{}\ text{}end{array}begin{array}{c}text{}\ {N}_{1}\ text{}end{array}begin{array}{c}text{}\ text{}\ {N}_{1}end{array} | begin{array}{c}{N}_{2}\ text{}\ text{}end{array}begin{array}{c}text{}\ {N}_{2}\ text{}end{array}begin{array}{c}text{}\ text{}\ {N}_{2}end{array} | cdots right]{leftlbrace begin{array}{c}(begin{array}{c}Umathrm{¹}\ Umathrm{²}\ Umathrm{³}end{array})mathrm{¹}\ (begin{array}{c}Umathrm{¹}\ Umathrm{²}\ Umathrm{³}end{array})mathrm{²}\ ⋮end{array}rightrbrace }^{e}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

This is abbreviated as:

$$
\left\lbrace \vec{\text{U}}\right\rbrace =\left[\text{N}\right]{\left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
$$

#### 3.4.3 Interpolation and discretisation of the displacement field

The gradient tensor of the displacement field is decomposed in the basis ${\text{A}}_{i}\otimes {\text{A}}_{j}$ :

| :math:text{H}equiv {{text{grad}}_{{text{Q}}_{0}}{text{U}left({text{Q}}_{0},text{t}right)}_{ | {text{Q}}_{0}={text{P}}_{0}}=text{H}}_{ij}{text{A}}^{i}otimes {text{A}}^{j}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

Only the components ${\text{H}}_{i\text{β}}$ are of interest; this tensor is rewritten as a column vector:

$$
\left(\tilde{\text{H}}\right)\equiv \left(\begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{1}\\ {\text{H}}_{2}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{3}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{4}\\ {\text{H}}_{5}\\ {\text{H}}_{6}\end{array}\end{array}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{11}\\ {\text{H}}_{21}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{31}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{H}}_{12}\\ {\text{H}}_{22}\\ {\text{H}}_{32}\end{array}\end{array}\end{array}\right)
$$

One then has the relation:

$$
\left\lbrace \tilde{\text{H}}\right\rbrace =\left[\text{G}\right]{\left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
$$

with $\left[\text{G}\right]$ a matrix of dimension $6x3\mathit{nne}$ such that:

$$
\left[\text{G}\right]\equiv \left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{N}}_{1,1}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{3}\end{array}\right]& {\text{N}}_{2,1}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{3}\end{array}\right]& \text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\dots \text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\\ {\text{N}}_{1,2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{1})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{2})}_{3}\\ ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{1}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{2}& ({{\text{A}}_{3})}_{3}\end{array}\right]& \dots & \text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\dots \text{ }\text{ }\text{ }\end{array}\right]
$$

The virtual fields, indicated by “*”, will be discretised in an identical manner.

#### 3.4.4 Discretisation of the principle of virtual power

Now that the interpolation functions are available, the principle of virtual power can be written in matrix form:

- Virtual power of the acceleration quantities:

$$
{\text{P}}^{\text{*}}\left({\text{ρ}}_{0}\ddot{\text{U}}\right)={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}} {\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}=\sum_{\text{e}}{⟨{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}⟩}^{\text{e}}{\left[\text{M}\right]}^{\text{e}}{\left\lbrace \ddot{\text{U}}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
avec \left[\text{M}\right] la matrice masse élémentaire:
$$

$$
{\left[\text{M}\right]}^{\text{e}}\equiv {\int}_{\text{e}} {\text{ρ}}_{0}{\left[\text{N}\right]}^{\text{T}}\left[\text{N}\right]\text{H}{\text{dS}}_{0}
$$

$$
\begin{align}\begin{aligned}  {\text{P}}^{\text{*}}\left({\text{ρ}}_{0}\ddot{\text{U}}\right)={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}} {\text{ρ}}_{0}\text{H}.\ddot{\text{U}}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{S}}_{0}=\sum_{\text{e}}{⟨{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}⟩}^{\text{e}}{\left[\text{M}\right]}^{\text{e}}{\left\lbrace \ddot{\text{U}}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}\end{aligned}\end{align}
$$

- Virtual power of the internal forces:

$$
{\text{P}}_{i\text{n}\text{t}}^{\text{*}}=-{\int}_{{\text{Ω}}_{0}} {\text{Π}}^{\text{T}}:{\text{grad}}_{{\text{Q}}_{0}}{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}{\text{d}\text{Ω}}_{0}=-\sum_{\text{e}}{⟨{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}⟩}^{\text{e}}{\left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}
$$

with:

$$
{\left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}\equiv {\int}_{\text{e}} {\left[\text{G}\right]}^{\text{T}}\left\lbrace \tilde{\text{Π}}\right\rbrace \text{H}{\text{dS}}_{0}
$$

In the same way as previously, only the components ${\text{Π}}^{i\text{β}}$ are of interest:

$$
\left\lbrace \tilde{\text{Π}}\right\rbrace \equiv \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}_{1}\\ {\text{Π}}_{2}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}_{3}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}_{4}\\ {\text{Π}}_{5}\\ {\text{Π}}_{6}\end{array}\end{array}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}^{11}\\ {\text{Π}}^{21}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}^{31}\\ \begin{array}{c}{\text{Π}}^{21}\\ {\text{Π}}^{22}\\ {\text{Π}}^{32}\end{array}\end{array}\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

Note: instead of using $\left\lbrace \tilde{\text{Π}}\right\rbrace$ , ${\left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}$ will subsequently be expressed as a function of Piola Kirchhoff II $(\text{Σ})$ so that the constitutive laws of paragraph  3.3.3 can be used.

- Virtual power of the external forces:

$$
{\text{P}}_{\text{e}\text{x}\text{t}}^{\text{*}}={\int}_{{\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{p}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{S}}_{0}+{\int}_{\partial {\text{S}}_{0}}^{\text{F}.{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}\text{d}{\text{s}}_{0}=\sum_{\text{e}}{⟨{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}⟩}^{\text{e}}{\left\lbrace \text{Φ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}+\sum_{\text{e}'}{⟨{\text{U}}^{\text{*}}⟩}^{\text{e}'}{\left\lbrace \text{Φ}'\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}'}}}
$$

with

$$
{\left\lbrace \text{Φ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}\equiv {\int}_{\text{e}} {\left[\text{N}\right]}^{\text{T}}\left\lbrace \text{p}\right\rbrace {\text{dS}}_{0}
$$

$\left\lbrace \text{p}\right\rbrace$ (resp. $\left\lbrace \text{F}\right\rbrace$ ) denotes the vector containing the three components of the surface-force vector $\text{p}$ (resp. line force $\text{F}$ ) in the orthonormal basis $({\text{e}}_{1},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{2},{\text{ }\text{e}}_{3})$ .

#### 3.4.5 Linearisation and expression of the tangent stiffness matrix

In the Newton-Raphson scheme, the problem is to compute the term $\frac{\partial \left\lbrace \text{R}\right\rbrace }{\partial \left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }$ , called the tangent stiffness matrix, of dimension $3\mathit{nn}x3\mathit{nn}$ with $\mathit{nn}$ the total number of nodes of the element. $\lbrace \text{R}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \text{Φ}\right\rbrace -\left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace$ is called the residual. One writes:

$$
\left[\text{K}\right]\equiv \frac{\partial \left\lbrace \text{R}\right\rbrace }{\partial \left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }=\frac{\partial \left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace }{\partial \left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }-\frac{\partial \left\lbrace \text{Φ}\right\rbrace }{\partial \left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }\equiv \left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Ψ}}\right]+\left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Φ}}\right]
$$

At the element level one has:

$$
{\left[\text{K}\right]}^{\text{e}}\equiv \frac{{\partial \left\lbrace \text{Ψ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}}{{\partial \left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}}-\frac{{\partial \left\lbrace \text{Φ}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}}{\partial {\left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e}}}\equiv {\left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Ψ}}\right]}^{\text{e}}+{\left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Φ}}\right]}^{\text{e}}
$$

${\left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Ψ}}\right]}^{\text{e}}$ is called the elementary tangent matrix due to the internal forces and ${\left[{\text{K}}_{\text{Φ}}\right]}^{\text{e}}$ the elementary tangent matrix due to the external forces.

In the case where there are also follower loads applied on the edge of the membrane, there also appears the elementary tangent stiffness matrix due to the external forces on the edge $e’$ :

$$
{\left[\text{K}'\right]}^{\text{e}'}\equiv \frac{-\partial {\left\lbrace \text{Φ}'\right\rbrace }^{\text{e'}}}{\partial {\left\lbrace \text{U}\right\rbrace }^{\text{e'}}}
$$

It will be noted that follower loads exist only in large strains. Indeed, since in small strains one assumes a fixed geometry in order to carry out the calculations, loads that depend on the displacement have no reason to appear.

#### 3.4.6 Computation of the stresses in the membrane

An important remark, although obvious in view of the equations, is that one works on the undeformed configuration of the membrane. The consequence is that one will be able to compute the second Piola-Kirchhoff stresses and not the Cauchy stresses. Now, there is no equation governing the evolution of the thickness, which therefore is also an unknown. It can be shown that the Cauchy stresses are related to the Piola-Kirchhoff stresses by the equation:

$$
\text{h}{\text{σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}=\frac{{\text{HΣ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}}{\frac{\Vert {\text{a}}_{1}\times {\text{a}}_{2}\Vert }{\Vert {\text{A}}_{1}\times {\text{A}}_{2}\Vert }}
$$

which gives two unknowns for one equation. One will therefore have access only to the Cauchy stresses integrated through the thickness, also called “generalised forces”, expressed by “ $\text{h}\text{σ}$ ”.

### 3.5 Expression of the elements coded in code_aster

This paragraph gives the expression of the various tensors as they are written in code_aster.

For the Saint Venant Kirchhoff constitutive law one has:

$$
{\text{Σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}={\text{Σ}}_{0}^{\text{α}\text{β}}+\frac{\text{E}}{1-\text{ν}\mathrm{²}}\left[\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\text{ν}\right)\left({\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{γ}}{\text{A}}^{\text{β}\text{δ}}+{\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{δ}}{\text{A}}^{\text{β}\text{γ}}\right)+{\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{A}}^{\text{γ}\text{δ}}\right]\frac{1}{2}\left({\text{a}}_{\text{δ}\text{γ}}-{\text{A}}_{\text{δ}\text{γ}}\right)
$$

And therefore:

$$
\frac{\partial {\text{Σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}}{\partial {\text{E}}_{\text{δ}\text{γ}}}=\text{μ}\left({\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{γ}}{\text{A}}^{\text{β}\text{δ}}+{\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{δ}}{\text{A}}^{\text{β}\text{γ}}\right)+\frac{2\text{λ}\text{μ}}{\text{λ}+2\text{μ}}{\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{A}}^{\text{γ}\text{δ}}
$$

And for the neo-Hookean law:

$$
{\text{Σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}={\text{Σ}}_{0}^{\text{α}\text{β}}+\text{μ}\left({\text{A}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}-{\text{a}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}\right)+\frac{\text{λ}}{2}\ln\left(\frac{\text{λ}\text{q}}{2\text{μ}}\text{W}\left(\frac{2\text{μ}}{\text{λ}\text{q}}\frac{{\text{e}}^{2\text{μ}}}{\text{λ}}\right)\right){\text{a}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}
$$

And therefore:

$$
\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial {\text{Σ}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}}{\partial {\text{E}}_{\text{δ}\text{γ}}}=\text{ }\frac{\text{μ}{\text{C}}_{33}}{\text{1 + 2μ}\frac{{\text{C}}_{33}}{\text{λ}}}{\text{a}}^{\text{α}\text{β}}{\text{a}}^{\text{γ}\text{δ}}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\text{μ}-\frac{\text{λ}}{2}\ln\det\text{C}\right)\left({\text{a}}^{\text{α}\text{δ}}{\text{a}}^{\text{γ}\text{β}}+{\text{a}}^{\text{α}\text{γ}}{\text{a}}^{\text{δ}\text{β}}\right)
$$

With ${\text{C}}_{33}=\frac{\text{λ}}{2\text{μ}}\text{W}\left(\frac{2\text{μ}}{\text{λ}\text{q}}{\text{e}}^{\frac{2\text{μ}}{\text{λ}}}\right)$ , $\text{q}=\frac{\det\left[{\text{a}}_{\text{α}\text{β}}\right]}{\det\left[{\text{A}}_{\text{α}\text{β}}\right]}$ , and $W$ the Lambert function $\text{w}\left(\text{z}\right){\text{e}}^{\text{w}\left(\text{z}\right)}=\text{z}$ ( $\text{z}\in \text{C}$ ).

In the following expressions $\text{δ}$ will denote the Kronecker symbol and one will have:

$$
i=3\left(\text{a}-1\right)+\text{p}
$$

With $i,\text{ }j\text{ }\in \left(1,3\times \text{n}\text{n}\text{e}\right)$ , $\text{a},\text{ }\text{b}\in \left(1,\text{n}\text{n}\text{e}\right)$ and $\text{p},\text{ }\text{q}\in \left(1,2,3\right)$ .

The mass matrix is written:

$$
{\left[\text{M}\right]}_{ij}^{\text{e}}\equiv {\int}_{\text{e}} {\text{δ}}_{\text{pq}}{\text{ρ}}_{0}{\text{N}}_{\text{a}}{\text{N}}_{\text{b}}\text{H}{\text{dS}}_{0}
$$

The elementary tangent matrix due to the internal forces:

$$
{{[\text{K}}_{\text{Ψ}}]}_{ij}^{\text{e}}={\int}_{\text{e}}^{\text{ }}{\text{N}}_{\text{a},\text{α}}{\text{N}}_{\text{b},\text{β}}\left({\text{δ}}_{\text{pq}}{\text{Σ}}^{\text{βα}}+{\text{x}}_{\text{p},\text{γ}}{\text{x}}_{\text{q},\text{δ}}\frac{1}{2}\left[{\left(\frac{\partial \text{Σ}}{\partial \text{E}}\right)}^{\text{γαβδ}}+{\left(\frac{\partial \text{Σ}}{\partial \text{E}}\right)}^{\text{γαδβ}}\right]\right)\text{H}{\text{dS}}_{0}

$$

## 4 Formulation of the GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements

For a uniaxial reinforcement layer, the strain energy can be written in the form:

$$
\Phi =\frac{1}{2}\intS\sigma \varepsilon \text{ds}
$$

with $S$ the reinforcement cross-section per unit length, $\sigma$ the stress (scalar) and $\varepsilon$ the strain (scalar). One seeks to obtain an expression of the type $\epsilon =\mathit{BU}$ where $U$ denotes the nodal values of the displacement. Following the approach of the previous section, one shows this time that:

$$
\varepsilon ={(\nabla u)}_{11}=(\frac{\partial u}{\partial {\xi}^{\beta}}\cdot {a}^{\alpha}){R}_{\alpha}^{1}{R}_{\gamma}^{1}{g}^{\beta \gamma }
$$

Introducing the derivative $\widehat{B}$ of the shape functions at the Gauss point under consideration, one has:

$$
\epsilon ={R}_{\alpha}^{1}{R}_{\gamma}^{1}{g}^{\beta \gamma }{\widehat{B}}_{\beta ,n}{\left({a}^{\alpha}\right)}_{i}{U}_{i,n}
$$

Hence the sought $B$. It will be noted that it has the form of a vector, owing to the scalar nature of the strain sought:

$$
{B}_{i,n}={R}_{\alpha}^{1}{R}_{\gamma}^{1}{g}^{\beta \gamma }{\widehat{B}}_{\beta ,n}{\left({a}^{\alpha}\right)}_{i}
$$

From $B$ , one recovers all the classical expressions for the strain, the nodal forces and the tangent matrix:

$$
\epsilon =\mathit{BU}
F=\int{B}^{T}\sigma
K=\int{B}^{T}\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }B
$$

It will be noted that it is the one-dimensional constitutive laws that are used to obtain the stress from the strain. All the constitutive laws available in one dimension can be used. Failing that, the three-dimensional laws can also be used, by means of the De Borst method.

## 5 Mass matrix

For the MEMBRANE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements, the terms of the mass matrix are obtained after discretisation of the following variational formulation:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\delta {W}_{\text{mass}}^{\text{ac}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{S}{\int}\rho \ddot{u}\delta u\mathit{dz}\mathit{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{m}(\ddot{u}\delta u+\ddot{v}\delta v+\ddot{w}\delta w)\mathit{dS}\end{array}
$$

With ${\rho}_{m}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \text{dz}$ .The discretisation of the displacement (for $N$ nodes) for this isoparametric element is:

$$
\text{u}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{k}\\ {v}_{k}\\ {w}_{k}\end{array}\right)\rbrace k=1,\cdots ,N
$$

The mass matrix, in the basis in which the degrees of freedom are grouped by translation direction, then has the expression:

$$
\text{M}=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}{\text{M}}_{m}& 0& 0\\ 0& {\text{M}}_{m}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\text{M}}_{m}\end{array}\right)
$$

With: ${\text{M}}_{m}=\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{m}{\text{N}}^{T}\text{N}\text{dS}$ and $\text{N}=({N}_{1}\cdots {N}_{k})$ .
