# v2.04.120 SDLV120 - Absorption of compression wave in elastic bar

**Summary**

The elements elastical elements of order 0 is tested to apply absorbent conditions at the border of a finished mesh elements to simulate infinity in direct transient calculations.

They are used to model an infinite elastic bar, in 3D or 2D, in which a pressure wave is created by imposing a displacement at one of the ends. One is interested in the non-reflection of the wave at the "infinite" end of the bar.

The two direct transient operators of *Code_Aster* , DYNA_VIBRA and DYNA_NON_LINE, are tested successively.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The system considered in the 3D case is that of an elastic bar with square cross section. The $x$ displacement is imposed on one of the vertical faces and the propagation of a compression wave is observed. The lateral surface of the bar is left free. The absorbent elements is placed on the opposite side of the excitation face to simulate the infinite character of the bar in this direction. In the 2D case, the principle is identical with a supposedly very wide bar whose vertical section is modeled only (see diagram).

## 2 reference solution

The solution must show the absorption of a compression wave by the absorbent surface. The imposed displacement is a uniform translation according to the $x$ axis. An identical displacement field must be obtained in this direction in all $x=\mathrm{Cte}$ planes. Moreover, the absorbent boundary is orthogonal at this axis. Therefore, the absorption of flat compression waves under normal incidence is studied. The theory [bib1] says that with a solid 0-order paraxial boundary, this absorption is perfect. This is what we have to check with this reference solution.

Thus, by observing the evolution of the point displacement given to the mesh, we will focus on finding in the signal obtained the duration of excitation and the return to rest after the passage of the wave, characteristic of its absorption.

### 2.1 Baseline

This paragraph gives the results obtained with *Code_Aster* in this configuration. We check that they are satisfactory and take them as a reference for the future.

The 3D case concerns the $200m$ bar length, the $x$ movement to a point bar located at $150m$ of the excited face in the $x$ direction and at the centre of the section in the $yz$ plane. For the 2D case, the $50m$ bar length, the point is located at $40m$ of the face on $x$ and in the middle of the section in the direction

(in 2D, you take a shorter and refined mesh).

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.120/100000000000041B00000236676A47DADAB1F87E.png)

As expected, the width of the signal measured in both cases is identical to that of the excitation function. Physically, the propagation of the compression wave is observed. The signal is not modified in its propagation and therefore the maximum amplitude of 1 mm is well found. It is also clear that the return to rest immediately after the passage of the wave and the absence of a reflected signal at the end of the mesh are clearly noted.

### 2.2 Uncertainties

This is a numerical study result. Qualitative predictions are found. Numerical values are linked to the accuracy of the calculation. Only the return to rest is precisely given by the analysis.

## 5 Summary of the results

The calculation with both modellings quantitatively shows the maximum displacement equal to the maximum amplitude of the signal and qualitatively the return to rest after wave passage.

The results obtained with DYNA_VIBRA and DYNA_NON_LINE operators are very similar. The difference comes from the fact that each time step has a balance of the second member's effort with the DYNA_NON_LINE operator, which explains why the time step results are a little better even with a larger time step. However, this difference remains minimal because the time step used with DYNA_VIBRA is small enough.
