# v8.01.107 FDLV107 - Rigidities added under ring flow

**Summary:**

This field test of fluid-structure interaction validates the calculation of stiffness added on a circular cylinder excited on its first rotulated-rotulated bending mode and subjected to ring flow at different speeds.

The added stiffness (speed function) is calculated on the first bending mode of the cylinder. The decrease of the mode's eigenfrequency is checked, up to zero for a critical flow speed of the fluid.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is an approached analytical solution. The analytical fluctuation potentials approached to calculate the added stiffness are written [bib1]:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{ccc}{\varphi}_{1}(r,\theta ,y)=& \frac{{R}_{e}^{2}}{{R}_{e}^{2}-{R}_{i}^{2}}(r+\frac{{R}_{i}^{2}}{r})& \sin\theta \sin\frac{\pi (y+l/2)}{l}\\ {\varphi}_{2}(r,\theta ,y)=& \frac{{R}_{e}^{2}}{{R}_{e}^{2}-{R}_{i}^{2}}(r+\frac{{R}_{i}^{2}}{r})& \sin\theta \cos\frac{\pi (y+l/2)}{l}\end{array}$

The rigidity added to the first bending mode of the outer cylinder considered as a rotulated-rotulated beam is written [bib1]:

${K}_{A}=-\frac{\rho}{2}\frac{{V}_{0}^{2}{\pi}^{3}{R}_{e}^{3}}{({R}_{e}^{2}-{R}_{i}^{2})l}({R}_{e}+\frac{{R}_{i}^{2}}{{R}_{e}})$

This rigidity, calculated on a cylindrical geometry, is then assigned to a model with an equivalent degree of freedom.

The equivalent degree of freedom system is an equivalent mass-spring system, which is assigned a mass equal to the mass of the system plus the mass added by the fluid and a rigidity equal to the rigidity of the system plus the stiffness added by the flow at different speeds.

The mass of the air system is:

$M=10292\mathit{kg}$

for an external cylindrical shell of thickness:

$C={2.10}^{-3}m$

For equivalent air rigidity of the "external hull" system, the rigidity of a beam subjected to a force distributed over its entire length shall be taken:

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.107/10000D66000027610000052B117D0A01DB11CF31.svg)

so $K=2.533{10}^{4}N/m$

The equivalent system coupled to the flow is represented by the following diagram:

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.107/1000027200000B0F0000052BB5A3FD7B4A90F759.svg)

with $m=M+{M}_{A}$ $k=K+{K}_{A}$

The proper pulse of the coupled system changes according to the square of the flow rate. If ${V}_{{0}_{C}}$ is called the critical flow rate for which the $k$ rigidity cancels:

$\exists {V}_{{0}_{C}},K+{K}_{A}({V}_{{0}_{C}})=0$ with ${V}_{{0}_{C}}^{2}=\frac{2({R}_{e}^{2}-{R}_{i}^{2})lK}{({R}_{e}+\frac{{R}_{i}^{2}}{{R}_{e}})\rho {\pi}^{3}{R}_{e}^{3}}$

then we show that:

$\omega ({V}_{0})={\omega}_{e}(0)\sqrt{1-{x}^{2}}$

where it was laid:

${\omega}_{e}(0)=\sqrt{\frac{K}{M+{M}_{A}}}$ (clean fluid spray at rest)

$x=\frac{{V}_{0}}{{V}_{{0}_{C}}}$ (reduced flow speed)

The fluid pulse at rest is $\omega =0.085\mathit{rad}/s$ .

## 4 Summary of the results

The difference on eigenfrequencies increases because when l'on is close to the critical flaming speed, the rigidity of the equivalent system must tend towards zero. However, with rounding errors (since the added rigidity values calculated by the operator to a discrete model are "hand-in-hand" affected) do not allow for a specific pulse of the zero system at critical speed.

Differences on added stiffness values also remain because the reference solution is built on a semi-analytic solution that starts from the approximation that the separation of variables between the y-quota and orthoradial coordinates is possible. It should be noted that the potentials chosen to describe the disturbance caused by the vibration of the structure in the fluid do not check the complete Laplace equation but only in a transverse section of the fluid into orthoradial coordinates. This approximation on the reference solution can explain some differences with numerical calculation.
