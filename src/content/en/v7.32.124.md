# v7.32.124 WTNP124 – test case from Liakopoulos: Draining a water column by gravity force only

**Summary:**

This test case is the simulation of the 1965 Liakopoulos experiment, which represents the drainage of a column of sand saturated with water. The latter will then de-naturalize by the force of gravity alone. To represent this, one considers a column of $1m$ height subjected to atmospheric gas pressure at the top.

This is a purely miscible hydraulic calculation. The geometry represented corresponds to a vertical bar. The transfer terms are described by a model of Mualem Van-Genuchten. The problem is dealt with by the various diagrams available for the modelling of diphasic flows: the classic finished elements and the Decented Arone Volumes.

## 1 Reference problem

This test case represents the deaturation of a column initially saturated with water by gravity effect (Liakopoulos' experience). Here we consider that air can dissolve in water (version proposed by VAUNAT in 1997).

### 1.1 Geometry

The domain is a $[\mathrm{0m};0,1m]\times [\mathrm{0m};\mathrm{1m}]$ size bar.

The saturation and permeability curves follow the Mualem-Van-Genuchten (HYDR_VGM) model. Therefore, the $n$, $\mathit{Pr}$, $\mathit{Sr}$, $\mathit{Smax}$ parameters must be defined in the materials.

It is recalled that these models are: ${S}_{le}=\frac{{S}_{l}-{S}_{\mathit{lr}}}{1-{S}_{\mathit{lr}}}$ and $m=1-\frac{1}{n}$

${S}_{\mathit{we}}=\frac{1}{{\left[1+{(\frac{{P}_{c}}{{P}_{r}})}^{n}\right]}^{m}}$

Water permeability is expressed by integrating the prediction model proposed by Mualem (1976) into the Van Genuchten capillary model: ${k}_{r}^{l}=\sqrt{{S}_{le}}{(1-{(1-{S}_{le}^{\frac{1}{m}})}^{m})}^{2}$

Gas permeability is formulated in a similar way: ${k}_{r}^{g}=\sqrt{(1-{S}_{le})}{(1-{S}_{le}^{\frac{1}{m}})}^{2m}$

It is recalled that for $S>\mathit{Smax}$ , these curves are interpolated by a polynomial of degree 2 $\mathit{C1}$ in $\mathrm{Smax}$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and initials

The limit conditions are as follows:

- Neumann conditions on the right and left edges of the domain:

$({F}_{l}^{w}+{F}_{g}^{w}).n=0$

$({F}_{l}^{c}+{F}_{g}^{c}).n=0$

- Dirichlet conditions on the upper part of the estate (open air surface):

${P}_{g}(x,y=1,t)={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$

- Dirichlet conditions on the lower part of the domain (saturated medium; water flows):

${P}_{g}(x,y=0,t)={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$

${P}_{l}(x,y=0,t)={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$

The initial state is a water saturated state at hydrostatic equilibrium. The initial conditions are as follows:

${P}_{l}(x,y,t=0)=\rho g(Y-1)+{10}^{5}$

${P}_{g}(x,y,t)=0={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$

### 1.4 Simulation time

The calculation is made over one year $({\mathrm{3.1536.10}}^{7}s)$ .

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case offers a Modelling of Liakopoulos's very classic experience. The simulations perfectly reproduce the expected results, even if we do not have numerical reference results. This test allows us to validate the correct consideration of gravity by the different numerical diagrams. This problem is indeed dealt with with with the 2 numerical diagrams available for the modelling of diphasic flows:

- the diagram of the finite volumes decentralized ridge.
- the classic elements finished.

The results obtained are the same. In terms of performance and reliability, the Finer Arest Volumes ( *_HH2SUDA) are strongly preferred.
