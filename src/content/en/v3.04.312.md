# v3.04.312 SSLV312 – Semi-elliptic fissure perpendicular to the interface of a bi-material

**Summary:**

This test case studies the influence of a semi-elliptic crack perpendicular to the interface of two elastic materials.

The first model represents a circular crack (modelization A) and the second a large axis-to-small axis-to-axis elliptical crack equal to 2. The models are massive three-dimensional with a uniform load by pressure on the lip of the crack.

The objective of the study is to calculate local $G$ along the crack bottom and to evaluate the mean quadratic the difference between the geometric correction factor ${F}_{I}$ along the crack bottom and an article reference value. ${F}_{I}$ is calculated from the local $G$ using the Irwin formula in flat deformations.

Because cracks are located in an infinite medium, the finite element model limits were selected based on the feedback gained during pre-validation of the cracked block.

## 5 Summary of the results

The results for the circular crack are consistent with those for the reference, since the difference quadratic mean is less than 5%.

For the elliptical crack, the curve of the local G evolution is significantly different from that of the reference solution. However, the average quadratic the difference remains below 10%.

## 6 Remarks

Differences between reference solutions and code_aster solutions may come from the insufficiently refined mesh in the particular area on the crack lip.

Moreover, the passage from ${F}_{I}$ to the local $G$ by the formula of Irwin in flat deformations is only correct at the heart of the part, and certainly not at the end of a crack that leads to a free surface (it is likely the same for a crack leading to the interface between two materials).
