# r3.07.09 Plate elements: Modelling Q4GG

**Summary**

This document presents the formulation of the thick-plate elements used in the modelling Q4GG.

For this modelling, two types of finite element are available, depending on the mesh cells:

- Quadrangular finite element (Q4G)
- Triangular finite element (T3G)

This modelling of a thick-plate element, like the modellings DKT, DKQ, DST, DSQ, DKTG and Q4G [7], is intended for analyses in small strains and large displacements of thin curved or plane structures. They are plane elements which do not take into account the geometric curvature of the structures, unlike the shell elements (COQUE_3D) which are curved: parasitic bendings result, which can be reduced by using more elements so as to be able to approximate curved geometries correctly. The formulation is therefore simplified and the number of degrees of freedom reduced.

Note:

The formulation of the quadrangular element Q4G used in the modelling Q4GG is described in document [7]. Consequently, one limits oneself here to the case of the triangular element T3G.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The details of the formulation of the thick-plate element T3G, as it was proposed in publication [6], are presented here. This element is constructed in a manner analogous to the element Q4G [7].

## 2 Formulation

### 2.1 Geometry of the plate elements

For the plate elements one defines a reference surface, or middle surface, which is plane (the plane $xy$ for example) and a thickness $h(x,y)$ . This thickness must be small compared with the other dimensions (extents, radii of curvature) of the structure to be modelled. The figure below illustrates the point.

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.09/100000000000030C0000036C74EFD203D3D81C6E.png)

One attaches to the middle surface $\omega$ a local orthonormal frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$ associated with the tangent plane of the structure, different from the global frame $\mathit{OXYZ}$ . The position of the points of the plate is given by the Cartesian coordinates $(x,y)$ of the middle surface and the elevation $z$ with respect to this surface.

### 2.2 Kinematics

The basic kinematics of the element T3G is that of Hencky-Mindlin (see [7] for the details), in which the 3D strain tensor, $\varepsilon$ , is defined as follows:

> ${\varepsilon}_{xx}={e}_{xx}+z{\kappa}_{xx}$ ${\varepsilon}_{yy}={e}_{yy}+z{\kappa}_{yy}$ ${\varepsilon}_{xy}={e}_{xy}+z{\kappa}_{xy}$
> ${\varepsilon}_{xz}={\gamma}_{x}$ ${\varepsilon}_{yz}={\gamma}_{y}$     (1)

where

> ${e}_{xx}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}$ ${e}_{yy}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}$ ${e}_{xy}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial v}{\partial x})$
> ${\kappa}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial x}$ ${\kappa}_{yy}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial y}$ ${\kappa}_{xy}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial x})$
> ${\beta}_{x}={\theta}_{y}$ ${\beta}_{y}=-{\theta}_{x}$
> ${\gamma}_{x}={\beta}_{x}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}$ ${\gamma}_{y}={\beta}_{y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$     (2)

${e}_{\alpha \beta }$ being the membrane extension tensor, ${\kappa}_{\alpha \beta }$ the curvature-change tensor, ${\beta}_{x}$ , ${\beta}_{y}$ the auxiliary variables defined above and ${\gamma}_{x}$ , ${\gamma}_{y}$ the transverse distortions. The principal variables are the displacements $u$ , $v$ , $w$ and the rotations ${\theta}_{x}$ , ${\theta}_{y}$ and ${\theta}_{z}$ . In the formulations considered here the rotation normal to the middle surface, ${\theta}_{z}$ , is introduced exclusively for reasons of compatibility between the nodal degrees of freedom for non-plane geometries.

### 2.4 Membrane-bending

The present formulation is based on 5 degrees of freedom, neglecting any effect of the rotation perpendicular to the middle surface. This third rotation, represented by ${\theta}_{z}$ in the local frame, is not presented in this formulation; see [7] for more details.

The membrane extension and the curvature change are discretised in an equivalent manner. The same shape functions are used for the displacements and the rotations, which leads to:

> ${U}_{i}={N}^{r}{u}_{i}^{r}$ , ${\beta}_{i}={N}^{r}{\beta}_{i}^{r}$ $(r=1à3)$

${u}_{i}^{r}$ and ${\beta}_{i}^{r}$ being the nodal values of the displacements and of the (transformed) rotations, respectively. Following equations (1) and (2) one has:

> ${e}_{ij}={B}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{r}{u}_{k}^{r}$ ,        ${\kappa}_{ij}={B}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{r}{\beta}_{k}^{r}$ ,

> ${B}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{r}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial {N}^{r}}{\partial {\xi}_{l}}({J}_{\mathit{lj}}^{-1}{\delta}_{\mathit{ik}}+{J}_{\mathit{li}}^{-1}{\delta}_{\mathit{jk}})$

Where one introduces the Jacobian of the transformation on the reference element:

> ${J}_{ij}=\frac{\partial {x}_{i}}{\partial {\xi}_{j}}=\frac{\partial {N}^{r}}{\partial {\xi}_{j}}{x}_{i}^{r}$

Thus, for the functions defined in Table 2.1 and by choosing the local frame of an element such that ${x}_{1}={y}_{1}={y}_{2}=0$ , one obtains:

> $\text{J}=(\begin{array}{cc}{x}_{2}& {x}_{3}\\ 0& {y}_{3}\end{array})$ ; ${\text{J}}^{-1}=\frac{1}{{x}_{2}{y}_{3}}(\begin{array}{cc}{y}_{3}& -{x}_{3}\\ 0& {x}_{2}\end{array})$
> $\frac{\partial {N}^{r}}{\partial {\xi}_{k}}=(\begin{array}{cc}-1& -1\\ 1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array})$

and finally the membrane strain tensor, uniform over the element:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{11}\\ {e}_{22}\\ 2{e}_{12}\end{array})=\frac{1}{{x}_{2}{y}_{3}}(\begin{array}{cccccc}-{y}_{3}& 0& {y}_{3}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& {x}_{3}-{x}_{2}& 0& -{x}_{3}& 0& {x}_{2}\\ {x}_{3}-{x}_{2}& -{y}_{3}& -{x}_{3}& {y}_{3}& {x}_{2}& 0\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{u}^{1}\\ {v}^{1}\\ {u}^{2}\\ {v}^{2}\\ {u}^{3}\\ {v}^{3}\end{array})$     (3)
> $(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{11}\\ {\kappa}_{22}\\ 2{\kappa}_{12}\end{array})=\frac{1}{{x}_{2}{y}_{3}}(\begin{array}{cccccc}0& -{y}_{3}& 0& {y}_{3}& 0& 0\\ -({x}_{3}-{x}_{2})& 0& -{x}_{3}& 0& -{x}_{2}& 0\\ {y}_{3}& {x}_{3}-{x}_{2}& -{y}_{3}& -{x}_{3}& 0& {x}_{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{3}\end{array})$     (4)

### 2.5 Transverse distortion

#### 2.5.1 Highlighting of a locking in transverse shear

In order to avoid shear locking, one must judiciously choose the discretisation of the transverse distortion. For example, by imposing

> ${\gamma}_{x}={\beta}_{x}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}$ and ${\gamma}_{y}={\beta}_{y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}$     (5)

That is, as a function of the degrees of freedom:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ -{\theta}_{x}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{N}^{\alpha}{\theta}_{y}^{\alpha}+\frac{\partial {N}^{\alpha}}{\partial {\xi}_{k}}{J}_{\mathit{k1}}^{-1}{w}^{\alpha}\\ -{N}^{\alpha}{\theta}_{x}^{\alpha}+\frac{\partial {N}^{\alpha}}{\partial {\xi}_{k}}{J}_{\mathit{k2}}^{-1}{w}^{\alpha}\end{array})$

The thin-plate limit, where $h\to 0$ and consequently ${\gamma}_{x}\to 0$ , ${\gamma}_{y}\to 0$, leads to the following equations at every point $(\xi ,\eta )$ :    $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{y}^{1}+\frac{1}{{x}_{2}}({w}_{2}-{w}_{1})+({\theta}_{y}^{2}-{\theta}_{y}^{1})\xi +({\theta}_{y}^{3}-{\theta}_{y}^{1})\eta \\ -{\theta}_{x}^{1}+\frac{1}{{x}_{2}{y}_{3}}(({x}_{3}-{x}_{2}){w}_{1}-{x}_{3}{w}_{2}+{x}_{2}{w}_{3})-({\theta}_{x}^{2}-{\theta}_{x}^{1})\xi -({\theta}_{x}^{3}-{\theta}_{x}^{1})\eta \end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\end{array}),\forall \xi ,\eta$ (6)

The conditions in equation $(6)$ lead directly to the relations ${\theta}_{x}^{3}={\theta}_{x}^{2}={\theta}_{x}^{1}$ and ${\theta}_{y}^{3}={\theta}_{y}^{2}={\theta}_{y}^{1}$ . The bending rotations are therefore all equal and necessarily zero if the rigid-body motions are correctly removed from the system. One thus observes shear locking: a thin plate ($h\approx 0$ ) with the kinematics of the transverse distortion described in equation $(5)$ cannot deform in bending.

#### 2.5.2 Remedy for the lockings by the T3G approach

In order to remedy the lockings, and in a manner similar to the Q4G formulation [7], the T3G formulation uses an interpolation of the transverse distortion independent of the displacements and of the rotations. Relations (5) are relaxed, thanks to three collocation points. First, one assumes that the spatial variation of the transverse distortion is linear, with a tangential component constant on each edge. Second, as in Q4G, compatibility is imposed at the mid-points of the three edges and only in the tangential direction.

The values of the transverse distortion along the edges can be computed directly as follows:

> ${\gamma}_{s}^{k}=\frac{{w}_{j}-{w}_{i}}{{L}_{k}}+\frac{1}{2}({\beta}_{s}^{\mathit{ki}}+{\beta}_{s}^{\mathit{kj}})$     (7)

where ${\gamma}_{s}^{k}$ is the tangential component of the transverse distortion on the edge $k$ , which lies between the nodes $i$ and $j$ . ${L}_{k}$ is the length of the edge $k$ *;* ${\beta}_{s}^{\mathit{ki}}$ and ${\beta}_{s}^{\mathit{kj}}$ are the projected values of the (transformed) rotations onto the tangent of the edge $k$ , corresponding to the nodes $i$ and $j$ , respectively:

> ${\beta}_{s}^{\mathit{ki}}={\beta}_{x}^{i}\cos{\Omega}_{k}+{\beta}_{y}^{i}\sin{\Omega}_{k}$

where ${\Omega}_{k}$ represents the angle of the edge $k$ with respect to the abscissa of the local frame. Equation (7) can be rewritten as follows:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{2}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{3}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}{C}_{1}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{1}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{1}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{1}& \frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{2}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& \frac{1}{{L}_{2}}\\ \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& \frac{1}{{L}_{3}}& 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{3}}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{1}\\ {w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{2}\\ {w}_{2}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{3}\\ {w}_{3}\end{array})$     (8)

where ${C}_{k}=\cos{\Omega}_{k}$ and ${S}_{k}=\sin{\Omega}_{k}$

On the other hand, one imposes the following form of the spatial variation, in the reference frame, of the transverse distortion:

> $\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}=\\ {\gamma}_{y}=\end{array}\begin{array}{c}{a}_{x}+{b}_{x}\xi +{c}_{x}\eta \\ {a}_{y}+{b}_{y}\xi +{c}_{y}\eta \end{array}$     (9)

where ${a}_{x}$ , ${a}_{y}$ , ${b}_{x}$ , ${b}_{y}$ , ${c}_{x}$ , ${c}_{y}$ are the parameters to be determined from the assumptions according to which the tangential components of $\gamma$ are constant on each edge.

One therefore obtains:

> ${\gamma}_{s}^{1}={\gamma}_{x}{\text{|}}_{\eta =0}={a}_{x}+{b}_{x}\xi$         $\to \text{}{a}_{x}={\gamma}_{s}^{1}\text{},\text{}{b}_{x}=0$

> ${\gamma}_{s}^{2}=({\gamma}_{x}{C}_{2}+{\gamma}_{y}{S}_{2}){\text{|}}_{\eta +\xi =1}=[{a}_{x}{C}_{2}+{a}_{y}{S}_{2}+{c}_{x}{C}_{2}+({b}_{y}{S}_{2}-{c}_{x}{C}_{2}-{c}_{y}{S}_{2})\xi ]$

> $\to \text{}{a}_{x}{C}_{2}+{a}_{y}{S}_{2}+{c}_{x}{C}_{2}={\gamma}_{s}^{2}\text{};$ ${b}_{y}{S}_{2}-{c}_{x}{C}_{2}-{c}_{y}{S}_{2}=0$

> ${\gamma}_{s}^{3}={({\gamma}_{x}{C}_{3}+{\gamma}_{y}{S}_{3})}_{\xi =0}={a}_{x}{C}_{3}+{a}_{y}{S}_{3}+({c}_{x}{C}_{3}+{c}_{y}{S}_{3})\eta$

> $\to \text{}{a}_{x}{C}_{3}+{a}_{y}{S}_{3}={\gamma}_{s}^{3}\text{};$ ${c}_{x}{C}_{3}+{c}_{y}{S}_{3}=0$

Which leads to the final result

> ${a}_{x}={\gamma}_{s}^{1}$ , ${a}_{y}=-\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}+\frac{1}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{3}$
> ${b}_{x}=0$ , ${b}_{y}=-\frac{1}{{\mathit{QS}}_{2}}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}+\frac{1}{{S}_{2}}{\gamma}_{s}^{2}-\frac{1}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{3}$

> ${c}_{x}=-{\gamma}_{s}^{1}+Q{\gamma}_{s}^{2}-Q\frac{{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{3}$ , ${c}_{y}=\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}-Q\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}{\gamma}_{s}^{2}+Q\frac{{C}_{3}{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}^{2}}{\gamma}_{s}^{3}$     (10)

where     $Q=1/({C}_{2}-{S}_{2}\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}})$

From equations $(9)$ and $(10)$ one can write:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{ccc}1-\eta & Q\eta & -\frac{{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta \\ -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{\mathit{QS}}_{2}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{2}}\xi -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{S}_{3}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}^{2}}Q\eta \end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{2}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{3}\end{array})$     (11)

By combining equations $(8)$ and $(11)$ one obtains:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{ccc}1-\eta & Q\eta & -\frac{{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta \\ -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{\mathit{QS}}_{2}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{2}}\xi -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{S}_{3}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}^{2}}Q\eta \end{array})\times \text{}$
> $(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}& 0& -\frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& \frac{1}{2}& 0& \frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{2}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& \frac{1}{{L}_{2}}\\ \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& \frac{1}{{L}_{3}}& 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{3}}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{1}\\ {w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{2}\\ {w}_{2}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{3}\\ {w}_{3}\end{array})$     (12)

The expressions above have been slightly simplified by using the same local frame as in [§2.4], that is, the one in which ${\Omega}_{1}=0$ and therefore ${C}_{1}=1$ and ${S}_{1}=0$ . The relation between the degrees of freedom of the element, ${U}_{i}^{\alpha}$ , and the transverse distortion at every point $(\xi ,\eta )$ , of equation $(12)$, is written in the form:

${\gamma}_{i}={B}_{\mathit{ik}\alpha }^{\mathit{ct}}(\xi ,\eta ){U}_{k}^{\alpha}$

Where

${\text{B}}^{\mathit{ct}}=(\begin{array}{ccc}1-\eta & Q\eta & -\frac{{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta \\ -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{\mathit{QS}}_{2}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{2}}\xi -\frac{{C}_{3}}{{S}_{3}}Q\eta & \frac{1}{{S}_{3}}-\frac{1}{{S}_{3}}\xi +\frac{{C}_{3}{S}_{2}}{{S}_{3}^{2}}Q\eta \end{array})\times \text{}$

$(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}& 0& -\frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& \frac{1}{2}& 0& \frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{2}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& \frac{1}{{L}_{2}}\\ \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& \frac{1}{{L}_{3}}& 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{3}}\end{array})$

and

> $U={(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}{\beta}_{x}^{1}& {\beta}_{y}^{1}& {w}_{1}& {\beta}_{x}^{2}& {\beta}_{y}^{2}& {w}_{2}& {\beta}_{x}^{3}& {\beta}_{y}^{3}& {w}_{3}\end{array})}^{T}$

Unlike the discretisation set out in [§2.5.1], and in equation $(5)$ , the limit $h\to 0$ applied to the independent kinematics leads to three equations which couple in a more balanced manner the displacements $w$ and the rotations ${\theta}_{x}$ , ${\theta}_{y}$ , according to equation $(8)$ .

> $(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})=(\begin{array}{ccccccccc}\frac{1}{2}{C}_{1}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{1}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{1}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{{L}_{1}}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{2}}& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{2}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{2}& \frac{1}{{L}_{2}}\\ \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{{S}_{3}}& \frac{1}{{L}_{3}}& 0& 0& 0& \frac{1}{2}{C}_{3}& \frac{1}{2}{S}_{3}& -\frac{1}{{L}_{3}}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{1}\\ {w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{2}\\ {w}_{2}\\ {\beta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\beta}_{y}^{3}\\ {w}_{3}\end{array})$     (13)

The conditions in equation $(13)$ cause no locking on the rotational degrees of freedom. Moreover, in equation $(13)$ one has used equation $(11)$ in order to deduce that

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\end{array})$

at every point, only if:

> $(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{s}^{1}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{2}\\ {\gamma}_{s}^{3}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})$

### 2.6 Computation of the stresses and of the internal forces

The application of the kinematics defined in [§2.4] to the computation of the internal forces is presented here. Two different cases will be considered:

- Linear elasticity,
- Global model of GLRC type.

For linear elasticity, a single integration point through the thickness is necessary, because the strains and the stresses vary linearly through the thickness.

The principal objective of the modelling Q4GG is the use of the model GLRC_DAMAGE [R7.01.31](doc:r7.01.31) within the framework of the element T3G and Q4G. A single integration point through the thickness is necessary, because the non-linearity is taken into account directly in terms of generalised forces and strains.

#### 2.6.1 Linear elasticity

For a linear elastic constitutive behaviour one defines the potential energy in the following manner:

> ${\Phi}^{L}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}{\int}_{\frac{-h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}{\varepsilon}_{ij}{C}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{el}}{\varepsilon}_{kl}\mathit{dz}\mathit{dS}+{\Phi}^{\mathit{ext}}$
> $\text{}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}[{e}_{ij}{H}_{ijkl}^{M}{e}_{kl}+{\kappa}_{ij}{H}_{ijkl}^{M}{\kappa}_{kl}+{\gamma}_{i}{H}_{ij}^{\mathit{CT}}{\gamma}_{j}]\mathit{dS}+{\Phi}^{\mathit{ext}}$     (14)

where $\epsilon$ and ${\text{C}}^{\mathit{el}}$ are, respectively, the $\mathrm{3D}$ strain tensor and the corresponding elastic tensor. For a linear material, the integral with respect to $z$ is carried out analytically. ${\Phi}^{\mathit{ext}}$ represents the contribution due to the boundary conditions. The tensors $e$ , $\kappa$ represent the membrane extension and the curvature change, and the vector $\gamma$ the transverse distortion, as introduced in [§2.4]. In equation $(14)$ , one also makes the assumption that the shell is symmetric with respect to the middle surface; otherwise an additional membrane-bending coupling term is obtained. The details of the computation in equation $(14)$  are provided in [7], where one finds the following expressions for the matrices $\text{H}$ :

${\text{H}}^{M}=\frac{\mathit{Eh}}{1-{\nu}^{2}}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}\end{array}\right)$ ${\text{H}}^{F}=\frac{{\mathit{Eh}}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& \nu & 0\\ \nu & 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}\end{array}\right)$ ${\text{H}}^{\mathit{CT}}=\frac{\mathit{kEh}}{2(1+\nu )}\left(\begin{array}{cc}1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right)$

where $h$ is the thickness, $E$  Young's modulus, $\nu$  Poisson's ratio and $k$ a parameter which can vary according to the theory applied. Most often one uses $k=\frac{5}{6}$ (Reissner model).

Next, one applies the kinematics introduced in [§2.3] and [§2.4]:

> $\left(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ {e}_{xy}\end{array}\right)={\text{B}}^{M}\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}^{1}\\ {v}^{1}\\ {u}^{2}\\ {v}^{2}\\ {u}^{3}\\ {v}^{3}\end{array}\right)$ $\left(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ {\kappa}_{xy}\end{array}\right)={\text{B}}^{F}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{3}\end{array}\right)$         $\left(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array}\right)={\text{B}}^{\mathit{CT}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{1}\\ {w}^{1}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{2}\\ {w}^{2}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{3}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{3}\\ {w}^{3}\end{array}\right)$

in order to obtain, from equation $(14)$ :

> ${\Phi}^{L}=\frac{1}{2}{U}_{\alpha}^{T}\underset{{K}_{\alpha \beta }^{M}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{[{\int}_{S}{B}_{ij\alpha }^{M}{H}_{ijkl}^{M}{B}_{kl\beta }^{M}\mathit{dS}]}}{U}_{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}{U}_{\alpha}^{T}\underset{{K}_{\alpha \beta }^{F}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{[{\int}_{S}{B}_{ij\alpha }^{F}{H}_{ijkl}^{F}{B}_{kl\beta }^{F}\mathit{dS}]}}{U}_{\beta}+\text{}$

> $\frac{1}{2}{U}_{\alpha}^{T}\underset{{K}_{\alpha \beta }^{\mathit{CT}}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{[{\int}_{S}{B}_{ij\alpha }^{\mathit{CT}}{H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{CT}}{B}_{kl\beta }^{\mathit{CT}}\mathit{dS}]}}{U}_{\beta}+{\Phi}^{\mathit{ext}}$

By having recourse to the principle of minimum free energy, $\partial {\Phi}^{L}=0$ , $\forall \delta {U}_{\alpha}$ , one obtains:

> $\underset{{\text{F}}^{int}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{({K}_{\alpha \beta }^{M}+{K}_{\alpha \beta }^{F}+{K}_{\alpha \beta }^{\mathit{CT}}){U}_{\beta}}}={F}_{\alpha}^{\mathit{ext}}$

where ${\text{F}}^{int}$ and ${\text{F}}^{\text{ext}}$ are, respectively, the internal and external forces.

The integration of all the contributions to the stiffness matrix ${\text{K}}^{M}$ , ${\text{K}}^{F}$ and ${\text{K}}^{\mathit{CT}}$ is carried out with a single integration point in the plane of the triangle (centre of gravity). This gives an exact integration for the terms ${\text{K}}^{M}$ and ${\text{K}}^{F}$ because ${\text{H}}^{M}$ , ${\text{H}}^{F}$ , ${\text{B}}^{M}$ , ${\text{B}}^{F}$ are constant per element (see [§4.5]). On the other hand, ${\text{B}}^{\mathit{CT}}$ is a linear function (see [§2.4]) and the exact integration of ${\text{K}}^{\mathit{CT}}$ would require three Gauss points.

As the element T3G, as proposed in [3] and discussed in [12], is under-integrated on the transverse-shear part, it has a spurious mode, that is, a zero-energy mode which is not a rigid-body mode. From two elements onwards, the T3G model nevertheless recovers a correct «rank».

#### 2.6.2 Global non-linear constitutive behaviour of GLRC type

When a global model is used, the relation between the stresses and the strains is defined in terms of the generalised variables, ${\varepsilon}^{G}$ , ${\sigma}^{G}$ :

> ${\varepsilon}^{G}=(\begin{array}{c}e\\ \kappa \\ \gamma \end{array})$             ${\sigma}^{G}=(\begin{array}{c}N\\ M\\ T\end{array})$

where the generalised strains are the membrane extension $e$ , the curvature change $\kappa$ , and the transverse distortion $\gamma$ (see [§2.4] for more precise definitions). The generalised stresses are the membrane force $\text{N}$ , the bending moment $\text{M}$ , and the transverse shear $\text{T}$ :

> $\text{N}=(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {N}_{xy}\end{array})=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array})\mathit{dz}$         $\text{M}=(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {M}_{xy}\end{array})=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}z(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array})\mathit{dz}$
> $\text{T}=(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array})={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array})\mathit{dz}$

The model GLRC_DAMAGE takes into account the elastoplasticity in membrane-bending, mainly due to the steels, and the damage in bending, mainly due to the concrete. The free energy corresponding to the model is defined as:

${\Phi}^{G}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}{(e-{e}^{P})}_{ij}{H}_{ijkl}^{M}{(e-{e}^{p})}_{kl}\mathit{dS}+\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}{(\kappa -{\kappa}^{P})}_{ij}{H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{Fd}}({d}_{1,}{d}_{2}){(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})}_{kl}\mathit{dS}$     (15)

$+\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}{\gamma}_{ij}{H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{CT}}{\gamma}_{kl}\mathit{dS}$

where ${e}^{p}$ and ${\kappa}^{p}$ are, respectively, the plastic membrane extension and the plastic curvature change; ${\text{H}}^{M}$ and ${\text{H}}^{\mathit{CT}}$ are the elastic matrices for the membrane and the transverse shear, the same as those defined in [§2.6.1]. For the bending part one defines the matrix ${\text{H}}^{\mathit{Fd}}$ , which is a function of the damage variables, ${d}_{1}$ and ${d}_{2}$ [8]. It is pointed out that no non-linearity is considered for the transverse-shear part. This assumption is not really justified, because damage of the concrete necessarily induces a decrease of the stiffness in transverse shear. Since the model was designed within the framework of the thin-plate assumption, it is inadvisable to use the model GLRC_DAMAGE in situations in which the transverse-shear energy becomes comparable to the contributions of the membrane or of the bending.

By imposing $\partial {\Phi}^{G}=0$ , one obtains:

> $\delta {\Phi}^{G}=\delta {U}^{\alpha}\underset{{\text{F}}_{int}^{M}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\int}_{S}{({\stackrel{ˆ}{B}}_{\alpha}^{M})}^{T}(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {N}_{xy}\end{array})\mathit{dS}}}+\delta {\theta}^{\alpha}\underset{{\text{F}}_{int}^{F}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\int}_{S}{({\stackrel{ˆ}{B}}_{\alpha}^{F})}^{T}(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {M}_{xy}\end{array})+{({\stackrel{ˆ}{B}}_{\theta \alpha }^{\mathit{CT}})}^{T}{\text{H}}^{\mathit{CT}}(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})\mathit{dS}}}\text{}$     (16)

> $+\delta {w}^{\alpha}\underset{{\text{F}}_{int}^{\mathit{CT}}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\int}_{s}{({\stackrel{ˆ}{B}}_{w\alpha }^{\mathit{CT}})}^{T}{\text{H}}^{\mathit{CT}}(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})\mathit{dS}}}$

where one uses the following relations:

> ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\text{B}}}^{M}={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}{\text{B}}^{M}\mathit{dz}$ ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\text{B}}}^{F}={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}z{\text{B}}^{F}\mathit{dz}$ ${\stackrel{ˆ}{\text{B}}}^{\mathit{CT}}={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}{\text{B}}^{\mathit{CT}}\mathit{dz}$

The constitutive law, practically all of whose characteristics follow from the definition of the free energy [eq. 4.7.3-1], can be summarised, according to equation $(16)$ , in terms of the relations:

> $\text{N}=\text{N}(e,\kappa )$ $\text{M}=\text{M}(e,\kappa )$ $\text{T}={\text{H}}^{\mathit{CT}}\gamma$

The details of the application of the model GLRC_DAMAGE within a «finite-element» framework are given in [4] and [8].

### 2.7 «Lumped» mass matrix

In this paragraph the approach used to build the «lumped» mass matrix is presented. One focuses on the terms due to bending, the terms corresponding to the membrane being obtained in a classical manner also applied to the $\mathrm{2D}$ elements. The mass matrix $\text{M}$ is defined from the kinetic energy ${E}^{\mathit{cin}}$ and from the kinematics proposed in [§2.2]:

> ${E}^{\mathit{cin}}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}{\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\text{}\frac{h}{2}}\rho {\dot{u}}^{2}\mathit{dz}\mathit{dS}=({\dot{\theta}}_{x}^{\alpha}\text{}{\dot{\theta}}_{y}^{\alpha}\text{}{\dot{w}}^{\alpha}){M}^{\alpha \beta }(\begin{array}{c}{\dot{\theta}}_{x}^{\beta}\\ {\dot{\theta}}_{y}^{\beta}\\ {\dot{w}}^{\beta}\end{array})+{E}_{\mathit{memb}}^{\mathit{cin}}$

where ${E}_{\mathit{memb}}^{\mathit{cin}}$ represents the contribution of the membrane, $\rho$ being the density and the mass matrix $\text{M}$ being equal to:

> ${M}^{\alpha \beta }={m}^{\alpha \beta }(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{h}^{3}}{12}& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})$     (17)

> ${m}^{\alpha \beta }={\int}_{s}\rho {N}^{\alpha}{N}^{\beta}\mathit{dS}$     (18)

One way of computing the integral $(18)$ is by Gauss quadrature, which leads to the consistent matrix coupling the contributions of the various degrees of freedom. It is generally recognised that the computation of a consistent mass matrix is not justified for fast-dynamics analyses, for which the «lumped» matrix always offers a better accuracy-cost ratio.

The same mass matrix is used for the T3G as for the DKT. In addition to the approximation of decoupling the various degrees of freedom, leading to the use of the lumped matrix, approximations are made on the inertia terms in order to increase the stability time step.

In [5] it is proposed to build the lumped matrix from equation $(18)$ by using Lobatto quadrature, whose variants are the trapezoidal scheme and Simpson's scheme, in which the integration points coincide with the nodes. Construction of the mass matrix is done through a beam finite element, linear and with two nodes, by using the trapezoidal scheme, leading to:

> ${M}_{0}^{\mathit{pout}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho \mathit{LA}(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{I}{A}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{I}{A}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 1\end{array})$     (19)

for which the vector of degrees of freedom is written as ${({\theta}_{1}{w}_{1}{\theta}_{2}{w}_{2})}^{T}$ . $A$ , $L$ and $I$ are the area of the cross-section, the length and the moment of inertia of the beam element, respectively. The use of the matrix $(\mathit{eq.}19)$ seeming too restrictive with respect to the stability condition of the explicit time-integration scheme, one rather proposes in [5]:

> ${M}_{0}^{\mathit{pout}}=\frac{1}{2}\rho \mathit{LA}(\begin{array}{cccc}\alpha & 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \alpha & 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 1\end{array})$

where the parameter $\alpha$ is introduced so that its adjustment can maximise the stability time step. According to [5] its optimal value would be $\alpha =\frac{1}{8}{L}^{2}$ . By applying these results directly by analogy to plates, one replaces the matrix of equation $(19)$ by:

> ${M}^{\alpha \beta }={m}^{\alpha \beta }(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{\mathit{hA}}^{e}}{8}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{\mathit{hA}}^{e}}{8}& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})$     (20)

where ${A}^{e}$ is the area of the element considered. In passing from the beam to the plate one has assumed a certain equivalence between the length of the beam element and the area of the plate element, so that ${A}^{e}\approx {L}^{2}$ . It is recalled that the approach proposed in [5] is not rigorous from the geometric point of view and that it focuses on the maximisation of the stability step. In the version implemented in *Code_Aster*  one makes sure of the desired effect of the modification of $(18)$ into $(19)$ by using (as was done for the elements of the DKT family [7]):

> ${M}^{\alpha \beta }={m}^{\alpha \beta }(\begin{array}{ccc}max(\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\text{},\text{}\frac{{\mathit{hA}}^{e}}{8})& 0& 0\\ 0& max(\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\text{},\text{}\frac{{\mathit{hA}}^{e}}{8})& 0\\ 0& 0& h\end{array})$     (21)

because equation $(20)$ is of interest only for coarse meshes, a priori more common, whereas equation $(17)$ becomes favourable for very fine meshes. Moreover, in *Code_Aster*  one decides to integrate $(18)$ by three-point Gauss quadrature, keeping only the diagonal terms:

${m}^{\alpha \beta }={\delta}_{\alpha \beta }{\int}_{s}\rho {N}^{\alpha}{N}^{\beta}\mathit{dS}$     (22)

Note: the classical consistent mass matrix has not been developed for this finite element in *Code_Aster* .

## 3 Implementation of the plate elements in Code_Aster

### 3.1 Description

These elements (of names MEQ4GG4, MET3GG3) are supported on plane mesh cells QUAD4 and TRIA3. These elements are not exact at the nodes and one must mesh with several elements in order to obtain correct results.

### 3.2 Use and developments introduced

These elements are used in the following way:

- AFFE_MODELE (MODELISATION='Q4GG',...) for the triangle and the quadrangle.
- AFFE_CARA_ELEM ( COQUE=_F(EPAISSEUR='EP'

ANGL_REP = ( ' $\alpha$ ' ' $\beta$ ' )

COEF_RIGI_DRZ = 'CTOR')

In order to carry out post-processings (generalised forces,...) in a frame chosen by the user which is not the local frame of the element, one gives a reference direction *d*  defined by two nautical angles in the global frame. The projection of this reference direction onto the plane of the plate fixes a reference direction $\mathit{X1}$ . The normal to the plane fixes a second one, and the vector product of the two vectors previously defined makes it possible to define the local triad in which the generalised forces and the stresses will be expressed. The user must take care that the reference axis chosen does not end up parallel to the normal of certain plate elements of the model. By default this reference direction is the axis $X$ of the global frame of definition of the mesh.

The value CTOR corresponds to the coefficient which the user can introduce for the treatment of the stiffness and mass terms associated with the rotation normal to the plane of the plate. This coefficient must be small enough not to disturb the energy balance of the element and not too small for the stiffness and mass matrices to be invertible. A value of ${10}^{-5}$ is set by default.

For a constitutive behaviour:

- Homogeneous isotropic elastic through the thickness, one uses the keyword ELAS in DEFI_MATERIAU, in which one defines the coefficients $E$ Young's modulus, $\nu$ Poisson's ratio, $\rho$ the density:

ELAS=_F (E = young NU =nu, RHO =rho... )

- Damageable elastoplastic of type GLRC_DAMAGE; this global model of a reinforced-concrete plate is capable of representing its constitutive behaviour up to failure. The characteristics of the concrete and of the reinforcements are given in DEFI_GLRC.

DEFI_GLRC(    RELATION = “GLRC_DAMAGE“,    BETON = _F(MATER = ... , EPAIS= ...) ,

### 3.3 Computation in linear elasticity

The stiffness matrix and the mass matrix (respectively the options RIGI_MECA and MASS_MECA) are integrated numerically. One does not check whether the mesh cell is plane or not. The computation takes into account the fact that the terms corresponding to the plate degrees of freedom are expressed in the local frame of the element. A transformation matrix makes it possible to pass from the local degrees of freedom to the global degrees of freedom.

The elementary computations currently available correspond to the options:

DEGE_ELNO: which gives the generalised strains per element at the nodes from the displacements in the user frame: EXX, EYY, EXY, KXX, KYY, KXY, GAX, GAY.

EFGE_ELNO: which gives the forces (generalised stresses) per element at the nodes from the displacements: NXX, NYY, NXY, MXX, MYY, MXY, QX, QY.

SIEF_ELGA: which gives the forces (generalised stresses) per element at the Gauss points from the displacements: NXX, NYY, NXY, MXX, MYY, MXY, QX, QY.

EPOT_ELEM: which gives the elastic strain energy per element from the displacements.

ECIN_ELEM: which gives the kinetic energy per element.

Finally one also computes the option FORC_NODA for the computation of the nodal forces for the operator CALC_CHAMP.

### 3.4 Non-linear computation

The stiffness matrix is here again integrated numerically. One calls the computation option STAT_NON_LINE, in which one defines, at the level of the non-linear constitutive behaviour, the number of layers to be used for the numerical integration.

```text
STAT_NON_LINE (....

COMPORTEMENT =_F (RELATION ='GLRC_DAMAGE'

....)
```

For the modelling Q4GG, the only constitutive laws used are global laws (since there is only one integration point through the thickness), relating the generalised strains to the generalised stresses.

The elementary computations currently available correspond to the options:

- DEGE_ELNO, which supplies the generalised strains per element at the nodes in the user frame from the displacements.
- SIGM_ELNO, which makes it possible to obtain the stress field through the thickness per element at the nodes for all the sub-points (all the layers and all the positions: on the lower skin, at the middle and on the upper skin of the layer).
- EFGE_ELNO, which makes it possible to obtain the forces (generalised stresses) per element at the nodes in the user frame.
- VARI_ELNO, which computes the field of internal variables and the stresses per element at the nodes for all the layers, in the local frame of the element.
