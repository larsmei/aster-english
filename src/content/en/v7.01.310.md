# v7.01.310 HPLA310 - Biblio_49 External radial crack in a circular bar subjected to thermal shock

**Summary:**

This test is based on the independent validation of version 3 in the mechanical of the breakage.

It is a static basic axisymmetric test under instationary thermal loading. The structure's behavior is isotropic linear thermoelastic.

It includes a single axisymmetric model.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v7.01.310/1000053E00001D8F00001491C83195C8A483FB73.svg)

External ring fissure in semi-infinite cylindrical bar

We'll take $a/R=0,5$ and $H/R\ge 5$.

$a=1m$

$R=2m$

$H=10m$

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is isotropic linear thermoelastic.

| Young's modulus | $E=2E11\mathrm{Pa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =0,3$ |
| Coefficient of linear expansion | $\alpha =1E-5C{°}^{-1}$ |
| Thermal conductivity | $\lambda =50W/\mathrm{m.C}°$ |
| Thermal diffusion | $\kappa =\lambda /\rho {C}_{p}=0,5{m}^{2}/s$ |
| Heat exchange coefficient | $h=250W/{m}^{2}C°$ |

Choose $h$ such as $\mathrm{Bi}=\mathrm{hR}/\lambda =10.$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

**boundary conditions mechanical**

$\mathrm{UX}={U}_{r}=0$ on $r=0$ revolution axis

$\mathrm{UY}={U}_{z}=0$ on the $0\le r\le a$ ligament

The resulting axial force zero at the upper edge will be translated into the boundary by an $(n-1)$ linear $\mathit{UY}(1)=\mathit{UY}(2)=\mathrm{...}=\mathit{UY}(n)$-relations set between the longitudinal $n$ nodes movements of the upper edge (free axial dilation, preservation of the planeity of the cross section of the bar).

Unilateral contact conditions on the lip of the crack to manage the closure of the crack.

**boundary conditions thermal**

Zero thermal flux on the $\mathrm{AB}$ revolution axis (by symmetry)

Zero thermal flux on $\mathrm{OA}$ ligament (by symmetry) and $\mathrm{AC}$ crack.

$\frac{\partial T}{\partial r}=h({T}_{\mathrm{ext}}-T)$ convection stream at the $r=R$ edge, ${T}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ indicating the outside environment temperature.

**Thermal charging**

The temperature of the outside environment undergoes an instant ${T}_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathrm{T0}\ast H(t)$ step where $H(t)$ is the Heaviside unit step function. Considering the boundary conditions the temperature does not vary according to $z$. ${T}_{0}=100°C$ will be taken to obtain the lip closure, near the skin of the room, at the beginning of the thermal shock.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

**Mechanical initial conditions**

Displacement, deformation and zero stresses at all points.

**Initial thermal conditions**

Initial temperature zero in all point.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The thermal instationary calculation precedes the mechanical calculation. Both calculations are done using the same mesh to avoid smoothing phenomena.

| ![Figure](/figures/v7.01.310/1000131600000A06000028D38BD41485CA8956F6.svg)**mesh complete** | [Figure](images/10000000000001040000009D241342BDB98FB4B3.png)**Zoom on crack tip** |
| --- | --- |

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh consists of 8651 nodes and 2772 elements, including 2732 elements QUA8 and 40 elements TRI6.

Mesh radial density is determined by successive tests to reduce $\text{1\%}$ the difference between the theoretical solution and the numerical solution, both thermally and thermomechanically, in the case of the uncracked bar.

The height of the half model is arbitrarily fixed to 5 times the $R$ radius. It is assumed a priori that the effect of the mesh size limitation in the $Z$ direction on the stress intensity factor is less than $\text{1\%}$.

An unformable block, located under the lip, was meshed to manage the friction-free contact induced by the lip closure.

### 3.4 Remarks

To calculate ${G}_{\mathrm{ref}}$ , IRWIN formulae in flat deformations are used:

${G}_{\mathrm{ref}}=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$ , ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}=0$

Maximum the difference is $\text{30\%}$ on $G$ ($\mathrm{Fo}=1$ ), $\text{14\%}$ on ${K}_{I}$ ($\mathrm{Fo}=1$ ).

The difference relative maximum on crack bottom temperature, compared to analytical solution (set on 900 terms), is less than $\text{1\%}$.

The difference maximum relative on ${\sigma}_{zz}$ in the bar before cracking, compared to the analytical solution that was set on 900 terms, at the location of the subsequent crack bottom, is less than $\text{0,5\%}$.

## 4 Summary of the results

For the uncracked bar, *Aster* temperature and stress results are very close to the reference (less than $\text{1 \%}$ maximum temperature and less than $\text{0,5 \%}$ maximum stress). On the other hand, for the energy return rate, *Aster* results are far from the reference since we are raising a maximum difference $\text{30 \%}$ for $\mathrm{Fo}=0,001$ , with an announced $\text{5 \%}$ accuracy on the reference solution.
