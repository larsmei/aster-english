# v6.03.114 FORMA03 - Practical work of the "Advanced use" training: limit load of a hole plate

**Summary:**

This $\mathrm{2D}$ test in quasi-static plane stresses allows to illustrate in a simple case the questions relating to elastoplastic modelling; it highlights the effects of structure, concentration of stresses, limit load.

It is a homogeneous rectangular plate, holed in its centre, consisting of an elastoplastic material with isotropic hardening, the initial state of which is not constrained, which is subjected to traction at its ends. It is interested in the charged elastoplastic solution.

The objective of the test is to show the possibilities of Modelling, use of the commandSTAT_NON_LINE and the post-processing with the platform Salome-Meca.

Modelling A is the force-based calculation of elasticity. It illustrates the use of theSTAT_NON_LINE command in a simplified configuration (a purely linear elastic calculation). It also serves as a reference for the other modellings.

Model B is the force-based calculation, reference with VMIS_ISOT_TRAC behavior, and illustrates the use of the various parameters of theSTAT_NON_LINE command, as well as the counting commands.

Model C specifies the procedure for calculating the limit load, using the load control by displacement.

Modelling D is identical to Modelling C except that it uses a follower load.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Elastic solution

In elasticity, for an infinite ** plate,** with a $a$ diameter hole, subject to $P$ loading on $y$ infinitely, the analytical solution in flat stresses and polar coordinates $(r,\theta )$ is:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{P}{2}.\left[(1-{(\frac{a}{r})}^{2})-(1-4.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{2}+3.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{4}).\cos(2\theta )\right]
$$

$$
{\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{P}{2}.\left[(1+{(\frac{a}{r})}^{2})+(1+3.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{4}).\cos(2\theta )\right]
$$

$$
{\sigma}_{r\theta }=\frac{P}{2}.\left[(1+2.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{2}-3.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{4}).\sin(2\theta )\right]
$$

In particular, at the edge of the hole ($r=a$ ), there is:

$$
{\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=p.\left[1+2.\cos(2\theta )\right]
$$

And along the $x$ axis:

$$
{\sigma}_{\theta \theta }={\sigma}_{yy}=\frac{P}{2}.\left[(1+{(\frac{a}{r})}^{2})+(1+3.{(\frac{a}{r})}^{4})\right]
$$

Digitally, for $P=1\mathit{MPa}$, and for an infinite plate, we have

| Point | Component | Calculation | $\mathrm{MPa}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | $\mathit{SIXX}$ | ${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }(r=a,\theta =\pi /2)$ | –1 |
| $B$ | $\mathit{SIYY}$ | ${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }(r=a,\theta =0)$ | 3 |

For a **-finished** plate, the abacus [bib1] allows to obtain the stress concentration coefficient, and it is found that for a traction of $1\mathrm{MPa}$, the maximum $\mathrm{SIGYY}$ is about $3.03\mathit{MPa}$ at point $B$.

### 2.2 Elastoplastic solution (limit load)

In elastoplasticity, by a static approach in flat stresses, a maximum limit load terminal can be obtained for a finite and infinite length $\mathrm{2L}$ width band, with a $\mathrm{2a}$ width hole and subject to a stress imposed on infinity $p$:

$$
{p}_{\lim}^{-}=\frac{{\sigma}_{y}.(L-a)}{L}
$$

Here we get as the upper terminal of the limit load: ${p}_{\lim}^{-}=0.9\times 270=243\mathit{MPa}$ . (We take ${\sigma}_{y}=270\mathrm{MPa}$ here , because the limit load is identical between a perfect elastoplastic material and a material whose tensile curve has a horizontal asymptote to $270\mathrm{MPa}$ ). In this test (in particular model B), we would like to find, by an elastoplastic calculation, an approximation of this limit load, knowing that the analytical methods allow us to know a higher terminal. We will therefore take the value ${p}_{\lim}^{-}$ as a reference.

#### 5.3.3 Elasto-plastic calculation in charge

If INST_FIN=230s is specified in the STAT_NIN_LINE command key wordINCREMENT, a force of $230\mathit{MPa}$ ** will be applied.**

The structure must be plasticized because the resulting stress in part of the structure is greater than the elastic limit (which is $\mathrm{200MPa}$).

With the 30 time step discretization of the load and the activation of the time step cutting, there will be no convergence. Newton's algorithm fails. To try to converge, you can play on several parameters:

- Increase the discretization of the load (be careful not to go too far, less than 100 steps) so that the calculation is not too long!).
- Increase the maximum number of iterations of Newton that is 10 by default (STAT_NON_LINE / CONVERGENCE / ITER_GLOB_MAXI ).
- Enable linear search (STAT_NON_LINE / RECH_LINEAIRE ).
- Increase the number of possible time step subdivisions in the management of non-convergence ( DEFI_LIST_INST/ECHEC/SUBD_NIVEAU ).
- The combination of all previous techniques.

Here is a combination that works in our case:

- 50-step load discretization;
- STAT_NON_LINE / CONVERGENCE / ITER_GLOB_MAXI = 20;
- Linear search enabled;
- Cuts up to five sublevels ( DEFI_LIST_INST/ECHEC/SUBD_NIVEAU=5 ).

With this combination, we obtain convergence in 358 steps (instead of the 30 initials, due to the cuts) and more than 4300 iterations.

Some interesting results to observe:

- At the final moment of calculation, for maximum loading, the isovalues of cumulative plastic deformation can be observed, the location of the plastic deformations (internal variable V1) in the vicinity of $B$. Visualisation at Gauss points can be used to visualize the equivalent plastic deformation accumulated at the places where it is calculated.
- For a load below $66,7\mathrm{MPa}$, there is no plasticization.
- Up to $230\mathit{MPa}$, we're constantly in charge.
- The maximum value of the Von Bets criterion at Gauss points is always less than or equal to $270\mathrm{Mpa}$ , which shows that the solution checks the constitutive law well.

#### 5.3.4 Elasto-plastic calculation in charge and then discharge

We will now proceed to the discharge. To this end, we define a new ramp in the shape of a hat:

1. $F=0.$ for $t=0$ .
2. $F=230.$ for $t=230.$ .
3. $F=0.$ for $t=300.$ .

Then you have to set a new list of moments in relation: Command DEFI_LIST_REEL(30 time step up to $t=230.$ , then 10 time step up to $t=300.$ ) and DEFI_LIST_INST command by activating the automatic cutting of time step with ECHEC / EVENEMENT='ERREUR' and ACTION='DECOUPE').

If we resume the strategy for conducting the calculation to the end of plasticity (i.e. up to $p=230\mathit{MPa}$ using STAT_NON_LINE / NEWTON / REAC_ITER=1 ), minimizing the time cut (previous exercise), we find that this strategy should be improved on the plastic discharge part because it does not converge with these settings.

The purpose is to complete the elasto-plastic calculation with isotropic hardening given by a tensile curve such that the uniaxial stress tends to a constant value ($270\mathrm{MPa}$ ).

#### 6.3.1 "Manual" limit load detection

Launch the AsterStudy module.

Then in the left column, click on the Case View tab.

The command file of the calculation case is set (click right at CurrentCase and choose Add Stage).

Note: add commands by Menu Commands→ Show All.

The command file is almost identical to previous Modelling.The only difference is that we propose here to activate the automatic management of the moment list, i.e. that the time discretization is entirely managed by STAT_NON_LINE.

- Read mesh in MED format: LIRE_MAILLAGE command.
- Orient the normal edge on which the traction load will be applied: Mesh Category / MODI_MAILLAGE / ORIE_PEAU_2D Command by assigning the high group in GROUP_MA. We keep the same name of the mesh using weer.
- Set the finished elements used: AFFE_MODELE command to affect the MECANIQUE phenomenon and the 2D (C_PLAN) flat stress Modelling to all elements.
- Read the traction curve provided in the file forma03c.21 Command LIRE_FONCTION.
- Set the material: DEFI_MATERIAU /ELAS and TRACTION command.
- Assign Material: AFFE_MATERIAU command.
- Assign boundary conditions kinematics: AFFE_CHAR_CINE/ MECA_IMPO command for symmetry on the plate quarter (left and bottom groups).
- Assign loading: MECA / FORCE_CONTOUR command for the load distributed on the top of the plate. The simplest way is to define a unit load ( FY=1.0 ), which will then be multiplied by a ramp function over time.
- Create a linear $f=t$ ramp function to multiply unit mechanical loading: DEFI_FONCTION command. For example, it varies between $(\mathrm{0.,}0.)$ and $(\mathrm{1000.,}1000.)$
- **Activate automatic time step** management with DEFI_LIST_INST / METHODE='AUTO' and DEFI_LIST command with PAS_MINI=1.e-6, PAS_MAXI=100 and VALE=(0., 50., 243) which gives the three mandatory moments of automatic list passage.
- Calculate the evolution of the elasto-plastic: CommandSTAT_NON_LINE/ COMPORTEMENT / RELATION='VMIS_ISOT_TRAC' with the previously defined moment list.

The first suggestion is to see "hand-held" the limit load. The limit load corresponds to the moment when a Gauss point reaches the equivalent value of Von Bets of about $\mathrm{270MPa}$. By analytical calculation (see § 2.2), a top terminal of the load was determined which causes plasticization at this limit value of $\mathrm{270MPa}$ (around the hole).

For this, proceed as in the previous model but load beyond $p=\mathrm{230MPa}$ . Initially, a value of $p=\mathrm{245MPa}$ is a good reference.

We see that beyond a certain load, the tangent matrix becomes singular: this is the sign that we have reached the limit load and therefore a horizontal tangent on the traction curve. The code will attempt to cut the time step to go beyond this point limit. By dichotomy (cut of the time step), it will approach the limit load value. According to the grids, we find that ${p}_{\lim}\approx \mathrm{243MPa}$ .

The cause of the difficult convergence is the proximity of the boundary load. Therefore, the time step must be subdivided. It can be seen from the loading value and the stress-moving curve at the top of the structure: it can be seen that for $p=240\mathit{MPa}$ the limit load n is not fully reached (no horizontal asymptote) but that the $p$ isovalues are close. $p$ isovalues show a plastic deformation concentration zone (similar to a slipline) inclined about $53°$ relative to the vertical, ranging from point $B$ to the right edge. This is quite well the theory that the sliplines are inclined from $54,44°$ (see [bib2]). This is of course an approximation of the slipline which is in theory of zero thickness. The maximum vertical displacement following $Y$ of point $G$ can also be raised. It is of approximately $\mathrm{5.7mm}$ .

#### 6.3.2 Calculation beyond limit load per pilot

The best solution if you want to reach the limit load and even go beyond (by solving an incremental elastoplastic problem) is to use the stress control imposed by moving a point. This is what we propose here.

For example, the point $G$ DY displacement can be used to control the ${\sigma}_{yy}$ stress imposed on $\mathit{FG}$. It will be increased up to $6\mathit{mm}$ for example (in the previous calculation, it was observed that the $Y$ point $G$ maximum displacement was about $\mathrm{5.7mm}$ , so $\mathrm{6mm}$ is well beyond the limit load).

We will take a coefficient equal to 1. So we will use a fictitious time $t$ such as $\Delta t=\Delta {U}_{Y}(G)\times 1$ . That is, the fictitious time here varies between $0$ and $6s$ (to represent a DY driving displacement of the $G$ between $0$ and $6\mathrm{mm}$ ).

Note: In alternative to this type of calculation, a method of calculating the limit load is available in Code_Aster(in $\mathrm{3D}$ , axisymmetric and flat deformation): it uses Norton-Hoff material, quasi-incompressible elements and direct limit analysis methods providing a framework for the limit load (see documents [U2.05.04](doc:u2.05.04) and [R7.07.01](doc:r7.07.01) ).

## 8 Summary of the results

This test shows how to conduct the calculation of an elasto-plastic structure and its counting, and in particular the benefit of using the steering for a limiting load problem. From this test we can remember some ideas:

- Even outside of perfect elasto-plastic behavior, there may be a limit load. This is the case with all the actual traction curves. The resolution method must then be adapted to the mechanical solution and for example use steering;
- Cutting into small load increments is often necessary to properly integrate the behavioral relationship. This can also help convergence, so it is advisable to use the automatic redistribution of the time step;
- Linear search can be used to help convergence, as well as automatic subdivision of time step. In case of discharge, elastic prediction is an effective solution.
