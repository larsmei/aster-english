# r5.03.05 Viscoplastic constitutive relation of Taheri

**Summary**

This document presents the implementation of the viscoplastic constitutive relation of Taheri, available for all the isoparametric elements (2D and 3D continuum) with the exception of plane stresses. After a presentation of the evolution equations of this law, the system obtained by implicit discretisation is described; it is shown in particular that it always admits a solution.

This model is well suited to describing the response of austenitic steels under cyclic loadings, and in particular the phenomenon of progressive strain. On the other hand, because of its complexity (two yield surfaces, semi-discrete internal variable), it does not appear desirable to use it for different applications (a monotonic loading path, for example).

## 1 Description of the model

The constitutive relation proposed by Taheri makes it possible to describe the response of austenitic steels under cyclic loadings: it is indeed well suited to representing the phenomenon of progressive strain. Before stating the equations proper, one may specify that this model differs from classical plasticity (von Mises criterion with kinematic and isotropic hardening) by two particularities, sources of difficulties in the numerical formulation. On the one hand, the evolution of the dissipative variables rests on two loading criteria instead of one: the first, classical, conditions the appearance of plastic strain, the second makes it possible to keep a record of the “maximum” hardening reached by the material in order to account for the ratchetting phenomenon. On the other hand, in order to represent progressive strain in a satisfactory manner, a semi-discrete internal variable has been introduced. Constant when the constitutive behaviour is dissipative, it evolves only in the elastic regime of the material. Original in appearance, this model nonetheless rests on physical bases, still set out in Taheri , . It is accessible, in an extended viscoplastic version (necessary in order to describe the constitutive behaviour at high temperatures), by the command STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword RELATION: VISC_TAHERI.

### 1.1 Plastic behaviour

A detailed description of the constitutive law is given in Taheri et al. , . Briefly, the state of the material is described by its strain state, its temperature and four internal variables:

| $\varepsilon$ | total strain tensor |
| --- | --- |
| $T$ | temperature |
| $p$ | cumulative plastic strain |
| ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ | plastic strain tensor |
| ${\sigma}^{p}$ | peak stress, memory of the maximum hardening |
| ${\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}$ | plastic strain tensor at the last unloading (semi-discrete variable). |

The state equations which express the associated thermodynamic forces as a function of the state variables are written:


$$
\sigma =K\text{Tr}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}})\mathrm{Id}+2\mu (\tilde{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}^{p})\quad{\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}^{\text{réf}})\text{Id}
$$



$$
R=\text{D}\left[{R}^{0}+{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)}^{a}A{({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p})}_{\text{eq}}^{a}\right]\quad\text{D}=1-m{e}^{-bp\left(1-\frac{{\sigma}^{p}}{S}\right)}
$$



$$
\mathrm{X}=\text{C}\left[S{\varepsilon}^{p}-{\sigma}^{p}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}\right]\quad\text{C}={C}_{\infty}+{C}_{1}{e}^{-bp\left(1-\frac{{\sigma}^{p}}{S}\right)}
$$


| $\tilde{a}$ | deviatoric part of a tensor $a$ |
| --- | --- |
| $R$ | isotropic hardening variable |
| $X$ | kinematic hardening variable |
| $K,\mu$ | bulk and shear moduli |
| $\alpha$ | thermal expansion coefficient |
| ${T}^{\text{réf}}$ | reference temperature |
| $S$ | ratchetting stress |
| $b,{R}^{0},A,a,m,{C}_{\infty},{C}_{1}$ | other hardening characteristics of the material |

Note that the elastic moduli and the thermal expansion coefficient are entered by the user by the command DEFI_MATERIAU, keyword ELAS, while the hardening characteristics are set by the keyword TAHERI. These characteristics may depend on the temperature, by using the keywords ELAS_FO and TAHERI_FO. Let us also specify that an example of identification of the hardening characteristics on uniaxial situations is given in Geyer .

The evolution of the internal variables is defined by two criteria. The first governs traditional plasticity with combined kinematic and isotropic hardenings:


$$
\text{F}={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}-R\le 0\text{ et }{\sigma}^{0}=\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}
$$


| ${(.)}_{\text{eq}}$ | equivalent norm: ${a}_{\text{eq}}={\left(\frac{3}{2}\tilde{a}:\tilde{a}\right)}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ |
| --- | --- |
| $\text{F}$ | plasticity criterion |
| ${s}^{0}$ | outward normal to the criterion $\text{F}$ |

This criterion is accompanied by the classical loading/unloading condition:


$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{si F}<0\text{ ou }\dot{\sigma}:{\mathrm{s}}^{0}\le 0\\ \text{si F}=0\text{ et }\dot{\sigma}:{\mathrm{s}}^{0}>0\end{array}\begin{array}{c}\text{}\dot{p}=0\\ \text{}\dot{p}\ge 0\text{ tel que }\dot{\text{F}}=0 \end{array}\begin{array}{c}\text{}(\text{élasticité})\\ (\text{plasticité})\end{array}\right.
$$


And the flow rule associated with the criterion $\text{F}$ is:


$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}{\mathrm{s}}^{0} \text{ et donc }\dot{p}=\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{eq}}^{p}
$$


The second criterion governs the evolution of the peak stress. Geometrically, in the space of the stress deviators, it expresses the fact that the first yield surface $(\text{F}=0)$ , represented by a sphere of centre $X$ and of radius $R$ , remains inside a sphere centred at the origin and of radius ${\sigma}^{p}$ . It is written simply:


$$
\text{G}={X}_{\text{eq}}+R-{\sigma}^{p}\le 0
$$


$\text{G}$ maximum-hardening criterion

According to the preceding geometric considerations, the evolution of the peak stress is:


$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{si }\text{G}<0\text{ ou }{\dot{X}}_{\text{eq}}+\dot{R}\le 0\quad{\dot{\sigma}}^{p}=0\\ \text{si }\text{G}=0\text{ et }{\dot{X}}_{\text{eq}}+\dot{R}>0\quad{\dot{\sigma}}^{p}\ge 0\text{ tel que }\dot{G}=0\end{array}\right.
$$


It must be noted that in the natural state of the material, the peak stress is not zero but is equal to the initial yield limit, namely:

${\sigma}^{p}(\text{initial})=(1-m){R}^{0}$

Up to now, we have not mentioned the evolution of the semi-discrete internal variable ${\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}$ . In fact, it evolves only in the elastic regime. More precisely, this variable takes into account the plastic strain state at the last unloading; in other words, at the beginning of each unloading, this variable should instantaneously take the value of the current plastic strain. However, in order to retain a continuous behaviour, one regularises the evolution of ${\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}$ in the following manner:

In the elastic regime:


$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{n}^{p}=\dot{\xi}({\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}-{\varepsilon}^{p})\left\{ \begin{array}{c}\text{si }{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}={\varepsilon}^{p}\\ \text{si }{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}\ge {\varepsilon}^{p}\end{array}\begin{array}{c}\quad\dot{\xi}=0\\ \quad\dot{\xi}\ge 0\text{ tel que }\dot{F}\text{=0}\end{array}\begin{array}{c}(\text{élasticité classique})\\ (\text{pseudo-décharge})\end{array}\right.
$$


In the plastic regime:

$\dot{{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}}=0$

The constitutive behaviour is thus completely determined. Before passing to the introduction of viscosity, the observation of the two yield surfaces calls for an important remark. One might think that the surface $\text{G}=0$ is effectively activated only in the plastic regime. In practice, this is not so. One may for example cite the case of a thermal loading: a cooling brings about (generally) an expansion of the yield surface $\text{F}=0$ , so that the peak stress is led to evolve in order to preserve $\text{G}\le 0$ , and this even in the elastic regime.

### 1.2 Taking viscosity into account

In order to model the constitutive behaviour of stainless steels under cyclic loading when the temperature is of the order of $550°C$ , it is no longer possible to neglect the creep terms. In order to account for these viscosity effects while retaining the properties of the preceding model, a simple method consists in making the evolution of the plastic strain viscous. In other words, viscosity intervenes only in the plastic regime: no direct influence on the semi-discrete internal variable nor on the yield surface $\text{G}=0$ . To this end, following Lemaitre and Chaboche , one replaces the consistency condition by:


$$
\dot{p}=\left(\frac{\langle \text{F}\rangle }{K{p}^{1/M}}\right)^{N}
$$


| $\langle \text{F}\rangle$ | positive part of F (Macauley brackets) |
| --- | --- |
| $K,N,M$ | viscosity characteristics of the material |

The viscosity characteristics of the material are entered in the command DEFI_MATERIAU, either by the keyword LEMAITRE if they do not depend on the temperature, or by the keyword LEMAITRE_FO in the opposite case. In the absence of one of these keywords, the constitutive behaviour is presumed plastic.

All the other equations of the model are left unchanged. One will see that such an introduction of viscosity entails only minor modifications of the implicit integration algorithm of the constitutive law.

## 2 Numerical formulation of the constitutive relation

In order to be able to treat plasticity and viscoplasticity in the same framework, one chooses to proceed to an implicit discretisation of the constitutive relations, (cf. [r5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02)). Let us note moreover that an explicit integration procedure is delicate for two reasons: on the one hand, the treatment of the semi-discrete variable is necessarily implicit and can lead to numerical oscillations (one pseudo-unloads, therefore one solves $\text{F}=0$ , and as a result, $\text{F}$ may be (very small but) greater than zero, hence loading at the next step instead of continuing the unloading), and on the other hand, the equation is not differentiable when $\varepsilon^{p} = \varepsilon_n^{\tilde{p}}$

### 2.1 Implicit discretisation of the constitutive equations

From now on, the following notation convention is adopted. If $u$ denotes a quantity, then:

| ${u}^{-}$ | quantity $u$ at the beginning of the time step |
| --- | --- |
| $\Delta\ u$ | increment of the quantity $u$ during the time step |
| $u$ | quantity $u$ at the end of the time step (no exponent $+$ ) |

Let us begin by introducing the elastic stress, that is to say the stress in the absence of a plastic-strain increment. One may moreover note that only the deviatoric term plays a role in the non-linear part of the constitutive behaviour:


$$
\sigma^e = K\,\mathrm{tr}\left(\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{\mathrm{th}}\right)\mathrm{Id} + \underbrace{2\mu\left(\tilde{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon^{\bar p}\right)}_{\tilde{\sigma}^{e}} \quad \text{et} \quad\tilde{\sigma} = \tilde{\sigma}^{e}- 2\mu\,\Delta\varepsilon^{p}
$$


Taking into account the state equations  and  and the flow rule , one has:


$$
s\stackrel{\text{déf}}{=}\tilde{\sigma}-X={\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\text{C}(S{\varepsilon}^{{p}^{-}}-{\sigma}^{p}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p})-\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +\text{C}S)\Delta p {s}^{0}
$$


Noting that ${s}^{0}$ is none other than the normalised $s$ , one immediately deduces:


$$
\left[s_{eq}+\frac{3}{2}(2\mu+CS)\Delta p\right]\,{s}^{0}   =    \underbrace{\tilde{\sigma}^{e}-C\left(S\varepsilon^{\bar p}-\sigma^{p}\varepsilon_{n}^{p}\right)}_{s^{e}}
$$


Consequently, $s$ is entirely determined by:


$$
s={s}_{\text{eq}}{s}^{0}\text{ avec }{s}^{0}=\frac{{s}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\text{ et }{s}_{\text{eq}}={s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +\text{C}S)\Delta p
$$


Finally, the loading functions are:


$$
\text{F}={s}_{\mathrm{eq}}-\text{D}\left[{R}^{0}+{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)}^{a}A{\left({\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}+\frac{3}{2}\Delta p {s}^{0}\right)}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{a}\right]
$$



$$
G=\text{C}{\left[S{\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\sigma}^{p}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}+\frac{3}{2}S\Delta p {s}^{0}\right]}_{\mathrm{eq}}+\text{D}\left[{R}^{0}+{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)}^{a}A{\left({\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}+\frac{3}{2}\Delta p {s}^{0}\right)}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{a}\right]-{\sigma}^{p}
$$


### 2.2 Taking the viscous terms into account

In the absence of viscous terms, the discretised consistency relation is:


$$
\begin{array}{cc}\text{Régime élastique :}& \text{F}\le 0\text{ et }\Delta p=0\\ \text{Régime plastique :}& \text{F}=0\text{ et }\Delta p\ge 0\end{array}
$$


On the other hand, in the presence of viscosity, the consistency condition is replaced by the equation which, discretised, is written:


$$
\frac{\Delta p }{\Delta t }={\left(\frac{\langle \text{F}\rangle }{K{p}^{1/M}}\right)}^{N}\text{}\iff \text{}\langle \text{F}\rangle =K{p}^{1/M}{\left(\frac{\Delta p }{\Delta t }\right)}^{\frac{1}{N}}
$$


In other words, by setting:


$$
\tilde{\text{F}}=\text{F}-K{p}^{1/M}{\left(\frac{\Delta p }{\Delta t }\right)}^{\frac{1}{N}}
$$


the increment of cumulative viscoplastic strain is determined by:


$$
\begin{array}{cc}\text{Régime élastique :}& \tilde{\text{F}}\le 0\text{ et }\Delta p=0\\ \text{Régime viscoplastique :}& \tilde{\text{F}}=0\text{ et }\Delta p\ge 0\end{array}
$$


Finally, by adopting an implicit discretisation, the only difference between the plastic and viscoplastic constitutive laws lies in the form of the loading function $\text{F}$ : one observes in it an additional term in the case of viscosity. In fact, incremental plasticity appears as the limiting case (without any associated numerical difficulty) of incremental viscoplasticity when the viscosity $K$ tends to zero. Let us note that this remark has already been mentioned by Chaboche et al. .

### 2.3 Discretisation of the consistency conditions

Before discretising the consistency conditions and describing the various possible constitutive-behaviour regimes, a remark is called for as regards the treatment of the semi-discrete variable. Since $\xi$ intervenes only in order to “drive” ${\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}$ , one can always reduce oneself, during a time step, to:


$$
{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}=\xi {\varepsilon}_{n}^{p-}+(1-\xi ){\varepsilon}^{p-}\quad 0\le \xi \le 1
$$


The value of $\xi$ is then fixed by the consistency conditions, which is what the evolution equation expresses at the continuous level. Such a parametrisation at each time step makes it possible to dispense with the storage of $\xi$ , provided of course that one keeps the values of ${\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}$ .

After this preliminary remark, one may turn to the consistency conditions. For the criterion $\text{G}$ which governs the evolution of the peak stress, the discretised form of the consistency condition is:


$$
\text{G}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )\le 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\ge 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\text{G}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )=0
$$


The consistency condition bearing on $\text{F}$ is more delicate insofar as it governs the evolution of the plastic strain in the plastic loading regime and the evolution of $\xi$ in the unloading regime. Once discretised, it is written:

In the plastic loading regime $(\xi =1)$ :


$$
\text{F}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)=0\quad \Delta p \ge 0\quad \Delta p \text{F}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)=0
$$


In the unloading regime $(\Delta p =0)$ :


$$
\text{F}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )=0\quad 0\le \xi \le 1\quad \xi \text{F}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )=0
$$


In order to be able to select the constitutive-behaviour regime of the material, and therefore the equations to be solved, the first question is:

Are we in a plastic situation or an elastic one?

In fact, there exists a solution in the elastic regime (pseudo-unloading $\xi >0$ or classical elasticity $\xi =0$ ) if one can find a peak-stress increment such that:

Incremental unloading condition with $\xi =1$ (scalar equation in $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ ):


$$
\begin{array}{}\text{F}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)\le 0\\ \text{G}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)\le 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\ge 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\text{G}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)=0\end{array}
$$


In the case of plastic loading, that is to say when there does not exist a $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ satisfying , one then has to solve the following non-linear system in $\Delta p$ and $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ :

Plastic regime (non-linear system in $\Delta p$ and $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ ):


$$
\begin{array}{}\text{F}(\Delta p,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =0)=0\quad \Delta p\ge 0\\ \text{G}(\Delta p,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =0)\le 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\ge 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\text{G}(\Delta p,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =0)=0\end{array}
$$


On the other hand, in an elastic situation, two choices are still possible: pseudo-unloading $(\xi >0)$ or classical elasticity $(\xi =0)$ . The second case being more favourable, one begins by examining whether it is not feasible, that is to say whether there exists a peak-stress increment such that:

Incremental condition of the classical elastic regime (scalar equation in $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ ):


$$
\begin{array}{}\text{F}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)\le 0\\ \text{G}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)\le 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\ge 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\text{G}(\Delta p=0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi =1)=0\end{array}
$$


Finally, should it be a pseudo-elastic unloading, it remains to solve the following non-linear system in $\xi$ and $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ :

Pseudo-elastic unloading (non-linear system in $\xi$ and $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ ):


$$
\begin{array}{}\text{F}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )=0\quad 0\le \xi \le 1\\ \text{G}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )\le 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\ge 0\quad \Delta {\sigma}^{p}\text{G}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )=0\end{array}
$$


Let us note from the outset that the non-linear systems  and  can reduce to the solution of a simple scalar equation if $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}=0$ makes it possible to obtain a solution.

### 2.4 Bracketing of the solutions

On reading the preceding paragraph, one will have noted the necessity of solving (numerically) a certain number of scalar equations or of non-linear systems. For this, it is always useful to have an interval on which to seek the solution. On the one hand, a bracketing of the solution demonstrates its existence (which greatly strengthens the chances of success of a solution algorithm!), and on the other hand, it allows an appropriate numerical treatment, hence a safer one.

Concerning $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ , a lower bound is of course 0. Moreover, the ratchetting stress $S$ represents a limit beyond which the model no longer has any meaning. In fact, on examination of the material constants obtained by identification, cf. Taheri et al. , $\text{G}$ indeed becomes negative when ${\sigma}^{p}=S$ if the gap between the plastic strain and the plastic strain at the last unloading is not too large (a few %):


$$
\frac{G\left(\Delta p,\sigma^p=S,\xi\right)}{S} = \underbrace{(C_\infty+C_1)}_{\approx 13} \underbrace{\left(\varepsilon^p-\varepsilon_n^p\right)_{eq}}_{\le 3\%} + \underbrace{(1-m)}_{\approx 0,75} \left(\underbrace{\frac{R^0}{S}}_{\approx 20\%} + \underbrace{\left(\frac{2}{3}\right)^a\frac{A}{S}}_{\approx 0,5} \underbrace{\left(\varepsilon^p-\varepsilon_n^p\right)_{eq}^{a}}_{\approx 0,7} \right) -1 \le 0
$$


One may also seek an upper bound for $\Delta p$ . By examining the expression of $\text{F}$ :


$$
\begin{array}{}\text{F}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )\le {s}_{\text{eq}}-\text{D}{R}^{0}\\ \text{}\le {s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +\text{C}S)\Delta p -\text{D}{R}^{0}\\ \text{}\le {s}_{\max}^{e}-\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +{C}_{\infty}S)\Delta p -\text{D}({p}^{-}){R}^{0}\end{array}
$$


One deduces from it an upper bound for $\Delta p$ , such that $\text{F}(\Delta p ,\Delta {\sigma}^{p},\xi )\le 0$ :


$$
\Delta p \le \frac{{s}_{\max}^{e}({\sigma}^{p})-\text{D}({p}^{-},{\sigma}^{p}){R}^{0}}{\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +{C}_{\infty}S)}
$$



$$
{s}_{\max}^{e}({\sigma}^{p})=\max\left\{ \begin{array}{c}{\left[{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\text{C}({p}^{-},{\sigma}^{p})(S{\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\sigma}^{p}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p})\right]}_{\text{eq}}\\ {\left[{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-{C}_{\infty}(S{\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\sigma}^{p}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p})\right]}_{\text{eq}}\end{array}\right.
$$


In particular, one can give a (crude) upper bound of $\Delta p$ independent of $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}$ :


$$
\Delta {p}_{\max}=\frac{{s}_{\max}^{e\max}-(1-m){R}^{0}}{\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +{C}_{\infty}S)}
$$



$$
{s}_{\max}^{e\max}={\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}+({C}_{1}+{C}_{\infty})\max\left\{\begin{array}{}{\left[S{\varepsilon}^{p-}-{\sigma}^{p-}{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}\right]}_{\mathrm{eq}}\\ {\left[S{\varepsilon}^{p-}-S{\varepsilon}_{n}^{p}\right]}_{\mathrm{eq}}\end{array}\right.
$$


One may then note that the systems  and  always admit a solution. Indeed, if for each system one writes respectively $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\Delta p )$ and $\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\xi )$ the solutions of $\text{G}=0$ , then one has:

- Non-linear system of plastic loading:


$$
\text{F}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\Delta p =0),\xi =1)\ge 0\text{ et }\text{F}(\Delta {p}_{\max},\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\Delta {p}_{\max}),\xi =1)\le 0
$$


- Non-linear system of pseudo-unloading:


$$
\text{F}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\xi =1),\xi =1)\le 0\text{ et }\text{F}(\Delta p =0,\Delta {\sigma}^{p}(\xi =0),\xi =0)\ge 0
$$


## 3 Numerical solution methods

The solution of the incremental equations confronts us either with a non-linear scalar equation, or with a non-linear system with two unknowns. The numerical methods employed are set out below. One also examines the computation of the tangent matrix, possibly used by the global algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE, (cf. [r5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)).

### 3.1 Scalar equation: secant method

The aim is to solve a non-linear scalar equation by seeking the solution in a confidence interval. For this, one proposes to couple a secant method with a control of the search interval. Let the following equation to be solved:


$$
\text{f}(x)=0\quad x\in \left[a,b\right]\quad \text{f}(a)<0\text{}\text{f}(b)>0
$$


The secant method consists in constructing a sequence of points ${x}^{n}$ which converges towards the solution. It is defined by recurrence (linear approximation of the function by its chord):


$$
{x}^{n+1}={x}^{n-1}-\text{f}({x}^{n-1})\frac{{x}^{n}-{x}^{n-1}}{\text{f}({x}^{n})-\text{f}({x}^{n-1})}
$$


Moreover, if ${x}^{n+1}$ were to leave the interval, then one replaces it by the bound of the interval in question:


$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{si }{x}^{n+1}<a\text{ alors }{x}^{n+1}:=a\\ \text{si }{x}^{n+1}>b\text{ alors }{x}^{n+1}:=b\end{array}\right.
$$


On the other hand, if ${x}^{n+1}$ is in the current interval, then one updates the interval:


$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}\text{si }{x}^{n+1}\in \left[a,b\right]\text{ et f}({x}^{n+1})<0\text{ alors }a:={x}^{n+1}\\ \text{si }{x}^{n+1}\in \left[a,b\right]\text{ et f}({x}^{n+1})>0\text{ alors }b:={x}^{n+1}\end{array}\right.
$$


One considers that one has converged when $\text{f}$ is sufficiently close to 0 (tolerance to be entered). As for the first two points of the sequence, one may choose the bounds of the interval, or else, if one has an estimate of the solution, one may adopt this estimate and one of the bounds of the interval.

### 3.2 Non-linear systems: Newton method and line search

A Newton method is presented here, to which a line-search technique and a control of the descent direction have been associated so as not to leave the search domain (bounds on the unknowns).

Let the following non-linear system:


$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{c}\text{F}(x,y)=0\\ \text{G}(x,y)=0\end{array}\right. \text{ avec }\left\{ \begin{array}{c}{x}_{\min}\le x\le {x}_{\max}\\ {y}_{\min}\le y\le {y}_{\max}\end{array}\right.
$$


If $(x,y)$ is a point of the search domain, then one constructs a sequence of points $({x}^{n},{y}^{n})$ which converges towards a solution (or at least, one hopes so) by the following process.

- Determination of the descent direction

A descent direction $(\delta x ,\delta y )$ is given by the solution of the 2 x 2 linear system:


$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{F}_{,x}^{n}& {F}_{,y}^{n}\\ {G}_{,x}^{n}& {G}_{,y}^{n}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta x \\ \delta y \end{array}\right]=-\left[\begin{array}{c}{F}^{n}\\ {G}^{n}\end{array}\right]
$$


- Correction of the descent direction

One corrects the descent direction $(\delta x ,\delta y )$ so that the points envisaged are in the search domain (with ${\rho}_{\max}$ the maximum length that one allows oneself to travel along the descent direction):


$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{cccc}\text{si }x+{\rho}_{\max}\delta x<{x}_{\min}& \delta x:=\frac{{x}_{\min}-x}{{\rho}_{\max}}& \text{si }x+{\rho}_{\max}\delta x>{x}_{\max}& \delta x:=\frac{{x}_{\max}-x}{{\rho}_{\max}}\\ \text{si }y+{\rho}_{\max}\Delta y<{y}_{\min}& \delta y:=\frac{{y}_{\min}-y}{{\rho}_{\max}}& \text{si }y+{\rho}_{\max}\delta y>{y}_{\max}& \delta y:=\frac{{y}_{\max}-y}{{\rho}_{\max}}\end{array}\right.
$$


- Line search

It only remains to minimise the quantity $\text{E}=({\text{F}}^{2}+{\text{G}}^{2})/2$ in the descent direction. Let us note that the norm $\text{E}$ which one thus minimises is a measure of the error committed in the solution of the system: it is zero when $(x,y)$ is a solution of the system . In order to minimise $\text{E}$ , one will simply seek to cancel its derivative, that is to say to solve the scalar equation:


$$
\underbrace{\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho}\left[E\left(x+\rho\,\delta x,\; y+\rho\,\delta y\right)\right]}_{\left(FF_{,x}+GG_{,x}\right)\delta x + \left(FF_{,y}+GG_{,y}\right)\delta y } = 0 \quad \text{et} \quad 0 \le \rho \le \rho_{\max}
$$


- Convergence criterion

One considers that one has converged when the error $\text{E}$ is less than a prescribed quantity (grandeur). Moreover, if the norm of the descent direction becomes too small (another quantity (grandeur) to be entered), one may think that the algorithm does not manage to converge.

### 3.3 Stopping criteria

Up to now, the stopping values and the maximum numbers of iterations of the preceding solution methods have not been specified. Two cases must be distinguished.

- When one seeks to verify the consistency conditions (scalar equation or non-linear system depending on the situation), one expects accurate results, whose relative tolerance $\eta$ is set by the user in the command STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword RESI_INTE_RELA, (cf. [u4.32.01](doc:u4.32.01)). Depending on whether one seeks to solve $\text{F}=0,\text{G}=0$ or simultaneously $\text{F}=\text{G}=0$ , the stopping criterion is expressed respectively:

$\left|\frac{F}{R^0}\right| \le \eta \;\text{ou}\; \left|\frac{G}{R^0}\right| \le \eta \;\text{ou}\; \frac{1}{R^0}\sqrt{\frac{F^2+G^2}{2}} \le \eta$

${R}^{0}$ initial yield limit, supplied by the user, (cf. r5.03.01-comportement-plastique).

Moreover, the user specifies a maximum number of iterations, still in the command STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword ITER_INTE_MAXI, (cf. [u4.32.01](doc:u4.32.01)).

- When one carries out line-search iterations, one seeks to obtain a faster convergence (or at least a more reliable one). One must not however devote an excessive time to it. This is why one has fixed once and for all a maximum number of iterations equal to 3, a maximum bound ${\rho}_{\max}$ equal to 2 and a relative stopping criterion of 1%:

${\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho }\left[\text{E}(x+\rho \delta x,y+\rho \delta y)\right]\mid }_{\rho}\le {10}^{-2}{\frac{\partial}{\partial \rho }\left[\text{E}(x+\rho \delta x,y+\rho \delta y)\right]\mid }_{\rho =0}$

### 3.4 Tangent matrix

With a view to a solution of the (global) equilibrium equations by a Newton method, it is indispensable to determine the consistent matrix of the tangent constitutive behaviour, (cf. Simo et al. ). This matrix is classically composed of an elastic contribution and a plastic contribution:


$$
\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }-2\mu \frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \varepsilon }
$$


One immediately deduces that in the elastic regime (classical or pseudo-unloading), the tangent matrix reduces to the elastic matrix:

Elastic regime:


$$
\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }
$$


On the other hand, in the plastic regime, the variation of the plastic strain is no longer zero. The rules of composite differentiation make it possible to obtain:


$$
\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}}=\frac{3}{2}\left[{s}^{0}\otimes \frac{\delta p }{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}}+\Delta p \frac{\delta {s}^{0}}{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}}\right]=\frac{3}{2}\left[{s}^{0}\otimes \frac{\delta p }{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}+\frac{\Delta p }{{s}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}}\left(\mathrm{Id}-\frac{3}{2}{s}^{0}\otimes {s}^{0}\right)\right]
$$


$\otimes$ tensor product

One may note that one has preferred to differentiate with respect to $\tilde{\sigma}^{e}$, knowing that one has:


$$
\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}.\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }=2\mu \frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}}.P\text{ avec }P:\left\{ \begin{array}{}S\to S\\ \varepsilon \to \tilde{\varepsilon}\end{array}\right.
$$


$S$ space of symmetric tensors

$P$ projector onto the deviators

Finally, it only remains to compute the variation of $p$ . For this, one must distinguish whether it is a classical plasticity regime $(\Delta {\sigma}^{p}=0)$ or a two-surface plasticity. Thus:

Classical plasticity: $\text{F}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})=0$


$$
{\text{F}}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})\delta p =-{\text{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}\frac{\delta p }{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=-\frac{{\text{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}{{\text{F}}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}
$$


Two-surface plasticity: $\text{F}(p,{\sigma}^{p},{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})=0\text{ et }\text{G}(p,{\sigma}^{p},{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})=0$


$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\text{F}}_{,p}& {\text{F}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}\\ {\text{G}}_{,p}& {\text{G}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta p \\ \delta {\sigma}^{p}\end{array}\right]=-\left[\begin{array}{c}{\text{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\\ {\text{G}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\end{array}\right].\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}\frac{\delta p }{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=\frac{\left|\begin{array}{cc}{\text{F}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}& {\text{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\\ {\text{G}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}& {\text{G}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\end{array}\right|}{\left|\begin{array}{cc}{\text{F}}_{,p}& {\text{F}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}\\ {\text{G}}_{,p}& {\text{G}}_{,{\sigma}^{p}}\end{array}\right|}
$$


A careful examination of the expressions  and  makes it possible to observe that the variations of $\text{F}$ and $\text{G}$ with respect to $\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}$ are not necessarily collinear with ${s}^{0}$ . Taking  into account, one then deduces that the tangent matrix is in general not symmetric in the plastic regime. Rather than imposing the use of a non-symmetric solver, much more costly in computation time, one prefers to symmetrise this matrix.

### 3.5 Plane stresses

The treatment of plane stresses adds a non-linear equation to be solved, coupled to the systems  and , (cf. [r5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02)). Faced with this non-negligible difficulty and the absence of any expressed need, one has preferred not to offer the possibility of forcing a state of plane stresses at the level of the constitutive law. In other words, the modelling C_PLAN is not available for the constitutive law VISC_TAHERI.
