# v7.22.137 FORMA41 - Practical work of the Civil Engineering training: taking into account withdrawals in the study of a 3 point bending beam

**Summary:**

This $\mathrm{3D}$ test illustrates the questions of modelling the removals in concrete on a simple case basis, and highlights the effect of drying and temperature on stress distribution.

It is a reinforced concrete beam subjected to a 3-point bending to which is added thermal and drying stress.

The objective of the test is to show the possibilities of Modelling withdrawals by thermal (THER_NON_LINE) and drying (SECH_NON_LINE) and mechanical MECA_STATIQUE.

Model A is the calculation of the elasticity bending. It is used as a comparison for the other modellings.

Model B is the calculation with regard to desiccation removal, endogenous withdrawal and thermal expansion.

### 1.2 Initial condition and thermal loading

The initial temperature is uniform to $20°C$ . Concrete is subjected to thermo-hydration.

The temperature is imposed on $20°C$ on the $\mathrm{EFGH}$ face, which is HAUT in the mesh.

The temperature is constant at $20°C$ for up to 10 days and then varies linearly from $20\mathrm{°}\mathrm{C}$ to $40\mathrm{°}\mathrm{C}$ for 10 days to 30 days, then is constant at $40\mathrm{°}\mathrm{C}$ on the $\mathrm{ABCD}$ face, rated BAS in the mesh.

### 1.3 Initial condition and drying loads

The initial water concentration is uniform at $120l/{m}^{3}$ .

The water concentration is imposed on $50l/{m}^{3}$ on the $\mathrm{EFGH}$ face, which is HAUT.

The water concentration is imposed on $70l/{m}^{3}$ on the $\mathrm{ABCD}$ side.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and mechanical loadings

**Conditions of symmetry**

The plate is blocked following $\mathrm{Ox}$ on the $\mathrm{BCGF}$ side noted SYME_X and following $\mathrm{Oz}$ on the $\mathrm{ABFE}$ side noted SYME_Z .

**Limited**

The plate is blocked following $\mathrm{Oy}$ on the $\mathrm{HE}$ side rated APPUI.

**Charge**

It is subjected to $\frac{F}{4}=3840N$ force following $\mathrm{Oy}$ distributed on the $\mathrm{BC}$ side, which is equivalent to a total $F=15360N$ force on the entire beam. The force is applied at the final moment of each calculation (in Modelling A to time step performed only and in Modelling B to $t=100\mathit{jours}$).

### 1.5 Thermal properties of materials

The characteristics of the concrete are:

- $\rho {C}_{p}=2.4{e}^{6}J/{m}^{3}/°C$ thermal capacity;
- $\lambda =1W/m/°C$ conductivity;

and the following characteristics relating to moisturizing behaviour:

- heat per degree of hydration: ${Q}_{0}={1.14e}^{8}J/{m}^{3}$
- affinity function of hydration (polynomial evaluation of known function by point) and temperature (naple):

$A(\xi ,T)=(50.12{\xi}^{6}-190.76{\xi}^{5}+258.38{\xi}^{4}-123.71{\xi}^{3}-11.82{\xi}^{2}+15.37\xi +2.43)\exp(\frac{-{\mathit{QSR}}_{K}}{(273.15+T)})$

- with constant d-Arrhenius: ${\mathit{QSR}}_{K}=4000K$ .

**Note:** The constant d-Arrhenius is always expressed in Kelvin degree. Temperatures are expressed in $°C$ .

### 1.6 Properties of drying materials

The SECH_GRANGER scattering law is used:

$D(C,T)=A\cdot \exp(\mathit{BC})\frac{T}{{T}_{0}}\exp\left[-\frac{{Q}_{s}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{{T}_{0}}\right)\right]$

- $A=3.3e-13{m}^{2}/s$ ;
- $B=0.05$ ;
- $\mathit{QSR}=4000K$
- ${T}_{0}=293°K=20°C$

### 1.7 Mechanical properties of materials

The behavior is elastic.

The characteristics of steels are:

- Young's modulus ${E}_{a}=200000\mathrm{MPa}$ ;
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.3$ ;

The characteristics of the concrete are:

- Young's modulus ${E}_{b}=32000\mathit{MPa}$ for Modelling A. In Modelling B, it varies linearly according to the temperature from ${E}_{b}=30000\mathrm{MPa}$ for $T=0°C$ to ${E}_{b}=40000\mathit{MPa}$ for $T=100°C$;
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.2$ ;
- $\alpha =1.2e-6$ thermal dilation at the reference temperature of ${T}_{\mathrm{ref}}=20°C$
- ${K}_{\mathit{des}}=8e-6$ Desiccation Removal Coefficient
- Endogenous withdrawal coefficient ${B}_{\mathit{endo}}=9e-5$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Elastic solution without withdrawal

The following assumptions are made:

- sections of the beams remain flat
- there is perfect adhesion between concrete and steel
- the behavior is linear elastic

![Figure](/figures/v7.22.137/1000000000000264000001CE80751FB5BF3E6AD8.png)

The position of the ${y}_{n}$ beam neutral axis can therefore be evaluated by the position of the steels. The following equation must be solved:

$$
{y}_{n}\left(2\frac{n}{b}\left({A}_{s}+{A}_{S}'\right)+2h\right)-\left(2\frac{n}{b}\left({A}_{S}'c'+{A}_{s}\left(h-c\right)\right)+{h}^{2}\right)=0
$$

With:

${A}_{s}$ and ${A}_{s}'$: steel surfaces, tensioned and compressed respectively in section

$c'$ and $c'$: concrete coating below the tensioned steels and above the compressed steels, respectively

$b$ and $h$: width and height of beam section respectively

$n=\frac{{E}_{a}}{{E}_{b}}$: ratio between concrete and steel Young's modulus

The ${\sigma}_{\mathit{xxC}}$ longitudinal compression stress as a function of the bending moment and the neutral axis position.

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{xxC}}=\frac{\mathit{Mf}}{\left({A}_{s}n\frac{{\left(h-c-{y}_{n}\right)}^{2}}{{y}_{n}}+{A}_{s}'n\frac{{\left({y}_{n}-c'\right)}^{2}}{{y}_{n}}+\frac{{\mathit{by}}_{n}^{2}}{3}+b\frac{{\left(h-{y}_{n}\right)}^{3}}{{y}_{n}}\right)}
$$

$$
\mathit{Mf}(x)=\frac{\mathit{Fx}}{2}
$$

The digital application gives:

$$
{y}_{n}=0.2674
$$

And in $x=2.5m$ and $y=0,25m$ , the maximum compression stress is:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{xxC}}=-2.05\mathit{MPa}
$$

Using the linearity of the stresses in the section, the maximum tensile stress is

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{xxT}}=1.78\mathit{MPa}
$$

### 2.2 Solution with withdrawals

Deformities due to desiccation removal and thermal expansion can easily be assessed where water concentration and temperature values are known. For endogenous withdrawal, the degree of hydration is estimated from the interpolation used to define hydration parameters.

- en $G$ à $t>30j$ :

$T=20°C$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})=0$

$C=50l/{m}^{3}$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{sec}}=-{K}_{\mathit{des}}({C}_{0}-C)=-5.6e-4$

$\xi =0,95$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{endo}}=-{B}_{\mathit{endo}}\xi =-\mathrm{8,55e-5}$

- en $G$ à $t>30j$ :

$T=40°C$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})=2.4e-5$

$C=70l/{m}^{3}$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{sec}}=-{K}_{\mathit{des}}({C}_{0}-C)=-4e-4$

$\xi =0,95$ so ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{endo}}=-{B}_{\mathit{endo}}\xi =-\mathrm{8,55e-5}$

$G$ stresses are not calculated analytically but are calculated on a very fine mesh (32000 HEXA20 and 155369 nodes).

## 5 Implementation of TP

### 5.1 Objectives of the TP

The aim is to complete a calculation that links thermo-hydration, drying and mechanical.

This TP allows:

- implement thermal and drying calculation in Code_Aster: management of loading, materials, behaviour and parameters of THER_NON_LINE and SECH_NON_LINE;
- understand and implement the concept of command variable;
- define parameters of an constitutive law that depend on command variables;

### 5.4 Step two: addition of the thermo-hydration calculation and consideration of the withdrawal of hydration

The objective here is to perform the thermo-hydration calculation prior to the mechanical calculation and then to take into account the thermal expansion and endogenous withdrawal in the mechanical calculation.

It is recalled that the idea is to do the thermal calculation with a linear mesh and the mechanical calculation with the quadratic mesh, which involves a projection step.

Step 1: Performing the thermohydration calculation (Stage 1)

| Create a new linear mesh from the initial quadratic mesh with CREA_MAILLAGE. (Note: the linear mesh can also be, or have been, obtained in Mesh) |
| --- |
| Create a model THERMIQUE using 3D_DIAG Modelling. Note: thermal calculation will only be done on concrete (steels are not taken into account). |
| Set the thermal parameters in DEFI_MATERIAU (*l'user can indifferently complete* *t* *er* *un* *concept existing or create* *un* *new* ). For the THER_NL law that simulates thermo-hydration, you must define: - conductivity, and heat dehydration of concrete - affinities and enthalpie functions using CALC_FONC_INTERP commance. Assign the material to the mesh cells concrete on the linear mesh or thermal model. |
| Set thermal loading on the affected faces in AFFE_CHAR_CINE_F with THER_IMPO. |
| Set a list of moments from 0 to 100 days that goes through 10 days and 30 days (attention list defined in seconds, use a sufficiently fine discretization especially in the first 10 days when the temperature varies quickly). Setting a list of archiving can also be useful to not store all time step for mechanical calculation. |
| Use THER_NON_LINE to solve the thermal problem using the THER_HYDR relationship |
| Calculate the node hydration field with CALC_CHAMP. |

Step 2: Update of the mechanical calculation (Stage 2)

| Create a temperature function defining the Young's modulus. |
| --- |
| Change the mechanical parameters in DEFI_MATERIAU with ELAS_FO. Add the endogenous expansion and withdrawal coefficient. |
| Use PROJ_CHAM to project the thermal results from the linear mesh to the quadratic mesh. |
| Add temperature and hydration control variables associated with the result to AFFE_MATERIAU. Do not forget to specify the reference values. |
| Set a list of relevant moments and load function for the already present MECA_STATIQUE command. Delete the bending load and stop the calculation one step before the end. |
| Add a MECA_STATIQUE command with the bending load that calculates the last moment. |
| Add the calculation of the EPVC_ELNO option in CALC_CHAM that calculates the deformations due to withdrawals. |
| Start the calculation and visualize the results. Do not hesitate to make vary the time discretization to check the relevance of the solution. |

## 6 Summary of the results

This test shows how to conduct a chained calculation of thermal, drying and mechanical on reinforced concrete. One can see, among other things, the increase in skin stresses due to the removal of desiccation.
