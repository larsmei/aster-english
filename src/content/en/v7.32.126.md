# v7.32.126 WTNP126 - Gas injection into a fractured porous massif

**Summary:**

The test presented here allows to check the correct functioning of the elements gas-saturated gas-hydromechanical coupling joints (law THM 'GAZ').

The injection of dihydrogen into a rock mass is modelled. It consists of two elastic parts of different permeability separated by a hydraulic joint. The constitutive laws interface used are the cubic law for flow and the law of Bandis for mechanics.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The field studied consists of two porous ($\mathrm{AEFD}$ and $\mathrm{EBCF}$ ) masses separated by a hydraulic joint. The game between the structure and the gas injection wall ($\mathrm{AGIB}$) is also modelled.

## 2 reference solution

We're doing non-regression tests.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The Modelling is performed in flat deformation with 100 elements QU4 for the game, 3202 elements TRI3 for the mass and 100 elements QU4 for discontinuity.

Time Discretization: 24 time step for a 1000-year simulation.

**Note:** The test is D_PLAN_THMS type but the thermal part is set so that the result is identical to the thermal-free case, to verify computerically that elements D_PLAN_THMS are correct.

### 3.2 Tested quantities and results

The figure shows the pressure profiles on the $x=4m$ vertical cut at different times. It shows the hydraulic influence of the crack. In fact, in the vicinity of the crack there are very large pressure gradients which correspond to flows directed towards each of the two massives. Moreover, the effect of the difference in permeability between the two massives is also seen. In accordance with expectations, the pressure is more rapidly uniform in the upper mass, of higher permeability.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The simulation characteristics are identical to those of the above Modelling but the interface becomes impervious across the board. Hydraulic Lagrange multipliers that control the equal pressure through the interface are required to be equal to zero. To do this, the AFFE_CHAR_CINE command is used with the keyword MECA_IMPO to put the degree of freedom $\mathrm{LH1}$ at 0 on the crack.

### 4.2 Tested quantities and results

The figure shows the pressure profiles along the vertical cut $x=4m$ at different times. It is clear that there is no exchange between the two massives and the crack. Moreover, the pressure fronts move at a different speed in each mass because of the difference in permeability.

## 5 Summary of the results

The element gasket with hydromechanical coupling and its compatibility with 'GAZ' coupling law and elements solid HM_DPTR6S is tested. The case of waterproof or non-waterproof gasket is considered.

In both cases, the expected results are obtained qualitatively.
