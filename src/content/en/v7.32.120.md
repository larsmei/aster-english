# v7.32.120 WTNP120 - Phase Appearance/Disappearance in a Diphasic Flow: Gas Injection in a Bar Saturated in Pure Water

**Summary:**

This test represents the simulation of gas injection into a saturated geological environment.

The purpose is to model and simulate the appearance of gas and the evolution of a water/hydrogen diphasic flow in a porous medium initially saturated with pure water. A "quasi"-1D situation is considered where the effects of gravity are neglected. The test case is presented in a 2D configuration but can be treated in an equivalent way as a 1D problem.

This is a purely miscible hydraulic calculation. The geometry represented corresponds to a bar. The transfer terms are described by a model of Mualem Van-Genuchten. The problem is dealt with by the various diagrams available for the modelling of diphasic flows: the classic finished elements, the Decent Arest Finished Volumes, the mesh cell Decented Finished Volumes and mesh cell Centres Finished Volumes.

| Initial status | Hair pressure $(\mathit{Pa})$Gas pressure $(\mathit{Pa})$ | $P_{c}^{0} = -10^{-6}$$P_{gz} = 0$ |

The saturation and permeability curves follow the Mualem-Van-Genuchten (HYDR_VGM) model. Therefore, the parameters $n$ , $\mathit{Pr}$ , $\mathit{Sr}$ , $\mathit{Smax}$ are defined in the materials. It is recalled that these models are:

$$
{S}_{le}=\frac{{S}_{l}-{S}_{\mathit{lr}}}{1-{S}_{\mathit{lr}}}
$$

Water permeability is expressed by integrating the prediction model proposed by Mualem (1976) into the Van Genuchten capillary model:

$$
{k}_{r}^{g}=\sqrt{(1-{S}_{le})}{(1-{S}_{le}^{\frac{1}{m}})}^{2m}
$$

Gas permeability is expressed in a similar way:

$$
{k}_{r}^{w}=\sqrt{(1-{S}_{we})}{(1-{S}_{we}^{1/m})}^{\mathrm{2m}}
$$

It is recalled that for $S>\mathit{Smax}$, these curves are interpolated by a two-degree polynomial ${C}^{1}$ in $\mathit{Smax}$.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and initials

The limit conditions are as follows:

- Neumann's conditions on the top and bottom of the estate:

$\begin{array}{c}({F}_{l}^{w}+{F}_{g}^{w})\cdot n=0\\ ({F}_{l}^{c}+{F}_{g}^{c})\cdot n=0\end{array}$

- Neumann conditions on the left side of the estate:

If $0<t<\mathit{TSIM}$ then $({F}_{l}^{w}+{F}_{g}^{w})\cdot n=0$

If $0<t<\mathit{TINJ}$ then $({F}_{l}^{c}+{F}_{g}^{c})\cdot n=Q$

If $\mathit{TINJ}<t<\mathit{TSIM}$ then $({F}_{l}^{c}+{F}_{g}^{c})\cdot n=0$

- Dirichlet conditions on the right side of the estate:

$\begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}(x=200,y,t)={10}^{-6}\mathit{Pa}\\ {P}_{g}(x=200,y,t)=0\mathit{Pa}\end{array}$

The initial conditions are as follows:

$\begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}(x,y,t=0)={10}^{-6}\mathit{Pa}\\ {P}_{g}(x,y,t=0)=0\mathit{Pa}\end{array}$

The hydrogen flow imposed on the left side, $Q$ , is:

$Q=1,76{.10}^{-13}\mathit{kg}/{m}^{2}s$

The injection time, $\mathit{TINJ}$ is $5.{10}^{5}\mathit{ans}$ and the simulation time is ${10}^{6}\mathit{ans}$.

## 10 Summary of the results

This test allows to reproduce a classic case of the literature and the Modelling of storage: the injection of gas into a saturated medium. We do not have precise reference solutions to which we compare, however the values and the look of the results are in line with those of the literature. So we make it a test case of non-regression.

This test case allows testing the various diagrams available for modelling the 2D and 3D diphasic flow:

- The classic elements finish
- Decentralized finite volumes (modelization *_HH2SUDA)

These patterns all give very close results.
