# r4.07.02 Modelling of turbulent excitations

**Summary:**

The modelling of the turbulent excitations available in *Code_Aster* is described, together with the way in which they are taken into account in a dynamic analysis. The turbulent excitations are characterised by a spectral density of forces, specified by means of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB [U4.44.31](doc:u4.44.31). Their taking into account in a dynamic analysis is done by projection of the spectrum onto the modal basis of the structure whose response one wishes to compute. The projection operations are carried out by means of the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE [U4. 63.14].

> **Contents**

## 1 Principle of the analysis

### 1.1 Determination of a modal basis of the system under flow and projection of the excitation

The computation of the dynamic response of a system to a turbulent excitation induced by a fluid flow is carried out by following the following stages:

|  | first, one computes the modal basis of the system out of flow by means of the operator CALC_MODES[U4.52.02], |
| --- | --- |
|  | one then defines the characteristics of the configuration studied, for taking into account the fluid-structure coupling phenomenon, by means of the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU[U4.25.01]. This operator makes it possible, for example, to supply the velocity profiles associated with the fluid-excitation zones, for configurations of the type ‘tube bundle under cross-flow’. It produces a concept of type [type_flui_stru] intended to be used by the operators implemented downstream in the command file, |
|  | the modal characteristics of the system under flow are then computed by means of the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU[U4.66.02]. One has as output a modal basis for each flow velocity, |
|  | the definition of the turbulent excitation is then done by a call to the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB[U4.44.31]. The modellings available are the following: |

- spectra of “correlation length” type, specific to configurations of the type “tube bundle under cross-flow”, for application to the vibrations of steam-generator tubes. The corresponding factor keywords are SPEC_LONG_COR_1, SPEC_LONG_COR_2, SPEC_LONG_COR_3 and SPEC_LONG_COR_4. These spectra are predefined; however, the user can adjust their parameters. This part is developed in paragraph [§2.2],
- distributed turbulent-excitation model. The corresponding factor keyword is SPEC_FONC_FORME. The excitation spectrum is defined by its decomposition on a family of shape functions by supplying, on the one hand a cross-spectral matrix, and on the other hand a list of shape functions associated with this matrix. The associated concepts [interspectre] and [fonction] must be generated upstream. In the case of the component “control-rod cluster”, the user can also use a predefined turbulence spectrum, identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1. This part is developed in paragraph [§2.3],
- localised turbulent-excitation model. The corresponding factor keyword is SPEC_EXCI_POINT. It is used in the case of an excitation spectrum associated with one or more point forces and moments. The definition of the excitation is then done by supplying:
- a cross-spectral matrix of excitations (the associated concept [interspectre] must be generated upstream),
- the list of the nodes of application of these excitations,
- the nature of the excitation applied at each of these nodes (force or moment),
- the directions of application of the excitations thus defined.

This part is developed in paragraph [§2.4].

|  | The projection of the previously defined turbulent-excitation spectrum onto the modal basis of the structure under flow is then carried out by means of the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE[U4.63.14]. |

### 1.2 Computation of the response to the turbulent excitation: frequency solution

#### 1.2.1 Introduction

The computation of the frequency response of the structure or of the coupled fluid-structure system is done in three stages:

1. computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations,
2. computation of the cross-spectra of modal response,
3. recombination on the physical basis.

First, one introduces for each mode the transfer function of the mechanical system (structure alone or coupled fluid-structure system). Each of the three stages above is then set out in detail.

#### 1.2.2 Computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations

The cross-spectra of modal excitations ${S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)$ are determined by projection of the turbulent-excitation spectrum onto the modal basis of the mechanical system (structure alone or coupled fluid-structure system). This projection stage is set out in paragraph [§2] for the various models applicable to line structures.

#### 1.2.3 Computation of the cross-spectra of modal response

The cross-spectra of modal displacements ${S}_{\text{qiqj}}(f,U)$ are then deduced from the cross-spectra of modal excitations ${S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)$ by means of the following relation:

${S}_{{q}_{i}{q}_{j}}(f,U)={H}_{i}^{\ast }(f,U){S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U){H}_{j}(f,U)$ **eq 1.2.3-1**

where ${H}_{i}^{\text{*}}(f,U)$ denotes the complex conjugate of the transfer function ${H}_{i}(f,U)$ of the mechanical system considered. Given a frequency $f$ and a flow velocity $U$ , the transfer function ${H}_{i}(f,U)$ of the mechanical system for the mode $i$ is defined by:

${H}_{i}(f,U)=\frac{1}{{M}_{i}{\omega}_{i}^{2}(-{(\frac{f}{{f}_{i}})}^{2}+\mathrm{2j}{\xi}_{i}(\frac{f}{{f}_{i}})+1)}$ **eq 1.2.3-2**

where ${M}_{i}$ denotes the modal mass of the mode $i,{\omega}_{i}$ and ${f}_{i}$ denote respectively, at the velocity $U$ , the angular frequency and the natural frequency of the mode $i,{\xi}_{i}$ denotes, at the velocity $U$ , the damping ratio of the mode $i$ , and $J$ denotes the complex number such that ${J}^{2}\text{= -}1$ .

The computation of the cross-spectra of modal displacements from the cross-spectra of modal excitations and from the transfer functions is carried out by means of the operator DYNA_SPEC_MODAL [U4.53.23](doc:u4.53.23).

One deduces in particular from [eq 1.2.3-2] the relation linking the auto-spectra of modal displacements to the auto-spectra of modal excitations:

${S}_{\text{qiqi}}(f,U)={\mid {H}_{i}(f,U)\mid }^{2}{S}_{\text{QiQi}}(f,U)$ **eq 1.2.3-3**

where ${\mid {H}_{i}(f,U)\mid }^{2}$ denotes the square of the modulus of ${H}_{i}(f,U)$

#### 1.2.4 Recombination on the physical basis

Given a flow velocity $U$ , the cross-spectrum of physical displacement ${S}_{{u}_{1}{u}_{2}}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},f)$ at the points of abscissae ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ , at the frequency $f$ , is obtained by modal recombination. This operation is written:

${S}_{{u}_{1}{u}_{2}}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},f)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}{\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{j}({x}_{2}){S}_{{q}_{i}{q}_{j}}(f,U)$ **eq 1.2.4-1**

Where $N$ denotes the number of modes of the basis; ${\phi }_{i}({x}_{k})$ is the component at the discretisation point ${x}_{k}$ of the shape of the $i$ -th mode in the spatial direction considered.

The recombination on the physical basis is carried out by means of the operator REST_SPEC_PHYS [U4.63.22](doc:u4.63.22). The spatial direction considered is specified at the time of the call to this operator.

#### 1.2.5 Statistical elements

The modal variance ${\sigma}_{i}^{2}(U)$ , associated with the velocity $U$ , is expressed as follows:

${\sigma}_{i}^{2}(U)=2\underset{0}{\overset{\infty}{\int}}{S}_{{q}_{i}{q}_{j}}(f,U)\text{df}$ **eq 1.2.5-1**

At the flow velocity $U$ , the RMS value ${\sigma}_{\text{RMS}}(x)$ of the response at a point $x$ of the structure is given by:

${\sigma}_{\text{RMS}}(x)=\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\phi }_{i}^{2}(x){\sigma}_{i}^{2}(U)}$ **eq 1.2.5-2**

Where $N$ denotes the number of modes of the basis and ${\phi }_{i}(x)$ is the component at the point $x$ of the shape of the $i$ -th mode in the spatial direction considered.

This operation is carried out by the operator POST_DYNA_ALEA [U4.84.04](doc:u4.84.04).

### 1.3 Computation of the response to the turbulent excitation: time solution

The time solution proceeds according to the following sequence of operations:

#### 1.3.1 Factorisation of the cross-spectral density

The operator GENE_FONC_ALEA [U4.36.05](doc:u4.36.05) carries out the factorisation of the cross-spectral density of modal excitations ${S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)$ , before application of the Monte-Carlo method.

#### 1.3.2 Generation of the random modal excitations

The operator GENE_FONC_ALEA [U4.36.05](doc:u4.36.05) generates random modal excitations ${Q}_{i}(t)$ by carrying out draws by the Monte-Carlo method. The operator RECU_FONCTION [U4.32.03](doc:u4.32.03) makes it possible to recover each of the evolutions ${Q}_{i}(t)$ .

#### 1.3.3 Modification of a modal basis and projection

The operator MODI_BASE_MODALE [U4.66.21](doc:u4.66.21) modifies the modal basis of the structure by substituting for the initial characteristics those obtained for a flow velocity considered.

The operator PROJ_MATR_BASE [U4.63.12](doc:u4.63.12) allows the projection of the assembled mass and stiffness matrices onto the new modal basis previously defined.

#### 1.3.4 Definition of the obstacles

The definition of the geometry of the obstacles is carried out, where applicable, by means of the operator DEFI_OBSTACLE [U4.44.21](doc:u4.44.21).

#### 1.3.5 Dynamic solution

The transient dynamic analysis for the mode $i(1\le i\le N)$ is carried out by means of a numerical integration scheme with the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03).

${M}_{ii}{\ddot{q}}_{i}(t)+{C}_{ii}{\dot{q}}_{i}(t)+{K}_{ii}{q}_{i}(t)={Q}_{i}(t)$ **eq 1.3.5-1**

Where ${M}_{ii},{C}_{ii}\text{et}{K}_{ii}$ denote respectively the generalised mass, damping and stiffness associated with the $i$ -th mode; ${q}_{i}(t)\text{et}{Q}_{i}(t)$ denote respectively the generalised displacement and excitation associated with the $i$ -th mode.

#### 1.3.6 Ritz projection

The recovery on the physical basis is carried out by means of a Ritz projection:

$U(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{u}_{i}(x){q}_{i}(t)$ **eq 1.3.6-1**

$U(x,t)$ denotes the assembled vector of the physical displacements; ${u}_{i}(x)$ is the assembled vector defining the $i$ -th modal shape and ${q}_{i}(t)$ the generalised displacement along the $i$ -th mode.

This last operation is carried out by means of the operator REST_GENE_PHYS [U4.63.31](doc:u4.63.31).

## 2 Turbulent-excitation models applicable to line structures

### 2.1 General principles

#### 2.1.1 Assumptions

It is assumed that the line excitation induced on the line structure by the turbulence of the flow can be modelled in the form of a stationary ergodic Gaussian random process of zero mean. This turbulent excitation is therefore entirely characterised by its **cross-spectral density** ${S}_{f}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},\omega )$ , where ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ are any two points of the beam and $w$ denotes the angular frequency. The turbulent excitation applied to the structure is therefore characterised by its cross-spectral density ${S}_{f}$ .

In addition, it is assumed that the turbulent forces are independent of the motion of the structure. The turbulent excitation is identified experimentally on a reference mock-up. It is then applicable to any real component in geometric similarity with the reference mock-up.

#### 2.1.2 Computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations

${f}_{t}(x,s)$ denotes the line density of turbulent excitation exerted on the beam; $x$ is the current abscissa of a point of the beam and $s$ the complex angular frequency (Laplace variable). The additional assumptions $\mathit{H1}$ and $\mathit{H2}$ are made:

$\mathit{H1}$ . The excited length ${L}_{e}$ is smaller than the total length $L$ of the beam.

$\mathit{H2}$ . The expression of ${f}_{t}(x,s)$ does not depend on the origin of the excited zone ${x}_{e}$ ; this is expressed by ${f}_{t}(x,s)={f}_{t}(x-{x}_{e},s)$ .

In this case, one can express the line density ${f}_{t}$ in the following form:

${f}_{t}(x,s)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}D\cdot {C}_{f}(\alpha ,\frac{D}{{D}_{h}},\frac{D}{{L}_{e}},{s}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.1.2-1**

with: $\begin{array}{ccc}\alpha =\frac{x-{x}_{e}}{{L}_{e}}& {s}_{r}=\frac{\text{sD}}{U}& \text{Re}=\frac{\text{UD}}{u}\end{array}$

Where $\rho$ denotes the mass density of the fluid, $U$ is the mean flow velocity of the fluid, $D$ and ${D}_{h}$ are respectively the diameter of the structure and the hydraulic diameter, ${C}_{f}$ represents the dimensionless coefficient of turbulent force, $x$ is the current abscissa of a point of the beam, ${x}_{e}$ denotes the abscissa of the origin of the excited zone, ${L}_{e}$ represents the excited length, $\alpha$ is the reduced space variable, $s$ is the complex angular frequency (Laplace variable), ${s}_{r}$ is the reduced complex angular frequency, $\nu$ is the kinematic viscosity of the fluid, and finally “ $\text{Re}$ ” denotes the Reynolds number.

By the assumption of geometric similarity of the real component with the reference mock-up, one obtains:

${f}_{t}(x,s)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathit{\rho U}}^{2}D\cdot {C}_{f}(\alpha ,{s}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.1.2-2**

Thus, the modal turbulent excitation ${Q}_{i}(s)$ can be written in the Laplace domain (assumption $\mathit{H2}$ ):

${Q}_{i}(x)=\underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}+{L}_{e}}{\int}}{f}_{t}(x,s){\phi }_{i}(x)\text{dx}={L}_{e}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}{f}_{t}(\alpha {L}_{e},s){\phi }_{i}(\alpha {L}_{e}+{x}_{e})d\alpha$ **eq 2.1.2-3**

where ${\phi }_{i}(x)$ is the component of the $i$ -th modal shape in the spatial direction in which the turbulent excitation acts.

By means of the expression [eq 2.1.2-2], one deduces:

${Q}_{i}(s)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathit{\rho U}}^{2}{\text{DL}}_{e}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}{C}_{f}(\alpha ,{s}_{r}\text{,Re}){\phi }_{i}(\alpha {L}_{e}+{x}_{e})d\alpha$ **eq 2.1.2-4**

The cross-spectral densities of modal turbulent excitations are then expressed in the form:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}{\mathit{\rho U}}^{2}{\text{DL}}_{e})}^{2}\frac{D}{U}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}{\Phi}_{t}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2},{f}_{r}\text{,Re}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{j}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e})d{\alpha}_{1}d{\alpha}_{2}$ **eq 2.1.2-4**

| with | $1\le i,j\le N$ , where $N$ is the number of modes retained in order to determine the response of the structure; |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${\Phi}_{t}$ : cross-spectrum of ${C}_{f}$ between ${\alpha}_{1}$ and ${\alpha}_{2}$ ; |
|  | ${f}_{r}=\frac{\text{fD}}{U}$ : reduced frequency. |

**Note:**

*In what follows, one keeps the assumptions* $\mathit{H1}$ *and* $\mathit{H2}$ *and one denotes by* ${I}_{ij}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ *the integral:*

${I}_{ij}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})=\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}{\Phi}_{t}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2},{f}_{r}\text{,Re}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{j}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{1}{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{2}$ **eq 2.1.2-5**

With the help of this notation, the cross-spectra of modal excitations are written:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}{\text{DL}}_{e})}^{2}\frac{D}{U}{I}_{ij}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.1.2-6**

The expression of the auto-spectra of modal excitations is analogous:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{i}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}{\text{DL}}_{e})}^{2}\frac{D}{U}{I}_{ii}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.1.2-7**

### 2.2 Spectra of “correlation length” type

#### 2.2.1 Keywords

The factor keywords SPEC_LONG_COR_i ($i$ varying from 1 to 4) of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB [U4.44.31](doc:u4.44.31) give access to spectra of “correlation length” type. These spectra, specific to configurations of the type “tube bundle under cross-flow”, are predefined but the user can adjust their parameters.

#### 2.2.2 Definition of the model

##### 2.2.2.1 Cross-spectral density

In the case of spectra of “correlation length” type, the cross-spectral density characterising the turbulent excitation is assumed to be able to be put in a form with separable variables such that:

${S}_{i}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},\omega )={S}_{0}(\omega ){\varphi}_{0}({x}_{1},{x}_{2})$ **eq 2.2.2.1-1**

In this expression, ${S}_{0}(\omega )$ represents the turbulence auto-spectrum and ${\varphi}_{0}({x}_{1},{x}_{2})$ denotes a spatial correlation function defined by:

${\varphi}_{0}({x}_{1},{x}_{2})=\exp\cdot (\frac{-\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}})$ **eq 2.2.2.1-2**

where ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ denote the abscissae of two observation points and ${\lambda}_{c}$ represents the correlation length.

Four analytical expressions are available in the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB [U4.44.31](doc:u4.44.31). These expressions each correspond to a particular representation of ${S}_{0}(\omega )$ .

The user defines a turbulence spectrum by choosing one of these analytical forms, whose parameters he can adjust.

##### 2.2.2.2 Modelling of the turbulence spectrum by an expression with separable variables

- General case

The function ${\Phi}_{\text{tt}}$ introduced in the relation is modelled by a form with separable variables:

${\Phi}_{t}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2},{f}_{r}\text{,Re})=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{s}}{\varphi}_{n}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2}){\Phi}_{n}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.2.2.2-1**

Where ${N}_{s}$ denotes the degree of the basis of the shape functions ${\varphi}_{n}$ and ${\Phi}_{n}$ is a function independent of the space variable. These two functions are stored in the database and can be selected by the user.

The auto-spectra of modal excitations are given by [eq 2.1.2-7] by introducing:

${I}_{ii}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{s}}{L}_{ni}^{2}\cdot {\Phi}_{n}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.2.2.2-2**

with: ${L}_{ni}^{2}=\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}{\varphi}_{n}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2})\cdot {\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e})\cdot {\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{1}{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{2}$ **eq 2.2.2.2-3**

The principle of the computation is the following: one first computes the values of the ${L}_{ni}^{2}$ by carrying out the computation of the double integrals; one then computes ${\Phi}_{n}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ for all the values of n; one finally obtains the expression of ${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{i}}(f,U)$ by means of the equation [eq 2.1.2-4].

- Particular case: model used for the steam-generator tubes

The particular case of the study of the steam-generator tubes corresponds to a particular case of the general case presented previously by setting $\text{Ns}=1$ . The cross-spectrum of turbulent excitation between two points of reduced abscissae ${\alpha}_{1}$ and ${\alpha}_{2}$ is then given by:

${\Phi}_{t}({\alpha}_{1},{\alpha}_{2},{f}_{r}\text{,Re})=\exp(-\frac{\mid {\alpha}_{1}-{\alpha}_{2}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}}\cdot {L}_{e})\cdot \Phi ({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.2.2.2-4**

where ${\lambda}_{c}$ represents the correlation length of the turbulent forces and ${L}_{e}$ is the excited length. In general, one takes ${\lambda}_{c}$ of the order of 3 to 4 times the outside diameter of the tube.

The auto-correlation spectra of modal excitations, in the case of constant velocity and mass-density profiles, are given by:

${S}_{\text{QiQi}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}{\text{DL}}_{e})}^{2}\cdot \frac{D}{U}{I}_{ii}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})$ **eq 2.2.2.2-5**

with:

${I}_{ii}({f}_{r}\text{,Re})=\Phi ({f}_{r}\text{,Re})\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}.\exp(-\frac{\mid {\alpha}_{2}-{\alpha}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}}{L}_{e}).{\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}).{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{1}{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{2}$

**eq 2.2.2.2-6**

In the general case of arbitrary mass-density and flow-velocity profiles, one has:

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}D)}^{2}\cdot \frac{D}{U}S({f}_{r})\\ \underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}{\text{+L}}_{e}}{\int}}\underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}+{L}_{e}}{\int}}\exp(-\frac{\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}})\cdot {\rho}_{e}({x}_{1}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{2})\cdot {U}_{e}^{2}({x}_{1}){U}_{e}^{2}({x}_{2}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{2}){\text{dx}}_{1}{\text{dx}}_{2}\end{array}$

**eq 2.2.2.2-7**

Where $D$ is the diameter of the structure, ${L}_{e}$ is the length of the excited zone, ${x}_{e}$ is the abscissa of the origin of the excited zone, $U$ is the mean velocity of the flow, $S({f}_{r})$ is a spectral density of excitation independent of the mean velocity of the flow $U$ , ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ are the curvilinear abscissae of two observation points on the tube, ${\rho}_{e}(x)$ is the mass-density profile of the fluid along the tube, ${U}_{e}(x)$ is the transverse-velocity profile of the flow along the tube and ${\lambda}_{c}$ denotes the correlation length.

The dimensionless profiles of mass density and of transverse velocity of the external flow are defined in the following way:

${\rho}_{e}(x)$ denoting the evolution of the mass density of the external fluid along the immersed zone ${L}_{\text{imm}}$ of the tube, one denotes by $\rho$ the mass density of the external fluid averaged over the immersed part of the tube:

$\rho =\frac{1}{{L}_{\text{imm}}}\underset{{x}_{\text{imm}}}{\overset{{x}_{\text{imm}}+{L}_{\text{imm}}}{\int}}{\rho}_{e}(x)\text{dx}$ **eq 2.2.2.2-8**

One denotes by $r(x)$ the dimensionless mass-density profile such that ${\rho}_{e}(x)=\rho \cdot r(x)$ .

${U}_{e}(x)$ denoting the evolution of the flow velocity of the external fluid over the excited length ${L}_{e}$ of the tube, one denotes by $U$ the flow velocity of the fluid averaged over the excited length of the tube:

$U=\frac{1}{{L}_{e}}\underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}+{L}_{e}}{\int}}{U}_{e}(x)\text{dx}$ **eq 2.2.2.2-9**

One denotes by $u(x)$ the dimensionless profile of transverse velocity of the external flow, such that ${U}_{e}(x)=U\cdot u(x)$ .

Introducing the mean quantities and the dimensionless profiles into the expression [eq2.2.2.2-7], one obtains:

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)={(\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}D)}^{2}.\frac{D}{U}S({f}_{r})\underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}+{L}_{e}}{\int}}\underset{{x}_{e}}{\overset{{x}_{e}+{L}_{e}}{\int}}\exp(-\frac{\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}})\\ {\rho}_{e}({x}_{1}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{2}){U}_{e}^{2}({x}_{1}){U}_{e}^{2}({x}_{2}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{j}({x}_{2}){\text{dx}}_{1}{\text{dx}}_{2}\end{array}$ **eq 2.2.2.2-10**

After having noted $\alpha =\frac{x-{x}_{e}}{{L}_{e}}$ , one has:

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{\text{QiQj}}(f,U)=\frac{1}{4}{\rho}^{2}{U}^{3}{D}^{3}{L}_{e}^{2}S({f}_{r})\times \underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}[\exp(-\frac{\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}})r({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e})r({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e})\\ {u}^{2}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){u}^{2}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{1}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e}){\phi }_{i}({\alpha}_{2}{L}_{e}+{x}_{e})]{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{1}{\mathrm{d\alpha }}_{2}\end{array}$

**eq 2.2.2.2-11**

Where $S({f}_{r})$ represents the turbulence spectrum, defined as a function of a reduced frequency ${f}_{r}$ (Strouhal number). For a tube in interaction with a cross-flow, ${f}_{r}$ is written:

${f}_{r}=\frac{\text{fD}}{U}$

where $f$ is the dimensional frequency, $D$ is the diameter of the tube and $U$ is the mean velocity of the flow.

The double integral of the expression [eq 2.2.2.2-11] is evaluated by the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE [U4.63.14](doc:u4.63.14).

- Case of multiple excitation zones

In the case where there exist several excitation zones, one introduces the following additional notation:

The excitation zone $k$ being located by its starting abscissa ${x}_{k}$ and its length ${L}_{k}$ , one denotes by ${U}_{k}(x)$ the transverse-velocity profile of the fluid flow at the level of this zone. The mean transverse velocity over the excitation zone $k$ is then given by:

${\bar{U}}_{k}=\frac{1}{{L}_{k}}\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}{U}_{k}(x)\text{dx}$

One deduces from it the dimensionless profile of transverse velocity, normalised over the zone $k$ :

${u}_{k}(x)=\frac{{U}_{k}(x)}{{\bar{U}}_{k}}$

$K$ denoting the total number of excitation zones, the mean transverse velocity over all the excitation zones is defined by:

$\bar{U}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\bar{U}}_{k}$

If ${V}_{\text{gap}}$ is the inter-tube velocity at the inlet of the steam generator (the range of flow velocities is defined in CALC_FLUI_STRU [U4.66.02](doc:u4.66.02) by means of the keyword VITE_FLUI), one proceeds to a second normalisation; the transverse velocity at a point $x$ situated in the excitation zone $k$ is given by:

${V}_{k}(x)={V}_{\text{gap}}\frac{{U}_{k}(x)}{\bar{U}}={V}_{\text{gap}}\frac{{\bar{U}}_{k}}{\bar{U}}{u}_{k}(x)$

Thanks to this normalisation, the arithmetic mean of the transverse velocity over all the excitation zones is equal to the inter-tube velocity; one indeed has:

$\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\frac{1}{{L}_{k}}\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}{V}_{k}(x)\text{dx})={V}_{\text{gap}}$

The computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations, carried out by the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE [U4.63.14](doc:u4.63.14), is done by adding the contributions of each of the excitation zones according to the relation:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(f,{V}_{\text{gap}})={(\frac{1}{2}D)}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\frac{D}{{\bar{V}}_{k}}\times {L}_{ij}^{k}\times S({f}_{r}^{k}))$

with:

${\bar{V}}_{k}={V}_{\text{gap}}\times \frac{{U}_{k}}{\bar{U}}$ and ${f}_{r}^{k}=\frac{\text{fD}}{{\bar{V}}_{k}}$

and ${L}_{ij}^{k}=\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\exp(\frac{-\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{1}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{2}){V}_{k}^{2}({x}_{1}){V}_{k}^{2}({x}_{2}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{2}){\text{dx}}_{1}{\text{dx}}_{2}$

that is: ${L}_{ij}^{k}={\bar{V}}_{k}^{4}\times \underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\exp(\frac{-\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{1}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{2}){u}_{k}^{2}({x}_{1}){u}_{k}^{2}({x}_{2}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{2}){\text{dx}}_{1}{\text{dx}}_{2}$

One sets:

${l}_{ij}^{k}=\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\underset{{x}_{k}}{\overset{{x}_{k}+{L}_{k}}{\int}}\exp(\frac{-\mid {x}_{2}-{x}_{1}\mid }{{\lambda}_{c}}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{1}){\rho}_{e}({x}_{2}){u}_{k}^{2}({x}_{1}){u}_{k}^{2}({x}_{2}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{1}){\phi }_{i}({x}_{2}){\text{dx}}_{1}{\text{dx}}_{2}$

The expression of the cross-spectra of modal excitations then becomes:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(f,{V}_{\text{gap}})={(\frac{1}{2}D)}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{K}(\frac{D}{{\bar{V}}_{k}}\times {\bar{V}}_{k}^{4}\times {l}_{ij}^{k}\times S({f}_{r}^{k}))$

whence:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(f,{V}_{\text{gap}})=\frac{1}{4}{D}^{3}\times \sum_{k=1}^{K}({\bar{V}}_{k}^{3}\times {l}_{ij}^{k}\times S({f}_{r}^{k}))$

### 2.3 Distributed turbulent-excitation model

#### 2.3.1 Keywords

The factor keyword SPEC_FONC_FORME of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB [U4.44.31](doc:u4.44.31) makes it possible to define an excitation spectrum by its decomposition on a family of shape functions. The user has the possibility of defining the spectrum by supplying a cross-spectral matrix and a list of associated shape functions. The concepts [interspectre] and [fonction] must then have been generated upstream. In the case of the component “control-rod cluster”, the user can also use a predefined turbulence spectrum, identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1.

#### 2.3.2 Decomposition on a family of shape functions

The distributed turbulent-excitation model assumes that the **instantaneous line density of the turbulent forces** ${f}_{t}(x,t)$ can be **decomposed on a family of shape functions** ${j}_{k}(x)$ of dimension $K$ in the following way:

${f}_{t}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\varphi}_{k}(x){\alpha}_{k}(t)$ **eq 2.3.2-1**

The coefficients ${\alpha}_{k}(t)$ define at each time instant the decomposition of the turbulent excitation on the family of shape functions.

The cross-spectral density of turbulent excitation between two points of the line structure of abscissae ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ is then written:

${S}_{f}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},\omega )=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\varphi}_{k}({x}_{1}){\varphi}_{l}({x}_{2}){\mathrm{S\alpha }}_{k}{\alpha}_{l}(\omega )$ **eq 2.3.2-2**

This formulation makes it possible to take into account an excitation whose spatial distribution is arbitrary.

#### 2.3.3 Setting into equations

##### 2.3.3.1 Application of a distributed turbulent excitation

The length of application $L$ is characterised intrinsically by the domain of definition of the shape functions associated with the excitation. The zone of application is determined by the data of the name of the node about which it is centred.

${x}_{n}$ denoting the abscissa locating this node, the turbulent excitation is imposed on the domain $\left[{x}_{n}-L/2,{x}_{n}+L/2\right]$ .

The turbulent excitation being able, on the other hand, to be developed in a correlated manner in the two directions $Y$ and $Z$ orthogonal to the axis of the line structure, the shape functions are a priori vectors with two components.

One therefore supplies, by convention in a table_fonction, two shape functions; the first is associated with the direction $Y$ and the other with the direction $Z$ . Each of the two functions is defined on the interval $\left[0,L\right]$ .

##### 2.3.3.2 Turbulent excitation identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1

The shape functions ${\varphi}_{k}$ are the first 12 flexural modal shapes of the structure identified experimentally, distributed according to the two directions orthogonal to the principal axis of the beam. The general analytical expression of these shapes is the following:

${\overrightarrow{\varphi}}_{k}(x)=(\begin{array}{c}{\varphi}_{\text{Yk}}(x)\\ {\varphi}_{\text{Zk}}(x)\end{array})$ **eq 2.3.3.2-1**

with:

${\varphi}_{\text{Yk}}(x)={A}_{\text{Yk}}\cdot \cos(\frac{{n}_{\text{Yk}}}{L}x)+{B}_{\text{Yk}}\cdot \sin(\frac{{n}_{\text{Yk}}}{L}x)+{C}_{\text{Yk}}\cdot \text{ch}(\frac{{n}_{\text{Yk}}}{L}x)+{D}_{\text{Yk}}\cdot \text{sh}(\frac{{n}_{\text{Yk}}}{L}x)$ **eq 2.3.3.2-2**

${\varphi}_{\text{Zk}}(x)={A}_{\text{Zk}}\cdot \cos(\frac{{n}_{\text{Zk}}}{L}x)+{B}_{\text{Zk}}\cdot \sin(\frac{{n}_{\text{Zk}}}{L}x)+{C}_{\text{Zk}}\cdot \text{ch}(\frac{{n}_{\text{Zk}}}{L}x)+{D}_{\text{Zk}}\cdot \text{sh}(\frac{{n}_{\text{Zk}}}{L}x)$ **eq 2.3.3.2-3**

where ${n}_{\text{Yk}}$ and ${n}_{\text{Zk}}$ denote wave numbers, $L$ is the length of application of the excitation and the coefficients ${A}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${B}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${C}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${D}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${A}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${B}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${C}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${D}_{\text{Zk}}$ are constant real coefficients characteristic of the shape function considered.

The first 6 shape functions are associated with the direction $Y$ and ${A}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${B}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${C}_{\text{Zk}}$ , ${D}_{\text{Zk}}$ are therefore zero, for $1\le k\le 6$ .

The last 6 shape functions are associated with the direction $Z$ and ${A}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${B}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${C}_{\text{Yk}}$ , ${D}_{\text{Yk}}$ are therefore zero, for $7\le k\le 12$ .

This family of shape functions is therefore characterised by $5\times 12=60$ real coefficients.

The turbulent excitation identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1 is homogeneous in the two directions orthogonal to the axis of the line structure, the turbulence being uncorrelated between these two directions.

The cross-spectral matrix $\left[{\mathrm{S\alpha }}_{k}{\alpha}_{l}\right]$ identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1 is therefore a matrix of dimension $12\times 12$ , constituted of two identical diagonal blocks of dimension 6:

$\left[{\mathrm{S\alpha }}_{k}{\alpha}_{l}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\left[{S}_{o}(\omega )\right]& \left[0\right]\\ \left[0\right]& \left[{S}_{o}(\omega )\right]\end{array}\right]$

By the property of Hermitian symmetry, this matrix is entirely defined by the data of the upper (or lower) triangular part of $\left[{S}_{o}(\omega )\right]$ , that is 21 cross-spectra. For each of them, the characteristic parameters are the plateau level, the cut-off frequency and the slope of the spectrum beyond this frequency.

The cross-spectral matrix of turbulent excitation identified on the mock-up GRAPPE1 is therefore characterised by 63 real coefficients ($3\times 21$ ).

**Note:**

*The GRAPPE1 excitations are available at* **two reference flow rates**. *The set of the data characterising these excitations therefore represents* **246 real coefficients** ( $[60+63]\times 2$).

##### 2.3.3.3 Projection of the excitation onto the modal basis

One notes:

${\phi }_{i}(x)=(\begin{array}{c}{\text{DY}}_{i}(x)\\ {\text{DZ}}_{i}(x)\end{array})$ the $i$ -th modal shape of the structure.

Let ${\beta}_{\text{ik}}$ be the coordinates of the $i$ -th modal shape of the structure on the basis of the shape functions ${\varphi}_{k}(x)$ :

${\phi }_{i}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\beta}_{\text{ik}}\cdot {\varphi}_{k}(x)$ **eq 2.3.3.3-1**

The cross-spectra of modal excitations ${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(\omega )$ applied to the structure are then written:

${S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(\omega )=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\beta}_{\text{ik}}\cdot {\beta}_{\text{jl}}\cdot {S}_{{\alpha}_{k}{\alpha}_{l}}(\omega )$ **eq 2.3.3.3-2**

For each mode $i$ of the structure, the coefficients ${\beta}_{\text{ik}}$ are determined by integrating the equation [eq2.3.3.3-1] premultiplied by the functions ${\varphi}_{j}$ , over the domain of application of the excitation. One thus obtains:

$\underset{{x}_{0}-L/2}{\overset{{x}_{0}+L/2}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x+L/2)\cdot {\phi }_{i}(x)\cdot \text{dx}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\beta}_{\text{ik}}\cdot \underset{{x}_{0}-L/2}{\overset{{x}_{0}+L/2}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x+L/2)\cdot {\varphi}_{k}(x+L/2)\cdot \text{dx}$

$\begin{array}{cc}\underset{{x}_{0}-L/2}{\overset{{x}_{0}+L/2}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x+L/2)\cdot {\phi }_{i}(x)\cdot \text{dx}=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\beta}_{\text{ik}}\cdot \underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{k}(x)\cdot \text{dx}& \forall (i,j)\end{array}$ **eq 2.3.3.3-3**

For each $i$ , the equation [eq 2.3.3.3-3] is written in matrix form:

$\left[\begin{array}{c}{a}_{\text{jk}}\end{array}\right]\cdot ({\beta}_{\text{ik}})=({b}_{ij})$ **eq 2.3.3.3-4**

with:

${a}_{\text{jk}}=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{k}(x)\cdot \text{dx}$

that is:

${a}_{\text{jk}}=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}({\varphi}_{\text{Yj}}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{\text{Yk}}(\varphi )+{\varphi}_{\text{Zj}}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{\text{Zk}}(x))\cdot \text{dx}$

and

${b}_{ij}=\underset{{x}_{0}-L/2}{\overset{{x}_{0}+L/2}{\int}}{\varphi}_{j}(x+L/2)\cdot {\phi }_{i}(x)\cdot \text{dx}$

that is:

${b}_{ij}=\underset{{x}_{0}-L/2}{\overset{{x}_{0}+L/2}{\int}}({\text{DY}}_{i}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{\text{Yj}}(x+L/2)+{\text{DZ}}_{i}(x)\cdot {\varphi}_{\text{Zj}}(x+L/2))\cdot \text{dx}$

The solution of each of the systems of linear equations leads to the ${\beta}_{\text{ik}}$ .

The computation of the scalar products is carried out in the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE [U4. 63.14].

**Notes:**

1. *The functions* ${\varphi}_{k}(x)$ *represent, in practice, the modal shapes recorded on the mock-up. The system (), with a dominant diagonal, is therefore well conditioned. In particular, when the mock-up line structure has a homogeneous line mass, the functions* ${\varphi}_{k}(x)$ *are orthogonal and the matrix* $\left[\begin{array}{c}{a}_{\text{jk}}\end{array}\right]$ *is diagonal.*
2. *Tests comparing the domain of application of the excitation with the domain of definition of the structure are carried out.*

### 2.4 Localised turbulent-excitation model

#### 2.4.1 Keywords

The factor keyword SPEC_EXCI_POINT of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB [U4.44.31](doc:u4.44.31) is used in the case of an excitation spectrum associated with one or more point forces and moments. The user can define the spectrum by supplying:

- a cross-spectral matrix of excitations (the associated concept [interspectre] must be generated upstream),
- the list of the nodes of application of these excitations,
- the nature of the excitation applied at each of these nodes (force or moment),
- the directions of application of the excitations thus defined.

He can also use a predefined turbulence spectrum, identified on the mock-up GRAPPE2.

#### 2.4.2 Foundations

The localised turbulent-excitation model is a particular case of the distributed turbulent-excitation model. Thus, one assumes, as in paragraph [§2.3.2], that the **instantaneous line density of the turbulent forces** ${f}_{t}(x,t)$ can be **decomposed on a family of shape functions** ${\varphi}_{k}(x)$ in the following way:

${f}_{t}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{\varphi}_{k}(x){\alpha}_{k}(t)$ **eq 2.4.2-1**

The coefficients ${\alpha}_{k}(t)$ define at each time instant the decomposition of the turbulent excitation on the family of shape functions.

The cross-spectral density of turbulent excitation between two points of the line structure of abscissae ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ is then written:

${S}_{f}({x}_{1},{x}_{2},\omega )=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{l=1}^{K}{\varphi}_{k}({x}_{1})\cdot {\varphi}_{l}({x}_{2})\cdot {S}_{{\alpha}_{k}{\alpha}_{l}}(\omega )$ **eq 2.4.2-2**

The particularity of the localised turbulent-excitation model lies in the **specificity of the shape functions** ${\varphi}_{k}(x)$ :

${\varphi}_{k}(x)=\delta (x-{x}_{k})$ makes it possible to represent a **point force** applied at the point of abscissa ${x}_{k}$

${\varphi}_{k}(x)={\delta}^{'}(x-{x}_{k})$ makes it possible to represent a **point moment** applied at the point of abscissa ${x}_{k}$

$\delta (x-{x}_{k})$ and ${\delta}^{'}(x-{x}_{k})$ denote respectively the Dirac distribution and the derivative of the Dirac distribution at the point of abscissa ${x}_{k}$ .

Given the specificity of the shape functions, the projection of a localised turbulent excitation onto a modal basis is much simpler than in the general case (distributed excitation), since one can compute analytically the expression of the projected excitation.

#### 2.4.3 Setting into equations

##### 2.4.3.1 Application of a localised turbulent excitation

One considers a turbulent excitation applied to a line structure and constituted of point forces and moments. This excitation is entirely characterised by the following data:

- list of the nodes of application of the point forces and moments,
- nature of the excitation applied at each node (force or moment),
- direction of the excitation applied at each node.

$\text{Ainsi}{f}_{t}(x,t)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{F}_{k}(s)\cdot \delta (x-{x}_{k})\cdot {n}_{k}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}{M}_{m}(s)\cdot {\delta}^{'}(x-{x}_{m})\cdot {n}_{m}$ **eq 2.4.3.1-1**

is the expression of a localised turbulent excitation, characterised by $K$ forces and $M$ point moments, applied respectively at the nodes of abscissae ${x}_{k}$ and ${x}_{m}$ in the directions ${n}_{k}$ and ${\overrightarrow{n}}_{m}$ .

One has: ${n}_{k}=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ \cos\cdot ({\theta}_{k})\\ \sin\cdot ({\theta}_{k})\end{array})$ and ${n}_{m}$ defined in an analogous way.

$\theta$ represents the azimuth giving the direction of application of the force (or of the moment) in the plane $P$ orthogonal to the neutral fibre at the node of application, as defined in the figure [Figure 2.4.3.1-a] below:

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.02/Object_306.svg)

**Figure 2.4.3.1-a: Definition of the direction of application**

The generalised excitation associated with the $i$ -th mode of the structure, ${Q}_{i}(s)$ , being defined by:

${Q}_{i}(s)=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\phi }_{i}(x)\cdot {f}_{t}(x,t)\cdot \text{dx}$ **eq 2.4.3.1-2**

where $L$ represents the length of the beam and ${\phi }_{i}(x)$ the shape of the mode $i$ , one obtains, given the expression [eq 2.4.3.1-1]:

${Q}_{i}(s)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}{F}_{k}(s)\cdot {\phi }_{i}({x}_{k})\cdot {n}_{k}-\sum_{m=1}^{M}{M}_{m}(s)\cdot {\phi }_{i}({x}_{k})\cdot {n}_{m}$ **eq 2.4.3.1-3**

The computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations then leads to:

$\begin{array}{c}{S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}(s)=\sum_{{k}_{1}=1}^{K}\sum_{{k}_{2}=1}^{K}{S}_{{F}_{{k}_{1}}{F}_{{k}_{2}}(s)}({\phi }_{i}({x}_{{k}_{1}}).{n}_{{k}_{1}}).({\phi }_{j}({x}_{{k}_{2}}).{n}_{{k}_{2}})\\ +\sum_{{k}_{1}=1}^{K}\sum_{{m}_{2}=1}^{M}{S}_{{F}_{{k}_{1}}{M}_{{m}_{2}}(s)}({\phi }_{i}({x}_{{k}_{1}}).{n}_{{k}_{1}}).({\phi }_{{j}^{¢}}({x}_{{m}_{2}}).{n}_{{m}_{2}})\\ +\sum_{{m}_{1}=1}^{M}\sum_{{k}_{2}=1}^{K}{S}_{{M}_{{m}_{1}}{F}_{{k}_{2}}(s)}({\phi }_{i}^{'}({x}_{{m}_{1}}).{n}_{{m}_{1}}).({\phi }_{j}({x}_{{k}_{2}}).{n}_{{k}_{2}})\\ +\sum_{{m}_{1}=1}^{M}\sum_{{m}_{2}=1}^{M}{S}_{{M}_{{m}_{1}}{M}_{{m}_{2}}(s)}({\phi }_{i}^{'}({x}_{{m}_{1}}).{n}_{{m}_{1}}).({\phi }_{{j}^{¢}}({x}_{{m}_{2}}).{n}_{{m}_{2}})\end{array}$ **eq 2.4.3.1-4**

**Note:**

*When the user defines the turbulent-excitation spectrum, he must supply the cross-spectral matrix of the point excitations whose terms appear above.* **This matrix has dimension** $K+M$ **(number of point forces and moments applied).**

##### 2.4.3.2 Turbulent excitation identified on the mock-up GRAPPE2

The turbulent excitation identified on the mock-up GRAPPE2 is represented by a resultant force and a resultant moment, applied at the same node in the two directions orthogonal to the axis of the structure. The line density of this excitation has the expression:

${f}_{t}(x,s)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}{D}_{h}\left[{L}_{p}\cdot {F}_{t}({s}_{r})\cdot \delta (x-{x}_{0})-{L}_{p}^{2}\cdot {M}_{t}({s}_{r})\cdot {\delta}^{'}(x-{x}_{0})\right](\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 1\end{array})$ **eq 2.4.3.2-1**

Where $\rho$ is the mass density of the fluid, $U$ is the mean velocity of the flow, ${D}_{h}$ is the hydraulic diameter, ${L}_{p}$ is the thickness of the housing plate (corresponding to the excited length), ${x}_{0}$ is the abscissa of the point of application of the excitation, ${s}_{r}=\frac{s\cdot D}{U}$ is the reduced complex frequency, ${F}_{t}({s}_{r})$ and ${M}_{t}({s}_{r})$ are the dimensionless coefficients representing the resultant force and the resultant moment.

The quantities $\rho$ , $U$ , ${D}_{h}$ and ${L}_{p}$ make it possible to scale the excitation.

By substituting the expression [eq 2.4.3.2-3] into the relation [eq 2.4.3.1-4] defining the modal excitation ${Q}_{i}(s)$ , one obtains:

${Q}_{i}(s)=\frac{1}{2}{\mathrm{\rho U}}^{2}{D}_{h}\left[{L}_{p}\cdot {F}_{t}({s}_{r})\cdot {\phi }_{i}({x}_{0})\cdot (\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 1\end{array})+{L}_{p}^{2}\cdot {M}_{t}({s}_{r})\cdot {\phi }_{i}^{'}({x}_{0})\cdot (\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 1\end{array})\right]$ **eq 2.4.3.2-2**

The point force and moment identified on the mock-up GRAPPE2 being uncorrelated, the computation of the cross-spectra of modal excitations finally leads to:

$\begin{array}{cc}{S}_{{Q}_{i}{Q}_{j}}={(\frac{1}{2}{\rho U}^{2}{D}_{h})}^{2}\frac{D}{U}\cdot & {L}_{p}^{2}\cdot {\phi }_{i}({x}_{0})\cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}{\phi }_{j}({x}_{0})\cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}\cdot {S}_{{F}_{t}{F}_{t}}({s}_{r})\\ & +{L}_{p}^{4}\cdot {\phi }_{i}^{\prime}({x}_{0})\cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}^{\prime}{\phi }_{j}^{\prime}({x}_{0})\cdot \begin{pmatrix}0\\ 1\\ 1\end{pmatrix}\cdot {S}_{{M}_{t}{M}_{t}}({s}_{r})\end{array}$ **eq 2.4.3.2-3**

In this expression, $D$ is the outside diameter of the structure, ${S}_{{F}_{t}{F}_{t}}({s}_{r})$ and ${S}_{{M}_{t}{M}_{t}}({s}_{r})$ represent respectively the dimensionless auto-spectra of force and of moment identified on the mock-up GRAPPE2. The operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE [U4.63.14](doc:u4.63.14) computes the cross-spectra of modal excitations according to the relation [eq 2.4.3.2-3] above.
