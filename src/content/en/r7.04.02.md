# r7.04.02 Estimation of fatigue under random loading

**Summary:**

This note presents two stress-cycle counting methods which lead to an analytical expression for the mechanical damage produced by a random loading:

- stress-peak counting method,
- method of counting exceedances of a given level.

The first method uses the signal, its first derivative and its second derivative. The second requires only knowledge of the signal and of its first derivative.

Once the stress cycles are known by these methods, the mean damage over the duration of the signal is determined using the Wöhler method.

## 1 Introduction

The assessment of damage is based on the use of endurance curves of the material, associating a stress variation of given amplitude with an allowable number of cycles defined by a fatigue curve.

The fatigue curves of the material are established by subjecting specimens to sinusoidal loadings of constant amplitude from the start of the test until failure.

In order to use these curves from a real loading, cycles must be identified in the stress history, which is done by cycle-counting methods. Many methods exist: document [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01) presents two cycle-counting methods in the case of a deterministic real loading.

However, many real mechanical loadings affecting nuclear components have a random character, which leads one to favour the use of statistical methods in order to assess the damage undergone by these structures.

Some stress-cycle counting methods have been given a statistical interpretation:

- stress-peak counting method
- method of counting exceedances of a given level.

The field of application of these two methods [bib1][bib2] is limited to random loadings that are ergodic (the analysis of a single sample is sufficient to characterise the parameters of the process) and Gaussian (the values of the measured signal are distributed according to a normal law).

Furthermore, the evolution of the signal is treated as a random process characterised by its statistical parameters (spectral moments of order 0, 2 and 4) [R7.10.01](doc:r7.10.01).

In both cases, the statistical event to be taken into account is simple to define:

- a stress peak is defined by a zero slope and a negative acceleration for a positive peak, a positive acceleration for a negative peak,
- an exceedance of a stress level ${S}_{0}$ is characterised by a signal value equal to ${S}_{0}$ and by a positive slope.

Once the stress cycles are known by these methods, one then proceeds to the computation of the number of cycles to failure from a fatigue curve. The Wöhler curves, which are established experimentally, have been approximated by various mathematical expressions characterising the different regions of these curves more or less correctly.

Three mathematical expressions are available in *Code_Aster*: a discretised form and two analytical forms.

Knowing the number of stress cycles (given by one of these two cycle-counting methods) and the elementary damage associated with them (determined by interpolation on the Wöhler curve of the material), one can compute a mean damage over the duration of the signal.

## 2 Assessment of the damage

For a structure with no geometrical defect and subjected to a purely alternating stress, the number of cycles to failure is determined from an endurance diagram, also called a Wöhler curve or an $S-N$ curve.

The number of cycles to failure is determined by interpolation of the Wöhler curve of the material for a given alternating (one-dimensional) stress level (to each elementary cycle there corresponds a stress-amplitude level $\mathrm{Ds}=\mid {s}_{\max}-{s}_{\min}\mid$ and an alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}=1/\mathrm{2Ds}$).

The damage of an elementary cycle is equal to the inverse of the number of cycles to failure:

$D=\frac{1}{{N}_{r({S}_{\text{alt}})}}$

### 2.1 Endurance diagram

The endurance diagram, also called the Wöhler curve or the $S-N$ curve (stress–number of cycles to failure curve), is obtained experimentally by subjecting specimens to cycles of periodic forces (generally sinusoidal) of normal amplitude $\sigma$ and of constant frequencies, and by recording the number of cycles ${N}_{r}$ at the end of which failure occurs [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01).

The various mathematical forms of the Wöhler curve are described in the document “Estimation of high-cycle fatigue”, [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01), together with the way of introducing them into *Code_Aster*.

### 2.2 Elastoplastic concentration coefficient

It may also be necessary to weight the stress value determined by the counting method by the elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$.

The elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$ (referred to in articles B3234.3 and B3234.5 of the RCC_M [bib4]) is defined as the ratio between the real strain amplitude and the strain amplitude determined by the elastic analysis.

The value of the coefficient ${K}_{e}$ is given in document [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01).

## 3 Number of stress cycles

### 3.1 Reminders: spectral moments and irregularity factor

The spectral moment of order $i$ is the following quantity [R7.10.01](doc:r7.10.01):

${\lambda}_{i}=\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{\mid \omega \mid }^{i}{G}_{\text{SS}}(\omega )d\omega$

where $\omega$ is the angular frequency and ${G}_{\text{SS}}$ the power spectral density, or PSD.

In particular one has: ${\lambda}_{0}={s}_{S}^{2}{\lambda}_{2}={s}_{{S}^{'}}^{2}{\lambda}_{4}={s}_{{S}^{''}}^{2}$, which are the standard deviations of $S$ and of its first derivatives.

The **irregularity factor** describes the frequency content of the signal. Lying between 0 and 1, it tends towards 1 when the process is narrow-band, whereas it tends towards 0 for a broad-band process.

Its expression is:

$I=\frac{{s}_{{S}^{'}}^{2}}{{s}_{{S}^{}}{s}_{{S}^{\text{''}}}}=\sqrt{\frac{{\lambda}_{2}^{2}}{{\lambda}_{0}{\lambda}_{4}}}$

These definitions are recalled because the evolution of the signal is treated as a random process characterised by its statistical parameters (spectral moments of order 0, 2 and 4).

For the stress-peak counting method, the random signal is fully characterised by the three spectral moments of orders 0, 2 and 4.

In the case of the level-exceedance counting method, the spectral moments of orders 0 and 2 are sufficient to characterise the random signal.

In practice, these values are determined by the command POST_DYNA_ALEA [U4.76.02](doc:u4.76.02), which performs statistical processing on a random loading. The definition of the various parameters is given in document [R7.10.01](doc:r7.10.01).

The operator for the computation of fatigue in the random domain, POST_FATI_ALEA [U4.67.05](doc:u4.67.05), uses the values of the three spectral moments computed by POST_DYNA_ALEA and computes the mean damage and the standard deviation of the damage by the methods described in this document.

### 3.2 Stress-peak counting method

The principle of this method consists in counting the local maxima (in absolute value) located on either side of the mean value of the stresses.

The signal being stationary and Gaussian, centred with respect to its mean value, the distribution of the peaks is symmetric with respect to this mean. One is therefore interested in the **distribution of the positive peaks**. In the general case, the distribution of the peaks of positive amplitude $S$ is written in the form:

${P}_{\text{pic}}^{+}\left(S\right)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi }{s}_{S}\left(1+I\right)}\left[\sqrt{1-{I}^{2}}{e}^{\text{}\frac{{S}^{2}}{{\mathrm{2s}}_{S}^{2}\left(1-{I}^{2}\right)}}+\frac{\text{IS}}{{s}_{S}}{e}^{\text{}\frac{{S}^{2}}{{\mathrm{2s}}_{S}^{2}}}\underset{-\infty }{\overset{\alpha}{\int}}{e}^{\text{}\frac{-{t}^{2}}{2}}\text{dt}\right]$

$\text{avec}\lbrace \begin{array}{c}I=\frac{{\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}^{2}}{{\sigma}_{S}{\sigma}_{{S}^{''}}}\\ \alpha =\frac{S}{{\sigma}_{S}}\frac{I}{\sqrt{1-{I}^{2}}}\end{array}$

This distribution of the positive peaks simplifies in the case of signals for which the irregularity factor equals $I=0$ or $I=1$:

- Broad-band signal: Gaussian law or normal law $(I=0)$

${P}_{\text{pic}}^{+}\left(S\right)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2\pi {\sigma}_{{S}^{2}}}}{e}^{-\frac{{S}^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{{S}^{2}}}}$

- Narrow-band signal: Rayleigh law $(I=1)$

${P}_{\text{pic}}^{+}(S)=\frac{S}{{\sigma}_{{S}^{2}}}{e}^{-\frac{{S}^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{{S}^{2}}}}$

The stress-peak counting method associates with each peak of positive amplitude $S$ a cycle of amplitude $\Delta S=2S$ (one therefore has directly $S=\text{Salt}$).

The number of peaks of positive amplitude is written: ${n}_{\text{pic}}^{+}(S)={P}_{\text{pic}}^{+}(S)\times {N}_{\text{pic}}^{+}$

where ${N}_{\text{pic}}^{+}=\frac{1}{4}(1+I)\times \frac{1}{\pi}\frac{\sigma {S}^{''}}{{\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}}$ = mean number of positive peaks per unit time.

Hence the number $N$ of cycles to be taken into account is: $N(S)=\frac{1+I}{4\pi }\frac{{\sigma}_{{S}^{''}}}{{\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}}{P}_{\text{pic}}^{+}(S)$

**Note:**

*One indeed observes that the expression for the number* $N$ *of cycles to be taken into account depends only on* ${s}_{S}$ *(for the computation of the irregularity factor* $I$ *),* ${\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}$ *and* ${\sigma}_{{S}^{''}}$*.*

### 3.3 Level-exceedance counting method

This method requires the stress variations to be divided into amplitude classes.

The number of cycles $N(S)$ is obtained from the difference between the numbers of level exceedances with a positive slope between two successive classes, starting from the class of maximum amplitude.

For a centred Gaussian process the expression for $N(S)$ is: $N(S)=\frac{1}{\pi}\frac{{\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}}{{\sigma}_{S}^{}}{e}^{-\frac{{S}^{2}}{2{\sigma}_{S}^{2}}}$

**Notes:**

*The expression for the number* $N$ *of cycles to be taken into account requires only knowledge of* ${\sigma}_{S}$ *and* ${\sigma}_{{S}^{'}}$ *(independence with respect to* ${\sigma}_{{S}^{''}}$*).*

*The irregularity coefficient* $I$ *does not appear in this method.*

## 4 Statistical estimation of the damage

The mechanical damage is computed using Miner's linear cumulative rule.

The damage $D$ produced by $N$ cycles of half-amplitude $S$ is expressed by $D=\frac{N}{\text{Nr}(S)}$

where $\text{Nr}(S)$ is the allowable number of cycles determined by the endurance curve of the material.

The mechanical damage is a random variable whose mean is determined.

### 4.1 Mean damage

The mean damage is written in the form of the mathematical expectation:

$E(D)=T\underset{{S}_{\min}}{\overset{{S}_{\max}}{\int}}\frac{N(S)}{{N}_{r}(S)}\text{dS}$ where $T$ is the duration of the signal

The two counting methods proposed compute the number of cycles $N(S)$ from stresses of positive amplitude, whence ${S}_{\min}=0$ (except when the Wöhler curve is given in the “current zone” form, in which case ${S}_{\min}={S}_{l}$ with ${S}_{l}$ the endurance limit of the material).

Furthermore, the distribution laws used being continuous, ${S}_{\max}=\infty$. However, experience shows that the expression to be integrated decays rapidly, and one therefore takes ${S}_{\max}=10{\sigma}_{S}$, where ${\sigma}_{S}$ is the standard deviation of the signal.

The computation of $E(D)$ is carried out by numerical integration, by the trapezoidal method, taking as the integration step $\frac{{S}_{\max}-{S}_{\min}}{300.}$.

**Note:**

*In the case of the level-exceedance counting method and for a Wöhler curve expressed in the mathematical form proposed by Basquin, the mean damage per unit time has an analytical expression (this expression is not used in the command POST_FATI_ALEA).*

## 5 Conclusion

In the case of a Gaussian, ergodic and stationary loading, two cycle-counting methods admit a statistical interpretation and provide an analytical expression for the mean damage, involving only the formulation of the endurance curve of the material and the standard deviations of the signal and of its first and second derivatives.

In *Code_Aster*, the computation of the damage under random loading is performed by the command POST_FATI_ALEA [U4.67.05](doc:u4.67.05).

The user may determine the damage by the stress-peak counting method (COMPTAGE = ‘PIC’) or by the method of counting exceedances of a given level (COMPTAGE = ‘NIVEAU’).

Depending on the method adopted, the random signal must be introduced by giving the spectral moments of orders 0 and 2, or by giving the spectral moments of orders 0, 2 and 4 (keywords MOMENT_SPEC_0, MOMENT_SPEC_2 and MOMENT_SPEC_4). The values of the spectral moments may also be retrieved from a table created by POST_DYNA_ALEA [U4.76.02](doc:u4.76.02) (keyword TABLE).

The Wöhler curve of the material may be introduced in three distinct forms (in accordance with the command POST_FATIGUE [U4.67.01](doc:u4.67.01) (computation of high-cycle damage) and the POSTDAM software).

The quantity (grandeur) determined is the mean damage over the duration of the signal, which is stored in a table of type POST_FATI_ALEA.
