## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Quantities and reference results

#### 2.1.1 Plate analytical model

The plate response is considered to be the response of a console beam (car $\nu =0$). In this case, the plate displacement is given by the movements of each section of the plate, following the direction $x(u)$ , $z(w)$ and their rotation in relation to the $y({\theta}_{y})$ axis.

${U}_{x}(x,z)=u(x)+z{\theta}_{y}(x)$ ${U}_{z}(x,z)=w(x)$

In all point, the deformations are:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}(x,z)=u'+z{\theta}_{y}'$ et $2{\varepsilon}_{xz}(x,z)={\theta}_{y}+w'={\gamma}_{z}$

> The beam is clamped in $x=0$ and subjected to a vertical force $F$ in $x=L$.

Resolution (virtual work principle and force balance) gives expression to the generalized forces in the case of an elastic linear response: $N$ is the axial force, ${M}_{y}$ the moment following the $y$ axis and $T$ the shear force.

$N(x)=EAu'(x)$ et $>$

${M}_{y}(x)=EI{\theta}_{y}'(x)$ $>$

$T(x)=GkA{\gamma}_{z}$ $>$

> where the parameter $k$ is the shear correction factor

With boundary conditions, the $u,w\mathit{et}{\theta}_{y}$ moves are deduced:

$>$ , $>$ , ${\theta}_{y}(x)=\frac{F}{EI}\frac{x(x-2L)}{2}$

The deformations and stresses are:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=z\frac{-F}{EI}(L-x)$ et ${\sigma}_{xx}=z\frac{-F}{I}(L-x)$

$2{\varepsilon}_{xz}=\frac{F}{GkA}={\gamma}_{z}$ et ${\sigma}_{xz}=\frac{-F}{A}6({z}^{2}/{h}^{2}-1/4)$

where $h$ is the height of the plate. The distribution of shear stresses is parabolic in the thickness of the plate to meet the conditions of free surface

( ${\sigma}_{xz}(z=h/2)={\sigma}_{xz}(z=-h/2)=0$ ).

The vertical displacement response of the point $x=L$ is:

${U}_{z}(L)=\frac{F}{3EI}{L}^{3}+\frac{FL}{GkA}$

### 2.2 Uncertainties about the solution

The reference solution is analytical, so there is no uncertainty.

## 9 Summary of the results

- For a plate with one or more layers, elements DST (QUAD4) gives good estimates of displacement, generalized forces, deformations and stresses (Modelization A and B).
- For a finer mesh, elements Q4G (QUAD4) leads to slightly greater errors, especially for SIXX axial stresses (C and D Modelling).
- The two types of elements DST (QUAD4) and Q4G (QUAD4) give the same results with one (Modelling A and C) or two layers (Modelling B and D) respectively.
- Elements DST (TRIA3) results much less well (modellings E and F). In particular, there are significant mesh effects: modellings E and F have the same number of elements but the mesh E is set while the mesh F is free. The error on the deflection is 13% for E and 1% for F. Similar differences are noted at the times. There is also a strong dissymmetry on the moments between the $\mathit{A1}$ and $\mathit{A4}$ points for the 2 meshes. This is due to the fact that the meshes are not symmetrical compared to the load. On a symmetrical mesh the problem no longer appears. This element has a very dependent bending behavior on the mesh, which undoubtedly deserves further study.
