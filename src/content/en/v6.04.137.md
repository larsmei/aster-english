# v6.04.137 SSNV137 - Prestress cable in a concrete straight beam

**Summary**

A square cross sectioned straight beam is considered to be concrete, crossed along its length by a steel prestress cable. In the resting state, the cable is parallel to the average fiber of the beam and out of the center of the two main planes. The beam and cable are clamped-free. The cable is pulled at its free end, in order to prestress the beam in bending-compression. The tension losses along the cable are neglected.

The purpose of this test case is to validate the equilibrium state calculation method of a prestressed concrete structure, when this structure is modelled by elements 3D, associated with the base elements representing the prestress cable.

The specific features to be tested are:

- DEFI_CABLE_BP operator: determination of kinematic relationships between degrees of freedom of nodes of a cable and degrees of freedom of nodes "neighbors" of a concrete structure modelled by elements 3D;
- STAT_NON_LINE operator, option COMPORTEMENT: calculation of the equilibrium state.

The results obtained are validated by comparison with a reference analytical solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The concrete beam is straight, square section.

Its dimensions are $L\times a\times a=3m\times 0,4m\times 0,4m$ .

The cable crosses the beam parallel to the medium fiber and is eccentricated from the two main planes. The eccentricities in the $y$ and $z$ directions are valid respectively

${e}_{y}=-0,12m$ et ${e}_{z}=-0,16m$ .

The right section of the cable is ${S}_{a}=2,5{.10}^{-3}{m}^{2}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

Concrete material forming beam: Young's modulus ${E}_{b}=4,5{.10}^{10}\mathit{Pa}$

Steel material making up cable: Young's modulus ${E}_{a}=1,85{.10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$

The Poisson's ratio is taken equal to 0 for both materials, so the Poisson effects are cancelled in the $y$ and $z$ directions.

The loss of tension in the cable is neglected, and the various parameters used for their estimation are set at 0.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The nodes of the beam on the $x=0$ side is blocked in translation in three directions. Among these nodes are the "neighbors" of the node left end of the cable, which is therefore blocked in translation by kinematic relations. Therefore, you should not impose additional boundary conditions in this node, which would be redundant with kinematic relations and make it impossible to resolve in motion (synthetic matrix).

The node right end of the cable is applied a normal $({F}_{\mathrm{0 }};\mathrm{0 };0)$ traction force, with ${F}_{0}={10}^{6}N$.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The concrete beam is represented by 60 elements MECA_HEXA20, supported by as many mesh cells hexahedrons at 20 nodes. The figure below gives a simplified representation of the mesh of the beam.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.137/Shape4.gif)

Concrete material is assigned to elements, for which the ELAS (Young's modulus ${E}_{b}=4,5{.10}^{10}\mathrm{Pa}$) and BPEL_BETON behaviors are defined: the characteristic parameters of this relationship are set to 0 because the voltage losses along the prestress cable are neglected.

The degrees of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$ , $\mathrm{DY}$ , and $\mathrm{DZ}$ of the nodes face $x=0$ are blocked.

The cable is represented by 30 elements MECA_BARRE, supported by as many mesh cells segments as 2 nodes. The left and right ends are the nodes $\mathrm{NC000001}$ and $\mathrm{NC000031}$ respectively.

A straight section area ${S}_{a}=2,5{.10}^{-3}{m}^{2}$ is assigned to elements, as well as a steel material for which the ELAS (Young's modulus ${E}_{a}=1,85{.10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$) and BPEL_ACIER behaviors are defined: the characteristic parameters of this relationship are set to 0 (loss of neglected voltage), with the exception of the elastic limit stress for which a null value is unlawful (${f}_{\mathit{prg}}=1,77{.10}^{9}\mathit{Pa}$).

To avoid redundancy with kinematic relationships, no blocking is imposed on node $\mathit{NC000001}$ (see note in paragraph [§2.3].

The ${F}_{0}={10}^{6}N$ voltage is applied to node $\mathit{NC000031}$ . This voltage value is consistent with the section and elastic limit values for a prestressed toron cable.

The equilibrium state of the beam and cable assembly is calculated in one step, the behaviour being elastic. A further calculation is then made to determine the stresses at nodes of the beam elements.

### 3.2 Calculation steps and tested features

The main steps in calculating are those that you want to validate:

- DEFI_MATERIAU operator: definition of BPEL_BETON and BPEL_ACIER behavior relationships, in the particular case where voltage losses along the prestress cable are neglected (default values of parameters);
- DEFI_CABLE_BP operator: determination of a constant voltage profile along the prestress cable, with losses neglected; calculation of the coefficients of kinematic relationships between degrees of freedom of the nodes of the cable and degrees of freedom of the nodes "neighbors" of the concrete beam, in the case of a beam modelled by elements 3D;
- AFFE_CHAR_MECA operator: definition of a RELA_CINE_BP load;
- STAT_NON_LINE operator, option COMPORTEMENT: calculation of the equilibrium state taking into account the RELA_CINE_BP type loading, in the case of a beam modelled by elements 3D.

## 5 Summary of the results

The calculated values correspond to those theoretically expected. A bending-compression state is obtained for the concrete beam.

The larger differences observed in some nodes closer to the free end can be explained by the more or less good fit of a 3D model for a beam structure. Thus the mesh remains coarse enough not to increase the cost of the calculation. Finally, it is recalled that the reference solution is established under the assumptions of beam theory.
