# r5.04.01 Non-local modelling with gradients of internal variables GRAD_VARI

**Summary**

The non-local modelling with a gradient of internal variables entitled GRAD_VARI in Code_Aster is presented here. This modelling stems from the work of E. Lorentz [Bib 1]. The algorithm making it possible to solve the equilibrium and regularisation equations has however been modified relative to the initial version of the model.

The non-local modellings of type GRAD_VARI are available in 3D (3D_GRAD_VARI), axisymmetric (AXIS_GRAD_VARI) and plane strain (D_PLAN_GRAD_VARI).

Unlike the former version, the use of GRAD_VARI is relatively simple, since it is sufficient to specify the modelling X_GRAD_VARI in AFFE_MODELE, to specify a characteristic length under the keyword NON_LOCAL in DEFI_MATERIAU, and to check that the constitutive law one wishes to use is indeed available in a non-local version.

The formulation and the numerical treatment of this model are presented.

## 1 Reminder of the theory of gradient models

The gradient models presented here were developed by E. Lorentz [bib1] in order to be able to describe the behaviour of materials subjected to strong gradients of the mechanical fields which appear in the damaged zones or else in the neighbourhood of geometric singularities. Indeed, in the case of strong gradients, the behaviour of a material point is no longer independent of its surroundings but depends on the behaviour of its neighbourhood, whence the introduction of gradients into the models.

From a numerical point of view, the analysis of a structure with a classical local damage law shows that the damaged zone always localises on a single layer of finite elements and therefore that the response of the structure depends on the mesh adopted: gradient models remedy this problem.

In what follows, a brief reminder of this theory is given.

### 1.1 Construction of gradient models

This formulation is restricted to generalised standard materials [Bib 2].

Models with a gradient of internal variables consist in introducing the gradient of internal variables into a generalised standard formulation [Bib 2].

Let $a$ be an internal variable and $A$ its associated thermodynamic force, and let $\Delta (\dot{a})$ be the dissipation potential. If one considers that $\Delta$ also depends on the gradient of $\dot{a}$, $\Delta =\Delta (\dot{a},\nabla \dot{a})$, one then does not know how to write the normality principle locally:

$A\in \partial \Delta (\dot{a},\nabla \dot{a})$

Indeed, such a formulation would require the introduction of 2 locally independent variables $a$ and ${a}_{\nabla}$, with which 2 thermodynamic forces would be associated, $A=-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial a}$, ${A}_{\nabla}=-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {a}_{\nabla}}$, such that:

$(A,{A}_{\nabla})\in \partial \Delta (\dot{a},{\dot{a}}_{\nabla})$

If $F$ denotes the elasticity threshold associated with the potential $\Delta (\dot{a},{\dot{a}}_{\nabla})$, the previous equation is equivalent to:

$\Delta (\dot{a},{\dot{a}}_{\nabla})=\underset{(A,{A}_{\nabla})/F(A,{A}_{\nabla})\le 0}{\text{Sup}\left[\dot{a}A+{\dot{a}}_{\nabla}{A}_{\nabla}\right]}$

And one has:

$\dot{a}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial F}{\partial A}$ , ${\dot{a}}_{\nabla}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial F}{\partial {A}_{\nabla}}$

The problem here is that the variables are not independent and are linked by the non-local constraint ${a}_{\nabla}=\nabla a$, so that one is not sure of satisfying:

${\dot{a}}_{\nabla}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial F}{\partial {A}_{\nabla}}=\nabla \dot{a}$

It is then proposed to drop the assumption of normal flow at each point of the structure while retaining the formalism of generalised standard materials, but at the scale of the structure, where the state variables are now the *field* of strain $\varepsilon$ and the *field* of internal variables $a$. The global free energy and the global dissipation potential are thus defined:

${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a)=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\Phi (\varepsilon (x),a(x),\nabla a(x))\mathrm{dx}$

$D(\dot{a})=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\Delta (\dot{a}(x),\nabla \dot{a}(x))\mathrm{dx}$

The global dissipation potential $D$ is now a function of the *field* $\dot{a}$, and the formulation $A\in \partial D(\dot{a})$ recovers a meaning.

The generalised constitutive relation is now written:

$\sigma =\frac{\partial {F}_{\Phi}}{\partial \varepsilon }$ , $A=-\frac{\partial {F}_{\Phi}}{\partial a}$ , $A\in \partial D(\dot{a})$

### 1.2 Dualisation and discretisation in time

It will be assumed throughout what follows that the energy of the regularised model is the sum of the energy of the local model and of an additional term depending only on the gradient of the internal variable that is being regularised:

${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a)=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\Phi}^{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon (x),a(x))+{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}(\nabla a(x))\mathrm{dx}$

This separation corresponds to the cases treated in Code_Aster, but it is possible to construct gradient models which do not correspond to this case.

The global free energy ${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a)$ involves the gradient of the internal-variable field $a$. Now, it is known that the treatment of the internal variables by the finite-element method in Code_Aster is carried out at the Gauss points, where no information on the gradients is available. An additional field $\alpha$ is therefore introduced, which will be defined at the nodes, on which the additional term depending on the gradient will bear, and which will be required to be equal to the field a at every point. The global free energy is then written: ${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha )=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\Phi}^{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon (x),a(x))+{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}(\nabla \alpha (x))\mathrm{dx}$

with the condition $\alpha =a$.

In order to remove the constraint $\alpha =a$, the latter is dualised by introducing a Lagrange multiplier. The function is introduced:

${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha ,\lambda )=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\Phi}^{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon (x),a(x))+{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}(\nabla \alpha (x))+\lambda (a-\alpha )\mathrm{dx}$

where $\lambda$ is a Lagrange multiplier and one has ${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha )=\underset{\lambda}{\max}{F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha ,\lambda )$

Finally, a quadratic penalisation term is added on the compatibility condition between the fields $a$ and $\alpha$:

${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha ,\lambda )=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\Phi}^{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon (x),a(x))+{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}(\nabla \alpha (x))+\lambda (\alpha -a)+\frac{r}{2}{(\alpha -a)}^{2}\mathrm{dx}$

where $r$ is a penalisation coefficient. This additional penalisation term does not modify the problem, since it must tend to 0 at convergence, but it helps convergence.

Relying on the assumption of convexity with respect to $a$ of the potentials ${F}_{\Phi}$ and $D$ and adopting an implicit Euler scheme, the time discretisation of the previous problem [eq 1.1-8] reduces to the solution of a minimisation problem bearing on the increment $\Delta a$ of the internal-variable fields. This problem is written, for time-independent behaviours:

$\underset{\Delta a}{\min}(\underset{\lambda}{\max}{F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,\alpha ,\lambda ,{a}^{-}+\Delta a)+D(\Delta a))$

where ${a}^{-}$ represents the field of internal variables at the previous time instant. This minimisation constitutes the constitutive law.

Likewise, relying on the assumption of convexity with respect to $(u,\alpha )$ of the potential ${F}_{\Phi}$ and adopting an implicit Euler scheme, the solution of the equilibrium equations reduces to a minimisation problem bearing on the increments $(\Delta u,\Delta \alpha )$. The discretised problem is then written:

$\underset{\Delta \lambda }{\max}\underset{\Delta u}{\min}\underset{\Delta \alpha }{\min}\underset{\Delta a}{\min}({F}_{\Phi}({\varepsilon}^{-}+\Delta \varepsilon ,{\alpha}^{-}+\Delta \alpha ,{\lambda}^{-}+\Delta \lambda ,{a}^{-}+\Delta a)+D(\Delta a)-{W}^{\text{ext}})$

where ${W}^{\text{ext}}$ represents the work of the external mechanical forces.

### 1.3 Spatial discretisation by finite elements

In order to solve the problem of searching for extrema, a spatial discretisation by finite elements is carried out for the following quantities:

Displacement: $\varepsilon (x)=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}{B}_{{k}^{u}}(x){u}_{k}$

“Regularised” internal variable: $\alpha (x)=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}{N}_{{k}^{\alpha}}(x){\alpha}_{k}$.

The gradient is then expressed by means of the gradient of the shape functions: $\nabla \alpha (x)=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}\nabla {N}_{{k}^{\alpha}}(x){\alpha}_{k}$

Lagrange multiplier: $\lambda (x)=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}{N}_{{k}^{\lambda}}(x){\lambda}_{k}$

where ${N}_{{k}^{\alpha}}$ (resp. ${N}_{{k}^{\lambda}}$) are the shape functions associated with the node $k$ for the field $\alpha$ (resp. $\lambda$), and ${B}_{{k}^{u}}$ are the strain shape functions computed from the derivatives of the shape functions associated with the node $k$ for the displacements.

The shape functions are differentiated according to the field, in so far as the degree of interpolation used may differ according to the quantity considered.

In the finite-element model with integration at the Gauss points, the integral over the volume of the structure is evaluated by summation over the Gauss points. The energy ${F}_{\Phi}$ is therefore written, for the spatially discretised problem:

${F}_{\Phi}(\varepsilon ,a,\alpha ,\lambda )=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\underset{{\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{nonlocal}}}({\varepsilon}_{g},{\alpha}_{g},{\lambda}_{g},{a}_{g})}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left[{\Phi}^{\text{loc}}({\varepsilon}_{g},{a}_{g})+{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}((\nabla \alpha {)}_{g})+{\lambda}_{g}({\alpha}_{g}-{a}_{g})+\frac{r}{2}{({\alpha}_{g}-{a}_{g})}^{2}\right]}}$

where ${\omega}_{g}$ corresponds to the weight of the Gauss points, and the index $g$ indicates that the field is evaluated at the Gauss points from the nodal values:

${\varepsilon}_{g}=\sum_{\text{k noeuds}}{B}_{k}^{u}({x}_{g}){u}_{k}$

${\alpha}_{g}=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}{N}_{{k}^{\alpha}}({x}_{g}){\alpha}_{k}$

$(\nabla \alpha {)}_{g}=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}\nabla {N}_{{k}^{\alpha}}({x}_{g}){\alpha}_{k}$

${\lambda}_{g}=\sum_{k\text{noeuds}}{N}_{{k}^{\lambda}}({x}_{g}){\lambda}_{k}$

The solution of the discretised incremental problem therefore corresponds to the following algorithm:

| Nodes | $\Delta {u}_{\mathit{pred}}$ , $\Delta {\alpha}_{\mathit{pred}}$ , $\Delta {\lambda}_{\mathit{pred}}$ |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${u}^{-}+\Delta u$ , ${\alpha}^{-}+\Delta \alpha$ , ${\lambda}^{-}+\Delta \lambda$ |
|  |  |
|  | ${\varepsilon}_{g}$ , ${\alpha}_{g}$ , ${\lambda}_{g}$ |
| Gauss points | $\underset{\Delta {a}_{g}}{min}\left[{\Phi}_{g}^{\mathit{non}\mathit{local}}({\varepsilon}_{g},{\alpha}_{g},{\lambda}_{g},{a}_{g}^{-}+\Delta {a}_{g})+{D}_{g}(\Delta {a}_{g})\right]$ |
|  | $\Delta {a}_{g}$ , ${\sigma}_{g}$ , tangent matrix |
|  |  |
| Nodes | $\underset{\Delta u}{min}\underset{\Delta \alpha }{min}\underset{\Delta \lambda }{max}\left[{F}_{\Phi}({u}^{-}+\Delta u,{\alpha}^{-}+\Delta \alpha ,{\lambda}^{-}+\Delta \lambda ,a)-{F}^{\mathit{ext}}\right]$ |

### 1.4 Integration of the constitutive law at the Gauss points

The constitutive law is computed at the Gauss point. The evolution of the internal variable $a$ at the Gauss point is the solution of the following minimisation:

$\underset{\Delta {a}_{g}}{\min}[{\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{nonlocal}}}({\varepsilon}_{g},{\alpha}_{g},{\lambda}_{g},{a}_{{g}^{-}}+\Delta {a}_{g})+{D}_{g}(\Delta {a}_{g})]$

Dualisation of the internal variable makes it possible to recover a normal flow law at the level of each Gauss point, as for the local modelling:

$-\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{nonlocal}}}}{\partial {a}_{g}}\in \partial ({D}_{g})$

When the thermodynamic force associated with the internal variable *a* is computed by differentiating the “non-local” energy, one sees that this simply amounts to adding, to the thermodynamic force of the local modelling, a term coming from the regularisation energy:

$-\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{nonlocal}}}}{\partial {a}_{g}}=-\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{loc}}}}{\partial {a}_{g}}+\left[{\lambda}_{g}+r({\alpha}_{g}-{a}_{g})\right]$

This additional term, depending on the gradient of the internal variable as will be seen below, has the effect of shifting the value of the threshold relative to the local modelling in the presence of a gradient.

In practice, in so far as this non-local modelling is applied to generalised standard constitutive laws, it “suffices” to modify the expression for the thermodynamic force in the criterion, and to adapt the solution of the non-linear system to this new criterion.

Once the evolution of the internal variable has been computed, the stress is computed by differentiating the energy with respect to the strain field:

${\sigma}_{g}=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{nonlocal}}}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{{g}^{\text{loc}}}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}$

It is immediately noted that the expression for the stress is unchanged relative to the local modelling.

Besides the evolution of the internal variable and the stress, the following terms must also be computed, which are useful for the tangent correction matrix (cf. section 1.5):

$\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{g}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}},\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}},\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\alpha}_{g}},\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\lambda}_{g}}$

It should be noted that $\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\alpha}_{g}}=r\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\lambda}_{g}}$. It is therefore sufficient to output $\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\lambda}_{g}}$

### 1.5 Calculation of the internal forces

The mechanical equilibrium equation and the regularisation equation are solved at the nodes by searching for local extrema:

$\underset{\Delta \lambda }{\max}\underset{\Delta u}{\min}\underset{\Delta \alpha }{\min}({F}_{\Phi}({\varepsilon}^{-}+\Delta \varepsilon ,{\alpha}^{-}+\Delta \alpha ,{\lambda}^{-}+\Delta \lambda ,a)-{W}^{\text{ext}})$

In what follows, the external mechanical forces are set aside; they are treated separately in Code_Aster. The internal forces associated with the nodal variables of the node $n$ $({u}_{n},{\alpha}_{n},{\lambda}_{n})$ have the expression:

${F}^{u}{\mid}_{n}=\frac{\partial {F}_{\Phi}}{\partial {u}_{n}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\frac{\partial {\Phi}^{\text{loc}}}{\partial {u}_{n}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\frac{\partial {\Phi}^{\text{loc}}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}:\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}{\partial {u}_{n}}=\underset{{\int}_{\Omega}^{}{B}^{T}\sigma d\Omega }{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{\sigma}_{g}:{B}_{n}^{u}}}$

${F}^{\alpha}{\mid}_{n}=\frac{\partial {F}_{\Phi}}{\partial {\alpha}_{n}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\left[\frac{\partial {\Phi}^{\text{grad}}}{\partial {(\nabla \alpha )}_{g}}{(\nabla N)}_{n}^{\alpha}+\left[{\lambda}_{g}+r({\alpha}_{g}-{a}_{g})\right]{N}_{n}^{\alpha}\right]$

${F}^{\lambda}{\mid}_{n}=\frac{\partial {F}_{\Phi}}{\partial {\lambda}_{n}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}({\alpha}_{g}-{a}_{g}){N}_{n}^{\lambda}$

The tangent matrix is written as follows:

$K=(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{\partial {F}^{u}}{\partial u}& \frac{\partial {F}^{u}}{\partial \alpha }& \frac{\partial {F}^{u}}{\partial \lambda }\\ \frac{\partial {F}^{\alpha}}{\partial u}& \frac{\partial {F}^{\alpha}}{\partial \alpha }& \frac{\partial {F}^{\alpha}}{\partial \lambda }\\ \frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}}{\partial u}& \frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}}{\partial \alpha }& \frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}}{\partial \lambda }\end{array})$

The framework of generalised standard materials within which the modelling with a gradient of an internal variable is set ensures the symmetry of the tangent matrix. It is therefore sufficient to compute the lower triangular matrix:

$\frac{\partial {F}^{u}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {u}_{m}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{B}_{m}^{u}:\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{g}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}:{B}_{n}^{u}$

$\frac{\partial {F}^{\alpha}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {u}_{m}}=-\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}r{B}_{m}^{u}\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}{N}_{n}^{\alpha}$

$\frac{\partial {F}^{\alpha}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {\alpha}_{m}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\left[{(\nabla N)}_{m}^{\alpha}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\Phi}^{\text{grad}}}{{\partial}^{2}{(\nabla \alpha )}_{g}}{(\nabla N)}_{n}^{\alpha}+{\text{rN}}_{m}^{\alpha}(1-\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\alpha}_{g}}){N}_{n}^{\alpha}\right]$

$\frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {u}_{m}}=-\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{B}_{m}^{u}\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{g}}{N}_{n}^{\lambda}$

$\frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {\alpha}_{m}}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{N}_{m}^{\alpha}\left[1-\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\alpha}_{g}}\right]{N}_{n}^{\lambda}$

$\frac{\partial {F}^{\lambda}{\mid}_{n}}{\partial {\lambda}_{m}}=-\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{N}_{m}^{\lambda}\frac{\partial {a}_{g}}{\partial {\lambda}_{g}}{N}_{n}^{\lambda}$

## 2 Choice of the finite elements

The introduction of new nodal variables requires the use of elements compatible with the new formulation. One is in the presence of three nodal unknowns: the displacements $u$, the regularised variable $\alpha$ and the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$.

The internal-variable field being linked to the strains through the criterion, it is preferable to take the same degree of interpolation for the regularised internal-variable field $\alpha$ as for the strain, that is one degree less than the displacements from which the strains are derived. The same degree of interpolation is chosen for the Lagrange multiplier $\lambda$ as for the regularised variable $\alpha$.

Shape functions ${P}^{2}$ for $u$, ${P}^{1}$ for $\alpha$ and ${P}^{1}$ for $\lambda$ are therefore considered.

The quadratic elements, TRIA6 and QUAD8 for 2D, TETRA10, PENTA15 and HEXA20 for 3D, have been developed. The displacement components are assigned to all the nodes of the element, whereas the components of the other two nodal variables are assigned only to the vertex nodes. ## 5 Advice / procedure for the implementation of a new constitutive law with gradients of internal variables

As was seen in section 1.4, the implementation of a new constitutive law with a gradient of internal variables, starting from a local law, is relatively simple, provided that the local law respects the framework of generalised standard materials and, more particularly, that the evolution criterion of the internal variables involves a threshold function convex with respect to the thermodynamic forces associated with the internal variables (the thermodynamic force associated with the variable $\alpha$ is defined as follows: ${F}^{a}=-\frac{\partial W}{\partial a}$ where $W$ is the free energy of the structure) and that a normal flow law is adopted in order to compute the evolution of the internal variables.

The table below summarises the steps for passing from a local constitutive law to a non-local constitutive law:

| Type | Local model | Non-local model |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thermodynamic force | ${F}^{{a}_{\text{local}}}$ | ${F}^{{a}_{\text{non}\text{local}}}={F}^{{a}_{\text{local}}}+\lambda +r\alpha -\text{ra}$ |
| Threshold function / criterion | $f\left[{F}^{{a}_{\text{local}}}\right]\le 0$ | $f\left[{F}^{{a}_{\text{non}\text{local}}}\right]\le 0$ This is the same threshold function, but depending on a modified thermodynamic force |
| Evolution (normal flow) ($\eta$ Lagrange multiplier) | $\Delta a=\Delta \eta \frac{\partial f}{\partial {F}^{{a}_{\text{local}}}}$ | $\Delta a=\Delta \eta \frac{\partial f}{\partial {F}^{{a}_{\text{non}\text{local}}}}$ In practice, it is sufficient to adapt the solution of the criterion to the new thermodynamic force |
| Stress | $\sigma (\varepsilon ,a)$ | $\sigma (\varepsilon ,a)$ The expression for the stress is unchanged |
| Tangent matrix | $\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }$ | $\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }$ unchanged $\frac{\partial a}{\partial \varepsilon }$ , $\frac{\partial a}{\partial \lambda }$ (additional terms) |

The developer of a new non-local constitutive law will have to rely on the sources of the laws already implemented in order to see how the additional terms (regularising terms and additional terms of the tangent matrix) must be passed into the various routines. The whole part concerning the construction of the internal-force vector and of the correction matrix at the global level does not have to be modified by the developer; it is sufficient to respect the programming rules in force for the laws already implemented.
