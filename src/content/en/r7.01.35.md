# r7.01.35 Constitutive relations BETON_BURGER and BETON_AGEING for the creep of concrete

**Summary:**

This document presents the constitutive models BETON_BURGER and BETON_AGEING, making it possible to represent the creep of concrete (basic and desiccation). These models draw heavily on the structure already set up in the model BETON_UMLV.

These models are implemented under MFront and integrated directly into code_aster.

## 1 Introduction

Within the framework of studies of the long-term behaviour of concrete structures, a predominant part of the strains measured on a structure concerns the delayed strains which appear in the concrete in the course of its life. One speaks of “shrinkage” when the strain is measured on a specimen not subjected to mechanical loading and of “creep” when the strain takes place under mechanical loading. The exchanges of water between the specimen and the environment influence the final strain considerably. This observation classically gives rise to further distinctions on the delayed strains. One then distinguishes the early-age shrinkages, the desiccation shrinkage, the basic creep and the desiccation creep. The classification of the delayed strains of a concrete structure is recalled below:

- at early age without load:

1. the autogenous shrinkage (1 day – 1 year)
2. the thermal shrinkage (1 hour – 1 day)

caused by a thermo-hydration reaction.

- in the medium term without load: the desiccation shrinkage (a few months – a few years), depending on the dimensions of the structure, caused by drying, which results in an evaporation of part of the water not used in the hydration process.
- in the long term under load:

1. the basic creep, which is the part of the creep of the concrete that one would observe during a test without exchange of water with the exterior.
2. the desiccation creep, in addition to the basic creep, is the part of the total creep directly linked to the departure of water affecting the concrete which undergoes a mechanical loading on the one hand and drying on the other.

The models presented here, under the names BETON_BURGER (r7.01.35-description_beton_burger) and BETON_AGEING (r7.01.35-description_beton_ageing), are devoted to the modelling of the delayed strain associated with creep, basic and desiccation.

## 2 Description of the model BETON_BURGER

### 2.1 Hypotheses

The law is written within the framework of small perturbations. The total strain tensor is decomposed into several terms relating to the processes considered. One admits that the total strain is written:

$$
\strain = \underbrace{\strain^e}_{\text{déf. élastique}} + \underbrace{\strain^{fp}}_{\text{fluage propre}} + \underbrace{\strain^{fdess}}_{\text{fluage de dessiccation}} + \underbrace{\strain^{re}}_{\text{retrait endogène}} + \underbrace{\strain^{rd}}_{\text{retrait de dessiccation}} + \underbrace{\strain^{th}}_{\text{déf. thermique}}
$$

In this document, one does not describe the taking into account of the various types of shrinkage (for that, see the documentation [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12)), nor of the thermal strain, so that it reduces to:

$$
\strain = \strain^e + \strain^{fp} + \strain^{fdess}
$$

In general, basic creep may be modelled by combining the elastic behaviour of the solid and the viscous behaviour of the fluid. For the law presented, basic creep is described as the combination of the elastic behaviour of the hydrates and of the aggregates and of the viscous behaviour of the water. In the case of the model BETON_BURGER, one makes the hypothesis that the basic creep may be decomposed into a process decoupling a spherical part and a deviatoric part. One then decomposes the tensors of the basic-creep strains and of the stresses as:

$$
\strain^{fp} = \underbrace{\varepsilon^{fp}_s\tensTwoUnit}_{\text{partie sphérique}} + \underbrace{\strain^{fp}_d}_{\text{partie déviatorique}} \quad \text{et} \quad \stress = \underbrace{\sigma_s\tensTwoUnit}_{\text{partie sphérique}} + \underbrace{\stress_d}_{\text{partie déviatorique}}
$$

The model BETON_BURGER assumes a total decoupling between the spherical and deviatoric components of the basic creep: the strains induced by the spherical stresses are purely spherical and the strains induced by the deviatoric stresses are purely deviatoric. On the other hand, the cumulative viscous strains have an effect on the viscous properties of the fluid, whatever their origin (spherical or deviatoric). In order to take account of the effect of the relative humidity $h$, the basic-creep strains are generated by the source term $h\stress$.

### 2.2 Elasticity

The elasticity is linear isotropic:

$$
\stress = \tensFour{C}:\strain^e
$$

with $\tensFour{C}$ the linear isotropic elasticity tensor, of Young's modulus $\youngModulus$ and of Poisson's ratio $\poissonCoef$.

### 2.3 Basic creep

In order to model the phenomenon of basic creep, the model proposed relies on simple rheological models (r7.01.35-schema_rheo) comprising in series an elastic body (described by the behaviour ELAS), a linear Voigt solid for the modelling of the reversible creep (recovery), and a Maxwell fluid with a non-linear viscosity in order to model the long-term creep. The spherical and deviatoric chains are equivalent in their construction. The Voigt stage has a strain limit governed by the elastic modulus. The particularity of the model rests on the choice of the non-linearity assigned to the viscosity of the Maxwell body.

#### 2.3.1 Description of the spherical part

The spherical strain of basic creep is written as the sum of a reversible part and of an irreversible part:

$$
\varepsilon^{fp}_s = \varepsilon^{fp}_{s,r} + \varepsilon^{fp}_{s, i}
$$

The process of spherical creep strain is governed by the following equations:

$$
h\sigma_s = k_{s,r}\varepsilon^{fp}_{s,r} + \eta_{s,r}\dot{\varepsilon}^{fp}_{s,r} \quad \text{et} \quad h\sigma_s = \eta_{s,i}\dot{\varepsilon}^{fp}_{s,i}
$$

with:

- $k_{s,r}$ the bulk modulus associated with the reversible spherical basic creep,
- $\eta_{s,r}$ the viscosity of the Voigt stage associated with the reversible spherical basic creep,
- $\eta_{s,i}$ the non-linear spherical viscosity (see ) of the Maxwell fluid.

#### 2.3.2 Description of the deviatoric part

The deviatoric strain of basic creep is likewise written as the sum of a reversible part and of an irreversible part:

$$
\strain^{fp}_d = \strain^{fp}_{d,r} + \strain^{fp}_{d, i}
$$

The process of deviatoric creep strain is governed by the following equations:

$$
h\stress_d = k_{d,r}\strain^{fp}_{d,r} + \eta_{d,r}\dot{\tensTwo{\varepsilon}^{fp}_{d,r} \quad \text{et} \quad h\stress_d = \eta_{d,i}\dot{\tensTwo{\varepsilon}^{fp}_{d,i}}}
$$

with:

- $k_{d,r}$ the shear modulus associated with the reversible deviatoric basic creep,
- $\eta_{d,r}$ the viscosity of the Voigt stage associated with the reversible deviatoric basic creep,
- $\eta_{d,i}$ the non-linear deviatoric viscosity (see ) of the Maxwell fluid.

#### 2.3.3 Description of the viscous non-linearity

The non-linearity of the viscosity is interpreted according to  as the result of a spherical consolidation of the specimen and of an entanglement or blocking of the displacements of the CSH sheets, constituents of the mortar. A “consolidation” coefficient is therefore introduced along the same idea in order to control the evolution of the viscosities. This additional coefficient acts on the evolution laws of the Maxwell bodies (spherical and deviatoric). It depends directly on the norm of the tensor of the cumulative irreversible delayed strains. This extension of the hypotheses set out by  makes it possible to take the non-linearity into account for every type of path (with or without spherical loading). The explicit formulation of the Maxwell bodies is the following:

$$
\eta_{s,i}=\eta_{s,i}^0\exp\left(\frac{\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|}{\kappa}\right) \quad \text{et} \quad \eta_{d,i}= \eta_{d,i}^0\exp\left(\frac{\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|}{\kappa}\right)
$$

with:

- $\eta_{s,i}^0$ the initial viscosity of the Maxwell fluid acting on the spherical part,
- $\eta_{d,i}^0$ the initial viscosity of the Maxwell fluid acting on the deviatoric part,
- $\kappa$ the non-linearity coefficient,
- $\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|$ the irreversible equivalent strain, defined as the norm of the complete tensor (spherical and deviatoric) of irreversible creep strains, the maximum value reached in the course of the loading.

Precisely, the construction of $\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|$ follows the following logic: $\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|=\max\left(\|\strainCmp_{m,i}^{fp}\|,\sqrt{\strain_{i}^{fp}:\strain_{i}^{fp}}\right)$.

#### 2.3.4 Restriction of the number of parameters of the model

The equivalence of the deviatoric and spherical rheological chains makes it possible to obtain, by complying with the following expression, an apparent creep Poisson's ratio $\poissonCoef$ that is constant:

$$
\frac{\eta_{s,i}^0}{\eta_{d,i}^0} = \frac{\eta_{s,r}}{\eta_{d,r}} = \frac{k_{r,s}}{k_{r,d}} = \frac{1+\poissonCoef}{1-2\poissonCoef}
$$

For the use of the model BETON_BURGER on uniaxial creep tests, one rarely has the radial strains of the specimens, which makes identification of the whole set of parameters of the model difficult. A first approximation consists in assuming the relation  and thus limits the number of parameters to be determined to four, $\eta_{s,i}^0, \eta_{s,r}, k_{r,s}, \poissonCoef$, as regards the basic creep (at constant temperature) of the model BETON_BURGER.

#### 2.3.5 Thermal activation of the basic-creep strains

The experimental results of a basic-creep test in temperature show that the creep strain is thermally activated. For temperatures $T$ below 55°C, it obeys an Arrhenius-type law:

$$
\strain^{fp}(T) = \strain^{fp}(T_0)\vartheta(T) \quad \text{avec} \quad \vartheta(T) = \exp\left(-\frac{E_{ac}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{T_0}\right)\right)
$$

with:

- ${T}_{0}$ the reference temperature,
- ${E}_{ac}$ the activation energy,
- $R$ the perfect-gas constant.

In order to satisfy the relation  while keeping a constant characteristic time, the stiffness of the springs and the viscosity of the dashpots are modified in the following manner:

$$
\frac{k_{s,r}(T)}{k_{s,r}(T_0)} = \frac{k_{d,r}(T)}{k_{d,r}(T_0)} = \frac{\eta_{s,r}(T)}{\eta_{s,r}(T_0)}
 = \frac{\eta_{d,r}(T)}{\eta_{d,r}(T_0)}
 = \frac{\eta_{s,i}^0(T)}{\eta_{s,i}^0(T_0)} = \frac{\eta_{d,i}^0(T)}{\eta_{d,i}^0(T_0)}
 = \frac{\kappa(T_0)}{\kappa(T)} = \frac{1}{\vartheta(T)}
$$

### 2.4 Desiccation creep

Following the experimental justifications presented in , desiccation creep is non-symmetric with respect to the variation of the relative humidity. It takes the strains into account only in the case of the most severe drying, as follows:

$$
\dot{\strain}^{fdess} = \frac{f(h,\dot h)\stress}{k^{fd}} \quad \text{avec} \quad
f(h,\dot h) =
\begin{cases}
-\dot{h} \quad &\text{si} \quad \dot{h} < 0 \quad \text{et} \quad h < \min_{\tau \leq t} h(\tau) \\
0 \quad &\text{sinon}
\end{cases}
$$

with $k^{fd}$ a material parameter, homogeneous to a stress.

## 3 Description of the model BETON_AGEING

### 3.1 Hypotheses

The model BETON_AGEING takes up to a large extent the hypotheses of the model BETON_BURGER. The total strain tensor is in particular always decomposed as:

$$
\strain = \strain^e + \strain^{fp} + \strain^{fdess} =
\strain^e + \strain^{fp}_r + \strain^{fp}_i + \strain^{fdess}
$$

The model BETON_AGEING is nevertheless distinguished from the model BETON_BURGER:

- by being a **linear viscoelastic** model,
- by taking account of an **ageing effect**,
- by being implemented in a **THHM framework**.

This last characteristic implies in particular that the mechanical behaviour is driven by the effective stress and not the total stress, but also that the relative humidity $h$ is not a command variable (unlike the practice of a chained analysis). It is computed with the aid of the capillary pressure $p_c$ and the temperature $T$, nodal unknowns in a THHM modelling, relying on Kelvin's law (see [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11)):

$$
h = \exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{M}_{vp}}{RT\rho_{lq}}p_c\right)
$$

with:

- $\mathcal{M}_{vp}$ the molar mass of water vapour,
- $\rho_{lq}$ the specific mass of liquid water.

The computation of the various components appearing in  is detailed below.

### 3.2 Elasticity

It is written:

$$
\stress' = \tensFour{C}:\strain^e
$$

with $\tensFour{C}$ the linear isotropic elasticity tensor, of Young's modulus $\youngModulus$ and of Poisson's ratio $\poissonCoef$, and $\stress'$ the effective-stress tensor, used by default in THHM ([R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11)). Indeed, in the case of the classical THHM framework, it is written in differential form by:

$$
d\stress' = d\stress + \left(dp_{gz}-S_{lq}(p_c)dp_c\right)\tensTwo{B}
$$

with:

- $p_{gz}$ the gas pressure,
- $S_{lq}$ the liquid saturation,
- $\tensTwo{B}$ the Biot tensor.

**Note**

The decomposition  corresponds to the particular case $\stress' = \stress + \stress^p$ in which the “hydraulic” stress $\stress^p$ follows a classical law in the poroelasticity of unsaturated media. Other options, for example the law HYDR_TABBAL, are presented in the documentation [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11).

### 3.3 Basic creep

#### 3.3.1 Description of the reversible strain

The evolution of the reversible strain of the basic creep $\stress^{fp}_r$ (Voigt chain) is governed by the equation:

$$
h\stress' = \vartheta(T)\left[\tensFour{C}_r:\strain^{fp}_r +\tensFour{V}_r:\dot{\strain}^{fp}_r\right]
$$

with $\vartheta(T)$ given in , $\tensFour{C}_r$ and $\tensFour{V}_r$ respectively two linear isotropic elasticity and viscosity tensors:

$$
\tensFour{C}_r = 3k_{s,r}\tensFour{J} + 2k_{d,r}\tensFour{K} \quad \text{et} \quad \tensFour{V}_r = 3\eta_{s,r}\tensFour{J} + 2\eta_{d,r}\tensFour{K}
$$

with:

- $k_{s,r}$ the bulk modulus associated with the reversible spherical basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$,
- $k_{d,r}$ the shear modulus associated with the reversible deviatoric basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$,
- $\eta_{s,r}$ the viscosity associated with the reversible spherical basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$,
- $\eta_{d,r}$ the viscosity associated with the reversible deviatoric basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$.

The fourth-order tensors $\tensFour{J}$ and $\tensFour{K}$ are defined in paragraph r7.01.35-notations.

#### 3.3.2 Description of the irreversible strain

The evolution of the irreversible strain of the basic creep $\strain^{fp}_i$ (Maxwell chain) is governed by the equation:

$$
h\stress' = \vartheta(T)\tensFour{V}_i(t):\dot{\strain}^{fp}_i
$$

where $\tensFour{V}_i(t)$ is a linear isotropic viscosity tensor, with ageing effect, written:

$$
\tensFour{V}_i(t) = \left[3k_{s,i}\tensFour{J} + 2k_{d,i}\tensFour{K}\right]\left\langle t-t_0\right\rangle_+
$$

with:

- $k_{s,i}$ the *slope* of the viscosity associated with the irreversible spherical basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$,
- $k_{d,i}$ the *slope* of the viscosity associated with the irreversible deviatoric basic creep at the reference temperature $T_0$,
- $\langle t-t_0\rangle_+ = \max\{t-t_0,0\}$ the time elapsed, at the current time instant $t$, since the time instant of casting of the concrete $t_0$ (the time instant $t_0$ is a **material parameter** which it is necessary to supply, out of concern for compliance with the principle of material objectivity).

### 3.4 Desiccation creep

The desiccation creep of the model BETON_AGEING keeps the same expression as that presented  for the model BETON_BURGER, substituting for the total stress $\stress$ the effective stress $\stress'$.
