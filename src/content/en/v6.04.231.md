# v6.04.231 SSNV231 – Internal pressure hollow sphere in large deformations

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the different incompressible formulations in large deformations. The advantage of this test case is that one has an analytical solution. Five modellings are used to validate formulations regardless of the type of finite element:

Modelling A: 3D mesh Quadratic HEXA20 (INCO_UPG, INCO_UP)

Modelling B: 3D mesh Quadratic TETRA10 (INCO_UPG, INCO_UP)

Modelling C: 3D mesh Quadratic PENTA15 (INCO_UPG, INCO_UP)

Modelling D: AXIS mesh Quadratic QUAD8 (INCO_UPG, INCO_UP)

Modelling E: AXIS mesh Quadratic TRIA6 (INCO_UPG, INCO_UP)

Modelling F: 3D mesh Quadratic HEXA20 (HHO)

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A hollow sphere of $1m$ external radius and $0.2m$ internal radius is considered.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.231/10000000000002540000023498B8D9326FB01D3F.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Cut of the sphere studied**

### 1.2 Material properties

The material has a perfectly plastic elastoplastic behavior. Its loading surface is defined by the criterion of von Mises. The material properties are:

- $E=200000\mathit{MPa}$
- $\nu =0.3$
- ${\sigma}_{y}=150\mathit{MPa}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Internal pressure is applied until the entire sphere is plastified. The outer surface is free of effort.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

None

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

In the following, all quantities defined in the initial configuration will be written in capital letters. quantities defined in the distorted configuration will be written in lowercase.

To establish the analytical solution, one considers an inner $\text{A}$ and outer $\text{B}$ ray sphere. The material is elastoplastic with perfect plasticity. The plastic flow is normal and associated with the criterion of von Mises, yield limit ${\sigma}_{y}$. The elasticity is isotropic defined by the compressibility and shearing modules, $K$ and $\mu$. The sphere is subjected to internal pressure $P$.

Given the spherical symmetry of geometry and loading, a solution is sought that has the same invariance properties, which excludes the search for bifurcated solutions. The solution depends only on the distance at the center of the sphere noted $R$ in the initial configuration and $r$ in the distorted configuration. The displacement is purely radial: $\vec{u}(r)=u(r){\vec{e}}_{r}$ , where $({\vec{e}}_{r},{\vec{e}}_{\theta},{\vec{e}}_{\phi })$ is the orthonormized base associated with the spherical coordinate system. Therefore, the definition of the operators $\text{grad}$ , $div$ , ... adequate. The stress tensor is expressed as follows:

$$
\sigma ={\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}(r){\vec{e}}_{r}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{r}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta }(r)({\vec{e}}_{\theta}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\theta}{\vec{e}}_{\phi }\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\phi })
$$

As for displacement, its expression in the reference configuration is: $\vec{u}(\vec{X})=U(r){\vec{e}}_{r}$ . It can be noted that, by definition, we have the relationships:

$r=R+U(R)$ et $u(r)=U(R)$

The following formula for derivatives is derived from this formula:

$$
\frac{\partial U}{\partial R}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial R}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\left(1+\frac{\partial U}{\partial R}\right)\Rightarrow \frac{\partial U}{\partial R}=\frac{\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}}{\left(1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right)}
$$

The transformation gradient shall be calculated:

$$
F=\left(1+\frac{\partial U}{\partial R}\right){\vec{e}}_{r}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{r}+\left(1+\frac{U}{R}\right)({\vec{e}}_{\theta}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\theta}{\vec{e}}_{\phi }\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\phi })
$$

*Note:*

$F$ * admits constant clean directions during transformation.*

Logarithmic deformation then simply admits as expression:

$$
E=\ln\left(1+\frac{\partial U}{\partial R}\right){\vec{e}}_{r}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{r}+\ln\left(1+\frac{U}{R}\right)({\vec{e}}_{\theta}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\theta}{\vec{e}}_{\phi }\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\phi })
$$

*Note:*

*In this particular case, the deformation rate* $\dot{E}$ *coincids with the Eulerian* $D$ * deformation rate, so that the stress tensor* $T$ * associated with the logarithmic deformations is equal to the Kirchhoff* $\tau$ * content. It is a favorable situation to display an analytical solution.*

In the distorted configuration, the $e(\vec{x})=E(\vec{X})$ deformation is expressed:

$$
e=-\ln\left(1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right){\vec{e}}_{r}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{r}+\left(\lnr-\ln\left(r-u\right)\right)({\vec{e}}_{\theta}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\theta}{\vec{e}}_{\phi }\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\phi })
$$

It is also useful to express the Jacobian of the transformation and its logarithm in the distorted configuration:

$$
j=\detF=\frac{1}{\left(1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right)}{\left(\frac{r}{r-u}\right)}^{2}
$$

The equilibrium equations can also be expressed in the deformed configuration as a function of the Kirchhoff $\tau =j\sigma$ tensor:

$$
{div}_{x}\left(\frac{\tau}{j}\right)=0\iff \frac{\partial {\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}}{\partial r}-{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}\frac{\partial \lnj}{\partial r}+\frac{2}{r}({\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}-{\tau}_{\theta \theta })=0
$$

Besides, we have the boundary conditions:

$$
{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}(b)=0
$$

Where $a$ and $b$ are the internal and external rays of the distorted sphere. Due to the spherical symmetry, it is equivalent to imposing internal pressure or radial displacement on the inner skin. It is preferable to pilot the calculation while moving, so that the second boundary condition is replaced by:

$$
u(a)={U}^{\mathit{imp}}
$$

The hydrostatic part of the behaviour is purely elastic:

$$
\text{tr}\tau =3K\text{tr}e\iff 3K\lnj={\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}+2{\tau}_{\theta \theta }
$$

For the deviatoric part, we have the classical relations of the plasticity of von Mises:

$$
{\tau}_{\mathit{eq}}\le {\sigma}_{y};\dot{p}\ge 0;\dot{p}({\tau}_{\mathit{eq}}-{\sigma}_{y})=0
$$

$$
{\tau}^{D}=2\mu ({e}^{D}+{e}^{p})
$$

When it comes to the response of the structure, it is expected that the plastic zone will gradually develop from the inside to the outside of the sphere. When it reaches the outer wall, all points of the sphere are in a plastic regime either:

$$
{\tau}_{\mathit{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}\iff {\tau}_{\theta \theta }-{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}={\sigma}_{y}
$$

In addition, at the moment when the outer wall is reached, the plastic deformation is still zero.

$$
{e}^{D}(b)=\frac{1}{2\mu }{\tau}^{D}(b)\iff \frac{({\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}(b)-{\tau}_{\theta \theta }(b))}{2\mu }={\ln\left(\frac{1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}}{r-u}\right)\mid }_{b}+\lnb
$$

When the plastic zone leads to the outer wall, one of the two components of the stress tensor is known in all point via (13). The equilibrium relationship (7) and the spherical part of the behavior (10) allow the entire determination of the stress field, as well as the Jacobian of the transformation. In fact, unlike the case of small deformations, these two equations are coupled. They are written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}}{\partial r}-{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}\frac{\partial \lnj}{\partial r}+\frac{2}{r}({\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}-{\tau}_{\theta \theta })=0\\ \mathrm{3K}\lnj={\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}+2{\tau}_{\theta \theta }\end{array}
$$

Substitute the definition of $\lnj$ in the first equation of (15) and obtain:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left({\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}-\frac{{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}}{\mathrm{2K}}\right)=\frac{2}{r}{\sigma}_{y}
$$

This equation is easily integrated by taking into account the boundary condition (8)

$$
{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}-\frac{{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}}{\mathrm{2K}}=2{\sigma}_{y}\ln\left(\frac{r}{b}\right)
$$

The stress field can be deduced by solving this second-order equation. The root choice is fixed by the fact that it is a compression stress, therefore negative. The $\lnj$ volume change field is deduced from it through (15):

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\tau}_{\mathit{rr}}(r)=K-\sqrt{{K}^{2}-\mathrm{4K}{\sigma}_{y}\ln\left(\frac{r}{b}\right)}\\ \lnj(r)=1-\sqrt{1-4\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{K}\ln\left(\frac{r}{b}\right)}+\frac{2{\sigma}_{y}}{\mathrm{3K}}\end{array}
$$

The kinematic relationship (6) may also be rewritten:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left({(r-u)}^{3}\right)=\frac{3{r}^{2}}{\exp\lnj}
$$

Taking into account the $b-u(b)=B$ definition, this is derived by integration:

$$
{(r-u)}^{3}={B}^{2}-{\int}_{r}^{b}\frac{3{\rho}^{2}}{\exp\lnj(\rho )}d\rho
$$

The primitive in (20) will have to be calculated numerically.

One can also rely on the relationship (19) to simply express the distorted radius in $b$. Indeed, in $r=b$, the volume change is:

$$
\lnj(b)=\frac{2{\sigma}_{y}}{\mathrm{3K}}
$$

The equation (19) is written in $r=b$:

$$
\left(1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right)=\frac{{b}^{2}}{{B}^{2}}\exp\left(-\frac{2{\sigma}_{y}}{\mathrm{3K}}\right)
$$

The condition of continuity (14) allows the radius of the distorted sphere to be fixed, as it is expressed again:

$$
\left(1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}\right)={\left(\frac{b}{B}\right)}^{-1}\exp\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{2\mu }\right)
$$

From (22) and (23), the deformed outer radius is deduced:

$$
\frac{b}{B}=\exp\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{3}\left(\frac{1}{2\mu }+\frac{2}{\mathrm{3K}}\right)\right)
$$

The knowledge of deformation and stress allows the determination of the cumulative plastic deformation field $p$ , Indeed, its evolution is governed by the tensor equation (12). Specifically, it turns out that the ${\tau}^{D}/{\tau}_{\mathit{eq}}$ direction is constant which allows the plastic deformation to be integrated simply:

$$
{e}^{p}=p\left({\vec{e}}_{r}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{r}-\frac{1}{2}{\vec{e}}_{\theta}\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\theta}-\frac{1}{2}{\vec{e}}_{\phi }\otimes {\vec{e}}_{\phi }\right)
$$

Then by substituting this expression in the first equation of (12), we deduce:

$$
p(r)=\frac{2}{3}({e}_{\theta \theta }-{e}_{\mathit{rr}})-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{3\mu }=\frac{2}{3}\left(\lnr-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{2\mu }+\ln\frac{1-\frac{\partial u}{\partial r}}{r-u}\right)
$$

Where the expression of the field of displacement is now known as appres (20).

Finally, the inner boundary condition allows to determine the critical loading level for which the plastic zone reaches the outer wall. Indeed, the inner radius decompressed is given implicitly by the relationship (20) expressed precisely in $r=a$:

$$
{\int}_{r}^{b}\frac{3{\rho}^{2}}{\exp\lnj(\rho )}d\rho ={B}^{3}-{A}^{3}
$$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The reference quantitiess are the stress trace and the cumulative plastic deformation in distorted configuration for the points of Gauss with the smallest and largest r.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The solution being analytical, we have no uncertainty.
