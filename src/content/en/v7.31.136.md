# v7.31.136 WTNV136 – 3D Modelling of clay swelling with GonfElas

**Summary:**

This test allows the so-called "GonfElas" model to be validated. This nonlinear suction-dependent elastic model describes the swelling behaviour of certain types of clay. Typically it is used to model the behavior of compacted clay caps - or bentonite - used to close radioactive waste storage cells. It is a very simplified version (elastic part) of the Barcelona model.

This model is written according to the following couple of variables: net stress and suction (suction is capillary pressure).

This test represents the swelling pressure of a clay cell that is filled with water. This test case is the test case WTNA110 declination to a $\mathrm{3D}$ geometry.

### 1.3 Initial conditions

A $t=0$ :

- $\mathit{Pgaz}=1\mathit{atm}$
- $S=0,5$ (i.e. $\mathit{Pc}=44,7\mathit{Mpa}$ and ${p}_{w}=-44.6\mathit{Mpa}$ )
- Total compression stress equal to -1 atm.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

All movements are blocked at the edge ($\mathit{DX}=\mathit{DY}=\mathit{DZ}=0$ ).

The flow is zero.

The initial saturation is $\text{50 \%}$: saturation is increased and the evolution of total stress is monitored. By definition, the swelling pressure is the stress obtained with complete resaturation.

For this purpose, a linear downward hair pressure load is imposed on the entire domain in $\mathrm{1s}$ between $44,7\mathrm{Mpa}$ and $-10\mathrm{Mpa}$.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

3D _HH2MS Modelling on 1 mesh cell HEXA20.

1 second is simulated by 500 time step.

### 2.2 Modelling result A

Figure 2.2-a shows the evolution of total stress as a function of homogeneous hair pressure in all point. The post-treatment is done here at node $N8$ coordinates $(0,5;0,5;0,5)$. In the saturated part ($\mathit{Pc}\le 0$), the decrease of the capillary pressure corresponds to an increase in water pressure and the total stress increases linearly. The slope of the curve is observed to be continuous.

The parameters $A$ and ${\beta}_{m}$ were calculated to recover a swelling pressure of $7\mathit{MPa}$. When saturation reaches 1 (or capillary pressure 0), the swelling pressure is given by the following formula:

$\frac{{P}_{\mathit{gf}}}{A}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2\sqrt{{\beta}_{m}}}+\frac{1}{2{\beta}_{m}}$

So we find the classic look of the swelling stress and we check that the curve cuts well the axis of the ordered ($\mathit{PC}=0$ ) with a value of $7\mathit{Mpa}$.
