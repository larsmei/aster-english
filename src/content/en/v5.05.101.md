# v5.05.101 SDNX101 - MISS3D‐Code_Aster Chain Verification for ISS Dynamic Foundation Detachment

**Summary:**

This non-regression test uses a *Code_Aster* mode of use to treat the nonlinear foundation detachment problem of a building with consideration of a soil-structure interaction (ISS) in dynamic analysis. L的ISS is then modelled by a recalated floor spring mat from the soil impedance determined by the *Code_Aster* chaining with the MISS3D software.

The standard case of a seismically excitation-treated building with a flexible foundation representation is presented as an application case, resulting in the total consideration of the radier deformation modes associated with RIGI_MISS_3D-type characteristics.

The maximum displacements obtained at the foundation edge by dynamic calculation with DYNA_NON_LINE are a first reference result.

In a second model, the same problem is addressed by using a dynamic building condensation method, which is then represented by a dynamic substructure macro‐element. The soil is represented directly by a soil impedance determined by the *Code_Aster* chaining with MISS3D software and also considered as a dynamic macro‐element. The dynamic problem resolution with DYNA_NON_LINE ss-oper on a mixed model consisting of macro‐elements and a physical model reduced to nonlinear contact springs. However, this method is more accurate and cost-effective than the previous one provides fairly close values for the maximum displacements obtained at the foundation edge.

In a third model, the same condensation method is used as in the second model, but the dynamic problem is solved in a linear calculation loop with transient dynamic operators where the nodal forces complement is recalculated over the entire time range, due to the nonlinearity of the delamination, which is even more economical than the previous one and provides satisfactory values for the maximum displacements obtained at the foundation edge.

In a fourth model, the same condensation method is always used, but only for the soil macro-element, keeping the physical model of the structure. The modes for the soil-structure interface are reduced modes to represent the flexibility of the foundation by a LIAISON_INTERF relationship. Only the proper modes of the structure-ground system are tested.

A fifth model uses a physical model of building and point ground springs of RIGI_PARASOL characteristics where the dynamic problem is solved in a linear calculation loop with transient dynamic operators where the nodal forces complement is recalculated over the entire time range, due to the nonlinearity of the delamination.

## 2 The flexible foundation method

Representing the flexible foundation hypothesis is tantamount to considering the limit condition for calculating the foundation modes. The following method is used for modelling test case A.

### 2.1 Description of the method

The flexible foundation condition is obtained by representing the static foundation modes by forced modes associated with all degrees of freedom foundation translation.

Therefore, for the calculation of the own blocked mode, all degrees of freedom translation ($\mathrm{DX}=\mathrm{DY}=\mathrm{DZ}=0$ ) of the node group $\mathrm{BRADIER}$ [Figure 3.1-a] of the foundation in the AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) command will be blocked. For the calculation of the static foundation modes, the forced modes associated with each of the degrees of freedom blocked in these nodes will be obtained. The number of static modes to be calculated then amounts to the number of nodes on the foundation multiplied by 3, the number of degrees of freedom translation associated.

Then, once the impedances dependent on the frequency by FICHIER option of the CALC_MISS command are calculated, we can then affect the aggregate reclagged characteristics of the foundation using AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01) option RIGI_MISS_3D.

### 2.2 Summary of impedance calculation

The calculation of soil impedances requires the following steps to sequence the commands of *Code_Aster*:

- Calculation of eigenmodes on blocked basis with block limit conditions compatible with foundation hypothesis by CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02) command,
- Definition of the dynamic interface, producing the type (CRAIGB (pre-set) or MCNEAL) and the mesh of the ground-structure interface, by the DEFI_INTERF_DYNA [U4.64.01](doc:u4.64.01) command,
- Definition of a complete modal base by the DEFI_BASE_MODALE [U4.64.02](doc:u4.64.02) command which calculates the static modes of the previously defined dynamic interface and completes the eigenmode base with the complete modal base of the CLASSIQUE type,
- Assembling the dynamic macro‐element by the MACR_ELEM_DYNA [U4.65.01](doc:u4.65.01) command, from the previously defined modal base.

The data to be transferred from *Code_Aster* to MISS3D is obtained by using a specific command:

- IMPR_MACR_ELEM [U7.04.33](doc:u7.04.33) allows the mesh of the soil-structure interface to be produced and the static and dynamic modes reduced to this interface. The terms of the dynamic macro-element are used to determine the structure's contribution to impedance.
- Then we get the ground impedance calculation by launching MISS3D by CALC_MISS [U7.03.12](doc:u7.03.12) option FICHIER. In this command data, in addition to the file produced by IMPR_MACR_ELEM, it is necessary to provide a calculation parameter file as well as a file of soil data as described in the [bib3] document.

## 3 Modelling of the delamination with ground springs

Simultaneous modelling of the delamination and the ISS by discrete elements requires combining the use of linear springs representing the ground with nonlinear springs working only in compression to represent contact by penalization. An example of Modelling adapted to the Modelling of a seismically-sustained building is described below.

### 3.1 Matching between ground springs and contact springs

In the case of a building subjected to seismic excitation whose soil-structure interaction (ISS) is to be studied, the foundation base must be geometrically represented by superfacial mesh cells, grouped here in $\mathrm{SRADIER}$ , which may not be modelled in the soft foundation hypothesis when they rely on density elements.

In the case of linear behavior, nodes $\mathrm{SRADIER}$ can be directly affected by stiffness characteristics calculated by AFFE_CARA_ELEM, RIGI_MISS_3D, for the soft foundation hypothesis. However, to represent the detachment, these soil characteristics must only be active when the ground is compressed and they must be cancelled in the event of traction. A classic means is to intercalate, between each point of the $\mathrm{SRADIER}$ foundation and a point spring affected by soil characteristics in this point, a nonlinear spring in series meshed by a segment supporting a rigid foundation DIS_TR Modelling or DIS_T in the case of a soft foundation. This group of discrete segments is called here $\mathrm{LIGRAD}$. The characteristics of these contact springs are very high in compression (${10}^{15}$ in this test) and zero in tensile. They are indicated with a material DIS_CONTACT behavior and then affected with the constitutive law DIS_CHOC in the last ground compression process (which is formed by the tensile X-type and therefore the last-typed by the ground-type X-typed by the ground-typed by the ground-typed by the ground-

To calculate the characteristics of the punctual ground springs grouped here in $\mathrm{BRADIER}$, the nodes of this group must belong to the superfacial mesh cells of the same geometry as $\mathrm{SRADIER}$. Or, $\mathrm{SRADIER}$ no longer contains nodes of $\mathrm{BRADIER}$ because the segments of $\mathrm{LIGRAD}$ are intercalated. Therefore, in the mesh a superfacial mesh-cell group called here $\mathrm{SRADIE2}$ is created by translating $\mathrm{SRADIER}$ with a very small distance value equal to the size of the segments of $\mathrm{LIGRAD}$ ($2.E-2$ in this test). This distance value is then used as a game in the material characteristics of DIS_CONTACT so that the ground and foundation are joined at the initial time. mesh-cell group superfacial $\mathrm{SRADIE2}$ does not need to be modelled by finished elements.

### 3.2 Calculation of the modes on floor spring mats

The calculation of the modes on floor springs mats is desirable. It is even necessary if modal damping is to be taken into account later in the dynamic calculation.

In advance, it is necessary to calculate the soil characteristics with the option of AFFE_CARA_ELEM, RIGI_MISS_3D, for the soft foundation hypothesis. These new characteristics will be assigned instead of the previous ones and associated with the limit condition of blocking both for the calculation of modes on floor mats but also in the calculation operators d-evolution.

For RIGI_MISS_3D, the mesh-cell group $\mathrm{BRADIER}$ point is entered on GROUP_MA_POI1. Simply enter the logical unit number of the soil impedance matrix calculated by MISS3D that has already been given in CALC_MISS option MISS_IMPE.

### 3.3 Dynamic calculation of the de-lamination

Before calculating the dynamic evolution of the de-lamination, it is necessary to calculate the static de-lamination due to the gravity. The following modelling steps are therefore taken:

- Definition of the vertical gravity load and horizontal gravity unit loads for seismic loading,
- Definition of the dynamic calculation moment list,
- Calculation of the static position under gravity by STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) command: the discrete segments $\mathrm{LIGRAD}$ are affected by the DIS_CHOC behavioral relationship. The rest of the structure has linear behavior in our case but the interest of this Modelling is also to be able to affect other behavioral relationships,
- Calculation of dynamic evolution, with recovery as the initial state of the previous static result, under the effect of the gravity always and also seismic loads by the DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) control. Discrete segments $\mathrm{LIGRAD}$ are also affected by the DIS_CHOC behavioral relationship. It is at this level that modal damping can be introduced associated with each of the previously calculated ground spring mat modes.

### 6.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 1057 nodes (5784 degrees of freedom), 1797 elements (998 elements plates and 799 elements discreet).

### 6.3 Calculation parameters

The dynamic calculation is conducted over a 0.4 seconds interval by time step of 0.01 seconds with a modal damping calculated by CALC_AMOR_MODAL, different for each mode with a frequency of less than $20\mathrm{Hz}$ and including a 5% material damping contribution for the building structure and a representative contribution of the radiative damping of the soil extracted from the imaginary part of the soil impedance.

### 6.4 Modelling results A

Non-regression tests are performed on the displacement values (vertical and horizontal) of the nodes top and centre of the radier, at the beginning of the transient and at the end.

## 7 Modelling B

### 7.1 Modelling characteristics

This Modelling implements the dynamic condensation method of structure and soil. The mixed model is used consisting of their macro‐elements and a physical model reduced to nonlinear contact springs. The Modelling ss is based on the same elements and the same mesh as the previous model unlike the $\mathit{BRADIER}$ soil springs replaced by the macro‐element directly consisting of the soil impedance calculated by MISS3D. This Modelling also allows testing the two modes of transient loading condensation in a macro-element from the VECT_ASSE_GENE and RESU_GENE operands in the CAS_CHARGE factor keyword CAS_CHARGE of MACR_ELEM_DYNA.

### 7.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 1057 nodes (5784 degrees of freedom), 1184 elements (998 elements plates and 186 elements discreet).

### 7.3 Calculation parameters

The dynamic calculation is conducted over a 3 seconds interval by time step of 0.01 seconds with a 5 % damping for each of the structural modes with a frequency of less than $20\mathrm{Hz}$ combined with a damping matrix extracted from the ground macro-element and representative of radiative damping, which is equivalent to the modal damping of the previous model.

### 7.4 Modelling results B

A test of non-regression is performed on the vertical displacement value at the top node at the beginning of the transient.

## 8 Modelling C

### 8.1 Modelling characteristics

This Modelling uses exactly the same mixed model consisting of macro-elements and a physical model reduced to nonlinear contact springs as in the second model. The difference lies only in the resolution method in a linear calculation loop with the transient calculation operators where the nodal force complement due to the nonlinearity of the delamination is recalculated each time using the CALC_FORC_NONL operator. 6 iterations of linear calculations are performed here.

### 8.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 1057 nodes (5784 degrees of freedom), 1184 elements (998 elements plates and 186 elements discreet).

### 8.3 Calculation parameters

The dynamic calculation is conducted over a 5 seconds interval by time step of $0.01$ second with a 5 % damping for each of the structural modes with a frequency of less than $20\mathit{Hz}$ combined with a damping matrix extracted from the ground macro-element and representative of radiative damping, which is equivalent to the modal damping of the first model.

### 8.4 Modelling results C

Non-regression tests are performed on the displacement values (vertical and horizontal) of the nodes top and centre of the radier, at the beginning of the transient and at the end.

## 9 Modelling D

### 9.1 Modelling characteristics

This Modelling always uses a mixed model, but only consisting of the soil macro-element and a physical model comprising both the restraint linear springs of the modellings B and C and the building model of Modelling A. The difference also lies in the calculation of modes to represent a flexible restraint at the ground-structure interface. The restraint between soil impedance and the structure at this interface is represented by an restraint type LIAISON_INTERF.

### 9.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 1057 nodes (5784 degrees of freedom), 1184 elements (998 elements plates and 186 elements discreet).

### 9.3 Modelling results D

The first eigenfrequencies of the soil-structure system are tested very close to those obtained with the other modellings.

## 10 Modelling E

### 10.1 Modelling characteristics

This Modelling uses the physical building model of model A and a model of point ground springs distributed by RIGI_PARASOL under the foundation of $\mathit{RADIER}$. As with model C, the resolution method is used in a linear calculation loop with the transient calculation operators where the nodal forces complement due to the nonlinearity of the delamination is recalculated each time using the CALC_FORC_NONL operator. 3 linear calculations iterations are performed here.

### 10.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 1057 nodes (5784 degrees of freedom), 1184 elements (998 elements plates and 186 elements discreet).

### 10.3 Calculation parameters

The dynamic calculation is conducted over a 5-second interval by time step of $0.01$ second with a material damping of Rayleigh equivalent to 5% of modal damping for each of the structural modes with a frequency of less than $20\mathit{Hz}$ combined with amortization characteristics of ground springs distributed by RIGI_PARASOL and representative of radiative damping, which is equivalent to the modal damping of the first model.

### 10.4 Modelling results E

Non-regression tests are performed on the displacement values (vertical and horizontal) of the nodes top and centre of the radier, at the beginning of the transient and at the end.

## 11 Summary of the results

The ISS representation method by a carpet of soil springs used in model A provides non-regression results in terms of maximum displacements obtained at the foundation edge at the initial moment and during a de-lamination.

An alternative method of representation of the ISS and dynamic condensation structure is used in model B. This method is more accurate (as it uses all the terms of soil impedance) and more economical (because of dynamic condensation) than the previous method, however, provides fairly close values for the maximum displacements obtained at the foundation edge.

It is therefore the first priority to use in dynamic de-lamination studies.

It is also used in model C where the dynamic problem resolution sseries in a linear calculation loop with the transient calculation operators where the nodal forces complement is recalculated over the entire time range, each time due to the nonlinearity of the delamination. This resolution method is even more cost-effective than the previous one and provides satisfactory values for the maximum displacements obtained on the foundation edge.
