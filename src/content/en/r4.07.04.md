# r4.07.04 Fluid–structure coupling for tubular structures and coaxial shells

**Summary:**

This document describes the various fluid–structure coupling models available from the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU. These models make it possible to simulate the fluid-elastic coupling forces in the following configurations:

- tube bundles under transverse flow (essentially for SG tubes),
- control-rod / housing-plate passage (exclusively for control-rod clusters),
- coaxial cylindrical shells under annular flow (vessel / core-barrel gap, ...),
- tube bundles under axial flow (fuel assemblies, ...).

For each configuration, the fluid-elastic force model is presented first. The solution of the modal problem is then described. The solution methods used incorporate the specific features of the various fluid-elastic force models.

## 1 General presentation

### 1.1 Reminders

The dynamic fluid forces acting on a structure in motion may be classified into two categories:

- forces **independent** of the motion of the structure, at least within the range of small displacements; these are mainly random forces generated by turbulence or by the two-phase nature of the flow,
- fluid forces **dependent** on the motion of the structure, known as “**fluid-elastic forces**”, which are responsible for the fluid–structure coupling.

In this document, the four models of **fluid-elastic** forces implemented in the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU are considered. The computational aspects related to the implementation of these models are the subject of specification notes [bib. 1], [bib. 2].

### 1.2 Modelling

The dependence of the fluid-elastic forces on the motion of the structure is expressed, for small amplitudes, by **a transfer matrix between the fluid-elastic force and the** **displacement vector**. The projection of the equation of motion of the coupled fluid–structure system onto the modal basis of the structure alone is written, in the **Laplace domain**, as:

$\lbrace [{M}_{ii}]{s}^{2}+[{C}_{ii}]s+[{K}_{ii}]-[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]\rbrace (q)=({Q}_{t})$ **eq. 1.2-** 1

| where | $[{M}_{ii}],[{C}_{ii}]\mathit{et}[{K}_{ii}]$ denote respectively the diagonal structural mass, damping and stiffness matrices in air; |
| --- | --- |
|  | $(q)$ denotes the vector of generalised displacements in air; |
|  | $({Q}_{t})$ is the vector of generalised random excitations (independent forces); |
| and | $[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]$ represents the transfer matrix of the fluid-elastic forces, projected onto the modal basis of the structure alone. This matrix depends in particular on![Figure](images/Object_6.svg), a characteristic flow speed, and on the frequency of the motion through the Laplace variable![Figure](images/Object_7.svg). |

A priori, $[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]$ is an arbitrary matrix whose extra-diagonal terms, if they are not zero, introduce a coupling between modes. Furthermore, the terms of $[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]$ evolve in a non-linear manner with the complex frequency $s$ .

Each fluid-elastic force model is associated with a specific transfer matrix. In every case, the formulation of the modal problem under flow may be characterised by the relation [eq. 1.2-1].

For the various types of configuration that can be simulated with the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU, the representations of the transfer matrices of the fluid-elastic forces are set out in the remainder of this document.

## 2 Fluid-elastic excitation acting on tube bundles under transverse flow (essentially for SG tubes)

Two methods for simulating the fluid-elastic excitation are available in *Code_Aster* .

The first dates back to the late 1970s. It is very widespread in the scientific community, in which it is known as the “Connors method”. The results provided by this method depend to a large extent on the value assigned to one of its main input parameters: the “Connors constant”. Conservative values have therefore had to be determined for this constant on the basis of numerous tests carried out worldwide. The Connors method is well suited to the design of tubular bundles against the vibration risk at the design stage. It is described below in paragraph § 2.5.

The second incorporates more physics than the Connors method. However, since a complete modelling of the phenomena is too complex relative to current knowledge, this second method remains based on a set of experimental correlations, known as fluid-elastic correlations. The implementation of this second fluid-elastic excitation model in *Code_Aster* is discussed in the specification note [bib.1]. The principle note of the FLUSTRU software [bib. 3] constitutes the theoretical reference documentation. It is recalled in outline in paragraphs § 2.1 to 2.4 below.

### 2.1 Description of the configuration studied

A tube bundle excited by an external transverse flow is considered. External transverse flows tend to destabilise the mechanical system as the flow speed increases. An industrial case is that of SG tube vibrations. On this component, transverse flows are observed at the inlet of the tube bundle (single-phase liquid flow) and in the bent part of the tubes (two-phase flow) [Figure 2.1-a].

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.04/10004B64000069D500004F60F7D0CC0ACF1E7E0C.svg)

**Figure 2.1-a: Diagram of a steam generator**

From the standpoint of fluid-elastic coupling, the study of the dynamic behaviour of the various tubes of a bundle subjected to a transverse flow is reduced to the study of an equivalent tube; the definition of the equivalent tube depends on the environment of the tube to be treated.

When the tube considered has vibration characteristics appreciably different from those of its neighbours, this tube may be treated as a single tube, vibrating in the midst of a bundle of rigid tubes.

Otherwise, the problem is more complex because a mechanical system with coupling between the tubes of the bundle must be considered, and therefore a large number of degrees of freedom.

To treat this kind of configuration, a model was developed in the TTA Department, “the global model” [bib. 7]; this model allows the definition of a system equivalent to one degree of freedom, which represents the complete coupled system.

The approach adopted for carrying out the analyses may be summarised as follows [Figure 2.1-b]:

- Given the slender nature of the structures to be studied, the analysis of the fluid-elastic coupling in the tube bundle is performed by describing the tube through its curvilinear abscissa.
- In the analysis, the fluid environment of the tube is characterised both by the physical properties of the fluid flowing inside the tube (primary fluid) and by those of the fluid flowing outside the tube (exciting secondary fluid). These physical properties, such as the density, may vary along the tube, as a function of the curvilinear abscissa.
- The flow speed taken into account for the fluid-elastic coupling analysis is the component, normal to the tube in the plane of the tube, of the secondary-fluid velocity. This speed may vary along the tube.
- In order to be able to take the various possible types of excitation into account, several excitation zones may be defined along the structure. In the case of the steam generator, for example, it is useful to distinguish, on the one hand, the zones in which the excitation is exerted by a fluid in the single-phase state, located at the tube foot, and, on the other hand, the zone in which the excitation is two-phase with a high void fraction, located in the bent part of the tube.
- The coupling analysis is performed from the mechanical characteristics of the structure in “fluid at rest”. The fluid-elastic coupling forces are estimated from dimensionless correlations obtained on analytical similarity experiments. On each excitation zone, the appropriate correlations can thus be applied; the excitation zones must be disjoint.

### 2.2 Stages of the analysis

- The first stage of the analysis consists in computing the characteristics of the structure in “fluid at rest”. This is done by considering an equivalent mass of the tube; this equivalent mass groups together, on the one hand, the mass of the tube alone and, on the other hand, the masses added by the internal and external fluids.

An equivalent density is thus defined along the tube as a function of the curvilinear abscissa

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.04/Object_36.svg)

by the expression:

${\rho}_{\mathit{eq}}(x)=\frac{1}{({d}_{e}^{2}-{d}_{i}^{2})}[{\rho}_{i}(x).{d}_{i}^{2}+{\rho}_{t}.({d}_{e}^{2}-{d}_{i}^{2})+{\rho}_{e}(x).{d}_{\mathit{eq}}^{2}]$ **eq. 2.2-** 1

with

${d}_{\mathit{eq}}^{2}=\frac{2.{C}_{m}.{d}_{e}^{2}}{\pi}$ **eq. 2.2-** 2

In the equation [:ref:` **éq. 2.2-1**  < **éq. 2.2-1** >`], the term ${\rho}_{e}(x).{d}_{\mathit{eq}}^{2}$ represents the mass added by the external fluid. This term depends, through the parameter ${C}_{m}$ , on the arrangement of the tube bundle (square or triangular pitch) and on the confinement of the bundle (reduced pitch). For fluid-elastic coupling analyses of tube bundles subjected to a transverse flow, analytical expressions determined from experimental results are commonly used to estimate the coefficient ${C}_{m}$ . All of the data required to estimate the coefficient ${C}_{m}$ are collected by the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU.

- Knowing the equivalent density of the tube, the elementary mass and stiffness matrices in water at rest are then computed by means of the equivalent-density profile, by the operator CALC_MATR_ELEM; the options MASS_FLUI_STRU and RIGI_FLUI_STRU are used. The operator CALC_MODES makes it possible, after assembly of the elementary matrices, to compute directly the modes in water at rest of the structure studied.
- The fluid-elastic coupling forces are computed by the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU from the dimensionless correlations established on analytical similarity mock-ups. These coupling forces, $[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]$ , which depend on the motion of the structure, are then taken into account in the general equation of motion [:ref:` **éq. 1.2-1**  < **éq. 1.2-1** >`] in order to compute the characteristics of the coupled flow–structure system for a given flow speed.

### 2.3 Expression of the transfer matrix of the fluid-elastic forces

In the case of tube bundles excited by a transverse flow, the fluid-elastic coupling forces are forces distributed along the structure. They are characterised by dimensionless linear coefficients of added damping and stiffness, denoted respectively ${C}_{d}$ and ${C}_{k}$ . The expression of the coefficients of the transfer matrix of the fluid-elastic forces projected onto the modal basis of the structure in “fluid at rest” is then as follows:

${B}_{ij}(U,s)=[(\underset{L}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x)V(x){d}_{e}{C}_{d}(x,{s}_{r}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx})s+\underset{L}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x){V}^{2}(x){C}_{k}(x,{s}_{r}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx}].{\delta}_{ij}$ **eq. 2.3-** 1

The dependence of the coefficients ${C}_{d}$ and ${C}_{k}$ on the motion of the structure and on the flow speed of the fluid is expressed by their evolution as a function of the complex reduced frequency , ${s}_{r}$ defined by:

${s}_{r}=\frac{s.D}{U}$ **eq. 2.3-** 2

The expression [:ref:` **éq. 2.3-1**  < **éq. 2.3-1** >`] shows that a diagonal transfer matrix is retained. This implies:

- the various eigenmodes of the structure are sufficiently far apart from one another that it may be assumed that there is no coupling between modes.
- the modal shapes of the structure in “fluid at rest” are not perturbed by setting the fluid in motion *.*

These two assumptions have been verified experimentally on tube bundles subjected to a transverse flow.

In practice, taking into account the various excitation zones considered along the structure, the diagonal coefficients of the matrix of fluid-elastic forces projected onto the modal basis are written:

${B}_{ii}(U,s)=\sum_{k}[(\underset{{L}_{k}}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x){V}_{k}(x){d}_{e}{C}_{\mathit{dk}}(\frac{{\mathit{sd}}_{e}\overline{U}}{{\mathit{UU}}_{k}}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx})s+\underset{{L}_{k}}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x){V}_{k}^{2}(x){C}_{kk}(\frac{{\mathit{sd}}_{e}\overline{U}}{{\mathit{UU}}_{k}}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx}]$ **eq. 2.3-** 3

where ${C}_{\mathit{dk}}$ and ${C}_{kk}$ denote respectively the dimensionless coupling coefficients, of damping and of stiffness, retained for excitation zone $k$ . The fluid speed $\frac{{\mathit{UU}}_{k}}{\overline{U}}$ appearing in the complex reduced frequency as the argument of the coupling coefficients corresponds to the mean speed over excitation zone $k$ , after renormalisation of the profile ${V}_{k}(x)$ , so that its mean over all the excitation zones equals $U$ .

**It is moreover very important to note that each modal shape taken into account in equations 2.3-1, 2.3-3, etc. is in reality taken into account only through its translational** **component in the lift direction. This is due to the fact that the added damping and stiffness coefficients that appear in these equations were determined (experimentally) only for the lift direction. This remark applies to all the methods for computing fluid-elastic instabilities of SG tubes presented in this document, including the Connors method presented in paragraphs § 2.5.1 and § 2.5.2. It follows in particular that the generalised mass, damping and stiffness matrices that appear in the equations associated with the fluid-elastic instability analyses of SG tubes (such as, for example, equation 2.4-1) are not generalised matrices in the usual sense of the term, that is to say based on the three translational components and on the three rotational components, but** **generalised matrices that may be described as “oriented along a preferred direction” in so far as they are all computed on the basis of the translational component alone of the modal shapes in the lift direction. This remark applies only to the application “SG tube vibrations” and, within this application, to the computation of fluid-elastic instabilities.**

### 2.4 Solution of the modal problem under flow

In the configuration “Tube bundle subjected to a transverse flow”, the problem is solved on the modal basis characterising the structure in “fluid at rest”.

In general, the characteristics of the coupled flow–structure system are obtained by seeking the solutions of the equation:

$\lbrace [{M}_{ii}]{s}^{2}+[{C}_{ii}]s+[{K}_{ii}]-[{B}_{ij}(U,s)]\rbrace (q)=0$ **eq. 2.4-** 1

| where | $[{M}_{ii}],[{C}_{ii}]\mathit{et}[{K}_{ii}]$ denote respectively the diagonal mass, damping and stiffness matrices characteristic of the structure in 'fluid at rest'; |
| --- | --- |
|  | $(q)$ denotes the vector of generalised displacements in 'fluid at rest'. |

Since the matrix of fluid-elastic forces that is retained is diagonal, and since the modal shapes are assumed not to be modified under flow, the fluid-elastic coupling problem reduces to the solution of $N$ scalar problems, $N$ denoting the number of modes taken into account in the modal basis.

For each mode $i$ and each flow speed $U$ , the problem to be solved is written:

${M}_{ii}{s}^{2}+[{C}_{ii}-\sum_{k}(\underset{{L}_{k}}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x){V}_{k}(x){d}_{e}{C}_{\mathit{dk}}(\frac{{\mathit{sd}}_{e}\overline{U}}{{\mathit{UU}}_{k}}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx})]s+{K}_{ii}-\sum_{k}(\underset{{L}_{k}}{\int}\text{}\frac{1}{2}{\rho}_{e}(x){V}_{k}^{2}(x){C}_{kk}(\frac{{\mathit{sd}}_{e}\overline{U}}{{\mathit{UU}}_{k}}){\varphi}_{i}^{2}(x)\mathit{dx})=0$

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.04/Object_66.svg)

> **eq. 2.4-** 2

It will be noted that the equation [:ref:` **éq. 2.4-2**  < **éq. 2.4-2** >`] is non-linear in $s$ ; its solutions are obtained by means of an iterative method of Broyden type.

For each mode $i$ , a solution ${s}_{i}$ of the equation [:ref:` **éq. 2.4-2**  < **éq. 2.4-2** >`] is obtained. From ${s}_{i}$ one then deduces, for this mode, the circular frequency ${\omega}_{i}$ and the damping ${\xi}_{i}$ of the coupled flow–structure system, using the relation:

${s}_{i}=-{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}+J{\omega}_{i}\sqrt{1-{\xi}_{i}^{2}}$ with ${J}^{2}=-1$ **eq.** **2.4-** 3

The coupled system becomes dynamically unstable when one of the damping coefficients ${\xi}_{i}$ becomes negative or vanishes.

#### 2.5.3 Variant of the method

In the Connors method presented in the paragraphs above (paragraphs § 2.5.1 and § 2.5.2), the computation of the instability ratio takes into account only one component of the modes: the direction defined in DEFI_FLUI_STRU. A variant of this method consists in taking into account the three translational components of the modes. The effective speed and the critical speed are then written:

${V}_{\mathit{en}}=\sqrt{\frac{\underset{\mathit{tube}}{\int}(\frac{{\rho}_{s}(s)}{\overline{{\rho}_{s}}}{V}^{2}(s){\mathrm{\phi }}_{n}^{2}(s)\mathit{ds})}{\frac{{M}_{n}}{\overline{m}}}}$

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.04/Object_487.svg)

Where ${M}_{n}$ denotes the generalised mass (not oriented along a preferred direction and therefore taking into account both the three translational components and the three rotational components) of mode $n$ , and ${\varphi}_{n}$ denotes the modal shape of mode $n$ . By ${\mathrm{\phi }}_{n}^{2}$ is meant here the sum of the squares of the three translational components of ${\varphi}_{n}$ . The three rotational components are not taken into account in the computation of ${\varphi}_{n}^{2}$ .

Setting:

$r(s)=\frac{{\rho}_{s}(s)}{\overline{{\rho}_{s}}}$ and $u(s)=\frac{V(s)}{{V}_{\mathit{moy}}}$

where ${V}_{\mathit{moy}}$ is the mean speed., the instability ratio is written:

${R}_{n}=\frac{{V}_{\mathit{en}}}{{V}_{\mathit{cn}}}=\frac{{V}_{\mathit{moy}}}{{{f}_{n}{D}_{e}K[\frac{2\pi {\xi}_{n}{M}_{n}}{\overline{{\rho}_{s}}{D}_{e}^{2}\underset{\mathit{tube}}{\int}(r(s){u}^{2}(s){\mathrm{\phi }}_{n}^{2}(s)\mathit{ds})}]}^{1/2}}$

The computation of the ratio according to this variant is systematically performed by Code_Aster when the implementation of the Connors method is requested.

The instability ratio computed according to this variant is provided alongside the instability ratio computed according to the method specified in paragraphs § 2.5.1 and § 2.5.2. Most of the time, the two results are identical. If a discrepancy exists, the reason for this discrepancy must be sought in the contribution of the rotational components of the mode considered, for example in the contribution of the rotations of the straight parts of the tubes about their axis. The more penalising of the two results will then be adopted.

Strictly speaking, one should consider the value of under flow. However, the Connors method does not provide for the computation of the frequency variation attributable to the flow. As a first approximation, one therefore takes for the value of the frequency in fluid at rest.

## 3 Fluid-elastic excitation acting on the control rod at the housing plate (exclusively for control-rod clusters)

The fluid-elastic forces acting on this type of configuration were identified on the GRAPPE2 mock-up of the TTA department. The theoretical aspects of the identification of these sources are developed in reference [bib. 4]. The implementation of the GRAPPE2 model in *Code_Aster* is discussed in the specification note [bib. 2].

### 3.1 Description of the configuration studied

The GRAPPE2 mock-up represents the control rod, the upper part of the cluster guide, and the thermal sleeve of a reactor of the 900 or 1300 MWe type [Figure 3.1-a].

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.04/10000E0A000069D500004F605B471BAEEFD81429.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Schematic diagram of the GRAPPE 2 mock-up**

This mock-up consists essentially of a hollow cylindrical tube of small thickness, fixed on a solid cylindrical central core. The hollow tube is entirely immersed in water at ambient temperature. A plate, representing the housing plate, makes it possible to reproduce the annular confinement. The flow through the plate may be upward or downward. The control rod may be centred or offset (50% of the mean clearance) at the housing plate.

Four experimental configurations are therefore possible, depending on the direction of the flow and on whether or not the control rod is centred. The fluid-elastic force coefficients have been identified for each of these configurations and are available in *Code_Aster* .

The GRAPPE2 mock-up was sized in geometric, hydraulic and reduced-frequency similarity with respect to the reactor configuration. The sole datum of the control-rod diameter therefore makes it possible, in particular, to deduce all of the other geometric quantities.

#### 4.3.1 Simplifying assumptions

A few assumptions on the nature of the flow make it possible to simplify the unsteady Navier–Stokes equations, which underlie the fluid–structure problem.

| **H1** | It is assumed that the flow is the superposition of a steady mean flow, obtained when the structures are fixed, and of an unsteady flow induced by the motion of the walls. |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| **H2** | It is assumed that the structural vibrations are of small amplitude with respect to the thickness of the mean annular flow. |
|  |  |
| **H3** | It is assumed that the velocity perturbations induced by the vibratory motions are, on average over a radius, essentially directed in the directions![Figure](images/Object_246.svg)and![Figure](images/Object_247.svg): it is thus assumed that the vibratory motion induces a helical motion of fluid around the structures rather than a radial motion with respect to the latter. These velocity perturbations define order 1. |
|  |  |
| **H4** | It is finally assumed that the velocity and pressure field is uniform, at order 1, in the radial direction. |

### 5.5 Taking into account the presence of the grids of the tube bundle

The modelling described previously, of the forces induced by an axial flow on a bundle of cylinders, does not take into account the presence of the grids of the bundle (for example, the mixing and support grids of the fuel assemblies). ### 5.6 Taking into account the damping in fluid at rest

Up to now, the damping imparted to a tube bundle by the presence of a fluid at rest has not been taken into account in the modelling. A model of damping in fluid at rest is therefore proposed here, of which annex 1 of the synthesis note of the CHAISE tests [bib8] constitutes the reference documentation.

#### 5.6.1 Modelling of the fluid-at-rest force exerted on a tube bundle

The method of computing the damping in fluid at rest that is implemented here is a generalisation of the method of CHEN [bib. 9].

It consists in computing the resultant force on each tube of the stresses due to shear in the boundary layer. It is a non-linear problem because the fluid damping coefficient depends on the frequency. One therefore introduces the following simplifications:

- the problem is written by means of the frequencies in water at rest computed without taking the fluid damping into account,
- the coupling between modes is neglected.
