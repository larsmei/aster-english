# v2.02.149 SDLL149 – Seismic calculation of the BM3 piping (test NRC)

**Summary:**

This test mainly validates the implementation of the GUPTA method (correlation between periodic and rigid parts of a modal response) on a seismic calculation by spectral method applied to a part of the piping (COMB_SISM_MODAL).

Other methods are also evaluated on the same configuration:

- SRSS,
- CQC,
- CQC with pseudo-mode.

This test was proposed by NRC to validate seismic design of nuclear facilities.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Issue

This test consists of performing a spectral analysis of the BM3 pipeline network used in a nuclear power plant. It allows validation of the different combination methods used, in other words the Gupta method. To perform this validation, the results of equivalent modelling conducted with ANSYS are extracted from the following two documents:

[1] Reassessment of Regulatory Guidance on Modal Response Combination Methods for Seismic Response Spectrum Analysis , NUREG/CR-6645 , BNL-NUREG-52576 , 1999.

[2] ANSYS Mechanical APDL Technology Demonstration Guide, 2010, Chapter 12: Dynamic Simulation of a Nuclear Piping System Using RSA Methods .

The support of this structure is modelled by stiffeners. To recover nodal reactions to the support, an additional node "double" is created for each support. It is shifted from $\mathrm{1,E-3}\mathit{inch}$ (Code_Aster tolerance) to $X$ axis compared to the point support. All its degrees of freedom are blocked and an element SEG2 is created between the support node and the "double". It is on this element that the stiffness support values will be applied. Thus, the nodal reactions will be raised to nodes "double".

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

This model compares the results of the multi-support and multi-direciton Code-Aster spectral modal calculations, with or without pseudo-mode consideration, with d-ANSYS results. The different modal cumulation methods tested are CQC and SRSS. In total, 4 calculations are compared:

- calculation 1: SRSS without taking into account the pseudo-mode
- calculation 2: CQC without taking into account the pseudo-mode
- calculation 3: CQC with pseudo-mode taken into account
- calculation 4: SRSS with pseudomode consideration

To be consistent with the model used in ANSYS, the elements model used in Modelling A, B and C is slightly modified at the elbows where the discretization used is finer (around 20 d的elements per elbow). The other characteristics (thickness, radius, material) are the same as those used in modellings A, B and C. The hose flexibility is taken into account via the coefficient of flexibility by the following formula:

${c}_{\mathit{flex}}=\frac{1,65}{\lambda}=\frac{1,65\ast E\ast {R}_{c}}{{(R-0,5\ast E)}^{2}}$

where: $E$ and $R$ are the thickness and radius of the cross section of the piping and ${R}_{c}$ is the radius of curvature of the elbow.

## 6 Summary of the results

The results obtained are generally satisfactory, whether with a complete mass matrix or with a diagonal mass matrix, the frequencies of the publications are found.

The results on nodal reactions from the spectral seismic method calculation are mostly close to the reference results of less than 5%. However, on a few points of support the differences on nodal reactions between *Code_Aster* and ANSYS ss is more than 30% (modelization A). They are explained by differences in the modal deformities calculated by the two softwares (up to 2% on B Modelling), as well as on reactions to mode support (up to 5% dVK5X in B Modelling). These differences can be traced back to separate implementations on the elements finished beam between the two codes. In particular for A Modelling, ANSYS uses "elements finite" interpolation for the elements beam while *Code_Aster* uses "exact" integration [R3.08.01 – elements "exact" beam.

However, they are far below the differences resulting from the choice of the method or the "missing mass" effect (high frequency mode effect).

As regards the Gupta method, it appears to be well validated by this external reference.

Model C is in the interest of validating the use of a modal base resulting from dynamic substructure calculation and the ACCE_ABSOLU single support option.

Modelling D is in the interest of validating multi-support and multi-direction calculations with or without taking into account the pseudo-mode.
