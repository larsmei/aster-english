# r5.03.23 Elastoplastic constitutive behaviour under irradiation of metals: application to reactor-vessel internals

**Summary:**

This document presents the writing of a constitutive law under irradiation for the stainless steels 304 and 316, the materials of which the internal structures of the vessels of nuclear reactors are made. The formalism of the model is identical for the two materials; only the parameters differ from one material to the other. The model takes into account, besides thermoelasticity, plasticity, irradiation creep, as well as a possible swelling under neutron flux.

The implementation of the model, as well as its limits, is detailed here.

This constitutive law, although developed for needs specific to EDF, can be used to account for the behaviour of any material presenting characteristics of plasticity, irradiation creep and swelling.

## 1 Introduction

The purpose of this document is to describe the development and the numerical implementation of a constitutive law under irradiation for the materials constituting the internal structure of the vessels of nuclear reactors: the cold-worked steels 316 and 316L for the bolting, as well as the solution-annealed steels 304 and 304L for the baffles, the reinforcements and the core shroud. This constitutive law is formally the same for the two types of steel; only the parameters vary from one material to the other.

The model developed must be capable of accounting for (besides thermoelasticity) the plasticity induced by the tightening of the bolts, the irradiation creep well known for metallic materials, and a possible swelling induced by irradiation. The effects of irradiation creep and of swelling are distinguished by the fact that the strain occurs at constant volume for creep, which is not the case for swelling. The various phenomena cited above are listed exhaustively in the document [ref] and will therefore not be the subject of an additional description in what follows.

The present document is organised as follows: the constitutive law chosen to describe the response of the solution-annealed steels 304/304L and of the cold-worked steels 316/316L is first set out, as well as the equations allowing its implementation in the form of an implicit integration. Each mechanism is detailed in a general way. The dependence of the material parameters on the command variables, which are the irradiation and the temperature, is made explicit. The numerical values of these parameters are set out in the note EDF R&D HT-26/05/045/A. One then presents the limits of the model and its application in Code_Aster.

It should be noted that the formalism presented here, although implemented in the framework of a very specific application, can be used to describe the behaviour of any type of material presenting characteristics of plasticity, irradiation creep and swelling. In that case, it will be incumbent on the user of Code_Aster to obtain the material coefficients necessary for the use of the model.

## 2 Formulation of the constitutive law

One proposes to employ an additive decomposition of the strains. The mechanical strain is then expressed as:

> ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{m}={\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}+{\underline{\varepsilon}}_{p}+{\underline{\varepsilon}}_{i}+{\underline{\varepsilon}}_{g}$ **eq** 2-1

The mechanical strain, ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{m}$ , is defined as the total strain minus the thermal strain: ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{m}=\underline{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{th}}$ . The other components are: the elastic strain ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}$ , the plastic strain ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{p}$ , the irradiation-creep strain ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{i}$ and the swelling strain ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{g}$ .

- The elastic strain is related to the stress by Hooke's law: $\underline{\sigma}=\underline{\underline{E}}:{\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}$ . The elasticity tensor $\underline{\underline{E}}$ depends on the temperature $T$ .
- The plastic strain is given by a law of von Mises type. The yield surface is expressed as:

> $f={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-{\sigma}^{f}$ **eq** 2-2

where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ is the von Mises stress and ${\sigma}^{f}$ the yield limit. The latter depends on the cumulative plastic strain, $p$ , on the temperature $T$ , and on the fluence $\Phi$ . The plastic flow is given by the normality rule, so that:

> ${\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{p}=\dot{p}\underline{n}$  with $\underline{n}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\underline{s}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}$ **eq** 2-3

where $\underline{s}$ is the stress deviator. $\dot{p}$  is given by the consistency condition $f=0$ and $\dot{f}=0$ . The irradiation-creep laws are expressed (for a test at constant stress and flux) [ref]:

> $\varepsilon =\max({A}_{i}\cdot \sigma \cdot \Phi -{A}_{0})$ **eq** 2-4

The coefficient ${A}_{0}$ expresses a threshold effect. In differential form, the previous law may be rewritten as:

> $\dot{\varepsilon}={A}_{i}\sigma \cdot \varphi$  if ${\eta}_{i}>{A}_{0}/{A}_{i}$ with ${\dot{\eta}}_{i}=\sigma \cdot \varphi$ **eq** 2-5

where $\varphi$ is the flux ($\varphi =\dot{\Phi}$ ). One therefore introduces an additional state variable, ${\eta}_{i}$ , in order to describe the threshold effect. This uniaxial law must be extended to the multiaxial case. Since irradiation creep takes place without change of volume, one will describe this mechanism by a viscoplastic model based on equipotentials given by the von Mises stress. One will also assume that the evolution of the variable ${\eta}_{i}$ is governed by this same stress. One then obtains the following laws:

> ${\dot{\eta}}_{i}={\zeta}_{f}\cdot \sigma \cdot \varphi$ **eq** 2-6
> ${\dot{p}}_{i}={A}_{i}\cdot \sigma \cdot \varphi$  if ${\eta}_{i}>{\eta}_{i}^{s}$ otherwise ${\dot{p}}_{i}=0$ **eq** 2-7
> ${\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{i}={\dot{p}}_{i}\underline{n}$ **eq** 2-8

where ${p}_{i}$ is the equivalent irradiation-creep strain. ${A}_{i}$  and ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ are coefficients of the model which may depend on the temperature (one may envisage a dependence with respect to the fluence, but the model will be more complex to identify). The function ${\zeta}_{f}$ makes it possible to introduce a dependence with respect to the temperature in the evolution law of the variable ${\eta}_{i}$ (see equation ). One will note that the model thus formulated will be able to operate for variable temperatures and fluxes.

**Note no. 1:**

*One will note that irradiation “creep” is entirely driven by the fluence and not by time. In the case where a thermal-creep mechanism were added, time would of course play a role explicitly* .

**Note no. 2:**

*In order to ensure that the threshold* ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ *is crossed correctly (from a numerical point of view), it is necessary to define an error criterion. This criterion "TOLER_ET" corresponds to the % of overshoot of the threshold that one allows during the numerical integration. The irradiation-creep strain begins as soon as the threshold* ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ *is crossed, but one forces Code_Aster to respect a reasonable evolution of the variable* ${\eta}_{i}$ *in the vicinity of the threshold* ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ *. If during the analysis the criterion is not respected, Code_Aster subdivides the time steps, provided that the subdivision of the time steps is authorised.*

Swelling may be described by laws of the type:

> $\frac{\Delta V}{{V}_{0}}={F}_{g}(\Phi )$ **eq** 2-9

where $\Delta V$ is the variation of volume and ${V}_{0}$ the initial volume. By differentiating this equation and assuming that the variation of volume remains small ($\Delta V\ll {V}_{0}$ ) one obtains:

> $\frac{\dot{V}}{V}=\frac{{\text{dF}}_{g}}{d\Phi }\varphi$ **eq** 2-10

This rate of variation is identified with the trace of the swelling rate tensor: $\dot{V}/V=\mathrm{trace}({\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{g})$ . One assumes here that the swelling takes place in an isotropic manner and therefore that ${\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{g}$ may be expressed as:

> ${\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{g}=\dot{g}\underline{1}$ **eq** 2-11

where $\underline{1}$ is the unit tensor. One therefore has $\dot{V}/V=3\dot{g}$

> That is: $\dot{g}={A}_{g}\varphi$ **eq** 2-12

with ${A}_{g}=\frac{1}{3}{\text{dF}}_{g}/d\Phi$ . ${A}_{g}$ is a new material parameter. It depends on the temperature and on the fluence. In a problem with variable temperature, it is therefore important to use the equation and not the equation (the latter being implicitly written for a constant temperature). A possible coupling between the stress state and swelling is neglected.

The definition of the model rests on five internal variables: $p$ , ${\eta}_{i}$ , ${p}_{i}$ , $g$ and ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}$ . The temperature and the fluence are regarded as parameters imposed for a given analysis. These values come from the neutronics and thermal analyses. The equations of the model are recalled in the table .

| Internal variables | Evolution equations |
| --- | --- |
| p | consistency $\dot{p}$ |
| ${\eta}_{i}$ | ${\dot{\eta}}_{i}={\zeta}_{f}\cdot {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\cdot \varphi$ |
| ${p}_{i}$ | ${\dot{p}}_{i}={A}_{i}\cdot \sigma \cdot \varphi$ if ${\eta}_{i}>{\eta}_{i}^{s}$ otherwise ${\dot{p}}_{i}={A}_{i}\cdot \sigma \cdot \varphi {\dot{p}}_{i}=0$ |
| g | $\dot{g}={A}_{g}\varphi$ |

## 3 Implementation: implicit integration

Implicit integration consists in finding the increments of the internal variables $\Delta {V}_{i}=(\Delta P,\Delta {\eta}_{i},\Delta {P}_{i},\Delta g,\Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e})$ over a discrete time increment $\Delta t$ [ref]. The discretisation of the equations of the table leads to the following system of non-linear equations:

| ${\underline{R}}_{e}=\Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}+\Delta p\underline{n}+\Delta {p}_{i}\underline{n}+\Delta g\underline{1}-\Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{m}=0$ |
| --- |
| ${R}_{p}={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-{\sigma}^{f}=0$ |
| ${R}_{\eta}=\Delta {\eta}_{i}-{\zeta}_{f}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\Delta \varphi =0$ |
| ${R}_{i}=\Delta {P}_{i}-H({\eta}_{i}-{\eta}_{i}^{s}){A}_{i}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\Delta \varphi =0$ |
| ${R}_{g}=\Delta g-{A}_{g}\Delta \Phi =0$ |

**Table** 3.1: **system of discretised equations**

Where $H$ is the Heaviside function and $\Delta \Phi =\phi \Delta t$ is the fluence increment. Setting $R=({\underline{R}}_{e},{R}_{p},{R}_{\eta},{R}_{i},{R}_{g})$ , one therefore seeks to solve the system: $R(\Delta {V}_{i})=0$ . The internal variables appearing directly or indirectly (for example ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ may be expressed as a function of ${\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}$ ) are expressed as: ${\text{v}}_{i}={\text{v}}_{i}^{0}+\theta \Delta {\text{v}}_{i}$ , where ${\text{v}}_{i}^{0}$ represents the values of the variables at the beginning of the increment. $\theta$ is a parameter varying between 0 and 1. For $\theta =0$ , one obtains an explicit Euler scheme (to be avoided); for $\theta =1$ one obtains a fully implicit scheme.

The system of equations of the table is solved by employing a Newton-Raphson method, which requires the computation of the Jacobian: $J=\partial R/\partial \Delta {\text{v}}_{i}$ . The latter may be computed block by block (the zero terms are omitted). One denotes $\underline{\underline{N}}=\partial \underline{n}/\partial \underline{\sigma}$ and ${\sigma}_{,p}^{f}=\partial {\sigma}^{f}/\partial p$ .

Derivatives of ${\underline{R}}_{e}$ :

> $\frac{\partial {\underline{R}}_{e}}{\partial \Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}}=\underline{\underline{1}}+\theta \cdot \Delta p\cdot \underline{\underline{N}}:\underline{\underline{E}}+\theta \cdot \Delta {p}_{i}\cdot \underline{\underline{N}}:\underline{\underline{E}}$     $\frac{\partial {\underline{R}}_{e}}{\partial \Delta p}=\underline{n}$
> $\frac{\partial {\underline{R}}_{e}}{\partial \Delta {p}_{i}}=\underline{n}$     $\frac{\partial {\underline{R}}_{e}}{\partial \Delta g}=\underline{1}$

Derivatives of ${R}_{p}$ :

> $\frac{\partial {R}_{p}}{\partial \Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}}=\theta \underline{n}:\underline{\underline{E}}$     $\frac{\partial {R}_{p}}{\partial \Delta p}=-\theta {\sigma}_{,p}^{f}$

Derivatives of ${R}_{\eta}$ :

> $\frac{\partial {R}_{\eta}}{\partial \Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}}=-{\zeta}_{f}\theta \cdot \Delta \varphi \cdot \underline{n}:\underline{\underline{E}}$     $\frac{\partial {R}_{\eta}}{\partial \Delta {\eta}_{i}}=1$

Derivatives of ${R}_{i}$ :

> $\frac{\partial {R}_{i}}{\partial \Delta {\underline{\varepsilon}}_{e}}=-{A}_{i}\cdot \theta \cdot \Delta \varphi \cdot \underline{n}:\underline{\underline{E}}$     $\frac{\partial {R}_{i}}{\partial \Delta {p}_{i}}=1$

Derivatives of ${R}_{g}$ :

> $\frac{\partial {R}_{g}}{\partial \Delta g}=1$

## 4 Material coefficients

In what follows, one presents the forms of evolution of the parameters of the equations as a function of the command variables, which are the temperature and the fluence. The values of these parameters for the materials 304 and 316 are recorded in the report HT-26/05/045/A.

### 4.1 Thermoelasticity

Young's modulus [ref] is given by:

> $E={C}_{0}^{E}+{C}_{1}^{E}\cdot T$ **eq** 4.1-1

Poisson's ratio is given by:

> $\nu ={C}_{0}^{\nu}+{C}_{1}^{\nu}\cdot T$ **eq** 4.1-2

The tangent thermal expansion coefficient is given by:

> $\alpha ={C}_{0}^{\alpha}+{C}_{1}\alpha \cdot T+{C}_{2}^{\alpha}\cdot {T}^{2}$ **eq** 4.1-3

The secant expansion coefficient is then given, taking a zero reference temperature, by:

> ${\alpha}^{\text{sec}}={C}_{0}^{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}{C}_{1}^{\alpha}\cdot T+\frac{1}{3}{C}_{2}^{\alpha}\cdot {T}^{2}$ **eq** 4.1-4

### 4.2 Plasticity

This part uses the report [ref] in order to compute the hardening curves of the steels constituting the reactor-vessel internals after irradiation, for various temperatures. One uses the expressions for the yield stress at 0.2% plastic strain ${R}_{0,2}$ , the ultimate stress ${R}_{m}$ and the uniform elongation ${e}_{u}$ as a function of the temperature $T$ , the irradiation $\Phi$ (the report [ref] uses the term $d$ to describe the irradiation) and the cold-work ratio $c$ . The report [ref] also supplies the elongation at rupture, but this datum is unusable in practice because it depends on the type of specimen studied.

The uniform elongation is expressed as:

> ${e}_{u}={e}_{u}^{0}(T){\eta}_{3}(c){\xi}_{3}(d)$ **eq** 4.2-1

The yield stress at 0.2% is expressed as:

> ${R}_{0,2}={R}_{0,2}^{0}(T){\eta}_{1}(c){\xi}_{1}(d)$ **eq** 4.2-2

The ultimate stress is not expressed directly. The difference $\Delta R={R}_{m}-{R}_{0,2}$ is first represented by a function:

> $\Delta R=({R}_{m}^{0}(T)-{R}_{0,2}^{0}(T)){\eta}_{2}(c){\xi}_{2}(d)$ **eq** 4.2-3

Fitting $\Delta R$ instead of ${R}_{m}$ makes it possible to ensure that: ${R}_{m}>{R}_{0,2}$ . ${R}_{m}$  is therefore obtained as: ${R}_{m}(T,c,d)={R}_{0,2}(T,c,d)+\Delta R(T,c,d)$

Note that the functions ${e}_{u}^{0}$ , ${R}_{0,2}^{0}$ and ${R}_{m}^{0}$ are valid for both materials (304 and 316) constituting the reactor-vessel internals. The functions ${\eta}_{1,2,3}$ and ${\xi}_{1,2,3}$ depend on the material. One proposes to represent the hardening curve of the materials, for given values of $T$ , $d$ and $c$ , by a power law of the type:

> ${\sigma}_{f}(p)=K{(p+{p}_{0})}^{n}$ **eq** 4.2-4

where $p$ is the equivalent von Mises plastic strain. $K$ , ${p}_{0}$ and *n*  are parameters to be computed in order to obtain the values of ${R}_{0,2}^{0}$ , ${R}_{m}$ and ${e}_{u}$ .

The value of the strain corresponding to the uniform elongation (denoted ${\varepsilon}_{u}=\log(1+{e}_{u})$ ) is obtained by the Considère condition (neglecting the elastic strain):

> $\frac{d{\sigma}^{f}}{\text{dp}}={\sigma}^{f}$ **eq** 4.2-5

that is:

> $nK{(p+{p}_{0})}^{n-1}=K{(p+{p}_{0})}^{n}\Rightarrow {p}^{0}=n-{\varepsilon}_{u}$ **eq** 4.2-6

The ultimate stress is equal to:

> ${R}_{m}={\sigma}^{f}({\varepsilon}_{u})\exp(-{\varepsilon}_{u})=K{n}^{n}\exp(-{\varepsilon}_{u})$ **eq** 4.2-7

That is:

> $K=\frac{{R}_{m}}{{n}^{n}}\exp({\varepsilon}_{u})$ **eq** 4.2-8

The elastic limit is given by (one neglects here the change of cross-section):

> ${R}_{0,2}=K{({p}_{e}+{p}_{0})}^{n}$  with ${p}_{e}=0,002$ **eq** 4.2-9

It therefore remains to solve a single non-linear equation with respect to *n* :

> $S={R}_{0,2}-\frac{{R}_{m}}{{n}^{n}}\exp({\varepsilon}_{u}){({p}_{e}+n-{\varepsilon}_{u})}^{n}=0$ **eq** 4.2-10

The search for the solution is carried out by bisection, taking ${\varepsilon}_{u}$ as the initial value of $n$ . One then computes $n$ and $K$ using the equations and .

In the case where the material presents little hardening (i.e. strong irradiation or very substantial cold working), it is not possible to find a solution of the equation . In that case one will use the power law of the form ${\sigma}^{f}(p)=K{p}^{n}$ with $n={\varepsilon}_{u}$ and ${\sigma}^{f}(p)=K{p}^{\mathrm{nK}}={R}_{m}\exp({\varepsilon}_{u})/{n}^{n}$ , that is ${p}_{0}=0$ .

The use of the previous laws for small values of *p*  may lead to unrealistic results. One may consider that the flow stress cannot be lower than $\kappa {R}_{0,2}$ (with $\kappa$ close to 1). The effect of the choice of $\kappa$ on the creep response of a structure is negligible for values between 0.8 and 1. One recommends, for an analysis in Code_Aster, retaining the value $\kappa =0,8$ . One will furthermore use a linear extrapolation between $p=0$ and $p={p}_{e}$ obtained from the values at ${p}_{e}$ of the stress and of the hardening. ### 4.3 Irradiation creep

The irradiation-creep data collected in [ref] make it possible to determine the values of ${A}_{i}$ and ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ . These values are a priori constant with temperature. It is however probable that at low temperature there is no irradiation creep. In order to model this evolution, it is possible to make the coefficient ${A}_{i}$ depend on the temperature:

> ${A}_{i}={A}_{i}^{0}{\zeta}_{f}(T)$ **eq** 4.3-1

where ${A}_{0}^{i}$ is the value of the parameter for high temperatures. In this equation ${\zeta}_{f}$ is a function which can make it possible to stop the creep phenomenon below a temperature threshold. It may be written in the form ${\zeta}_{f}(T)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\tanh({\mu}_{T}(T-{T}_{c})))$ , where ${T}_{c}$ makes it possible to set the temperature at which irradiation creep begins and where ${\mu}_{T}$ makes it possible to set the width of the transition between the temperature domains with and without irradiation creep.

### 4.4 Swelling

One will use a bilinear Foster law, which makes it possible to represent an incubation time and then a linear swelling [ref,ref]. One then has:

> $\frac{\Delta V}{{V}_{0}}={F}_{g}(\Phi )=R\cdot (\Phi +\frac{1}{\alpha}\text{Log}(\frac{1+\exp(\alpha ({\Phi}_{0}-\Phi ))}{1+\exp(\alpha {\Phi}_{0})}))$ **eq** 4.4-1

For $\Phi \to \infty$ one obtains:

> $\frac{\Delta V}{{V}_{0}}=R\cdot \Phi -\frac{R}{\alpha}\text{Log}(1+\exp(\alpha {\Phi}_{0}))$ **eq** 4.4-2

The incubation fluence is therefore: $\text{Log}(1+\exp(\alpha {\Phi}_{0}))/\alpha$ (n.b.: if $\alpha {\Phi}_{0}\gg 1$ it is equal to ${\Phi}_{0}$ ). By differentiating the equation one obtains:

> ${A}_{g}=\frac{1}{3}R(1-\frac{\exp(\alpha ({\Phi}_{0}-\Phi ))}{1+\exp(\alpha ({\Phi}_{0}-\Phi ))})$ **eq** 4.4-3

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.23/100000000000018D0000014CCEA47ED1FD381A13.png)

**Figure** 4.4-1: **Bilinear Foster law** [ref].

The material parameters of the swelling law are therefore R, $\alpha$ , and ${\Phi}_{0}$ .

As in the case of irradiation creep, it may be necessary to introduce a dependence with respect to the temperature in order not to impose a swelling at low temperature. One may make $R$ depend on the temperature by using a function ${\zeta}_{g}$ analogous to that used for irradiation creep:

> $R={R}^{0}{\zeta}_{g}(T)$  with ${\zeta}_{g}(T)=\frac{1}{2}(1+\tanh({\mu}_{g}(T-{T}_{c}^{g})))$ **eq** 4.4-4

## 5 The limits of the model

The model proposed was established from an experimental basis in which the tests are carried out for monotonic loadings: (i) tensile tests on pre-irradiated materials under isothermal conditions; irradiation-creep (and swelling) tests under isothermal conditions for a constant flux. This law must however be used to simulate the behaviour of the reactor-vessel internals under real conditions; that is, with variable fluxes and temperatures.

Thermal cycling induces a cyclic mechanical loading. The equations of the model, written in differential form, make it possible to handle variable temperatures and fluxes, but certain physical effects are not taken into account. This is for example the case of kinematic hardening, which could come into play during thermal cycling. To our knowledge, there are no cyclic tension/compression tests on irradiated materials. Other questions may also arise: (i) what is the irradiation-creep behaviour of a pre-irradiated material (at a different temperature and/or under stress)? (ii) is there a coupling between irradiation creep and plasticity?

Swelling is treated as an irreversible volumetric strain. It is however the appearance of cavities which may grow under tensile stress (or even close up under compressive stress, but that remains highly hypothetical). This harmful effect, since the void fraction increases more rapidly, is however not taken into account.

The domain of use of the model in temperature, fluence and creep stress is specified in the note HT-26/05/045/A for the materials 304 and 316. However, it is possible to use such a model in the framework of an analysis with Code_Aster for any material other than these. In that case, it is the responsibility of the user of the code to ensure that the material coefficients used are consistent with the domain of validity of the analysis.
