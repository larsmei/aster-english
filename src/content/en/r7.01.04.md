# r7.01.04 Constitutive law ENDO_ISOT_BETON

**Summary:**

This documentation presents the theoretical writing and the numerical integration of the constitutive law ENDO_ISOT_BETON, which describes a mechanism of asymmetric local damage of concretes, with a stiffness-restoration effect.

## 1 Introduction – Field of application

The constitutive law ENDO_ISOT_BETON aims to model as simply as possible an elastic brittle behaviour of concrete. It can be seen as an extension of the law ENDO_FRAGILE (removed from the code) (with which it retains a definite closeness of formulation) for Civil Engineering applications.

As for the law ENDO_FRAGILE, the material is isotropic. The stiffness can decrease, the loss of stiffness being measured by a scalar evolving from 0 (sound material) to 1 (fully damaged material).

On the other hand, unlike ENDO_FRAGILE, the loss of stiffness distinguishes tension from compression, in order to favour damage in tension. Moreover this loss of stiffness can disappear on return to compression: this is the phenomenon of stiffness restoration on closure. It must also be noted that this damage law aims to describe the failure of concrete in tension; it is therefore not at all suited to the description of the non-linear behaviour of concrete in compression. It therefore assumes that the concrete remains in a state of moderate compression.

The law ENDO_ISOT_BETON exhibits softening, which generally leads to a loss of ellipticity of the equations of the problem and consequently to a localisation of the strains, hence a pathological mesh dependence.

Finally, the softening character of the behaviour also leads to the appearance of instabilities, physical or spurious, which result in snap-backs on the global response and make control of the loading indispensable in statics. Piloting of type PRED_ELAS [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80) then appears as the most suitable mode of control of the loading.

## 2 Constitutive law

### 2.1 Theoretical writing

If one seeks to take the closure effect into account, great attention must be paid to the continuity of the stresses as a function of the strains (which is an indispensable condition for a constitutive law in a finite-element analysis software), see [bib1]. Indeed, if this effect is modelled in too simplistic a way, the constitutive law has a good chance of presenting a discontinuous response.

In order to take closure into account (i.e. the transition between tension and compression), one must begin by describing finely what is called tension and compression, knowing that in tension (resp. compression) the crack will be considered “open” (resp. “closed”). A natural solution is to place oneself in a principal frame of strain. In such a frame, the elastic free energy is written ($\lambda$ and $\mu$ denoting the Lamé coefficients):

$\Phi (\varepsilon )\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}$                                     eq 2.1-1

One can then define:

- a volumetric tension or compression, according to the sign of $t\varepsilon$ ,
- a tension or compression in each principal direction, according to the sign of ${\varepsilon}_{i}$ .

According to the fairly reasonable following principle — in a case of tension (“open crack”), the elastic energy is corrected by a damage factor; in a case of compression (“closed crack”), the expression of the elastic energy is kept — the damageable free energy is written:

$\Phi (\varepsilon ,d)=\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}(H(-\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H(\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon ))+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{i})+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))$ eq 2.1-2

One notes that the free energy is continuous at each change of regime. It is even continuously differentiable with respect to the strains, since it is a sum of differentiable functions (the function ${x}^{2}H(x)$ is differentiable) and the continuity of the partial derivatives at the points $t\varepsilon =0$ and ${\varepsilon}_{i}=0$ is immediate. The stresses are then made explicit (knowing that they will everywhere be continuous functions of the strains). As in elasticity, the principal frame of the stresses coincides with the principal frame of the strains, a result demonstrated in the appendix.

The stresses are written in the principal frame:

${\sigma}_{ii}=\lambda (\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )(H(-\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon ))+2\mu {\varepsilon}_{ii}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{ii})+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{ii}))$     eq 2.1-3

In this form, the continuity of the stresses with respect to the strains is clear. The figure opposite shows the stress ${\sigma}_{11}$ in the plane $({\varepsilon}_{1,}{\varepsilon}_{2})$ at constant damage (2D case, plane strain). The effect of closure as well as the continuity of the stresses are clearly visible.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.04/1000000000000142000001110C89B230FC93C5FF.png)

**Figure 2-a: illustration of the continuity.**

The thermodynamic force ${F}^{d}$ associated with the internal damage variable is written:

${F}^{d}=-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial d}=\frac{1+\gamma }{{(1+\gamma d)}^{2}}(\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))$                 eq 2.1-4

It remains to define the evolution of the damage. The scheme retained is that of the generalised standard models. A criterion must be defined, which is taken in the form:

$f({F}^{d})={F}^{d}(\varepsilon ,d)-k$                                 eq 2.1-5

where k defines the damage threshold. In order to take into account, at the level of the evolution of the damage, the effect of confinement, the threshold $k$ depends on the strain state, in the form:

$k={k}_{0}-{k}_{1}(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )H(-\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )$                                 eq 2.1-6

One constrains oneself to remain in the domain:

$f({F}^{d})\le 0$                                             eq 2.1-7

The evolution of the damage variable is then determined by the Kuhn–Tucker conditions:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\dot{d}=0\mathit{pour}f<0\\ \dot{d}\ge 0\mathit{pour}f=0\end{array}$                                     eq 2.1-8

For a uniaxial loading, the resulting curve is shown in figure 2-b, for two values of the confinement (see paragraph 2.3.2.2). In compression, the behaviour remains approximately linear, which represents the behaviour of the material only up to 3–4 times the value of the (uniaxial) tensile strength, here $\mathit{SYT}$ . It is clear that the use of the law is then indicated as long as one remains within this bound. In figure 2-b, the specimen is first loaded in tension and thus undergoes damage. Then, it is unloaded and subsequently loaded in compression in the same direction. The recovery of stiffness in compression (crack closure) will therefore be noted.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.04/10000000000003C60000028BA57886CC08130E51.jpg)

**Figure 2-b: response to a uniaxial loading.**

It is observed that in pure damaging uniaxial loading ($\dot{d}\ge 0$ ), the thermodynamic force is expressed: ${F}^{d}(d)=-E\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}.{\xi}_{,d}(d)}{2}$ , having denoted $\xi (d)=\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}$ . The consistency condition is then written:

$\dot{f}=-E\varepsilon \dot{\varepsilon}{\xi}_{,d}-E\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}\dot{d}{\xi}_{,\mathrm{dd}}}{2}=0$                     eq 2.1-9

Whence: $\dot{d}=-\frac{2\dot{\varepsilon}{\xi}_{,d}}{\varepsilon {\xi}_{,\mathrm{dd}}}$ and therefore the damaging pure uniaxial membrane loading law is:

$\dot{\sigma}=E({\xi}_{,d}\dot{d}\varepsilon +{\xi}_{\dot{\varepsilon}})=E\dot{\varepsilon}(\xi -2\frac{{\xi}_{,d}^{2}}{{\xi}_{,\mathrm{dd}}})=\frac{-E}{\gamma}\dot{\varepsilon}$                     eq 2.1-10

which makes it possible to interpret the role of the parameter $\gamma$ : the slope being constant, which is the justification of the algebraic form of the function $\xi$ .

**Note:**

*From a formal point of view, generalised standard materials are characterised by a dissipation potential which is a positively homogeneous function of degree 1, the Legendre–Fenchel transform of the indicator function of the reversibility domain, which is therefore worth here:*

$\Delta (\dot{d})=\underset{{F}^{d}/f({F}^{d})\le 0}{\sup}{F}^{d}\dot{d}=k\dot{d}+{I}_{{\mathit{IR}}^{+}}(\dot{d})$                         eq 2.1-11

The presence of an indicator function bearing on $\dot{d}$ will be noted, which ensures that the damage is increasing.

It still remains to take into account the fact that the damage is bounded above by 1. From an intuitive point of view, this seems easy. In order to keep a writing entirely compatible with the generalised standard formalism, it is sufficient to introduce an indicator function of the admissible domain into the expression of the free energy:

$\begin{array}{cc}\Phi (\varepsilon ,d)=& \frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}(H(-\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon ))+\\ & \mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{i})+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))+{I}_{\left]-\infty ;1\right]}(d)\end{array}$             eq 2.1-12

The introduction of this indicator function prevents the damage from exceeding 1; indeed, for $d=1$ , ${F}^{d}=\frac{-\partial f}{\partial d}=-\infty$ , and the damage no longer evolves.

### 2.2 Taking into account shrinkage and temperature

The constitutive law takes into account a possible drying shrinkage, a possible autogenous shrinkage and a possible thermal strain. The strain $\varepsilon$ referred to in this document is then the “elastic strain” $\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}}-{\varepsilon}^{\mathit{rd}}-{\varepsilon}^{\text{re}}$ .

On the other hand, the material parameters referred to in the next paragraph are considered as constants (in particular, they cannot depend on the temperature, in the current state of development)

### 2.3 Identification of the parameters

The parameters of the constitutive law are 4 or 5 in number (see the following paragraphs). They are classically supplied in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU.

#### 2.3.1 Elastic parameters

These are the simplest: they are the two classical parameters, Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio, supplied under the keyword ELAS or ELAS_FO of DEFI_MATERIAU.

#### 2.3.2 Damage parameters

Depending on whether the user wants to use the dependence of the threshold on the confinement or not, 2 or 3 parameters must be supplied to control the damage law.

##### 2.3.2.1 Use without dependence on confinement

In this case, the parameter ${k}_{1}$ is considered to be zero. It should be noted that the state of compression of the concrete must remain moderate for the law to remain valid (compressive stress of the order of a few times the stress at the tensile peak, in absolute value).

The user must enter, under the keyword BETON_ECRO_LINE of DEFI_MATERIAU, the values of:

- SYT: limit of the stress in simple tension,
- D_SIGM_EPSI: slope of the post-peak curve in tension.

The value of SYC is then computed automatically in order to have k1=0 and takes its minimum value, namely:

$\mathit{SYC}=\mathit{SYT}\sqrt{\frac{1+\nu -2{\nu}^{2}}{2{\nu}^{2}}}$

##### 2.3.2.2 Use with dependence on confinement

In this case, the dependence on confinement allows the concrete to keep a realistic behaviour in compression up to the order of magnitude of appearance of the non-linearity in compression, given by SYC, cf. below (classically, a compressive stress of the order of ten times the stress at the tensile peak, in absolute value).

The user must enter, under the keyword BETON_ECRO_LINE of DEFI_MATERIAU, the values of:

- SYT: limit of the stress in simple tension,
- SYC: limit of the stress in simple compression,
- D_SIGM_EPSI: slope of the post-peak curve in tension.

Figure 2-b shows the behaviour with two values of the confinement. The increase of the parameter SYC has the effect of extending the linear behaviour of the material.

##### 2.3.2.3 Passage from the “user” values to the “model” values

For information, the values of $\gamma$ , ${k}_{0}$ and possibly ${k}_{1}$ (if the user has entered SYC) are obtained by the following formulae, cf. [eq 2.1-10]:

$\gamma =\frac{-E}{\text{D\_SIGM\_EPSI}}$

${k}_{0}={(\mathrm{SYT})}^{2}(\frac{1+\gamma }{2E})(\frac{1+\nu -2{\nu}^{2}}{1+\nu })$

${k}_{1}=\mathrm{SYC}\frac{(1+\gamma ){\nu}^{2}}{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}-{k}_{0}\frac{E}{(1-2\nu )(\mathrm{SYC})}$

### 2.4 Numerical integration

Two points must be settled before implementing the model: the first concerns the evaluation of the damage; the second consists in computing the tangent matrix, a computation made a little more delicate than usual by the passage into a principal frame of strain.

One places oneself here within the framework of the implicit integration of the constitutive laws. The dependence of the criterion on the confinement [eq 2.1-6] is taken into account in explicit form, i.e. the threshold $k$ is entirely determined by the strain state of the previous step, this in order to simplify the integration of the model.

#### 2.4.1 Evaluation of the damage

As will be seen, a simple scalar equation makes it possible to obtain the damage, which makes it possible to avoid recourse to iterative methods.

${d}^{-}$ denotes the damage at the previous step and ${d}^{+}$ the evaluation of the damage at the current step at the current iteration, which will be the damage at the current step when convergence has been reached. The simplest way to evaluate the damage of the current iteration is to assume that the criterion is reached at the current instant, which is expressed by:

$f({F}^{d})=0\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\frac{1+\nu }{{(1+\gamma d)}^{2}}(\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))=k$     eq 2.4.1-1

which gives:

${d}^{\mathit{test}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}(\sqrt{\frac{1+\gamma }{k}(\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))}-1)$         eq 2.4.1-2

3 cases arise:

- ${d}^{\mathrm{test}}\le {d}^{-}$ : this means that at the current instant, the criterion is not reached; one concludes that ${d}^{+}={d}^{-}$ ,
- ${d}^{-}\le {d}^{\mathrm{test}}\le 1$ : the criterion is therefore reached, the consistency condition implies ${d}^{+}={d}^{\mathrm{test}}$ ,
- ${d}^{\mathrm{test}}\ge 1$ : the material is then ruined at this point, whence ${d}^{+}=1$ .

#### 2.4.2 Computation of the tangent matrix

The tangent matrix is the sum of two terms, the first expressing the stress/strain relation at constant damage, the second coming from the condition $f=0$ . Indeed, one can write:

$\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}={\left\lbrace \frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}\right\rbrace }_{d={c}^{\mathit{te}}}+\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial d}{\left\lbrace \frac{\partial d}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}\right\rbrace }_{f=0}$                     eq 2.4.2-1

If the user requests the analysis with the tangent matrix (cf. documentation of STAT_NON_LINE, [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)), the constitutive law supplies the expression given by [eq 2.4.2-1]. On the other hand, if the user requests the analysis with the unloading matrix, the constitutive law supplies the secant matrix, that is to say the first term of the right-hand side of [eq 2.4.2-1].

##### 2.4.2.1 Tangent matrix at constant damage

As has been emphasised previously, the computation of the tangent matrix is a little delicate because of the writing of the model in the principal frame of strain. Thus, the tangent matrix at constant damage is easily known in the principal frame of strain, whereas what is sought is this same tangent matrix in the global frame.

In the case where the damage does not evolve, in the principal frame of strain, the sought matrix expresses a simple degraded elasticity relation:

${\left\lbrace \frac{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}_{i}}}{\delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{j}}}\right\rbrace }_{d={C}^{\mathit{te}}}=\lambda (H(-\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon ))+2\mu {\delta}_{ij}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{j})+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{j}))$         eq 2.4.2.1-1

The passage from the global frame to the principal frame of strains must now be expressed, at least in the case where the strain eigenvalues are different. The tangent matrix being necessary only for the **numerical** solution algorithms (Newton scheme), one will allow oneself, during the computation of the tangent matrix (and **only** in this case) to perturb numerically any identical eigenvalues (in order to make them distinct). It will be noted in particular that this makes it possible, at zero damage, to recover the elastic stiffness matrix.

Tensors in the principal frame of strain (which, it is recalled, is also the principal frame of stresses) are denoted with a tilde. By definition, denoting by ${U}_{i}$ the eigenvector associated with the i-th eigenvalue, and the change-of-basis matrix $Q=({U}_{1}{U}_{2}{U}_{3})$ , one has:

$\sigma =Q\tilde{\sigma}{Q}^{T}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\delta {\sigma}_{ij}={Q}_{\text{im}}{Q}_{\text{jm}}\delta \tilde{{\sigma}_{m}}+\delta {Q}_{\text{im}}{Q}_{\text{jm}}\tilde{{\sigma}_{m}}+{Q}_{\text{im}}\delta {Q}_{\text{jm}}\tilde{{\sigma}_{m}}$

In the case where the strain eigenvalues are distinct, the evolution of the eigenvectors and eigenvalues is given by (cf. previously [§2]):

$\dot{{U}_{j}}.{U}_{k}=\frac{\dot{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{jk}}}}}{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{j}}-\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}$ for $j\mathrm{\ne }k$                             eq 2.4.2.1-2

$\dot{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{i}}}=\dot{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{ii}}}$ for $j\mathrm{\ne }k$                             eq 2.4.2.1-3

One easily deduces $\delta Q$ :

$\delta {Q}_{ij}=\sum_{k\mathrm{\ne }j}\frac{\delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{jk}}}}{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{j}}-\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}{({U}_{k})}_{i}=\sum_{k\mathrm{\ne }j}\frac{\delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{jk}}}}{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{j}}-\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{k}}}{Q}_{\mathit{ik}}$                     eq 2.4.2.1-4

Then using (the last expression serving only to obtain a clearly symmetric matrix):

$\delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{ij}}={Q}_{\mathit{ki}}{Q}_{\mathit{lj}}{\varepsilon}_{kl}=\frac{1}{2}({Q}_{\mathit{ki}}{Q}_{\mathit{lj}}+{Q}_{\mathit{li}}{Q}_{\mathit{kj}}){\varepsilon}_{kl}$

One therefore obtains:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.04/Object_58.svg)

eq 2.4.2.1-5

The tangent matrix at constant damage is therefore written:

${A}_{ijkl}=\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}=\sum_{m,n}{Q}_{\text{im}}{Q}_{\text{jm}}{Q}_{\text{kn}}{Q}_{\ln}{\left\lbrace \frac{\delta \tilde{{\sigma}_{m}}}{\delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{n}}}\right\rbrace }_{d={C}^{\mathit{te}}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{m,n:n\mathrm{\ne }m}(\frac{({Q}_{\text{km}}{Q}_{\ln}+{Q}_{\text{lm}}{Q}_{\text{kn}})({Q}_{in}{Q}_{\text{jm}}{Q}_{\text{jn}}{Q}_{\text{im}})}{\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{n}}-\tilde{{\varepsilon}_{m}}})\tilde{{\sigma}_{m}}$ eq 2.4.2.1-6

##### 2.4.2.2 Term of the tangent matrix due to the evolution of the damage

The expression to be evaluated is written:

$\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial d}{\left\lbrace \frac{\partial d}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}\right\rbrace }_{f=0}$                                 eq 2.4.2.2-1

Equation [eq 2.4.1-1] is written in the form:

$\frac{1+\gamma }{{(1+\gamma d)}^{2}}\left[W(\varepsilon )\right]=k$                             eq 2.4.2.2-2

with: $W(\varepsilon )=\frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\mu \sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i})$ .

Differentiating this expression, one obtains:

$-\frac{2\gamma (1+\gamma )}{{(1+\gamma d)}^{3}}W(\varepsilon )\delta d+\frac{1+\gamma }{{(1+\gamma d)}^{2}}{\sigma}^{\mathit{el}}\cdot \delta \varepsilon =0$                 eq 2.4.2.2-3

with: ${\sigma}^{\mathit{el}}=\frac{\partial W}{\partial \varepsilon }$

The following equality is then used:

$\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial d}=-\frac{1+\gamma }{{(1+\gamma d)}^{2}}{\sigma}^{\mathit{el}}$                             eq 2.4.2.2-4

One concludes:

${\left\lbrace \frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{kl}}\right\rbrace }_{f=0}=-\frac{1+\gamma }{2\gamma (1+\gamma d)W(\varepsilon )}{\sigma}_{ij}^{\mathit{el}}{\sigma}_{kl}^{\mathit{el}}$                     eq 2.4.2.2-5

#### 2.4.3 Case of the fully damaged material

In the case of the fully damaged material, $d=1$ , the stiffness of the material point can vanish. This poses no problem at all for the stress; on the other hand, it can lead to zero pivots in the stiffness matrix. To overcome this difficulty, one allows oneself to define a minimum stiffness, for the tangent matrix or the unloading matrix. This minimum stiffness does not affect the value of the damage (which can reach 1) or the stress (which can reach 0).

In order to preserve a reasonable conditioning of the stiffness matrix, the minimum stiffness is taken as ${10}^{-5}$ of the initial stiffness. An indicator $\chi$ specifies the behaviour during the current time step:

1. $\chi =0$ : no evolution of the damage during the step,
2. $\chi =1$ : evolution of the damage during the step,
3. $\chi =2$ : saturated damage

## 3 Piloting by elastic prediction

Piloting of type PRED_ELAS controls the intensity of the loading in order to satisfy a certain equation linked to the value of the threshold function ${f}^{\mathit{el}}$ during the elastic trial. Consequently, only the points where the damage is not saturated will be taken into account. The algorithm which handles this mode of piloting, cf. [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80), requires the solution at each of these Gauss points of the following scalar equation in which $\Delta \tau$ is a datum and $\eta$ the unknown:

$\tilde{{f}^{\mathit{el}}}(\eta )=\Delta \tau$                                         eq 4-1

The method used up to version 8 was the following: the function $\tilde{{f}^{\mathit{el}}}$ supplies the value of the threshold function during an elastic trial when the displacement field is decomposed in the following way as a function of the scalar parameter $\eta$ :

$u={u}_{0}+\eta {u}_{1}$                                     eq 4-2

where ${u}_{0}$ and ${u}_{1}$ are given. Thanks to the linearity in small strains of the strain operators (computation of the strains from the displacements) and regularised strain, one also obtains the following decompositions:

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{0}+\eta {\varepsilon}_{1}$ and $\bar{\varepsilon}=\bar{{\varepsilon}_{0}}+\eta \bar{{\varepsilon}_{1}}$                 eq 4-3

The function ${f}^{\mathrm{el}}$ having the good property of being convex, equation [eq 4-1] presents zero, one or two solutions, which are sought as follows:

1. Determination of the number of solutions by a study at the bounds $\pm \infty ` and possibly (if the value at the two bounds is each time positive) determination of whether :math:`{f}^{\mathrm{el}}$ presents a negative minimum;
2. Determination of a bracket for each solution from the previous study
3. Determination of the solution (for a convex function knowing the bracket, this search is simple and rapid)

Since version 9, for greater simplicity of use, the parameter $\Delta \tau$ corresponds to the damage increment which one seeks to obtain for at least one point of the structure.

One then no longer seeks a piloting parameter $\eta$ which makes the criterion leave by a value $\Delta \tau$ with the damage coming from the previous time step (cf. Eq 4-1), but a parameter $\eta$ which brings one back onto the criterion with a damage increased by $\Delta \tau$ :

1. ${\tilde{f}}^{\text{el}}(\eta ,{d}^{-})=\Delta \tau \mathrm{\Rightarrow }{\tilde{f}}^{\text{el}}(\eta ,{d}^{-}+\Delta \tau )=0$

This coefficient $\Delta \tau$ is computed in the following way:

$\Delta \tau =\frac{\Delta t}{\text{COEF\_MULT}}$

where $\Delta t$ corresponds to the time increment defined in the list of instants of the analysis and COEF_MULT is the coefficient specified by the keyword COEF_MULT of the option PILOTAGE in the operator STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03).

**Demonstration of the principal frame of stress**

The trace term in the energy poses no problem: it is invariant under any change of frame.

There remains the term in $\sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{i})+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))$ .

**Notation**: one writes with **one** index (for example ${\varepsilon}_{i}$ ) the $i$ -th eigenvalue of a tensor which is written (making explicit its **two** indices) ${\varepsilon}_{kl}$ .

- If the eigenvalues of the strain are all distinct, one then shows that $\dot{{\varepsilon}_{i}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}_{ii}}$ , with $\dot{{\varepsilon}_{kl}}$ the components of $\dot{\varepsilon}$ in the fixed frame coinciding with the principal frame of strain at the instant considered (in this frame one therefore has ${\varepsilon}_{kl}={\varepsilon}_{k}{\delta}_{kl}$ ).

Indeed, let us write the strains in the form:

> $\varepsilon =\sum_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}{U}_{i}\otimes {U}_{i}$

Differentiating this expression, one obtains:

$\dot{\varepsilon}=\sum_{i}\dot{{\varepsilon}_{i}}{U}_{i}\otimes {U}_{i}+{\varepsilon}_{i}\dot{{U}_{i}}\otimes {U}_{i}+{\varepsilon}_{i}{U}_{i}\otimes \dot{{U}_{i}}$

Using the fact that the eigenvectors are orthonormal:

${U}_{i}\cdot {U}_{j}={\delta}_{ij}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\dot{{U}_{i}}\cdot {U}_{j}+{U}_{i}\cdot \dot{{U}_{j}}=0$

one obtains the variations of the eigenvalues and of the eigenvectors:

$\dot{{\varepsilon}_{i}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}_{ii}}$ and $\dot{{U}_{j}}\cdot {U}_{k}=\frac{\dot{{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{jk}}}}{{\varepsilon}_{j}-{\varepsilon}_{k}}$ for $j\mathrm{\ne }k$

This is obviously valid only if the eigenvalues are distinct (as can be seen clearly on the expression of the variations of the eigenvectors). This comes from the fact that the eigenvectors are not continuous functions of the elements of the matrix.

- In the case where two strain eigenvalues are equal (and apart from the very particular case where they are also zero), they are either positive or negative. Let us take the case where they are positive (the other case lends itself to a demonstration similar in every respect). The energy concerning these two eigenvalues is then written: ${\sum}_{i=2}^{3}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}$ (the two equal eigenvalues are considered to have the indices 2 and 3). Differentiating this expression, one obtains:

$2\sum_{i=2}^{3}{\varepsilon}_{i}d{\varepsilon}_{i}+2\varepsilon \sum_{i=2}^{3}d{\varepsilon}_{i}$ denoting by $\varepsilon$ the common eigenvalue.

By invariance of the trace of a matrix, here the restriction of the strain to the principal plane considered, one obtains:

$\sum_{i=2}^{3}d{\varepsilon}_{i}=\sum_{i=2}^{3}d{\varepsilon}_{ii}$ , whatever the evolution undergone by the principal frame at that moment.

For the remaining eigenvalue (distinct from the other two and of index 1 with the notations chosen), one has: $d{\varepsilon}_{1}=d{\varepsilon}_{11}$ .

Gathering these expressions, one obtains:

$d(\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))=d({\varepsilon}_{1}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{1}))+d(\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))=2\sum_{i=1}^{3}{\varepsilon}_{ii}H({\varepsilon}_{ii})d{\varepsilon}_{ii}$

In conclusion, whether the eigenvalues are distinct or not, one obtains:

$d({\sum}_{i}{\varepsilon}_{i}^{2}H({\varepsilon}_{i}))=2{\sum}_{i}{\varepsilon}_{ii}H({\varepsilon}_{ii})d{\varepsilon}_{ii}$ with the notations adopted.

This reasoning generalises easily to the case of three equal eigenvalues.

The differential of the energy at constant damage is then written:

$\begin{array}{cc}d\Phi (\varepsilon ,d)\mid {d={C}^{\mathit{te}}}_{}=& \lambda (\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )d(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )(H(-\mathit{tr}\varepsilon )+\frac{1-d}{1+\gamma d}H(\mathit{tr}\varepsilon ))+\\ & 2\mu {\sum}_{i}{\varepsilon}_{ii}d{\varepsilon}_{ii}(H(-{\varepsilon}_{ii})+\frac{1-d}{1-\gamma d}H({\varepsilon}_{ii}))\end{array}$

On this expression, one clearly observes that the principal frame of strain is also a principal frame of stress.
