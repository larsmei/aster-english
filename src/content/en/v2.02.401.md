This test is based on the independent validation of version 4 of the beam models.

It allows to check the internal forces on an inclined beam, for sinusoidal loadings as a function of time (modelling with elements POU_D_T, straight beam from Timoshenko), applied in traction-compression and then in pure torsion.

## 2 Reference solutions

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solutions

#### 2.1.1 Distributed loading of traction-compression

A straight beam length $L$ working solely in traction-compression is subjected to a constant load distributed according to $x$ but varying sinusoidally as a function of time. It is clamped at both ends.

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\rho S\frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial {t}^{2}}-\mathit{ES}\frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial {x}^{2}}=f(t)\\ u(0)=0,u(L)=0\end{array}$

To solve, the Fourier transformation is applied to the equation in time:

$\frac{{\partial}^{2}\stackrel{ˆ}{u}}{\partial {x}^{2}}=-\frac{\rho}{E}4{\pi}^{2}{\omega}^{2}\stackrel{ˆ}{u}+\frac{1}{\mathit{ES}}\stackrel{ˆ}{f}(\omega )$

| $\stackrel{ˆ}{u}$ | : | Fourier transformer from $u$ , |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\stackrel{ˆ}{f}$ | : | Fourier $f$ transformer. |

So we have for $f(t)=F\cos(2\pi {\omega}_{0}t)$:

## 4 Modelling B

> The beam is very steep on its traction-compression modes:

${f}_{0\mathit{traction}/\mathit{compression}}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{(a)}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{(\frac{E}{\rho})}=806\mathit{Hz}$

In comparison, the frequency of the $\frac{1}{2\pi }\mathit{Hz}$ pull-compression forces can be considered quasi-static. This is done in Modelling B: the results of a calculation are compared with the linear static operator of *Code_Aster* with the results of Modelling A on the case of distributed force.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test ensures that the internal forces of the dynamic elements beam are correct. The results show a very good agreement with the analytical solution, for an mesh composed of only two elements POU_D_T.

It is also shown that for a very steep system relative to the frequency of the stress, a quasi-static calculation gives as good results as dynamic calculation.
