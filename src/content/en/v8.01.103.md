# v8.01.103 FDLV103 - Concentric spheres separated by an incompressible fluid

**Summary:**

This test concerns the field of fluids in the inertial coupling aspect. A modal analysis of a rigid sphere connected to the massif by springs is performed and plunged into a volume of fluid incompressible of spherical form.

The test validates the 3D added mass calculation and the modal calculation for a 3D coupled fluid-structure system.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.103/10000000000001360000010A5387FB2F1F17128C.png)

The inner sphere $({S}_{1})$ has an ${R}_{1}=0.35m$ radius.

The inner sphere $({S}_{2})$ has an ${R}_{2}=0.45m$ radius.

### 1.2 Material properties

The inner sphere $({S}_{1})$ is made of steel:

${\rho}_{s}=7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

$E=2.1{10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$

$\nu =0.3$

The fluid filling the volume between $({S}_{1})$ and $({S}_{2})$ is water of density ${\rho}_{f}=\mathrm{1 000 }\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ (equivalent thermal characteristics: $\lambda =1$ , ${\rho}_{f}{C}_{p}=1000$ ).

The fluid is modelled by 3D thermal modelling.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A zero temperature is assumed in a fluid point mesh.

Springs are built into the massif at ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ points.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The analytical result [bib1] is:

In the case of two concentric spheres immersed in the same fluid, the added mass induced by the fluid contained in the internal sphere $({S}_{1})$ is shown to be:

${m}_{a}=\frac{2}{3}{\rho}_{f}\pi \left[\frac{1+2{(\frac{{R}_{1}}{{R}_{2}})}^{3}}{1-{(\frac{{R}_{1}}{{R}_{2}})}^{3}}\right]{R}_{1}^{3}$

If the sphere is assumed to have only one degree of freedom following $\mathrm{Oz}$, the proper $({S}_{1})$ sphere translation mode following $\mathrm{Oz}$ is given by:

$f=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{2K}}{m+{m}_{a}}}$

Digital application:

| $K={10}^{5}N/m$ |
| --- |
| $m=12\mathrm{kg}$ |
| ${m}_{a}=329.17\mathrm{kg}$ |
| $F=3.8534\mathrm{Hz}$ |

The nodes of the internal hull is blocked according to all of its rotational degrees of freedom, as well as their two degrees of freedom translation DX and DY (GROUP_NO: NOINT).

The springs are clamped in the solids at the points $\mathrm{P1}$ and $\mathrm{P2}$ (GROUP_NO: ENCAST).

The hulls are $\mathrm{1 }\mathrm{mm}$ thick.

## 4 Summary of the results

The values of the analytical results are well reflected.

The small difference observed on the added mass may come from:

- a rather thin discretisation of the sphere surface,
- or a rather thin discretization of the fluid volume.
