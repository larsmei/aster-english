# v6.04.180 SSNV180 - Taking into account thermal expansion and drying creep in BETON_UMLV and BETON_BURGER models

**Summary:**

This test validates the consideration of thermal expansion and desiccation creep in constitutive laws BETON_UMLV and BETON_BURGER. The results of this test are compared with a numerical solution obtained with Scilab 2.7.2. in the case of 3D Modelling (BETON_UMLV) and a numerical solution obtained with python for BETON_BURGER (SSNV180B.44).

Modelling A: Fluid test with thermal expansion for model BETON_UMLV

Modelling B: Fluid test with thermal expansion for model BETON_BURGER

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In this test, a homogeneous drying field is created in the structure, which varies linearly over 750 days, the initial humidity is $100\%$ (condition of a sealed test piece) and gradually decreases to $50\%$ on the 750th day.
In model B, re-wetting is then performed by applying a linear variation of 750 days to 1500 days, from $50\%$ to $100\%$. This allows verification that the desiccation creep remains stable during this phase.

The degree of hydration varies linearly from 0 to 1 between the initial moment and the 28th day.

The reference temperature is $20°C$ . The thermal loading corresponds to a temperature rise varying from $20°C$ and $40°C$ between the initial and final moments.

The mechanical load corresponds to a one-way compression in the vertical direction ($z$ in $\mathrm{3D}$); its intensity is $12[\mathrm{MPa}]$. The load is applied in $\mathrm{1s}$ and is kept constant for 100 days.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The beginning of the calculation is assumed at the moment $–1$ . At this moment there is no drying field, no mechanical stress.

At moment 0, a drying field corresponding to $100\%$ d-hygrometry, a hydration field corresponding to zero advancement and a thermal field at the reference temperature shall be applied.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The analytical solution has not been developed for this hydro-mechanical loading. Also, the reference solution is obtained numerically using the software Scilab 2.7.2 for BETON_UMLV or python for BETON_BURGER. Each deformation component is calculated separately.

- The endogenous withdrawal deformations are determined from the $e_{re} = k_{re} \beta$ relationship where $\beta$ is the degree of hydration of the material.
- The desiccation removal deformations are determined from the $e_{rd} = k_{rd} C$ relationship where the moisture content of the material is defined.
- The thermal expansion deformations are determined from the $e_{th} = \alpha (T-T_{ref})$ relationship where $T$ and $T_{ref}$ refer to the current temperature and the material reference temperature, respectively
- The own creep deformations are calculated numerically using a discretization identical to that of BETON_UMLV and an implantation following an explicit scheme for BETON_BURGER. The temporal discretization is then necessarily finer for the explicit scheme.
- The creep deformations of desiccation are calculated analytically.

BETON_UMLV law still uses the old relationship:

$$
\dot{e_{th}} = \frac{|\dot{h}|\sigma}{\eta_{fd}}
$$

where $h=f(C)$ means the moisture of the material.

The BETON_BURGER law uses the following relationship:

$$
\dot{e}_{th} = \frac{<\dot{h}>\sigma}{\eta_{fd}}
$$

où

$$
<\dot{h}> =  \left \{
\begin{array}{r c l}
   \dot{h} & & \text{si} &  \dot{h}<0 & \text{et} & h(t)<\min_{\tau \in [0,t]} h(\tau)\\
   0      & & \text{sinon} \\
\end{array}
\right .
$$

## 5 Summary of the results

The values obtained are consistent with the values of the numerical reference solution.
The errors of the order of 2% are due to the fact that BETON_BURGER Law now works with a relative humidity calculated at the end of time step, whereas the reference values were obtained with a relative humidity calculated at the beginning of time step.
