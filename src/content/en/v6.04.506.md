# v6.04.506 SSNV506 - Elasto-plastic indentation of a block by an elastic spherical indenter

**Summary:**

This test is for Modelling the indentation of an elastic sphere on a half-plane with elasto-plastic behavior. The objective is to test the contact-related features on an example with non-linear material.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.506/100000000000012C00000168470D7C91D38568BB.png)

| Rayon of the sphere | $R=500\mathrm{mm}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Movement imposed | $100\mathrm{mm}$ |

### 1.2 Material properties

Two different modellingss to represent the rigid sphere:

Hardware Rigidification: $E=\mathrm{2,1E9}\mathrm{Mpa}$ and $\nu =0,3$

Rigidification by kinematic conditions

Block: Steel, constitutive law elasto-plastic perfect.

| Young's modulus | $E=210000\mathrm{MPa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =0,3$ |
| Nutrient module | $\mathrm{Et}=0$ |
| Elastic limit | ${\sigma}_{y}=50\mathrm{MPa}$ |

The deformations are axisymmetric and the block forming the plane is assumed to be clamped on its base.

A forced displacement is applied:

- Loading from 0 to $–100\mathrm{mm}$ on the upper part of the sphere in models A and D
- Loading from 0 to $–100\mathrm{mm}$ on the sphere contact surface in models B and C

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference results are derived from the book cited below [bib1].

${p}_{m}=3{\sigma}_{0}$ with ${p}_{m}$ the contact pressure (page 171).

$\mathrm{Rjohnson}={p}_{m}a=3{\sigma}_{0}a$ if $a$ is the contact surface.

$\delta =0,368{a}^{2}/R$ perfect plasticity gold after Richmond analysis (page 200)

Finally, we get:

$\mathrm{Rjohnson}=3\pi R{\sigma}_{0}\delta /0,368$

$\mathrm{Rjohnson}$: Normal contact reaction of the massif on the sphere

$R$ : Rayon of the sphere

$\delta$ : Moving the top of the massif

${\sigma}_{0}$ : Elastic limit of the solid

This result is valid under the following assumptions:

- problem axisymmetric,
- perfectly plastic material (the coefficient 0.368 is derived from this hypothesis)
- small deformations
- rigid sphere.

### 2.2 Baseline

The reference results are obtained from the previous formula. It is valid for the complete 3D model.

**Note:**

*In our study, Rjohnson depends solely on the displacement, the relationship can be written in the following form using the data of the problem:* $\mathrm{Rjohnson}=640270\delta$ *with* $\mathrm{Rjohnson}$ *in newton and* $\delta$ *in millimetres.* $\delta$ *is directly connected to the calculation moment.*

The normal contact result value from ASTER is given on a 1radian 2D axisymmetric opening quarter and on a $\pi /2$ quarter for the 3D model (by symmetry, you only need to model a quarter of the problem).

Thus, the reference values are:

| en 2D axisymmetric | : | $\mathrm{Rref}=\mathrm{Rjohnson}/2\pi =101902,1\delta$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| en 3D | : | $\mathrm{Rref}=\mathrm{Rjohnson}/4=160067,5\delta$ |

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

analytical solution.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The problem revolution symmetry allows axisymmetric Modelling: The sphere and block are represented by a half disk and the cut of half of the block, gridded with elements 2D axisymmetric respectively.

A contact type node-mesh cell is defined between the two structures.

A load on the move is applied to the upper part of the sphere rigidified by a high Young's modulus.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.506/1000000000000179000001D8DC22DCCDBD5A4E55.png)

**boundary condition:**

- revolution symmetry: nodes on the $Y$ (node group $«\mathrm{LB}»$ and $«\mathrm{LS}»$ ) axis are blocked in the $X$ direction (DX = 0),
- clamped support of the base: nodes of the $«\mathrm{PLANX}»$ group is blocked according to $X$ and $Y$ directions (DX = DY = 0),
- rigid body movements are removed by imposing an restraint following $y$ between the node $E$ belonging to the sphere and the node $D$ belonging to the massif.

**Charges:**

A forced displacement is applied to the upper part of the sphere (node group $«\mathrm{NDPL}»$ ) in the direction $Y$: Loading from 0 to $–100.\mathrm{mm}$

## 4 Modelling B

A load on the move is applied to all mesh cells representing the sphere, rigidified by kinematic conditions.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.506/10000000000001AF000001D6A65BAEBA48EE634C.png)

## 10 Summary of the results

The results are good. However, a larger difference between the reference and the 3D results exists. It is possible to fill it by refining the mesh even further, but it must be paid in memory and in time of calculation.

The size of the elements is very large. If they are too large, one can see the reaction curve showing a function of the displacement of the "vagues" (loss of linearity of this curve). Each "vag" corresponds to the contact of an element. Moreover, if the mesh is not sufficiently refined, the reaction given by *Aster* is significantly different from that given by the reference.

Only Modelling the sphere by its contact surface rigidified by kinematic conditions allows time saving.
