# r7.01.46 Elasto-visco-plastic constitutive law NLH_CSRM for geomaterials

**Summary:** This document presents the principal equations of the constitutive law NLH_CSRM (Non-Linear Hardening Critical State Rock Model) as well as its numerical integration algorithm.

## 1 Introduction

The model NLH_CSRM will, in due course, replace the constitutive laws LKR and LETK . This work of “modernisation” forms part of the development actions of the EDF-R&D project “Management of Radioactive Waste” (GDR2).

The scope of use of the model NLH_CSRM is for the moment limited to **isotropic mechanical** behaviour. The taking into account of **structural anisotropy** and of the dependence of the hardenings on **temperature** — effective in the latest version of the model LKR — will be the subject of forthcoming submissions; likewise the dependence of the criteria on the third invariant of the stress deviator (Lode angle).

The model NLH_CSRM lies within the **thermodynamic framework of generalised standard materials**  with two irreversible mechanisms,

- **Plastic mechanism** — **instantaneous** irreversible behaviour: hardening and/or softening, contractant or dilatant volumetric behaviour, influence of the mean stress on the brittle character of the response in compression, …;
- **Viscoplastic mechanism** — **delayed** irreversible behaviour (of Perzyna type): primary, secondary and tertiary creep (failure).

A **coupling between these two mechanisms**  makes it possible — among other things — to represent the dependence of the maximum strength of certain geomaterials on the loading rate.

These works of “modernisation” are motivated by the wish to increase the robustness and the efficiency of the model used to describe the behaviour of the Callovo-Oxfordian argillite — the host rock constituting the first geological barrier of Cigéo — in order to be able to envisage its use on 3D problems that are demanding in memory and in computation time.

**Note:** IniSigTime is not an unknown of the non-linear system to be solved, but it nevertheless intervenes in the integration algorithm, i.e. **it is not solely a post-processing variable** . IniSigTime makes it possible to initialise the elastic strain tensor in the case of non-zero initial stresses (but remaining inside the initial elasticity domains); which is often the case in geomechanics (example of the excavation of a tunnel). Particular attention must therefore be paid to the initialisation of this variable in STAT_NON_LINE and DINA_NON_LINE in the case of a restart of analyses.
