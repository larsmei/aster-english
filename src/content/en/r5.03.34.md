# r5.03.34 Viscoelastic constitutive law REGU_VISC_ELAS

**Summary:**

In order to regularise certain forms of quasi-static instabilities, a regularisation technique accessible in STAT_NON_LINE via the keyword REGU_VISC relies on the superposition of the constitutive stresses with viscoelastic stresses, the latter being able to confer on the system the expected stabilisation. The constitutive law REGU_VISC_ELAS governs the evolution of these regularisation stresses via a Maxwell model. It is accessible only through this regularisation functionality.

## 1 Field of application

### 1.1 Purpose

In order to regularise certain forms of quasi-static instabilities, a regularisation technique accessible in STAT_NON_LINE via the keyword REGU_VISC relies on the superposition of the constitutive stresses with viscoelastic stresses, the latter being able to confer on the system the expected stabilisation, see [Lorentz, Drouet, Hamon, 2020]. Provided that sufficient stiffness is supplied, the evolution of the solution then becomes continuous with respect to time and the subdivision of the time step once again constitutes a response to possible convergence difficulties. Such a superposition is described in the figure below, where LDC represents the “true” constitutive law of the material and the Maxwell system of the lower branch corresponds to the viscous stiffness. The constitutive law REGU_VISC_ELAS describes the behaviour of this viscoelastic branch.

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.34/1000020100000216000000F1A97455F5621E1441.png)

This constitutive model is compatible:

- with a small-perturbation kinematics (HPP) or a large-strain kinematics (GDEF_LOG);
- with a local constitutive law or an internal-variable-gradient constitutive law in which the gradient of an internal variable is involved, see [Lorentz & Andrieux, 1999] or [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01).

### 1.2 Material parameters

The viscoelastic model relies on two parameters: the stiffness of the spring, denoted $k$, and the viscosity of the damper. Rather than entering the latter, it is preferred to introduce the characteristic time of the viscoelastic branch, denoted $\tau$ . These parameters are entered under the factor keyword VISC_ELAS of the command DEFI_MATERIAU (keywords K and TAU).

### 1.3 Strain measures

The constitutive model is fed by the mechanical strain (i.e. without the thermal-shrinkage terms and the others) if such is the case for the main constitutive law. Otherwise, it is the total strain (the displacement gradient). The kinematics PETIT and GDEF_LOG are both supported. The strain will be denoted $\epsilon$ below, without loss of generality.

### 1.4 Internal variables

The constitutive law introduces eight internal variables. They are, on the one hand, the six components of the stress in the viscoelastic branch, which accounts for the history effect of the model. And, on the other hand, two additional variables are computed essentially for post-processing purposes, in order to measure the extent of the viscous effects linked to the regularisation: they are the volumetric elastic energy stored in the spring and the volumetric energy dissipated by viscosity by the damper (accumulated over time).

The internal variables are grouped in the following table. The corresponding indices do not correspond to the internal-variable number; indeed, they are stored after the internal variables of the main constitutive law (if the latter relies, for example, on five internal variables, those linked to the viscoelastic regularisation will be available under V6 to V13).

| SIVXX - SIVYZ | 1 - 6 | Components of the viscous stress |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VISCELAS | 7 | Stored volumetric elastic energy |
| VISCDISS | 8 | Volumetric energy dissipated by viscosity |

## 2 Continuous model

### 2.1 Constitutive equations

The Maxwell model is written simply:

$$
{\sigma}^{v}=k(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{v});{\sigma}_{v}=k\tau {\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}
$$

where ${\sigma}^{v}$ denotes the viscous stress tensor and ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}$ the viscous strain tensor associated with the damper. The viscous strain can be eliminated in order to obtain the evolution equation of the viscous stress:

$$
{\dot{\sigma}}^{v}+\frac{1}{\tau}{\sigma}^{v}=k\dot{\epsilon}
$$

The default initial state corresponds to a zero viscous stress.

### 2.2 Definition of the energies

The volumetric energy stored in the spring of the viscous branch is:

$$
\text{VISCELAS}=\frac{1}{2k}{\sigma}^{v}:{\sigma}^{v}
$$

As for the volumetric energy dissipated by viscosity, it is written:

$$
\text{VISCDISS}=\frac{1}{k\tau }{\int}_{0}^{t}{\sigma}^{v}:{\sigma}^{v}\mathit{dt}
$$

## 3 Numerical integration

### 3.1 Discretised equations

The time discretisation of the constitutive equations relies on a first-order scheme. Let ${Q}_{n}$ denote the value of a quantity $Q$ at the beginning of the time step, $\Delta Q$ its increment during the time step and (simply) $Q$ its value at the end of the time step. The mechanical state at the beginning of the time step $({\epsilon}_{n},{{\sigma}^{v}}_{n})$ is assumed known, as is the strain increment $\Delta \epsilon$ (and therefore also the strain $\epsilon$ ). The aim is then to compute the stress ${\sigma}^{v}$ at the end of the time step.

The discretisation of the stress–strain relation () is written as follows:

$$
{\dot{\sigma}}^{v}+\frac{1}{\tau}{\sigma}^{v}=k\frac{\Delta \epsilon }{\Delta t};{\sigma}^{v}({t}_{n})={{\sigma}^{v}}_{n}
$$

The right-hand side is then constant, so that the solution of this differential equation is written:

$$
{\sigma}^{v}={A}_{\Delta t}{{\sigma}^{v}}_{n}+{B}_{\Delta t}\Delta \epsilon ;{A}_{\Delta t}=\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau}\right);{B}_{\Delta t}=\frac{k\tau }{\Delta t}\left[1-\exp\left(-\frac{\Delta t}{\tau}\right)\right]
$$

The computation of the viscous stress is therefore explicit.

### 3.2 Tangent matrix

The derivative of the expression () immediately supplies the tangent matrix:

$$
\frac{d{\sigma}^{v}}{d\epsilon }={B}_{\Delta t}{I}_{4}
$$

where ${I}_{4}$ denotes the fourth-order identity tensor.

From the perspective of a prediction by the tangent matrix, the zeroth-order term of the Taylor expansion of the discretised constitutive law () does not correspond to the viscous stress at the beginning of the time step but to:

$$
{\sigma}^{0}={A}_{\Delta t}{{\sigma}^{v}}_{n}
$$

This is the stress to be supplied to the option RIGI_MECA_TANG.

### 3.3 Computation of the energies

The stored volumetric energy () is an explicit function of the viscous stresses and poses no difficulty. The volumetric energy dissipated by viscosity () introduces an integral which will be evaluated by a midpoint method (which is compatible with order 1 of the integration scheme of the constitutive law):

$$
\text{VISCDISS}:=\text{VISCDISS}+\frac{\Delta t}{k\tau }{\left[\frac{{{\sigma}^{v}}_{n}+{\sigma}^{v}}{2}\right]}^{2}
$$
