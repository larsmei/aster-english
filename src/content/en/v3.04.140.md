# v3.04.140 SSLV140 - Calculation of actual modules by Python method

**Summary:**

A test with an analytical reference is presented here. The geometry treated is a set of two cubes with different elastic properties. The aim is to find the Young's modulus of the mixture made up of these two cubes in two directions.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.140/Object_1.svg)

The following surfaces are defined:

- $\mathrm{YZ1}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P1},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P5}$ and $\mathrm{P7}$ .
- $\mathrm{YZ2}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P9},\mathrm{P10},\mathrm{P11}$ and $\mathrm{P12}$ .
- $\mathrm{XY1}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P1},\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P9},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4}$ and $\mathrm{P10}$ .
- $\mathrm{XY2}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P5},\mathrm{P6},\mathrm{P11},\mathrm{P7},\mathrm{P8}$ and $\mathrm{P12}$ .
- $\mathrm{XZ1}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{P10},\mathrm{P7},\mathrm{P8}$ and $\mathrm{P12}$ .
- $\mathrm{XZ2}$ face: containing nodes $\mathrm{P1},\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P9},\mathrm{P5},\mathrm{P6}$ and $\mathrm{P11}$ .

and the following elements:

- $\mathrm{M1}$ element: containing nodes $\mathrm{P1},\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P3},\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{P5},\mathrm{P6},\mathrm{P7}$ and $\mathrm{P8}$ .
- $\mathrm{M2}$ element: containing nodes $\mathrm{P2},\mathrm{P9},\mathrm{P4},\mathrm{P10},\mathrm{P6},\mathrm{P11},\mathrm{P8}$ and $\mathrm{P12}$ .

### 1.2 Material properties

Two materials are used:

- Material $\mathit{MAT1}$ assigned to the element $\mathrm{M1}$:

Young's modulus : $\mathit{E1}=200000\mathit{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : ${\nu}_{1}=0.3$

- Material $\mathit{MAT2}$ assigned to the element $\mathrm{M2}$:

Young's modulus : $\mathrm{E2}=100000\mathrm{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : ${\nu}_{2}=0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

**First calculation:**

It is a simple tensile calculation according to the $X$ direction:

- A linear ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=1$ elastic deformation is imposed on the $\mathrm{YZ2}$ surface.
- The $\mathrm{YZ1}$ surface does not move in the direction $X$ .

**Second calculation:**

It is a simple tensile calculation according to the $Y$ direction:

- A linear ${\varepsilon}_{yy}=1$ elastic deformation is imposed on the $\mathrm{XZ2}$ surface.
- The $\mathrm{XZ1}$ surface does not move in the direction $Y$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

According to the general theory of homogenization of composite materials [bib1], the effective ${E}_{xx}^{\mathit{eff}}$ and ${E}_{yy}^{\mathit{eff}}$ modules in $X$ and $Y$ directions of a mixture of the above-mentioned shape are given by the following formulae:

$\frac{1}{{E}_{xx}^{\mathit{eff}}}=\frac{{f}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}+\frac{{f}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}$

${E}_{yy}^{\mathit{eff}}={f}_{1}{E}_{1}+{f}_{2}{E}_{2}$

${f}_{1}$ and ${f}_{2}$ are the density fractions of each material, in our case:

${f}_{1}={f}_{2}=0.5$

### 2.2 Bibliographical references

  1. BORNET, T. BRETHEAU and P. GILORMINI: Homogenization in Materials Mechanics (T1). Hermes Science Publications - 2001.
