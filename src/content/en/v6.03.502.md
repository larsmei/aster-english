# v6.03.502 SSNP502 - Crushing of a polyurethane ring between two non-reformable plates with friction

**Summary:**

The test consists of simulating the flat stress crushing of a polyurethane elastic circular ring by two unformable symmetrical plates. The objective is to test the functionality of the contact. This test includes a re-collage on a contact area of significant length with the presence of large elastic deformations.

A symmetrically imposed displacement is applied to both plates; the resulting force and contact pressure for different points in contact are compared with the results obtained in the reference article.

In the three modellingss proposed, the ring is modeled with mesh cells QUAD4 in flat constraints:

- **modelization B** , a contact **with friction** treated with the method of penalisation was defined between the plate and the ring,
- **modelization C** , a contact **with friction** treated with the continuous method was defined between the plate and the ring.

| Young's modulus: | $E=407N/{\mathrm{cm}}^{2}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio: | $\nu =0,48$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The constraints are flat.

An incremental displacement imposed from $0$ to $4,45\mathrm{cm}$ is applied to the nodes of the unformable plates.

**Note on units:**

The dimensions and movements are in centimetres, so to remain homogeneous, the pressures must be entered in $N/{\mathit{cm}}^{2}$ .

### 1.4 Initial conditions

None.

The solution is derived from a calculation code and an experimental test.

From the reference solution valid for whole plate Modelling, the normal result must be split by two to obtain a reference valid for half a plate.

## 3 Modelling B

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

A model testing friction contact functionality treated with the penalty method has been implemented and, given the symmetry of the problem, it includes a quarter of the ring as well as the mesh of an unformable plate.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.502/1000000000000170000001C015F9DD4B7346FD34.png)

**boundary condition:**

| Symmetry conditions: | nodes of the $\mathrm{LAB}$ group located in the $X=0$ plane are blocked in the $X$ ($\mathrm{DX}=0$) direction, nodes of the $\mathrm{LCD}$ group located in the $Y=0$ plane are blocked in the $Y$ ($\mathrm{DY}=0$) direction, all nodes of the mesh cell $«\mathrm{Plaque}»$ group are blocked in the $X$ ($\mathrm{DX}=0$) direction |
| --- | --- |

To avoid rigid body movements, nodes $A$ and $\mathrm{P1}$ even have vertical displacement.

**Charges:**

The $Y$ is the $Y$-imposed move on all nodes plates: $\mathrm{DY}$ varies from $0$ to $2,225\mathrm{cm}$.

(the value of $4,45\mathrm{cm}$ is the vertical approximation of the two symmetrical plates.)

**Note:**

*The mesh was made in * $\mathrm{cm}$ *.*

## 5 Summary of the results

Whatever the type of Modelling of the contact area, the results obtained are satisfactory. The differences observed on the reaction force are small. But the reference values are very approximate because they are extracted from a paper curve.

The mesh of the calculation code taken as a reference and the mesh used by Aster are different. Furthermore, it is not explained in the reference how the normal contact pressure is extracted. Thus, no reference tests were performed on this pressure. However, non-regression tests are performed on the contact pressure ($\mathrm{SIYY}$ at the node in contact). The appearance of this pressure and the contact area are identical between the two calculation codes.
