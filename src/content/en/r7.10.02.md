# r7.10.02 Post-processing of modal analyses with shock

**Summary:**

This document presents the principle of post-processing of transient analyses by modal recombination with shock non-linearities available in the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T.

Two post-processing options may be used. The first, usable for vibration–wear problems, determines mean and RMS values of displacements, shock forces and wear power dissipated at the supports with clearances; the second is applicable to the detailed analysis of the impacts occurring during transient loadings. The instantaneous maximum force, the duration of the shock time, the impulse exchanged and the velocity before impact are determined for each shock.

## 1 Introduction

Numerical developments have been carried out in *Code_Aster* in order to allow the transient analysis of structures presenting vibrations with shock at certain points. In certain cases, friction forces may also appear and lead to a phenomenon of localised wear.

Whether the damage is by pure impact or by impact–friction, the engineer wishes to access the quantities associated with this damage, which requires a specific post-processing downstream of the non-linear transient analysis.

This post-processing information is also valuable when one wishes to validate the non-linear analysis module by comparing its results with what can be measured on a specific test rig. Test programmes (MASSIF and MULTICHOC) have been implemented for this purpose and were the first users of these post-processing features.

The objective of this note is to specify the quantities to be analysed in vibrations with shock and their specific character. It is then a matter of determining the appropriate statistical treatments to apply to these signals in order to extract the most characteristic instantaneous or mean quantities.

First, the treatments applied in the case of a problem with shock and friction will be seen (option 'USURE' of the command POST_DYNA_MODA_T).

The following chapter will be devoted to the treatments applied in the case of a pure vibration–impact phenomenon, in which the quantities of each impact are analysed in more detail (option 'IMPACT' of the command POST_DYNA_MODA_T).

## 2 Quantities considered in vibrations with shocks

The primary quantities considered in vibrations with shock are the same whether they come from experimental measurements or from the numerical analysis; they concern the shock forces and the displacements at the shock points. The experimental results nevertheless present an additional difficulty of analysis, due to the errors or biases introduced by the measurement systems.

The two quantities mentioned above will be examined in turn.

### 2.1 Shock forces

The first concern regarding structures vibrating with shocks is to know better the forces received by the structure during the shocks on its supports with clearances, or between the structures. These data are computed in time by the algorithm of DYNA_VIBRA; they are then archived with a step defined in this same operator. The shock data having a very significant frequency content, care will be taken to have sufficient archiving (do not exceed PAS_ARCH : 10 ). These forces, expressed in a local frame attached to the obstacle $(\mathrm{Yloc},\mathrm{Zloc})$ , are traditionally decomposed into a part normal to the obstacle ($\mathrm{Fn}$ in the figure below) and a tangential part ($\mathrm{Ft}$ ) if friction is taken into account between the structures. The shock conditions are such that the normal shock force has a constant sign, taken by convention as positive in *Code_Aster*.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.02/Object_1.svg)

### 2.2 Shock displacements

The displacements of the structure at its supports with clearance are another important piece of computed information. Their analysis nevertheless poses fewer problems, because the spectral content is less rich. In the case of circular obstacles, or obstacles described in a polar manner, a polar description of the displacement may be of interest.

### 2.3 Secondary quantities

#### 2.3.1 Shock time

The contact time between the structure and the supports with clearances is an indirect quantity characteristic of the vibration motion with shock. It may be deduced in various ways, on a displacement criterion or on a criterion of positive reaction force. A notion of overall shock time, decomposed into elementary shock (or rebound), will be introduced in [§3.4].

#### 2.3.2 Computed quantities

Other secondary quantities may be important in the analysis of the shock conditions; these are the **impulse** during the impact (integral of the force exchanged), the **wear power**, the **maximum force** during an impact, ... These quantities are specific to each post-processing and they will be specified in the two chapters that follow, for the post-processing option **'** USURE **'** and **'** IMPACT **'**.

## 3 Modal transient post-processing — option 'USURE'

The characterisation of transient measurements is the aim of signal processing. It teaches us that a signal is entirely determined by the data of all its statistical moments. In practice it is out of the question to compute all the statistical moments; the post-processing is limited to the quantities classically computed in signal processing (simple mean, standard deviation and RMS value). They are characteristic of the signals that one wishes to analyse and compare. Similar signals must necessarily have these first statistical moments close to one another (the converse being false). The statistical quantities retained here are well suited to the analysis, the comparison or the classification of vibration signals under random excitation with shock non-linearities.

The averaged quantities and their computation will therefore be examined, distinguishing the various quantities mentioned in the previous chapter:

- the displacements,
- the shock forces,
- the determination of the contact and of the contact time.

Other composite information may be computed from the previous ones, in particular the wear power.

### 3.1 Statistical treatment by blocks

In order to analyse the stationarity of the signals and of the statistical treatments carried out on the signals, a splitting of the time signals into blocks is carried out. Thus the post-processing duration, defined between the initial time instant (INST_INIT) and the final time instant (INST_FIN), is split into a number of time blocks (NB_BLOC) of identical duration. The computation of the statistical values — mean, standard deviation, ... — is carried out for each of the blocks; a general value for the signal over the set of blocks is also computed.

In the case of an analysis of the response of a structure to a random loading, this technique of computation by blocks makes it possible to make sure that the transient phase of the analysis is over and that the value announced is indeed stationary over an observation time associated with the duration of the analysis.

### 3.2 Statistical treatments applied to the shock displacements

Consider the time signal $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x(t)$ , of which an archiving is carried out at a certain frequency ${F}_{\mathrm{acquis}}$ over $N$ points. The starting data is therefore a vector $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x(i)$ with $N$ components.

The **mean displacement** is defined in this case by:

$\overline{\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x}=\frac{\sum_{1}^{N}\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x(i)}{N}$

This mean value characterises the central value about which the displacement signal evolves. For the displacements, it will therefore make it possible to determine whether a centred configuration is observed (displacements with zero mean), or an off-centre one (non-zero mean).

The **variance of the displacement** is by definition:

$\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x)=\frac{\sum_{1}^{N}{(\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x(i)-\overline{\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x})}^{2}}{N}$

The **standard deviation of the displacement** is then:

$\sigma (\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x)=\sqrt{\mathrm{var}(\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x)}$

The standard deviation of a signal characterises its dispersion about its mean value. A small standard deviation will concern rather a signal with small variations of amplitude; a large standard deviation, larger variations.

For a centred variable, that is with zero mean, the standard deviation is equal to the RMS mean of the signal (Root Mean Square).

For an arbitrary variable, the **RMS mean** of the signal is defined by:

$\mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x)=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{1}^{N}\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x{(i)}^{2}}{N}}$

The **absolute minimum and maximum** of the signal are also an interesting and very simple piece of information to obtain, which determines the range of the signal.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.02/Object_8.svg)

**Figure 3.2-a: Example of a displacement signal and visualisation** **of the statistical quantities**

**A polar representation** of the set of signals $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x$ and $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}y$ is also interesting to analyse in the case of an obstacle of circular or similar geometry. Let us agree to call $R$ the radial displacement and $\theta$ the angular displacement, equivalents of $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x$ and $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}y$ in polar coordinates.

By definition one has:

$R(i)=\sqrt{\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x{(i)}^{2}+\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}y{(i)}^{2}}$

$\theta (i)=\mathrm{Arctg}(\frac{\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}y(i)}{\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x(i)})$

This representation makes it possible, among other things, to distinguish:

- orbital motions with permanent contact (mean radial displacement of the order of the clearance and small standard deviation of the radial displacement),
- pure-impact motions (large standard deviation of the radial displacement, small variation of the angular displacement),
- other configurations: orbital motion with impacts ...

**Note:**

*In the local frame chosen for the shock obstacles, the quantities called here* $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}x$ and $\mathrm{Depl}\text{\_}y$ *are in fact* $\mathrm{DYloc}$ *and* $\mathrm{DZloc}$ *, the axis* $\mathrm{Xloc}$ *having been chosen by convention perpendicular to the plane of the obstacle.*

**In summary**, the post-processing option **'USURE'** of the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T will determine, for the local displacements $\mathrm{DYloc},\mathrm{DYloc},\mathrm{DZloc}$ as well as for their polar decomposition $R$ and $\theta$ , the statistical quantities by blocks, with the principle stated above:

- mean value,
- RMS value,
- standard deviation,
- minimum value,
- maximum value.

### 3.3 Statistics for the shock forces

It is assumed that, as for the displacements, a discrete signal over $N$ points is available: $\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)$ . The signal obtained should be composed of time ranges where the shock force is zero (no contact) and of others where the shock force is significant (effective contact), which is the case in numerical analyses. In fact, for experimental signals, because of the dynamics of the measurement system, a noise level may be observed outside the shock period (cf. [Figure 3.3-a]). The statistical treatment must therefore be carried out only when the signal leaves the noise level. This requires the introduction of a detection threshold (**SEUIL_FORCE**) which, although superfluous in the numerical domain, has been reproduced in the post-processing of *Code_Aster*.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.02/Object_24.svg)

**Figure 3.3-a: Example of an experimental shock-force signal**

Let $S\max$ be the value determining the maximum level of the noise considered; one will then compute:

- The **number of time instants in shock**:

$\mathrm{Nchoc}=\mathrm{card}\left\lbrace i/\mid \mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)\mid >S\max\right\rbrace$

- The **mean shock force** over the **total time**:

$\overline{\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}}=\frac{1}{N}\cdot (\sum_{i/\mid \mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)\mid >S\max}^{N}\mid \mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)\mid )$

- The **mean shock force** reduced to the **shock time** is:

$\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}=\overline{\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}}\cdot \frac{N}{\mathrm{Nchoc}}$

- The **RMS mean of the shock force** over the **total time** is computed as follows:

$\mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc})={(\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i/\mid \mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)\mid >S\max}^{N}{\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}(i)}^{2})}^{1/2}$

- The **RMS mean** reduced to the **shock time** is:

$\mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc})=\mathrm{RMS}(\mathrm{Fx}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc})\cdot \frac{N}{\mathrm{Nchoc}}$

As for the displacement signals, one may also be interested in the **absolute maximum or minimum** of the force signal, thus determining its range. For the normal force, the minimum is always equal to zero, whereas the tangential force alternates.

**In summary**, the post-processing option 'USURE' of the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T will determine, for the normal and tangential shock forces, the statistical quantities by blocks, with the principle stated above:

- mean value computed over the shock time or the total time,
- RMS value computed over the shock time or the total time,
- maximum value of the signal.

### 3.4 Statistics for the shock times

The **percentage of shock time** is defined by:

$\text{\%}\mathrm{Tchoc}=\mathrm{Nchoc}/N$

If one looks at the information available on an experimental system, the shock-force signal is the most appropriate for determining the occurrence of a contact precisely. As mentioned above, one feels the need to introduce a maximum noise level, and to count the shock phases when the signal exceeds this threshold (SEUIL_FORCE).

In the figure below, one can distinguish a notion of **elementary shock**, determined as a successive passage above and then below the threshold, and a more general notion of **overall shock**, gathering several elementary shocks separated by short time instants of return below the threshold.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.02/Object_32.svg)

**Figure 3.4-a**

A characteristic rest time $\mathrm{Tr}$ (**DUREE_REPOS**) is therefore introduced; the end of an overall shock time occurs if the signal remains at rest for a time at least greater than $\mathrm{Tr}$ . This notion of characteristic rest time $\mathrm{Tr}$ is of course rather vague and will have to be determined by the user in the light of the transient results. It is nevertheless essential, because it alone makes it possible to gather a train of very closely spaced impacts constituting in fact a single contact phase.

The notion of elementary shock time being defined, the statistical treatment of the shock time will consist in determining the following information:

- **number of elementary shocks**: $\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{elem}$
- **number of overall shocks**: $\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{glob}$
- **number of elementary shocks per overall shock**: $\frac{\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{elem}}{\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{glob}}$
- **mean elementary shock time**:

${\bar{T}}_{\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{elem}}=\frac{\mathrm{Nchoc}\cdot \Deltat }{\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{elem}}$

- **mean overall shock time**

${\bar{T}}_{\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{glob}}=\frac{\mathrm{Nchoc}\cdot \Deltat }{\mathrm{Nb}\text{\_}\mathrm{choc}\text{\_}\mathrm{glob}}$

- **maximum overall shock time**, the largest overall shock time observed on the block analysed.

**In summary**, the post-processing option **'USURE'** of the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T will determine, for the shock times, the statistical quantities by blocks, with the principle stated above:

- mean value of the overall shock time,`
- maximum value of the overall shock time,
- mean value of the elementary shock time,
- the number of overall shocks per second,
- the mean number of elementary shocks per overall shock.

### 3.5 Wear power

The quantity generally computed in vibrations with shock and friction is the wear power defined by ARCHARD [bib1], which expresses the mean power developed by the friction forces during the motion. These forces are the driving force of wear by friction. The wear power in the case of discrete signals is computed as follows:

$\overline{{P}_{\mathrm{usure}}}=\frac{\sum_{i/\mid \mathrm{Fn}\mid >S\max}^{N}\mid \mathrm{Fn}(i)\cdot \mathrm{Vt}(i)\mid }{N}$

This power may, for example, be correlated with a wear or a removal of material by means of a wear coefficient ${K}_{T}$ , by a relation of the type: $V(T)={K}_{T}\ast {P}_{\mathrm{usure}}\ast T$ where $V(T)$ is the volume removed during the duration $T$ .

Other, more sophisticated wear laws may be used in another post-processing operator: POST_USURE, described in [R7.01.10](doc:r7.01.10).

### 3.6 Table data structure POST_DYNA associated with the option 'USURE'

A structure of type table for the option **USURE** of the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T gathers the results described previously.

This table contains the names of the statistical-result sub-tables associated with the various quantities analysed: displacements, shock forces, shock counting and wear power.

The access variables of this table are 10 in number:

- **for the displacement variables**: DEPL_X, DEPL_Y, DEPL_Z, DEPL_RADIAL, DEPL_ANGULAIRE, which correspond respectively to the displacements in local X, Y and Z and to their cylindrical decomposition in the plane of the obstacle.
- **for the shock-force variables**: FORCE_NORMALE, FORCE_TANG_1, FORCE_TANG_2, which correspond respectively to the forces normal and tangential to the obstacle, the first being in the plane of the obstacle, the second orthogonal to the plane of the obstacle.
- **for the shock-counting variables**: STAT_CHOC.
- **for the wear-power variable**: PUIS_USURE.

The sub-tables associated with the 10 quantities above contain a certain number of access variables for each shock connection:

- **for the displacement variables**: MOYEN, ECART_TYPE, RMS, MAXI, MINI, which correspond respectively to the mean value, the standard deviation, the RMS or effective value, and the maximum and minimum values of the corresponding displacement variable.
- **for the shock-force variables**: MOYEN_T_TOTAL, MOYEN_T_CHOC, RMS_T_TOTAL, RMS_T_CHOC, MAXI, which correspond respectively to the mean value over the total time, the mean over the shock time, the RMS or effective value averaged over the total time, the RMS or effective value over the shock time, and the maximum value of the corresponding force variable.
- **for the shock-counting variables**: NB_CHOC_S, NB_REBON_CHOC, T_CHOC_MOYEN, T_CHOC_MAXI, T_CHOC_MINI, T_REBON_MOYEN, %_T_CHOC, which correspond respectively to the values of the number of shocks per second, of the number of rebounds per shock, of the mean shock time, the maximum shock time, the minimum shock time, the mean rebound time and the percentage of shock time.
- **for the wear-power variable**: PUIS_USURE, which corresponds to the wear power computed according to ARCHARD.

## 4 Modal transient post-processing — option 'IMPACT'

### 4.1 Current practice for post-processing core analyses

Before the development of the post-processing in *Code_Aster*, SEPTEN used, for its design-verification needs, the code CLASH [bib2] developed by BELGONUCLEAIRE. This software computes the seismic response of a row of assemblies. This code supplies a set of detailed information for each shock point and for each impact.

Each result consists of a table per shock point, an example of which is in Annex 1. This table contains the following information:

- the time instant of the impact peak,
- the maximum impact force reached,
- the impulse exchanged, defined as the integral of the shock force over time,
- the total duration of the shock,
- the relative velocity before impact.

These elements are particularly interesting for SEPTEN because, in addition to the very limited contractual information, they make it possible to know the number and the composition of the impacts, as well as essential physical quantities associated with them. The relative velocity before impact and the impulse are, for example, very valuable information in the specification of experimental tests of dynamic buckling of the assembly grids.

### 4.2 Computations for the post-processing of the impacts

It is considered, as for the previous post-processing, that the impact conditions are determined as previously by the exceeding of a force threshold $\mathrm{Smax}$ , and the overall shock and the elementary shock are distinguished in the same way by the notion of rest time.

The computation carries out a loop over all the shock non-linearities and an identical treatment for each of them.

Then, for each overall shock identified, the following quantities will be determined:

- Shock start time: ${T}_{\mathrm{début}}\text{tel que}{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}({T}_{\mathrm{début}})>{S}_{\max}$
- End time of the overall shock:

${T}_{\mathrm{fin}}\text{tel que}{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}({T}_{\mathrm{fin}})\le {S}_{\max},{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}({T}_{\mathrm{fin}}-\Deltat )\ge {S}_{\max}$

$\text{et}\forall t\in [{T}_{\mathrm{fin}},{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}+{T}_{\mathrm{repos}}]{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}(t)\le {S}_{\max}$

$\text{où}\Deltat \text{est le pas de temps d'intégration}$

- Total duration of the shock: ${T}_{\mathrm{choc}}={T}_{\mathrm{fin}}-{T}_{\mathrm{début}}$
- Maximum force during the shock: ${F}_{\max}={\max}_{T\in [{T}_{\mathrm{début}},{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}]}({F}_{\mathrm{choc}}(t))$
- The time instant of the maximum of the shock force,
- The impulse exchanged during the shock: $I=\underset{t={T}_{\mathrm{début}}}{\overset{{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}}{\int}}{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}(t)\cdot \mathrm{dt}$
- The relative normal velocity before impact: ${V}_{\mathrm{choc}}=V({T}_{\mathrm{début}}-\Deltat )$
- The number of elementary impacts accumulated in the overall shock:

${N}_{\mathrm{impacts}\mathrm{élémentaires}}=\mathrm{card}\left\lbrace t\in [{t}_{\mathrm{début}},{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}]/{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}(t)>{S}_{\max}\text{et}{F}_{\mathrm{choc}}(t+\deltat )\le {S}_{\max}\right\rbrace$

In order to synthesise the information, one will in addition determine:

- the absolute maximum of the shock force, on a given shock connection, for the duration of the analysis,

The maximum shock force, in order to be determined more precisely, will not be obtained as the maximum in time over the set of shocks for each shock node (in order to avoid the bias of the archiving precision), but determined in the transient analysis over all the computation steps and archived in the result concept **tran_gene**. It is this information that will be used.

- the mean value of the extrema of the shock force, as well as their standard deviation.
- a histogram of the probability density of the maximum impact forces.

This histogram will be relatively summary and will give, for ${N}_{C}$ classes, the probability density of the maximum shock force.

The classes will be defined as follows:

${\mathrm{classe}}_{i={\mathrm{1..N}}_{C}}=\left\lbrace {F}_{\max}/\frac{i-1}{{N}_{C}}{F}_{\max}^{\mathrm{absolu}}\le {F}_{\max}\le \frac{i}{{N}_{C}}{F}_{\max}^{\mathrm{absolu}}\right\rbrace$

### 4.3 Table data structure POST_IMPACT associated with the option 'IMPACT'

#### 4.3.1 Table POST_IMPACT

A data structure of type table for the option **IMPACT** of the operator POST_DYNA_MODA_T of *Code_Aster* is produced.

The result structure will be a table indexed by the names of the shock connections, of type **POST_IMPACT**, containing the names of the tables that it contains.

The content of each cell of this table is a table name stored as CHARACTER*24. Three types of table are contained: a table called IMPACT, a table called GLOBAL and a table called PROBA.

It therefore has 3 parameters: **IMPACT**, **GLOBAL** and **PROBA**. The access variable corresponds to the name of the shock connection considered.

#### 4.3.2 Table IMPACT

The IMPACT table is of type **TABL_IMPACT** and has 6 access parameters: **INST**, **F_MAX**, **T_CHOC**, **IMPULS**, **V_IMPACT**, **NB_IMPACT**.

The content of each cell of this table is a **REAL*8**.

#### 4.3.3 Table GLOBAL

The GLOBAL table is of type **TABL_FMAX** and has 3 access parameters:

- **F_MAX_ABS**, which gives access to the absolute maximum of the shock force over all the shocks observed,
- **F_MAX_MOY**, which gives access to the mean value of the maxima of the shock force observed,
- **F_MAX_ETYP**, which gives access to the standard deviation of the extrema of the shock forces.

The content of each cell of this table is a **REAL*8**.

#### 4.3.4 Table PROBA

The PROBA table is of type **TABL_HISTO** and has 3 access parameters:

- **DEBUT**, which gives access to the minimum force value of class $i$ ,
- **FIN**, which gives access to the maximum force value of class $i$ ,
- **PROBA**, which gives access to the probability density of the maximum-force variable for class $i$ .

The content of each cell of this table is a REAL*8.

## 5 Conclusion

This document has presented the post-processing methods applicable to transients with shock computed by modal synthesis on structures with clearance. Depending on the concerns, a post-processing oriented towards a diagnosis of the wear undergone by the components during the shocks may be carried out; a set of important statistical quantities is then determined. If the concern is rather the impacts and their level, another option allows a detailed analysis of each impact.

These two features make it possible to synthesise the transient results obtained by time integration, to classify different numerical simulations by level of severity, or to compare, for validation purposes, computed and measured quantities.
