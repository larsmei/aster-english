# v6.01.103 SSNA103 - Calibration of Weibull Model Parameters

**Summary:**

This test validates the RECA_WEIBULL command allowing the identification of $m$ and ${\sigma}_{u}$ parameters of the Weibull model.

The identification is made using a database consisting of 45 tests, all of which are performed on smooth cylindrical test pieces at three different temperatures, $-150°C$ , $-100°C$ and $–50°C$ . This database is obtained by random drawing of a sample representative of the statistical law of Weibull corresponding to values of $m$ and ${\sigma}_{u}$ arbitrarily fixed.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

No calculation is required to obtain the reference solution. The $m$ and ${\sigma}_{u}$ (M and SIGM_REFE in WEIBULL option of DEFI_MATERIAU) values that are being identified are known and allow the database to be generated. Thus, break elongations are determined as follows:

For each $m$ and ${\sigma}_{u}$ torque associated with a d-test temperature, a sample of 15 stress values of Weibull at rupture was determined by random drawing, taking into account the following statistical law:

${P}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})=1-\exp[-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{w}}{{\sigma}_{u}})}^{m}]$

The Weibull constraint is defined by:

${\sigma}_{w}=\sqrt[m]{\sum_{i}{({\sigma}_{I}^{i})}^{m}\frac{{V}_{i}}{{V}_{0}}}$

The summation covers the volumes of plasticized ${V}_{i}$ material, ${\sigma}_{I}^{i}$ designating the maximum main stress in each of these volumes (the volume ${V}_{0}$ (VOLU_REFE in WEIBULL option) is equal to $(50\mu {m}^{3})$).

In the case of single tensile stress with the hypothesis of small deformations, the stress of Weibull, ${\sigma}_{W}$, is expressed as a function of $(l-{l}_{0})/{l}_{0}$ break elongation, depending on:

${\sigma}_{w}=[{E}_{t}(\frac{l-{l}_{0}}{{l}_{0}})+(1-\frac{{E}_{t}}{E}){\sigma}_{Y}]\sqrt[m]{\frac{V}{{V}_{0}}}$

The values of the break elongations carried forward in the table of the v6.01.103-conditions-limits-loads are therefore derived from this expression and from the previous random drawing.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The uncertainty of the solution cannot be determined precisely, but it can be quite high. Indeed, the reference values can only be found if we consider experimental populations composed of an infinite number of samples.

### 3.3 Remarks

Using the maximum likelihood method, although the difference between the values of ($m$ , ${\sigma}_{u}$ ) obtained with RECA_WEIBULL and their reference values remains significant, it is consistent with the result sought given the relatively small number of samples used for the re-scaling (15 per temperature). To obtain the reference values, the number of samples per temperature ($N>1000$) would have to be significantly increased. However, the difference found remains reasonable (about 10%). Furthermore, the growth of ${\sigma}_{u}$ according to temperature is respected.

With the linear regression method, the difference is more important.

## 4 Summary of the results

The results obtained show that the automatic setting procedure for the parameters of the Weibull models works and gives results consistent with the expected theoretical results.
