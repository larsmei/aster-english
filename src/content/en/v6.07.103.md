# v6.07.103 COMP003 – Test of concrete-specific behaviors. Simulation in one point hardware

**Summary:**

This test involves a simulation of a loading path in stress or deformation in a material point, i.e. on a model such that the stress and deformation states are homogeneous at any time. It allows testing a number of concrete-specific behaviour models, with the aim of verifying the robustness of their digital integration, their insensitivity to a change in units, the correct consideration of the control variables on which the coefficients of the model depend, l-invariance with an overall rotation applied to the problem.

Modelling A: This Modelling allows to validate the BETON_RAG model in $\mathrm{3D}$.

Modelling B: This Modelling allows to validate the BETON_UMLV model in $\mathrm{3D}$ .

Modelling C: This Modelling allows to validate the BETON_BURGER model in $\mathrm{3D}$ .

Modelling D: This Modelling allows to validate the ENDO_LOCA_EXP model in $\mathrm{3D}$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 Characteristics of the loading journey

The proposed load makes each component of the tensor of the deformations vary in a decoupled manner by successive steps. A cyclic load-discharge path is proposed covering the tensile and compression states and an inversion of the shear signs to test a wide range of values.

Schematically, it follows a course on 8 segments $[O-A-B-C-O-C’-B’-A’-O]$ where the second part of the $[O-C’-B’-A’-O]$ route is symmetrical with the origin of the first $[O-A-B-C-O]$.

#### 1.3.2 Application of solicitations

The study of a material point (using SIMU_POINT_MAT [U4.51.12](doc:u4.51.12) macro-command) is re-examined by applying for a element in a homogeneous way by imposing in $\mathrm{3D}$ , the 6 components of the strain tensor:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]$

For a more general writing, the strain tensor imposed will be decomposed into a hydrostatic and deviatoric part on shear bases:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]=p\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]+{d}_{1}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& -1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]+{d}_{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& -1\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& 0& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& 0\end{array}\right]$ en $\mathrm{3D}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

## 2 reference solution

This test performs, for each model, an intercomparison between the reference solution (obtained with a fine time step), the solution with medium coarse discretization, the solution with temperature effect (or another control variable), the solution by changing the unit system ($\mathit{Pa}$ to $\mathit{MPa}$), and the solution obtained after rotation.

### 2.1 Definition of robustness test cases

Two analytical angles are proposed to test the robustness of constitutive laws integration:

- equivalent problem studies
- study of time step discretization

For each of these, the evolution of relative differences is studied between several calculations using the same law but with different parameters or calculation options. The exploitation is concerned with the invariants of the stress tensor: tensor trace, Von-Mises stress and internal variables of a scalar nature.

The overall convergence criteria are the default values for Code_Aster. (RESI_GLOB_RELA=10-6, ITER_GLOB_MAXI=10) A common Newton schema for the re-update of the tangent matrix was adopted:

- calculation of the prediction tangent matrix at each converged increment (REAC_INC=1)
- calculation of the coherent tangent matrix at each Newton iteration (REAC_ITER=1).

### 2.2 Equivalent problem studies

For rough discretization of the journeys: 1 time step for each segment of the journey, the solution obtained for each law is compared to 2 strictly equivalent problems for the material point state:

- $\mathrm{Tpa}$ , same route with a unit change, substitutes $\mathrm{Pa}$ for $\mathit{MPa}$ in material data and possible law parameters,
- $\mathrm{Trot}$ , route by imposing the same $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after rotation: ${t}^{}R\cdot \stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}\cdot R$ where $R$ is a rotation matrix, corresponding to a rotation of 30 degrees around the $\mathit{Oz}$ axis.

For each of these problems, the solution (invariants in the stresses, scalar internal variable) must be identical to the basic solution, obtained with the same discretization in time. The reference value of the difference is therefore 0. This means in practice that the difference found must be of the order of machine precision is about $1.E-15$.

### 2.3 Study of time step discretization

The behaviour of law integration in relation to discretization is studied. For the same Modelling, therefore a given behavior, two discretizations are studied at different times, multiplying by 5 the number of steps of the loading path. This leads to the following discretization:

| **Calculated** | ${T}_{1}$ | ${T}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ **reference solution** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Number of intervals per loading segment | 5 | 25 |
| Total number of steps on the entire journey | 40 | 200 |

The reference solution, ${T}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ , is the $N=25$ , 200 steps for the entire journey. These solutions allow you to judge the sensitivity to the large time step and the robustness of integration.

The maximum differences values between the two solutions for the entire loading journey are reported in §3.3.

## 7 Executive summary

For BETON_RAG behaviour, the results are satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathrm{Pa}$ and $\mathit{MPa}$ )
- Following rotation, the results are identical.

For BETON_UMLV_FP behavior, the results are very satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathit{Pa}$ to $\mathit{MPa}$)
- Following rotation, the results are identical
- The results converge with the time step, and the integration schemes allow the use of large time step.

For BETON_BURGER_FP behavior, the results are very satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathit{MPa}$)
- Following rotation, the results are identical
- The results converge with the time step, and the integration schemes allow the use of large time step.

For ENDO_LOCA_EXP behavior, the results are very satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathit{Pa}$ to $\mathit{MPa}$)
- Following rotation, the results remain the same
- results converge with the time step and integration schemes allow the use of large time step.
