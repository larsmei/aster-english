# r5.06.03 Modelling of impacts and of friction in transient analysis by modal recombination

**Summary:**

This document describes the physical laws of contact with friction between structures and the modelling of them in the algorithm for transient analysis by modal recombination DYNA_VIBRA (TYPE_CALCUL='TRAN' and BASE_CALCUL='GENE') with COMPORTEMENT='DIS_CHOC'. For the various non-linear contact connections that can be used, the computation of the quantities defining the contact conditions is detailed.

The usage schemes employed are described in [R5.06.04](doc:r5.06.04).

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The problems of impact with friction of interest to EDF concern, for example, the modelling of the vibrations of tubular structures held by supports with gaps, or separated by small gaps and thus able to come into contact. The tubes of the steam generators, the rods of the control-rod clusters and the fuel assemblies are examples of structures whose vibrations one wishes to model.

The major consequence of vibrations in the presence of a gap is to cause impacts as well as friction between the structure and its supports, or between the structures, whence risks of wear. This document describes the type of non-linearities introduced by the presence of these gaps, as well as the modelling used to take them into account in the modal-recombination algorithm.

## 2 Contact relations between two structures

Two relations govern the contact between two structures:

- The unilateral contact relation, which expresses the non-interpenetrability of solid bodies,
- the friction relation, which governs the variation of the tangential forces in the contact. For the present developments one will retain a simple relation: Coulomb's friction law.

### 2.1 Unilateral contact relation

Let two structures ${\Omega}_{1}$ and ${\Omega}_{2}$ . One denotes by ${d}_{N}^{1/2}$ the normal distance between the structures and by ${F}_{N}^{1/2}$ the normal reaction force of ${\Omega}_{1}$ on ${\Omega}_{2}$ (see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/100009340000175C000012805317E9CFA5F97175.svg)

Figure 2-1: Normal distance and normal reaction

The law of action and reaction imposes:

$$
{F}_{N}^{1/2}=-{F}_{N}^{2/1}
$$

The unilateral contact conditions, also called Signorini conditions (see [1]), are expressed as follows:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{d}_{N}^{1/2}\ge 0\\ {F}_{N}^{1/2}\ge 0\\ {d}_{N}^{1/2}{F}_{N}^{1/2}=0\end{array}
$$

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/100003FE00001AC50000159A8FEBFA504DAA0327.svg)

Figure 2-2: Graph of the unilateral contact relation

The graphical representation of the unilateral contact law in the figure expresses a force-displacement relation which is not differentiable. It is therefore not usable in a simple way in a dynamic analysis algorithm.

If one restricts the study to the case of a tubular structure in the presence of a non-deformable support, one denotes by ${d}_{n}\left({d}_{n}={d}_{N}^{1/2}\right)$ the normal distance to the support, and by ${F}_{n}$ the reaction of the latter ( ${F}_{n}={F}_{N}^{2/1}=-{F}_{N}^{1/2}$ see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10002ED800002DAF00000F816C0D28EB8C40FB75.svg)

Figure 2-3: Normal distance and normal reaction between a structure and a support

The expression of the normal contact conditions, expressing the limitation of the displacements due to the support, is:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{d}_{n}\ge 0\\ {F}_{n}\ge 0\\ {d}_{n}{F}_{n}=0\end{array}
$$

### 2.2 Coulomb's friction law

Coulomb's law expresses a limitation of the tangential force ${F}_{T}^{1/2}$ of tangential reaction of ${O}_{1}$ on ${O}_{2}$ . Let ${\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}$ be the relative velocity of ${\Omega}_{1}$ with respect to ${O}_{2}$ at a contact point and let $\mu$ be the Coulomb friction coefficient; one has (see [1]):

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}s=\Vert {F}_{T}^{1/2}\Vert -\mu {F}_{N}^{1/2}\le 0\\ \exists \lambda \text{tel que}{\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}=\lambda {F}_{T}^{1/2}\\ \lambda \le 0\\ \lambda .s=0\end{array}
$$

and the law of action and reaction:

$$
{F}_{T}^{1/2}=-{F}_{T}^{2/1}
$$

The graphical representation of Coulomb's law in the figure likewise expresses the non-differentiable character of the law and is therefore not simple to use in a dynamic algorithm.

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10000000000001C1000001647CD67B3488EB0697.png)

Figure 2-4: Graph of Coulomb's friction law

If one restricts the study to the case of a tubular structure in the presence of a non-deformable support, only the tangential force ${F}_{T}^{2/1}={F}_{T}$ is used; the friction law is expressed as follows:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}s=\Vert {F}_{T}\Vert -\mu {F}_{n}\le 0\\ \exists \lambda \text{tel que}{\dot{u}}_{T}=\lambda {F}_{T}\\ \lambda \le 0\\ \lambda s=0\end{array}
$$

A common extension of Coulomb's law, drawn from experience, consists in having two friction coefficients: one for adherence, denoted ${\mu}_{s}$ , the other for sliding, denoted ${\mu}_{d}$ , with ${\mu}_{s}>{\mu}_{d}$ . One then has, in the adherence phase, $\Vert {F}_{T}\Vert \le {\mu}_{s}{F}_{n}$ and, in the sliding phase, $\Vert {F}_{T}\Vert ={\mu}_{d}{F}_{n}$ .

## 3 Modelling of contact/friction by penalisation

### 3.1 Model of the normal contact force

The principle of penalisation applied to the graph of the figure consists in introducing a single-valued relation ${F}_{N}^{1/2}={f}_{{\varepsilon}}\left({d}_{N}^{1/2}\right)$ by means of a parameter $\varepsilon$ . The graph of ${f}_{\varepsilon}$ must tend towards the Signorini graph when $\varepsilon$ tends towards zero (see [2]). One possibility consists in proposing a linear relation between ${d}_{N}^{1/2}$ and ${F}_{N}^{1/2}$ :

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{N}^{1/2}=-\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}{d}_{N}^{1/2}\text{si}{d}_{N}^{1/2}\le 0\\ {F}_{N}^{1/2}=0\text{sinon}\end{array}
$$

If one denotes ${K}_{N}=\frac{1}{{\varepsilon}}$ (commonly called the **impact stiffness**) one recovers the classical relation, modelling an elastic impact:

$$
{F}_{N}^{1/2}=-{K}_{N}{d}_{N}^{1/2}
$$

The approximate graph of the contact law with penalisation is in the figure .

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/100003DE00001AC50000159A9623BDABCE16CAF0.svg)

Figure 3-1: Graph of the unilateral contact relation approximated by penalisation

In order to take into account a possible loss of energy in the impact, one introduces an impact damping ${C}_{N}$ . The expression for the normal contact force is then written:

$$
{F}_{N}^{1/2}=-{K}_{N}{d}_{N}^{1/2}-{C}_{N}{\dot{u}}_{N}^{1/2}
$$

Where ${\dot{u}}_{N}^{1/2}$ is the relative normal velocity of ${O}_{1}$ with respect to ${O}_{2}$ . In order to respect the Signorini relation (no adherence in contact), one must on the other hand check a posteriori that ${F}_{N}^{1/2}$ is positive or zero. One will therefore take only the positive part ${⟨\cdot ⟩}^{+}$ of the expression ():

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{⟨x⟩}^{+}=x\text{si}x\ge 0\\ {⟨x⟩}^{+}=0\text{si}x<0\end{array}
$$

The complete relation giving the normal contact force which is retained for the algorithm is the following:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\text{Si}{d}_{N}^{1/2}\le 0\text{alors}{F}_{N}^{1/2}={⟨-{K}_{N}{d}_{N}^{1/2}-{C}_{N}{\dot{u}}_{N}^{1/2}⟩}^{+}\text{et}{F}_{N}^{1/2}=-{F}_{N}^{2/1}\\ \text{Sinon}{F}_{N}^{1/2}={F}_{N}^{2/1}=0\end{array}
$$

### 3.2 Model of the tangential contact force

The graph describing the tangential force with Coulomb's law is non-differentiable for the adherence phase ${\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}=0$ . One therefore introduces a single-valued relation linking the relative tangential displacement ${d}_{T}^{1/2}$ and the tangential force ${F}_{T}^{1/2}={f}_{\xi}\left({d}_{T}^{1/2}\right)$ by means of a parameter $\xi$ . The graph of ${f}_{\xi}$ must tend towards the Coulomb graph when $\xi$ tends towards zero (see [2]). One possibility consists in writing a linear relation between ${d}_{T}^{1/2}$ and ${F}_{T}^{1/2}$ for an incremental writing:

$$
{F}_{T}^{1/2}-{F}_{T,0}^{1/2}=-\frac{1}{\xi}\left({d}_{T}^{1/2}-{d}_{T,0}^{1/2}\right)
$$

With ${\left(\cdot \right)}_{T,0}$ the quantities at the previous time step. If one introduces a tangential stiffness ${K}_{T}=\frac{1}{\xi}$ , one obtains the relation:

$$
{F}_{T}^{1/2}={F}_{T,0}^{1/2}-{K}_{T}\left({d}_{T}^{1/2}-{d}_{T,0}^{1/2}\right)
$$

The approximate graph of Coulomb's friction law modelled by penalisation is in the figure . For numerical reasons, linked to the dissipation of parasitic vibrations (see [3]) in the adherence phase, one is led to add a tangential damping ${C}_{T}$ in the expression for the tangential force. Its final expression is:

$$
{F}_{T}^{1/2}={F}_{T,0}^{1/2}-{K}_{T}\left({d}_{T}^{1/2}-{d}_{T,0}^{1/2}\right)-{C}_{T}{\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}
$$

This force must moreover satisfy the Coulomb criterion, that is:

$$
\Vert {F}_{T}^{1/2}\Vert \le \mu {F}_{N}^{1/2}
$$

If this is not the case, one corrects the friction force by the following formula:

$$
{F}_{T}^{1/2}=-\mu {F}_{N}^{1/2}\frac{{\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}}{\Vert {\dot{u}}_{T}^{1/2}\Vert }
$$

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10000000000001EC0000018288E62FC50E8DE2E5.png)

Figure 3-2: Graph of the friction law approximated by penalisation

In the case of the extension of Coulomb's law with the distinction between the adherence coefficient ${\mu}_{s}$ and the sliding coefficient ${\mu}_{d}$ , the approximate graph of the law is modified (see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10000000000001EC00000182A44AC0925169196C.png)

Figure 3-3: Graph of the variant of the friction law approximated by penalisation

## 4 Types of contact connection modelled

The developments presented here concern the implementation of non-linear connections with unilateral contact and friction between a node and an obstacle, or between two given nodes.

The nodes in contact are assumed to belong to two slender structures of beam type, or to a beam and a non-deformable obstacle. The nodes on which the contact condition will bear are assumed to lie on the centre-line of the beams.

### 4.1 Connections between a node and a non-deformable obstacle

#### 4.1.1 Node-on-plane-obstacle contact connections

One considers a slender structure represented by elements of beam type. Its displacement is limited at a point by the presence of an obstacle constituted of two infinite half-planes in the direction $Y$ (see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/100007040000153000000DF40A990AD041BBB253.svg)

Figure 4-1: Slender structures with node-on-plane contact

In order to analyse the contact conditions, one places oneself in the frame perpendicular to the axis $\mathrm{Xloc}$ , the direction of the neutral fibre or of a generatrix of the beam. Let $\mathrm{NO1}$ be the node of the connection considered on the beam; the geometry of the node-on-plane contact connection (called PLAN_Y) is described in the figure ().

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/1000078C00001A9000000E431098779BB9DD9366.svg)

Figure 4-2: Geometry of the node-on-plane-obstacle connection

Let $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{Y}_{\mathit{loc}}\\ {Z}_{\mathit{loc}}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ be the coordinates of the node $\mathrm{NO1}$ in the frame $\left({Y}_{\mathit{loc}},{Z}_{\mathit{loc}}\right)$ ; the origin of this frame is the point ORIG_OBST. The normal distance ${d}_{N}$ in this case, neglecting the rotations of the sections, is then expressed by:

| :math:{d}_{N}=- | {Y}_{mathit{loc}} | +mathit{jeu}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

The contact in this connection is supposed to take place whatever the offset in ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$ between the two structures.

The normal vector $n$ in the frame $\left({Y}_{\mathit{loc}},{Z}_{\mathit{loc}}\right)$ has components:

#### 4.1.2 Node-on-concave-circular-obstacle contact connections

One considers a slender structure, represented by elements of beam type. Its displacement is limited at a point by the presence of an obstacle constituted of an infinite plane pierced by a circular hole (see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/100006020000154A00000DF47CE3A7E0879F1B17.svg)

Figure 4-3: Slender structures with node-on-circular-obstacle contact

In order to analyse the contact conditions, one places oneself in the frame perpendicular to the axis ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ , the direction of the neutral fibre or of a generatrix of the beam. Let $\mathrm{NO1}$ be the node of the connection considered; the geometry of the node-on-circle contact connection (called CERCLE) is described in the figure ().

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/1000056A00001A4000000C9CA691845FF103F232.svg)

Figure 4-4: Geometry of the node-on-circular-obstacle connection

Let $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{Y}_{\mathit{loc}}\\ {Z}_{\mathit{loc}}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ be the coordinates of the node $\mathrm{NO1}$ in the frame $\left({Y}_{\mathit{loc}},{Z}_{\mathit{loc}}\right)$ ; the origin of this frame is the point ORIG_OBST. The normal distance ${d}_{N}$ in this case, neglecting the rotations of the sections, is then expressed by:

$$

## 5 Use of the localised non-linear impact and friction forces in modal recombination

The non-linear forces expressed above are explicit functions of the position and of the velocity of the nodes on which the contact conditions bear. One chooses to use the pseudo-force technique in order to solve the projected dynamic problem. If the direct dynamic system is written:

$$
M{\ddot{X}}_{t}+C{\dot{X}}_{t}+K{X}_{t}={F}_{\mathit{ext}}(t)+{F}_{\mathit{choc}}({X}_{t},{\dot{X}}_{t})
$$

The pseudo-force technique consists in projecting onto the basis of the linear system and in keeping the non-linear forces on the right-hand side. The projected dynamic system takes the form:

$$
{\Phi}^{t}M\Phi {\ddot{\eta}}_{t}+{\Phi}^{t}C\Phi {\dot{\eta}}_{t}+{\Phi}^{t}K\Phi {\eta}_{t}={\Phi}^{t}{\Phi}_{\mathit{ext}}(t)+{\Phi}^{t}{\Phi}_{\mathit{choc}}(\Phi {\eta}_{t},\Phi {\dot{\eta}}_{t})
$$

The projected problem is integrated numerically by an explicit scheme. Recommendations are given in [U2.06.04](doc:u2.06.04) for the choice of this basis.

## 6 Details on the use of the impact non-linearities with friction

The impact non-linearities between a structure and an obstacle, or between two structures, have been introduced into the modal-recombination algorithms of *Code_Aster*  : a first-order Euler algorithm and a fourth-order Devogelaere algorithm (see [R5.06.04](doc:r5.06.04)).

These algorithms are used by the operator DYNA_VIBRA. The type of impact connection between the two nodes is specified by a specific command: DEFI_OBSTACLE.

### 6.1 Definition of the type of impact connection

The type of impact connection is a generic notion, which contains no physical information such as a distance or any dimension. The type of connection simply specifies the geometric shape of the connection considered.

The types of two-node connection with impact accepted by the command DEFI_OBSTACLE are described by the following keywords: PLAN_Y, PLAN_Z, CERCLE, BI_PLAN_Y, BI_PLAN_Z or BI_CERCLE (see figure ).

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10001B8A000069D5000047BAF1FB66F7177F4EC1.svg)

Figure 6-1: Geometries of the impact connections

The prefix specifies that it is a two-node connection.

### 6.2 Definition of the local frame for the contact conditions

The structures treated, being regarded as slender cylinders (circular or rectangular cross-section), are modelled by beam elements. The contact is treated in a plane perpendicular to the direction ${X}_{\text{loc}}$ of the generatrix of the cylinders.

In order to define this change of frame completely, one introduces a local frame $({X}_{\text{loc}},{Y}_{\text{loc}},{Z}_{\text{loc}})$ . The vector ${X}_{\text{loc}}$ is the three-component vector supplied after the keyword NORM_OBST.

By means of the first two nautical angles, one passes in a unique way from the global frame $(X,Y,Z)$ to a frame having ${X}_{\text{loc}}$ as its first basis vector (see figure ). A third rotation, whose angle is supplied after the keyword ANGL_VRIL, gives a unique correspondence between the principal frame and the local frame.

**Warning:**

*the orientation of this local frame is important, because it is in this frame that the contact conditions are analysed, and that the local positions of the impact nodes are supplied.*

![Figure](/figures/r5.06.03/10000E7400002D7A000011773D7D33DB9D86BEC1.svg)

Figure 6-2: Rotations defining the local frame

**Figure 6.2-a: Rotations defining the local frame**

The operand ORIG_OBST makes it possible to define the origin of the local frame $(\text{Orig},{X}_{\text{loc}},{Y}_{\text{loc}},{Z}_{\text{loc}})$ *.*  This operand is optional and in principle will not be used in the case of impacts between two nodes. The code then considers that the origin is situated at the middle of the segment joining the two nodes.

### 6.3 Definition of the nodes of the connections

One specifies, after the keywords NOEU_1 and NOEU_2, the names of the two nodes of the structures on which the impact conditions will bear. If it is a connection between a node and an obstacle, only NOEU_1 is supplied.

### 6.4 Definition of the characteristic dimensions of the cross-sections

The operand JEU is used for the contact conditions between a node and an obstacle.

The operands DIST_1 and DIST_2 make it possible to specify the characteristic dimensions of the cross-sections of the structures surrounding the impact nodes. In the case of plane-on-plane connections, these are the thicknesses of material surrounding the impact node in the direction considered.

In the case of circle-on-circle connections, these are the radii of the cross-sections surrounding the impact nodes.

### 6.5 Definition of the contact parameters

The impact stiffness and damping parameters were introduced in § 3.1  and § 3.2 ; one specifies here the keywords making it possible to define them for a given connection:

- The operand RIGI_NOR is mandatory; it makes it possible to give the value of the normal impact stiffness ${K}_{N}$ .
- The operand AMOR_NOR is optional; it makes it possible to give the value of the normal impact damping ${C}_{N}$
- The operand RIGI_TAN is optional; it makes it possible to give the value of the tangential stiffness ${K}_{T}$ ;
- The operand AMOR_TAN is optional; it makes it possible to give the value of the tangential impact damping ${C}_{T}$ ;
- The operand COULOMB makes it possible to give the value of the Coulomb coefficient.

**Note:**

*If a stiffness* ${K}_{T}$ *is defined and the keyword* *AMOR_TAN is absent, the code computes an optimised damping so as to minimise the residual oscillations in adherence* *(see*  [3] *):*

${C}_{T}=2\cdot \sqrt{({k}_{i}+{K}_{T}).{m}_{i}}-2\cdot {x}_{i}\cdot \sqrt{{k}_{i}.{m}_{i}}$ *,*

*where* $i$ *is the index of the mode predominant* in the tangential direction *in the response of the structure (* the largest *modal mass* normalised by the tangential displacement *).*

### 6.6 Use of a unidirectional impact

For obstacles of type PLAN_Y, PLAN_Z, BI_PLAN_Y or BI_PLAN_Z it is possible to activate the friction in a single direction of the plane of the obstacle. The operand UNIDIRECTIONNEL makes it possible to activate this unidirectional friction in order to cancel the friction in the direction given by the operand NORM_OBST. This option can be used both with friction of type COULOMB and with friction of type COULOMB_STAT_DYNA.

With a friction COULOMB, the friction coefficient is zero along the axis supplied in NORM_OBST and mu in the perpendicular direction.

With a friction COULOMB_STAT_DYNA, the adherence coefficient is zero along the axis supplied in NORM_OBST and mus in the perpendicular direction. The sliding coefficient is zero along the axis supplied in NORM_OBST and mud in the perpendicular direction.
