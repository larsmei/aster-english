# v2.04.111 SDLV111 - Homogenization of a beam network in an incompressible fluid

**Summary:**

Modal analysis test, used to validate the elements of 3D_FASCEAU Modelling: 8 nodes hexahedron or 20 nodes hexahedron. These elements represent the homogenized medium of a beam network bathing in an incompressible fluid, initially at rest.

The eigenfrequencies of homogenized beams are tested without or with fluid.

The mass, centre of gravity position and inertia in this point are calculated for both the fluid and the non-fluid.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

**No fluid:**

Consider the heterogeneous domain described in [§1] in the absence of the fluid. It is assumed that the beams respect the assumptions of Modelling of a Bernoulli straight beam d'Euler. Since the boundary conditions applied to all beams are the same as for each one of them, the search for the frequencies of the whole to that of a single beam can be reduced.

The following problem is therefore being studied:

A bi-clamped beam [fig 2.1-a] of the same geometric and material characteristics as the beams of the heterogeneous medium. $A$ is noted the area of the section, $L$ its length, and $I$ the moment of inertia of bending.

By the dynamic rigidity method it is shown that such a beam allows double frequencies of the shape:

${f}_{i}=\frac{{\lambda}_{i}^{2}}{2\pi {L}^{2}}{\left[\frac{\mathit{EI}}{\rho A}\right]}^{\frac{1}{2}}$

${\lambda}_{i}=(2i+1)\pi /2i=1,2,\mathrm{...}$ for the second case of boundary conditions: [fig 2.1-a].

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.111/1000055C00000D20000014420B0ABC794F5D4296.svg)

**Figure 2.1-a**

The domain contains $N$ beams independent between them (no boundary conditions that pair the movements of two different beams), so the multiplicity of frequencies is equal to $\mathrm{2N}$ (2 modes of bending per beam).

For the homogenized medium discretized by the elements 8 nodes hexahedron or 20 nodes hexahedron finished $N$, the number $N$ shall be replaced by the number of parallel beams axis lines.

**Cas with fluid:**

The case with fluid is more difficult to resolve analytically: no analytical results were found until the writing of this test case. The reference results that we have established are therefore derived from a numerical resolution by elements of the complete heterogeneous problem. For this reason, version 3.6.2 of *Code_Aster* was used.

Each beam is represented in the mesh by its medium fiber modeled by POU_D_E (straight beam d-Euler). For all beams, each node of the medium fiber is linked to the nodes of the lateral surface, located in the same transverse section as the node in question, by LIAISON_SOLIDE. The fluid beam interface is modelled by FLUI_STRU which reflects the continuity of normal speeds at the walls. The fluid, was to be perfect incompressible, its Modelling was deduced from that of the perfect compressible fluid 3D_FLUIDE by removing the contribution of pressure.

The boundary conditions imposed on the domains [§1.3], and especially the relationship that links the movement of all beams to the $\mathrm{Sh}$ level, reveal two kinds of modes of structure:

Set modes: all beams are deformed in the same way and the top surface allows non-zero displacement.

Local modes: they correspond to clamped-clamped beams modes. The top surfaces therefore allow zero displacement. None of these modes can correspond to a set mode.

The action of the fluid results in an added mass effect and thus a lowering of frequencies compared to the case without fluid. It also has the effect, in the case of local modes, of spreading the associated frequency spectrum. In the case without fluid, it has been seen that this spectrum is concentrated in a single frequency of vibration.

### 2.2 Baseline

Value of eigenfrequencies.

For mass and inertia at the centre of gravity:

- In the absence of fluid, the mass is determined by the product of the volume occupied by the beams and the density of these elements: ${\mathrm{MASSE}}_{\mathrm{solide}}={\rho}_{\mathrm{poutre}}\times {\mathrm{Vol}}_{\mathrm{poutres}}$ where ${\mathrm{Vol}}_{\mathrm{poutres}}$ is determined by the product of the number of beam, the area of the section and the length of the beam. From the data defined above, ${\mathrm{MASSE}}_{\mathrm{solide}}$ can be calculated: this is ${\mathrm{MASSE}}_{\mathrm{poutres}}=7641\times 16\times (1.5/10)\mathrm{²}\times 4.1=11278.116\mathrm{kg}$.
- Since the mass is evenly distributed in the volume (due to the position of the beams in the volume), the centre of gravity is located in its centre, i.e. point coordinates $(0.42,0.42,2.05)$ . This result is confirmed analytically by calculating the following integrals:

${X}_{G}={Y}_{G}=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}}{\int}_{V}x.\rho \text{dV}$ et ${Z}_{G}=\frac{1}{\text{Vol}}{\int}_{V}z.\rho \text{dV}$

- Inertialities at the centre of gravity $G$ are calculated by: .Analytically obtained:

${I}_{xx}(G)={\int}_{V}({(y-{y}_{G})}^{2}+{(z-{z}_{G})}^{2})\rho \text{dV}$ ; ${I}_{yy}(G)={\int}_{V}({(x-{x}_{G})}^{2}+{(z-{z}_{G})}^{2})\rho \text{dV}$ ; ${I}_{zz}(G)={\int}_{V}({(y-{y}_{G})}^{2}+{(x-{x}_{G})}^{2})\rho \text{dV}$

Analytically, we obtain:

${I}_{xx}(G)={I}_{\text{YY}}(G)=\frac{8\rho }{3}(4\times {0.15}^{2}\times {2.05}^{2}+4.1\times 0.15\times ({0.39}^{3}-{0.24}^{3}+{0.18}^{3}-{0.03}^{3}))=16441.61$ ; ${I}_{zz}(G)=\frac{16\rho \times 4.1\times 0.15}{3}({0.39}^{3}-{0.24}^{3}+{0.18}^{3}-{0.03}^{3})=1285.71$

- In the presence of fluid, the total mass corresponds to the sum of the solid mass and the fluid mass. Knowing the total volume, the volume occupied by the fluid is determined:

${\text{Vol}}_{\text{fluide}}=\text{Vol}-{\text{Vol}}_{\text{poutres}}={0.84}^{2}\times 4.1–16\times {(1.5/10)}^{2}\times 4.1=1.41696{m}^{3}$ ; ${\text{MASSE}}_{\text{fluide}}=1000\times 1.41696=1416.96\mathrm{kg}$ ; ${\text{MASSE}}_{\text{totale}}={\text{MASSE}}_{\text{solide}}+{\text{MASSE}}_{\text{fluide}}=12695.076\mathrm{kg}$

The density of l-element (with fluid) is derived from the following:

$\rho =\frac{{\text{MASSE}}_{\text{totale}}}{\text{Vol}}=4388.265\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

- The centre of gravity remains unchanged with or without fluid.
- The inertias at the centre of gravity $G$ are:

${I}_{xx}(G)={I}_{\text{YY}}(G)=\frac{2\rho \times 0.84(4.1\times {0.42}^{3}+0.84\times {2.05}^{3})}{3}=18530.155$ ; ${I}_{zz}(G)=\frac{2\rho \times 0.84\times 4.1\times 2\times {0.42}^{3}}{3}=1492.941$

## 5 Summary of the results

The results show the good modal behavior of the elements of the 3D_FASCEAU model in bending, in the absence of the fluid. They also show a very good agreement of the frequencies of the modes d' ensemble with the *Aster* calculation in heterogeneous, when there is fluid.

For the frequencies of the overall modes, there are no differences between mesh HEXA8 and HEXA20 (this is not true for other modes).

The numerical results of the masses and inertias obtained by *Code-Aster* are very close to the analytical results ($\text{}<{6.10}^{-6}$ error).
