# v7.31.145 WTNV145 - Application of a distributed pressure on the lips of a XFEM crack junction for hydromechanical cases

**Summary:**

This is a first validation test to ensure the correct functioning of the extended elements finite method in the fractured saturated HM coupling model. In this test we are looking to verify that there is a discontinuity of the displacement field on both sides of the interface, compared to an analytical solution and the results obtained with a mechanical XFEM test (similar to ssnv203, modellings F and H) in the case where the pore pressure is taken uniformly throughout the field of study ( *i.e* in order to fit in a "classical" mechanical case without coupling).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of problem 2D (modelization A)

This block is a square block on the $L=10m$ side. This block has two discontinuities of the interface type (unmesh interface which is introduced into the model via the level-set via the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator). The first is identified by the normal level-set of the ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}=Y-\mathrm{0.5X}-0.2$ equation and crosses the block entirely in the horizontal direction. The second interface is identified by the normal level-set of the ${\mathit{lsn}}_{2}=Y+\mathrm{0.5X}+0.2$ equation. It connects to the lower lip of the first interface. The second interface therefore exists only in the part of the block such as ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}<0$. The point junction between the two interfaces checks ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}={\mathit{lsn}}_{2}=0$ and has $(-0.4,0)$ coordinates. The domain is thus cut into 3 blocks, a lower block, a higher block and an intermediate block located between the two interfaces. The $A(5,0)$, $B(-3,-1.3)$, $C(3,-1.7)$ and $D(3,1.7)$ points will be used for the application of the boundary conditions and the evaluation of the quantities tested.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

*Case 2D*

The boundary conditions that can be applied to the domain are of two types:

- Dirichlet type conditions,
- Neuman type conditions.

The conditions of Dirichlet are:

- the following $x$ moves are blocked throughout the domain. The problem is thus unidirectional following $y$,
- the following $y$ moves are blocked on the lower and upper side of the block,
- In order to block the rigid body movements of the block between the two interfaces, the point $A$ is built-in.

Neuman's conditions are:

- a constant distributed mechanical pressure $P=10\mathit{MPa}$ is applied to each of the interface lips,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{1}=0.2\mathit{MPa}$ in the lower block,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{2}=0.4\mathit{MPa}$ in the intermediate block between the two interfaces,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{3}=0.6\mathit{MPa}$ in the upper block.

* 3D cas*

The boundary conditions that can be applied to the domain are of two types:

- Dirichlet type conditions,
- Neuman type conditions.

The conditions of Dirichlet are:

- the following $x$ moves and the following $y$ moves are blocked throughout the domain. The problem is thus unidirectional following $z$ ,
- the following $z$ moves are blocked on the lower and upper side of the block,
- In order to block the rigid body movements of the block between the two interfaces, the ${A}_{1}$ and ${A}_{2}$ points are built into the system.

Neuman's conditions are:

- a constant distributed mechanical pressure $P=10\mathit{MPa}$ is applied to each of the interface lips,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{1}=0.2\mathit{MPa}$ in the lower block,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{2}=0.4\mathit{MPa}$ in the intermediate block between the two interfaces,
- the pore pressure is fixed to ${p}_{3}=0.6\mathit{MPa}$ in the upper block.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

Taking into account boundary conditions, displacements can be obtained from the analytical resolution of the motion quantity conservation equation.

By neglecting the gravity, the equation is written (in total constraints):

$\text{Div}(\sigma )=0$

In the case of coupled modelling, the total stress tensor is written:

$\sigma =\sigma '-\mathit{bp}1$

$\sigma '$ is the stress tensor in the skeleton, $b$ the Biot and $p$ coefficient and pore pressure in the solid. The Poisson $\nu$ module being zero, and being in the elastic case, we have $\sigma '=Eϵ$.

$\nu$ being zero, the boundary conditions and the loading make the problem one-dimensional according to $y$. Only ${ϵ}_{yy}$ is not zero and therefore in each block:

$\sigma =E{ϵ}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}-{\mathit{bp}}_{i}1$

with ${p}_{i}$ the pore pressure imposed in the current block.

So in each block, the tensor of total stresses is written in the form:

$\sigma ={\sigma}_{xx}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}+{\sigma}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$

Now in each block, the boundary conditions at the lip level of the interfaces that delimit blocks write $\sigma .n=-Pn$, so:

$\begin{array}{c}({\sigma}_{xx}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}+{\sigma}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}).n=-Pn\\ {\sigma}_{xx}({e}_{x}.n){e}_{x}+{\sigma}_{yy}({e}_{y}.n){e}_{y}=-Pn\\ ({\sigma}_{xx}({e}_{x}.n){e}_{x}+{\sigma}_{yy}({e}_{y}.n){e}_{y}).{e}_{y}=-P({e}_{y}.n)\\ {\sigma}_{yy}({e}_{y}.n)=-P({e}_{y}.n)\\ {\sigma}_{yy}=-P\text{car}{e}_{y}.n\ne 0\end{array}$

Finally in each block

${\sigma}_{yy}=E{ϵ}_{yy}-{\mathit{bp}}_{i}=-P$ i.e. ${ϵ}_{yy}=\frac{-P+{\mathit{bp}}_{i}}{E}$

with ${p}_{i}$ the pore pressure imposed in the current block.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

In particular, the $y$ directional displacements in each block are considered:

- in the lower block, based on boundary conditions, ${u}_{y}(y)=\frac{-P+{\mathit{bp}}_{1}}{E}\ast (\frac{L}{2}+y)$
- In the lower block, the movements are symmetrical with respect to the $(\mathit{Ox})$ axis. Indeed, this block is symmetrical with respect to the $(\mathit{Ox})$ axis and the boundary conditions and the loads applied to it also follow this symmetry (mechanical pressure distributed on the crack lips and clamped support of the point $A$). The vertical movements are therefore written: ${u}_{y}(y)=\frac{-P+{\mathit{bp}}_{2}}{E}\ast y$
- in the upper block, based on boundary conditions, ${u}_{y}(y)=\frac{-P+{\mathit{bp}}_{3}}{E}\ast (\frac{-L}{2}+y)$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

None, it's a analytical solution.

## 5 Conclusion

For D_PLAN_HM Modelling and 3D_HM Modelling, the results are consistent with analytical solution. For the elements HM-XFEM multi-Heaviside, the following features are now validated:

- MODI_MODELE_XFEM
- CHAR_MECA_PRES
- POST_CHAM_XFEM
- the calculation of the elementary matrices and vectors in the case of mechanical and hydraulic degrees of freedom Heaviside introduced into the development of the HM coupling model.
