# v6.04.104 SSNV104 - Contact of two spheres

**Summary:**

The calculation consists of crushing two hemispheres on each other to test the static unilateral contact algorithm. The solution is compared with Hertz's analytical result.

Modellings implements axisymmetric and $\mathrm{3D}$ calculations with linear/quadratic elements and different contact resolution methods (dualization, penalization, continuous formulation, Mortar matching).

This classic test for which an analytical solution is available is also used to validate several matching algorithm options.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The geometry consists of two half-spheres of identical radius, a cut of which is shown in the figure below.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Crush imposed on the spheres:

    - $\mathrm{DY}$ imposed $–2\mathrm{mm}$ for nodes $\mathrm{AB}$
    - $\mathrm{DY}$ imposed $+2\mathit{mm}$ for nodes $\mathit{CD}$

    - $\mathrm{DX}$ blocked on $\mathit{AC}$ axis

For $\mathrm{3D}$ calculations (2 quarters of modelled spheres):

    - symmetry conditions on each quarter of a sphere

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

3D modelling is used. Due to symmetry, only one quarter of the hemisphere is represented, a unilateral restraint condition is used to complete the modelling.

In this Modelling, the points $H$ and ${H}_{p}$ (respectively $I$ and ${I}_{p}$) are the same points (compared to those described in geometry) but located on both sides supporting symmetry conditions.

The results obtained with this Modelling are used as a reference (AUTRE_ASTER) for the three-dimensional modellings I and J.

## 16 Summary of the results

The comparison in each modellings with the analytical reference (limited to point $G$) is satisfactory.

The non-regression results are ensured by testing the nodes two-way movements of the edge, $H$ and $I$ . These nodes positions are slightly different depending on the modellings, so you should not try to compare them between modellings.

This test in curved geometry highlights the usefulness of certain matching parameters such as:

- smoothing: this is a process of modification of normals that are used to write contact conditions. Although sphere meshes are symmetrical, in practice there is always a slight offset on the last point of the contact surface. Smoothing of normals allows us to find symmetry. This parameter is also useful for improving the geometric convergence of modellings using coarse meshes (this is the case of modellings $\mathrm{2D}$ of this test).
- Nodal pairing or average normal (slave master): not used in general, these two pairing options, such as smoothing, allow to find the symmetry of the problem.

Modelling A where contact is replaced by a unilateral restraint also highlights the very important role played by geometric updating on the accuracy of the stresses. Indeed, this Modelling allows to remove the geometric non-linearity of this problem, so it provides **an reference solution**. Thus when comparing the results obtained on the same $\mathrm{3D}$ problem by the modellings A, I and J, we find that:

- the displacement values obtained by model I have differences up to 4% compared to the other two modellings
- modellings A and J find the same Hertz pressure, while model I finds 10% lower pressure
- in model I the geometric criterion is met to the nearest 5% whereas it is met to 1% in model J

It therefore seems important to satisfy the geometric criterion as best as possible to obtain the best possible precision on the stress values.
