# v5.01.122 SDND122 – Nonlinear mode calculation – 1 degree of freedom system impacting an elastic stop

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the nonlinear mode calculation with the MODE_NON_LINE operator. post-processings is also performed with CALC_STABILITE and REST_MODE_NONL.

A analytical solution connecting frequency and energy is available for a material point that impacts against an elastic stop.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A system consisting of a spring fixed at one end and fitted with a point mass at the other end is considered.

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.122/10000000000003DB000002249BF4C427AF27020F.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

spring stiffness: $k=10N/m$

Mass: $m=1\mathit{kg}$

Rigidity of the wall: $K=50N/m$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The mass is fixed to one end of the spring and the other end is fixed. It moves in the direction u and can impact against the wall at $e=0.01m$ of its equilibrium position.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

For nonlinear mode calculations, the behaviour depends on the energy stored in the system.

## 2 reference solution

The calculation of periodic solutions of the system characterizing the nonlinear mode [1] is being studied.

### 2.1 Method of calculation

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.122/10000000000003DB000002249BF4C427AF27020F.png)

If $u$ is noted the displacement of the mass and $F$ the reaction force, the system equation is written:

$$
\ddot{u}(t)+\text{ku}(t)=-F(u(t))
$$

Où:

> $F(u)=0$             si     $u\le e$
> $F(u)=K(u-e)$     si     $u>e$

The following initial conditions are assumed:

> $\dot{u}(0)=-\sqrt{\frac{2E}{m}}$         et        $u(0)=0$

Where $E$ is the mechanical energy stored.

The problem is solved by considering two linear problems. The first corresponds to the free flight (excluding impact), and the second corresponds to the system with the solid mass at the wall (during impact).

After calculation, the ${T}_{1}$ duration of the free flight phase is obtained:

> ${T}_{1}=2\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}\arccos(-e\sqrt{\frac{k}{2E}})$

And the duration of the ${T}_{2}$ impact is given by the following relationship:

> ${T}_{2}=2\sqrt{\frac{m}{K+k}}\arccos(ek\sqrt{\frac{1}{2E(K+k)}})$

The frequency of the periodic solution is therefore equal to: $N(E)=\frac{1}{{T}_{1}+{T}_{2}}$

The stability of the periodic solution is calculated based on Floquet's theory, by a Newmark diagram and a calculation at the own values.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The selected reference quantities are the frequency-energy torque and stability of the periodic solution obtained.

The periodic solution is stable for frequency torque – energy such as:

$0.644\mathit{Hz}<f<0.6475\mathit{Hz}$     et    $6.2{10}^{-3}J<E<6.9{10}^{-3}J$

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The relationship between frequency and energy is obtained analytically.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

    1. MOUSSI, Analysis of vibrating structures with non-linearities located at play using non-linear modes. PhD Thesis 2013.

## 6 Summary of the results

This test case validates the nonlinear mode calculation operator (MODE_NON_LINE), the nonlinear mode stability calculation operator (CALC_STABILITE) and the periodic solution return operator (REST_MODE_NONL).

The matrix of the system is quasi-singular, the value of the solution obtained may depend on the accuracy of the machine on which the calculation is initiated. The value of the relative frequency for a given energy is tested. This frequency is obtained by interpolation from the table produced by MODE_NON_LINE. It is found that the relationship between energy and frequency is always verified on the results obtained.
