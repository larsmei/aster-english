# r7.04.04 Multiaxial fatigue-initiation criteria

**Summary:**

In this note we propose a formulation of the MATAKE, DANG VAN and FATEMI-SOCIE criteria and of the formula criteria adapted to the framework of damage accumulation under multiaxial **periodic** and **non-periodic** loading. These criteria can be used in the command CALC_FATIGUE.

The first part of this document is devoted to the MATAKE and DANG VAN criteria adapted to periodic multiaxial loadings. In this part, after having addressed the notions of endurance and of damage accumulation and the general form of the fatigue criteria, we describe the two models of DANG VAN and of MATAKE (critical plane) intended to carry out damage-accumulation analyses under multiaxial loading. One details there the definition of the various shear planes associated with the Gauss points or with the nodes, as well as the definition of a loading amplitude through the circle circumscribed about the loading path in the shear plane. Finally the criteria available in *Code_Aster* are presented.

In the second part we propose a formulation of the MATAKE, DANG VAN and FATEMI-SOCIE criteria within the framework of damage accumulation under non-periodic multiaxial loading. In order to define a cycle in the variable-amplitude case, we reduce the loading history to a one-dimensional function of time by projecting the tip of the shear vector onto an axis, and we use a cycle-counting method. Here we choose the RAINFLOW method. The MATAKE, DANG VAN and FATEMI-SOCIE criteria adapted to damage accumulation under non-periodic loading are implemented in *Code_Aster* .

In addition to the well-established criteria, one has available in Code_Aster **formula criteria** allowing the user to construct new criteria as functions of the predefined quantities. This type of criterion is detailed in the third part of this document.

Finally, the option VMIS_TRESCA makes it possible to compute the maximum variation, over time, of a stress tensor according to the Von Mises and Tresca criteria.

## 1 Introduction

The multiaxial fatigue endurance models under periodic loading are models of the following type:

${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}+a\times {\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}<b$ ,

where $b$ is the endurance threshold in simple shear, and *a* a positive dimensionless constant. ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}$ is a certain definition of the amplitude (half the variation) of the loading cycle and ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ is a variable related to the stress (or sometimes the strain) or to the mean stresses (or sometimes the mean strains). The models are distinguished by different definitions of ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}$ and ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ .

In order to pass from endurance to damage accumulation, one introduces an equivalent stress defined by:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}+a\times {\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ .

This equivalent stress gives us a unit damage on the fatigue curve. Since the right-hand side of the inequality $b$ corresponds to the shear threshold, a fatigue curve in shear is required. But fatigue curves in shear are rare because they are difficult to obtain, so one therefore tries to use the fatigue curves in alternating tension–compression. For this it is necessary to multiply the equivalent stress by a corrective coefficient of the order of $\sqrt{3}$ .

The macroscopic models of MATAKE (critical plane) and the micro–macro model of DANG-VAN are described. One shows that under certain assumptions the DANG-VAN model is similar to the macroscopic model of MATAKE. The only difference lies in the variable ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ : DANG-VAN uses the hydrostatic pressure, whereas MATAKE uses the normal stress on the plane of maximum shear amplitude.

After having defined the shear plane, we express the shear stress in this plane. The shear planes are then explored according to a method described in the reference, which consists in cutting the surface of a sphere into pieces of equal size.

The normal vectors being known, we then determine for each plane the points which are the furthest from one another. Among these we find the two points which are the furthest from each other. This being done, we use, if necessary, the method of the circle passing through three points in order to obtain the circle circumscribed about the loading path.

In the first part of this document we present the multiaxial fatigue endurance models under periodic loading, as well as the notion of damage accumulation. The passage from endurance to damage accumulation is also addressed.

In the second part the MATAKE and DANG-VAN criteria are then presented from the points of view of the endurance limit and of damage accumulation under periodic loading.

The third part is devoted to the definition of the shear plane, to the expression of the shear stresses in this plane and, finally, to the way of exploring the shear planes.

The fourth part is dedicated to the determination of the circle circumscribed about the shear path in the plane of the same name. Finally we describe the criteria and the quantities which are introduced in *Code_Aster* .

After having extended the MATAKE and DANG-VAN models to damage accumulation under periodic loading, we present the adaptation of these models to damage accumulation under non-periodic loading. Thus, the fifth part is devoted to the definition of the elementary equivalent stress. We also describe the modified FATEMI-SOCIE criterion.

The sixth part is devoted to the way of selecting the axis (or the two axes) onto which the history of the shear traction is projected.

The seventh part is dedicated to the projection proper of the tip of the shear-traction vector onto this axis or these two axes. Finally, concerning the MATAKE and DANG-VAN criteria formulated in damage accumulation under non-periodic loading, we describe the quantities which are introduced in *Code_Aster.*

## 2 Preliminaries

In this part we treat the notions of endurance limit and of damage accumulation. We also present the general form of the fatigue criteria.

### 2.1 Endurance limit and damage accumulation, uniaxial case

In the uniaxial case, the rigorous definition of the endurance threshold is the half-amplitude (half the variation) of loading defined in stress below which the life is infinite. However, since in practice the life can never be infinite, one defines endurance limits at ${10}^{7}$ , ${10}^{8}$ , etc. loading cycles. There exists another way of looking at things: since in practice an infinite life does not exist, one uses the notion of damage accumulation. The damage-accumulation approach consists in defining a limit in number of cycles beyond which the cumulated damage is equal to one. Thus the limit at ${10}^{7}$ means that after ${10}^{7}$ cycles the cumulated damage is equal to 1.

### 2.2 Fatigue criterion, multiaxial case

In the literature a certain number of criteria have been proposed in order to define the **endurance threshold** under multiaxial cyclic loading. The general form of these criteria is:

$$
{\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}+a\times {\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}<b
$$

where $b$ is the endurance threshold in simple shear, $a$ is a positive dimensionless constant. ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}$ is a certain definition of the half-amplitude (half the variation) of the cycle and ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ is a variable related to the stress (or sometimes the strain) or to the mean stresses (or sometimes the mean strains). Different models are distinguished by different definitions of ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}$ and ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ .

In order to pass from endurance to **damage accumulation** , one can define an equivalent stress (or an equivalent strain):

$$
{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}+a\times {\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}
$$

This equivalent stress gives us a unit damage on the fatigue curve. Since the right-hand side of the inequality corresponds to the shear threshold, a fatigue curve in shear is required. But fatigue curves in shear are rare because they are difficult to obtain, so one therefore tries to use the fatigue curves in alternating tension–compression. For this one must be consistent at least at the level of the endurance threshold, that is multiply ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ by a constant of the order of $\sqrt{3}$ in order to be able to use the fatigue curve in tension. The value $\sqrt{3}$ is the exact value for a criterion of Mises type; experimentally this coefficient is smaller than $\sqrt{3}$ .

### 2.3 Definition of a loading amplitude in the multiaxial case

In *code_aster* , there exist two definitions of the loading amplitude in the multiaxial case:

$A$ : radius (half-diameter) of the sphere circumscribed about the loading path;

$B$ : half the maximum of the distance between any two points of the path.

It is clear that in the case of a loading defined on a sphere, $A$ and $B$ give the same amplitude. On the other hand, if one takes a (two-dimensional) path in the form of an equilateral triangle of side $l$ , definition $A$ gives us $l/\sqrt{3}$ , whereas definition $B$ gives us $l/2$ . In order to work within a conservative framework we take as the definition of the amplitude (half-variation) of a loading path the radius of the circumscribed sphere (or circle for the 2D case).

### 2.4 Definition of the shear plane

At a point $M$ of a continuous medium we express the stress tensor $\sigma$ in an orthonormal frame $(O,x,y,z)$ . To the unit normal $n$ of components $({n}_{x},{n}_{y},{n}_{z})$ in the orthonormal frame, we associate the stress vector $F=\sigma .n$ of components $({F}_{x},{F}_{y},{F}_{z})$ . This vector $F$ can be decomposed into a vector normal to $n$ and a scalar carried by $n$ , that is:

$$
F=Nn+\tau
$$

where $N$ represents the normal stress and the vector $\tau$ the shear stress. In the frame $(O,x,y,z)$ , the components of the vector $\tau$ are denoted: $({\tau}_{x},{\tau}_{y},{\tau}_{z})$ . The vector $\tau$ is deduced directly from and from the normal stress:

$$
N=F.n
$$

whence $\tau =F-F.nn$

## 3 Criteria of MATAKE (critical plane) and of DANG VAN

Here we make explicit the MATAKE and DANG VAN criteria both from the point of view of the endurance limit and from the point of view of damage accumulation.

### 3.1 MATAKE criterion

In this type of criterion the computation of the stress and strain fields is carried out under the assumption of elasticity, confer the reference. As was said in chapter 2, in the multiaxial case the endurance criterion is generally written in the form:

$$
{\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}+a\times {\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}<b
$$

**Loading amplitude** : In the case of the MATAKE criterion, at each point of the structure (or Gauss point for a finite-element analysis), in order to compute ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{amplitude}}$ one proceeds in the following way:

1. for each plane of normal $n$ one computes the shear amplitude by determining the circle circumscribed about the shear path in this plane;
2. one seeks the normal $n\ast$ for which the amplitude is maximum. This amplitude is denoted by $\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })$ .

**Mean stress** : For the computation of ${\text{VAR}}_{\text{moyenne}}$ one proceeds in the following way:

1. on the plane of normal $n\ast$ one computes over a cycle the maximum normal stress denoted by ${N}_{\max}(n\ast )$ .

The endurance criterion is written:

$\frac{\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })}{2}+a{N}_{\max}(n\mathrm{\ast })\le b$ ,

where $a$ and $b$ are two positive constants and $b$ represents the endurance limit in simple shear.

**Identification of the constants** : in order to determine the constants $a$ and $b$ one must use two simple tests. Two possibilities exist:

A pure-shear test plus an alternating tension–compression test. In this case the constants are given by: $b={\tau}_{0}$ $a=({\tau}_{0}-\frac{{d}_{0}}{2})/\frac{{d}_{0}}{2}$ , where ${\tau}_{0}$ represents the endurance limit in alternating pure shear and ${d}_{0}$ the endurance limit in alternating pure tension–compression.

Two tension–compression tests, one alternating and the other not. The constants are given by:

$\begin{array}{}a=\frac{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{1}-\Delta {\sigma}_{2})-2{\sigma}_{m}},\\ b=\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})+2{\sigma}_{m}}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{2}\end{array}$

where $\Delta {\sigma}_{1}$ is the loading amplitude for the alternating case and $\Delta {\sigma}_{2}$ for the case where the mean stress is non-zero.

### 3.2 DANG VAN criterion

One assumes that the material remains globally elastic while it yields locally. The interesting physical assumption of the model is that the material shakes down locally (it becomes elastic after having passed through plasticity) below the endurance limit, which corresponds to the non-initiation of a crack. Above the endurance limit there is locally plastic accommodation and therefore crack initiation.

The basic assumptions of the micro–macro interaction, Lin–Taylor, make it possible to write:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{Loc}}(t)={\sigma}_{ij}(t)+{\rho}_{ij}(t)\\ {\rho}_{ij}(t)=-2\mu {\varepsilon}_{ij}^{p}(t)\end{array}$

One denotes the local stress by ${\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{Loc}}(t)$ , the global stress by ${\sigma}_{ij}(t)$ , the local residual stress by ${\rho}_{ij}(t)$ and by ${\varepsilon}_{ij}^{p}(t)$ the local plastic strain. As soon as there is shakedown the local plastic strain becomes constant and therefore the local residual stress likewise.

**Plasticity criterion:**

At a point of the continuous medium (where there is a random distribution of the crystallographic directions of the grains), one assumes that there is a single grain which is yielded, and this along a single slip system. This slip system will be the one which is the most favourably oriented, that is, the grain in which the greatest resolved shear will occur (the projection of the shear vector onto a given direction). The slip takes place in the planes of normal $n=({n}_{1},{n}_{2},{n}_{3})$ and the slip direction is defined by the vector $m=({m}_{1},{m}_{2},{m}_{3})$ . The two vectors $n$ and $m$ are orthogonal.

**Schmid's** law says that in order for there to be no irreversible slip (plastic strain) the resolved shear must not exceed a certain threshold, that is:

$$
\forall m\forall n\mid {\tau}^{\text{Loc}}(n,m,t)\mid -{\tau}_{y}^{\text{Loc}}(t)\le 0
$$

where

${\tau}^{\text{Loc}}(t)={a}_{ij}{\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{loc}}\text{et}{a}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({m}_{i}{n}_{j}+{n}_{i}{m}_{j})$

The drawing of r7.04.04-fig-3-1 shows that the maximum value of ${\tau}^{\text{Loc}}$ , denoted by ${\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}$ , is obtained by the orthogonal projection of ${F}^{\text{Loc}}={\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{Loc}}{n}_{j}$ onto the plane of normal $n$ . The relation must in particular be verified in the case where one replaces ${\tau}^{\text{Loc}}$ by its upper bound ${\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}$ ; it is then written:

$$
\forall n\mid {\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)\mid -{\tau}_{y}^{\text{Loc}}(t)\le 0
$$

where ${\tau}_{y}^{\text{Loc}}(t)$ is the threshold of the microscopic or local resolved shear. ${\tau}_{y}^{\text{Loc}}(t)$ depends on the hardening variables.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/projection_orthogonale.png)

*Projection of ${F}^{\text{Loc}}$ onto the plane of normal $n$*

One chooses a microscopic hardening of linear isotropic type. This makes it possible to show the existence of a shakedown domain.

In the shakedown state, by analogy with the formula:

${\sigma}_{ij}^{\text{Loc}}(t)={\sigma}_{ij}(t)+{\rho}_{ij}^{\text{*}}$

one has, if one places oneself in the plane $(n,m)$ in such a way that the resolved shear is maximum, the following formula:

${\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)=\tau (n,t)+{\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$

where $\tau (n,t)$ is the macroscopic shear vector defined in r7.04.04-fig-3-2 and where ${\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$ is the microscopic residual shear vector (independent of time since we are in the shakedown state).

**Fatigue criterion**

Introduction of the maximum pressure: DANG VAN uses, in place of the normal stress on a plane, as is done in the MATAKE model, the maximum hydrostatic pressure over a cycle. The criterion is therefore written:

$\underset{n,t}{\text{MAX}}(\mid {\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)\mid +a{P}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}})\le b$

Since the local and global hydrostatic pressures are identical the criterion becomes:

$\underset{n,t}{\text{MAX}}(\mid {\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)\mid +a{P}_{\max})\le b$

For a positive maximum pressure we have:

$\underset{n,t}{\text{MAX}}(\mid {\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)\mid )+a{P}_{\max}\le b$

For a **pressure that is always negative** one can take ${P}_{\max}=0$ in order to remain conservative.

**Assumption on** ${\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$

In the radial case where the direction of the maximum shear is defined in advance one can compute ${\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$ exactly. In the general case DANG VAN proposes the following method in order to carry out a simplified computation of ${\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$ . One gives for a plane $n$ the macroscopic path of the shear vector defined previously. The residual shear vector, taking the previous assumption into account, is defined by $\mathit{MO}$ , where $M$ is the centre of the circle circumscribed about the path of the tip of the shear vector in the shear plane.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/trajets_micro_macro.png)

*Micro/macro paths in the shear plane*

**Final formulation:** taking the two formulae into account:

${\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)=\tau (n,t)+{\tau}^{\text{*}}(n)$ and $\underset{n,t}{\text{MAX}}(\mid {\tau}_{\max}^{\text{Loc}}(n,t)\mid )+a{P}_{\max}\le b$

one ends up with

$\underset{n,t}{\text{MAX}}(\mid \mathit{MP}\mid )+a{P}_{\max}\le b$

where $P$ is a current point of the shear path in the plane of normal $n$ .

**Identification of the constants** : in order to determine the constants $a$ and $b$ one must use two simple tests. Two possibilities exist:

- **A pure-shear test plus an alternating tension–compression test** . In this case the constants are given by: $b={\tau}_{0}a=({\tau}_{0}-{d}_{0}/2)/({d}_{0}/3)$ .
- **Two tension–compression tests, one alternating and the other not** . The constants are given by:

$a=\frac{3}{2}\times \frac{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{1}-\Delta {\sigma}_{2})-2{\sigma}_{m}}b=\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})+2{\sigma}_{m}}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{2}$ .

with $\Delta {\sigma}_{1}$ the loading amplitude for the alternating case and $\Delta {\sigma}_{2}$ for the case where the mean stress is non-zero.

### 3.3 MATAKE and DANG VAN modified for damage accumulation

The MATAKE and DANG VAN models were initially proposed for the study of the endurance limit. Since an infinite life does not exist one uses endurance limits at ${10}^{6}$ , ${10}^{7}$ , ${10}^{n}$ loading cycles. Thus the initial MATAKE and DANG VAN criteria are presented as criteria of exceedance of a threshold and do not give a damage accumulation. The use, in particular of the DANG VAN criterion, in the automotive industries is appropriate since the objective sought is the non-exceedance of an endurance threshold, unlike the issues of EDF where one wishes to follow the damage.

Thus we use for the damage accumulation an equivalent MATAKE or DANG VAN stress defined by:

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{MATAKE}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}(n\text{*})+a{N}_{\max}(n\text{*}).\\ \text{DANG VAN}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={\text{MAX}}_{n,t}(\mid \mathit{MP}\mid )+{\mathit{aP}}_{\max}\end{array}$

The account taken of the surface treatment is summarised as the account taken of the harmful effect of pre-hardening on the life in strain control. In the MATAKE and DANG VAN models the effect of pre-hardening is taken into account by multiplying the half-amplitude of the shear stress by a corrective coefficient greater than unity, denoted ${c}_{p}$ :

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{MATAKE}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}(n\text{*})+a{N}_{\max}(n\text{*}),\\ \text{DANG VAN}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={c}_{p}{\text{MAX}}_{n,t}(\mid \mathit{MP}\mid )+{\mathit{aP}}_{\max}\end{array}$

These equivalent stresses are to be used on a fatigue curve in shear. For use on a tension–compression fatigue curve one must multiply these equivalent stresses by a corrective coefficient, denoted here $\alpha$ :

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{MATAKE}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}(n\text{*})+a{N}_{\max}(n\text{*})),\\ \text{DANG VAN}& {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\alpha ({c}^{p}{\text{MAX}}_{n,t}(\mid \mathit{MP}\mid )+{\mathit{aP}}_{\max})\end{array}$

## 4 Computation of the plane of maximum shear

We use here the definition of the shear plane introduced in r7.04.04-definition-plan-cisaillement. Practically, for us the point $M$ of the continuous medium will be a Gauss point.

### 4.1 Expression of the shear stresses in the plane $\Delta$

For reasons of symmetry we vary the unit normal $n$ over a half-sphere by means of the angles $\gamma$ and $\varphi$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-4-1.

In the frame $(O,x,y,z)$ , the unit normal vector $n$ is defined by:

$$
{n}_{x}=\sin\gamma \cos\varphi {n}_{y}=\sin\gamma \sin\varphi {n}_{z}=\cos\gamma
$$

We introduce a new frame $(O,u,v,n)$ where $n$ is perpendicular to the shear plane $\Delta$ and where $u$ and $v$ are in this plane, cf. r7.04.04-fig-4-1. In the frame $(O,x,y,z)$ the unit vectors $u$ and $v$ are respectively defined by:

$$
{u}_{x}=-\sin\varphi {u}_{y}=\cos\varphi {u}_{z}=0
$$

$$
{v}_{x}=-\cos\gamma \cos\varphi {v}_{y}=-\cos\gamma \sin\varphi {v}_{z}=\sin\gamma
$$

*Location of the normal $n$ to a plane by the angles $\gamma$ and $\varphi$*

In the plane $\Delta$ , the components ${\tau}_{u}$ and ${\tau}_{v}$ of the vector $\tau$ representing the shear stress are obtained by the relations:

$$
{\tau}_{u}=u\cdot \tau ={u}_{x}{\tau}_{x}+{u}_{y}{\tau}_{y}+{u}_{z}{\tau}_{z}
$$

$$
{\tau}_{v}=v\cdot \tau ={v}_{x}{\tau}_{x}+{v}_{y}{\tau}_{y}+{v}_{z}{\tau}_{z}

In r7.04.04-fig-4-2, we have represented the shear stresses in the plane $\Delta$ as well as the frame $(O,u,v,n)$ .

Our problem now is to determine, for each Gauss point or each node of a mesh, the plane of normal $n$ such that $\mid \tau \mid$ is maximum. To do this we vary the unit normal $n$ .

### 4.2 Exploration of the shear planes

The method that we present here comes from the reference. Its principle is the following. As indicated in r7.04.04-expression-contraintes-cisaillement, for reasons of symmetry we vary the unit normal $n$ over a half-sphere by means of the angles $\gamma$ and $\varphi$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-4-1. The question which immediately arises is what the step of variation of the angles $\gamma$ and $\varphi$ must be. Indeed, one must find an optimum between the fineness of the exploration and a reasonable computation time, inasmuch as it is necessary to carry out this operation at each Gauss point of the mesh. The author of the reference proposes to divide the surface of the half-sphere into facets of equal areas at the centres of which the unit normal $n$ is positioned, cf. r7.04.04-fig-4-3. In practice the areas are not strictly equal but of the same order of magnitude.

The value of the step of variation of $\gamma$ , $\Delta \gamma$ , is equal to 10 degrees. The angle $\varphi$ varies according to a step $\Delta \varphi$ which is a function of the angle $\gamma$ . The smaller $\gamma$ is, or the closer it is to 180 degrees, the larger $\Delta \varphi$ must be in order to keep a facet area that is approximately constant. It is in the neighbourhood of $\gamma =90°$ that $\Delta \varphi$ is the smallest. r7.04.04-tab-4-1 summarises the subdivision which has been retained.

With this method the number of facets, hence the number of normal vectors to be explored, is equal to 209 for a half-sphere.

## 5 Computation of the shear half-amplitude

The problem is therefore to find the circle circumscribed about a certain number of points located in a plane. The shear half-amplitude will be equal to the radius of the circumscribed circle.

### 5.1 General presentation of the computation of the circumscribed circle

The method that we use is an exact method which is decomposed into four steps.

**Step 1**

We frame the points and we determine the coordinates of the four corners of the frame in the frame $(0,u,v)$ , and the coordinates of the centre of the frame $O$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-1 and r7.04.04-fig-5-3. **In the particular case** where the frame reduces to a horizontal or vertical line, the half-length of the line is equal to the shear half-amplitude.

**Step 2**

The objective of the second step is to select the two furthest points. In order not to examine the distance between all the possible pairs of points, we construct four sectors, cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-1 and r7.04.04-fig-5-3. These sectors are located at the four corners of the frame and are delimited on the one hand by the contour of the frame and on the other hand by an arc of a circle whose centre is the opposite corner and the radius the long side of the frame, which in fact underestimates the distance between the two furthest points. Finally, we evaluate the distances between the points of the four sectors two by two:

distances between the points of sector 1 and the points of sector 2;

distances between the points of sector 1 and the points of sector 3;

distances between the points of sector 1 and the points of sector 4;

distances between the points of sector 2 and the points of sector 3;

distances between the points of sector 2 and the points of sector 4;

distances between the points of sector 3 and the points of sector 4.

**In the particular case** where the ratio of the short side of the frame to the long side is strictly less than $\sqrt{3/4}$ we do not evaluate the distances between the points belonging to sectors 1 and 2 nor the distances between the points of sectors 3 and 4, the case of the example of r7.04.04-fig-5-1.

**Step 3**

In the third step we construct domains 1 and 2 in which we shall seek the points which lie outside the initial circumscribed circle, cf. Step 4. The constitution of domains 1 and 2 has the aim of reducing the number of points to be explored during step 4. The principles of construction of these two domains are the following.

- From the mid-points of the two long sides of the frame (${\text{Omi}}_{1}$ and ${\text{Omi}}_{2}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-2 and r7.04.04-fig-5-4) we draw an arc of a circle whose radius corresponds to the lower bound of the value of the shear half-amplitude and is equal to half the length of the long side of the frame.
- From the centre of the frame $O$ we draw four arcs of a circle whose radius is likewise the lower bound of the value of the shear half-amplitude.

If ${O}_{i}$ , the centre of the initial circumscribed circle, has a component along the axis $u$ which places it between ${\text{Omi}}_{1}$ and $O$ , then if there exists a point whose distance to ${O}_{i}$ is greater than ${R}_{i}$ , the radius of the initial circumscribed circle, it can only be in domain 1, cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-2.

**Step 4**

The aim of the fourth step is to find the circumscribed circle by the method of the circle passing through three points, cf. r7.04.04-description-methode-cercle. To do this, we compute the mid-point ${O}_{1}$ associated with the two furthest points, which we denote ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ , and we deduce from it the value of a first radius denoted ${R}_{1}$ . Depending on the position of ${O}_{1}$ with respect to the long axis of the frame passing through its centre, we seek either in domain 1 or in domain 2 whether there is a point located at a distance greater than the half-distance measured between the two furthest points ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ . Let ${P}_{3}$ denote such a point. If there is no point such as ${P}_{3}$ then the shear half-amplitude is equal to ${R}_{1}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-3. On the other hand, if ${P}_{3}$ exists we seek the coordinates of the point located at equal distance from ${P}_{1}$ , ${P}_{2}$ and ${P}_{3}$ ; we denote this point ${O}_{2}$ . We thus obtain a new radius, ${R}_{2}$ , hence a new shear half-amplitude. Again, depending on the position of ${O}_{2}$ with respect to the long axis of the frame passing through its centre, we seek either in domain 1 or in domain 2 whether there is a point located at a distance greater than ${R}_{2}$ from ${O}_{2}$ . Let ${P}_{4}$ denote such a point. If there is no point such as ${P}_{4}$ then the shear half-amplitude is equal to ${R}_{2}$ . On the other hand, if ${P}_{4}$ exists we seek the smallest circle circumscribed about the four points: ${P}_{1}$ , ${P}_{2}$ , ${P}_{3}$ and ${P}_{4}$ by using successively the method of the circle passing through three points, cf. r7.04.04-description-methode-cercle. This provides us with a new centre ${O}_{3}$ and a new radius ${R}_{3}$ . As previously, depending on the position of ${O}_{3}$ with respect to the long axis of the frame passing through its centre, we seek either in domain 1 or in domain 2 whether there is a point located at a distance greater than ${R}_{3}$ from ${O}_{3}$ . Let ${P}_{5}$ denote such a point. If there is no point such as ${P}_{5}$ then the shear half-amplitude is equal to ${R}_{3}$ . On the other hand if a point such as ${P}_{5}$ exists we have five points; if we wish to use the previous method, in which only four points are involved, it is necessary to eliminate one of the five points. This cannot be the last one: ${P}_{5}$ , so we keep from the previous iteration the three points which made it possible to determine ${O}_{3}$ and ${R}_{3}$ , that is the smallest circumscribed circle. Suppose that ${P}_{1}$ is thus eliminated. We therefore seek the smallest circle circumscribed about the four points: ${P}_{2}$ , ${P}_{3}$ , ${P}_{4}$ and ${P}_{5}$ by using successively the method of the circle passing through three points, cf. r7.04.04-description-methode-cercle. This provides us with a new centre ${O}_{4}$ and a new radius ${R}_{4}$ . Depending on the position of ${O}_{4}$ with respect to the long axis of the frame passing through its centre, we seek either in domain 1 or in domain 2 whether there is a point located at a distance greater than ${R}_{4}$ from ${O}_{4}$ . If this is not the case the shear half-amplitude is equal to ${R}_{4}$ and the circumscribed circle has centre ${O}_{4}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-6. Conversely, if such a point exists we repeat an iteration identical to the previous one.

### 5.2 Description of the method of the circle passing through three points

In this paragraph we shall treat the general case, then the particular cases.

#### 5.2.1 General case

In order to determine the circle circumscribed about three points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-7, we proceed in three steps.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/determination_cercle.png)

*Determination of the circle passing through three points*

**Step 1**

We compute the coordinates of the three mid-points: ${M}_{0}$ , ${M}_{1}$ and ${M}_{2}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-7.

**Step 2**

We determine the normals passing through the three mid-points: ${M}_{0}$ , ${M}_{1}$ and ${M}_{2}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-5-7. These normals are straight lines of the type $v=au+b$ where $a$ and $b$ are constants which it is possible to compute with the coordinates of the points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ , ${P}_{2}$ , ${M}_{0}$ , ${M}_{1}$ and ${M}_{2}$ . Let us now describe the way of determining these normals.

**1) Normal to the segment** ${P}_{0}$ ${P}_{1}$ **passing through** ${M}_{1}$

We determine the coordinates of a point ${M}_{1}^{'}$ by a rotation of 90° of the segment ${P}_{0}$ ${M}_{1}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{U}_{M{1}^{'}}={U}_{\mathit{M1}}+({V}_{\mathit{M1}}-{V}_{\mathit{P0}})\\ {V}_{M{1}^{'}}={V}_{\mathit{M1}}+({U}_{\mathit{P0}}-{U}_{\mathit{M1}})\end{array}
$$

where ${U}_{k}$ and ${V}_{k}$ with $k=M{1}^{'},\mathit{M1},\mathit{P0}$ represent the components $u$ and $v$ of the points ${M}_{1}^{'}$ , ${M}_{1}$ and ${P}_{0}$ . We deduce from them the constants ${a}_{0}$ and ${b}_{0}$ of the straight line representing the normal to the segment ${P}_{0}$ ${P}_{1}$ passing through ${M}_{1}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{a}_{0}=({V}_{M{1}^{'}}-{V}_{\mathit{M1}})/({U}_{M{1}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathit{M1}})\\ {b}_{0}=({U}_{M{1}^{'}}{V}_{\mathit{M1}}-{U}_{\mathit{M1}}{V}_{M{1}^{'}})/({U}_{M{1}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathit{M1}})\end{array}
$$

**In the particular case** where $({U}_{\mathit{M1}'}-{U}_{\mathit{M1}})=0$ , we force ${a}_{0}$ and ${b}_{0}$ to zero and we obtain the coordinates of the centre $O$ of the circle circumscribed about the points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ by a specific method described in r7.04.04-cas-particuliers.

**2) Normal to the segment** ${P}_{0}$ ${P}_{2}$ **passing through** ${M}_{0}$

We determine the coordinates of a point $\mathit{M0}'$ by a rotation of 90° of the segment ${P}_{0}$ ${M}_{0}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{U}_{\mathit{M0}'}={U}_{\mathit{M0}}+({V}_{\mathit{M0}}-{V}_{\mathit{P0}})\\ {V}_{\mathit{M0}'}={V}_{\mathit{M0}}+({U}_{\mathit{P0}}-{U}_{\mathit{M0}})\end{array}
$$

where ${U}_{k}$ and ${V}_{k}$ with $k=\mathit{M0}',\mathit{M0},\mathit{P0}$ represent the components $u$ and $v$ of the points $\mathit{M0}'$ , ${M}_{0}$ and ${P}_{0}$ . We deduce from them the constants ${a}_{1}$ and ${b}_{1}$ of the straight line representing the normal to the segment ${P}_{0}$ ${P}_{2}$ passing through ${M}_{0}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{a}_{1}=({V}_{\mathit{M0}'}-{V}_{\mathit{M0}})/({U}_{\mathit{M0}'}-{U}_{\mathit{M0}})\\ {b}_{1}=({U}_{\mathit{M0}'}{V}_{\mathit{M0}}-{U}_{\mathit{M0}}{V}_{\mathit{M0}'})/({U}_{\mathit{M0}'}-{U}_{\mathit{M0}})\end{array}
$$

**In the particular case** where $({U}_{\mathit{M0}'}-{U}_{\mathit{M0}})=0$ , we force ${a}_{1}$ and ${b}_{1}$ to zero and we obtain the coordinates of the centre $O$ of the circle circumscribed about the points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ by a specific method described in r7.04.04-cas-particuliers.

**3) Normal to the segment** ${P}_{1}$ ${P}_{2}$ **passing through** ${M}_{2}$

We determine the coordinates of a point ${M}_{2}^{'}$ by a rotation of 90° of the segment ${P}_{1}$ ${M}_{2}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{U}_{\mathit{M2}'}={U}_{\mathit{M2}}+({V}_{\mathit{M2}}-{V}_{\mathit{P1}})\\ {V}_{M{2}^{'}}={V}_{\mathit{M2}}+({U}_{\mathit{P1}}-{U}_{\mathit{M2}})\end{array}
$$

where ${U}_{k}$ and ${V}_{k}$ with $k=\mathit{M2}',\mathit{M2},\mathit{P1}$ represent the components $u$ and $v$ of the points ${M}_{2}^{'}$ , ${M}_{2}$ and ${P}_{1}$ . We deduce from them the constants ${a}_{2}$ and ${b}_{2}$ of the straight line representing the normal to the segment ${P}_{1}$ ${P}_{2}$ passing through ${M}_{2}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{}{a}_{2}=({V}_{M{2}^{'}}-{V}_{\mathrm{M2}})/({U}_{M{2}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M2}})\\ {b}_{2}=({U}_{M{2}^{'}}{V}_{\mathrm{M2}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M2}}{V}_{M{2}^{'}})/({U}_{M{2}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M2}})\end{array}
$$

**In the particular case** where $({U}_{\mathit{M2}'}-{U}_{\mathit{M2}})=0$ , we force ${a}_{2}$ and ${b}_{2}$ to zero and we obtain the coordinates of the centre $O$ of the circle circumscribed about the points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ by a specific method described in r7.04.04-cas-particuliers.

**Step 3**

In the general case, we deduce from the constants ${a}_{0}$ , ${b}_{0}$ , ${a}_{1}$ , ${b}_{1}$ , ${a}_{2}$ and ${b}_{2}$ the coordinates of the centre $O$ of the circle circumscribed about the points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ in three different ways. Let us denote by ${O}_{0}$ , ${O}_{1}$ and ${O}_{2}$ the same centre $O$ , obtained in three different ways, and by ${U}_{k}$ and ${V}_{k}$ , where $k={O}_{0},{O}_{1},{O}_{2}$ , the components $u$ and $v$ of the points ${O}_{0}$ , ${O}_{1}$ and ${O}_{2}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{}{U}_{{O}_{0}}=({b}_{1}-{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{1})\\ {V}_{{O}_{0}}=({a}_{0}{b}_{1}-{a}_{1}{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{1})\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{}{U}_{{O}_{1}}=({b}_{2}-{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{2})\\ {V}_{{O}_{1}}=({a}_{0}{b}_{2}-{a}_{2}{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{2})\end{array}
$$

$$
\begin{array}{}{U}_{{O}_{2}}=({b}_{2}-{b}_{1})/({a}_{1}-{a}_{2})\\ {V}_{{O}_{2}}=({a}_{1}{b}_{2}-{a}_{2}{b}_{1})/({a}_{1}-{a}_{2})\end{array}
$$

After having checked that the equalities: ${U}_{{O}_{0}}\equiv {U}_{{O}_{1}}{U}_{{O}_{2}}$ and ${V}_{{O}_{0}}\equiv {V}_{{O}_{1}}{V}_{{O}_{2}}$ are satisfied we determine the radius of the circumscribed circle by computing the distance between $O$ and one of the three points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ or ${P}_{2}$ .

#### 5.2.2 Particular cases

In this paragraph we treat the three particular cases of step 2 of r7.04.04-cas-general.

**Particular case where** $({U}_{M{1}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M1}})=0$

In this case we immediately obtain the components $u$ and $v$ of the centre $O$ by:

$$
\begin{array}{}{U}_{O}={U}_{\mathrm{M1}}\\ {V}_{O}=({a}_{1}{b}_{2}-{a}_{2}{b}_{1})/({a}_{1}-{a}_{2})\end{array}
$$

**Particular case where** $({U}_{M{0}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M0}})=0$

Here the components $u$ and $v$ of the centre $O$ are given by:

$$
\begin{array}{}{U}_{0}={U}_{\mathrm{M0}}\\ {V}_{O}=({a}_{0}{b}_{2}-{a}_{2}{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{2})\end{array}
$$

**Particular case where** $({U}_{M{2}^{'}}-{U}_{\mathrm{M2}})=0$

In this last case, the $u$ and $v$ of the centre $O$ are given by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{U}_{0}={U}_{\mathit{M2}}\\ {V}_{O}=({a}_{0}{b}_{1}-{a}_{1}{b}_{0})/({a}_{0}-{a}_{1})\end{array}
$$

The value of the radius of the circumscribed circle is obtained in the same manner as in the general case; that is, by computing the distance between $O$ and one of the three points ${P}_{0}$ , ${P}_{1}$ or ${P}_{2}$ .

### 5.3 Criteria with critical planes

In this paragraph we give the list of the criteria with critical planes, cf., which are programmed, together with a brief description.

Notation:

| $n\ast$ | normal to the plane in which the shear amplitude is maximum; |
| --- | --- |
| $\Delta \tau (n)$ | shear amplitude in a plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ ; |
| ${N}_{\max}(n)$ | maximum normal stress on the plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ during the cycle; |
| ${\tau}_{0}$ | endurance limit in alternating pure shear; |
| ${d}_{0}$ | endurance limit in alternating pure tension–compression; |
| ${N}_{m}(n)$ | mean normal stress on the plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ during the cycle; |
| ${\varepsilon}_{\max}(n)$ | maximum normal strain on the plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ during the cycle; |
| ${\varepsilon}_{m}(n)$ | mean normal strain on the plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ during the cycle; |
| $P$ | hydrostatic pressure; |
| ${c}_{p}$ | harmful effect of pre-hardening in strain control, ${c}_{p}\ge 1$ . |

**MATAKE criterion**

$$
\frac{\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })}{2}+a{N}_{\max}(n\mathrm{\ast })\le b
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are two constants given by the user; they depend on the material characteristics and are equal to:

$a=({\tau}_{0}-\frac{{d}_{0}}{2})/\frac{{d}_{0}}{2}b={\tau}_{0}.$

Furthermore, we define an equivalent stress in the sense of MATAKE, denoted ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(n\mathrm{\ast })$ :

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(n\mathrm{\ast })=({c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })}{2}+a{N}_{\max}(n\mathrm{\ast }))\frac{f}{t},$

where $f/t$ represents the ratio of the endurance limits in alternating bending and torsion.

**DANG VAN criterion**

$$
\frac{\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })}{2}+aP\le b
$$

where $a$ and $b$ are two constants given by the user; they depend on the material characteristics and are equal to:

$a=\frac{3}{2}\times \frac{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{1}-\Delta {\sigma}_{2})-2{\sigma}_{m}}b=\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})+2{\sigma}_{m}}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{2}.$

Moreover, we define an equivalent stress in the sense of DANG VAN, denoted ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(n\mathrm{\ast })$ :

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(n\mathrm{\ast })=({c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau (n\mathrm{\ast })}{2}+aP)\frac{c}{t}$ ,

where $c/t$ represents the ratio of the endurance limits in alternating shear and tension.

### 5.4 Number of cycles to failure and damage

From ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(n\mathrm{\ast })$ and a Wöhler curve we deduce the number of cycles to failure: $N(n\mathrm{\ast })$ , then the damage corresponding to one cycle: $D(n\mathrm{\ast })=1/N(n\mathrm{\ast })$ .

## 6 Variable-amplitude criteria

The variable-amplitude criteria are implemented when the loading is not periodic. When the loading is not periodic it is necessary to decompose the loading path undergone by the structure into elementary sub-cycles by means of a cycle-counting method. In the case where the loading is non-radial there is no proven multiaxial counting method. Consequently we choose, as in the literature, to use the RAINFLOW counting method, which needs a scalar as input. This is why we reduce to one dimension the shear traction, which is the orthogonal projection of the stress vector onto a plane, by projecting the tip of the shear-traction vector onto one or two axes. Another important difference with the critical-plane criteria is that it is not the shear amplitude which makes it possible to select the critical plane but the damage accumulation which results from the elementary sub-cycles.

The projection method that we use is made explicit in chapters 7 and 8. In what follows we describe the way in which we have made the MATAKE and DANG VAN criteria evolve in order to adapt them to the cases where the loading is non-periodic.

### 6.1 Modified MATAKE criterion

In the context of damage accumulation and of a periodic loading, the MATAKE criterion is written in the following way:

$$
{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau ({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})}{2}+a{N}_{\max}({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})
$$

where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ represents the equivalent stress in the sense of the MATAKE criterion and with:

| ${\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}}$ | normal to the plane for which the shear amplitude is maximum; |
| --- | --- |
| $\Delta \tau ({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})/2$ | maximum shear half-amplitude; |
| $a$ | constant which can be defined by an alternating pure-shear test and an alternating tension–compression test, or by an alternating tension–compression test and a non-alternating tension–compression test; |
| ${N}_{\max}({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})$ | maximum normal stress on the plane of normal ${\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}}$ during the cycle; |
| ${c}_{p}$ | harmful effect of pre-hardening in strain control ${c}_{p}\ge 1$ . |

In order to compute the cumulated damage in the case where the loading is non-periodic, the first step consists in determining the shear traction (shear vector) in a plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ at all the instants of the loading. The technique which is used to do this is described in the reference. In the second step we begin by reducing the history of the shear traction to a one-dimensional function of time by projecting the tip of the shear-traction vector onto one or two axes defined in the plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ under consideration, cf. chapters 7 and 8. Thus the evolution of the projected shear traction reduces to the relation: ${\tau}_{p}=f(t)$ , which makes it possible to use the RAINFLOW counting method. In r7.04.04-fig-6-1 we show the values reached by the tip of the shear vector in a plane of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ before projection onto one axis or two axes, and in r7.04.04-fig-6-2 these same values after projection onto one axis. At this stage we must introduce the notion of elementary equivalent stress ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}$ . Practically this notion has the same meaning as the notion of equivalent stress defined by the relation, but it applies to the elementary sub-cycles coming from the RAINFLOW counting method. Therefore from the projected shear traction ${\tau}_{p}$ we compute elementary equivalent stresses ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}$ .

*Tips of the shear-traction vector before projection*

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/points_vecteur_cission_apres_projection.png)

*Tips of the shear-traction vector after projection onto one axis*

The RAINFLOW method decomposes ${\tau}_{p}=f(t)$ into periodic elementary sub-cycles and breaks the loading history, as we show in r7.04.04-fig-6-3. Thus, for a given normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ the RAINFLOW method supplies for each elementary sub-cycle two values, high and low points, of the tip of the shear-traction vector ${\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ and ${\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ associated with two values of maximum normal stress ${N}_{1}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ and ${N}_{2}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ .

For the MATAKE criterion we define the elementary equivalent stress in the following manner:

$$
{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})={c}_{p}\frac{\text{Max}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))-\text{Min}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))}{2}+a\text{Max}({N}_{1}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{N}_{2}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),0)
$$

For the damage accumulation, this elementary equivalent stress is to be used with a fatigue curve in shear. If one uses a fatigue curve in tension–compression one must multiply by a corrective coefficient which corresponds to the ratio of the endurance limits in alternating bending and torsion and which we denote $\alpha$ :

$$
{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\text{Max}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))-\text{Min}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))}{2}+a\text{Max}({N}_{1}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{N}_{2}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),0))
$$

From ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ and a Wöhler curve we deduce the number of cycles to failure ${N}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ and the elementary damage ${D}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})=1/{N}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})$ corresponding to an elementary sub-cycle. We use a linear damage accumulation. Let $k$ be the number of elementary sub-cycles; for a fixed normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ , the cumulated damage is equal to:

$$
D(\overrightarrow{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{D}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})
$$

In order to determine the normal vector ${\overrightarrow{n}}^{\mathrm{\ast }}$ corresponding to the maximum cumulated damage it is sufficient to vary $\overrightarrow{n}$ and to compute. The normal vector ${\overrightarrow{n}}^{\mathrm{\ast }}$ corresponding to the maximum cumulated damage is then given by:

$D({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})=\underset{\overrightarrow{n}}{\text{Max}}(D(\overrightarrow{n}))$

### 6.2 Modified DANG VAN criterion

Within the framework of damage and of a periodic loading, the DANG VAN criterion is written:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})={c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau ({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})}{2}+aP$

where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ represents the equivalent stress in the sense of the DANG VAN criterion and with:

| ${\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}}$ | normal to the plane for which the shear amplitude is maximum; |
| --- | --- |
| $\Delta \tau ({\overrightarrow{n}}^{\text{*}})/2$ | maximum shear half-amplitude; |
| $a$ | constant which can be defined by an alternating pure-shear test and an alternating tension–compression test, or by an alternating tension–compression test and a non-alternating tension–compression test; |
| $P$ | maximum hydrostatic pressure during the cycle; |
| ${c}_{p}$ | harmful effect of pre-hardening in strain control ${c}_{p}\ge 1$ . |

When the loading is non-periodic, we compute the damage by the same procedure as that used for the MATAKE criterion. The only difference lies in the definition of the elementary equivalent stress:

$$
{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})=\frac{\text{Max}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))-\text{Min}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))}{2}+a\text{Max}({P}_{1}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{P}_{2}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),0)
$$

where ${P}_{1}^{i}$ and ${P}_{2}^{i}$ represent the two values of the hydrostatic pressure attached to each elementary sub-cycle. This elementary equivalent stress is to be used with a fatigue curve in shear. If one must use a fatigue curve in tension–compression it is necessary to multiply by the corrective coefficient $\alpha$ :

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\text{Max}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))-\text{Min}({\tau}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\tau}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))}{2}+a\text{Max}({P}_{1}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{P}_{2}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),0))$

After having defined the MATAKE and DANG-VAN criteria within the framework of damage accumulation and of a non-periodic loading, it remains for us to specify the projection technique that we propose.

### 6.3 Modified FATEMI-SOCIE criterion

#### 6.3.1 Description

The criterion of FATEMI and SOCIE is a criterion of critical-plane type. Initially formulated for periodic loadings, we propose a version adapted to non-periodic loadings.

In this criterion the parameter $a$ is defined as follows: $a=k/{\sigma}_{y}$ where ${\sigma}_{y}$ is the yield stress and $k$ a coefficient which depends on the material. We shall return to the way of computing $k$ . This criterion mixes the shear strain and the maximum normal stress. We propose to define an “elementary” equivalent strain in the following manner:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n})=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\text{Max}({\gamma}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\gamma}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))-\text{Min}({\gamma}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{\gamma}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}))}{2}\left[1+a\text{Max}({N}_{{1}_{}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),{N}_{{2}_{}}^{i}(\overrightarrow{n}),0)\right])$

where ${\gamma}_{{p}_{1}}^{i}$ and ${\gamma}_{{p}_{2}}^{i}$ represent the extreme shear strains of sub-cycle number $i$ .

Apart from a different definition of the criterion, the procedure used to compute the damage is identical to the two previous criteria. Finally, it is likewise the maximum damage which makes it possible to select the critical plane.

**One will note that the shear strains used in the criterion of FATEMI and of SOCIE are engineering shear strains** ${\gamma}_{ij}$ ($i\ne j$ ) **. If one uses shear strains of tensorial type** ${ϵ}_{ij}$ ($i\ne j$ ) **, one must multiply them by a factor 2 because** ${\gamma}_{ij}=2{ϵ}_{ij}$ **.**

#### 6.3.2 Identification of the coefficient k

The author proposes to identify the coefficient $k$ from tests in pure tension–compression and in pure alternating torsion on a thin tube. In order not to introduce any bias, the two kinds of test must be carried out on the same type of specimen. Before presenting the formula which defines $k$ we introduce the following notation:

| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio (generally equal to $0,3$ for our materials); |
| --- | --- |
| ${\nu}_{p}$ | coefficient of incompressibility of the plastic strains (equal to $0,5$ in and); |
| $E$ | Young's modulus of elasticity; |
| $G$ | Elastic shear modulus; |
| ${N}_{f}$ | Number of cycles to failure. |

## 7 Choice of the projection axes

As regards the projection of the tip of the shear-traction vector we propose two options:

- a projection onto one axis,
- a projection onto two axes.

The axis of option 1 is determined in the same way as the first axis of option 2. The second axis of option 2 is orthogonal to the first axis of this option.

### 7.1 Projection onto one axis

We place ourselves in a plane of given normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ where each point represents the position of the tip of the shear vector at an instant; for more details see the reference. In this plane we construct the smallest frame which contains all the points representing the tip of the shear-traction vector at each instant. The two diagonals of the frame allow us to define two axes: axis 1 corresponds to the segment $\overline{\text{AC}}$ , and axis 2 corresponds to the segment $\overline{\text{DB}}$ , cf. r7.04.04-fig-7-1.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/Object_564.svg)

*Definition of the projection axes*

We choose a priori the projection axis among axes 1 and 2 because the diagonal of the frame is larger than the long side of the frame, which has the virtue of dilating the projected points a little. On the other hand the loadings which interest us are of thermal origin, which means that the points representing the evolution of the tip of the shear-traction vector, in the planes of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ , are most often aligned on an axis, as we show in r7.04.04-fig-6-1.

Sectors 1, 2, 3 and 4 are constructed in the same manner as in the reference. Only the points which lie in these sectors are projected orthogonally onto axes 1 and 2.

We define the projection axis as being the axis on which the distance between two projected points is the greatest.

For example, in r7.04.04-fig-7-1 the projection axis is axis 1 since the length of the segment $\overline{{P}_{3}{P}_{4}}$ is greater than the length of the segment $\overline{{P}_{1}{P}_{2}}$ . This definition of the projection axis makes it possible to ensure that the projection axis retained will make it possible to account for the greatest projected shear amplitude.

Depending on the presence or the absence of points in sectors 1, 2, 3 and 4 the determination of the projection axis can be immediate; it is then not necessary to implement the selection procedure described above. For more details the reader may refer to appendix 1.

A second axis is necessary in order to distinguish the case where the points representing the tip of the shear-traction vector are aligned on an axis from the case where these points describe a circle.

### 7.2 Construction of the second axis

The second projection axis is orthogonal to the initial projection axis and it passes through the point $O$ .

Since we know the coordinates of the points $A$ , $B$ , $C$ and $D$ , in order to characterise the second axis completely it is sufficient to determine the coordinates of a point $M$ such that:

$\overrightarrow{\text{DB}}.\overrightarrow{\text{OM}}=0$ if the initial axis is axis 1,

$\overrightarrow{\text{AC}}.\overrightarrow{\text{OM}}=0$ if the initial axis is axis 2.

## 8 Projection of the shear

In this chapter we describe the procedure of projection onto the initial axis, or first axis, and the second axis. We recall that the projection onto these two axes is orthogonal.

### 8.1 Case where axis 1 is the initial axis

This case is represented in r7.04.04-fig-8-1. Let us place ourselves in the frame $(O,\overrightarrow{u},\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{n})$ . The definitions of $\overrightarrow{u}$ , $\overrightarrow{v}$ and $\overrightarrow{n}$ are given in the reference. In the plane $(\overrightarrow{u},\overrightarrow{v})$ of normal $\overrightarrow{n}$ the points $A$ , $B$ , $C$ , $D$ and $O$ have respectively, as coordinates, $({U}_{\min},{V}_{\max})$ , $({U}_{\max},{V}_{\max})$ , $({U}_{\max},{V}_{\min})$ , $({U}_{\min},{V}_{\min})$ and $({U}_{O},{V}_{O})$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/projection_cas_axe_1.png)

*Projection in the case where axis 1 is the initial axis*

#### 8.1.1 Determination of the second axis

Here, in order to determine the second axis, we solve the equation:

$$
\overrightarrow{\text{DB}}.\overrightarrow{\text{OM}}=0
$$

where the coordinates ${U}_{M},{V}_{M}$ of the point $M$ are the unknowns.

Equation is also written in the following form:

$({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})({U}_{M}-{U}_{O})+({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})({V}_{M}-{V}_{O})=0$

which leads to:

${V}_{M}={V}_{O}-\frac{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}{({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}({U}_{M}-{U}_{O})$

By giving oneself a value of ${U}_{M}$ different from ${U}_{O}$ we immediately obtain ${V}_{M}$ .

#### 8.1.2 Projection of an arbitrary point onto the initial axis

Starting from an arbitrary known point $P$ , the first step consists in computing the coordinates of a point $P'$ such that:

$\overrightarrow{\mathrm{DB}}.\overrightarrow{{\mathrm{PP}}^{'}}=0$

Proceeding as previously, we obtain the relation:

${V}_{{P}^{'}}={V}_{P}\frac{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}{({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}({U}_{{P}^{'}}-{U}_{P})$

where ${V}_{{P}^{'}}$ results from a value of ${U}_{{P}^{'}}$ different from ${U}_{P}$ .

In the plane $(u,v)$ the initial axis and the segment $\bar{\mathrm{PP}'}$ are affine straight lines respectively described by $\nu ={a}_{i}u+{b}_{i}$ and $\nu ={a}_{P}u+{b}_{P}$ , so in order to know the coordinates of the point projected onto the initial axis ${P}_{P}$ we solve the equation:

${a}_{i}u+{b}_{i}={a}_{P}u+{b}_{P}$

where

| ${a}_{i}=\frac{({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}$ , | ${b}_{i}=\frac{({U}_{\max}{V}_{\min}-{U}_{\min}{V}_{\max})}{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}$ , |
| --- | --- |
| ${a}_{P}=\frac{({V}_{{P}^{'}}-{V}_{P})}{({U}_{{P}^{'}}-{U}_{P})}$ , | ${b}_{P}=\frac{({U}_{P'}{V}_{P}-{U}_{P}{V}_{P'})}{({U}_{P'}-{U}_{P})}$ . |

One obtains:

${U}_{{P}_{i}}=\frac{{b}_{p}-{b}_{i}}{{a}_{i}-{a}_{p}}$

${V}_{{P}_{i}}=\frac{{a}_{i}{b}_{P}-{a}_{P}{b}_{i}}{{a}_{i}-{a}_{P}}$

The projection of an arbitrary point onto the second axis is described in appendix 2.

### 8.2 Case where axis 2 is the initial axis

This case is represented in r7.04.04-fig-8-2. As previously, in the plane $(\overrightarrow{u},\overrightarrow{v})$ the points $A$ , $B$ , $C$ , $D$ and $O$ have respectively, as coordinates, $({U}_{min}, {V}_{max})$ , $({U}_{max}, {V}_{max})$ , $({U}_{max}, {V}_{min})$ , $({U}_{min},{V}_{min})$ and $({U}_{0,}{V}_{0})$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.04/projection_cas_axe_2.png)

*Projection in the case where axis 2 is the initial axis*

#### 8.2.1 Determination of the second axis

Here, in order to determine the second axis, we solve the equation:

$$
\overrightarrow{\text{AC}}.\overrightarrow{\text{OM}}=0
$$

where the coordinates $({U}_{M},{V}_{M})$ of the point $M$ are the unknowns.

Equation is also written in the following form:

$({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})({U}_{\text{M}}-{V}_{\text{O}})({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})({V}_{\text{M}}-{V}_{\text{O}})=0$

which leads to:

${V}_{M}={V}_{O}+\frac{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}{({v}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}({U}_{M}-{U}_{O})$

By giving oneself a value of ${U}_{M}$ different from ${U}_{O}$ we immediately obtain ${V}_{M}$ .

#### 8.2.2 Projection of an arbitrary point onto the initial axis

Starting from an arbitrary known point $P$ , the first step consists in computing the coordinates of a point $P'$ such that:

$\overrightarrow{\mathrm{AC}}.\overrightarrow{\mathrm{PP}'}=0$

Proceeding as previously, we obtain the relation:

${V}_{P'}={V}_{P}\frac{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}{({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}({U}_{P'}-{U}_{P})$

where for a value of ${U}_{P'}$ different from ${U}_{P}$ we compute ${V}_{P'}$ .

In the plane $(u,v)$ the initial axis and the segment $\bar{\mathrm{PP}'}$ are affine straight lines respectively described by $\nu ={a}_{i}u+{b}_{i}$ and $\nu ={a}_{p}u+{b}_{p}$ , so in order to know the coordinates of the point projected onto the initial axis ${P}_{p}$ we solve the equation:

${a}_{i}u+{b}_{i}={a}_{p}u+{b}_{p}$

where

| ${a}_{i}=-\frac{({V}_{\max}-{V}_{\min})}{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}$ , | ${b}_{i}=\frac{({U}_{\max}{V}_{\max}-{U}_{\min}{V}_{\min})}{({U}_{\max}-{U}_{\min})}$ , |
| --- | --- |
| ${a}_{p}=\frac{({V}_{{P}^{'}}-{V}_{P})}{({U}_{{P}^{'}}-{U}_{P})}$ , | ${b}_{p}=\frac{({U}_{P'}{V}_{p}-{U}_{P}{V}_{P'})}{({U}_{P'}-{U}_{P})}$ . |

One obtains:

| ${U}_{{P}_{i}}=\frac{{b}_{P}-{b}_{i}}{{a}_{i}-{a}_{P}}$ , | ${V}_{{P}_{i}}=\frac{{a}_{i}{b}_{P}-{a}_{P}{b}_{i}}{{a}_{i}-{a}_{P}}$ . |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

The projection of an arbitrary point onto the second axis is described in appendix 2.

### 8.3 Definition of the modulus and of the orientation of the projection axis

We propose to define the sign of the modulus of the projected point with respect to the initial axis. Let $(O,\overrightarrow{u},\overrightarrow{v},\overrightarrow{n})$ be the frame in which the shear traction evolves. In this frame if the component ${U}_{{P}_{i}}$ of the projected point is greater than or equal to zero the sign of the modulus is positive, otherwise it is negative. In summary the modulus and the sign of the modulus of the projected point are defined in the following manner:

$\begin{array}{cc}{P}_{\text{mod}}=\sqrt{{\overline{{\text{OP}}_{i}}}^{2}+{\overline{{\text{OP}}_{s}}}^{2}}& \text{si}\quad {U}_{{P}_{i}}\ge 0,\\ {P}_{\text{mod}}=-\sqrt{{\overline{{\text{OP}}_{i}}}^{2}+{\overline{{\text{OP}}_{s}}}^{2}}& \text{si}\quad {U}_{{P}_{i}}<0.\end{array}$

The definition of the modulus distinguishes affine loadings from circular loadings. In accordance with experiment a circular loading will be considered as being more damaging than an affine loading.

## 9 Formula criteria

### 9.1 For the periodic loading

*For the periodic loading, the computation of the damage is carried out only on the first complete cycle. The first part of the loading history corresponding to the monotonic loading is not taken into account because the latter has the objective of imposing a non-zero mean loading. For the elastic behaviour, the computation is carried out between the maximum value and the minimum value of the cycle under consideration. For the elastoplastic behaviour, the computation is carried out* *between the first unloading and the second unloading.*

## 11 Other criteria

### 11.1 Criterion VMIS_TRESCA

In this part we describe the option VMIS_TRESCA which makes it possible to compute the maximum variation, over time, of a stress tensor according to the Von Mises and Tresca criteria.

This computation can be carried out at the nodes or at the Gauss points according to the user's request.

We give below the algorithm which is programmed in *Code_Aster* .

**Notation:**

$N$ : Number of instants

${T}_{i}$ : Tensor at instant $i$

${\mathit{DIFF}}_{T}$ : difference between two tensors

$\mathit{VAVMIS}=0.0$

$\mathit{VATRES}=0.0$

For $i$ from $1$ to $(N-1)$

> For $j$ from $(i+1)$ to $N$

> ${\mathit{DIFF}}_{T}={T}_{i}-{T}_{j}$
> $\mathit{VMIS}=$ Von Mises of ${\mathit{DIFF}}_{T}$
> $\mathit{TRES}=$ Tresca of ${\mathit{DIFF}}_{T}$
> If $\mathit{VMIS}>\mathit{VAVMIS}$ then

> $\mathit{VAVMIS}=\mathit{VMIS}$

> If $\mathit{TRES}>\mathit{VATRES}$ then

> $\mathit{VATRES}=\mathit{TRES}$

### 11.2 Components of Code_Aster used

The field computed by CALC_FATIGUE has as components:

| VAVMIS | Maximum amplitude of variation of the Von Mises criterion |
| --- | --- |
| VATRES | Maximum amplitude of variation of the Tresca criterion |

### 15.2 Appendix 2

The projection of an arbitrary point onto the second axis is briefly described in this appendix. Starting from an arbitrary known point $P$ , we compute the coordinates of a point $P'$ such that:

$\overrightarrow{\mathrm{OM}}.\overrightarrow{{\mathrm{PP}}^{'}}=0$

After simplification one arrives at the relation:

where a value of ${U}_{{P}^{'}}$ different from ${U}_{P}$ gives us ${V}_{{P}^{'}}$ .

In the plane $(u,v)$ the second axis and the segment are affine straight lines respectively described by $v = a_s u + b_s \quad \text{et} \quad v = a_p u + b_p$ , so in order to know the coordinates of the point projected onto the second axis ${P}_{P}$

we solve the equation:

$a_s u + b_s = a_p u + b_p$

where

| ${a}_{s}=\frac{({V}_{M}-{V}_{O})}{({U}_{M}-{U}_{O})}$ , | ${b}_{s}=\frac{({U}_{M}{V}_{O}-{U}_{O}{V}_{M})}{({U}_{M}-{U}_{O})}$ , |
| --- | --- |
| ${a}_{P}=\frac{({V}_{{P}^{'}}-{V}_{P})}{({U}_{{P}^{'}}-{U}_{P})}$ , | . |

One obtains:

$$
U_{P_s} = \frac{b_P - b_s}{a_s - a_P}, \qquad
V_{P_s} = \frac{a_s b_P - a_P b_s}{a_s - a_p}.
$$
