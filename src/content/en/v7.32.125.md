# v7.32.125 WTNP125 - Expansion of a tank

Summary:

The test presented here allows to verify the correct functioning of the elements joints with hydromechanical coupling in saturated medium.

The depletion of a reservoir (reduction of the size of a deposit due to its operation) is modelled. It is initially completely crossed by a discontinuity and contains a single liquid. The constitutive laws interface used are the cubic law for flow and the law of Bandis for mechanics.

## 1 Reference problem

The test case proposed by Guiducci et al.

### 1.1 Geometry

A separate container in two equal parts is considered by a horizontal discontinuity $[\mathrm{EF}]$.

### 1.2 Material properties

- Properties of interstitial fluid (liquid water):

| Density | $1000{\mathrm{kg.m}}^{-3}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Viscosity | ${1.10}^{-3}\mathrm{Pa.s}$ |
| Compressibility | ${3.10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$ |

- Properties of the rock matrix:

The matrix is elastic and has the following properties:

| Young's modulus | $200\mathrm{MPa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $0,25$ |
| Porosity | $0,4055$ |
| Intrinsic permeability | $1,688{.10}^{-17}{m}^{2}$ |

- Discontinuity properties:

The constitutive laws of the discontinuity is detailed in the [R7.02.15](doc:r7.02.15) documentation.

The mechanical behavior of discontinuity is given the law of Bandis.

$\varepsilon$ is noted the opening of the crack, ${U}_{\max}$ the asymptronic opening under zero stress and $U={U}_{\max}-\varepsilon$ the closing of the crack.

In the normal direction at the crack, the

$$
d\sigma {'}_{n}=-{K}_{ni}\frac{\mathrm{dU}}{{(1-\frac{U}{{U}_{\max}})}^{\gamma}}
$$

In the tangential direction, we have

$$
\sigma {'}_{t}={K}_{t}〚{u}_{t}〛
$$

| Initial normal rigidity ${K}_{ni}$ | ${1.10}^{9}{\mathrm{Pa.m}}^{-1}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Asymptotic opening ${U}_{\max}$ | $2,17\mathrm{mm}$ |
| $\gamma$ coefficient | $2$ |
| Tangential Rigidity ${K}_{\text{t}}$ | ${1.10}^{12}{\mathrm{Pa.m}}^{-1}$ |

Flow into the crack is given by cubic law.

### 1.3 Initial conditions

The initial conditions are as follows:

- ${\varepsilon}_{0}$ initial opening: $3,04{.10}^{-4}m$
- initial pressure in the massif: $48,7\mathrm{MPa}$
- compression stress in both directions of the plane: $62\mathrm{MPa}$

### 1.4 boundary conditions

The mechanical and hydraulic boundary conditions are given by the figures at .

On $[\mathrm{AB}]$: $y$ blocked movements and zero hydraulic flow

On $[\mathrm{BC}]$: $x$ blocked movements and zero hydraulic flow

On $[\mathrm{CD}]$: mechanical pressure of $62\mathrm{MPa}$ and zero hydraulic flow

$[\mathrm{DA}]$: $62\mathrm{MPa}$ mechanical pressure and ${p}^{\text{*}}$ pressure imposed

Note: mechanical pressure is applied only on the massif (not on the crack)

The ${p}^{\text{*}}$ pressure evolution imposed by the $x=0$ well is as follows (see also figure ):

- Linear decrease from $48,7\mathrm{MPa}$ to $33,7\mathrm{MPa}$ during the first $7,5$ years of operation
- Maintains $33,7\mathrm{MPa}$ for the following $12,5$ years.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics A

The overall marker is chosen so that the discontinuity is horizontal in this marker ($\alpha =0°$ so an Euler angle of $90°$ ).

The Modelling is performed in flat deformation with 1092 elements QU4 for the solid and 91 elements QU4 for discontinuity.

Time Discretization:

- 25 time step for the first $7,5$ years
- 25 time step for the next $12,5$ years.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

To verify that the results are independent of the discontinuity orientation (given by the Euler angles defined in AFFE_CARA_ELEM), $A$ modelling is repeated with a rotation of $\alpha =-90°$. The direct marker defined by normal and the tangent at crack is then identical to the global marker. Euler angle is therefore equal to zero.
