# v2.04.131 SDLV131 - Simulation of a strain gauge by the OBSERVATION command

**Summary:**

This test validates the operation of the calculation of the average value of a deformation field on a given entity by the user. Thus, the deformation field that would have been measured by a deformation gauge is estimated. This calculation is performed via the OBSERVATION macro-command. The case treated is a single tensile beam modelled by elements density.

This test case also validates the observation of mixed fields: one call to the OBSERVATION command for the field reading of different nature (DEPL, VITE, ...).

Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

${u}_{z}=0.0$ displacement is imposed on nodess on the centre lines of the side faces.

${F}_{B}$ is applied to the $\mathrm{surf2}$ face in the $x$ direction,

${F}_{B}=1000.N/{m}^{2}$ ,

These boundary conditions provide simple traction beam behavior.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The deformation is estimated from the relative lengthening of the beam.

The length extension of a $L$ beam following a longitudinal force $F$ is written:

$\Delta L=\frac{FL}{ES}$

In our case, one force per unit surface ${F}_{B}$ is applied to the free end of the beam, so the relative lengthening of the beam is as follows:

$\frac{\Delta L}{L}={\epsilon}_{xx}=\frac{{F}_{B}}{E}$

For this test case, the results of a static calculation, a harmonic calculation, a transient calculation and a modal calculation are calculated.

For the static case, one obtains:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\frac{{F}_{B}}{E}$     et    ${\varepsilon}_{yy}={\varepsilon}_{zz}=-\nu {\varepsilon}_{xx}$

For dynamic cases, the system is governed by the following equation:

$M\frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial {t}^{2}}+Ku={F}_{\mathrm{ext}}$

For a traction beam - compression, if one model contains only one element, the $M$ mass and $K$ rigidity matrices are as follows:

$M=\frac{\rho SL}{6}\left[\begin{array}{cc}2& 1\\ 1& 2\end{array}\right]$ $K=\frac{\mathrm{ES}}{L}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]$ with: $u=\left[\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}\\ {u}_{2}\end{array}\right]$

${u}_{1}$ and ${u}_{2}$ are the nodes moves of the element.

In the harmonic response of $\omega$ pulse, nodes displacements of element are governed by the following relationship:

$\frac{\mathrm{ES}}{L}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}\\ {u}_{2}\end{array}\right]-{\omega}^{2}\frac{\rho SL}{6}\left[\begin{array}{cc}2& 1\\ 1& 2\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}\\ {u}_{2}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{F}_{1}\\ {F}_{2}\end{array}\right]$

Using the second line of this relationship, and applying the boundary conditions (${u}_{1}=0$ and ${F}_{2}=F={F}_{B}S$), we obtain:

${u}_{2}=\frac{{F}_{B}}{\frac{E}{L}-{\omega}^{2}\frac{\rho L}{3}}=\Delta L$

The deformation at the free end of the beam is written:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\frac{\Delta L}{L}=\frac{{F}_{B}}{E-{\omega}^{2}\frac{\rho {L}^{2}}{3}}$     et     ${\varepsilon}_{yy}={\varepsilon}_{zz}=-\nu {\varepsilon}_{xx}$

The reference solution for the transient solution can be obtained in the same way. If an $F(t)=Ft={F}_{B}St$ longitudinal force is applied with an initial equilibrium beam condition (zero initial displacement and zero initial speed), it is obtained:

${u}_{2}(t)=\frac{3{F}_{B}}{\rho L{\omega}_{0}^{2}}\left[t-\frac{\sin({\omega}_{0}t)}{{\omega}_{0}}\right]=\Delta L$ with : ${{\omega}_{0}}^{2}=\frac{3E}{\rho {L}^{2}}$

And the deformation at the free end of the beam is written:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\frac{\Delta L}{L}=\frac{3{F}_{B}}{\rho {L}^{2}{\omega}_{0}^{2}}\left[t-\frac{\sin({\omega}_{0}t)}{{\omega}_{0}}\right]$     et     ${\varepsilon}_{yy}={\varepsilon}_{zz}=-\nu {\varepsilon}_{xx}$

In the case of the modal calculation, a test of non-regression shall be performed on the deformations calculated at the middle point of the beam.

A 90 degree rotation is also simulated to verify the change of the reference in OBSERVATION.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The average deformation value is tested on the surfaces: $\mathrm{surf3}$ , $\mathrm{surf4}$ , $\mathrm{surf5}$ and $\mathrm{surf6}$ . The results are then projected on the "measurement" model which includes only the nodes $\mathrm{P3}$ , $\mathrm{P4}$ , $\mathrm{P5}$ and $\mathrm{P6}$ associated with the $\mathrm{surf3}$ , $\mathrm{surf4}$ , $\mathrm{surf5}$ and $\mathrm{surf6}$ surfaces.

For the validation of the static solution, we chose: ${F}_{B}=1000.N/{m}^{2}$

For the validation of the harmonic solution, we chose: ${F}_{B}=1000.(1+\mathrm{2j})N/{m}^{2}$ and: $\omega =2\pi 200\mathrm{rd}{s}^{-1}$

For the validation of the transient solution, we chose: ${F}_{B}=1000.tN/{m}^{2}$ and the solution is tested at the moment $t=1s$

Field values obtained by mixed observation are also tested.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Analytical solution for static, harmonic and transient cases.

A solution of non-regression is proposed for modal deformation.

[R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) elements "exact" beams (right and curved).

## 7 Summary of the results

The purpose of these tests is to verify the correct calculation of the mean deformity field value using OBSERVATION macro-command. The reference solution is analytical for static response, harmonic response and transient response.

This test case also validates the mixed field observation (DEPL, VITE, ...) with a single call to the OBSERVATION operator.

Differences between Aster solutions and analytical solutions are very small. For results from a modal calculation, a non-regression test is proposed.
