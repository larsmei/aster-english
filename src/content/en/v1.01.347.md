# v1.01.347 ZZZZ347 – Validation of the CABLE_GAINE rubbing cables

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate CABLE_GAINE prestress cable modelling.

Modelling A is used to validate the use of DEFI_CABLE_BP/CALC_PRECONT with elements BARRE in the absence of friction in curved cases, and serves as a reference to validate the CABLE_GAINE Modelling in the ADHERENT case, tested in Modelling B.

Modelling C validates CABLE_GAINE Modelling in the GLISSANT case.

The D Modelling validates the use of DEFI_CABLE_BP/CALC_PRECONT with elements BARRE in the presence of friction. It also serves as a reference to Modelling E, which validates the CABLE_GAINE Modelling in the case of FROTTANT, with consideration for linear and curved friction, in the case of only one or both active anchors, and finally, with and without anchoring back.

Modellings F and G are copies of the E Modelling with the inshell modeled concrete (DKT and Q4GG), and are used to validate the new prestressing procedure with the hull elements.

Finally, H Modelling tests PETIT_REAC kinematics in comparison with elements BARRE.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

In the case of a slippery cable without friction (modelling C), with concrete and cable sheath clamped (only the slip of the cable is then allowed), the following analytical solution is displayed.

The imposed slip is noted $\stackrel{̃}{g}$ , the length of the cable $L$ , its section $A$

The deformation of the cable is uniform (no friction) and is then $\frac{\tilde{g}}{L}$ , the $E\frac{\tilde{g}}{L}$ stress and the $T=AE\frac{\tilde{g}}{L}$ voltage .

The reaction on the whole concrete is $R=\mathrm{2T}$.

On a cable section, the reaction is $S=T\Delta \alpha$ with $\Delta \alpha$ the angular deviation.

If the cable is discretized in $n$ elements of the same size, we have $S=T\frac{\pi}{n}$ .

For BPEL voltage calculations, analytical references are obtained using the formulae given in [R7.01.02](doc:r7.01.02).

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

#### 2.2.1 Voltage and reactions for Modelling C

Taking $\tilde{g}=0.1$ , $L=r\pi$ , $A=2.5e-3$ , $E=185000\mathit{MPa}$ , we find:

- $T=2.9443664{10}^{6}N$ voltage
- reaction on all $R=5.888734{10}^{6}N$ concrete and $S=462500N$ cable cross section with $n=20$ .

#### 2.2.2 BPEL voltage for modellings E, F and G

The Gauss points tested are the Gauss $1$ points of the mesh cells $105$ and $96$ for Modelling E corresponding to the mesh cells $59$ and $50$ for the modellings F and G. All point $P$ owned by the cable can be defined by the $a=\stackrel{ˆ}{\overrightarrow{({\mathit{OA}}_{1},\mathit{OP})}}$ or $b=\stackrel{ˆ}{\overrightarrow{({\mathit{OA}}_{2},\mathit{OP})}}$ angle value where $O$ is the center of the semi-circle, ${A}_{1}$ is ANCR1 and ${A}_{2}$ is ANCR2.

The first node of the mesh cell $96$ is defined by $a=67,5$ or $b=112,5$ . The first point of the mesh cell $96$ is then defined by ${a}_{96}=67,5+\frac{4,5}{\sqrt{(3)}}$ or ${b}_{96}=112,5-\frac{4,5}{\sqrt{(3)}}$ .

The first node of the mesh cell $105$ is defined by $a=148,5$ or $b=31,5$ . The first point of the mesh cell $105$ is then defined by ${a}_{96}=148,5+\frac{4,5}{\sqrt{(3)}}$ or ${b}_{96}=31,5-\frac{4,5}{\sqrt{(3)}}$ .

The cumulative angular deviation $\alpha$ of these points is required to be calculated and used for the calculation of voltages. The cumulative angular deviation used in the BPEL formula of the voltage must be calculated from the nearest active anchor. In the E Modelling, two calculations are made: one with passive ANCR1 and active ANCR2 and the other with both active anchors. The mesh cell $105$ is closer to ANCR2, which is still active, so we have both: ${\alpha}_{105}={b}_{105}\mathrm{\ast }\pi /180$ .

We note that in our case the cumulative angular deviation is equal to the angles $a$ or $b$ , we only pass in radians.

The mesh cell $96$ is closer to ANCR1 so we have two different values:

${\alpha}_{96}={b}_{96}\mathrm{\ast }\pi /180$ for PASSIF/ACTIF

${\alpha}_{96}={a}_{96}\mathrm{\ast }\pi /180$ for ACTIF/ACTIF

The curvilinear abscisses of these points are also necessary for the calculation of the voltage, they are also given in relation to the nearest active anchor. We then have: ${s}_{105}={\alpha}_{105}\mathrm{\ast }r$ and ${s}_{96}={\alpha}_{96}\mathrm{\ast }r$ .

For the case without anchoring back, the formula ${F}_{c}={F}_{0}\exp(-f\alpha -\phi s)$ is used, with ${F}_{0}={10}^{6}N$ , $\phi =0,01$ ; $f=0,03$ , we obtain:

- for PASSIF/ACTIF: ${F}_{105}=960448.709086365N$ and ${F}_{96}=857741.905702382N$
- for ACTIF/ACTIF: ${F}_{105}=960448.709086365N$; ${F}_{96}=906761.8988894981N$

The EPSI_ELGA deformation field is tested in Modelling E in the case PASSIF/ACTIF, on the same Gauss points as the N voltage. The calculation is elastic, therefore:

${\mathit{EPXX}}_{105}={F}_{105}/E/A$

${\mathit{EPXX}}_{96}={F}_{96}/E/A$

For the case with the anchoring rearrangement, with the ratings of [R7.01.02](doc:r7.01.02), the distance $d$ on which the anchoring rearrangement acts is shown to be:

$d=\frac{-1}{f}\ln(1-\sqrt{(f\ast {E}_{a}\ast {S}_{a}\ast \Delta /{F}_{0})})$

With an $\Delta =5.{10}^{4}$ anchoring step, you get here $d=3.9222$. By keeping the ratings defined above, this means that an point is affected if $a<44,94$ or $b<44,94$. The mesh cell 105 is therefore affected by the modification due to the anchoring step, but not the mesh cell 96.

Using ${F}_{c}=\frac{{(F(d))}^{2}}{{F}_{0}}\exp(-f\alpha -\phi s)$ for mesh cell 105 and ${F}_{c}={F}_{0}\exp(-f\alpha -\phi s)$ for mesh cell 96, we obtain:

${F}_{105}=918367.3641803192$ ; ${F}_{96}=906761.8988894981$

Due to the load imposed, DEFORMATION = 'PETIT' would not have any constraints in the cable. However, DEFORMATION = 'PETIT_REAC' will have to show constraints. The reference solution in this case is obtained by calculating with elements BARRE.

## 11 Summary of the results

The results show that under the same assumptions, CABLE_GAINE Modelling gives the same results as BARRE Modelling, whether in the case of adhesion with curved friction or in the case of rubbing. In the latter case, it is found that the setting procedure set up gives the same profiles as those calculated by the BPEL formulae, including with regard to an anchoring step. The same results are also found between the two modellings with PETIT_REAC kinematics.

In addition, CABLE_GAINE Modelling in GLISSANT version is validated by an analytical test on tension and reactions in concrete and cable.

The new energizing procedure is operational with 3D or inshell modeled concrete (DKT or Q4GG Modelling).
