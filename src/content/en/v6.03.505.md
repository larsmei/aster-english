# v6.03.505 SSNP505 - Multi-cracked bi-crack plate with X-FEM

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the multi-crack approach with X-FEM and junction. A flat deformation plate is required in bi-crack and shear. Eight cracks are introduced in the centre of the plate, two of which are joined. RAFF_XFEM is used to refine around crack bottoms and CALC_G to calculate stress intensity factors.

A first test allows the contactless approach to be validated by comparing stress intensity factors with a semi-analytic solution. Then, in a second test, it is shown that the addition of contact, which corrects certain interpenetrations, influences the results.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The cracks are introduced with X-FEM using GROUP_MA_FISS and GROUP_MA_FOND operators' DEFI_FISS_XFEM operands. The points given in Table 1 allow for easy generation of gridded lines representing the cracks in Figure 1. The possible contact between crack lips is not taken into account to respect the free-flow stress hypothesis on the lips of the cracks in the reference solution.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh initial set consisting of 400 mesh cells type QUAD4 in Figure 3 is adapted to 3 iterations using RAFF_XFEM and MACR_ADAP_MAIL commands. The mesh final refined around the crack bottoms in Figure 4, consisting of 17105 mesh cells type QUAD4 and 2076 mesh cells type TRIA3, is obtained.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The characteristics are the same as those of Modelling A. However, the potential contact forces between crack lips via the DEFI_CONTACT operator are taken into account in order to correct interpenetrations. The free-flow stress hypothesis on crack lips made for the calculation of the reference solution is not respected for cracks where the contact is active.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh is the same as for Modelling A.

### 4.3 Tested quantities and results

The stress intensity factors on crack bottom points as for Modelling A are tested. The differences associated with the consideration of contact at certain points are not able to validate the approach with the semi-analytic solution in section 2.1. Therefore, an non-regression test is performed. However, the error obtained with respect to an exact solution should be of the same order as that obtained between the quantity and the A Modelling solution. Indeed, the kinematic approximation for the calculation of stress intensity factors is exactly the same for Modelling A and B. Only the contact forces on certain crack lips are added here compared to Modelling A.

## 5 Summary of the results

The first contact-free calculation validates the multi-crack approach with X-FEM junctions by comparing stress intensity factors to a semi-analytical reference solution (which does not take into account the crack force on the lips). However, interpenetrations are noted, although the structure is mainly required in traction. The correction made by the second calculation, with consideration of the contact, shows that the new values obtained for stress intensity factors can lead to slight deviations of the propagation directions [2].
