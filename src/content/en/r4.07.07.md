# r4.07.07 Identification of forces on a modal model

**Summary:**

The macro-command CALC_ESSAI brings together the Code_Aster features for analysis–test correlation. Within the scope of this documentation one describes the “force identification” feature, which is intended to make it possible to identify the forces applied to a structure in the form of an interspectrum, from the interspectrum of operating measurements, a modal model, identified or computed, and the choice of the location of the points of application of the forces.

## 1 Introduction, analysis hypotheses

This documentation describes the methods used in the macro-command CALC_ESSAI.

### 1.1 Statement of the problem

One considers a structure, which constitutes a dynamic system that one assumes to be **linear (hypothesis H1).**

One assumes that this structure is subjected to an excitation assumed to be random (typical example: turbulent fluid forces), but which one can describe perfectly by means of its PSD (power spectral density, see the document “Modelling of turbulent excitations” R4.07.02, section 2.1). This vibration is measured at a certain number of points of abscissae ${x}_{k}(1\le k\le \text{nmes})$ . The forces applied to the structure are, for their part, not measurable. One therefore wishes to recover the forces from the measurement. This is an inverse problem, for the solution of which it is necessary to take a great many precautions in order to obtain a result which, if it is not exact, is nevertheless relevant.

### 1.2 Hypothesis of modal behaviour

**The structure is assumed to be linear (H1)**, so one can describe its mechanical behaviour on a modal basis. Each mode i of the structure is defined by the following parameters:

- ${\omega}_{i}$ : natural angular frequency, ${f}_{i}$ associated natural frequency,
- ${\xi}_{i}$ : reduced damping,
- ${m}_{i}$ : modal mass,
- ${\varphi}_{i}(\underline{x})$ : mode shape.

The flow exerts on the structure a force $f(\underline{x},t)$ . One assumes that these forces are applied in one direction. The two measurement directions will be treated separately, assuming that they are not coupled.

Displacement on a modal basis: one assumes that the motion of the structure is fairly well described by its first $N$ modes:

$u(\underline{x},t)\approx \sum_{i=1}^{N}{q}_{i}(t).{\varphi}_{i}(\underline{x})$

The generalised coordinates then satisfy the uncoupled equations:

${m}_{i}({\ddot{q}}_{i}+2{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}{\dot{q}}_{i}+{\omega}_{{i}^{2}}{q}_{i})=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\varphi}_{i}(\underline{x})f(\underline{x},t)\text{dx}={Q}_{i}(t)\mathrm{\iff }{m}_{i}(-{\omega}^{2}{\stackrel{}{q}}_{i}+2{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}\omega {\stackrel{}{q}}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}{q}_{i})={Q}_{i}(\omega ),{}^{}1\le i\le N$

## 2 Modelling and computation of the forces

The assumed linearity of the behaviour of the structure allows the decomposition of the model on a modal basis:

$\underline{y}=\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}.\underline{\underline{{Z}^{-1}}}.\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}.\underline{f}$

Using the notion of interspectrum, favoured in the macro-command 1
:

$\underline{\underline{{S}_{yy}}}=\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}.\underline{\underline{{Z}^{-1}}}.\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}.\underline{\underline{{S}_{\text{ff}}}}.{\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}}^{H}.\underline{\underline{{Z}^{-1}}}.{\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}}^{H}$ (1)

$\underline{\underline{C}}$ and $\underline{\underline{B}}$ are the observability and command matrices. $\underline{\underline{C}}$ makes it possible to project a field defined on a model onto a more restricted field of sensors. $\underline{\underline{B}}$ projects this same field onto the points of application of the forces. The matrices $\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}$ and $\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}$ are the mode-shape matrices defined on a sensor mesh and on a command mesh. They can be obtained with the operator PROJ_CHAMP or with the operator OBSERVATION, which makes it possible, in addition to the previous operator, to define for each node or node group unmeasured directions, or to define local frames.

$\underline{\underline{Z}}$ is the impedance matrix $\text{diag}{(-{\omega}^{2}+\mathrm{2j}{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}\omega +{\omega}_{i}^{2})}_{1\le i\le N}$ associated with the basis of the mode shapes $\underline{\underline{\Phi}}$ . This basis is in general defined on a model of good quality. A basis of good quality must indeed possess modal parameters close to reality. The mode shapes must also be close to reality, and furthermore be sufficiently regular. Thus, an experimental basis of mode shapes is defined on a reduced number of sensors, and is therefore not very regular (one risks a problematic solution of the inverse problem). One will prefer to it the basis of mode shapes of an updated numerical model or, better still, an experimental basis expanded onto a numerical model. This point is clarified below.

*Note on modal expansion*

One has an experimental modal basis $({f}_{i},{\xi}_{i},{m}_{i},{\underline{{\varphi}_{\exp}}}_{i})$ . The modal parameters are assumed to be identified with a good approximation, but the mode shapes, of good quality, are defined only on a restricted number of sensors. One therefore attempts to extend this basis of mode shapes onto a numerical model which is sufficiently representative of the structure (but not necessarily updated). The expansion basis can be the basis of the modes of the numerical model (obtained with CALC_MODES). Carrying out a modal expansion therefore means finding the vector of generalised parameters $\eta$ minimising:

${\mathrm{\parallel }{\underline{{\varphi}_{\exp}}}_{i}-\underline{\underline{{\Phi}_{\text{num}}}}.\underline{\eta}\mathrm{\parallel }}^{2}$

The “correlation” tab of CALC_ESSAI makes it possible to carry out this expansion, thanks to the use of PROJ_MESU_MODAL. For more details on the principle of the expansion, one should refer to the documentation of PROJ_MESU_MODAL (U4.73.01).

### 2.1 Solution of the inverse problem

Equation (1) of the direct problem can, under certain conditions, be inverted:

$\underline{\underline{{S}_{\text{ff}}}}={\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}}^{\oplus}.\underline{\underline{Z}}.{\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}}^{\oplus}.\underline{\underline{{S}_{yy}}}.{\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}}^{{H}^{\oplus}}.\underline{\underline{Z}}.{\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}}^{{H}^{\oplus}}$

The matrices $\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}$ and $\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}$ being rectangular, the sign $\oplus$ denotes their Moore–Penrose pseudo-inverse. This pseudo-inverse can be obtained by the use of an SVD algorithm (Singular Value Decomposition, cf. document R6.03.01). In CALC_ESSAI, two regularisation possibilities are available:

1. truncation of the small singular values: one denotes by ${\sigma}_{\max}$ the largest singular value. The SVD consists in setting, for all the singular values lower than $\varepsilon {\sigma}_{\max}$ , for a given $\varepsilon$ , the value 0. They are therefore not taken into account in the inverse computation. The truncation eliminates information on the matrices to be inverted, but improves the conditioning;
2. Tikhonov regularisation: the inverse of the matrix of singular values is not equal to $\text{diag}{(\frac{1}{{s}_{i}})}_{1\le i\le N}$ but to $\text{diag}{(\frac{{s}_{i}}{{s}_{{i}^{2}}+\alpha })}_{1\le i\le N}$ . The parameter $\alpha$ is called the Tikhonov parameter, and makes it possible to limit the divergence of the inverse solution.

In CALC_ESSAI, one computes successively the interspectra:

- of the generalised displacements (inversion of $\underline{\underline{C\Phi }}$ ),
- of the reconstructed physical displacements (in order to check the quality of the inversion),
- of the generalised forces (multiplication by the impedance matrix),
- of the physical forces (inversion of $\underline{\underline{{\Phi}^{T}B}}$ ),
- of the reconstructed generalised forces (for checking)
- of the physical displacements by synthesis on the modal basis.

A good criterion of the quality of the results of the inversion can be the comparison between the measured displacements and those reconstructed on the same modal basis which was used for the inversion (impedance matrix, and mode-shape matrices).

The notion of interspectrum, or of PSD, makes it possible to describe random phenomena. For a deterministic phenomenon, $\underline{\underline{{S}_{yy}}}=\underline{y}.\underline{{y}^{H}}$, where $H` denotes the Hermitian operator. For a random signal, :math:$underline{underline{{S}_{yy}}}` contains, on its diagonal, the spectral powers (auto-spectra) and, on its off-diagonal terms, the correlations between the signals. For example, in the case of a fluid force applied to a line-like structure, one readily imagines that the vortices created at the base tend to propagate along the structure. The off-diagonal terms therefore describe this propagation, together with the phase (see R4.07.02 for more details).
