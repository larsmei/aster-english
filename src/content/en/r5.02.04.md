# r5.02.04 Non-linear thermal analysis in a moving frame

**Summary**

The formulation and the algorithm of the stationary non-linear thermal convection–diffusion problem introduced within the command THER_NON_LINE_MO [u4.33.04](doc:u4.33.04) are presented.

The aim is to solve the heat equation in a moving frame tied to a loading and travelling in a given direction and at a given speed.

The non-linearities of the problem come both from the characteristics of the material, which depend on the temperature, and from boundary conditions of radiation type.

Problems of this type may be treated with models using plane, axisymmetric and three-dimensional structural finite elements.

## 1 Presentation of the problem

The heat equation presents strong non-linearities under certain conditions. This is the case when the material undergoes phase changes: these are accompanied by an abrupt variation of the characteristic quantities (heat capacity, enthalpy). This non-linearity is all the more accentuated when one treats the convection–diffusion problem, in which the transport term depending on the enthalpy function appears. The aim of this modelling is to treat this latter problem in the steady regime (stationary case).

In every case, one assumes that the velocity field is known a priori. The case of a moving solid is fairly frequent in practice. It concerns in particular welding applications or surface treatment, which involve a heat source travelling in a given direction and at a given speed. The thermal problem is then studied in a frame tied to the source.

The partial differential problem results from the equation of the global thermal balance over any domain $\Omega$ , which is written:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{d}{\text{dt}}\underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathit{\rho \beta d}\Omega =& \underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathit{Qd}\Omega -& \underset{\partial \Omega }{\int}q.nd\Gamma \\ \text{accumulation}& \text{création}+& \text{entrée-sortie}\end{array}
$$

In this equation, $O$ represents a connected domain, interior to the system studied, which one follows in its motion, $\beta$ represents the specific enthalpy of the material and $\rho$ denotes its density. $Q$ is a volumetric heat source, $q$ is the heat flux through the boundary $\partial \Omega$ ($n$  being the outward normal), and $d/\text{dt}$ is the **material derivative** .

The first term of the equation  is written (see for example ):

$$
\frac{d}{\text{dt}}\underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathit{\rho \beta d}\Omega =\underset{\Omega}{\int}(\frac{\partial (\rho \beta )}{\partial t}+div(\rho \beta V))d\Omega
$$

or again, taking into account the conservation of mass $(\frac{\partial \rho }{\partial t}+div(\rho V)=0)$  :

$$
\frac{d}{\text{dt}}\underset{\Omega}{\int}\rho \beta d\Omega =\underset{\Omega}{\int}(\rho \frac{\partial \beta }{\partial t}+\rho V.\text{grad}\beta )d\Omega
$$

where $V$ is the velocity vector of the displacement of the domain $\Omega$ . $V$ is entered under the simple keyword CONVECTION of the commands AFFE_CHAR_THER and AFFE_CHAR_THER_F.

The second term of the right-hand side of the equation  is written, taking into account the divergence theorem and Fourier’s law $(q=-k(T)\text{grad}T)$ :

$$
\underset{\partial \Omega }{\int}q.nd\Gamma =\underset{\Omega}{\int}divqd\Omega =-\underset{\Omega}{\int}div(k(T)\text{grad}T)d\Omega
$$

where $T$ is the temperature and $k(T)$ is the thermal conductivity of the material, a function of the temperature.

The equation  having to be satisfied for every domain $\Omega$ , one then has:

$$
\rho \frac{\partial \beta }{\partial t}+\rho V.\text{grad}\beta -div(k(T)\text{grad}T)=Q \quad \text{dans } \Omega
$$

**Note:**

Let us note that the classical case with $k(T)=k$ (constant) and $V=0$ , and where the specific enthalpy is a linear function of the temperature, $\beta (T)=\text{CT}$ , gives back the well-known classical equation:

$\rho C\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}-\mathit{kDT}=Q$ in $\Omega$

where $\Delta$ is the Laplacian and $C$ (constant) represents the specific heat.

The partial differential problem treated by the command THER_NON_LINE_MO [u4.33.04](doc:u4.33.04) consists in solving the equation  in the stationary case (directly at the steady state) with boundary conditions on the boundary $\partial \Omega$ .

This problem is formally written in the following form:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}V.\text{grad}u(T)-div(k(T)\text{grad}T)=Q,& \text{dans}\Omega ,\\ +\text{conditions aux limites}& \text{sur}\partial \Omega \end{array}
$$

where we have adopted the notation, valid for all that follows, $u(T)=\rho \beta (T)$ where $\rho$ is constant, defining the volumetric enthalpy.

## 2 Boundary conditions. Reference problem to be solved

One will refer, for example, to [r5.02.01](doc:r5.02.01) for further information on the thermal boundary conditions of Dirichlet, Neumann and linear Fourier type, and to [r5.02.02](doc:r5.02.02) for the boundary conditions of non-linear normal-flux type (non-linear Fourier).

In an enthalpy formulation, the **stationary**  thermal problem therefore consists in solving, in a domain $O$ with boundary on $\partial \Omega$ ,

$$
V.\text{grad}u(T)-div(k(T)\text{grad}T)=Q \quad \text{dans } \Omega
$$

with

$$
k(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}=\gamma ({T}_{\text{ext}}-T) \quad \text{sur } {\partial}_{1}\Omega
$$

$$
k(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}={q}_{0} \quad \text{sur } {\partial}_{2}\Omega
$$

$$
k(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}=\alpha (T)\quad \text{sur } {\partial}_{3}\Omega
$$

$$
T={T}_{0} \quad \text{sur } {\partial}_{4}\Omega
$$

where:

- ${T}_{0}$ : is the temperature imposed on ${\partial}_{4}\Omega$ ;
- ${q}_{0}$ : is the normal flux imposed on ${\partial}_{2}\Omega$ ;
- $\gamma$ : is the heat-exchange coefficient;
- ${T}_{\text{ext}}$ : is the external temperature;
- $\alpha (T)$ : is the normal flux of non-linear Fourier type (radiation).

The equations ,  represent the boundary conditions of the types, respectively: linear Fourier, Neumann, non-linear Fourier and Dirichlet.

The reference problem ,  is strongly non-linear because of the non-linearities in $k(T),u(T)$ (phase change) and $\alpha (T)$ (radiation).

## 3 Variational formulation of the problem

Let $\Omega$ be an open set of ${R}^{3}$ , with boundary $\partial \Omega ={\partial}_{1}\Omega \cup {\partial}_{2}\Omega \cup {\partial}_{3}\Omega \cup {\partial}_{4}\Omega$ such that,

for $i\mathrm{\ne }j$ and $i,j=1,\mathrm{...},4$ , one has: ${\partial}_{i}\Omega \cap {\partial}_{j}\Omega =\varnothing$ .

Let furthermore $\psi$ be a sufficiently regular function which vanishes on ${\partial}_{4}\Omega$ : $\psi \in V=\left\lbrace y\text{régulière}\text{et}\psi {\mid}_{{\partial}_{4}\Omega }=0\right\rbrace$ .

Multiply by $\psi$ the two sides of the equation , then integrate over $\Omega$ . An integration by parts then gives:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathrm{Qyd}\Omega & =\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}u(T)\mathrm{yd}\Omega -& \underset{\Omega}{\int}div(k(T)\text{grad}T)\mathrm{yd}\Omega \\ & =\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}u(T)\mathrm{yd}\Omega +& \underset{\Omega}{\int}k(T)\text{grad}T.\text{grad}\mathrm{yd}\Omega -\underset{\partial \Omega -{\partial}_{4}\Omega }{\int}(k(T)\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}y)\mathrm{dG}\end{array}
$$

since $\psi$ is zero on ${\partial}_{4}\Omega$ .

Whence, taking into account the boundary conditions ,  and , the variational formulation of the reference problem, which is given by the following equation:

$\forall \psi \in V$

$$
\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{cc}\underset{\Omega}{\int}k(T)\text{grad}T.\text{grad}\mathrm{yd}\Omega & +\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}u(T)\mathrm{yd}\Omega +\underset{{\partial}_{1}\Omega }{\int}\mathrm{gTydG}-\underset{{\partial}_{3}\Omega }{\int}\alpha (T)\mathrm{ydG}\end{array}\\ =\underset{\Omega}{\int}\mathrm{Qyd}\Omega +\underset{{\partial}_{1}\Omega }{\int}{\gamma T}_{\text{ext}}\mathrm{ydG}+\underset{{\partial}_{2}\Omega }{\int}{q}_{0}\mathrm{yd}\Omega ,\end{array}
$$

## 4 Treatment of the non-linearities

With a view to the numerical solution of the non-linear problem which we consider, it is necessary to treat all the non-linearities.

In our case, let us mention the strong non-linearity linked to the enthalpy function $u(T)$ , which takes into account the solid–liquid phase change, as well as the non-linearity linked to the possible presence of a boundary condition of non-linear normal flux (radiation).

Recall that in the classical case of non-linear transient thermal problems without convection, that is $V=0$ , several solution methods are proposed in the literature. There exist both methods using enthalpy formulations and methods using temperature formulations, all having the aim of treating as well as possible the non-linearity linked to the enthalpy (phase change).

We refer the reader to the reference  for a summary of the principal methods encountered in the literature. However, let us note that, because of the difficulty linked to the presence of the transport term $V.\mathit{grad}u(T)$ in the problem, none of these methods will be employed in what follows.

As in every iterative process, the aim of the numerical scheme in view is to find a temperature field ${T}^{n+1}$ at iteration $n+1$ , from the temperature field ${T}^{n}$ , solution of the preceding iteration.

### 4.1 Treatment of the non-linearity linked to the enthalpy

In order to treat this non-linearity, the strategy employed in this study was inspired by a technique for solving free-boundary problems , which was originally proposed in .

Consider the enthalpy function $u(T)$ as being given in a reciprocal form: temperature as a function of the enthalpy (the inverse of the function $u(T)$ ). In other words, one will have to treat the following temperature–enthalpy relation:

$$
T=\tau (u)
$$

The reason for this choice will be clearer in what follows. Indeed, we shall have to treat a two-field problem: a temperature field and an enthalpy field. The discretisation of the inverse function  makes it possible to increment the enthalpy field as a function of the current temperature field (and not the converse), as follows:

The first-order expansion of the function $\tau (u)$ is the following,

$$
{T}^{n+1}=\tau ({u}^{n})+\tau '({u}^{n})({u}^{n+1}-{u}^{n})
$$

where $\tau$ is the derivative of the function defined by  with respect to its argument.

In order to take this non-linearity into account, and starting from , one replaces ${u}^{n+1}$ by an approximation as a function of the unknown temperature field ${T}^{n+1}$ in the following way:

$$
{u}^{n+1}-{u}^{n}=\omega ({T}^{n+1}-\tau ({u}^{n}))
$$

where $\omega$ is a relaxation parameter, constant over the whole domain and throughout the iterative process, representing the term $\frac{1}{\tau '({u}^{n})}$ .

Because of the non-convexity of the function $\tau (u)$ , this relaxation parameter must necessarily satisfy the following condition ,  :

$$
\omega \le \frac{1}{\underset{n}{\max}\tau '({u}^{n})}
$$

In practice one takes $\omega =\frac{1}{\underset{n}{\max}\tau '({u}^{n})}$ .

Taking the approximation  into account, the discretisation of the second term of the equation  is expressed in the following way:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}{u}^{n+1}\psi d\Omega & =\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}{u}^{n}\psi d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\omega V.\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}\psi d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}\omega V.\text{grad}\tau ({u}^{n})\psi d\Omega ,\end{array}
$$

### 4.2 Treatment of the non-linearities linked to the non-linear Fourier condition and to the thermal conductivity

The non-linearity linked to the non-linear normal-flux condition is treated by considering the first-order expansion of the function (assumed sufficiently regular) $\alpha (T)$ , which is given by:

$$
\alpha ({T}^{n+1})=\alpha ({T}^{n})+\alpha '({T}^{n})({T}^{n+1}-{T}^{n})
$$

where $(.)'$ denotes the derivative of the function $(.)$ with respect to its argument.

It appeared necessary to decide on a strategy for discretising the term $k(T)\mathit{grad}T$  in the equation  in order to be able to treat this non-linearity for the stationary problem which we consider. For this, we adopted the following approximation:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}k({T}^{n+1})\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}& =k({T}^{n})\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}-\left[k({T}^{n})-k({T}^{n-1})\right]\text{grad}{T}^{n}\end{array}
$$

This discretisation is in fact a simplification of the first-order expansion of the term $k(T)\text{grad}T$ . It turns out to be effective, notably because of the weak non-linearity of the function $k(T)$ in practice.

**Note**

Let us also note that the following purely explicit approximation:

$k({T}^{n+1})\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}\approx k({T}^{n})\text{grad}{T}^{n+1},$

likewise gives satisfactory results. This observation has been verified from several numerical experiments.

## 5 Algorithm implemented in Code_Aster

The numerical scheme employed for the solution of the reference problem ,  is deduced from the variational formulation  and from the treatment of the various non-linearities, , , , discussed in the preceding section.

The solution algorithm is constituted by the succession of two successive operations at each iteration of the analysis.

Knowing the solution fields at iteration $n$ : ${T}^{n}$ at the nodes and ${u}^{n}$ at the Gauss points, one seeks the solutions ${T}^{n+1}\text{et}{u}^{n+1}$ at iteration $n+1$ as follows:

$\forall \psi \in V,$

$$
\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{cc}\underset{\Omega}{\int}k({T}^{n})\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}.\text{grad}\psi d\Omega & +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\omega V.\text{grad}{T}^{n+1}\psi d\Omega \\ & +\underset{{\partial}_{1}\Omega }{\int}\gamma {T}^{n+1}\psi d\Gamma -\underset{{\partial}_{3}\Omega }{\int}\alpha '({T}^{n}){T}^{n+1}\psi d\Gamma \end{array}\\ =\underset{\Omega}{\int}Q\psi d\Omega +\underset{{\partial}_{1}\Omega }{\int}\gamma {T}_{\text{ext}}\psi d\Gamma +\underset{{\partial}_{2}\Omega }{\int}{q}_{0}\psi d\Omega \\ +\underset{{\partial}_{3}\Omega }{\int}(\alpha ({T}^{n})-\alpha '({T}^{n}){T}^{n})\psi d\Gamma +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[k({T}^{n})-k({T}^{n-1})\right]\text{grad}{T}^{n}.\text{grad}\psi d\Omega \\ +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\omega V.\text{grad}t({u}^{n})\psi d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}V.\text{grad}{u}^{n}\psi d\Omega ,\end{array}
$$

$$
{u}^{n+1}={u}^{n}+\omega ({T}^{n+1}-\tau ({u}^{n}))
$$

At each iteration, a linear convection–diffusion problem is solved in order to obtain the field at the nodes ${T}^{n+1}$ , and then a simple local correction is carried out in order to obtain the field at the Gauss points ${u}^{n+1}$ .

The stopping criterion adopted in *code_aster* involves the two solution fields at the same time: the temperature field, and the enthalpy field.

The algorithm continues the iterations for as long as at least one of the relative variations of the iterates is greater than the corresponding tolerance given by the user:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{{(\sum_{i=1,...,\text{nddl}}{({T}_{i}^{n+1}-{T}_{i}^{n})}^{2})}^{1/2}}{{(\sum_{i=1,...,\text{nddl}}{({T}_{i}^{n+1})}^{2})}^{1/2}}>\text{tole 1}\\ \\ \frac{{(\sum_{i=1,...,\text{npg}}{({u}_{i}^{n+1}-{T}_{i}^{n})}^{2})}^{1/2}}{{(\sum_{i=1,...,\text{npg}}{({u}_{i}^{n+1})}^{2})}^{1/2}}>\text{tole 2}\end{array}$

where $\mathit{nddl}$ is the total number of degrees of freedom at the nodes, and $\mathit{npg}$ is the total number of Gauss points.

$\mathit{tole}1$ is entered under the keyword crit_temp_rela of the factor keyword convergence of the operator ther_non_line_mo.

$\mathit{tole}2$ is entered under the keyword crit_enth_rela of the factor keyword convergence of the operator ther_non_line_mo.
