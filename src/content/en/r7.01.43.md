# r7.01.43 Elastoplastic constitutive law with a smoothed Mohr-Coulomb criterion

**Summary:**

This document presents the solution method of the non-associated elastoplastic constitutive law with a smoothed Mohr-Coulomb yield surface dedicated in particular to granular geomaterials, accessible via the *MFront* environment. The original Mohr-Coulomb criterion is a pyramid: the edges and the apex constitute difficulties for the solution algorithm, cf. [R7.01.28](doc:r7.01.28). One adopts here the formulation in invariants and the smoothing (regularisation) solution proposed by Abbo and Sloan in [  2 ], with a direct implicit time-integration method. It is implemented in the *MFront* environment, cf. [  4 ], using the algorithm developed by [  3 ] within the community
[http://www.opengeosys.org](http://www.opengeosys.org/)
`  <[http://www.opengeosys.org/](http://www.opengeosys.org/)>`_.

## 1 Introduction

This elastoplastic constitutive relation, dedicated in particular to granular geomaterials, such as sands, describes the following phenomenological aspects: cohesion, dilatancy and internal friction. This model is designed above all to treat monotonic loading paths, up to the failure of the structure, knowing that the absence of hardening causes losses of stability, cf. [  5 ] , and also of controllability of the loading, and therefore solutions depending on the mesh.

The original Mohr-Coulomb criterion is a pyramid characterised by the intersection of 6 planes in the space of the principal stresses; the major and minor principal stresses come into play but not the intermediate stress. It is not bounded in the direction of the spherical compressive stresses.

The edges and the apex of the Mohr-Coulomb pyramid constitute difficulties for the solution algorithm, in particular because this requires a management of the evolution mechanisms dedicated to each face of the pyramid and also for the apex regime, cf. [R7.01.28](doc:r7.01.28). These difficulties possibly cause a lack of robustness. One adopts here the smoothing (regularisation) solution of the criterion proposed by Abbo and Sloan in [ 2 ], and implemented in the *MFront* environment, cf. [ 4 ], using the algorithm developed by [ 3 ]. The *MFront* programming [ 4 ] has been modified in order to add a few specific features to it.

At the apex of the Mohr-Coulomb pyramid, the smoothing consists of a hyperbolic approximation.

The parameters of the constitutive relation, besides the elastic parameters, assumed here isotropic, are 5 in number: the cohesion $c$ , the friction angle ${\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ (supplied in °), the dilatancy angle $\psi$ supplied in °), which must be lower than or equal to the internal friction angle ${\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ , identical to the original Mohr-Coulomb model, and two parameters associated with the smoothing: the transition Lode angle ${\theta}_{T}$ (supplied in °), and the tension cut-off ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}$ . The plastic flow is assumed non-associated and perfect. If $\psi ={\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ , then the flow is associated and the consistent tangent matrix is then symmetric. One uses a direct implicit time-integration method (*MFront*).

The internal variables are also 4 in number: norm of the deviatoric strains (equivalent plastic strain: the only true thermodynamic internal variable); volumetric plastic strain; volumetric density of dissipation; indicator of activation of the plasticity (1) or not (0).

This simple, robust model is very widely used for first analyses, under monotonic loading, of geotechnical structures.

## 2 Theoretical formulation

### 2.1 State variables, state law

The state variables are: the total strain $\epsilon \left(\mathrm{u}\right)$ , the plastic strain ${\epsilon}^{p}$ .

The free-energy density is written:

$$
w\left(\epsilon \left(\mathrm{u}\right),{\epsilon}^{p}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left(\epsilon \left(\mathrm{u}\right)-{\epsilon}^{p}\right):\mathrm{A}:\left(\epsilon \left(\mathrm{u}\right)-{\epsilon}^{p}\right)
$$

where $\mathrm{A}$ denotes the linear elasticity tensor; one assumes it isotropic (2 independent elastic coefficients in 3D). One does not consider any hardening.

The three-dimensional elastoplastic constitutive law is then written (state law):

$$
\sigma =\mathrm{A}:\left(\epsilon \left(\mathrm{u}\right)-{\epsilon}^{p}\right)
$$

### 2.2 Smoothed criterion (yield surface)

The criterion delimiting the elastic domain (yield surface) is formulated with the 3 tensor invariants:

$$
\text{f}\left(\sigma \right)=\frac{1}{3}{I}_{1}.\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}+\sqrt{{J}_{2}.K\left({\theta}_{L}\right)+{a}_{\mathit{tt}}^{2}{\sin}^{2}{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}}-c.\cos{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}⩽\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}0
$$

with ${I}_{1}=\text{tr}\left(\sigma \right)=3p$ three times the mean pressure, ${J}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left({\sigma}^{D}:{\sigma}^{D}\right)=\frac{1}{3}{\left({\Vert \sigma \Vert }_{\mathit{VM}}\right)}^{2}$ ; ${J}_{3}=\det\left({\sigma}^{D}\right)$ , having denoted by ${\sigma}^{D}=\sigma -p\mathrm{Id}$ the deviator of the stress tensor, and the modified Lode angle defined by: ${\theta}_{L}=\frac{1}{3}\arcsin\left(-\frac{3\sqrt{3}{J}_{3}}{2\sqrt{{J}_{2}^{3}}}\right)\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\in \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left[-\frac{\pi}{6},\frac{\pi}{6}\right]$ , and the three parameters: the cohesion $c$ , the friction angle ${\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ and the “tension cut-off” ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}$ , which serves for the hyperbolic approximation used for the smoothing at the apex of the Mohr-Coulomb pyramid. cf. [ 2 ].

*Note* *1* **:**

*According to* [ 2 *],* *if* ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}=0$ *, the criterion (3) recovers the apex of the* *Mohr-Coulomb* *pyramid,* *therefore it is no longer smoothed.* *If* ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}=\frac{1}{2}c.\mathit{cotan}{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ *,* *then at the apex of the smoothed criterion, one checks:* ${I}_{1}=0$ *.*

One knows that the principal stresses may be written as:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{I}=\frac{1}{3}{I}_{1}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}.\sqrt{{J}_{2}}.\sin\left({\theta}_{L}+\frac{2\pi }{3}\right)\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{II}}=\frac{1}{3}{I}_{1}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}.\sqrt{{J}_{2}}.\sin\left({\theta}_{L}\right)\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{III}}=\frac{1}{3}{I}_{1}+\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{3}.\sqrt{{J}_{2}}.\sin\left({\theta}_{L}-\frac{2\pi }{3}\right)\end{array}
$$

The function $K\left({\theta}_{L}\right)$ in (3) is defined by:

$$
K\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{\left(\cos{\theta}_{L}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\sin{\theta}_{L}\right)}^{2}& \mathit{si}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left|{\theta}_{L}\right|<{\theta}_{T}\\ {\left(A\left({\theta}_{L}\right)+B\left({\theta}_{L}\right).\sin3{\theta}_{L}+C\left({\theta}_{L}\right).{\sin}^{2}3{\theta}_{L}\right)}^{2}& \mathit{si}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left|{\theta}_{L}\right|⩾{\theta}_{T}\end{array}
$$

with an additional parameter ${\theta}_{T}\in \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left[0,\frac{\pi}{6}\right]$ called the transition angle and the functions $A\left({\theta}_{L}\right),B\left({\theta}_{L}\right),C\left({\theta}_{L}\right),$ defined by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}A\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}-B\left({\theta}_{L}\right).\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}-C\left({\theta}_{L}\right).{\sin}^{2}3{\theta}_{T}+\cos{\theta}_{T}\\ B\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\frac{\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin6{\theta}_{T}\left(\cos{\theta}_{T}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}}\right)-6\cos6{\theta}_{T}\left(\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\cos{\theta}_{T}}\right)}{18{\cos}^{3}3{\theta}_{T}}\\ C\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\frac{-\cos3{\theta}_{T}\left(\cos{\theta}_{T}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}}\right)-3\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L}\sin3{\theta}_{T}\left(\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}.\cos{\theta}_{T}}\right)}{18{\cos}^{3}3{\theta}_{T}}\end{array}
$$

*Note* *2* **:**

*According to* [ 2 *],* *one must not take a value of* ${\theta}_{T}$ *too close to* $\frac{\pi}{6}$ *, at the risk of poor conditioning;* *and* *a typical value of* ${\theta}_{T}$ *may be taken equal to* $\frac{5\pi }{36}$ *.* *With this value of* ${\theta}_{T}$ *and with* ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}=\frac{1}{20}c.\mathit{cotan}{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ *, the proposed hyperbolic smoothing leads to an error at most equal to 0.13% in the deviatoric plane relative to the* *original Mohr-Coulomb pyramid.*

An enrichment of the criterion () is introduced by adding a hardening term to the cohesion, in the following form:

$$
-c.\cos{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}.}\left(1+{r}_{\mathit{eg}}\cdot {\Vert {\epsilon}^{p}\Vert }_{\mathit{VM}}\right)
$$

where the parameter ${r}_{\mathit{eg}}$ (expressed without units) makes it possible to introduce a linear cohesion hardening.

### 2.3 Plastic flow potential

The plastic flow of this law is not standard (non-associated law), and therefore not oriented along the normal to the yield surface. The authors [ 2 ] propose an expression of the plastic flow by means of another potential of the same analytical form as that of the criterion (), bringing in the dilatancy angle $\psi$ :

$$
\text{g}\left(\sigma \right)=\frac{1}{3}{I}_{1}.\sin\psi +\sqrt{{J}_{2}.{K}_{g}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)+{a}_{\mathit{tt}}^{2}{\tan}^{2}{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}.}{\cos}^{2}\psi }-c.\cos\psi
$$

When $\psi ={\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ , the plastic flow law becomes associated.

The function ${K}_{g}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)$ in (7) is defined by:

$$
{K}_{g}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{\left(\cos{\theta}_{L}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\sin\psi .\sin{\theta}_{L}\right)}^{2}& \mathit{si}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left|{\theta}_{L}\right|<{\theta}_{T}\\ {\left({A}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)+{B}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right).\sin3{\theta}_{L}+{C}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right).{\sin}^{2}3{\theta}_{L}\right)}^{2}& \mathit{si}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\left|{\theta}_{L}\right|⩾{\theta}_{T}\end{array}
$$

the functions ${A}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right),{B}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right),{C}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right),$ being defined by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{A}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3}\sin\psi .\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}-{B}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right).\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}-{C}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right).{\sin}^{2}3{\theta}_{T}+\cos{\theta}_{T}\\ {B}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\frac{\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin6{\theta}_{T}\left(\cos{\theta}_{T}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin\psi .\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}}\right)-6\cos6{\theta}_{T}\left(\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin\psi .\cos{\theta}_{T}}\right)}{18{\cos}^{3}3{\theta}_{T}}\\ {C}_{\text{g}}\left({\theta}_{L}\right)=\frac{-\cos3{\theta}_{T}\left(\cos{\theta}_{T}-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin\psi .\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}}\right)-3\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L}\sin3{\theta}_{T}\left(\mathit{sgn}{\theta}_{L.}\sin{\theta}_{T}+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{3\sin\psi .\cos{\theta}_{T}}\right)}{18{\cos}^{3}3{\theta}_{T}}\end{array}
$$

When the stress state lies on the yield surface $\text{f}\left(\sigma \right)=0$ , the plastic strain rate is normal to the potential $\text{g}\left(\sigma \right)$ and is written with the plastic multiplier $d\lambda$ :

$$
d{\epsilon}^{p}=d\lambda .\frac{\partial \text{g}}{\partial \sigma }=d\lambda .{\mathrm{n}}_{\text{g}}
$$

where the second-order tensor outwardly normal to the surface is:

$$
{\mathrm{n}}_{\text{g}}=\frac{\partial \text{g}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial {I}_{1}\left(\sigma \right)}\cdot \mathrm{Id}+\left(\frac{\partial \text{g}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial {J}_{2}\left(\sigma \right)}+\frac{\partial \text{g}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial {\theta}_{L}}\cdot \frac{\partial {\theta}_{L}}{\partial {J}_{2}\left(\sigma \right)}\right){\sigma}^{D}+\frac{\partial \text{g}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial {\theta}_{L}}\cdot \frac{\partial {\theta}_{L}}{\partial {J}_{3}\left(\sigma \right)}\cdot \frac{\partial {J}_{3}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial \sigma }
$$

The only true thermodynamic internal variable is the equivalent plastic strain ${\Vert {\epsilon}^{p}\Vert }_{\mathit{VM}}$ .

One naturally has $\frac{\partial \text{g}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial {I}_{1}\left(\sigma \right)}=\frac{1}{3}\cdot \sin\psi$ and one recalls that: $\frac{\partial {J}_{2}\left(\sigma \right)}{\partial \sigma }={\sigma}^{D}$ .

*Note* *3* **:**

*The chosen flow potential, of the same form as the criterion, and therefore the* *flow* *tensor* ${\mathrm{n}}_{\text{g}}$ *, is not affected by the possible introduction of a linear cohesion hardening, cf.* *()* *.* *Indeed, the evolution of the cohesion does not modify the direction normal to the faces of the pyramid* $\text{g}\left(\sigma \right)=0$ *.*

### 2.4 Energy dissipation by plastic flow

The density of mechanical power dissipated by the plasticity mechanism is:

$$
D=\sigma :{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}
$$

The mechanical energy dissipation is obtained by cumulation in the course of the loading path.

## 3 Numerical integration of the constitutive law

The choice in *MFront* is made for the local integration of the first-order non-linear systems of differential equations written in the following manner:

$$
\dot{\mathrm{Y}}=\mathrm{G}\left(\mathrm{Y},t\right)
$$

*Note* *4* **:**

*One must note that the interface requires the supply of the components of the elastic strains at the initial state. By default they are assumed zero.*

### 3.1 Direct implicit integration

An implicit integration of $\theta$ -method type is chosen in [ 4 ] for the numerical integration of the non-linear system; in practice one takes $\theta =1$ (fully implicit scheme so as to satisfy the criterion exactly at the end of the time step).

The prediction is linear elastic:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{pred}}^{+}={\sigma}^{-}+\mathrm{A}.d\epsilon
$$

then one computes the prediction values of the three tensor invariants ${I}_{1}$ , ${J}_{2}$ , ${J}_{3}$ , and one evaluates the criterion (), cf. § 2.2 .

If one is not in plastic loading, then $d\lambda =0$ and ${\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}^{-}+\mathrm{A}.d\epsilon$ . Otherwise, one begins the solution of the zero of the non-linear equations.

The iterative Newton-Raphson method is chosen for the computation of the zero of the non-linear equations expressing the satisfaction of the consistency equation $\dot{\text{f}}=0$ , for the criterion () cf. § 2.2  and the expression of the constitutive relation () with the plastic flow direction, cf. § 2.3 , supplying the plastic multiplier rate $d\lambda$ and the stress rate by using the elastic prediction ${\sigma}^{e}$ ; the updated Jacobian matrix is used at each local iteration.

The final stress ${\sigma}^{+}$ is computed by:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}^{e}-\mathrm{A}.d{\epsilon}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}-d\lambda .\mathrm{A}.{n}_{\text{g}}
$$

The tolerance chosen for the non-linear local integration is supplied by the keyword RESI_INTE_RELA in the factor keyword RELATION of the non-linear analysis operators. This value is transmitted into the variable @Epsilon of *MFront*, whose default value is: ${10}^{-14}$ . It is advised not to adopt a value greater than ${10}^{-10}$ . The keyword ITER_INTE_MAXI in the factor keyword RELATION is also assigned to the variable @iterMax.

The specific positive numerical parameter denoted ${r}_{\mathit{eg}}$ (expressed without unit) is supplied with the smoothing parameters: the transition Lode angle ${\theta}_{T}$ and the tension cut-off ${a}_{\mathit{tt}}$ . It adds a contribution of hardening type to the criterion, which will be chosen small compared with $1$ . The term $-c.\cos{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}$ in the criterion ( ) becomes, cf.( ) :

$$
-c.\cos{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}.}\left(1+{r}_{\mathit{eg}}(\lambda +d\lambda )\right)
$$

After convergence of the local integration the update of the variables is carried out.

### 3.2 Update at the end of the integration step

Once the non-linear equations have been solved one has directly the update of the final stress tensor ${\sigma}^{+}$ .

One also computes the equivalent plastic strain EquPlasticStrain, equal to ${\lambda}^{+}$ , the volumetric plastic strain VolPlasticStrain, the cumulated volumetric density of mechanical dissipation Dissipation.

### 3.3 Expression of the consistent tangent matrix

The analytical consistent tangent matrix of the non-linear system to be solved by the Newton method uses the derivatives of the various quantities (grandeurs) defined in order to express the consistency equation $\dot{\text{f}}=0$ for the criterion (), cf.§ 2.2  and in order to express the constitutive relation () with the plastic flow direction, cf. § 2.3 , with respect to $\lambda$ (also taking () into account), and with respect to $\sigma$ . The consistent tangent matrix of the non-linear system is thus defined by:

$$
\mathrm{J}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}\mathrm{A}.\left({\mathrm{Id}}_{4}+d\lambda \frac{\partial {\mathrm{n}}_{\text{g}}}{{\mathrm{A}}^{-1}.\partial \sigma }\right)& \mathrm{A}.{\mathrm{n}}_{\text{g}}\\ \frac{\partial \text{f}}{\partial \sigma }& \frac{\partial \text{f}}{\partial \lambda }=-c\cdot {r}_{\mathit{eg}}\cdot \cos{\varphi}_{\mathit{pp}}\end{array}\right)
$$

The dimensionalisation of the row associated with the consistency equation uses Young's modulus in order to have a dimension of the order of the stresses and thus to condition this matrix better.

One also sees thereby the advantage of choosing the hardening parameter ${r}_{\mathit{eg}}$ non-zero, which makes it possible to add a non-zero contribution on the diagonal of this matrix.
