# v5.01.102 SDND102 - Seismic response of a multi-supported non-linear mass-spring system

**Summary**

The problem is to analyse the response of a mechanical structure, modelled by two unamortized spring mass systems, subjected to a seismic load of harmonic type, with the possibility of impact.

Discrete tensile compression l-element is tested, eigenmodes and static modes are calculated, the transient response is calculated by nonlinear modal recombination of a structure subject to accelerogram (modelization A) and the direct transient seismic response of a nonlinear structure (modelization B).

This test case also serves to validate an explicit acceleration and impact resolution calculation (modellings C and D) by comparing the results from DYNA_NON_LINE with an implicit time schedule, then explicit non-dissipative of centered and, finally, explicit dissipative TCHAMWA.

Modellings A, B, C, D use DIS_CHOC behavior.

The modellings E and F are the same as the C and D respectively, with the DIS_CONTACT behavior.

The G and H models test the CHOC_ENDO_PENA behavior. The material data (including stiffeners and masses) are those of the sdnd109 test and thus differ from the other modellings. The signal was modified to introduce an increase in amplitude over time.

The results obtained are in very good agreement with the reference results.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The answer to this problem is to compare the response of a symmetrical system consisting of two mass-spring systems identical to the response of a mass-spring system. Both problems, described in detail in the reference [bib2], are asked by the same accelerogram.

The ${f}_{i}$ eigenfrequencies, associated vectors standardized to the ${\Phi}_{\text{Ni}}$ modal mass and $\Psi$ static modes of the system (analytical values) are first calculated. The generalised response of the multi-supported system is then calculated by the analytically-solvented integral Duhamel [bib1]. Finally, the relative displacement of the nodes shock is restored on the physical basis, allowing us to calculate the absolute displacement field after calculating the drive displacement field.

The $\mathit{diff}$ function defined as the difference between the absolute displacement of the shocking node on a mobile obstacle and the shocking node on a fixed obstacle is calculated. It is verified that it is zero for different moments.

For G Modelling, only problem 2 is modelled. There are no reference solutions other than material data (criterion and stiffness discharge).

### 2.2 Baseline

Relative and absolute displacements to the nodes impact.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

## 5 Modelling B

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The mass-spring systems are modelled, as in Modelling A, by a discrete element at 3 degrees of freedom DIS_T.

**Modulating problem 1:**

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.102/100012E8000069D50000538285D9052D66FC5C28.svg)

**Figure** 5.1-a **: Modelling a mass-spring system impacting a rigid wall**

The node $\mathrm{NO1}$ is subject to an ${\gamma}_{1}(t)$-imposed acceleration. The relative displacement of the node $\mathrm{NO2}$ , its drive displacement and its absolute displacement are calculated.

A element DIST_T type on a mesh cell POI1 is selected to simulate the impact of the beam on a rigid wall: any impact between the beam and the obstacle is considered to be internal forces to this element. It is affected by a nonlinear shock-type behaviour (stiffness) via the constitutive law DIS_CONTACT DEFI_MATERIAU command.

The material thickness surrounding the node impact in the direction considered is specified by the DIST_1 operand of the DEFI_MATERIAU command. In the case treated, DIST_1 $=0.4495$ and JEU $=0.45$ are selected for the initial moment, the node impact and obstacle are separated from the $j=5.{10}^{-4}\mathrm{mm}$ game (see figure).

The seismic load, due to the required node $\mathrm{NO1}$ displacements, is calculated by the CALC_CHAR_SEISME operator. A load concept is then created from the VECT_ASSE operand of the AFFE_CHAR_MECA command.

DYNA_NON_LINE's NEWMARK implicit integration scheme is used with keyword SCHEMA_TEMPS (FORMULATION=的DEPLACEMENT) with time step from ${10}^{-3}s$ and default settings.

**Modification of problem 2:**

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.102/10000000000002150000010171A1E0D25B82715C.jpg)

**Figure** 5.1-b **:** Modelling of two mass-spring systems that intersect

The node $\mathrm{NO1}$ is subject to an accelerated ${\gamma}_{1}(t)$ , the node $\mathrm{NO4}$ to ${\gamma}_{2}(t)=–{\gamma}_{1}(t)$ . The relative and absolute displacements of the nodes $\mathrm{NO2}$ and $\mathrm{NO3}$ , their drive displacement and absolute displacement are calculated.

The potential impact between the two beams is considered to be internal forces to a element with two nodes. This element is assigned a nonlinear shock-type behavior (stiffness) via the RIGI_NOR key word of the constitutive law DIS_CONTACT command. The normal direction of contact is the $x$ local axis of the element discreet to two nodes.

The material thicknesses surrounding the nodes impact in the direction considered are specified by the DIST_1 and DIST_2 operating units of the DEFI_MATERIAU command. In the case treated, DIST_1=DIST_2 $=0.4495$ is selected to separate the two nodes impact units from the $J=2.j={10}^{-3}m$ set at the initial moment (see figure).

The seismic loading, due to the required displacements of the anchors (node $\mathrm{NO1}$ and $\mathrm{NO4}$ , is calculated by the CALC_CHAR_SEISME operator. A load concept is created from the VECT_ASSE operating AFFE_CHAR_MECA command.

The time integration is done with the Newmark algorithm and an time step of ${10}^{-3}s$ . The calculations are archived every 8 time step.

A reduced $\xi$ depreciation of 7% is considered for all calculated modes (keyword AMOR_MODAL from the DYNA_NON_LINE operator).

### 7.1 Features of modellings

The Modelling is primarily a DYNA_NON_LINE test with keyword SCHEMA_TEMPS (FORMULATION=的ACCELERATION) which is compared with DYNA_NON_LINE with keyword SCHEMA_TEMPS (FORMULATION=的DEPLACEMENT)

## 15 Summary of the results

The results obtained with *Code_Aster* are consistent with those expected (less than one thousandth error).

In this example, direct nonlinear calculation is much more costly in calculation time, by a factor 20, than on a modal basis.

Modellings C and E show that there are many similar results with an explicit time integration method with keyword SCHEMA_TEMPS (FORMULATION=的ACCELERATION) and implicit (DYNA_NON_LINE with keyword SCHEMA_TEMPS (FORMULATION=的ACCELERATION)).

The modellings D and F show that the results are also similar with an explicit dissipative time integration method of TCHAMWA (the difference being due to this added digital damping).
