# v1.01.164 ZZZZ164 - Validation of keywords DEFORME, TRANSLATION, ROTATION, MODI_BASE and ECHELLE of the MODI_MAILLAGE command

**Summary:**

This test validates TRANSLATION, ROTATION, MODI_BASE and ECHELLE keywords. To this end, two key words are required to be combined, one 3D and the other 2D. The first one consists of a translation, two rotations, and a scale-up. The two possibilities of definition of the rotation axis are tested: either by two points or by an point and the direction. The second one will combine a basic change and a scale-up. Thus, all cases of figures allowed by these key words will be tested.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is an analytical.

$M(x,y,z)$ is a space point, and is required to have $T$ translation of the $(\mathit{tx},\mathit{ty},\mathit{tz})$ vector, and $R$ rotation of $\alpha$ angle (in radians) with $P(\mathit{px},\mathit{py},\mathit{pz})$ axis and $D(\mathit{dx},\mathit{dy},\mathit{dz})$ direction.

Then $M$ becomes ${M}_{T}$ after translation: ${M}_{T}(x+\mathit{tx},y+\mathit{ty},z+\mathit{tz})$ .

${M}_{T}$ becomes ${M}_{\mathit{TR}}$ after rotation:

${M}_{\mathit{TR}}=P+\cos\alpha \cdot P{M}_{T}+(1-\cos\alpha )\cdot ({\mathit{PM}}_{T}\cdot D)\cdot D+\sin\alpha \cdot (D\mathrm{\wedge }{\mathit{PM}}_{T})$

with ${M}_{T}=M+T$

The scale of a factor of the Ech, gives:

${M}_{\mathit{TRE}}=\mathit{ech}\cdot {M}_{\mathit{TR}}$

The basic change functionality is expected to be inputted by the user of two orthogonal vectors in 3D (one vector in 2D). This data is supplemented to generate a direct orthogonal database, in 3D or 2D. Tests are carried out to verify whether the input data will allow the definition of a direct orthogonal base. A normalization of the vectors of the database is then carried out.

In 3D, we therefore expect the data of $U$ and $V$ , the first two vectors of the new database:

$W(x,y,z)=U(x,y,z)\mathrm{\wedge }V(x,y,z)$

$\mathrm{\Rightarrow }B=(U,V,W)$: matrix formed by the basic vectors

$M(U,V,W)={B}^{T}M(x,y,z)$

In 2D, the second vector of the base is generated by a 90° rotation of the vector entered by the user.

This basic change can be combined with scaling and translation, for example.

Programming of these transformations is done differently in 3D and 2D, in order to optimize each of these two cases.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

We will control the new coordinates of point $\mathit{P1}$ , $\mathit{P7}$ and $\mathit{P8}$ in 3D ($\mathit{P1}$ , $\mathit{P3}$ and $\mathit{P4}$ in 2D).

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Uncertainties arise from the numerical accuracy in *Code_Aster* (platform dependence) and in the calculation of the analytical solution reference. Thus, a relative accuracy criterion of the order of $1.E-13$ in the tests can be considered.

- a translation of the vector $(2.5;3.9;-12.3)$,

- a second rotation of $-161$ angle and d-axis through $(-3.;0.5;3.8)$ and $(0;1;0)$ direction,

- a basic change of $(1.23;0.23;0)$ and $(-2.3;12.3;0)$ vectors,
