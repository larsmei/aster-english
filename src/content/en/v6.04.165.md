# v6.04.165 FORMA42 - Practical work of the Civil Engineering training: tensioning a variable section prestressed beam

**Summary:**

The $\mathrm{3D}$ test is designed to implement the two techniques for tensioning prestress cables and compare the solutions thus obtained.

In $A$ Modelling all cables are energized simultaneously from the beginning of the calculation using simply the STAT_NON_LINE operator.

In modellings $B$ and $C$ the idea is to use the CALC_PRECONT operator. In the first case, all cables are energized simultaneously, in the second case, the phase of prestressing is modelled.

The objective of the TP is to implement these different strategies and to compare the differences in results achieved in each case.

In all cases, restraints conditions between the cable and the concrete and the BPEL91 voltage calculation are performed using the DEFI_CABLE_BP operator.

When the TP is done in limited time, it can be wise to start from $A$ Modelling, and only to achieve modellings $B$ and $C$.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The base of the beam is blocked in the $Z$ direction. The two translation movements relative to $\mathrm{OX}$ and $\mathrm{OY}$ are blocked as well as the rotational movement around $\mathrm{OZ}$.

The loading sequence is as follows:

- 300 days, tensioning of 2 cables (1 and 2) at the lower end,
- 450 days, tensioning of 2 additional cables (3 and 4) at the lower end,
- 600 days, the last cable (5) is energized by both ends.

The beam is also subject to gravity.

## 2 Modelling A: Powering the beam without taking into account the phase with STAT_NON_LINE

### 2.1 Implementation of TP

In this part, the phase is not modelled, all loads are applied to an time step.

- Import mesh in MED format (ssnv164a.mmed) and view it
- Build the command file:

  - Read mesh in med format: LIRE_MAILLAGE
  - Creating nodes groups from mesh cells groups: DEFI_GROUP on mesh cells groups 的SU3 的PP的, because we want to apply boundary conditions to these groups without node groups being created at the time of mesh.
  - Model definition: $3D$ mechanical Modelling for mesh cell group density ‘VOLTOT' (the complete beam) and BARRE mechanical Modelling for mesh cells group cables (‘CAB1', ‘CAB2', ...): AFFE_MODELE.
  - Definition of cable characteristics: the cable radius is $R=2.8209{10}^{-2}m$ , AFFE_CARA_ELEM, BARRE key word.
  - Creating a concrete material ($E=4.E10\mathit{Pa}$ , $\nu =0.2$ , $\rho =2500\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ ): DEFI_MATERIAU with ELAS key word and BPEL_BETON (default creep/retread).
  - Creating a cable material ($E=1.93E11\mathit{Pa}$ , $\nu =0.3$ , $\rho =7850\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ ). Use elastic law and regulatory prestress specifications (BPEL_ACIER keywords with F_PRG $\text{}=1940.E6$ , FROT_COURB $\text{}=0$ , FROT_LINE $\text{}=1.5{10}^{-3}$ ).
  - Assigning concrete material to ‘VOLTOT' and steel material to cables: AFFE_MATERIAU.
  - Boundary conditions definitions (loads, blockages): AFFE_CHAR_MECA:
- $\mathit{DX}=0$ on the node group ‘PY
- $\mathit{DY}=0$ on the node group ‘PX
- $\mathit{DX},\mathit{DY}=0$ on the previously created node group ‘PP
- $\mathit{DZ}=0$ on the node group ‘SU3';
- Inform the gravity (keyword gravity).

  - Definition of prestressing cables: DEFI_CABLE_BP. For all cables, adopt an initial voltage of $3.75{10}^{6}N$ and an anchoring step of $0.001m$; the active anchor is that at the narrow end of the beam (e.g. ‘PC1D' for the first cable), the passive at the wide end (e.g. ‘PC1F' for the first cable).
  - AFFE_CHAR_MECA to define the relationship between cables and concrete. Be careful to establish the kinematic relationship between cables and concrete (RELA_CINE='OUI') and the load linked to the prestress (SIGM_BPEL='OUI').
  - set the moment list that will be used to solve the mechanical problem using DEFI_LIST_REEL.
  - Define STAT_NON_LINE for mechanical calculation. Concrete and cables are elastic.
  - Calculate with CALC_CHAMP the variables you want to post-treat with *Salomé* (a minimum of the constraints) and print them in MED format.
  - Recover the effort that will be applied in cable 1 using the RECU_TABLE command. Print it with IMPR_TABLE.
  - Retrieve the effort in the various cables after tensioning the prestress cables with POST_RELEVE_T. Print them with IMPR_TABLE.
  - To compare curves more easily, it is advisable to draw the voltage curve on the same graph in relation to the curvilinear abscisse (1) as defined in the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP, (2) at the initial step and (3) at the final time step in the data structure resulting from STAT_NON_LINE. To do this, use the operator RECU_FONCTION and then IMPR_FONCTION(FORMAT='XMGRACE').
- Open the file produced by IMPR_FONCTION with *XMGRACE* . What do you notice?
- You can then vary the input parameters for the calculation of the cable voltage: anchoring back, active/passive anchors, relaxation loss and compare the tension in the cables.

## 4 Modelling C: Modelling of prestressing phase

### 4.1 Implementation of TP

The aim is now to repeat the previous study but to model the prestress phase.

- At first, the cables are not yet in place (there is only the sheath), however there is the effect of gravity on the concrete.
- The ‘CAB1' and ‘CAB2' cables are energized at the moment 300 days (other cables are inactive!!).
- The ‘CAB3' and ‘CAB4' cables are energized at 450 days. At 600 days, the ‘CAB5' cable is energized.

Change the control file accordingly and compare the tension profiles in the cables compared to previous cases.

The actions / commands to use:

- Duplicate DEFI_CABLE_BP if necessary to be able to bind the 3 cable groups separately. Do the same for the loads associated with these cables. These loads should only include the kinematic restraints.
- Change of the d-minute list for prestressing calculation: DEFI_LIST_REEL, values (0.150,300,450,600) days.
- Make a first STAT_NON_LINE for the calculation of the gravity (1 time step). Be careful, to avoid taking the cables into account, it is necessary to assign them the constitutive law SANS, which is to apply a zero Young's modulus.
- Then make 3 calls to CALC_PRECONT for the successive voltage setting of the cables. The boundary conditions changes in all 3 cases.

Take the same post-processings as before but plotting curves at different moments or a minimum at t = 600 days.

Observe the differences in the tension in the cables and the stress state in the concrete.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test allows testing three different ways of imposing the tensioning of prestressing cables. Each of these ways leads to voltage profiles in different cables and therefore to different stresses in concrete. The user must be aware of the differences.

Furthermore, when combining this calculation of the tension of the cable with a nonlinear calculation, it is important not to accumulate losses. Thus, if concrete is modelled by means of a visco-elastic law, it is not necessary to take into account the losses by creep of concrete in the calculation of the tension of the cable.

It should also be noted that the tension is far from uniform, it is in the interests of these operators to impose a prestress in the form of a pre-deformation or a fictitious thermal field.
