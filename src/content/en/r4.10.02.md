# r4.10.02 Residual error estimator

**Summary:**

The residual error estimator makes it possible to estimate the discretisation error due to the finite-element method on the elements of a 2D or 3D mesh. It is an explicit error estimator involving the residuals of the equilibrium equations and the jumps of the normal stresses at the interfaces, unlike the Zhu‑Zienkiewicz estimator, which uses an a posteriori stress-smoothing technique [R4.10.01](doc:r4.10.01) and [bib5].

This estimator is implemented in *Code_Aster* in 2D and 3D elastoplasticity.

## 1 Introduction

The residual error estimator was developed in 1993 by Bernardi-Métivet-Verfurth [bib1]. It is an explicit error estimator involving the residuals of the equilibrium equations (whence its name). It applies to elliptic problems (Poisson, Stokes, or linear elasticity) in dimension 2 or 3. These problems are assumed to be discretised by finite elements associated with a regular triangulation.

Historically, the first explicit error estimator relating to equilibrium defects is due to Babuska and Rheinbolt [bib2] for 1D problems with linear elements. Gago extended this estimator to 2D and added to the formulae the traction jumps at the element interfaces [bib3] and [bib4]. New estimators were then proposed, in which the surface-traction defects on the boundaries of the domain were also taken into account, together with an improvement of the estimation of the inter-element jumps giving more reliable results.

One is interested here in the residual estimator applied to the case of linear elasticity. The aim is, at the end of an elastic analysis, to determine the error map on the mesh with a view, possibly, to adapting the latter (by refinement and/or unrefinement) or simply for information. The adaptation can be done by chaining with the Homard cutting software.

## 2 Formulation of the residual estimator

Let $\Omega$ be an open set of ${R}^{n}$ , $n=2$ or $3$ , of boundary $\Gamma$ , and $T$ a regular triangulation of $\Omega$ .

In linear elasticity, the continuous problem is written:

find $(u,\sigma )$ such that:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}div\sigma +f=0& \text{dans}\Omega \\ u={u}_{D}& \text{sur}{\Gamma}_{D}\\ \sigma .n={g}_{N}& \text{sur}{\Gamma}_{N}\end{array}$

${\Gamma}_{D}$ is the Dirichlet boundary of the mesh

${u}_{D}$ is the displacement imposed on this boundary

${\Gamma}_{N}$ is the Neumann boundary

$n$ the unit normal to ${\Gamma}_{N}$

${g}_{N}$ is the loading applied on this boundary; it may be continuous or discretised.

$f$ is a force of body-force type (gravity, rotation); it may be continuous or discretised.

${\sigma}_{h}$ is the stress obtained by the solution of the discrete problem:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}div{\sigma}_{h}+f=0& \text{dans}\Omega \\ {u}_{h}={u}_{D}& \text{sur}{\Gamma}_{D}\\ {\sigma}_{h}.n={g}_{N}& \text{sur}{\Gamma}_{N}\end{array}$

with the relation ${\sigma}_{h}=\text{DB}{u}_{h}$ where:

$D$ is the Hooke matrix

$B$ is the linearised strain operator

If $K$ denotes a current element of the mesh, the error estimator (denoted $\eta (\Omega )$ ) is defined as being the root-mean-square of the local error indicators, denoted $\eta (K)$ :

$\eta (\Omega )={\left[\sum_{K\in T}\eta {(K)}^{2}\right]}^{1/2}$

**The local residual indicator**

The indicator is composed of three terms; the first represents the residual of the equilibrium equation on each mesh cell, the second term the jump of the normal stresses on the interfaces, the third term the difference between the normal stresses and the loading imposed on ${\Gamma}_{N}$ if the element intersects ${\Gamma}_{N}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r4.10.02/10000CC8000069BB000038BE5FC4A4679132172E.svg)

**Figure 2-a: Elements internal to a mesh**

- the first term of the estimator is the ${L}^{2}$ norm of the residual of the equilibrium equation on the mesh cell $K$ , multiplied by ${h}_{K}$ which is either the diameter of the circumscribed circle for a triangular finite element, or the maximum diagonal for a quadrangle,
- the second term is the integral, over $S(K)$ defined [Figure 2-a], of the jumps of normal stresses integrated on each edge $F$ of the element which has a neighbour, and multiplied by the square root of ${h}_{F}$ , which is the length of the edge $F$ ,

![Figure](/figures/r4.10.02/1000164C000069D500004D9E20F5585200AA3625.svg)

**Figure 2-b: Elements located on the boundary of a mesh**

- the third term is the integral, over the intersection of each edge $F$ of the edges $\partial K$ of the current element $K$ with the Neumann boundary ${\Gamma}_{N}$ , of the jumps between the normal stresses of the element and the Neumann force ${g}_{N}$ , multiplied by the square root of ${h}_{F}$ , the length of the edge $F$ .

One therefore has the following formula for the residual estimator:

$\eta (K)={h}_{K}{\parallel f+div{\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(K)}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{F\in S(K)}{h}_{F}^{1/2}{\parallel \left[{\sigma}_{h}.n\right]\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(F)}+\sum_{F\subset \partial K\subset {\Gamma}_{N}}{h}_{F}^{1/2}{\parallel {g}_{N}-{\sigma}_{h}.n\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(F)}$     eq 2-1

For the choice of the various terms of [eq 2-1], one refers to [bib1].

## 3 Properties of the residual estimator

| One denotes | ${\eta}_{\text{EX}}(K)$ the exact error ${\parallel u-{u}_{h}\parallel }_{{H}^{1}(K)}$ on the element $K$ (unknown a priori) |
| --- | --- |
| and | ${\eta}_{\text{EX}}(\Omega )$ the global exact error ${\parallel u-{u}_{h}\parallel }_{{H}^{1}(O)}$ |

One then has the following properties ([bib1]):

- whatever the element $K$ , the elementary error $\eta (K)$ is bounded above by the exact local error (multiplied by a constant independent of the triangulation),

that is $\forall K\eta (K)\le {C}_{1}\times {\eta}_{\text{EX}}(K)$

- the exact global error is bounded above by the estimated global error $\eta (\Omega )$ (multiplied by a constant independent of $T$ )

that is ${\eta}_{\text{EX}}(\Omega )\le {C}_{2}\times \eta (\Omega )$

The constants ${C}_{1}\text{et}{C}_{2}$ depend a priori on the type of finite element and on the boundary conditions of the problem. Kelly and Gago [bib3] proposed in 2D a constant ${C}_{2}$ depending only on the degree $p$ of the interpolation polynomial used:

$\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{2}={(\frac{1}{24{p}^{2}})}^{1/2}\text{soit}& {C}_{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2p}\sqrt{6}}\text{pour les TRIA3 et QUAD4}(\text{degré 1})\\ & {C}_{2}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{4p}\sqrt{6}}\text{pour les TRIA6 et QUAD8}(\text{degré 2})\end{array}$

For 3D, one does not have an evaluation of the constant. One can nevertheless say that the estimated global error overestimates the exact global error in every case. This result is not necessarily true at the local level.

## 4 Implementation in Aster

The residual estimator is implemented in 2D and 3D on all types of elements. It is computed by the command CALC_ERREUR by activating the option 'ERME_ELEM'.

This option computes on each element:

- the absolute error $\eta (K)$ (see [eq 2-1]),
- the norm of the stress tensor ${\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(K)}$ which serves to normalise the absolute error,
- the relative error ${\eta}_{\text{rel}}(K)=100\times \frac{\eta (K)}{\sqrt{\eta {(K)}^{2}+{\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(K)}^{2}}}$ .

**Note:**

*This definition of the relative error implies that in the zones where the stresses are very small, the relative error can be large and not significant. It is then the absolute error which must be considered.*

It also computes at the global level:

- the absolute error $\eta (\Omega )={\left[\sum_{K\in T}\eta {(K)}^{2}\right]}^{1/2}$ ,
- the global norm of the stress tensor ${\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(\Omega )}={\left[\sum_{K\in T}{\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]}^{1/2}$ ,
- the relative error ${\eta}_{\text{rel}}(\Omega )=100\times \frac{\eta (\Omega )}{\sqrt{\eta {(\Omega )}^{2}+{\parallel {\sigma}_{h}\parallel }_{{L}^{2}(\Omega )}^{2}}}$ .

From the expression [eq 2-1], one sees that in order to compute the error indicator on the mesh cell $K$ , one must know:

- the possible loadings $f$ on $K$ and ${g}_{N}$ on $\partial K\cap {\Gamma}_{N}$ (or their discretisation ${f}_{h}$ and ${g}_{\text{Nh}}$ ),
- the quantities ${h}_{K}$ , ${h}_{F}$ and $n$ linked to the geometry of the element,
- the stress field ${\sigma}_{h}$ ,
- the list of the neighbours of $K$ in order to recover the stresses on these elements, necessary for the computation of the 2nd term of [eq 2-1].

1 and 2 can be computed or recovered easily.

3 must have been computed beforehand by one of the options 'SIGM_ELNO' or 'SIEF_ELNO'.

Otherwise, a fatal error message is issued.

4 requires the computation of a particular mesh-cell–mesh-cell connectivity, in addition to the standard mesh-cell–node connectivity. This new object is stored in the data structure of type maillage.

For the detail of the implementation in *Aster*, see [bib6].

For the validation of the estimator, see [bib7].
