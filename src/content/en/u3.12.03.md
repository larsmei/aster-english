# u3.12.03 Modelling COQUE_3D

**Summary:**

The COQUE_3D modelling presented here corresponds to a general shell and plate kinematics formulation, of Naghdi-Reissner type, in which the generalised stresses are obtained from the three-dimensional constitutive laws of the constituent materials [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04). Unlike the modellings DKT-DST, Q4G corresponding to the plate-element formulations developed by J.L. BATOZ [bib1]_ which use plane finite elements, the COQUE_3D modelling makes it possible to carry out analyses of shell structures of arbitrary shapes, with a good approximation of the geometry. Furthermore, this modelling is capable of representing the large rotations of the structures [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05) under the assumption of small strains.

The degrees of freedom are the displacements and the rotations, taken at the nodes of the elements.

The non-linear constitutive behaviour is discretised by polynomials $\mathit{P2}$ , which allows a precise analysis of the stresses.

It can be used for problems of three-dimensional shell structures in linear and non-linear mechanical analysis.

This formulation also exists for slice problems, or problems of revolution. It is based on 1D elements: (see [U3.12.02]).

The thermomechanical analyses are chained from the thermal shell finite elements (see [U3.22.01](doc:u3.22.01)).

## 1 Discretisation

### 1.1 Degrees of freedom

For the three-dimensional shell modelling, the discretisation degrees of freedom are, at each node of the support mesh cell, the six displacement components (three translations and three rotations) at the vertex nodes and at the mid-side nodes of the support mesh cell. At the central node of the support mesh cell, the degrees of freedom are reduced to the rotations only. These nodes belong to the middle surface of the shell.

| **Modelling** | **Degrees of freedom (at each node)** |
| --- | --- |
| COQUE_3D | DX DY DZ DRX DRY DRZ DRX DRY DRZ only at the central node |

### 1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness matrices, mass matrices ...

The support mesh cells of the finite elements, in displacement formulation, may be 7-node triangles or 9-node quadrilaterals. These mesh cells are not assumed to be plane; they are created from meshes in TRIA6 and QUAD8 by the command CREA_MAILLAGE (see [U4.23.02](doc:u4.23.02)).

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Finite element** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COQUE_3D | TRIA7 QUAD9 | MEC3TR7H MEC3QU9H |

### 1.3 Support mesh cell of the loadings

All the loadings applicable to the facets of the shell elements are treated by direct discretisation on the support mesh cell of the element in displacement formulation.

No loading support mesh cell is therefore necessary for the faces of the shell elements.

For the loadings applicable on the edges of the shell elements, a support mesh cell of type SEG3 can be used:

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Finite element** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| COQUE_3D | SEG3 | MEBOCQ3 |

## 2 Assignment of the characteristics

For these $\mathrm{2D}1/2$ structural elements, it is necessary to assign geometrical characteristics which are complementary to the mesh data. The definition of these data is carried out with the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM associated with the following factor keyword:

- COQUE

Makes it possible to define and assign to the mesh cells the thickness, the shear coefficient, the eccentricity, ...

## 3 Supported loadings

The available mechanical loadings are the following:

- 'FORCE_ARETE'

Makes it possible to apply linear forces, to an edge of a volume element.

- 'FORCE_COQUE'

Makes it possible to apply surface forces.

- 'PESANTEUR'

Makes it possible to apply a gravity-type loading.

- 'PRES_REP'

Makes it possible to apply a pressure to a continuum domain.

- 'ROTATION'

Makes it possible to define the rotation speed and the rotation vector.

The application of a thermal expansion loading is carried out by defining the factor keyword AFFE_VARC under AFFE_MATERIAU [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03).

## 4 Non-linear possibilities

### 4.1 Constitutive laws

All the plane-stress relations, usable under COMPORTEMENT in STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE, are available (*cf.* [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)).

### 4.2 Strains

The available strains, used in the constitutive relations under the keyword DEFORMATION for the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE, are (Cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

/     'PETIT'

The strains used for the constitutive relation are the linearised strains.

/    'PETIT_REAC'

The strain increments used in the incremental constitutive relation are the linearised strains of the displacement increment in the updated geometry.

/    'GROT_GDEP'

Makes it possible to carry out analyses in large displacements and in large rotations. The strains used in the constitutive relation are the GREEN‑LAGRANGE strains.

**Note:**

1. *Warning: the analysis of the strains using 'PETIT_REAC' is only an approximation of the large-displacement assumptions. It requires very small loading increments to be performed. In order to take large displacements, and especially large rotations, into account correctly, it is recommended to use* *DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP'.*
2. *It is possible, by using under the factor keyword* *EXCIT(TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV') to take a follower pressure into account.*

## 5 Examples of implementation: test cases

- Linear statics

SSLP304C [V3.02.304](doc:v3.02.304): Analysis of an orthotropic square plate subjected to a uniaxial tension outside the axes of orthotropy

- Non-linear statics

HPLA100C [V7.01.100](doc:v7.01.100) : Thermoelastic analysis of a heavy hollow cylinder in uniform rotation.

- Linear dynamics

SDLS01E [V2.03.01](doc:v2.03.01): Analysis of the frequencies and of the associated bending modes of a thin square plate in free-free conditions and clamped on one edge.
