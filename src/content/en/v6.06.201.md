# v6.06.201 CONT201 - Projection-intersection for 3D linear mesh cells beam matching

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to verify the projection-intersection procedure in the case of beam-launch matching (mortar approach).
Specifically, this test is limited to the case of linear 3D mesh cells (so mesh cells skin type QUAD4 or TRIA3).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 General formalism adopted

The focus here is on the matching procedure and the construction of the mortar integrals for the calculation of contact.
In this case, it is limited to elementary cases in 3D. As a reminder, pairing and projection-intersection decomposes
in several stages:

1. projection of the nodes slaves on the master mesh cell,
2. calculation of an intersection on the master mesh cell,
3. Inverse projection and expression of intersection points on the slave mesh cell.

Then, a quadrature law is built by re-mailing the polygon obtained from the barycentre (using triangles).
The associated documentation can be consulted for more information.

**ATTENTION**: if the intersection domain is a triangle, you do not rematch, and you use the triangle obtained directly
to build the quadrature law.

### 1.2 Modellings frame presented here

Here we consider the matching processes between two linear mesh cells density.
So we're just going to consider mesh cells TETRA4 and HEXA8.
to validate cases of projections-intersection between skin surfaces TRIA3 and QUAD4.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 General principles adopted for the data collection of case-tests

The different modellings correspond to distinct elementary geometries.
Each model corresponds to a couple of a slave mesh cell volume and a volume mesh cell
master (each linear, i.e. either an mesh cell TETRA4 or an mesh cell HEXA8).
Both mesh cells are vertically translatable (according to the $z$ axis) from a distance $h$.
In addition, in order to generate various configurations, either:

- translation of the top solid in the remaining two directions (according to $x$ and $y$)
- a relative rotation of the upper solid. This may be done analytically to obtain precise intersections, or by using relative rotation by Euler angles.

The different modellings are built to have analytical cases and others that are not.
In addition, several types of polygons are being generated to verify the calculation of intersection areas and re-mailing for quadrature weights.

### 2.2 Reminders on the relative rotation of an point by Euler angles

The 3x3 rotation matrix is used according to three angles $\psi$, $\theta$, $\phi$. These angles correspond to elementary rotations in 3D space,
and are often called Euler angles. To rotate a vector, apply an $\mathbf{R}$ matrix.

This matrix allows to rotate a 3D vector according to the angles d
in the order of the axes $x$, $y$, then $z$. The corresponding rotational matrix used is:

$$
\mathbf{R} =
\begin{bmatrix}
\cos \psi \cos \phi - \sin \psi \cos \theta \sin \phi & -\cos \psi \sin \phi - \sin \psi \cos \theta \cos \phi & \sin \psi \sin \theta \\
\sin \psi \cos \phi + \cos \psi \cos \theta \sin \phi & -\sin \psi \sin \phi + \cos \psi \cos \theta \cos \phi & -\cos \psi \sin \theta \\
\sin \theta \sin \phi & \sin \theta \cos \phi & \cos \theta
\end{bmatrix}
$$
