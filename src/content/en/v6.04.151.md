# v6.04.151 SSNV151 - Traction / Compression with constitutive law BETON_DOUBLE_DP

**Summary**

This validation case is intended to verify the $\mathrm{3D}$ BETON_DOUBLE_DP behavior model formulated in the thermoplasticity framework, for the description of the nonlinear behaviour of concrete in traction and compression, with consideration given to irreversible variations in the thermal and mechanical characteristics of concrete, particularly sensitive at high temperature.

The description of cracking is treated as part of plasticity, using an energy equivalence, by identifying the density of cracking energy in $I$ mode, with the plastic work of an equivalent homogeneous medium, where plastic deformation is evenly distributed in an "elementary" zone. This approach preserves the continuity of model formulation, over the whole of its behavior, and helps to avoid possible numerical difficulties when changing the material's condition.

The pathological sensitivity of the digital solution to spatial discretization (mesh), generated by the introduction of a softening behaviour of concrete in traction and compression, is partially resolved by introducing a cracking or breaking energy, depending on a characteristic length ${l}_{c}$, related to the size of the elements.

The solution of the model's constituent equations is made by an implicit schema.

This is a cube with 8 nodes subjected to uniaxial compression, while on the move imposed, to which is added a two-axial traction when a high tensile hardening has been achieved. This load leads to the particular case of a state of hydrostatic stress, solved by projection at the top of the tensile cone, when placed in an equivalent stress diagram/hydrostatic stress. It is a test case non-regression.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

It is a cube with 8 nodes, three faces of which have a normal zero displacement, and the three opposite sides have a normal imposed and identical displacement.

The cube is $1\mathrm{mm}$ sidewise. In Modelling A, the cube is oriented according to the $\mathrm{Oxyz}$ marker.
