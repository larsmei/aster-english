# v5.02.141 SDNL141 – Oscillating submerged beam subject to wave field

**Summary:**

This test modeled the response of a submerged oscillating beam subjected to Morison's hydraulic forces (trail and inertial forces) in a fluid at rest or in the presence of a regular wave field.

The reference solution comes from a comparison with the EDF-R&DCALHYPSO code.

The proposed modellings are as follows:

- Modelling A: In this Modelling, the calculation of drag forces for an oscillating beam in a resting fluid is tested.
- Modelling B: In this Modelling, the calculation of drag forces and the weight added for an oscillating beam in a resting fluid is tested.
- Modelling C: In this Modelling, the calculation of drag and inertia forces for an oscillating beam in a regular wave field is tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and boundary conditions

The figure below shows the Reference problem: a hollow cylindrical beam of length $L=1m$ , external radius $R=0.5m$ and thickness $\mathit{ep}=0.01m$ . This one is immersed at $2.1m$ under the free surface in a column of water of height $h=30m$ . The ends of the beam ss- press on two identical springs, themselves clamped at their ends.

${S}_{w}=\pi {R}^{2}$ is noted the hydraulic cross section (seen by the fluid) transversal to the beam and $S=\pi (2.R.\mathit{ep}-{\mathit{ep}}^{2})$ the actual cross section.

![Figure](/figures/v5.02.141/100002010000041E0000037822C654519AEB00DB.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

The material has an isotropic elastic behavior:

| Young's modulus | $200\mathit{GPa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $0,3$ |
| Density of beam $\rho$ | $7800\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| Volumetric mass ${\rho}_{w}$ | $1000\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| Rigidity of springs following z | $50.0N/m$ |
| Trail coefficient ${C}_{d}$ | $1.0$ |
| ${C}_{a}$ added mass coefficient | $0.9$ |

### 1.3 Swell Cinematic

A single-directional, single-directional, single-directional swell is considered to be in the direction of +y, of order 1 (d-Airy) height, H-period, T-period, and $\lambda$ wavelength, see diagram below.

![Figure](/figures/v5.02.141/10000201000002FE000002147714A742CEA040D3.png)

*The free surface* $\eta$ depending on $y$ space and $t$ time is expressed:

$$
\eta (y,t)=\frac{H}{2}\cos(ky-\omega t)
$$

with $k=\frac{2\pi }{\lambda}$ the number of wave and $\omega =\frac{2\pi }{T}$ the pulse.

*The speed of the fluid* ${V}_{w}=(u,v,w)$ is as follows:

$$
\begin{array}{c}u(y,z,t)=0\\ v(y,z,t)=\frac{H}{2}\omega \frac{\sinh(k(h+z))}{\sinh(\mathit{kh})}\sin(k.y-\omega t)\\ w(y,z,t)=\frac{H}{2}\omega \frac{\sinh(k(h+z))}{\sinh(kh)}\sin(k.y-\omega t)\end{array}
$$

*L-acceleration of the fluid* is noted ${a}_{w}=\frac{\partial {v}_{w}}{\partial t}$.

### 1.4 Loading

The initial equilibrium position of the problem is, starting from the reference position described in Geometry , to apply a translational displacement of the $1m$ beam in the +z direction. This imposed displacement is then released leading the beam to adopt a dynamic oscillatory motion.

Note that Archimedes' weight and thrust are not taken into account in this problem.

**Model A**

The oscillating beam is subjected to Morison's (trailer term only) efforts in a resting fluid.

$$
{F}_{\text{mor}}=\frac{1}{2}{C}_{d}{\rho}_{w}{S}_{w}({v}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}-{v}_{s}^{\text{ortho}})({v}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}-{v}_{s}^{\text{ortho}})
$$

where ${v}_{w}$ and ${v}_{s}$ respectively refer to the fluid speed (nil here) and solid speed (inlet parameter of mechanical loading FORCE_POUTRE). The symbol $\text{ortho}$ represents the projection in the normal direction at the neutral fibre of beam 1
It is noted that these are linear forces which are applied in a distributed way on the beam.

**Model B**

The oscillating beam is subjected to the forces of Morison (trailer term and inertia terms) in a resting fluid

$$
{F}_{\text{mor}}=\frac{1}{2}{C}_{d}{\rho}_{w}{S}_{w}({v}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}-{v}_{s}^{\text{ortho}})({v}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}-{v}_{s}^{\text{ortho}})+{\rho}_{w}{S}_{w}{a}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}+{C}_{a}{\rho}_{w}{S}_{w}({a}_{w}^{\text{ortho}}-{a}_{s}^{\text{ortho}})
$$

The first term (corresponding to drag) is identical to model A, the second and third correspond to the terms of inertia. The acceleration of the fluid being zero, the latter reduce to the term of solid added mass ${-C}_{a}{\rho}_{w}{S}_{w}{a}_{s}^{\text{ortho}}$ . This is taken into account in the system mass matrix by changing the density of the beam:

$$
{\rho}^{\text{*}}=\rho +{C}_{a}{\rho}_{w}\frac{{S}_{w}}{S}
$$

*Note: Note that this change in density must be accompanied by a suitable change in gravity to properly reflect the weight of the beam. However, it is recalled that this force is not taken into account in our problem.*

**Model C**

The oscillating beam is subjected to the forces of Morison (see model B) in a fluid subjected to a wave of height $H=3m$ and period $T=12s$. The fluid speed and acceleration are defined in Swell Cinematic.

The term solid added mass is taken into account in the mass matrix in the same way as for model B by changing the density of the beam.

*General note: Morison's forces depend on the position of the beam, they must be mentioned as follow-up loads in the calculations.*

This projection also requires switching to the input parameter of FORCE_POUTRE l-beam orientation.
