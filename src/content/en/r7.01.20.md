# r7.01.20 Behaviour of steel subjected to corrosion

**Summary:**

This documentation presents the constitutive model of steel subjected to corrosion, which makes it possible to describe the mechanical behaviour of corroded reinforcement in reinforced-concrete structures. This model is developed in 1D and 3D; it is elastoplastic and damageable, with isotropic hardening, and it is based on the Lemaître model. This behaviour is used for analyses predicting the load-bearing capacity of reinforced-concrete structures affected by corrosion in the case of unidimensional loading.

It is implemented under the name CORR_ACIER in 3D and in 1D (for the elements BARRE and multifibre beams). It also works with the option DEBORST for plane stresses (shell elements); the equations are integrated numerically by an implicit scheme of M. Ortiz and J.C. Simo, with the plastic multiplier obtained by linearising the threshold function with respect to the internal variables and by imposing satisfaction of the criterion at convergence.

## 1 Introduction

This document presents a constitutive law for steel subjected to corrosion in civil-engineering structures. This law was formulated on the basis of the Lemaître model (plasticity coupled with damage). Following tensile tests carried out on corroded bars, an influence of corrosion on the plastic strain at failure is observed (the plastic strain of the corroded bars decreases as the degree of corrosion increases). The plastic strain at failure of the Lemaître model is related to the corrosion rate (the diameter of the corroded bar relative to that of the uncorroded bar in 1D, or the thickness of the corroded flat reinforcement relative to that of the uncorroded reinforcement in 3D).

## 2 Description of the model

From a thermodynamic point of view, in order to represent the behaviour of the hardenable and damageable plastic material, the model replaces, in the threshold surface, the stress $\sigma$ by the effective stress:

$\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{\sigma}{1-D}$                                         eq 2-1

where one has:

- $\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma$ for a virgin material
- $\tilde{\sigma}=0$ at the moment of failure

A particular form of the Helmholtz free energy is postulated:

$\text{Y=}\frac{1}{\rho}\left[\frac{1}{2}(1-D)(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{p})E(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{p})\text{+R}(r)\right]$                         eq 2-2

$\rho$ the density; $\Psi$ the state potential; $E$ Young's modulus; $D$ the damage variable; $\varepsilon$ the total strain; ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ the plastic strain; $R(r)$ the isotropic hardening function; $r$ the variable associated with $R$.

Thus posed, the potential is separated into two distinct parts. The first corresponds to the classical damage–elasticity coupling, the second term to the hardening.

The state laws describing this potential are:

Assuming that the material obeys the von Mises criterion, the flow criterion is expressed by:

$\text{f=}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{1-D}-R-{\sigma}_{y}\le 0$                                         eq 2-4

where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={(\frac{3}{2}\sigma \text{':}\sigma ')}^{\frac{1}{2}}$ is the equivalent stress in the Mises sense, with $\sigma'=\sigma-{\sigma}_{H}1$ the deviator of the stress matrix and ${\sigma}_{H}=\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}(\sigma)$ the hydrostatic stress.

The damage potential is chosen as a power function of the variable associated with the damage $-Y$:

${\phi}_{D}^{\text{*}}=(\frac{{S}_{0}}{{s}_{0}+1})(\frac{1}{1-D}){(-\frac{Y}{{S}_{0}})}^{{s}_{0}+1}$                             eq 2-5

where ${s}_{0}$ and ${S}_{0}$ are coefficients characteristic of the material.

The generalised normality rule supplies the flow law and the evolutions of the internal variables:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma}\\ \dot{r}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}\\ \dot{D}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{D}^{\text{*}}}{\partial Y}\end{array}$                                     eq 2-6

$\dot{\lambda}$ is the plastic multiplier.

In this model CORR_ACIER, the internal variables introduced in *Code_Aster* are:

- $p$: accumulated plastic strain, such that $\dot{p}={(\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}:{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p})}^{1/2}$;
- $D$: scalar isotropic-damage variable.

If $\text{f=}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{1-D}-R-{\sigma}_{y}>0$, one is in the plastic domain:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}(\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{1-D})\frac{\sigma'}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\\ \dot{r}=\lambda=\dot{p}(1-D)\\ \dot{D}={(-\frac{Y}{{S}_{0}})}^{{s}_{0}}\dot{p}\end{array}$                                 eq 2-7

In the one-dimensional case, the equivalent stress in the Mises sense is ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\mid \sigma\mid$ and the accumulated plastic strain is equal to the absolute value of the one-dimensional plastic strain: $p=\mid {\varepsilon}^{p}\mid$

In order to formulate an isotropic damage criterion, it is postulated that the damage mechanism is governed by the total elastic strain energy (distortion energy + volumetric strain energy). By analogy with the equivalent stress in plasticity, writing that the energy of a three-dimensional state is equal to that of the equivalent one-dimensional state, defined by an equivalent damage stress ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{*}}$, one finds:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{*}}={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}{\left[\frac{2}{3}(1+\nu)+3(1-2\nu){(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}})}^{2}\right]}^{1/2}$                 eq 2-8

On the other hand, it is shown that the variable associated with the damage is expressed, in the isotropic case, by $-\text{Y=}\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}_{\text{eq}}^{{\text{*}}^{2}}}{2E}$, hence:

$-\text{Y=}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}{2E{\left(1-D\right)}^{2}}\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\nu \right)+3\left(1-2\nu \right){\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\right)}^{2}\right]$                 eq 2-9

whence:

$\dot{D}={\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}{2{\text{ES}}_{0}(1-D{)}^{2}}\right)}^{{s}_{0}}{\left[\frac{2}{3}\left(1+\nu \right)+3\left(1-2\nu \right){\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\right)}^{2}\right]}^{{s}_{0}}\dot{p}$         eq 2-10

Ductile damage intervenes only beyond a threshold:

${f}_{\text{end}}\text{=p}-{p}_{D}>0$                                         eq 2-11

where ${p}_{D}$ is the strain which corresponds to the onset of damage (see [r7.01.02-figure-2-a]). One may then consider that the hardening is saturated in this domain, that is that the behaviour of the equivalent undamaged material would be perfectly plastic. This simplifying assumption allows the analytical integration of the model and leads to an evolution linear in the plastic strain. Indeed, one has in this case:

$\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{1-D}={\tilde{\sigma}}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}=\mathrm{Cte}$                                     eq 2-12

If one restricts oneself to the case of radial loading, for which the triaxiality ratio ${\sigma}_{H}/{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ is constant, one obtains, taking as initial condition $\text{D=}0$ for ${\text{p<p}}_{D}$:

$D={(\frac{{\sigma}_{y}^{2}}{2{\text{ES}}_{0}})}^{{s}_{0}}{\left[\frac{2}{3}(1+\nu)+3(1-2\nu){(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}})}^{2}\right]}^{{s}_{0}}\langle p-{p}_{D}\rangle$         eq 2-13

This expression can be simplified by introducing the failure condition $p={p}_{R}$, which is the accumulated plastic strain at failure => $D={D}_{c}$ (critical damage)

${D}_{c}={(\frac{{\sigma}_{y}^{2}}{2{\text{ES}}_{0}})}^{{s}_{0}}{\left[\frac{2}{3}(1+\nu)+3(1-2\nu){(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}})}^{2}\right]}^{{s}_{0}}\langle {p}_{R}-{p}_{D}\rangle$         eq 2-14

Substituting into the expression for *D*, one obtains:

$\text{D=}\frac{{D}_{c}}{{p}_{R}-{p}_{D}}(p-{p}_{D})$                                     eq 2-15

The hardening law of the steel integrated into the steel model is the following (excluding damage):

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-{\sigma}_{y}{\text{=Kp}}^{1/m}$                                             eq 2-16

$K$ *,* $m$ *,* ${\sigma}_{y}$ are the material parameters, supplied in DEFI_MATERIAU / CORR_ACIER by the keywords ECRO_K, ECRO_M.

## 3 Calculation of the material parameters

The results of the tensile tests of A.A. Almusallam were used to identify the constitutive law of the uncorroded steel and the dependence of the plastic strain at failure on the corrosion rate.

### 3.1 Constitutive law of the steel

Unalloyed steel is the principal type of steel used in civil engineering. The unidimensional constitutive model of unalloyed steel under monotonic unidirectional loading must be determined from the results of a tensile test on an uncorroded bar or flat specimen. An example of the numerical simulation required in order to determine this constitutive law is presented in [Figure 3.2-a].

### 3.2 Taking corrosion into account

The presence of corrosion has two effects on the reinforcement in reinforced-concrete structures:

- a reduction of the cross-section;
- a reduction of ${\epsilon}_{R}$ as a function of ${T}_{c}$:

The reduction of cross-section takes the form of a reduction of the diameter for bars, or of a reduction of thickness for plates:

${T}_{c}=100\left(\frac{{d}_{\text{corrodé}}^{2}}{{d}_{\text{noncorrodé}}^{2}}\right)$ or ${T}_{c}=100\left(\frac{{e}_{\text{corrodé}}}{{e}_{\text{noncorrodé}}}\right)$                     eq 3.2- 1

Note: The reduction of cross-section is not treated at the level of the constitutive model; it must be taken into account at the level of the command file, in AFFE_CARA_ELEM for example.

In the uniaxial case, the plastic strain at failure ${\varepsilon}_{R}$ depends on the corrosion rate. This evolution is presented in [Figure 3.2-b].

From these experimental data (tensile tests) one deduces the variation of the plastic strain at failure as a function of the corrosion rate:

${T}_{c}<\text{15\%}\Rightarrow \text{}{\epsilon}_{R}\text{=-0}.0111\text{Tc+}0.2345$                             eq 3.2- 2

${T}_{c}>\text{15\%}\Rightarrow {\epsilon}_{R}\text{=-0}.0006\text{Tc+}0.051$                             eq 3.2- 3

By analysis of various tensile tests, it is found that the behaviour of the corroded reinforcement is quasi-brittle and ${\epsilon}_{D}=0.8\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\epsilon}_{R}$ (${\epsilon}_{D}$ at the peak).

In order to integrate the model in 3D, the critical accumulated plastic strain is computed using ${p}_{R}={\left(\frac{2}{3}{\epsilon}_{R}:{\epsilon}_{R}\right)}^{1/2}$, taking into account that the uniaxial state is defined by a one-dimensional state in stress but a three-dimensional state in strain:

${\epsilon}_{R}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\epsilon}_{R}& 0& 0\\ 0& -{\nu}^{\text{*}}{\epsilon}_{R}& 0\\ 0& 0& -{\nu}^{\text{*}}{\epsilon}_{R}\end{array}\right]$                         eq 3.2-4

where ${\nu}^{\text{*}}$ is the contraction coefficient, equal to Poisson's ratio $\nu$ in elasticity:

${\nu}^{\text{*}}=\nu \frac{{\varepsilon}^{e}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}}{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{{\varepsilon}^{e}}{\varepsilon}(\frac{1}{2}-\nu )$                     eq 3.2-5

here $\epsilon ={\epsilon}_{R}$ and ${\varepsilon}^{e}$ is approximated by: ${\varepsilon}_{y}=\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E}$

${\nu}^{\text{*}}=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{{\varepsilon}_{y}}{{\varepsilon}_{R}}(\frac{1}{2}-\nu )$                                 eq 3.2-6

For the calculation of ${p}_{D}$, it is considered that the triaxiality ratio at the damage threshold is identical to that at failure:

${p}_{D}=0,8{p}_{R}$                                 eq 3.2-7

## 4 Numerical solution

### 4.1 Integration of the model

Integration of the model is carried out in two steps; first an elastic prediction of the stress:

1st step: elasticity $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\dot{\epsilon}={\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}+{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}\\ \dot{\sigma}=E{\dot{\epsilon}}^{e}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}=0\\ \dot{p}=0\\ \dot{D}=0\end{array}$                             eq 4.1-1

It is noted in particular that the damage $D$ does not appear in the elastic stress–strain relation (no coefficient $(1-D)$ in the elastic coefficients).

The second step consists in an implicit updating of the internal variables of the model by corrections applied at zero total strain. In this step, one iterates between plasticity and damage.

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\dot{\varepsilon}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{e}+{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}\text{=0}\\ \dot{\sigma}=E{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}\\ {\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\\ \dot{r}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial R}\end{array}$                                     eq 4.1-2

The expression for the plastic multiplier is obtained by linearising the threshold function with respect to the internal variables and by imposing satisfaction of the criterion at convergence:

${\text{f=f}}^{-}+\frac{\partial {f}^{-}}{\partial \sigma }(\sigma -{\sigma}^{-})+\frac{\partial {f}^{-}}{\partial R}(R-{R}^{-})=0$                     eq 4.1-3

Consider the discretisation of the relaxation equations:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\sigma -{\sigma}^{-}\text{=-}\Delta \lambda \mathrm{E}\frac{\partial {f}^{-}}{\partial \sigma }\\ R-{R}^{-}{\text{=R}}^{'}(r)\Delta r\\ \Delta \text{p=}\frac{\Delta \lambda }{1-{D}^{-}}\\ {R}^{'}(p)=\frac{K}{m}{\left[\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{(1-{D}^{-})K}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{K}\right]}^{(1-m)}\end{array}$                     eq 4.1-4

The expression for the plastic multiplier is obtained by substituting [eq 4.1-3] into [eq 4.1-4], ($\Delta \text{r=}\Delta \lambda$ and, for $D$ fixed at ${D}^{-}$, $\text{R'}(p)\text{=R'}(r)$)

$\Delta \lambda =\frac{{f}^{-}}{\frac{\partial {f}^{-}}{\partial \sigma }E\frac{\partial {\phi }^{-}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}^{-}}{\partial R}\frac{K}{m}{\left[\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{K(1-{D}^{-})}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{K}\right]}^{(1-m)}}$                 eq 4.1-5

The expressions for the various derivatives of the threshold surface and of the dissipation potential are given in the following equations:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\sigma '}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(1-D)}\\ \frac{\partial \phi }{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\sigma '}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(1-D)}\\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial R}=-1\end{array}$                                     eq 4.1-6

In the 1D case:

$\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \sigma }=\frac{\text{sgn}(\sigma )}{1-D}$                                     eq 4.1-7b

The value obtained for the plastic multiplier at each integration is re-injected into the relaxation equations until convergence (return of the stress onto the threshold surface). The criterion retained consists in stopping when the value of the threshold at the following iteration has become sufficiently small relative to ${\sigma}_{y}$:

$\mid f\mid \le \text{tol}.{\sigma}_{y}$                                         eq 4.1-8

For a direct use of the normality properties of the threshold surfaces, the calculation to be carried out at each iteration reduces solely to the calculation of the plasticity multiplier allowing the corrections if necessary (no calculation of a Jacobian as for Newton methods) [bib1], [bib6].

The following step is the damage, which takes into account the variation of the damage variable. The threshold function of the damage is the following:

${f}_{\text{end}}\text{=p}-{p}_{D}$                                         eq 4.1- 9

if ${f}_{\text{end}}>0$ the damage is computed and one then returns to the plasticity correction:

$\text{D=}\frac{{D}_{C}}{{p}_{R}-{p}_{D}}(p-{p}_{D})$                                 eq 4.1-10

Note: in practice, $D$ evolves even if ${\text{p>p}}_{R}$; $D$ is limited to 0.99 at most. ${D}_{c}$ is defined by the keyword D_CORR of DEFI_MATERIAU / CORR_ACIER

The same approach is retained for convergence:

$\mid f\mid \le \text{tol}.{\sigma}_{y}$                                     eq 4.1- 11

The reader is referred to reference [bib1] and to [bib6] for all the details concerning this method and this algorithm.

### 4.2 Calculation of the tangent matrix

The aim is to compute the tangent matrices (continuous and consistent) for the plastic part and the damage part. The options RIGI_MECA_TANG and FULL_MECA of the tangent matrix are computed for the 1D and 3D finite elements:

#### 4.2.1 1D finite element

In the plastic domain:

$\delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\delta \lambda =\frac{\delta {f}^{-}}{E+\frac{K}{m}{p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}}=\frac{E\delta \varepsilon }{E+\frac{K}{m}{p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}}$                 eq 4.2.1-1

$\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=E(\delta \varepsilon -\delta {\varepsilon}^{p})=\frac{\frac{K}{m}{p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}}{1+\frac{K}{\text{mE}}{p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}}$                     eq 4.2.1-2

In the damage domain:

$\frac{\delta \sigma }{1-D}+\frac{\sigma}{{(1-D)}^{2}}\delta D-R'(p)\delta p=-\delta {f}^{-}$

$\frac{-E}{{(1-D)}^{2}}\delta \lambda +\frac{\sigma}{{(1-D)}^{2}}\frac{{D}_{c}}{{p}_{R}-{p}_{D}}\frac{\delta \lambda }{(1-D)}-\frac{K}{m}{p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}\frac{\delta \lambda }{(1-D)}=-\delta {f}^{-}=\frac{E}{(1-D)}\delta \varepsilon$

$\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=E(\delta \varepsilon -\delta {\varepsilon}^{p})=E\frac{\frac{K}{m}(1-D){p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}-\frac{\sigma}{(1-D)}\frac{{D}_{c}}{{p}_{R}-{p}_{D}}}{E+\frac{K}{m}(1-D){p}^{\frac{1}{m}-1}-\frac{\sigma}{(1-D)}\frac{{D}_{c}}{{p}_{R}-{p}_{D}}}$

#### 4.2.2 3D finite element

The absence of damage in the plastic part makes it possible to use the tangent matrix computed for the elastoplastic behaviour with linear kinematic hardening and non-linear isotropic hardening [bib7]:

$\frac{\partial \sigma }{\Delta \varepsilon }={\lambda}^{\text{*}}\overrightarrow{1}\otimes \overrightarrow{1}+2{\mu}^{\text{*}}\text{Id}-\xi \frac{9{\mu}^{2}}{H(p)}(1-\frac{{R}^{'}(p).\Delta p}{(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})})\frac{1}{{R}^{'}(p)+3\mu }(\frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})}\otimes \frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})})$ eq 4.2.2-1

with ${\lambda}^{\text{*}}=K-\frac{2\mu }{\mathrm{3H}(\Delta p)}2{\mu}^{\text{*}}=\frac{2\mu }{H(\Delta p)}H(\Delta p)=1+\frac{3\mu \xi .\Delta p}{(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})}$

The initial tangent matrix, used by the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, is obtained by adopting the behaviour of the previous step (elastic or plastic, indicated by an internal variable $\xi$ equal to 0 or 1) and by taking $\Delta p=0$ in equation **eq 4.2.2-2**.

Taking the presence of the damage into account, the following tangent matrix is obtained:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }={\lambda}^{\text{*}}\overrightarrow{1}\otimes \overrightarrow{1}+2{\mu}^{\text{*}}\text{Id}-\xi \frac{9{\mu}^{2}}{H(p)}(1-\frac{\Delta p((1-D){R}^{'}(p)-R(p){D}^{'}(p))}{(1-D)(R(p)-{\sigma}_{y})})\frac{1}{(3\mu +(1-D){R}^{'}(p)-R(p){D}^{'}(p))}\\ (\frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{(1-D)(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})}\otimes \frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{(1-D)(R(p)+{\sigma}_{y})})\end{array}$ eq 4.2.2-2

with ${\lambda}^{\text{*}}=K-\frac{2\mu }{\mathrm{3H}(\Delta p)}2{\mu}^{\text{*}}=\frac{2\mu }{H(\Delta p)}H(\Delta p)=1+\frac{3\mu \xi .\Delta p}{(1-D)(R(p)-{\sigma}_{y})}$

for the option FULL_MECA: ${\sigma}^{\mathrm{dev}}=\tilde{\sigma}$

for the option RIGI_MECA_TANG: ${\sigma}^{\mathrm{dev}}=\tilde{\sigma}$
