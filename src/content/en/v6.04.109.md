# v6.04.109 SSNV109 - Evaluation of cracking time (corrosion stress initiation) by BabY (Barbier-Bystricky)

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to capitalize the method of determining cracking time by the BabY (Barbier-Bystricky) model. The cracking time corresponds to the stress corrosion time. The cracking time obtained by the BaBy model is compared to the cracking time obtained by the index model.

Model A proposes a determination of crack time by a simple post-processing of a static nonlinear calculation (decoupled method).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A square plate of 50 mm side is considered, the top of which is dug by a disc of radius 40 mm, the centre of which is 80 mm from the bottom of the plate. The geometry is illustrated in the figure below. The top of the plate (line $\mathit{CD}$) is in contact with the corrosive medium.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.109/1000020100000391000002BD306CB4D798CEB53D.png)

The case is treated in axisymmetric, which amounts to considering a cylinder whose upper face is dug by a sphere.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is L-Alliage 600 at 350°C. Its behaviour is considered nonlinear visco-elastic, isotropic.

The elastic properties are:

- $E=200000\mathit{MPa}$
- $\nu =0,3$

Visco-elastic behaviour is modelled by a Lemaitre law, whose parameters are:

- $n=3,77$
- $m=4,26$
- $K(T)=\mathrm{17,67.}\exp(\frac{47500}{8,31(T+273,15)})$ with $T$ in °C. For $T=350°C$ , $K=170182$

The parameters to be entered in the DEFI_MATERIAU operator under the factor keyword LEMAITRE [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) are $n$ , $1/m$ and $1/K$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

As the case is axisymmetric, the $x$ axis displacement on the $\mathit{AC}$ side is blocked. To block rigid modes, the $y$ axis displacement on the point $A$ is blocked.

The load consists of applying $\mathit{BD}$ on the side of $0,1\mathit{mm}$ a displacement of $0,1\mathit{mm}$ on the $x$ axis, gradually up to $t=1h$, and then the load is kept constant up to $t=1000h$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Quantities tested

The quantities of interest are the cracking times obtained by the index method and by the BaBy (Barbier-Bystricky) model [1].

The crack time (in hours) of the index model is given by the following expression:

${t}_{f}=\frac{10000}{{i}_{T}.{i}_{M}.{i}_{\sigma}}$

où

- ${i}_{T}={\mathrm{9,49.10}}^{15.}\exp(\frac{-22000}{T+273,15})$ with $T$ in °C. For $T=350°C$ , ${i}_{T}=4,41$
- ${i}_{M}=1$
- ${i}_{\sigma}={\mathrm{2,44.10}}^{-11}{\sigma}^{4}$ , with $\sigma$ the largest main constraint (expressed in $\mathit{MPa}$ )

The cracking time of the BaBy model is obtained by solving a coupled mechanical-corrosion problem. A simplified version of the problem resolution consists of decoupled equations and treating corrosion equations only in post-processing of a nonlinear mechanical calculation. This is the strategy adopted in model A.

From the $\sigma$ stress field, post-processing evolution of the $p$ cumulative plastic deformation and ${c}_{h}$ corrosive concentration is determined:

$\begin{array}{c}\dot{p}={(\frac{{\Vert \sigma \Vert }_{\mathit{VM}}}{K(T)(1-{c}_{h})})}^{n}.{p}^{-n/m}\\ \dot{{c}_{h}}=\frac{3}{2}\beta \dot{p}\frac{⟨1/3\mathit{tr}\sigma ⟩}{{\Vert \sigma \Vert }_{\mathit{VM}}}\end{array}$

then the cracking time is the time such as ${c}_{h}({t}_{f})=1$ .

The $\beta$ parameter is a material parameter identified from a creep experiment alone.

This is $\beta =889,5$.

## 4 Summary of the results

This test allows capitalization of the time-to-crack method by the BabY (Barbier-Bystricky) model. The crack time is the stress corrosion time. The crack time obtained by the BaBy model is compared to the crack time obtained by the index model.
