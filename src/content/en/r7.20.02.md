# r7.20.02 Extrapolation of measurements onto a numerical model in dynamics

**Summary:**

A procedure for extrapolating results of experimental measurements in dynamics (displacements, velocities, accelerations, strains, stresses) onto a numerical model is presented. Based on a representation of the structure on a previously chosen projection basis, it consists in the solution of the inverse problem defined by the identification of the generalised coordinates relative to the projection basis. The solution proposed uses a minimisation, in the least-squares sense, using the LU decomposition or the singular value decomposition (SVD), of a functional possibly regularised by the addition of a criterion of proximity to a solution known a priori. In the case of a temporal identification, an explicit formulation of the a priori information is proposed.

> **Contents**

## 1 Problem statement

One wishes to estimate numerically the behaviour at every point of a structure from the measurements carried out at a few points of the structure. Given the costs and the constraints of accessibility, the experimental measurements are generally limited in number and located at places which are not necessarily the most highly loaded. Thus, in dynamics, knowledge of the stress-concentration zones and of the local values of the stresses is crucial in order to check the mechanical integrity of the equipment. One is then led to extrapolate results of localised measurements over the whole of the numerical mesh of the structure.

The extrapolation procedure proposed is based on a representation of the structure on a judiciously chosen projection basis (eigenmodes, static response, ...). It consists in the determination of the generalised coordinates relative to this projection basis. The solution proposed uses a minimisation, in the least-squares sense, by LU or singular value decomposition (SVD), of a functional possibly regularised by the addition of a criterion of proximity to a solution known a priori. In the case of a temporal identification, an explicit formulation of the a priori information is proposed.

## 3 Extrapolation procedure

The procedure which one wishes to put in place in order to extrapolate results of vibratory measurements onto a numerical model is broken down into 4 stages[bib1] :

![Figure](/figures/r7.20.02/Shape1.gif)

This procedure relies on the notion of projection of a field into a space of dimension lower than the dimension of the space of the numerical model, and then extrapolation onto the space of the numerical model. The fact of projecting the field into a space of reduced dimension necessarily generates a loss of information during the extrapolation. One therefore sees here the importance of the choice of the projection basis. This basis may be formed of a modal response and/or of a static response. It is assumed here that the numerical model is linear.

## 4 Relations between the physical quantities and the generalised quantities

One assumes that the intrinsic behaviour of the structure is represented in a space spanned by the ${N}_{\text{num}}$ projection-basis vectors. The Rayleigh-Ritz transformation establishes the relation between the degrees of freedom of the structure in the physical frame and its generalised coordinates:

$q=\Phi \eta$

In this formulation, the matrix of the basis vectors contains all the spatial information; the generalised coordinates, for their part, depend:

- on the time, in the case of a time-response analysis: $q(M,t)=\Phi (M)\eta (t)$
- on the angular frequency, in the case of a harmonic-response analysis: $q(M,\omega )=\Phi (M)\eta (\omega )$

One may therefore deduce very simply the following relations:

|  | Harmonic response | Time response |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Displacement | $q(M,\omega )=\Phi (M)\eta (\omega )$ | $q(M,t)=\Phi (M)\eta (t)$ |
| Velocity | $\dot{q}(M,w)=j\omega \Phi (M)\eta (\omega )$ | $\dot{q}(M,t)=\Phi (M)\dot{\eta}(t)$ |
| Acceleration | $\ddot{q}(M,w)=-{\omega}^{2}\Phi (M)\eta (\omega )$ | $\ddot{q}(M,t)=\Phi (M)\ddot{\eta}(t)$ |
| Strains | $\epsilon (M,\omega )={\Phi}_{\epsilon}(M)\eta (\omega )$ | $\epsilon (M,t)={\Phi}_{\epsilon}(M)\eta (t)$ |
| Stresses | $\sigma (M,\omega )={\Phi}_{\sigma}(M)\eta (\omega )$ | $\sigma (M,t)={\Phi}_{\sigma}(M)\eta (t)$ |

All these formulations therefore present an equivalent form: in the remainder of the document, we shall treat essentially the case of the time displacement, but the results obtained may be transposed to all the other quantities: velocity, acceleration, strain and stress.

Likewise, the relations established as a function of time are applicable in the spectral domain:

$\begin{array}{}\mathrm{TF}(q(M,t))=\Phi (M)\text{TF}(\eta (t))=\Phi (M)\eta (\omega )\\ {\text{TF}}^{-1}(q(M,\omega ))=\Phi (M){\text{TF}}^{-1}(\eta (\omega ))=\Phi (M)\eta (t)\end{array}$

## 5 Computation of the generalised coordinates

### 5.1 Formulation of the problem

The computation of the generalised coordinates $\eta$ is carried out on the matrix of the displacements (respectively velocities, accelerations, strains, stresses) restricted to the measured degrees of freedom, by solution of the matrix system:

${q}_{\exp}=\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\eta$

The dimensions of the matrix $\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}$ to be "inverted" are (${N}_{\exp},{N}_{\text{num}}$ ).

One sees here that the computation of the generalised coordinates is carried out in a restricted space: the dimension of the space spanned by the basis vectors is lower than the dimension of the numerical model; one exploits only the information at the measured degrees of freedom.

### 5.2 Determination of a quasi-solution

For the solution of the inverse problem, 3 cases may arise:

- ${N}_{\exp}={N}_{\text{num}}$ : the number of measured degrees of freedom is equal to the number of projection-basis vectors whose generalised coordinates one wishes to identify.

In this case, there exists a unique solution to the inversion problem: $\eta ={\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{-1}{q}_{\exp}$

- ${N}_{\exp}>{N}_{\text{num}}$ : the number of measured degrees of freedom is greater than the number of projection-basis vectors of the numerical model whose generalised coordinates one wishes to identify.

In this case, there exists no exact solution to the inversion problem. A quasi-solution may nevertheless be defined, which minimises the distance: $\mid {q}_{\exp}-\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\eta \mid$ . The formula $\eta ={\left[{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\right]}^{+}{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}{q}_{\exp}$ then supplies the (unique) solution in the least-squares sense. In this expression, the matrix ${\left[{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\right]}^{+}{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}$ denotes the generalised inverse matrix of $\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}$ . The computation of the pseudo-inverse may be carried out using the LU decomposition or the singular value decomposition (SVD).

- ${N}_{\exp}<{N}_{\text{num}}$ : the number of measured degrees of freedom is less than the number of projection-basis vectors whose generalised coordinates one wishes to identify (which corresponds to the most common case).

In this case, there exists an infinity of solutions to the inversion problem and the objective is to determine an acceptable solution by introducing an additional condition (minimum norm of the solution, or application of methods known as "regularisation methods").

### 5.3 Determination of a regularised inverse solution

#### 5.3.1 Principles of the regularisation methods

The aim of the regularisation methods [bib4], [bib5] is to propose an approximate solution which is stable with respect to the variations of the input data. One no longer seeks to solve the minimisation equation resulting from the formulation: ${q}_{\exp}=\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\eta$ , but to determine an approximate (or regularised) solution meeting two requirements:

- it satisfies a proximity condition: one seeks ${\eta}_{\delta}$ such that $\mid {q}_{\exp}-{\Phi}_{\text{num}}{\eta}_{\delta}\mid <\delta$ ,
- it meets an additional condition known as "a priori information".

The regularisation methods therefore consist in completing the statement of the problem by introducing a priori information in order to extract, from the family of solutions which are compatible with the experimental data, the one which corresponds best to the problem. This is done by merging, in a single criterion, a measure of the fidelity of the solution with respect to the experimental data and a measure of its fidelity to the a priori information [bib2].

An approach which may easily be implemented in finite dimension is regularisation by optimisation. To be compared with Tikhonov's regularisation method [bib3], it consists in considering an a priori solution ${\eta}_{\text{priori}}$ of the minimisation problem and in seeking the solution of the approximate system which is closest to this solution. One then seeks to minimise the following functional:

${\mid {q}_{\exp}-\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}\eta \mid }^{2}+a{\mid \eta -{\eta}_{\text{priori}}\mid }^{2}$

The parameter $a$ determines the weight assigned to the a priori information.

The solution of the minimisation equation is given by:

$\eta ={\left[{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}+aI\right]}^{-1}({\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}{q}_{\exp}+a{\eta}_{\text{priori}})$

or, by bringing out explicitly the deviation from the a priori solution:

$\eta ={\eta}_{\text{priori}}+{\left[{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}+aI\right]}^{-1}{\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}({q}_{\exp}-\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}{\eta}_{\text{priori}})$

If one sets ${\eta}_{\text{priori}}=0$ , this formulation consists in seeking the solution known as of "minimum norm" (or Tikhonov of order 0).

The addition of the regularising term linked to the matrix $aI$ has the role of shifting the spectrum of ${\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}}^{T}\stackrel{ˉ}{\Phi}$ in order to ensure the matrix-inversion stage. This analysis procedure therefore makes it possible to implement a better-conditioned computation procedure, which softens the effects of the noise and which supplies a physically acceptable solution.

Furthermore, the choice of the values of the matrix $aI$ results from a compromise between the stability of the sought solution and the confidence which one may place in the a priori solution.

#### 5.3.2 Choice of the a priori information

In the case of the regularisation methods, the choice of the a priori information constitutes a key stage which determines the representativeness of the final results. This choice may rely on a physical knowledge of the solution or on a knowledge of its evolution as a function of the parameter retained. We provide, in what follows, an example applied to the determination by minimisation of a time variable [bib1].

The minimisation of a variable as a function of time may be carried out at each time step independently of the preceding time step. The introduction of a priori information nevertheless makes it possible to enrich the functional by assuming a slow evolution of the variables determined:

${\eta}_{\text{priori}}(t)=\eta (t-\text{dt})$

This assumption is acceptable only when the sampling step is sufficiently small. Indeed, the solution at a given time instant is approximated by (Taylor expansion):

$\eta (t)=\eta (t-\text{dt})+\text{dt}\dot{\eta}(t-\text{dt})+o(\text{dt})$

The maximum response frequency of the structure is determined by the angular frequency of the mode of highest order ${\omega}_{\max}$ taken in the modelling. One therefore has:

$\mid \frac{\eta (t)-\eta (t-\text{dt})}{\eta (t-\text{dt})}\mid <{\omega}_{\max}\text{dt}+\mid o(\text{dt})\mid$

In order that the corrective term be small (and therefore that the a priori information constitute a first-order approximation of the sought solution), the sampling step must satisfy:

$\text{dt}\text{<<}\frac{1}{{\omega}_{\max}}$

At the initial time instant (t=0), given that one has no information on the a priori solution, the analysis is carried out by seeking the minimum-norm solution. In order to avoid propagating the error which results from this, it may be necessary to assign a low confidence to the a priori information over the first time steps (by means of the parameter ) and to exploit the results only from the time instant at which one may consider that the errors have been sufficiently attenuated. If need be, complementary studies will be carried out in order to determine the optimal parameters for use of the feature developed in *Code_Aster* .

In the frequency domain, numerous possibilities are offered for determining the a priori information. They rely either on a physical knowledge of the solution (experimental highlighting of resonances or of forced responses), or on a formulation of the generalised displacements as a function of the frequency (type: gain functions), in which case the minimisation finally leads to characterising the dynamic loadings.

## 6 Implementation in Code_Aster

The projection basis is constituted either of the dynamic modes computed by the command CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02) stored in a concept of type mode_meca, or of the dynamic modes and the static modes computed by the command DEFI_BASE_MODALE [U4.64.02](doc:u4.64.02) stored in a concept of type base_modale.

The phase of computation of the generalised coordinates is treated by the command PROJ_MESU_MODAL [U4.73.01](doc:u4.73.01). The data are gathered there under 4 factor keywords.

The data relating to the numerical model (projection basis) are gathered under the factor keyword MODELE_CALCUL. One specifies there the numerical model and the projection basis.

The data relating to the measurements are gathered under the factor keyword MODELE_MESURE. One specifies there in particular the model associated with the structure and the measurement read by the command LIRE_RESU.

The possible manual spatial association of the nodes is given under the factor keyword CORR_MANU.

The data concerning the solution of the inverse problem are gathered under the factor keyword RESOLUTION. One specifies there the decomposition method employed (LU, SVD) and the taking into account of the regularisation term.

The restitution of the results in the physical basis may then be carried out by the command REST_GENE_PHYS [U4.63.31](doc:u4.63.31).
