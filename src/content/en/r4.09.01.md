# r4.09.01 Energy balance in thermo-mechanics

**Summary:**

This documentation presents the writing of the energy balance for an analysis with *Code_Aster*. The computation of the energy balance can be activated in the operators STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03), DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) and DYNA_VIBRA[U4.53.03] on a physical basis thanks to the option ENERGIE=_F(). This functionality therefore covers the thermo-hydro-mechanical analyses in quasi-statics and in dynamics. Excluded are the thermal analyses (operators THER_LINEAIRE, THER_NON_LINE and THER_NON_LINE_MO) and the modal analyses (operator DYNA_VIBRA on a modal basis).

## 1 Computation of the energy balance of an analysis

The energy balance of an analysis can be very useful information. On the one hand, it makes it possible to take a critical look at the quality of the results, in the same way as an equilibrium residual in the Newton algorithm. On the other hand, it provides information on the physical phenomena involved and brings answers to the following questions: what is the energy transmitted to my system? What becomes of it?

To do this, one establishes in what follows the writing of the energy balance for an analysis with *Code_Aster* on the complete model. We distinguish the nature of each energy term and we take care to define its origin.

### 1.1 Work of a force

Let us consider the evolution at various time instants ${t}_{i}$ of a deformable solid subjected to a variable external force $\mathrm{F}(t)$ . Let ${\mathrm{U}}_{i}$ be the displacement-field solution for each time instant ${t}_{i}$ and ${\mathrm{F}}_{i}=\mathrm{F}({t}_{i})$ . The curve of the external force as a function of the displacement is given on the . From the energy point of view, the increment of external work supplied to the system between two consecutive time instants $({t}_{0},{t}_{1})$ represents the area under the curve, which is computed by the trapezium method:

$$
{A}_{1}={\int}_{{t}_{0}}^{{t}_{1}}\mathrm{F}(t).\dot{\mathrm{U}}(t)=\frac{{\mathrm{F}}_{0}+{\mathrm{F}}_{1}}{2}.({\mathrm{U}}_{1}-{\mathrm{U}}_{0})={\bar{\mathrm{F}}}_{1}.{\Delta \mathrm{U}}_{1}
$$

where:

- ${\bar{\mathrm{F}}}_{1}$ represents the mean force over the time increment (the index is chosen as being that of the end time),
- ${\Delta \mathrm{U}}_{1}$ represents the displacement increment between ${t}_{0}$ and ${t}_{1}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r4.09.01/100000000000039400000313AD6E8EBF7C706BA0.png)

Figure 1.1-1: Force–displacement curve.

The increment of work of any force may therefore be considered as the scalar product of two vectors: the mean force over the time step and the displacement increment.

This method of computing the increment of work of a force is practical within the framework of numerical simulation, since the solution is known at various time instants only, corresponding to the chosen time discretisation.

### 1.2 Energy balance

Let us consider the dynamic equilibrium equation of a system in its form discretised in space:

$$
\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{U}}(t)+\mathrm{C}(t)\dot{\mathrm{U}}(t)+\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{U}(t),\dot{\mathrm{U}}(t),t)=\mathrm{L}(t)
$$

where:

- $\mathrm{U}$ , $\dot{\mathrm{U}}$ and $\ddot{\mathrm{U}}$ are the vectors of the displacements, velocities and accelerations at the nodes,
- $\mathrm{M}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ are the mass and damping matrices,
- $\mathrm{R}$ represents the internal forces (in linear elasticity, this is the term $\mathrm{K}\mathrm{U}$ ), from which one has removed what is considered as the damping forces,
- $\mathrm{L}$ is the vector of the external forces.

This writing, inspired by [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05), is here simplified with respect to the original writing which brings in Lagrange multipliers for the Dirichlet boundary conditions and the unilateral conditions. Note that the damping matrix $\mathrm{C}\left(t\right)$ may depend on time if its construction is based on the tangent stiffness matrix and not the elastic one (option AMOR_RAYL_RIGI in the operator DYNA_NON_LINE).

Each term of equation ( ) is homogeneous to a force. The energy evolution of the system between two consecutive analysis time instants ${t}_{0}$ and ${t}_{1}$ is translated directly by the work of each of these forces. Taking up again the notations of mean quantity (grandeur) and increment of quantity used in equation ( ), one obtains:

- the increment of kinetic energy:

$$
\Delta {E}_{\mathit{cin}}={\int}_{{\mathrm{U}}_{0}}^{{\mathrm{U}}_{1}}{\left(\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{U}}\right)}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathrm{dU}={\int}_{{t}_{0}}^{{t}_{1}}{\left(\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{U}}\right)}^{\mathrm{T}}\dot{\mathrm{U}}\mathit{dt}={\left[\frac{1}{2}{\dot{\mathrm{U}}}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathrm{M}\dot{\mathrm{U}}\right]}_{{t}_{0}}^{{t}_{1}}={\overline{\dot{\mathrm{U}}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathrm{M}\Delta {\dot{\mathrm{U}}}_{1}
$$

One recovers an expression identical to equation ( ) by considering as force $\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{U}}$ , the operator $\mathrm{M}$ being assumed constant.

- the increment of energy dissipated by damping:

$$
\Delta {W}_{\mathit{amor}}={\overline{\mathrm{C}\left(\mathrm{t}\right)\dot{\mathrm{U}}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}.\Delta {\mathrm{U}}_{1}
$$

As the damping matrix may be time-dependent, one must evaluate the quantity $\mathrm{C}\left(\mathrm{t}\right)\dot{\mathrm{U}}$ at ${t}_{0}$ and at ${t}_{1}$ , then take its mean. This stage is neglected, and one in fact computes the following quantity:

$$
\Delta {W}_{\mathit{amor}}=\overline{{\dot{\mathrm{U}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}}\mathrm{C}\left({t}_{1}\right)\Delta {\mathrm{U}}_{1}
 L'énergie dissipée par amortissement est donc calculée de manière approchée.
$$

- The increment of total strain energy:

$$
\Delta {E}_{\mathit{tot}}={\overline{\mathrm{R}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\Delta {\mathrm{U}}_{1}
$$

- The increment of work of the external forces:

$$
\Delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}={\overline{\mathrm{L}}}_{1}^{\mathrm{T}}\Delta {\mathrm{U}}_{1}
$$

In the presence of frictional contact, an additional force term appears in the equilibrium equation, corresponding to the contact force. From the energy point of view, its contribution is computed easily in a manner similar to ( ), but it cannot be taken into account in any of the energies defined above. One therefore creates a new energy which represents the energy stored and/or dissipated by the link forces, which we name $\Delta {W}_{\mathit{liai}}$ . The mechanical energy balance of the system is then written:

$$
\Delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}=\Delta {E}_{\mathit{cin}}+\Delta {E}_{\mathit{tot}}+\Delta {W}_{\mathit{amor}}+\Delta {W}_{\mathit{liai}}
$$

Under the assumption that the solution is strictly exact, the left-hand term is equal to the sum of the right-hand terms. In reality, the numerical solution scheme induces discrepancies for various reasons:

- the Newton algorithm is an iterative solution algorithm, whose convergence is detected when the relative equilibrium residual of the forces is lower than a certain criterion. By definition, the solution found is therefore correct to within a tolerance, which inevitably creates an equilibrium discrepancy in the energy balance. One can then improve the energy balance by tightening the convergence criterion, but the accuracy reached with the default criterion is generally satisfactory.
- Among the numerical time-integration schemes, only some are not dissipative. Modifying the default values of these schemes, such as the Newmark scheme, leads to a numerical dissipation. The user may refer to the documentation [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05) for more information on the dissipation of the numerical time-integration schemes.

The energy balance finds here a first utility of importance: it makes it possible to quantify the energy dissipated by the numerical scheme. In shock analyses for example, it is common to use dissipative numerical schemes in order to filter the high frequencies. One thus defines the energy dissipated by the numerical scheme:

$$
\Delta {W}_{\mathit{sch}}=\Delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}-\Delta {E}_{\mathit{cin}}-\Delta {E}_{\mathit{tot}}-\Delta {W}_{\mathit{amor}}-\Delta {W}_{\mathit{liai}}
$$

### 1.3 Construction of the various energy terms

An energy is considered as the work of a force. Numerically, the computation of an energy term consists first of all in building a global vector corresponding to the sum of all the forces whose contribution to the energy balance is stored in the same term. There exist numerous force vectors and we define in this part those which feed each of the energy terms.

#### 1.3.1 Work of the external forces

One defines as external work the work:

- of all the forces associated with the commands AFFE_CHAR_MECA and AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F, with the exception of FORCE_SOL accounted for in the work of the link forces,
- of the forces coming from the analysis by substructuring (use of macro-elements),
- of all the forces associated with the commands AFFE_CHAR_CINE and AFFE_CHAR_CINE_F. In the strict sense of the term, the command AFFE_CHAR_CINE makes it possible to define boundary conditions of imposed displacement, whose work ought to be accounted for as link energy. For practical reasons, its contribution is added to the external work. In general, it is possible to recover the force corresponding to this imposed displacement. Certain cases may nevertheless pose a problem, as for example the test case ssnp155a (see §  2.1.2.1 ).

#### 1.3.2 Work of the link forces

One defines as work of the link forces the work:

- of the contact and friction forces coming from the command DEFI_CONTACT,
- of the force coming from the absorbing-boundary elements,
- of the force coming from the option FORCE_SOL in the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

#### 1.3.3 Work of the damping forces

One defines as work of the damping forces the work:

- of the damping force coming from the damping matrix (term $\mathrm{C}\dot{\mathrm{U}}=(\alpha \mathrm{K}+\beta \mathrm{M})\dot{\mathrm{U}}$ ),
- of the modal damping force (keyword AMOR_MODAL in the analysis operators).

#### 1.3.4 Total strain energy

The total strain energy groups all the contributions which require integrating a constitutive law. This may lead to arbitrary choices. For example, one accounts in the total strain energy for the contribution of the joint elements or of the cohesive elements, although it would be more natural to include them in the computation of the energy linked to the links. Likewise, when one uses constitutive laws to represent the contact with the discrete elements, the contribution is included in the total strain energy whereas it would be more in its place in the energy linked to the links. The rule is that, when it is a constitutive behaviour carried by finite elements defined in the model, then the contribution is added to ${E}_{\mathit{tot}}$ .

### 1.4 Taking an initial state into account

Let us consider the example of a mass–spring system subjected to an initial shock. In *Code_Aster*, the user can simply give an initial acceleration. This corresponds to the following equation:

| $\begin{array}{c}m\ddot{u}\left(t\right)+ku\left(t\right)=F\left(t\right)\\ u=0\\ F=0\\ \ddot{u}\ne 0\end{array}$ with as initial conditions: $\begin{array}{c}u(0)=0\\ \dot{u}(0)=0\\ F(0)=0\\ \ddot{u}(0)\ne 0\end{array}$ |  |
| --- | --- |

One notes that there is an incompatibility: the user supplies an unbalanced initial state. Strictly speaking, the equations of dynamics being of second order in time, there can be only a displacement or a velocity imposed at the initial time instant. If one wishes to impose an initial acceleration, one should in principle give the force which balances the equation of the system. The numerical scheme does not need it because this does not influence the solution. Nevertheless, this influences the computation of the energy balance because without this initial force, the balance is incorrect. Indeed, the energy equilibrium over the first time step is then written:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{W}_{\mathit{ext}}={E}_{\mathit{tot}}+{E}_{\mathit{cin}}\\ {W}_{\mathit{ext}}=0\\ {E}_{\mathit{tot}}>0\\ {E}_{\mathit{cin}}>0\end{array}
$$

The contribution of the external force which corresponds to this initial acceleration is missing. By taking it into account, one then obtains a non-zero term ${W}_{\mathit{ext}}$ .

In the general case, the energy balance can be correct only if the analysis starts from a balanced state. In the absence of an initial state, this condition is implicitly fulfilled. Particular attention must therefore be paid to the various possibilities of introducing a non-virgin initial state.

#### 1.4.1 Case of the restarts from a state computed beforehand

In this part, one is interested in restarts from a state computed beforehand, namely the use in ETAT_INIT of the keywords:

- EVOL_NOLI for STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE,
- RESULTAT for DYNA_VIBRA.

In order to compute each of the energy terms of equation ( ), one must therefore have available at the restart step:

- the nodal vectors of displacement, velocity and accelerations,
- the mass and damping matrices,
- the internal forces resulting from the stress state,
- the force vectors ${\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{ext}}$ , ${\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{amor}}$ and ${F}_{\mathit{liai}}$ .

The nodal vectors of displacement, velocity and acceleration, as well as the mass matrix and the internal forces, are always available. The damping matrix is in fact not necessary since one uses equation ( ) to evaluate the energy dissipated by damping, where this matrix is evaluated at the time instant ${t}_{1}$ . Only the last three force vectors require particular attention.

The vectors ${\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{amor}}$ and ${\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{liai}}$ must have been archived because it is impossible to recreate them. Thus, when one activates the computation of the energy balance, one automatically activates the archiving of two additional nodal fields named FORC_AMOR and FORC_LIAI. These fields are intended to be re-read in the event of a restart in order to initialise correctly the computation of the energy balance.

Finally, concerning the vector ${\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{ext}}$ of the external forces, for STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE, it is recomputed at the restart time ${t}_{0}$ in accordance with the equilibrium equation ( ) of the system:

$$
{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{ext}}\left({t}_{0}\right)=\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{U}}\left({t}_{0}\right)+\mathrm{C}\left({t}_{1}\right)\dot{\mathrm{U}}\left({t}_{0}\right)+\mathrm{R}\left(\mathrm{U}\left({t}_{0}\right),\dot{\mathrm{U}}\left({t}_{0}\right)\right)
$$

In the case of an analysis with DYNA_VIBRA on a physical basis, one saves directly the external force FORC_EXTE.

#### 1.4.2 Case of an analysis with an initial state given explicitly

The user may be led to supply a non-virgin initial state in the form of a combination of the following fields:

- a displacement field (keyword DEPL)
- a velocity field (keyword VITE)
- an acceleration field (keyword ACCE)
- a stress field (keyword SIGM)
- a field of internal variables (keyword VARI)

The datum of the displacement, velocity and acceleration fields is automatically handled as in the case of an initial state coming from a previous analysis, by means of equation ( ). Note that the time schemes of the dynamics operators DYNA_NON_LINE and DYNA_VIBRA are led to compute an initial acceleration if the user does not supply one. This case is handled in the same manner. By way of example, in the case of the mass–spring system subjected to an initial acceleration, we build the following initial vector ${F}_{\mathit{ext}}$ :

$$
{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathit{ext}}({t}_{0})=m\ddot{u}({t}_{0})
$$

The datum of a stress field or of internal variables is also handled automatically, since these quantities (grandeurs) come into play in the computation of the term $\mathrm{R}(\mathrm{U}({t}_{0}),\dot{\mathrm{U}}({t}_{0}))$ of equation ( ).

#### 1.4.3 Initial energy state

It is important to note that the energy balance is supplied in incremental form. Thus, it is possible to compute only a variation of the various energy terms between two analysis time instants. It is moreover this information which is useful in order to know the various energy transfers. When the initial state of an analysis is supplied from a result (see  1.4.1 ), the initial energy state is recovered. On the other hand, when the initial state is not given by a “result” data structure, one can neither recover nor compute the initial energy state. Consequently, the various terms of the energy balance are initialised to 0.

## 2 Help with use and applications

### 2.1 Advice

In what follows, we indicate a few good practices facilitating the obtaining of a satisfactory energy balance.

- *Use a suitable formalism*

The set of the analyses presented falls within a Lagrangian configuration. Indeed, one brings in a mean-force term over the time step, obtained by summing a force written at the previous step with a force written at the current step. This does not pose a problem for the operator DYNA_VIBRA. But this may generate an incorrect energy balance for STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE, when the formalism used, defined under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT, is:

- PETIT_REAC
- SIMO_MIEHE

In these two cases, it is advised to take small time steps in order to limit the discrepancy between the analysis configurations from one time step to the next.

- *Start from a balanced state*

A first precaution is to start from a balanced initial state. Equilibrium is ensured by computing the external force ${F}_{\mathit{ext}}$ which balances the initial state (see §  1.4.1 ). In the majority of cases, this corresponds to reality. It is nevertheless strongly advised to start from an already balanced state when this is possible.

- *Use suitable convergence criteria*

Another important point to mention is the influence of the convergence criterion on the quality of the energy balance. The convergence of the analysis is ensured to within a tolerance, through the value of RESI_GLOB_RELA or RESI_GLOB_MAXI. When this criterion is too loose, the numerical dissipation may become non-negligible. In case of doubt, it is advised to re-run the analysis with a tighter criterion, in order to check that the energy dissipated by the integration decreases.

- Prefer the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA to the command AFFE_CHAR_CINE

The command AFFE_CHAR_CINE is particular. It consists, during the solution, in removing the degrees of freedom where a displacement is imposed. This functionality is of interest, since it makes it possible to reduce the size of the system to be solved. Nevertheless, it may pose a problem in the evaluation of the energy balance under certain conditions, admittedly rather rare. An example is given in part  2.1.2.1 .

### 2.2 A few applications

In this part, we choose a few test cases in order to decipher their energy balance. The analyses are carried out with version 11.1.12 of *Code_Aster*.

#### 2.2.1 Test case ssnp155a

This test models a stamping of a sheet by a punch in a die (figure ). The structure is modelled in plane strains. One takes the symmetry into account in order to represent only one half. The punch and the die are modelled with boundary elements, on which one imposes a displacement by means of the command AFFE_CHAR_CINE. One then obtains at the end of the analysis an energy balance which is manifestly incorrect, since ${W}_{\mathit{ext}}$ is zero and since ${W}_{\mathit{sch}}$ ought to be very small compared with the other energy terms (table ).

The explanation is the following: the fact of using the command AFFE_CHAR_CINE removes from the solution the set of the degrees of freedom of the punch. In order to compute the contribution to the external work of the displacements imposed via the command AFFE_CHAR_CINE, one builds the vector of the displacements restricted to the nodes on which a displacement is imposed, and one uses equation ( ) with as force the vector of the internal forces. In this test case, the external work is solely that due to the imposed displacement of the punch. But as it is modelled only with boundary elements, which have no stiffness, their contribution to the vector of the internal forces is zero. One is therefore unable to recover the force which corresponds to the imposed displacement, and one obtains a zero external work.

In order to obtain a correct balance, it is sufficient to use the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA to impose the displacement. With the latter, in order to guarantee the satisfaction of the imposed-displacement conditions, Lagrange multipliers are built corresponding in fact to the force to be taken into account in the computation of the external work. Another solution would have been to mesh the punch in order to be able to compute the internal forces there. In both cases, one then arrives at the energy balance indicated in the table below:
