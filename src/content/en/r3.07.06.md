# r3.07.06 Treatment of the offset for the plate elements DKT, DST, DKQ, dsq and q4g

**Summary:**

The plate elements [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) are intended for the analyses of thin three-dimensional structures. The middle surface of these structures does not always coincide with the layout plane or the plane of the mesh. One therefore introduces the notion of offset of the middle surface with respect to the layout plane. It can be used for elements with account taken of transverse shear, or without this hypothesis.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

With the aim of being able to analyse the behaviour of slender structures of plate type, or of curved surfaces approximated by facets, whose middle surface is offset with respect to the plane of application of the forces, one introduces the notion of offset of the middle surface with respect to the meshing surface. The displacement fields varying linearly through the thickness of the plate have as their origin the meshing surface, that is to say that, at the level of the meshing surface, only the translational degrees of freedom are necessary for the description of the displacement.

The introduction of the kinematics into the expression of the strain work makes it possible to obtain the membrane, bending and transverse-shear stiffnesses of the offset element from those of the equivalent non-offset element and from the offset distance. The whole of the analyses (apart from the specific post-processing) is therefore carried out in a layout frame attached to the plane of the mesh. By default the results are therefore obtained in the frame of the mesh. For certain post-processings, it is possible to have these results automatically in other frames insofar as the user indicates the position of the post-processing plane with respect to the plane of the mesh.

The offset distance between the plane of the mesh and the middle surface of the plate is given in AFFE_CARA_ELEM at the same level as the thickness. A positive offset $d$ means that the middle surface of the plate is in reality at a distance $dn$ from the meshed plate element, the direction $n$ being given by the normal to the element (see [§4.1] of the Reference documentation [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) of the plate elements for the construction of this normal).

The notation adopted is that of the note [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) on the plate elements DKT, DST, DKQ, DSQ and Q4G.

## 2 Formulation

### 2.1 Geometry

For the offset plate elements, the reference surface is given by the layout plane or the plane of the mesh (the plane $xy$ for example). The middle surface of the element is positioned with respect to this reference surface. The thickness $h(x,y)$ must be small compared with the other dimensions (extents, radii of curvature) of the structure to be modelled. The figure [Figure 2.1-a] below illustrates the point. Concerning the value of the offset $d$ , and because of the linearisation conditions of the bending adopted in the theory, one will take $d$ such that an element of thickness $d+h$ remains within the plate theory.

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.06/100002000000080000000763054B07CA1ECBBFFC.png)

**Figure 2.1-a**

One attaches to the layout plane (the plane of the mesh) a local orthonormal frame $\mathrm{0xyz}$ associated with the plane of the mesh, different from the global frame $\mathit{OXYZ}$ . The position of the points of the plate is given by the Cartesian coordinates $(x,y)$ in the layout plane (plane of the mesh) and the elevation $z$ with respect to this plane.

### 2.2 Kinematics

The straight sections, which are the sections perpendicular to the middle surface of the plate, remain straight. The material points situated on a normal to the undeformed middle surface remain on a straight line in the deformed configuration. It follows from this approach that the displacement fields vary linearly through the thickness of the plate. If one denotes by $u,v,w$ the displacements of a point of the layout plane $q(x,y,z)$ along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ , the Hencky–Mindlin kinematics gives us:

$(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}(x,y,z)\\ {u}_{y}(x,y,z)\\ {u}_{z}(x,y,z)\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}u(x,y)\\ v(x,y)\\ w(x,y)\end{array})+z(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{y}(x,y)\\ -{\theta}_{x}(x,y)\\ 0\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}u(x,y)\\ v(x,y)\\ w(x,y)\end{array})+z(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}(x,y)\\ {\beta}_{y}(x,y)\\ 0\end{array})$

| where: | $u,v,w$ are the displacements of the layout plane; |
| --- | --- |
|  | ${\theta}_{x}$ and ${\theta}_{y}$ are respectively the rotations of this plane with respect to the axis $x$ and the axis $y$ respectively. |

One prefers to introduce the two rotations ${\beta}_{x}(x,y)={\theta}_{y}(x,y),{\beta}_{y}(x,y)=-{\theta}_{x}(x,y)$ . The three-dimensional strains at every point, with the kinematics introduced previously, are thus given by:

$\begin{array}{}{\epsilon}_{xx}={e}_{xx}+{\mathrm{z\kappa }}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}={e}_{yy}+{\mathrm{z\kappa }}_{yy}\\ {\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{xy}={\gamma}_{xy}=2{e}_{xy}+{\mathrm{2z\kappa }}_{xy}\\ {\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{xz}={\gamma}_{x}\\ {\mathrm{2\epsilon }}_{yz}={\gamma}_{y}\end{array}$

| where: | ${e}_{xx}$ , ${e}_{yy}$ and ${e}_{xy}$ are the membrane strains of the middle surface; ${\gamma}_{x}$ and ${\gamma}_{y}$ the strains associated with the transverse shears; ${\kappa}_{xx}$ , ${\kappa}_{yy}$ , ${\kappa}_{xy}$ the bending strains of the middle surface, which are written: |
| --- | --- |

$\begin{array}{}{e}_{xx}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\\ {e}_{yy}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\\ 2{e}_{xy}=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial u}{\partial y}\\ {\kappa}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial x}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial y}\\ {\mathrm{2\kappa }}_{xy}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {\beta}_{y}}{\partial x}\\ {\gamma}_{x}={\beta}_{x}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}={\beta}_{y}+\frac{\partial w}{\partial y}\end{array}$

**Note:**

- *In the plate theories, the introduction of* ${\beta}_{x}$ *and* ${\beta}_{y}$ *makes it possible to symmetrise the formulations of the strains and the equilibrium equations [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03). In the shell theories, one rather uses* ${\theta}_{x}$ *and* ${\theta}_{y}$ *and the associated couples* ${M}_{x}$ *and* ${M}_{y}$ *with respect to* $x$ *and* $y$*,*
- *the degrees of freedom which have been chosen are the displacements and rotations of the layout plane and not those of the middle surface. Indeed if one envisages the superposition of several offset plates in order to produce a sandwich material, there can correspond to the nodes of the mesh only a single displacement field and not the various displacement fields of the layers composing the material.*

### 2.3 Constitutive law

The behaviour of the plates is a 3D behaviour in “plane stress”. **The transverse stress** ${\sigma}_{zz}$ **is taken as zero**  because it is negligible compared with the other components of the stress tensor (plane-stress hypothesis). The most general constitutive law is then written as follows:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\\ {\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array})=C(\epsilon ,\alpha )(\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ {\gamma}_{xy}\\ {\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array})=\text{Ce}+\mathrm{zC\kappa }+\mathrm{C\gamma }$ with $e=\left(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ 2{e}_{xy}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right),\text{}\kappa =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{et}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\gamma =\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\\ {\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array}\right)$

where: $C(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ is the local tangent stiffness matrix in plane stress;

$\alpha$  represents the set of the internal variables when the behaviour is non-linear.

For constitutive behaviours (for example multilayers) for which the distortions are coupled to the membrane and bending strains, $C(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ is put in the form:

$C=(\begin{array}{cc}H& {H}_{c\gamma }\\ {H}_{{c\gamma }_{}}^{T}& {H}_{\gamma}\end{array})$

| where: | $H(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ is a symmetric $3\times 3$ matrix; ${H}_{\gamma}(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ a symmetric $2\times 2$ matrix; ${H}_{c\gamma }(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ a $3\times 2$ coupling matrix between the membrane or bending effects and the transverse shear. |
| --- | --- |

In the case where it is uncoupled, one has ${H}_{c\gamma }(\varepsilon ,\alpha )=0$ . The determination of ${H}_{\gamma}(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ within the framework of Reissner's theory ([§2.2.3.2] of [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03)) is given in the annex. One shows that it is equivalent to that of the non-offset plates.

## 3 Principle of virtual work

### 3.1 Strain work

The general expression of the 3D strain work for the plate element offset by the distance $d$ with respect to the reference plane is:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}\text{}\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}({\epsilon}_{xx}{\sigma}_{xx}+{\epsilon}_{yy}{\sigma}_{yy}+{\gamma}_{xy}{\sigma}_{xy}+{\gamma}_{x}{\sigma}_{xz}+{\gamma}_{y}{\sigma}_{yz})\text{dV}$

where $S$ is the middle surface, $\mathit{dV}=\mathit{dxdydz}$ and where the position through the thickness of the plate varies between $d‑h/2$ and $d+h/2$ .

#### 3.1.1 Expression of the stress resultants

Adopting the kinematics of [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03), one identifies the work of the internal forces:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}({e}_{xx}{N}_{xx}+{e}_{yy}{N}_{yy}+2{e}_{xy}{N}_{xy}+{\kappa}_{xx}{M}_{xx}+{\kappa}_{yy}{M}_{yy}+2{\kappa}_{xy}{M}_{xy}+{\gamma}_{x}{T}_{x}+{\gamma}_{y}{T}_{y})\text{dS}$

where:

$N=\left(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {N}_{xy}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}\right)\text{dz}$

$M=\left(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {M}_{xy}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}\right)z\text{dz}$

$T=\left(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array}\right)\text{dz}$

| where: | ${N}_{xx}$ , ${N}_{yy}$ , ${N}_{xy}$ are the membrane stress resultants (in $N/m$ ); ${M}_{xx}$ , ${M}_{yy}$ , ${M}_{xy}$ are the bending stress resultants or moments with respect to the layout plane (in $N$ ); ${T}_{x}$ , ${T}_{y}$ are the shear stress resultants or shear forces (in $N/m$ ). |
| --- | --- |

#### 3.1.2 Relation between stress resultants and generalised strains

The expression of the strain work is also written:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}[\text{εC}(\epsilon ,\alpha )\epsilon ]\text{dV}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}[\text{eCe}+z\text{eC}\kappa +\text{eC}\gamma +\mathit{z\kappa }\text{Ce}+{z}^{2}\mathit{\kappa C\kappa }+\mathit{z\kappa C\gamma }+\mathit{\gamma C}(e+\mathit{z\kappa }+\gamma )]\text{dSdz}$

where: $C(\varepsilon ,\alpha )$ is the local tangent stiffness matrix (symmetric matrix).

This is also written:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}[\text{eCe}+(\zeta +d)\text{eC}\kappa +\text{eC}\gamma +\kappa (\zeta +d)\text{Ce}+(\zeta +d{)}^{2}\mathit{\kappa C\kappa }+\kappa (\zeta +d)\mathit{C\gamma }+\mathit{\gamma C}(e+(\zeta +d)\kappa +\gamma )]\text{dSd}\zeta$

Using the expression obtained for Wdef in the previous paragraph, one finds the following relation between the stress resultants and the generalised strains:

$\begin{array}{c}N={H}_{m}e+({H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m})\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma \\ M=({H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m})e+({H}_{f}+2{\mathit{dH}}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{H}_{m})\kappa +({H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{m\gamma }})\gamma \\ T={H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+({H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{T}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T})\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}$

with:

${H}_{m}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\mathit{Hd\zeta }\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{H}_{\text{mf}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\mathit{H\zeta d\zeta }\text{}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{}{H}_{f}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\mathit{H\zeta }}^{2}\mathit{d\zeta }$

$\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{H}_{\text{ct}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{H}_{\gamma}\mathit{d\zeta }\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{}{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{H}_{\mathit{c\gamma }}\mathit{d\zeta }\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{}{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{H}_{\mathit{c\gamma }}\mathit{\zeta d\zeta }\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}$

and:

$e=\left(\begin{array}{c}{e}_{xx}\\ {e}_{yy}\\ 2{e}_{xy}\end{array}\right),\kappa =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\end{array}\right),\gamma =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{x}\\ {\gamma}_{y}\end{array}\right)$

The matrices ${H}_{m}$ , ${H}_{f}$ and ${H}_{\mathit{ct}}$ are the stiffness matrices in membrane, bending and transverse shear, respectively, for the non-offset plate element. The matrix ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}$ is a stiffness matrix of coupling between the membrane and the bending for the non-offset plate element. It is zero if the plate element is symmetric with respect to its middle surface. The matrix ${H}_{m}\gamma$ is a stiffness matrix of coupling between the membrane and the transverse distortion. The matrix ${H}_{f}\gamma$ is a stiffness matrix of coupling between the bending and the transverse distortion. These matrices are zero for a zero offset, except in the case of multilayers where they remain non-zero.

For a homogeneous isotropic elastic behaviour, these matrices have the expression:

${H}_{m}=\frac{\text{Eh}}{1-{v}^{2}}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& v& 0\\ v& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-v}{2}\end{array}\right),\text{}{H}_{f}=\frac{{\text{Eh}}^{3}}{12(1-{v}^{2})}\left(\begin{array}{ccc}1& v& 0\\ v& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-v}{2}\end{array}\right),\text{}{H}_{\text{ct}}=\frac{\text{kEh}}{2(1+v)}\left(\begin{array}{cc}1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right)$

and ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}={H}_{m}\gamma ={H}_{f}\gamma =0$ because there is a material symmetry with respect to the plane $\zeta =0$ .

For the determination of the shear coefficient $k$ one refers to [§2.2.3] of [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03).

The system of relations between the stress resultants and the generalised strains can also be written:

$\begin{array}{c}N={H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma \\ \begin{array}{c}M={H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}e+{H}_{f}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}\gamma \\ T\\ T={H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{}+{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}\hfill \end{array}$

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}={H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m}\\ {H}_{f}^{'}={H}_{f}+2{\mathit{dH}}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{H}_{m}\end{array}$

${H}_{\mathit{fg}}^{'}={H}_{\mathit{fg}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{mg}}$

Thus, in the case of a plate possessing material symmetry with respect to the plane $\zeta =0$ , one has ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}=0$ but ${H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}={\mathrm{dH}}_{m}$ . The offset of the plate entails a coupling between the membrane and bending terms.

**Notes:**

*The relations linking* ${H}_{m}$ *,* ${H}_{f}$ *,* ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}$ *to* $H$ *and* ${H}_{\mathit{ct}}$ *to* ${H}_{\gamma}$ *are valid whatever the tangent elastic constitutive law, with anelastic strains (thermoelasticity, plasticity,...).*

For a plate constituted of $N$ orthotropic layers in elasticity, the matrices ${H}_{m}$ , ${H}_{f}$ , ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}$ and ${H}_{\mathit{ct}}$ are written:

${\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{mi}},\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}({\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}i}+{\eta}_{i}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{mi}}),$ ${\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}({\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{fi}}+2{\eta}_{i}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}i}+{\eta}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{mi}}),\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}i}$

where: ${\eta}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}\left({z}_{i+1}+{z}_{i}\right)$

${H}_{\mathit{mi}}$ , ${H}_{\mathit{fi}}$ , ${H}_{\mathit{mfi}}$ , ${H}_{\gamma i}$ represent the membrane, bending, membrane–bending coupling and transverse-shear matrices for the layer $i$ . One notes the analogy between these expressions and the form established above:

$\begin{array}{c}{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}={H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m}\\ {H}_{f}^{'}={H}_{f}+2{\mathit{dH}}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{H}_{m}\end{array}$

One then deduces that the offset for such a plate is obtained by substituting ${\eta}_{i}+d$ for ${\eta}_{i}$ .

#### 3.1.3 Elastic internal energy of the plate

In view of the previous notes, the elastic internal energy of the plate is expressed more usually, for this kind of geometry, in the following way:

${\Phi}_{int}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}[e({H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma )+\kappa ({H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}e+{H}_{f}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}\gamma )+\gamma ({H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'T}\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma )]\text{dS}$ .

#### 3.1.4 Note

One may choose to express the bending stress resultants or moments with respect to the middle surface of the element and no longer with respect to the reference plane. In this case one obtains:

$N=\left(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {N}_{xy}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}\right)\text{dz}$ , ${M}^{'}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx}^{'}\\ {M}_{yy}^{'}\\ {M}_{xy}^{'}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}\right)(z-d)\text{dz}$ , $T=\left(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array}\right)=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array}\right)\text{dz}$

and the expression of the work of the internal forces becomes:

$\begin{array}{c}{W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{S}{\int}\left({e}_{xx}{N}_{xx}+{e}_{yy}{N}_{yy}+2{e}_{xy}{N}_{xy}+{\kappa}_{xx}({M'}_{xx}+{\text{dN}}_{xx})+{\kappa}_{yy}({M}_{yy}^{'}+{\text{dN}}_{yy})\right)\mathit{ds}+\\ \underset{S}{\int}\left(2{\kappa}_{xy}({M}_{xy}^{'}+{\text{dN}}_{xy})+{\gamma}_{x}{T}_{x}+{\gamma}_{y}{T}_{y}\right)\text{dS}\end{array}$

One then deduces, using the 3D expression of the strain work, that:

$\begin{array}{c}N={H}_{m}e+({H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m})\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma \\ {M}^{'}+\mathit{dN}=({H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m})e+({H}_{f}+2{\mathit{dH}}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{H}_{m})\kappa +({H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{m\gamma }})\gamma \\ T={H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+({H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{T}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T})\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}$

That is, again:

$\begin{array}{c}N={H}_{m}e+({H}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{dH}}_{m})\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma \\ {M}^{'}={H}_{\text{mf}}e+({H}_{f}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\text{mf}})\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}\gamma \\ T={H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+({H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{T}+{\mathit{dH}}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T})\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}$ .

The expression of the internal energy of the plate remains of course unchanged as for itself. In the case of elasticity, it is always written:

${\Phi}_{int}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{S}{\int}[e({H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma )+\kappa ({H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}e+{H}_{f}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}\gamma )+\gamma ({H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+{H'}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{T}\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma )]\text{dS}$

The question of the choice of the plane which it is of interest to use for the expression of the moments may vary from one situation to another.

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.06/100002000000061500000800E33AC5D5A6457F8C.png)

In the case of the figure on the right, the approach developed above is preferable because the expression of the loadings is defined with respect to the middle surface of each plate. In the case of the figure on the left, if one wishes to replace the multilayer shell by two offset shells, the reference axis is the middle surface of the multilayer shell. One therefore has an interest in defining everything with respect to the layout plane. It is this approach which is retained in the code. All the applied loadings are considered as being defined by default in the layout frame or the plane of the mesh. If ever certain loadings are defined with respect to other planes (middle surface, upper or lower surface) it is for the user to make the appropriate changes of frame, by hand or by means of the command file by specifying the plane of application of the forces when that is possible (see [§5]), in order to reduce to a loading defined in the plane of the mesh.

### 3.2 Work of the external forces and couples

The work of the forces and couples acting on the plate is expressed in the following manner:

${W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{S}{\int}\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{v}.U\text{dV}+\underset{S}{\int}{F}_{s}.U\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{c}.U\text{dz}\text{ds}$

| where: | ${F}_{v}$ **,** ${F}_{s}$ **,** ${F}_{c}$ are the body, surface and contour forces acting on the plate, respectively. $C$ is the part of the contour of the plate on which the contour forces ${F}_{c}$ are applied. |
| --- | --- |

With the kinematics of [§2.2], one thus determines:

$\begin{array}{c}{W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}u+{f}_{y}v+{f}_{z}w+{c}_{x}{\theta}_{x}+{c}_{y}{\theta}_{y})\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}({\phi }_{x}u+{\phi }_{y}v+{\phi }_{z}w+{\chi }_{x}{\theta}_{x}+{\chi }_{y}{\theta}_{y})\text{ds}\\ =\underset{S}{\int}({f}_{x}u+{f}_{y}v+{f}_{z}w+{c}_{y}{\beta}_{x}-{c}_{x}{\beta}_{y})\text{dS}+\underset{C}{\int}({\phi }_{x}u+{\phi }_{y}v+{\phi }_{z}w+{\chi }_{y}{\beta}_{x}-{\chi }_{x}{\beta}_{y})\text{ds}\end{array}$

where there are present on the plate:

- ${f}_{x},{f}_{y},{f}_{z}$  the surface forces acting along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ ;
- ${f}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{v}.{e}_{i}\text{dz}+{F}_{s}.{e}_{i}$ where ${e}_{x}$ and ${e}_{y}$ are the basis vectors of the tangent plane and ${e}_{z}$ their normal vector;
- ${c}_{x},{c}_{y}$  : surface couples acting about the axes $x$ and $y$ ;
- ${c}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}[(z+d){e}_{z}\mathrm{\wedge }{F}_{v}].{e}_{i}\text{dz}+[(d\pm \frac{h}{2}){e}_{z}\mathrm{\wedge }{F}_{s}].{e}_{i}$ where ${e}_{x}$ , ${e}_{y}$ , ${e}_{z}$ are the basis vectors defined previously.

and where there are present on the contour of the plate:

- ${\phi }_{x},{\phi }_{y},{\phi }_{z}$  the line forces acting along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ ;
- ${\phi }_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{c}.{e}_{i}\text{dz}$ where ${e}_{x}$ , ${e}_{y}$ , ${e}_{z}$ are the basis vectors defined previously;
- ${\chi }_{x},{\chi }_{y}$  the line couples about the axes $x$ and $y$ ;
- ${\chi }_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}[(z+d){e}_{z}\mathrm{\wedge }{F}_{c}].{e}_{i}\text{dz}$ where ${e}_{x}$ , ${e}_{y}$ , ${e}_{z}$ are the basis vectors defined previously.

**Note:**

*The moments with respect to* $z$ *are zero. The forces and the couples are expressed in the frame of the mesh. All the analyses are done by default in the layout frame. If forces or couples are expressed in another frame (that of the middle surface of the plate for example) the user will have to make the conversions by hand if he uses the default options or else specify the plane of application of the forces (see the paragraph [§ 5 *]).*

## 4 Numerical discretisation of the variational formulation arising from the principle of virtual work

### 4.1 Introduction

The variational formulation for the internal energy allows us to write:

${\mathit{\delta W}}_{int}=\underset{S}{\int}[\mathit{\delta e}({H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}\gamma )+\text{δκ}({H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}e+{H}_{f}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}\gamma )+\text{δγ}({H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}e+{H'}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{T}\kappa +{H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma )]\text{dS}$

with:

$e=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{,x}\\ {v}_{,y}\\ {u}_{,y}+{v}_{,x}\end{array}),\text{}\kappa =(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x,x}\\ {\beta}_{y,y}\\ {\beta}_{x,y}+{\beta}_{y,x}\end{array}),\text{}\gamma =(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,x}+{\beta}_{x}\\ {w}_{,y}+{\beta}_{y}\end{array})$

The five degrees of freedom are the displacements in the plane of the mesh $u$ and $v$ , out of plane $w$ and the two rotations ${\beta}_{x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}$ .

The elements DKT and DST are triangular isoparametric elements. The elements DKQ, DSQ and Q4gamma are quadrilateral isoparametric elements. They are represented below:

One defines the reduced frame of the element as the frame $(\xi \eta )$ of the reference element. The local frame of the element, in the layout plane $(x,y)$ is defined by the user, by the keyword ANGLE_REP. The direction $\mathit{X1}$ of this local frame is the projection of a reference direction $\underline{d}$ onto the plane of the element. This reference direction $\underline{d}$ is chosen by the user, who defines it by two nautical angles in the global frame. The normal $N$ to the plane of the element ($12\mathrm{\wedge }13$  for a triangle numbered $123$ and $12\mathrm{\wedge }14$ for a quadrangle numbered $1234$ ) fixes the second direction. The vector product of the two vectors defined previously $\mathit{Y1}=N\mathrm{\wedge }\mathit{X1}$ makes it possible to define the local triad in which the generalised forces representing the state of stress will be expressed. The user must take care that the chosen reference axis does not end up parallel to the normal of certain plate elements. By default, the reference direction $\underline{d}$ is the axis $X$ of the global frame of definition of the mesh.

**Note:**

*For the plate elements* *QUAD4* , the use of a non-coplanar element may lead to irregularities ([bib1]). In this case, the user is warned.

### 4.2 Discretisation of the displacement field

The Jacobian matrix $J(\xi ,\eta )$ is:

$J=(\begin{array}{cc}{x}_{,\xi }& {y}_{,\xi }\\ {x}_{,\eta }& {y}_{,\eta }\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\xi }{x}_{i}& \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\xi }{y}_{i}\\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\eta }{x}_{i}& \sum_{i=1}^{N}{N}_{i,\eta }{y}_{i}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11}& {J}_{12}\\ {J}_{21}& {J}_{22}\end{array})$

In addition:

$(\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\end{array})=j(\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial}{\partial \xi }\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial \eta }\end{array})\text{avec}j=(\begin{array}{cc}{j}_{11}& {j}_{12}\\ {j}_{21}& {j}_{22}\end{array})={J}^{-1}=\frac{1}{J}(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{22}& -{J}_{12}\\ -{J}_{21}& {J}_{11}\end{array})\text{où}J=\detJ={J}_{11}{J}_{22}{\text{-J}}_{12}{J}_{21}$

The displacement field is discretised by:

$(\begin{array}{c}u\\ v\end{array})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )(\begin{array}{c}{u}^{k}\\ {v}^{k}\end{array})$

$(\begin{array}{c}w\\ {\beta}_{x}\\ {\beta}_{y}\end{array})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}(x,h)(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array})+[\sum_{k=N+1}^{\mathrm{2N}}(\begin{array}{c}0\\ {P}_{\text{xk}}(x,h)\\ {P}_{\text{yk}}(x,h)\end{array}){\alpha}_{k}]$

In this last expression, the term in square brackets is present for the elements of type DKT, DST, DKQ or DSQ, but not for the elements Q4

### 4.3 Account taken of the transverse distortion

One recalls that the essential difference between the elements DKT, DKQ on the one hand and DST, DSQ, Q4 on the other hand comes from the fact that for the former the transverse distortion is zero, that is again  = 0. The difference between Q4 and the elements DST and DSQ comes from a different choice of interpolation for the representation of the transverse shear. The introduction of the offset leads to a particular treatment of the transverse shear.

One replaces in the expression of the internal energy established in [§4.1] $\gamma$ by $\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}$ where the $\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}$ are substitute strains satisfying $\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}=\gamma$ in a weak manner (integral over the sides of the element), and such that:

$\begin{array}{}N={H}_{m}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}\\ \begin{array}{}M={H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}e+{H}_{f}^{'}\kappa +{H}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{'}\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}\\ T\\ T={H}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}^{T}e+{H}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{}+{H}_{\text{ct}}\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}\end{array}\end{array}$

One thus verifies that on the sides ij of the element, one has: $\underset{i}{\overset{j}{\int}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}}_{s}-{\gamma}_{s})\text{ds}=0$ with ${\gamma}_{s}={w}_{,s}+{\beta}_{s}$ .

#### 4.3.1 For the elements Q4

One discretises linearly the field $\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}$ constant per side in such a way that:

${\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}}^{\xi}=(\begin{array}{c}{\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}}_{\xi}\\ {\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}}_{\eta}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}\frac{1-\eta }{2}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{12}+\frac{1+\eta }{2}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{34}\\ \frac{1-\xi }{2}{\gamma}_{\eta}^{23}+\frac{1-\xi }{2}{\gamma}_{\eta}^{41}\end{array})$

Using then the relations:

$\begin{array}{}\underset{-1}{\overset{+1}{\int}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{x}-({w}_{,x}+{\beta}_{x}))\mathrm{dx}=0;\\ \underset{-1}{\overset{+1}{\int}}({\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{h}-({w}_{,h}+{\beta}_{h}))\mathrm{dh}=0\end{array}$

one establishes that:

$\begin{array}{}{\gamma}_{\xi}^{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({w}_{j}-{w}_{i}+{\beta}_{\mathrm{\xi i}}+{\beta}_{\mathrm{\xi j}})\\ {\gamma}_{\eta}^{\text{kp}}=\frac{1}{2}({w}_{p}-{w}_{k}+{\beta}_{\mathrm{\eta p}}+{\beta}_{\mathrm{\eta k}})\end{array}$ for $(ij)=(12,34)$ and $(\mathit{kp})=(23,41)$ .

Carrying the two results above back into the expression of ${\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}}^{\xi}$ , one establishes that:

${\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}^{x}=(\begin{array}{c}{\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{x}\\ {\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{h}\end{array})={B}_{x}^{'}{u}_{x}$

where: ${u}_{x}=(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{h1}}\\ M\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{xN}}\end{array})$ and ${B}_{x}^{'}=({B}_{\mathrm{x1}}^{'},L,{B}_{\mathrm{xN}}^{'})$ with ${B}_{\mathrm{xk}}^{'}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k,x}& {x}_{k}{N}_{k,x}& 0\\ {N}_{k,h}& 0& {h}_{k}{N}_{k,h}\end{array})$

Moreover, as:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{xi}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{hi}}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11}& {J}_{12}\\ {J}_{21}& {J}_{22}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{xi}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yi}}\end{array})$

one deduces from this that

${\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}^{x}={B}_{x}{u}_{f}$

where: ${u}_{f}=(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{1}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{x1}}\\ {\beta}_{\mathrm{y1}}\\ M\\ {w}_{N}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xN}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yN}}\end{array})$ and ${B}_{x}=({B}_{\mathrm{x1}},L,{B}_{\mathrm{xN}})$

with: ${B}_{\mathrm{xk}}=(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k,x}& {x}_{k}{N}_{k,x}{J}_{11}& {x}_{k}{N}_{k,x}{J}_{12}\\ {N}_{k,h}& {h}_{k}{N}_{k,h}{J}_{21}& {h}_{k}{N}_{k,h}{J}_{22}\end{array})$

Finally:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{g}=(\begin{array}{c}{\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{x}\\ {\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{j}_{11}& {j}_{12}\\ {j}_{21}& {j}_{22}\end{array}){\stackrel{ˉ}{g}}^{x}={B}_{c}{u}_{f}$ with ${B}_{c[2´\mathrm{3N}]}={\text{jB}}_{x}$

**Note:**

*This treatment is equivalent to that of the non-offset elements* *Q4* of [§4.3.2.1] of [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03).

### 4.4 Elementary stiffness matrix

#### 4.4.1 Elementary stiffness matrix for the elements Q4

One takes up again the expressions of the stiffness matrices given in [§4.4.1] of the Reference documentation [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) and one replaces ${H}_{\text{mf}}$ by ${H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}$ , ${H}_{f}$ by ${H}_{f}^{'}$ and ${H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}$ by ${H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}$ . One will note that in [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) the results were presented without a membrane–transverse-shear or bending–transverse-shear coupling term. One adds them here.

#### 4.4.2 Elementary stiffness matrix for the elements DKT, DKQ

One takes up again the expressions of the stiffness matrices given in [§4.4.1] of the Reference documentation [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) and one replaces ${H}_{\text{mf}}$ by ${H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}$ , ${H}_{f}$ by ${H}_{f}^{'}$ . Since the relation $\stackrel{ˉ}{\gamma}=0$ is satisfied, the membrane–transverse-shear or bending–transverse-shear couplings do not exist.

**Note:**

*The offset introducing a membrane–bending coupling, an incompatibility between the approximation spaces may appear. This is due to the fact that the “pure membrane contributions” are constant per element and they arise from the linear approximation of the* *translations whereas the “membrane contributions linked to the offset” are linear per element and they arise from the approximation of the rotations.*

*In order to avoid this incompatibility, one uses a mixed-approximation method to compute the coupling term. To this end one makes a distinction between the coupling term contributing to the membrane* ${\kappa}^{\mathit{tran}}$ *and the coupling term contributing to the bending* ${\kappa}^{\mathit{rot}}$ *. In the simplest case of a homogeneous offset plate, one has:*

$\begin{array}{c}N={H}_{m}e+{\mathit{dH}}_{m}{\kappa}^{\mathit{tran}}\\ M={\mathit{dH}}_{m}e+({H}_{f}+{d}^{2}{H}_{m}){\kappa}^{\mathit{rot}}\\ T={H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma \end{array}$

*In order to avoid the incompatibility, one discretises* ${\kappa}^{\mathit{tran}}$ *in the same approximation space as* $e$ *whereas* ${\kappa}^{\mathit{rot}}$ *is discretised in the classical manner.*

#### 4.4.3 Elementary stiffness matrix for the elements DST, DSQ

One has:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{\delta W}}_{int}^{e}=\underset{e}{\int}\mathrm{\delta e}({\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m}}\mathrm{e}+{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\kappa +{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}\gamma -{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\mathrm{T})+\text{δκ}({\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\mathrm{e}+{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f}}^{'}\kappa +{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{'}\gamma -{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{'}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\mathrm{T})+\delta {\text{TH}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}\mathrm{T}\text{dS}=\\ \underset{e}{\int}\mathrm{\delta e}([{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m}}-{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}^{\mathrm{T}}]\mathrm{e}+[{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{mf}}^{'}-{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{\mathrm{H}'}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{\mathrm{T}}]\kappa )+\text{δκ}([{\mathrm{H}'}_{\text{mf}}-{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{'}{\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{m\gamma }}^{\mathrm{T}}]\mathrm{e}\text{dS}\\ +\underset{e}{\int}[{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f}}^{'}-{\mathrm{H}}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{'}{\mathrm{H}}_{{\text{ct}}^{-1}}{\mathrm{H}'}_{\mathrm{f\gamma }}^{\mathrm{T}}]\kappa )+\delta {\text{TH}}_{{\text{ct}}^{-1}}\mathrm{T}\text{dS}\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{c}T\\ {\mathit{\delta W}}_{int}^{e}=\underset{e}{\int}\mathit{\delta e}({H}_{m}^{''}e+{H}_{\text{mf}}^{''}\kappa )+\text{δκ}({H}_{\text{mf}}^{}+{H}_{f}^{''}\kappa )+\delta {\text{TH}}_{{\text{ct}}^{-1}}T\text{dS}\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{c}{H}_{m}^{''}={H}_{m}-{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}{H}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}^{T}\\ \begin{array}{c}T\\ {H}_{\text{mf}}^{''}={H}_{\text{mf}}^{'}-{H}_{\mathit{m\gamma }}{H}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{}\end{array}\\ T\\ {H}_{f}^{''}={H}_{f}^{'}-{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{'}{H}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{H}_{\mathit{f\gamma }}^{}\end{array}$

with:

${K}_{m}=\underset{s}{\int}[{B}_{m}^{T}{H}_{m}^{''}{B}_{m}+{B}_{\text{cm}}^{T}{H}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}{B}_{\text{cm}}]\text{dS}$

One also knows that ${U}_{f}=({U}_{\mathit{f\beta }},\alpha )$ whence it results that:

${K}_{f}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{f11}& {K}_{f12}\\ {K}_{f12}^{T}& {K}_{22}\end{array}\right)$ with: $\begin{array}{c}{K}_{f11}=\underset{s}{\int}{B}_{\mathit{f\beta }}^{T}{H}_{f}^{''}{B}_{\mathit{f\beta }}\text{dS}\\ {K}_{f12}=\underset{s}{\int}{B}_{\mathit{f\beta }}^{T}{H}_{f}^{''}{B}_{\mathit{f\alpha }}\text{dS}\\ {K}_{f22}=\underset{s}{\int}{B}_{\mathit{f\alpha }}^{T}{H}_{f}^{''}{B}_{\mathit{f\alpha }}\text{dS}\end{array}$

### 4.5 Elementary mass matrix

The terms of the mass matrix are obtained after discretisation of the following variational formulation:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{\delta W}}_{\text{mass}}^{\text{ac}}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}=\underset{d-h/2}{\overset{d+h/2}{\int}}\underset{S}{\int}\rho \ddot{u}\mathit{\delta u}\text{dzdS}\\ \phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}=\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{m}(\ddot{u}\mathit{\delta u}+\ddot{v}\mathit{\delta v}+\ddot{w}\mathit{\delta w})+({\rho}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathit{d\rho }}_{m})(\ddot{u}{\text{δβ}}_{x}+\ddot{v}{\text{δβ}}_{y}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{x}\mathit{\delta u}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{y}\mathit{\delta v})\text{dS}+\\ \phantom{\rule{4em}{0ex}}\underset{S}{\int}({\rho}_{f}+2{\mathit{d\rho }}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{\rho}_{m})({\ddot{\beta}}_{x}{\text{δβ}}_{x}+{\ddot{\beta}}_{y}{\text{δβ}}_{y})\text{dS}\end{array}\hfill \\ \end{array}$

with    ${\rho}_{m}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \text{dz},{\rho}_{\text{mf}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \text{zdz},$ and ${\rho}_{f}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{\mathit{\rho z}}^{2}\text{dz}$ .

**Note:**

*If the plate is homogeneous or symmetric with respect to its middle surface then* ${\rho}_{\mathit{mf}}=0$ *.*

#### 4.5.1 Classical elementary mass matrix

##### 4.5.1.1 Element Q4 gamma

The discretisation of the displacement for this isoparametric element is:

$u=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{k}\\ {v}_{k}\\ {w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array})\rbrace k=1,...,N$

The mass matrix, in the basis where the degrees of freedom are grouped according to the directions of translation and of rotation, then has the expression:

$M=(\begin{array}{ccccc}{M}_{m}& 0& 0& {M}_{\text{mf}}& 0\\ 0& {M}_{m}& 0& 0& {M}_{\text{mf}}\\ 0& 0& {M}_{m}& 0& 0\\ {M}_{\text{mf}}^{T}& 0& 0& {M}_{f}& 0\\ 0& {M}_{\text{mf}}^{T}& 0& 0& {M}_{f}\end{array})$

with: ${M}_{m}=\underset{S}{\int}{\rho}_{m}{N}^{T}N\text{dS}$

${M}_{\text{mf}}=\underset{S}{\int}({\rho}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathrm{d\rho }}_{m}){N}^{T}N\text{dS}$

${M}_{f}=\underset{S}{\int}({\rho}_{f}+{\mathrm{2d\rho }}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{\rho}_{m}){N}^{T}N\text{dS}$

where: $N=({N}_{1}{\ln}_{k})$ .

For what follows, one sets ${\rho}_{\text{mf}}^{'}={\rho}_{\text{mf}}+{\mathrm{d\rho }}_{m}$ and ${\rho}_{f}^{'}={\rho}_{f}+{\mathrm{2d\rho }}_{\text{mf}}+{d}^{2}{\rho}_{m}$ .

##### 4.5.1.2 Elements of type DKT, DST

As:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}w\\ {\beta}_{x}\\ {\beta}_{y}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )\left(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array}\right)+\sum_{k=N+1}^{2N}\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ {P}_{\text{xk}}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {P}_{\text{yk}}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}\right){\alpha}_{k}$

where: $\alpha ={P}_{m}{U}_{m}+{P}_{\beta}{U}_{f\beta }$

one deduces from this that:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}w\\ {\beta}_{x}\\ {\beta}_{y}\end{array}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left(\begin{array}{ccccc}0& 0& {N}_{k}(\xi ,\eta )& 0& 0\\ {N}_{\text{kxu}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kxv}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kxw}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kxx}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kxy}}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{\text{kyu}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kyv}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kyw}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kyx}}(\xi ,\eta )& {N}_{\text{kyy}}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{k}\\ {v}_{k}\\ {w}_{k}\\ {\beta}_{\text{xk}}\\ {\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array}\right)$ .

The mass matrix then has the expression:

$M=(\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{m}^{'}& {M}_{\text{mf}}^{'}\\ {M}_{\text{fm}}^{'}& {M}_{f}^{'}\end{array})$

The membrane part ${M}_{m}^{'}$ of the elementary mass matrix is composed of the blocks $\mathit{kp}$ ( $k$ th row and $p$ th column ) following:

$\begin{array}{c}{\rho}_{m}(\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{k}{N}_{p}& 0\\ 0& {N}_{k}{N}_{p}\end{array})+{\rho}_{\text{mf}}^{'}(\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pxu}}+{N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{p}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{p}\\ {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pyu}}+{N}_{\text{kxv}}{N}_{p}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pyv}}+{N}_{\text{kyv}}{N}_{p}\end{array})\\ +{\rho}_{f}^{'}(\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{\text{pxu}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{\text{pyu}}& {N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{\text{pyv}}\\ {N}_{\text{pxu}}{N}_{\text{kxv}}+{N}_{\text{pyu}}{N}_{\text{kyv}}& {N}_{\text{kxv}}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyv}}{N}_{\text{pyv}}\end{array})\end{array}$

The bending part ${M}_{f}^{'}$ is composed of the blocks $\mathit{kp}$ ( $k$ th row and $p$ th column ) following:

${\rho}_{m}(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k}{N}_{p}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})+{\rho}_{f}^{'}(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{\text{pxw}}+{N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{\text{pxx}}+{N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{\text{pxy}}+{N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\\ {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{\text{pxw}}+{N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{\text{pxx}}+{N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{\text{pxy}}+{N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\\ {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{\text{pxw}}+{N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{\text{pxx}}+{N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{\text{pxy}}+{N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\end{array})$

The coupling part between the membrane and the bending ${M}_{\text{mf}}^{'}$ is composed of the blocks $\mathit{kp}$ ($k$ th row and $p$ th column) following:

$\begin{array}{c}{\rho}_{\text{mf}}^{'}(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pxw}}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pxx}}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pxy}}\\ {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{k}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\end{array})+\\ {\rho}_{f}^{'}(\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{\text{pxw}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{\text{pxx}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{\text{kxu}}{N}_{\text{pxy}}+{N}_{\text{kyu}}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\\ {N}_{\text{kxv}}{N}_{\text{pxw}}+{N}_{\text{kyv}}{N}_{\text{pyw}}& {N}_{\text{kxv}}{N}_{\text{pxx}}+{N}_{\text{kyv}}{N}_{\text{pyx}}& {N}_{\text{kxv}}{N}_{\text{pxy}}+{N}_{\text{kyv}}{N}_{\text{pyy}}\end{array})\end{array}$

The coupling part between the bending and the membrane ${M}_{\text{fm}}^{'}$ is composed of the blocks $\mathit{kp}$ ($k$ th row and $p$ th column) following:

${\rho}_{\text{mf}}^{'}(\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{p}& {N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{p}\\ {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{p}& {N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{p}\\ {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{p}& {N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{p}\end{array})+{\rho}_{f}^{'}(\begin{array}{cc}{N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{\text{pxu}}+{N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{\text{pyu}}& {N}_{\text{kxw}}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyw}}{N}_{\text{pyv}}\\ {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{\text{pxu}}+{N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{\text{pyu}}& {N}_{\text{kxx}}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyx}}{N}_{\text{pyv}}\\ {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{\text{pxu}}+{N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{\text{pyu}}& {N}_{\text{kxy}}{N}_{\text{pxv}}+{N}_{\text{kyy}}{N}_{\text{pyv}}\end{array})$

#### 4.5.2 Improved elementary mass matrix

As the deflection of a plate in bending only can hardly be represented by a linear approximation, one can enrich the shape functions for the bending terms. This approach is used in *Code_Aster*  for the elements of type DKT, DST and Q4G where the shape functions used in the computation of the bending mass matrix are of order 3. The interpolation for $w$ is thus written:

$w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{3(k-1)+1}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{3(k-1)+2}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{,\mathrm{\xi k}}+{N}_{3(k-1)+3}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{,\mathrm{\eta k}}$

##### 4.5.2.1 Elements of type DKT

One knows that in the Love–Kirchhoff approximation one has ${\beta}_{x}=-{w}_{,x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}=-{w}_{,y}$ at every point of the element.

Because of the discretisation stated above one has:

$\begin{array}{}w=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{3(k-1)+1}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+({J}_{11}{N}_{3(k-1)+2}(\xi ,\eta )+{J}_{21}{N}_{3(k-1)+3}(\xi ,\eta )){w}_{,\text{xk}}+\\ ({J}_{12}{N}_{3(k-1)+2}(\xi ,\eta )+{J}_{22}{N}_{3(k-1)+3}(\xi ,\eta )){w}_{,\text{yk}}\end{array}$

since:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,\mathit{\xi k}}\\ {w}_{,\mathit{\eta k}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{11}& {J}_{12}\\ {J}_{21}& {J}_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{c}{w}_{,\text{xk}}\\ {w}_{,\text{yk}}\end{array}\right)$

This is also written:

$\begin{array}{cc}w& \sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{3(k-1)+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{3(k-1)+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{N}_{3(k-1)+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{yk}}\\ & \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{N}_{\text{kww}}(\xi ,\eta ){w}_{k}+{N}_{\text{kwx}}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{xk}}+{N}_{\text{kwy}}(\xi ,\eta ){\beta}_{\text{yk}}\end{array}$

where:

$\begin{array}{c}{N}_{3(k-1)+1}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )={N}_{3(k-1)+1}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{3(k-1)+2}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )=-{J}_{11}{N}_{3(k-1)+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{21}{N}_{3(k-1)+3}(\xi ,\eta )\\ {N}_{3(k-1)+3}^{'}(\xi ,\eta )=-{J}_{12}{N}_{3(k-1)+2}(\xi ,\eta )-{J}_{22}{N}_{3(k-1)+3}(\xi ,\eta )\end{array}$ .

Thus:

## 5 Implementation and post-processings

The offset is introduced by the optional keyword EXCENTREMENT at the level of AFFE_CARA_ELEM in the same manner as the thickness according to the arrangements defined in the introduction. When this keyword is not present the offset is zero by default.

### 5.1 Application of the forces and couples

All the analyses are done in the layout frame (plane of the mesh). If one defines forces or couples with respect to another frame, the user will have to make, for FORCE_ARETE and FORCE_NODALE, the transformations necessary in order to reduce to the mesh frame. For FORCE_COQUE the user will be able to specify the plane of application of the forces and the conversion towards the computation frame will be automatic.

One thus introduces in AFFE_CHAR_MECA the notion of plane of application of the forces by the keyword PLAN under FORCE_COQUE. This plane of application is different from the reference plane or layout plane on which the mesh rests. For this keyword one will define the following four possibilities of application of the forces:’INF’ ‘MOY’ ‘SUP’ ‘MAIL’ . ‘INF’‘MOY’ and ‘SUP’ mean that one applies the forces on the lower, mean and upper skin of the plate respectively. ‘MAIL’ means that one applies the forces at the level of the reference plane or the plane of the mesh. By default the forces will be applied on the plane of the mesh of the plate. Concerned are the forces of type FORCE_COQUE of TE0032.

In the frame local to the element, when the forces and the couples are applied on ‘MOY’ one uses the simple transfer relation:

$\begin{array}{c}{c}_{x}^{'}={c}_{x}-{\text{df}}_{y}\\ {c}_{y}^{'}={c}_{y}+{\text{df}}_{x}\end{array}$

in order to bring the forces and the couples back into the frame of the mesh where one does the analyses.

In the frame local to the element, when the forces and the couples are applied on ‘SUP’ one uses the simple transfer relation:

$\begin{array}{c}{c}_{x}^{'}={c}_{x}-(d+h/2){f}_{y}\\ {c}_{y}^{'}={c}_{y}+(d+h/2){f}_{x}\end{array}$

In the frame local to the element, when the forces and the couples are applied on ‘INF’ one uses the simple transfer relation:

$\begin{array}{c}{c}_{x}^{'}={c}_{x}-(d-h/2){f}_{y}\\ {c}_{y}^{'}={c}_{y}+(d-h/2){f}_{x}\end{array}$

If the forces are given in the global frame of the element , one uses transfer relations of the type: ${c}^{'}=c+(d+\varepsilon h/2)n\mathrm{\wedge }f$ where $c$ is defined with respect to the frame ’INF’ ‘MOY’ ‘SUP’ with $\varepsilon$ equal to -1, 0 and 1, respectively. When there is no offset, the previous formula reduces to ${c}^{'}=c+\varepsilon h/\mathrm{2n}\mathrm{\wedge }f$ .

**Note:**

*For loadings of type* *FORCE_ARETE or* *FORCE_NODALE* *the forces and couples can be expressed only with respect to the frame of the mesh. If the user knows them* *only with respect to the middle surface of the plate, he will have to carry out the change of frame himself in order to have the expression of the forces and of the couples with respect to the meshing surface. The relation to be used is* ${c}^{'}=c+\mathit{dn}\mathrm{\wedge }f$ *where* $d$ *is the distance between the computation plane and the loading plane, oriented by the normal to the shell. It is obvious that the user has an interest in the loading plane being the plane of the mesh, but it is not always possible to make these two planes coincide, as one can see on the left-hand part of Figure 2.1-a.*

### 5.2 Application of the displacement boundary conditions

For boundary conditions of displacement type the user will have to take care of the fact that they can be applied only on the mesh frame. The transfer relations with respect to conditions given on the middle surface are the following:

$\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{\text{ref}}={\theta}_{\text{moy}}\\ {u}_{\text{ref}}={u}_{\text{moy}}-{\theta}_{\text{moy}}\mathrm{\wedge }\mathit{dn}\end{array}$

### 5.3 Post-processings

For the post-processings, the default results of generalised-force type are given in the frame corresponding to the layout plane. In order to have them in the other frames, the user will have to indicate the post-processing plane and the changes of frame will be automatic.

For the post-processing of the generalised forces, one will be able to compute them in the middle surface of the shell via the command POST_CHAMP / COQUE_EXCENT.

## 7 Conclusion

The plate finite elements which we describe here are used in the analyses of slender thin structures whose ratio of thickness to characteristic length is lower than $1/10$ . The middle surface of these structures does not coincide with the plane of the mesh (layout plane). The offset therefore corresponds to the distance of the middle surface with respect to the layout surface. A positive offset $d$ means that the middle surface of the plate is at a distance $dn$ from the meshed plate element, the direction $n$ being given by the normal to the element.

The values of displacements and of generalised forces obtained are given by default in the frame of the mesh. For the generalised forces, one can however define a post-processing frame — a frame associated with the middle surface — different from the layout frame. In the same manner, the applied forces are considered as being given by default in the layout frame. In the case of FORCE_COQUE, one can however specify a frame of application of the forces and couples — a frame associated with the middle surface — different from the layout frame.

Equivalent elements are not available in thermal analysis; the thermo-mechanical chainings are therefore not available for the offset plate elements.

**Transverse-shear correction factors for offset orthotropic or laminated plates**

The matrix ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ is defined so that the surface density of transverse-shear energy obtained in the case of the three-dimensional distribution of the stresses arising from the solution of the equilibrium is equal to that of the plate model based on the Reissner hypotheses, for a simple bending behaviour. One must thus find ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ such that:

$\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau {H}_{g}^{-1}\tau =\frac{1}{2}{\text{TH}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}T=\frac{1}{2}\gamma {H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma$ with $\tau =(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array})$ and $T=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\tau \text{dz}={H}_{\text{ct}}\gamma$ .

In order to obtain ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ one uses the distribution of $\tau$ along $z$ obtained from the solution of the 3D equilibrium equations without external couples:

${\sigma}_{xz}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}({\sigma}_{xx,x}+{\sigma}_{xy,y})d\zeta ;{s}_{yz}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}({\sigma}_{xy,x}+{\sigma}_{yy,y})d\zeta$ with ${\sigma}_{xz}={\sigma}_{yz}=0$ for $z=\pm h/2$ .

In the case where there is no membrane–bending coupling (symmetry with respect to $z=0$ ), the stresses in the plane of the element ${\sigma}_{xx},{\sigma}_{yy},{\sigma}_{xy}$ have the expression, in the case of a pure bending behaviour:

$\sigma =\mathit{zA}(z)M$ with $A(z)=H(z){H}_{f}^{-1}$ .

If $H(z)$ and ${H}_{f}$ do not depend on $x$ and $y$ one can determine ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ . Indeed:

$\tau (z)={D}_{1}(z)T+{D}_{2}(z)\lambda$ where $T=(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx,x}+{M}_{xy,y}\\ {M}_{xy,x}+{M}_{yy,y}\end{array})$ and $\lambda =(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx,x}-{M}_{xy,y}\\ {M}_{xy,x}-{M}_{yy,y}\\ {M}_{yy,x}\\ {M}_{xx,y}\end{array})$

as well as:

${\mathrm{D}}_{1}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}\frac{z}{2}(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}+{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}+{A}_{32}\\ {A}_{31}+{A}_{23}& {A}_{22}+{A}_{33}\end{array})\mathit{dz}$ ,

${\mathrm{D}}_{2}=-\underset{-h/2}{\overset{z}{\int}}\frac{z}{2}(\begin{array}{cccc}{A}_{11}-{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}-{A}_{32}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{12}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{31}\\ {A}_{31}-{A}_{23}& {A}_{33}-{A}_{22}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{32}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{21}\end{array})\mathit{dz}$ .

It results from this that $\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}t{\mathrm{H}}_{g}^{-1}t=\frac{1}{2}(\begin{array}{c}\mathrm{T}\\ \lambda \end{array})(\begin{array}{cc}{\mathrm{C}}_{11}& {\mathrm{C}}_{12}\\ {\mathrm{C}}_{12}^{T}& {\mathrm{C}}_{22}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}\mathrm{T}\\ \lambda \end{array})$ with: $\begin{array}{}{C}_{11}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{D}_{1}^{T}{H}_{g}^{-1}{D}_{1}\text{dz};\\ {C}_{12}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{D}_{1}^{T}{H}_{g}^{-1}{D}_{2}\text{dz};\\ {C}_{22}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{D}_{2}^{T}{H}_{g}^{-1}{D}_{2}\text{dz}\end{array}$

As moreover $\frac{1}{2}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}t{H}_{g}^{-1}t=\frac{1}{2}{\text{TH}}_{\text{ct}}^{-1}T$ one proposes to take ${H}_{\text{ct}}={C}_{11}^{-1}$ in order to satisfy as well as possible the two equations whatever $T$ and $\lambda$ .

By comparing ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ thus computed with ${\stackrel{ˉ}{H}}_{\text{ct}}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{H}_{g}\text{dz}$ one brings out the following transverse-shear correction coefficients: ${k}_{1}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{11}/{\stackrel{ˉ}{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{11};{k}_{12}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{12}/{\stackrel{ˉ}{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{12};{k}_{2}={H}_{\text{ct}}^{22}/{\stackrel{ˉ}{H}}_{\text{ct}}^{22}$ .

For a homogeneous plate, isotropic or anisotropic, one thus finds: ${\mathrm{H}}_{\text{ct}}=\mathit{kh}{\mathrm{H}}_{g}$ with $k=5/6$ .

**Notes:**

*This method is valid only when the composite plate is symmetric with respect to z=0.*

- For a multilayer material, one establishes that:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{C}}_{11}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{{h}_{i}}{4}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{h}_{p}{\mathrm{A}}_{p}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{A}}_{i}^{T}){\mathrm{H}}_{g}^{-1}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{h}_{p}{\mathrm{A}}_{p}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{A}}_{i})+\\ \frac{1}{24}({z}_{i+1}^{3}-{z}_{i}^{3})[{\mathrm{A}}_{i}^{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{g}^{-1}(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{h}_{p}{\mathrm{A}}_{p}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{A}}_{i})+(\sum_{p=1}^{i-1}{h}_{p}{h}_{p}{\mathrm{A}}_{p}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}{z}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{A}}_{i}^{T}){\mathrm{H}}_{g}^{-1}{\mathrm{A}}_{i}]\\ +\frac{1}{80}({z}_{i+1}^{5}-{z}_{i}^{5}){\mathrm{A}}_{i}^{T}{\mathrm{H}}_{g}^{-1}{\mathrm{A}}_{i}\end{array}$

where: ${h}_{i}={z}_{i+1}-{z}_{i},{h}_{i}=\frac{1}{2}({z}_{i+1}+{z}_{i})$ and ${A}_{i}$ represents the matrix $(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}+{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}+{A}_{32}\\ {A}_{31}+{A}_{23}& {A}_{22}+{A}_{33}\end{array})$ for layer i.

- The validity of the choice ${H}_{\text{ct}}={C}_{11}^{-1}$ can be examined a posteriori when one has an estimate of the solution (displacement fields and plane stresses, in particular). One can then estimate the difference between the two estimates of the energy. A two-step computation procedure for multilayer plates and shells (with ${H}_{\text{ct}}$ diagonal and two coefficients ${k}_{1}$ and ${k}_{2}$ ) has moreover been developed by Noor and Burton [bib10] [bib11].
- In the case of a homogeneous isotropic or anisotropic plate the equality between the two energies is satisfied in the strict sense since ${D}_{2}=0$ . The choice made above is then valid and no a posteriori examination is necessary.

**Computation of the shear stress**

One makes explicit here the term $\mathit{d1iel}(z)$ which makes it possible to compute the shear stress and the shear strain in the presence of an offset.

Indeed, according to the documentation R3.07.03, the shear-stress – shear-force relations are:

${\sigma}_{xz}={T}_{x}\ast \mathit{d1iel}(z)$ ${\sigma}_{yz}={T}_{y}\ast \mathit{d1iel}(z)$

In the classical cases without offset, one has: $\mathit{d1iel}(z)=3/\mathrm{2h}\ast (1-4{z}^{2}/{h}^{2})$ .

In more general cases (in the presence of the offset for example) $\mathit{d1iel}(z)$ must be modified. In order to approximate the shear stress correctly, one chooses to postulate a general quadratic form for $\mathit{d1iel}(z)=a\ast {z}^{2}+b\ast z+c$ such that the following conditions are respected:

- $\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\mathit{d1iel}(z+d)\mathit{dz}=1$ shear-force–stress relation
- $\mathit{d1iel}(z+d=-h/2)=0;\mathit{d1iel}(z+d=h/2)=0$ free-edge condition

Thus with an offset of $d$ , the coefficients of $\mathit{d1iel}(z)$ are:

$a=-6/\mathit{quotient}$ , $b=(6\ast (\mathit{zmin}+\mathit{zmax}))/\mathit{quotient}$ , $b=(6\ast \mathit{zmin}\ast \mathit{zmax})/\mathit{quotient}$

$\mathit{quotient}=({\mathit{zmax}}^{3}-3\ast {\mathit{zmax}}^{2}\ast \mathit{zmin}+3\ast \mathit{zmax}\ast {\mathit{zmin}}^{2}-{\mathit{zmin}}^{3}),\mathit{zmin}=-h/2+d,\mathit{zmax}=h/2+d$
