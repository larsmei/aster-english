# v7.32.118 WTNP118 - Gravial rebalancing of a column saturation

**Summary:**

The test presented here allows to check the correct account of gravity for modelling unsaturated flows. This test represents a column initially uniformly desaturated. There is no load: only gravity is motor of evolution.

This test will be the subject of an test case Alliances.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The field studied is a bar of $3m$ .

### 1.2 Material properties

Here we take data that bring back to a quasi-unitary problem, and then the units are no longer physically meaningful.

The gravity is taken in the direction of the positive $x$ (which corresponds to the vertical axis).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and initials

We are in zero flux everywhere. Initially the medium is desaturated with a saturation of $S=0,5$ on the whole domain which corresponds to a capillary pressure:

> $\mathrm{Pc}=3,6\mathrm{Pa}$ .

We take an initial gas pressure of $1\mathrm{Pa}$.

### 1.4 time step

$1s$ is modelled as follows:

- de $0$ à $0,1s$ : 5 time step
- de $0,1$ à $1s$ : 9 time step

### 1.5 reference solution

In the stationary state hydrostatic equilibrium shall be achieved.

So ${\mathrm{\Delta P}}_{\text{lq}}=\rho .g.\mathrm{\Delta x}$ has to be

With the data we have, that gives us ${\mathrm{\Delta P}}_{\text{lq}}=9,81\ast 3=29,43\mathrm{Pa}$

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics A

The Modelling is HHD in flat deformations. The mesh is composed of 80 elements Q8.

### 2.2 Tested Features

This Modelling allows in particular to verify the use of the PESA_MULT time function introduced into the THM_DIFFU card. This function is added as a factor of the terms PESA_X, PESA_Y and PESA_Z, and makes gravity time dependent.

### 2.3 Results

The following figures show the profiles of capillary pressures, gas pressures and saturation along the bar for different moments. Rebalancing is observed to achieve a stationary state (no more moves after $0,5s$).

In this stationary state and in $\mathrm{Pa}$:

$\mathrm{Pgz}(x=0)=0,991\mathrm{Pa}$ et $\mathrm{Pgz}(x=3)=1,014\mathrm{Pa}$

$\mathrm{Pc}(x=0)=22,8\mathrm{Pa}$ et $\mathrm{Pc}(x=3)=-6,62\mathrm{Pa}$

This gives ${\mathrm{\Delta P}}_{\text{lq}}=29,44\mathrm{Pa}$ which corresponds well to hydrostatic equilibrium.
