# u3.11.01 Modellings POU_D_T, POU_D_E, BARRE

**Summary:**

The four modellings POU_D_T, POU_D_E and BARRE correspond to the classical formulations of beam and bar elements, inspired by Strength of Materials.

They can be used for three-dimensional problems in linear or non-linear isotropic mechanical analysis.

## 1 Discretisation

### 1.1 Degrees of freedom

For the three beam modellings in three dimensions, the discretisation degrees of freedom are, at each node of the support mesh cell, the six displacement components (three translations and three rotations). These nodes are assumed to describe a segment of the mean fibre of the beam.

For the bar modelling in three dimensions, the discretisation degrees of freedom are, at each node of the support mesh cell, the three translational displacement components.

| **Finite element** | **Degrees of freedom (at each vertex node)** |  |  |  |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POU_D_T | DX | DY | DZ | DRX | DRY | DRZ |
| POU_D_E | DX | DY | DZ | DRX | DRY | DRZ |
| BARRE | DX | DY | DZ |  |  |  |

### 1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness matrices

The support mesh cells of the finite elements, in a displacement formulation, are two-node segments SEG2:

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Finite element** | **Notes** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POU_D_T | SEG2 | MECA_POU_D_T |  |
| POU_D_E | SEG2 | MECA_POU_D_E |  |
| BARRE | SEG2 | MECA_BARRE |  |

### 1.3 Support mesh cell of the loadings

All the loadings applicable to the beam and bar elements are treated by direct discretisation on the support mesh cell of the element in a displacement formulation.

*No loading support mesh cell is therefore necessary for the edge of the beam or bar elements.*

### 1.4 Main characteristics of the modellings

The POU_D_E modelling (straight Euler beam) corresponds to the Euler–Bernoulli assumption, that is to say that the sections remain straight and perpendicular to the mean fibre (high-slenderness assumption).

The POU_D_T modelling (straight Timoshenko beam) takes into account the effects of transverse shear.

The BARRE modelling treats only the axial forces and strains.

The beam with warping is dealt with in [U3.11.04](doc:u3.11.04).

## 2 Assignment of the characteristics

For these 1D structural elements, it is necessary to assign geometric characteristics which are complementary to the mesh data. The definition of these data is carried out with the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM associated with the following factor keywords:

- POUTRE

Makes it possible to define and to assign the characteristics of the cross-section and the orientation of the principal axes of inertia about the neutral fibre.

Supported modellings: POUT_D_T, POU_D_E

- BARRE

Makes it possible to define and to assign the characteristics of the cross-section.

Supported modelling: BARRE

- ORIENTATION

Makes it possible to define and to assign the principal axes of the cross-sections of beam-type elements.

Supported modellings: POUT_D_T, POU_D_E

**Note on the discretisation:**

*As regards the meshing of beams into mesh cells* *SEG2* , it is unnecessary to refine these elements excessively, since their integrated formulation makes it possible to obtain exact solutions at the nodes in linear statics [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). In modal analysis and in dynamics, care will be taken to mesh sufficiently to represent the expected modes, but without excess: the elements must remain of a sufficient length, depending on the dimensions of the section, *so that the beam assumption is valid.*

*For example, for a beam of length 1, and a circular section of external radius 0.05 and of thickness 0.01, 10 elements are sufficient to capture correctly the first 10 modes. But if one refines enormously, for example with 1000 elements, then each beam element is very short: length 0.001 for an external radius of 0.05. The element matrices are very ill-conditioned, in particular for the element* *POU_D_E (for* *POU_D_T the* *transverse shear terms improve the conditioning somewhat). On solution, one then loses 8 decimal places for the* *POU_D_E.*

## 3 Supported loadings

The loadings available are the following:

- 'CONTACT'

Makes it possible to define the zones subjected to contact conditions.

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E

- 'EPSI_INIT'

Makes it possible to apply an initial-strain loading.

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E

- 'FORCE_ELEC'

Makes it possible to apply the LAPLACE force acting on a principal conductor, due to the presence of a straight secondary conductor.

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E

- 'FORCE_POUTRE'

Makes it possible to apply line forces

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E,BARRE

- 'INTE_ELEC'

Makes it possible to apply the LAPLACE force acting on a principal conductor, due to the presence of a secondary conductor which is not necessarily straight with respect to this principal conductor.

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E

- 'PESANTEUR'

Makes it possible to apply a gravity-type loading.

Supported modellings: POU_D_T, POU_D_E, BARRE

Note:

Contact possible between a beam and a surface [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50).
