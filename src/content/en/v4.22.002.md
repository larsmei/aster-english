# v4.22.002 TTNL02 - Thermal transition with phase change

**Summary:**

This elementary test allows to treat a unidirectional problem in nonlinear transient thermal analysis and to verify the consideration of a liquid/solid phase change by *Code_Aster* by introducing via the volume enthalpy the latent fusion heat. The solution is analytical and involves the error functions $\mathrm{erf}$ and $\mathrm{erfc}$. The problem is treated in both plane and volume cases.

For the modellings presented here, the differences of the results obtained by *Code_Aster* are between 1 and 4% of the analytically calculated reference.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

A semi-analytic solution involving error functions is available:

$\mathit{erf}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}{\int}_{0}^{x}{e}^{-{t}^{2}}\mathit{dt}$ et $\mathrm{erfc}(x)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}{\int}_{x}^{+\infty }{e}^{-{t}^{2}}\mathrm{dt}$

This solution is valid for a semi-infinite medium, so it can only be used in a limited range of time variable variation.

Either

![Figure](/figures/v4.22.002/Object_5.svg)

the position of the solid/liquid interface. Soient ${s}_{t}=\frac{L}{\sqrt{{t}_{\mathrm{total}}}}$ and $\lambda =\frac{{s}_{t}}{2\sqrt{{d}_{s}}}$ where ${d}_{s}$ and ${d}_{l}$ denote the diffuseness of solid and liquid media $({d}_{s}=\frac{{k}_{s}}{{c}_{s}},{d}_{l}=\frac{{k}_{l}}{{c}_{l}})$ . The solution of the heat equation is of the form:

${T}_{s}(x,t)={T}_{0}+\frac{{T}_{m}-{T}_{0}}{\mathrm{erf}(\lambda )}\mathrm{erf}(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{{d}_{s}t}})$ si $x\le {x}_{t}$

${T}_{l}(x,t)={T}_{i}+\frac{{T}_{m}-{T}_{i}}{\mathrm{erfc}(\lambda \sqrt{\frac{{d}_{s}}{{d}_{l}}})}\mathrm{erfc}(\frac{x}{2\sqrt{{d}_{l}t}})$ si $x\ge {x}_{t}$

${t}_{\mathrm{total}}$ data is enough to set the solution, so you fix ${t}_{\mathrm{total}}=420.$

## 10 Summary of results

The error obtained with the analytical solution remains reasonable for the observation points listed in the tables. However, the thermal shock imposed at the beginning of the transient causes oscillations (when the temperature change in a point over time is observed) that are rapidly amortized and have disappeared at the time $t=0.5s$.
