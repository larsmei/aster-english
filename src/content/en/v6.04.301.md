# v6.04.301 SSNV301 - Rotating cylindrical ring subjected to thermal shock and internal pressure – Von Mises (isotropic scrubbing)

**Summary:**

This $\mathrm{3D}$ quasi-static nonlinear mechanical test concerns the modelling of a cylindrical ring piece and checks for the resumption of a deformation field after a transient linear thermal calculation (thermal shock: application of a thermal flux inside the cylindrical ring). Then, the calculation is repeated and the transfer of the deformation field of thermal origin is checked and the use of a follower load (centrifugal force) is used. Then, the calculation is repeated by applying an internal pressure (change of the boundary conditions). The Von Mises criterion with isotropic hardening defined by a function that depends on temperature.

Comparison with numerical results of Modelling with Samcef V7.0 (module THERNL for the calculation of the transient temperature field and recapture with the MECANL module for the geometrical and material nonlinear mechanical calculation).

Modelling with elements $\mathrm{3D}$ HEXA20 and elements axisymmetric of the meaxtr6 type. A fine mesh is used to check the robustness and performance of *Code_Aster.*

In both modellings A and B, the applied pressure is not monitored. The results obtained by SAMCEF are provided with and without following pressure.

In addition, Modelling A is used to validate the use of the 'ERME_ELEM' mechanical residual error indicator on 3D modelling in the presence of rotations.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Cylinder with:

| Internal radius | ${R}_{i}=0.04m$ |
| --- | --- |
| Outside radius | ${R}_{e}=0.08m$ |
| Height | $L=0.04m$ |

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.301/100009DC000069D5000047BA1E25246611987200.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is homogeneous isotropic, linear thermoelastic. The mechanical properties of materials depend on temperature. The properties vary linearly between the 2 extreme temperatures: $0°C$ and $100°C$.

Constitutive law with linear isotropic hardening:

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.301/Object_1.svg)

Thermal properties and density:

$\lambda = 72\,\frac{J}{\mathrm{m\,s\,K}}$

$\rho = 7860\,\frac{\mathrm{kg}}{\mathrm{m}^3}$

$C_p = 452\,\frac{J}{\mathrm{kg\,K}}$

$\alpha = 12\cdot 10^{-6}\,\frac{1}{K}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The thermal shock is modelled by a thermal flow of $300000W/{m}^{\mathrm{2s}}$ inside the cylinder. The structure has an initial temperature of $0°C$.

At the same time a centrifugal force is applied: over an interval of $15s$, the rotation speed is increased up to $2400\mathrm{rad}/s$ , then kept constant.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.301/10001068000069BB00003ED7EA8EE8FC95C97DBD.svg)

After $15s$, an internal pressure is applied: the pressure increases linearly to a value of $5.5\mathrm{MPa}$ which is reached at $20s$, then the pressure drops to zero.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.301/Object_3.svg)

The thermal calculation and mechanical calculation are performed with 50 time step identical.

A quarter of the cylinder is modelled by attaching the transverse components of the displacements to the two cross-sections of the cylinder. In addition, the degree of translation according to the length of the cylinder is fixed to the inner radius of the lower side of the cylinder.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Comparison with numerical results of volume modelling with Samcef V7.0 with 50 time step (module THERNL for the calculation of the transient temperature field and recapture with the MECANL module for nonlinear mechanical calculation).

An implicit schema is used for the integration of the heat equation.

The nonlinear mechanical calculation was performed with SAMCEF using axisymmetric Modelling with 5 time step (non-linear geometric Modelling) and volume modelling (one quarter of cylinder) with 5 time step (non-linear geometric Modelling).

## 5 Summary of the results

It has been verified that the same results are obtained with or without re-run, with or without modification of boundary conditions.

Note that the nodal forces that are provided to Aster are divided by $2\pi$ compared to those provided to SAMCEF in axisymmetric.

The 'ERME_ELEM' mechanical residual error indicator works correctly for modellings 3D rotational.
