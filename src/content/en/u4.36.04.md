# u4.36.04 Operator GENE_ACCE_SEISME

## 1 Purpose

This operator makes it possible to generate artificial seismic accelerograms for transient dynamic analyses. It makes it possible to generate two types of signals:

1. Non-stationary seismic signals according to the evolutionary Kanai-Tajimi power spectral density (DSP) model, option DSP;
2. Seismic signals compatible with a target oscillator response spectrum (SRO), option SPEC_MEDIAN, SPEC_MOYENNE, SPEC_UNIQUE or SPEC_FRACTILE.

In both cases, one must associate with them a modulation function which determines the time evolution of the seismic signals, by the factor keyword MODULATION. If one does not wish to apply a time modulation, one must choose a “constant” modulation over the time interval. One should consult the document [R4.05.05](doc:r4.05.05) for a detailed description of the modellings and of the parameters to be supplied.

It produces a concept of type table_fonction.

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Keyword DUREE_PHAS_FORT

> ♦ DUREE_PHAS_FORT    =    TSM                                                [R]

Duration of the strong phase of the signal to be generated ([R4.05.05](doc:r4.05.05)).

The parameters of the modulation functions GAMMA and of Jennings & Housner (JENNINGS_HOUSNER) are identified so that the mean strong phase of the signals corresponds to the one given. In the case of a constant modulation, the total duration of the signal corresponds to the strong-phase duration supplied.

### 3.2 Keyword NB_POIN

> ◊    NB_POIN = nb_poin                                                    [I]

Number of discretisation points of the interspectrum to be used in the generation algorithm. nb_poin must be an even number.

If NB_POIN is supplied, then the duration of the simulation is determined by this value: $T=\mathit{dt}(N-1)$ and the time-discretisation points are: ${t}_{j}=j \mathit{dt},j=0,\mathrm{...},N-1$ .

If the keyword NB_POIN is not supplied, the simulation duration is taken equal to three times the duration of the strong phase plus ${t}_{\mathit{ini}}$ : $T=\mathrm{3 TSM}+{t}_{\mathit{ini}}$ . This makes it possible to simulate the accelerogram over its whole length if the variation of the signal is defined by a Gamma or *Jennings & Housner* modulation function. The number of points NB_POIN is then computed from this value. In the case of a constant modulation (TYPE=CONSTANT), one takes $T=\mathit{TSM}$ and NB_POIN is not requested.

### 3.3 Keyword FREQ_FILTRE

> ◊     FREQ_FILTRE = ff                                             [R]

Optional keyword used to supply a frequency (in $\mathit{Hz}$ ) in order to filter the low frequencies of the seismic signals (accelerograms) in the time domain. It is therefore a high-pass filter. This filter makes it possible to remove a possible drift of the displacement signals obtained by integration of the accelerograms. By default, no time filtering is applied (ff=0.0).

**Note:**

*One must take care not to choose ff too large (ff=0.05Hz constitutes a reasonable reference value). If ff is too large, then one does not only remove the drift but one also removes a large part of the low-frequency content of the signals.*

### 3.4 Keyword FREQ_CORNER

```text
◊     FREQ_CORNER = fc                                             [R]
```

This frequency is known in the seismological community by the term *corner frequency*. One can observe that the frequency content of natural seismic signals tends towards 0 very rapidly from a certain minimum frequency, namely for frequencies lower than the *corner frequency*.

In the case of the generation of signals compatible with an SRO, one can use this command to optimise the adjustment of the low-frequency content of the signals.

In the case of the generation of signals by the Kanai-Tajimi DSP, this filter must be applied in order to obtain a physical model in agreement with the seismological data, in particular the property that the spectral content must tend towards zero beyond the “*corner frequency*” (without a filter, the Kanai-Tajimi DSP is not zero at the origin). By default, one uses a filter with $\mathit{fc}=0.05\times \text{FREQ\_FOND}$ for the Kanai-Tajimi DSP.

### 3.5 Keyword FREQ_FILTRE_ZPA

```text
◊     FREQ_FILTRE_ZPA =fz                                            [R]
```

Optional keyword used to supply a frequency (in $\mathit{Hz}$ ) in order to filter the high frequencies beyond the ZPA frequency. It is therefore a low-pass filter. This filter is applied to the DSP in the frequency domain. This filter makes it possible to remove residual frequency content beyond the ZPA value, which can produce erroneous asymptotic values if one computes the SRO for dampings lower than the value used to identify the DSP (SPEC_MOYENNE, SPEC_UNIQUE, SPEC_MEDIANE). By default, no low-pass filter is applied.

**Note**

*One must take care not to choose fz too small; the cut-off frequency fz of the low-pass Butterworth filter is not the frequency at which filtering begins but the central point of the transition zone.*

### 3.6 Keyword PAS_INST

```text
♦    PAS_INST = dt                                                 [R]
```

Time step of the seismic signals. This value is used to determine the cut-off frequency for the simulations by the formula $F=1/(2\mathit{dt})$ (Shannon). One must ensure that the cut-off frequency is large enough to model the phenomenon well.

### 3.7 Keyword FREQ_PENTE

```text
◊    FREQ_PENTE = fp                                            [R]
```

Slope for the evolution of the central frequency [ $\mathit{Hz}/s$ ]: $f(t)={f}_{0}+{f}_{p}({t}_{0}-t)$ , where ${t}_{0}$ is the reference time instant and ${f}_{0}$ the central frequency at that time instant. In GENE_ACCE_SEISME, the reference time instant is chosen as the time instant in the middle of the strong phase: ${t}_{0}=\mathrm{0.5 TSM}+{t}_{\mathit{ini}}$ . Constant values are taken before and after the strong phase (it is advised to consult R4.05.05 for more details). One observes that, generally, the fundamental frequency decreases with time. In that case one must give a negative slope: ${f}_{p}<0$ . The code stops in error if the function $f(t)$ produces negative central frequencies over the duration of the strong phase. If FREQ_PENTE is not supplied, then a constant fundamental frequency equal to ${f}_{0}$ is taken. This must be supplied for the option DSP; in the case of SPEC_UNIQUE, SPEC_MEDIANE or SPEC_FRACTILE, the central frequency of the process is deduced from the target response spectrum.

**Note**:

*If one does not supply FREQ_PENTE and if one chooses a constant modulation function, one obtains a stationary process. This process corresponds to the classical Kanai-Tajimi DSP (but filtered at low frequencies).*

### 3.8 Keyword NB_TIRAGE

```text
◊    NB_TIRAGE        =    Nbt                                    [I]
```

The number of signals to be simulated. The default value is $1$ .

### 3.9 Keyword INIT_ALEA

```text
◊    INIT_ALEA    =    ni                                        [I]
```

If the keyword INIT_ALEA is supplied, one initialises the seed of the random sequences with this value. Two consecutive analyses with the same initialisation then produce the same seismic signals. If the keyword is not supplied, then the seed used is displayed in the message file.

### 3.10 Keyword COEF_CORR

```text
◊    COEF_CORR = rho                                                    [R]
```

This keyword makes it possible to supply a correlation coefficient for the generation of correlated signals. The correlation coefficient can take positive or negative values: $-1<rho<1$ .

If COEF_CORR is supplied, then one simulates pairs of correlated signals, with correlation coefficient rho. These signals can represent the two components of correlated horizontal seismic signals or else time loadings for multi-support studies.

In the TABLE_FONCTION at the output of the operator, the two components are recorded by 'NOM_PARA', which is equal to 'ACCE1' for the first signal and 'ACCE2' for the second.

### 3.11 Keyword RATIO_HV

```text
◊    RATIO_HV = ratio                                                    [R]
```

If RATIO_HV and COEF_CORR are both supplied, then one generates 3D signals with correlated horizontal components (defined by the correlation coefficient rho, which may be zero) and a vertical component uncorrelated with the other two, but to which the horizontal/vertical ratio supplied by RATIO_HV is applied. This ratio is often taken equal to $\frac{3}{2}$ but any other value greater than zero may be chosen. In the TABLE_FONCTION at the output of the operator, the three components are recorded by 'NOM_PARA', which is equal to 'ACCE1' and 'ACCE2' for the horizontal signals and 'ACCE3' for the vertical component.

**Note**

*RATIO_HV is available only for the option SPEC_FRACTILE and if COEF_CORR is supplied. In the other cases, it is enough to generate vertical signals by an independent call to GENE_ACCE_SEISME and by giving the target spectrum or the DSP parameters for the vertical component.*

### 3.12 Factor keyword MATR_COHE

This factor keyword must be supplied if one wishes to generate spatial seismic fields. This is useful for carrying out a time study with a seismic excitation that varies in space or of multi-support type. The coherence function defines the spatial correlation of the seismic motion. One must then supply the type of coherence function and, where applicable, the associated parameters. More details on this coherence function are found in the documentation of the operator DYNA_ISS_VARI [U4.53.31](doc:u4.53.31).

In the TABLE_FONCTION at the output of the operator, the signals are listed by their node name, for example NOEUD = N1.

#### 3.12.1 Operand MAILLAGE

Mesh of the modelling.

#### 3.12.2 Operand GROUP_NO_INTERF

```text
♦    GROUP_NO_INTERF    =    gni [grno]
```

With this keyword, one defines the node group for which one wishes to generate a space-time seismic field.

#### 3.12.3 Operand TYPE

> ♦    TYPE    =    coherence model [txt]

One can choose between the coherence function of Mita & Luco (MITA_LUCO) or those of Abrahamson (ABRAHAMSON, ABRA_ROCHER, ABRA_SOLMOYEN).

If one chooses MITA_LUCO, then one must in addition supply VITE_ONDE. The parameter PARA_ALPHA is optional; by default one takes alpha = 0.1.

### 3.14 Keyword PESANTEUR

```text
♦    PESANTEUR    =    g                         [R]
```

In general, one takes PESANTEUR=9.81 ($m/{s}^{2}$ ). In this case, one must supply ACCE_MAX (PGA), and ECART_TYPE or, where applicable, the target spectrum (SPEC_OSCI) in $g$ . This normalisation quantity is also used for the computation of the Arias intensity. As an example, giving ACCE_MAX= 0.2 corresponds to a PGA of $\mathrm{0.2g}$ with $g=\mathrm{9.81m}/{s}^{2}$ . The accelerograms generated will then be accelerations in $m/{s}^{2}$ .

### 3.15 Factor keyword DSP

The earthquake is modelled by a non-stationary stochastic process. Evolutionary power spectral densities (DSP) make it possible to take into account a phenomenon that is non-stationary in amplitude and in frequency content, such as the earthquake. The variation of the amplitude is taken into account by a modulation function, whereas the evolution of the frequency content is modelled by the evolutionary Kanai-Tajimi DSP. The Kanai-Tajimi DSP is furthermore filtered in order to remove the very low-frequency content, which can lead to numerical problems (non-zero drifts for the integrated signals).

The parameters linked to the variation of the amplitude are the Arias intensity, the duration of the strong phase and the instant of the start of the strong phase. The parameters linked to the DSP and to the evolution of the frequency content are the damping and the fundamental frequency of the Kanai-Tajimi DSP as well as the slope describing the evolution of the latter in the course of time. These parameters can be determined from a given accelerogram, from an SRO (oscillator response spectrum) or by calling on the data available in the literature. Furthermore, the model being parameterised, it is possible to take into account the variability and the uncertainty of these parameters by means of random draws.

The simulation algorithm and the model are described in more detail in the reference document [R4.06.04](doc:r4.06.04).

#### 3.15.1 Operands AMOR_REDUIT, FREQ_FOND

```text
♦    AMOR_REDUIT = amo    [R]
```

Value of the critical damping ratio of the Kanai-Tajimi DSP.

```text
♦    FREQ_FOND = f0        [R]
```

Central frequency of the Kanai-Tajimi DSP; it is the frequency at which the most energy is concentrated.

### 3.16 Factor keywords SPEC_MEDIANE, SPEC_MOYENNE, SPEC_UNIQUE and SPEC_FRACTILE

This factor keyword makes it possible to supply the data of a target SRO in order to generate seismic signals in agreement with the target SRO. One must then determine a DSP “compatible” with the data of the target SRO. There are several options:

- SPEC_UNIQUE: Generation of a seismic signal whose SRO is very close to the target SRO: one must proceed by iterations in order to adjust the spectral content of the signal as well as possible. If several signals are requested (NB_TIRAGE), then the adjustment is done signal by signal.
- SPEC_MOYENNE, SPEC_MEDIANE: Generation of $\mathrm{Nbt}$ signals whose median/mean spectrum satisfies the target. If the target SRO is a physical spectrum, then the set of signals generated generally has a median SRO close to the target. One can however carry out iterations in order to improve the adjustment of the SRO, either in the median (SPEC_MEDIANE) or in the mean (SPEC_MOYENNE). If one does not carry out iterations (NB_ITER not supplied) in order to improve the adjustment, then the two options produce the same result.
- SPEC_FRACTILE: Generation of $\mathit{Nbt}$ signals whose median spectrum and one-sigma spectrum satisfy the target. This is a method for “*best-estimate*” studies. The signals generated have a variability close to that of the real signals available in the databases, in particular as regards the variability between realisations (*record-to-record variability*). For this, one must supply the median spectrum as well as the one-sigma spectrum. The median spectrum as well as the one-sigma spectrum are generally provided by the attenuation laws.

#### 3.16.1 Operand SPEC_OSCI

> ♦    SPEC_OSCI    =    spec_cible

One supplies here the target SRO in the form of a function with the frequencies on the abscissa and the spectral accelerations on the ordinate (the latter must be normalised according to PESANTEUR). One can use DEFI_FONCTION to build it. If one has chosen the option SPECTRE_FRACTILE, then this spectrum must correspond to the median spectrum (as defined by the attenuation laws).

#### 3.16.2 Operand SPEC_1_SIGMA

> ♦    SPEC_1_SIGMA    =    spec_cible_1s

Mandatory keyword for the option SPEC_FRACTILE. One supplies here the target one-sigma SRO in the form of a function with the frequencies on the abscissa and the spectral accelerations on the ordinate (the latter must be normalised according to PESANTEUR). One can use DEFI_FONCTION to build it. The one-sigma spectrum is, for example, provided by the attenuation laws, which assume a log-normal distribution of the spectral accelerations and therefore of the SRO values. This implies that the one-sigma SRO corresponds to the 84% fractile of the SRO. It is advised to consult the reference document [R4.05.05](doc:r4.05.05) for more details on this modelling.

#### 3.16.3 Operand AMOR_REDUIT

> ♦    AMOR_REDUIT = amo                                        [R]

Value of the reduced damping of the SRO supplied under SPEC_OSCI.

#### 3.16.4 Operand CORR_ZPA

```text
◊    CORR_ZPA         =    /'NON'           [DEFAUT]
                           /'OUI'            [TXM]
```

This keyword is optional and can be used only with the option SPEC_UNIQUE. If CORR_ZPA = 'OUI' then the time signals will be modified by a correction function so that they have the right PGA value, namely the ZPA value which corresponds to the asymptote of the target spectrum for the high frequencies.

#### 3.16.5 Operands ERRE_ZPA, ERRE_RMS and ERRE_MAX

```text
◊    ERRE_MAX         =    (cmax,emax)            [R]

◊    ERRE_ZPA         =    (czpa,ezpa)            [R]

◊    ERRE_RMS         =    (crms,erms)            [R]
```

These keywords are optional; one can supply the weighting coefficients as well as the maximum error desired by the user
for the three adjustment criteria: maximum error over the frequency band, error on the ZPA (zero period acceleration) and
RMS error. It is possible to supply a weighting coefficient (cmax, czpa, crms) and a target maximum error (emax, ezpa, erms).
The three weightings must be supplied together. The error computed is the relative error, namely the difference between the value
of the spectrum obtained and the value of the target spectrum divided by the value of the target spectrum:

$$
\text{erri}_i = \frac{\text{vale}_i - \text{target}_i}{\text{target}_i} , \quad i=1,2,3
$$

where:

- $\mathrm{vale}_i$ is the value of the spectrum obtained for criterion $i$
- $\mathrm{target}_i$ is the value of the target spectrum for criterion $i$
- $i=1$ corresponds to ERRE_MAX, $i=2$ to ERRE_ZPA, and $i=3$ to ERRE_RMS

The weighting coefficients are used to determine a weighted multi-objective error for each iteration:

$$
\text{error} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{3} \left( c_1^2 , \text{err}_1^2 + c_2^2 , \text{err}_2^2 + c_3^2 , \text{err}_3^2 \right)}
$$

where:

- $c_i$ is the weighting coefficient associated with criterion $i$
- $\mathrm{err}_i$ is the relative error computed for criterion $i$ (expressed as a percentage)
- $i=1,2,3$ represent respectively ERRE_MAX, ERRE_ZPA, and ERRE_RMS

This criterion is used to choose, among the results of the iterations, the accelerogram (or the accelerograms) at the output of the analysis
which best satisfy the criterion (it is not always the last iteration). If one of the errors computed is greater than the
value supplied (emax, ezpa, erms), an alarm is emitted.

#### 3.16.6 Operand NB_ITER

> ◊    NB_ITER         =    nbiter                    [I]

One supplies the number of iterations in order to improve the adjustment of the accelerogram to the target spectrum (SRO). This keyword is optional: by default, no iterations are carried out. With the option SPEC_FRACTILE, one cannot iterate.

#### 3.16.7 Operand METHODE

```text
◊    METHODE         =    /'HARMO'            [DEFAUT]
                          /'NIGAM'            [TXM]
```

This optional keyword makes it possible to choose the method for the computation of the response spectra. These are the same methods as those available for the computation of an SRO with CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04). By default, one uses the method HARMO. The response spectrum is then obtained by successive harmonic analyses (for different oscillator eigenfrequencies) by way of an FFT/IFFT of the input signal. The method NIGAM is detailed in the document [R5.05.01](doc:r5.05.01).

#### 3.16.8 Operand FREQ_PAS

> ◊    FREQ_PAS = fpas                             [R]

With this optional keyword, one can choose the discretisation step of the SRO used to determine the compatible DSP. The SRO values supplied via SPEC_OSCI are then interpolated in order to obtain the desired discretisation step fpas. By default, one takes the same frequency step as the one used for the generation of the signals. The latter is deduced from the cut-off frequency and from the number of points NB_POIN.

### 3.17 Factor keyword MODULATION

The parameters of the modulation functions GAMMA and of Jennings & Housner (JENNINGS_HOUSNER) are identified so that the mean strong phase TSM of the signals corresponds to the one given. For a CONSTANT modulation (no modulation), the duration of the simulated signals corresponds to TSM. One uses the definition based on the Arias intensity. One writes $\mathit{TSM}={T}_{2}-{T}_{1}$ where ${T}_{1}$ and ${T}_{2}$ are respectively the time instants at which 5% and 95% of the Arias intensity (the total energy) are reached. The time instant ${T}_{1}$ corresponds to the start of the strong phase ${t}_{\mathit{ini}}$ .

If NB_POIN is given for the Gamma and Jennings & Housner modulations, then the total duration of the signals corresponds to (NB_POIN-1)*PAS_INST.

#### 3.17.1 Operand TYPE

```text
♦    TYPE        =    /    'JENNINGS_HOUSNER'        [TXM]
                      /    'GAMMA'
                      /    'CONSTANT'
```

Definition of the type of modulation (cf. also [R4.05.05](doc:r4.05.05)).

The modulation of type CONSTANT corresponds to a signal without modulation of the amplitude. If one furthermore assumes that the fundamental frequency of the DSP is constant, then one comes back to a stationary process.

#### 3.17.2 Operands INT_ARIAS, ACCE_MAX, ECART_TYPE

If one has chosen the option DSP, then one must supply one of the three parameters Arias intensity, PGA (peak ground acceleration) or standard deviation in order to define the energy contained in the signals. If one has chosen one of the three factor keywords SPEC_UNIQUE, SPEC_MEDIANE or SPEC_FRACTILE, then this information is already contained in the target spectrum.

> ◊    INT_ARIAS    =    arias                                            [R]

Mean Arias intensity: $\mathit{Arias}=E(\frac{\pi}{2g}{\int}_{0}^{\infty}{X}^{2}(t)\mathit{dt})$ with $X$ the process modelling the seismic motion (acce) and $g$ the gravity.

> ◊    ACCE_MAX         =    pga                                    [R]

Peak ground acceleration (PGA). One associates this value with the median maximum of the signals to be generated. The corresponding standard deviation is determined from the peak factor and for the strong phase TSM.

One must supply ACCE_MAX (PGA) in $g$ . The value of $g$ is to be supplied by the keyword PESANTEUR. Thus, ACCE_MAX= 0.2 corresponds to a PGA of $\mathrm{0.2 g}$ with $g=\mathrm{9.81m}/{s}^{2}$ . The accelerograms generated will then be accelerations in $m/{s}^{2}$ .

> ◊    ECART_TYPE         =    ect                                     [R]

Standard deviation of the underlying stationary stochastic process. One then applies the modulation of the amplitude (GAMMA or JENNINGS_HOUSNER).

One must supply ECART_TYPE in $g$ (see also above). The value of $g$ is to be supplied by the keyword PESANTEUR. One must take PESANTEUR=9.81 ($m/{s}^{2}$) in order to obtain accelerations in $m/{s}^{2}$.

#### 3.17.3 Operand INST_INIT

> ♦/◊    INST_INIT    =    t_ini                                                [R]

Instant of the start of the strong phase in the case of the GAMMA modulation function.

The parameters of the GAMMA modulation function are identified (by least squares) so that $\mathit{TSM}$ and ${t}_{\mathit{ini}}$ are satisfied.
