# r5.07.01 Computation of non-linear modes with the operator MODE_NON_LINE

**Summary:**

The operator MODE_NON_LINE makes it possible to compute the non-linear modes of an autonomous conservative linear system endowed with localised impact non-linearities. The numerical methods used for the computation of the non-linear modes are described here.

## 1 Introduction

Non-linear modes are a mathematical tool which makes it possible to interpret non-linear dynamic phenomena. They are an extension of the eigenmodes [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) used to interpret linear dynamic systems.

The operator MODE_NON_LINE makes it possible to compute the non-linear modes of dynamic systems with localised impact non-linearities.

The definition of the non-linear modes will be recalled, then the impact non-linearities supported by the operator (unilateral, bilateral and annular contact) will be described, and finally the algorithm employed will be described. The latter is a combination of the harmonic balance method (EH) and of the asymptotic numerical method (MAN), which is called EHMAN (cf. [Bib1]).

This documentation is not an exhaustive presentation of non-linear modes. It merely recalls the main assumptions under which non-linear modes may be sought using MODE_NON_LINE, together with the principles adopted for the representation of the non-linearities and the basic algorithmic elements. For a more complete presentation, and the details of the implementation, the reader is referred to reference [Bib1].

## 2 Definitions of non-linear modes

The first definition was given by Rosenberg in the 1960s, but it was at the beginning of the 1990s that the theoretically most solid definition of non-linear modes was given, using the theory of dynamical systems. Thus a non-linear mode is defined as “a two-dimensional invariant manifold of the phase space, tangent at a stable equilibrium point of the linearised system” (cf. [Bib2]).

For conservative systems, a non-linear mode may be defined as a “family of periodic solutions”. This definition is much more attractive, because it gives access to powerful numerical tools such as continuation methods. This is why it is this definition that has been chosen here; consequently MODE_NON_LINE allows the computation of a family of periodic solutions.

## 3 Modelling

It is assumed that, in the absence of contact, the structure under study has a linear behaviour and that the equations governing its dynamic equilibrium have been discretised by finite differences or by finite elements. A discrete system of second-order differential equations with $n$ degrees of freedom is obtained.

In general, these equations take the following form:

$\mathrm{M}.\ddot{\mathrm{U}}(t)+\mathrm{K}.\mathrm{U}(t)+{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(\mathrm{U}(t))=0,$

- $\mathrm{M}$ is the mass matrix of the system,
- $K$ is the elastic stiffness matrix of the system,
- ${\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ is the vector of the non-linear impact forces,

One places oneself in the framework of localised non-linearities, that is to say that the forces are applied to a relatively small number of degrees of freedom. Let ${I}_{\mathit{nl}}$ denote the set of the indices of these degrees of freedom, and ${I}_{l}$ its complement, that is ${I}_{n}=\lbrace 1,\dots ,n\rbrace ={I}_{\mathit{nl}}\cup {I}_{l}$. The size of the vector ${I}_{\mathit{nl}}$ is equal to ${n}_{\mathit{nl}}$.

In order to be able to use the EHMAN algorithm, which combines the harmonic balance method (EH) and the asymptotic numerical method (MAN), a certain formalism must be adopted. It consists in rewriting the problem in the form of a first-order system, possessing non-linearities that are at most quadratic, that is a system in the unknown $S$ of the form

$$
A(\dot{S})=C+L(S)+Q(S,S),
$$

where $C$ is a constant, $L$ is a linear form and $Q$ a quadratic form. The terms on the right-hand side are in general grouped under the name $R(S)$; the problem to be solved is then written in condensed form

$$
A(\dot{S})=R(S)
$$

To this end, two additional vectors of unknowns are introduced, ${\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)$ and $\mathrm{Z}(t)$, together with additional equations defining these vectors. The vector ${\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)$ describes the contact force and the vector $\mathrm{Z}(t)$ is called the vector of auxiliary variables. The vector $\mathrm{Z}(t)$ is of size ${n}_{z}$. This relatively abstract rewriting, detailed by means of examples, leads to the solution of a system of the form

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\dot{\mathrm{U}}(t)=\mathrm{V}(t),\\ \mathrm{M}.\dot{\mathrm{V}}(t)=-\mathrm{K}\mathrm{U}(t)-\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ {\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(\mathrm{U}(t))\end{array}\right),\\ 0=\mathrm{G}({\mathrm{U}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t),{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t),\mathrm{Z}(t)),\end{array}
$$

where $\mathrm{G}$ is a quadratic operator, which defines the contact implicitly. $\mathrm{G}$ may be decomposed in the following way

$$
\mathrm{G}(\mathrm{S}(t))={\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{c}}+{\mathrm{G}}_{l}(\mathrm{S}(t))+{\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{q}}(\mathrm{S}(t),\mathrm{S}(t)).
$$

${\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{c}}$ is a constant mapping, ${\mathrm{G}}_{l}$ a linear one and ${\mathrm{G}}_{\mathrm{q}}$ a bilinear one.

### 3.1 Unilateral contact

| ![Figure](images/1000000000000251000001C246248D4DB86753D8.png) | ![Figure](images/100000000000035A0000029DD35F6E0747EBC59F.png) |

The unilateral-contact non-linearity, in its non-regular form, is written:

$$
{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-g)\mathit{si}{u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)⩾g,\\ 0\mathit{si}{u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)⩽g,\end{array}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the contact stiffness, $g$ the gap between the node of the structure and the contact node. The regularisation proposed is written in the implicit form

$$
{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)({\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)-\alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-g))-\alpha \eta =0,
$$

where $\eta$ represents the regularisation parameter.

### 3.2 Bilateral contact

| ![Figure](images/1000000000000388000001C63E14CC67E35C27B0.png) | ![Figure](images/1000000000000351000002A464ADF2963A80BE90.png) |

The bilateral-contact non-linearity, in its non-regular form, is written

$$
{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-g)\mathit{si}{u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)⩾g,\\ 0\mathit{si}\mid {u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)\mid ⩽g,\\ \alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)+g)\mathit{si}{u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)⩽-g,\end{array}
$$

where $\alpha$ is the contact stiffness, $g$ the gap between the node of the structure and the contact node. The regularisation proposed is written in the following implicit form

$$
{f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)({f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-\alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-g))({f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-\alpha ({u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)+g))-{\alpha}^{2}\eta {u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)=0,
$$

where $\eta$ represents the regularisation parameter. It may be rewritten in a quadratic form,

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\alpha}^{2}{g}^{2}{f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-\eta {\alpha}^{2}{u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-{f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)z(t)=0,\\ z(t)-{({f}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-\alpha {u}^{\mathit{nl}}(t))}^{2}=0.\end{array}
$$

### 3.3 Annular contact

| ![Figure](images/10000000000002430000024A75452B565D1CA9A2.png) | ![Figure](images/10000000000003700000030292CC0E0A415B890B.png) |

One introduces $z(t)$, the radial distance from the equilibrium position $({e}_{x},{e}_{y})$ of the impact node, whose current position is $({u}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}},{u}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}})$. $z(t)$ is written

$$
z(t)=\sqrt{{\left({u}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-{e}_{x}\right)}^{2}+{\left({u}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-{e}_{y}\right)}^{2}}.
$$

The annular-contact non-linearity, in its non-regular form, is written

$$
{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\alpha (z(t)-g)\mathit{si}z(t)>g,\\ 0\mathit{si}z(t)⩽g,\end{array}
$$

where $g$ denotes the radius of the ring (in other words the gap) and $\alpha$ ($\alpha >0$) denotes the associated stiffness coefficient. Introducing the components ${f}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}}$ and ${f}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}}$ of the non-linear force in the directions $x$ and $y$, the regularisation proposed is written in the following implicit form

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)\left({\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)-\alpha (z(t)-g)\right)-\alpha \eta =0,\\ {z}^{2}(t)-{({u}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-{e}_{x})}^{2}-{({u}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)-{e}_{y})}^{2}=0,\\ {f}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)z(t)–{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t){u}_{x}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)=0,\\ {f}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)z(t)–{\mathrm{f}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t){u}_{y}^{\mathit{nl}}(t)=0,\end{array}
$$

where $\eta$ represents the regularisation parameter.

## 4 Procedure for computing the non-linear modes

One seeks the periodic solutions of the system

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\mathrm{M}.\ddot{\mathrm{U}}(t)+\mathrm{K}\mathrm{U}(t)+\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ {\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(\mathrm{U}(t))\end{array}\right)=0,\\ \mathrm{G}({\mathrm{U}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t),{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t),\mathrm{Z}(t))=0,\end{array}
$$

by writing the vector $\mathrm{U}(t)$ by means of a Fourier series truncated at order ${H}_{l}$ (assuming that the influence of the higher harmonics is negligible), that is

$$
\mathrm{U}(t)={\mathrm{U}}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{{H}_{l}}{\mathrm{U}}_{\mathrm{Ck}}\cos(k\omega t)+{\mathrm{U}}_{\mathrm{Sk}}\sin(k\omega t).
$$

The same expansion is performed for the vectors of unknowns ${\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)$ and $\mathrm{Z}(t)$, but with a different truncation order, called ${H}_{\mathit{nl}}$. One then has

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)={\mathrm{F}}_{0}^{\mathrm{nl}}+\sum_{k=1}^{{H}_{\mathit{nl}}}{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{Ck}}^{\mathrm{nl}}\cos(k\omega t)+{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{Sk}}^{\mathrm{nl}}\sin(k\omega t),\\ \mathrm{Z}(t)={\mathrm{Z}}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{{H}_{\mathit{nl}}}{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathrm{Ck}}\cos(k\omega t)+{\mathrm{Z}}_{\mathrm{Sk}}\sin(k\omega t).\end{array}
$$

*Nota Bene:* **The choice of a different order follows directly from the quantities represented. For a system with little impact, in the neighbourhood of the linear mode, the response** $\mathrm{U}(t)$ **will be close to a pure sine. A small number of harmonics will therefore be chosen. One will increase** ${H}_{l}$ **when seeking to obtain solutions associated with high energy levels. On the other hand, even for weak impact forces, the spectral content may be significant, in particular if the impact is stiff. A large number of harmonics must therefore be retained, and one will therefore in general have** ${H}_{\mathit{nl}}\gg {H}_{l}$ **.**

**In practice, one may begin by carrying out an analysis by choosing relatively low orders, in order to have an idea of the principal behaviour. On the other hand, with a small number of harmonics, one will not be able to capture the bifurcations correctly. The values of** ${H}_{l}$ **and** ${H}_{\mathit{nl}}$ **will therefore be increased gradually in order to bring out more complex behaviours.**

After a harmonic balance has been performed, an under-determined algebraic system is obtained whose unknowns are the Fourier coefficients of $\mathrm{U}(t)$, ${\mathrm{F}}^{\mathrm{nl}}(t)$ and $\mathrm{Z}(t)$ and the additional unknown, the natural angular frequency $\omega$. These unknowns are gathered into a single vector in order to put the system in the form (). The MAN may then be used as indicated in reference [Bib3]. To this end, the vector of unknowns $S$ is expanded as a power series in a path parameter $a$

$$
S(a)={S}_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{{N}_{\mathit{MAN}}}{a}^{k}{S}_{k},
$$

where ${S}_{0}$ corresponds to the initialisation vector of the algorithm, and ${S}_{k}$ for $k=1,\dots ,{N}_{\mathit{MAN}}$ are the coefficients of the power series that remain to be determined. Next, the function $R$ is expanded as a Taylor series, in the neighbourhood of the initialisation vector ${S}_{0}$

$$
0=R(S(a))=R({S}_{0})+\frac{\mathit{dR}}{\mathit{dS}}({S}_{0})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{{N}_{\mathit{MAN}}}{a}^{k}{S}_{k}\right)+\frac{{d}^{2}R}{{\mathit{dS}}^{2}}({S}_{0})\left(\sum_{k=1}^{{N}_{\mathit{MAN}}}{a}^{k}{S}_{k}\right)\left(\sum_{l=1}^{{N}_{\mathit{MAN}}}{a}^{l}{S}_{l}\right)
$$

This relation is true for any $a$; one then proceeds to the solution of a sequence of ${N}_{\mathit{MAN}}$ linear systems possessing the same tangent matrix, depending on one another recursively. The solution of these systems makes it possible to obtain the vectors ${S}_{k}$. Note that this matrix can be computed analytically, since the function $R$ is quadratic, that is

$$
\frac{\mathit{dR}}{\mathit{dS}}({S}_{0}){e}_{i}=L({e}_{i})+Q({e}_{i},{S}_{0})+Q({S}_{0,}{e}_{i}),
$$

where ${e}_{i}$ is the vector of the canonical basis. A branch of solutions $S(a)$ with $a\in [0,{a}_{max}]$ is thus obtained, where ${a}_{max}$ represents the domain of validity of the power series. From then on, the branches of periodic solutions which form the non-linear modes of the model may be obtained.

The method also makes it possible to locate simple bifurcations by the detection of a geometric series in the power-series representation.
