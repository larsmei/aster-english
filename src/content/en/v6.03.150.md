# v6.03.150 SSNP150 – 2D contact manufactured solutions method and large deformations

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to verify the modelling of the 2D contact in large deformations using the method of manufactured solutions [bib1].

### 1.1 Geometry

A square is considered on the $1m$ side.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution analytical is given by:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\mathit{Ux}& =& -0.2\times Y\times Y\times Y\times (X-0.5)\\ \mathit{Uy}& =& -0.05\times (X-0.5)\times (X-0.5)\times (1+Y)-0.01\times Y\end{array}
$$

Dirichlet, Neumann and source terms are obtained by the Bib1 manufactured solutions method.

The $\underline{\underline{F}}$ transformation gradient is first determined:

$$
\underline{\underline{F}}=\underline{\nabla}\underline{U}+\underline{\underline{\mathit{Id}}}
$$

Knowing the normal $\underline{N}={[0,-1]}^{T}$ at the slave surface in the undeformed configuration, one obtains its expression in the configuration distorted by the formula of Nanson:

$$
\underline{n}=\frac{{\underline{\underline{F}}}^{-T}\underline{N}}{\parallel {\underline{\underline{F}}}^{-T}\underline{N}\parallel }
$$

Knowing the Hooke $\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{A}}}}$ tensor and the Green-Lagrange $\underline{\underline{E}}$ tensor, we calculate the second Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{S}}$ tensor:

$$
\underline{\underline{E}}=\frac{1}{2}({\underline{\underline{F}}}^{T}\cdot \underline{\underline{F}}-\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Id}}})
$$

$$
\underline{\underline{S}}=\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{A}}}}:\underline{\underline{E}}
$$

It is recalled that the second tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{S}}$ allows undeformed configuration forces per unit of undeformed surface:

$$
\frac{d\underline{{f}_{0}}}{\mathit{dA}}=\underline{\underline{S}}\cdot \underline{N}
$$

As we are looking to determine the forces in distorted configuration, we will determine the first tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{\Pi}}$

$$
\underline{\underline{\Pi}}=\underline{\underline{F}}\cdot \underline{\underline{S}}
$$

Volume forces ${\underline{f}}_{\mathit{vol}}$ can be determined as follows:

$$
{\underline{f}}_{\mathit{vol}}=\text{-div}\underline{\underline{\Pi}}
$$

Knowing the normal in initial configuration on the different faces and the first tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{\Pi}}$ , we can calculate the surface forces in distorted configuration:

$$
{\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}=\underline{\underline{\Pi}}\cdot \underline{N}
$$

On the $\text{BAS}$ surface that is in contact, one needs a special treatment. Indeed, normal efforts are taken into account by the contact:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}{\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}& =& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{contact}}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& p\ast \underline{n}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\end{array}
$$

Where $p$ is the contact pressure. It can be determined by the expression:

$$
p=(\underline{\underline{\Pi}}\cdot \underline{N})\cdot \underline{n}
$$

Therefore, it is only necessary to apply tangential efforts. They are calculated by the expression:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}& =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}-{\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}-({\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}\cdot \underline{n})\underline{n}\end{array}
$$

With regard to contact efforts, * it is absolutely essential to construct the manufactured solution so that they check the equations of contact* bib2, namely:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\text{gap}(\underline{U})& \text{}⩾\text{}& 0\\ p& \text{}⩽\text{}& 0\\ p\cdot \text{gap}(\underline{U})& =& 0\end{array}
$$

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.150/verification_posteriori_validite_solution.png)

* Checking the validity of the solution*

This verification is done after having calculated the pressure and displacement associated with the manufactured solution in an analytical way, usually with a formal calculation tool (in this case, it is the Python *sympy* module). They must then be viewed to verify that the solution that was checked well (). In the case of this test, we represented pressure and leap of analytical displacement in fig.. It is noted that they check $p<0$ and $\text{gap}(\underline{U})=0$ , which is characteristic of a fully contactant surface, and conforms to ().

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

La valeur de l'écart entre solutions analytique et calculée sur le maillage : $\sum^{\text{noeuds}n}\mid {\underline{U}}_{\text{n}}^{\text{calc}}-{\underline{U}}_{\text{n}}^{\text{ref}}\mid$ .

In the case of modellings that perform convergence analysis with mesh finesse, the convergence speed with mesh finesse of the solution calculated to analytical solution in ${L}_{2}$ standard:

- the largest ${\alpha}_{U}>0$ real, such as ${\parallel {\underline{U}}^{\text{calc}}-{\underline{U}}^{\text{ref}}\parallel }_{0,\Omega }<{C}_{U}\times {h}^{{\alpha}_{U}}$, where ${C}_{U}$ is independent of $h$ for displacement;
- the largest real ${\alpha}_{p}>0$ such as ${\parallel {p}^{\text{calc}}-{p}^{\text{ref}}\parallel }_{0,{\Gamma}_{C}}<{C}_{p}\times {h}^{{\alpha}_{p}}$ where ${C}_{p}$ is independent of $h$ for contact pressure.
