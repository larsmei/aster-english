# r7.01.24 Viscoplastic constitutive law LETK

**Summary:**

The L&K law describes an elasto-visco-plastic behaviour of rocks. Elastoplasticity is characterised by a positive hardening in the pre-peak regime and a softening behaviour beyond the strength. Viscoplasticity expresses the effect of time on the behaviour. It is described by a Perzyna power law.

The initiation of the elastoplastic or viscoplastic phenomena is triggered as soon as the corresponding threshold is crossed. The behaviour linked to each phase is described by the evolution of these various thresholds. This evolution is governed by plastic or viscoplastic hardening functions.

For the elastoplastic mechanism, a loading surface evolves through various thresholds:

- A damage threshold coincident with the initial viscosity threshold,
- A macroscopic peak threshold, defined from laboratory tests,
- An intermediate threshold, termed the cleavage limit, determined analytically,
- A characteristic threshold defined as the envelope of the damage threshold and of the cleavage limit, also called the contractancy/dilatancy limit (this limit is coincident with the maximum viscoplasticity threshold),
- A residual strength threshold.

For the viscous mechanism, a viscoplastic surface evolves through:

- An initial threshold coincident with the damage threshold
- A maximum viscosity threshold considered coincident with the contractancy/dilatancy limit

## 2 Introduction

This document presents the L&K rheological model developed at the CIH by F. Laigle and A. Kleine. It is an elasto-visco-plastic model dedicated to rocks. The specificity of the elastoplasticity lies in the modelling of a non-linear behaviour in the pre-peak phase and of a softening post-peak behaviour. The viscosity characterises the effect of time on the behaviour of the rock. The initiation of each of these phenomena is triggered as soon as a threshold is crossed. The behaviour linked to each phase is described by the evolution of these various thresholds, governed by plastic or viscoplastic hardening functions.

## 3 Equations of the L&K model

### 3.1 Simplification of the model

In order to describe this version of the model as well as possible and in a concise way, it is necessary to give an overview of the original model. The difference between the two versions will be better perceived.

#### 3.1.1 Brief overview of the thresholds of the original model

In the original version of the L&K model as it is developed under the Flac software at the CIH (cf. ) or the thesis of A. Kleine (cf.  R 4 ), there exist three distinct mechanisms:

- A pre-peak elastoplastic mechanism, governed by a positive hardening,
- A viscoplastic mechanism likewise governed by a positive hardening,
- A post-peak elastoplastic mechanism governed by a negative hardening describing the fracturing.

The particularity of this original model lies in the fact that the coupling of the two pre-peak mechanisms triggers the fracturing and therefore the post-peak mechanism. Indeed, the extension cracks induce a degradation of the mechanical properties of the materials with the increase of the dilatancy.

For the elastoplastic mechanism, a loading surface evolves through various thresholds. For the viscous mechanism, a viscoplastic surface evolves from an initial threshold to a final threshold.

The various thresholds bound domains associated with particular physical mechanisms:

- A damage threshold coincident with the initial viscosity threshold,
- An intermediate threshold, termed the cleavage limit,
- A characteristic threshold defined as the envelope of the damage threshold and of the cleavage limit, also called the contractancy/dilatancy limit (this limit is coincident with the maximum viscoplasticity threshold),
- A macroscopic peak threshold, defined from laboratory tests,
- A purely conceptual intrinsic threshold defined as an extrapolation of the peak threshold (this threshold is eliminated in the simplified version of the model),
- A residual strength threshold.

#### 3.1.2 Characteristics of the simplified model

The simplified version proposed by the CIH (cf. R 2 ) rests only on two mechanisms: an elastoplastic mechanism and a viscoplastic mechanism.

- The intrinsic threshold is eliminated in this version.
- The characteristic threshold bounding the domains of contractancy and of dilatancy in the pre-peak phase is linearised in order to avoid any numerical problem. It is assumed coincident with the maximum viscosity threshold.

### 3.2 Description of the mechanisms

#### 3.2.1 The viscoplastic mechanism

This mechanism is activated as soon as the loading point exceeds the initial viscoelastic threshold (assimilated to the initial elastic limit). Irreversible strains are generated. These rates of irreversible strain are proportional to the distance of the loading point from the viscosity threshold. The surface associated with the viscoplastic mechanism evolves from the initial elastic limit towards the maximum viscoplastic threshold as a function of the irreversible strains generated.

#### 3.2.2 The elastoplastic mechanism

##### 3.2.2.1 pre-peak

The elastoplastic mechanism is activated at the same time as the viscoplastic mechanism. As soon as the loading point exceeds the initial elastic limit, the loading surface begins to harden positively.

##### 3.2.2.2 post-peak

In the simplified version of the model, this mechanism is governed by:

- a negative hardening from the peak threshold towards the intermediate threshold,
- a negative hardening from the intermediate threshold towards the residual threshold.

#### 3.2.3 The volumetric behaviour

The volumetric behaviour, during the pre-peak phase, may be contractant or dilatant.

Below the initial elastic limit, the behaviour is contractant.

Below the contractancy/dilatancy limit, the volumetric behaviour is plastic contractant.

Beyond this limit, the volumetric behaviour is dilatant.

N.B.: In the simplified version of the model, the contractancy/dilatancy limit is coincident with the maximum viscoplastic threshold.

### 3.3 Decomposition of the strain tensor

The decomposition of the total strain increment is written:

$\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}={\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{e}+{\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{p}+{\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{\text{vp}}$

where ${\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{e}$ , ${\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{p}$ and ${\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{\text{vp}}$ are the increments of the elastic, instantaneous irreversible (plastic) and delayed irreversible (viscoplastic) tensors.

#### 3.3.1 Hypo-elasticity

The elastic law chosen is a hypo-elastic law:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{e}=\frac{1+\nu }{E}{\dot{\sigma}}_{ij}-\frac{3\nu }{E}\dot{p}{\delta}_{ij}$ or ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{e}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{2G}}{\dot{s}}_{ij}+\frac{1}{\mathrm{3K}}\dot{p}{\delta}_{ij}$

which one also writes: ${\dot{\sigma}}_{ij}={D}^{e}{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{e}$ .

The shear modulus $G$ and the bulk modulus $K$ depend on the stress state: $K={K}_{0}^{e}{\left[\frac{{I}_{1}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3P}}_{a}}\right]}^{{n}_{\text{elas}}}$ and $G={G}_{0}^{e}{\left[\frac{{I}_{1}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3P}}_{a}}\right]}^{{n}_{\text{elas}}}$ with ${I}_{1}^{-}=\text{tr}({\sigma}^{-})$ .

${I}_{1}^{-}=\text{tr}({\sigma}^{-})$ being the trace of the stresses at the instant $-$ .

#### 3.3.2 Plasticity

As in the simplified version of the model only the deviatoric mechanism is taken into account, the instantaneous irreversible strain is written:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}{G}_{ij}$

$\lambda$ being the plastic multiplier and $G$ the flow function.

Let ${f}^{d}$ be the plasticity criterion:

If ${f}^{d}\le 0$ then $\lambda =0$

If ${f}^{d}=0$ then $\lambda >0$

The expression of $G$ rests on several works cited in note R 2 and is of the form:

${G}_{ij}=\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{kl}}{n}_{kl}){n}_{ij}$ , ${n}_{ij}=\frac{{\beta}^{'}\frac{{s}_{ij}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}-{\delta}_{ij}}{\sqrt{{\beta}^{'2}+3}}$ , ${\beta}^{'}=-\frac{2\sqrt{6}\sin(\Psi )}{3-\sin(\Psi )}$

The expressions of $\sin(\Psi )$ are detailed in paragraphs 3.6.1 and 3.6.2 .

The computation of $\dot{\lambda}$ is the subject of paragraph 4.2.1.2 .

The computation of $\frac{\partial f}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}$ is detailed in paragraph 3.7.1

The evolution of the elastoplasticity induces a plastic strain: ${\epsilon}_{p}$ linked through its deviatoric component ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{p}$ to the hardening parameter ${\gamma}_{p}$ such that: ${\gamma}_{p}=\int\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\tilde{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{p}{\tilde{\dot{\varepsilon}}}_{p}}\text{dt}$

whence the relation ${\dot{\gamma}}_{p}=\dot{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\tilde{G}}_{ij}{\tilde{G}}_{ij}}=\dot{\lambda}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{G}_{\text{II}}$

#### 3.3.3 Viscoplasticity

The computation of the delayed irreversible strains ${\underline{\underline{\dot{\varepsilon}}}}^{\text{vp}}$ rests on the theory of Perzyna.

${\underline{\underline{\dot{\epsilon}}}}^{\text{vp}}=\langle \Phi ({f}^{\text{vp}})\rangle {G}_{ij}^{\text{visc}}$ where $\Phi ({f}^{\text{vp}})$ and ${G}^{\text{visc}}$ characterise the amplitude and the direction of the rate of the irreversible strains:

$\Phi ({f}^{\text{vp}})={A}_{v}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}}{\text{Pa}})}^{{n}_{v}}$ and ${G}_{ij}^{\text{visc}}=\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-(\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{kl}}{n}_{kl}){n}_{ij}$

${f}^{\text{vp}}$ being the viscoplasticity criterion, ${A}_{v}$ and ${n}_{v}$ are parameters of the model. ${P}_{a}$ is the atmospheric pressure.

The evolution of the viscoplasticity induces a viscous strain: linked through its deviatoric component ${\tilde{\epsilon}}_{\text{vp}}$ to the hardening parameter ${\gamma}_{\text{vp}}$ such that: ${\gamma}_{\text{vp}}=\int\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\tilde{\dot{\epsilon}}}_{\text{vp}}{\tilde{\dot{\epsilon}}}_{\text{vp}}}\text{dt}$ .

**Note:**

Let ${S}^{\text{vp}}$ be the surface defined in the stress space by: ${S}^{\text{vp}}=\left\lbrace \sigma ,{f}^{\text{vp}}(\sigma ,{\xi}^{\text{vp}})=0\right\rbrace$

The creep rate for a stress state $\sigma$ is proportional to the distance from $\sigma$ to ${S}^{\text{vp}}$ . Let ${P}_{\sigma}^{\text{vp}}$ be the projection of $\sigma$ onto ${S}^{\text{vp}}$ and $d=\parallel \sigma -{P}_{\sigma}^{\text{vp}}\parallel$ .

One can also write $d=\parallel \frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial \sigma }({P}_{\sigma}^{\text{vp}})\parallel C$ . $C$ being a constant which depends on the viscous parameters. As a first approximation, one writes: $d=\parallel \frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial \sigma }(\sigma )\parallel C$ . But this approximation poses a problem inasmuch as the function ${f}^{\text{vp}}$ may not be defined for the value $\sigma$ whereas it is for ${P}_{\sigma}^{\text{vp}}$ . For the moment, in *Code_Aster*  one does not compute this distance. If the situation arises, an alarm message warns the user.

### 3.4 Expressions of the criteria

The expressions of the two criteria, viscoplastic ${f}^{\text{vp}}$ and elastoplastic ${f}^{d}$ , depend on the stresses and on the hardening functions. In these expressions one finds ${I}_{1}$ the first invariant of the stresses and ${s}_{\text{II}}$ the second invariant of the deviatoric stress tensor. In the two criteria, the same definitions are adopted for:

$H(\theta )=\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}+{H}_{0}^{e}}{2}+(\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e}}{2})(\frac{\mathrm{2h}(\theta )-({h}_{0}^{c}+{h}_{0}^{e})}{{h}_{0}^{c}-{h}_{0}^{e}})$

$h(\theta )={(1-\gamma \cos\mathrm{3\theta })}^{\frac{1}{6}}$ , ${h}_{0}^{c}={H}_{0}^{c}=h({0}^{°})={(1-\gamma )}^{\frac{1}{6}}$ , ${h}_{0}^{e}=h({60}^{°})={(1+\gamma )}^{\frac{1}{6}}$ ,

${H}_{0}^{e}$ is a parameter of the model. $\theta$ is the Lode angle.

#### 3.4.1 The viscoplastic criterion ${f}^{\text{vp}}$

${f}^{\text{vp}}(\sigma )={s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )-{\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){I}_{1}+{D}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})\right]}^{{a}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})}$

with ${A}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})=-\frac{{m}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){k}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})}{\sqrt{6}{\sigma}_{c}{h}_{c}^{0}}$ , ${B}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})=\frac{{m}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){k}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})}{{\mathrm{3\sigma }}_{c}}$ , ${D}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})={s}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){k}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ , ${k}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})={(\frac{2}{3})}^{\frac{1}{{\mathrm{2a}}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})}}$

The hardening functions ${A}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ , ${B}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ and ${D}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ depend on the hardening parameters ${a}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ , ${m}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ and ${s}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})$ whose expressions evolve with the hardening variables ${\xi}_{\text{vp}}$ (see §  3.5 ). When ${\xi}_{\text{vp}}$ reaches certain particular values, the surface ${f}^{\text{vp}}$ reaches the corresponding thresholds.

Given that the viscoplastic threshold hardens only through the viscosity, one always has ${\dot{\xi}}_{\text{vp}}=\text{Min}\left[{\dot{\gamma}}_{\text{vp}},{\xi}_{v-\max}-{\xi}_{\text{vp}}\right]$ . ${\xi}_{\text{vp}-\max}$ corresponds to the maximum viscoplastic criterion and is a parameter of the model

#### 3.4.2 The elastoplastic criterion ${f}^{d}$

${f}^{d}(\sigma )={s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )-{\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){I}_{1}+{D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})\right]}^{{a}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}$

with ${A}^{d}({\xi}_{p})=-\frac{{m}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}{\sqrt{6}{\sigma}_{c}{h}_{c}^{0}}$ , ${B}^{d}({\xi}_{p})=\frac{{m}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}{{\mathrm{3\sigma }}_{c}}$ , ${D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})={s}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ , ${k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})={(\frac{2}{3})}^{\frac{1}{{\mathrm{2a}}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}}$

The hardening functions ${A}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ , ${B}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ and ${D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ depend on the hardening parameters ${a}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ , ${m}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ and ${s}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$ whose expressions evolve with the hardening variables ${\xi}_{p}$ (see §  3.5 ). When ${\xi}_{p}$ reaches certain particular values, the surface ${f}^{d}$ reaches the corresponding thresholds.

The elastoplastic hardening depends on the position of the loading point relative to the contractancy/dilatancy limit:

- if the loading point is below this limit, ${\dot{\xi}}_{p}={\dot{\gamma}}_{p}$ ,
- if the loading point is above this limit, ${\dot{\xi}}_{p}={\dot{\gamma}}_{p}+{\dot{\gamma}}_{\text{vp}}$ .

### 3.5 Hardening functions

#### 3.5.1 Hardening functions of the viscous criterion

The viscoplastic criterion is governed by the following hardening functions:

$a({\xi}_{\text{vp}})={a}_{0}+({a}_{v-\max}-{a}_{0})\frac{{\xi}_{\text{vp}}}{{\xi}_{v-\max}}$ with ${a}_{v-\max}=1.$

$m({\xi}_{\text{vp}})={m}_{0}+({m}_{v-\max}-{m}_{0})\frac{{\xi}_{\text{vp}}}{{\xi}_{v-\max}}$

$s({\xi}_{\text{vp}})={s}_{0}+({s}_{v-\max}-{s}_{0})\frac{{\xi}_{\text{vp}}}{{\xi}_{v-\max}}$ with ${s}_{v-\max}={s}_{0}$

#### 3.5.2 Hardening functions of the elastoplastic criterion and their derivatives

The expressions of the hardening functions which govern the elastoplastic criterion vary according to the value of the parameter ${\xi}_{p}$ :

**Evolution between the damage threshold and the peak threshold** : If $0\le {\xi}_{p}<{\xi}_{\text{pic}}$

$a({\xi}_{p})={a}_{0}+\ln(1+\frac{{\xi}_{p}}{{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})(\frac{{a}_{\text{pic}}-{a}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})$         $\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=(\frac{{a}_{\text{pic}}-{a}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})(\frac{1}{{\xi}_{p}+{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})$

$m({\xi}_{p})={m}_{0}+\ln(1+\frac{{\xi}_{p}}{{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})(\frac{{m}_{\text{pic}}-{m}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})$         $\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=(\frac{{m}_{\text{pic}}-{m}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})(\frac{1}{{\xi}_{p}+{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})$

$s({\xi}_{p})={s}_{0}+\ln(1+\frac{{\xi}_{p}}{{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})(\frac{{s}_{\text{pic}}-{s}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})$             $\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=(\frac{{s}_{\text{pic}}-{s}_{0}}{\ln(1+1/{x}_{\text{ams}})})(\frac{1}{{\xi}_{p}+{x}_{\text{ams}}{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})$

with ${s}_{\text{pic}}=1.$

**Evolution between the peak threshold and the intermediate threshold**  or the cleavage limit: If ${\xi}_{\text{pic}}\le {\xi}_{p}<{\xi}_{e}$

$a({\xi}_{p})={a}_{\text{pic}}+({a}_{e}-{a}_{\text{pic}})(\frac{{\xi}_{p}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}}{{\xi}_{e}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})$             $\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{{a}_{e}-{a}_{\text{pic}}}{{\xi}_{e}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}}$

$s({\xi}_{p})=1-(\frac{{\xi}_{p}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}}{{\xi}_{e}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}})$                         $\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{-1}{{\xi}_{e}-{\xi}_{\text{pic}}}$

$\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{\partial m}{\partial a}\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}+\frac{\partial m}{\partial s}\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}$

$\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int1}}\left[(-\frac{{a}_{\text{pic}}}{a{({\xi}_{p})}^{2}}){({m}_{\text{pic}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int1}}{{\sigma}_{c}}+{s}_{\text{pic}})}^{\frac{{a}_{\text{pic}}}{a({\xi}_{p})}}\ln({m}_{\text{pic}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int1}}{{\sigma}_{c}}+{s}_{\text{pic}})\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}-\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\right]$

**Evolution between the intermediate threshold and the residual threshold**  : If ${\xi}_{e}\le {\xi}_{p}<{\xi}_{\text{ult}}$

$a({\xi}_{p})={a}_{e}+\ln(1+\frac{1}{\eta}\frac{{\xi}_{p}-{\xi}_{e}}{{\xi}_{\text{ult}}-{\xi}_{e}})(\frac{{a}_{\text{ult}}-{a}_{e}}{\ln(1+1/\eta )})$         $\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=(\frac{{a}_{\text{ult}}-{a}_{e}}{\ln(1+1/\eta )})(\frac{1}{{\xi}_{p}+{\text{ηξ}}_{\text{ult}}-(1+\eta ){\xi}_{e}})$

$s({\xi}_{p})=0$             $\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}^{p}}=0$

$m({\xi}_{p})=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int2}}{({m}_{e}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int2}}{{\sigma}_{c}})}^{\frac{{a}_{e}}{a({\xi}_{p})}}$ $\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int2}}\left[(-\frac{{a}_{e}}{a{({\xi}_{p})}^{2}})\ln({m}_{e}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int2}}{{\sigma}_{c}}){({m}_{e}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{po}int2}}{{\sigma}_{c}})}^{\frac{{a}_{e}}{a({\xi}_{p})}}\right]\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}$

**On the residual criterion** : If ${\xi}_{p}\ge {\xi}_{\text{ult}}$

$a({\xi}_{p})={a}_{\text{ult}}=1.$         $\frac{\partial a}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=0$

$s({\xi}_{p})=0$                 $\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=0$

$m({\xi}_{p})={m}_{\text{ult}}$             $\frac{\partial m}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=0$

### 3.6 Dilatancy laws

The elastoplastic and viscoplastic mechanisms are non-associated. The evolution laws of ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij}^{p}$ and of ${\dot{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}}$ are governed respectively by a function $G$ and a function ${G}^{\text{visc}}$ , such that:

${G}_{ij}=\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{kl}}{n}_{kl}){n}_{ij}$ and ${G}_{ij}^{\text{visc}}=\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-(\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{kl}}{n}_{kl}){n}_{ij}$ with

${n}_{ij}=\frac{{\beta}^{'}\frac{{s}_{ij}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}-{\delta}_{ij}}{\sqrt{{\beta}^{{'}^{2}}+3}}$ and ${\beta}^{'}=-\frac{2\sqrt{6}\sin(\Psi )}{3-\sin(\Psi )}$

The computation of the dilatancy angle $\Psi$ differs according to the viscous or pre-peak elastoplastic mechanisms and the post-peak elastoplastic mechanism.

#### 3.6.1 Dilatancy angle of the pre-peak elastoplastic and viscoplastic mechanisms

$\sin(\Psi )={\mu}_{0,v}(\frac{{\sigma}_{\max}-{\sigma}_{\lim}}{{\xi}_{0,v}{\sigma}_{\max}+{\sigma}_{\lim}})$ with ${\mu}_{0,v}$ and ${\xi}_{0,v}$ parameters of the model.

where

${\sigma}_{\lim}={\sigma}_{\min}+{\sigma}_{c}{({m}_{v-\max}\frac{{\sigma}_{\min}}{{\sigma}_{c}}+{s}_{v-\max})}^{{a}_{v-\max}}$ with ${s}_{v-\max}={s}_{0}$ and ${a}_{v-\max}=1$ . ${\sigma}_{c}$ and ${m}_{v-\max}$ are parameters of the model.

There exist conditions on the parameters ${\mu}_{0,v}$ and ${\xi}_{0,v}$ which are:

- ${\mu}_{0,v}<{\xi}_{0,v}$ or
- $\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\mu}_{0,v}>{\xi}_{0,v}\\ \frac{{({s}_{\text{pic}})}^{{a}_{\text{pic}}}}{{({s}_{0})}^{{a}_{0}}}\le \frac{1+{\mu}_{0,v}}{{\mu}_{0,v}-{\xi}_{0,v}}\end{array}$

#### 3.6.2 Dilatancy angle of the post-peak elastoplastic mechanism

$\sin(\Psi )={\mu}_{1}(\frac{\alpha -{\alpha}_{\text{res}}}{{\xi}_{1}\alpha +{\alpha}_{\text{res}}})$ with ${\mu}_{1}$ and ${\xi}_{1}$ parameters of the model

where

$\alpha =\frac{{\sigma}_{\max}+\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\min}+\tilde{\sigma}}$ and ${\alpha}_{\text{res}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\max}}{{\sigma}_{\min}}=1+{m}_{\text{ult}}$

$\tilde{\sigma}=\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\frac{\tilde{c}({\xi}_{p})}{\tan(\tilde{\varphi}({\xi}_{p}))}\text{si}{\xi}_{p}\le {\xi}_{e}\\ 0\text{si}{\xi}_{p}>{\xi}_{e}\end{array}$

with $\tilde{c}({\xi}_{p})=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}.s{({\xi}_{p})}^{a({\xi}_{p})}}{2\sqrt{1+a({\xi}_{p})m({\xi}_{p})s{({\xi}_{p})}^{a({\xi}_{p})-1}}}$ and $\tilde{\varphi}({\xi}_{p})=2.\text{arctg}(\sqrt{1+a({\xi}_{p})m({\xi}_{p})s{({\xi}_{p})}^{a({\xi}_{p})-1}})-\frac{\pi}{2}$

${\sigma}_{\min}$ and ${\sigma}_{\max}$ are computed from the stress invariants:

${\sigma}_{\min}=\frac{1}{3}({I}_{1}-(\frac{3}{2}-\frac{\mathrm{2H}(\theta )-({H}_{0}^{c}+{H}_{0}^{e})}{2({H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e})})\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{s}_{\text{II}})$

${\sigma}_{\max}=\frac{1}{3}({I}_{1}+(\frac{3}{2}+\frac{\mathrm{2H}(\theta )-({H}_{0}^{c}+{H}_{0}^{e})}{2({H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e})})\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{s}_{\text{II}})$

### 3.7 Derivatives of the criterion

#### 3.7.1 Computation of $\frac{\partial f}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}$

$\frac{\partial {I}_{1}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\frac{\partial \text{tr}({\sigma}_{ij})}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}={\delta}_{ij}$

$\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {s}_{kl}}\frac{\partial {s}_{kl}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=(\frac{\partial H(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}{s}_{\text{II}}+H(\theta )\frac{\partial {s}_{\text{II}}}{\partial {s}_{kl}})\frac{\partial {s}_{kl}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}$

$\frac{\partial {s}_{\text{II}}}{\partial {s}_{kl}}=\frac{{s}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}$ ; ${s}_{\text{II}}=\sqrt{{s}_{kl}.{s}_{kl}}$

$\frac{\partial {s}_{kl}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\frac{\partial ({\sigma}_{kl}-\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\sigma ){\delta}_{kl})}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}={\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl}$

**Note:** ${s}_{kl}.({\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl})={s}_{ij}$

$\frac{\partial H(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}=(\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e}}{{h}_{0}^{c}-{h}_{0}^{e}})\frac{\partial h(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}$

whence $\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=((\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e}}{{h}_{0}^{c}-{h}_{0}^{e}})\frac{\partial h(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}{s}_{\text{II}}+H(\theta )\frac{{s}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}).({\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl})$

One has the relation: $\cos(\mathrm{3\theta })=\sqrt{54}\frac{\det(s)}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}$ (see Documentation R7.01.13-A: CJS law in mechanics)

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial h(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}=\frac{1}{6}{(1-\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta }))}^{-\frac{5}{6}}\frac{\partial (1-\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta }))}{\partial {s}_{kl}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\frac{\partial}{\partial {s}_{kl}}(\frac{{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}-\gamma \sqrt{54}\det(s)}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}})\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial h(\theta )}{\partial {s}_{kl}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\left\lbrace \left[\frac{\partial {s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}{\partial {s}_{kl}}-\gamma \sqrt{54}(\frac{\partial \det(\underline{\underline{s}})}{\partial {s}_{kl}})\right]\frac{{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{6}}-({s}_{\text{II}}^{3}-\gamma \sqrt{54}\det(\underline{\underline{s}}))\frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}{s}_{\text{II}}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{6}}\right\rbrace \\ =\frac{1}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\left\lbrace \frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{2}}-\gamma \sqrt{54}(\frac{\partial \det(\underline{\underline{s}})}{\partial {s}_{kl}})\frac{1}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}-(1-\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta }))\frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{2}}\right\rbrace \\ =\frac{\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta })}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma \sqrt{54}}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}(\frac{\partial \det(\underline{\underline{s}})}{\partial {s}_{kl}})\end{array}$

One therefore finds:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\\ ((\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e}}{{h}_{0}^{c}-{h}_{0}^{e}})(\frac{\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta })}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma \sqrt{54}}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}(\frac{\partial \det(\underline{\underline{s}})}{\partial {s}_{kl}})){s}_{\text{II}}+H(\theta )\frac{{s}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}).({\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl})\end{array}$

Finally:

**For the elastoplastic criterion** :

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\\ \frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-{a}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){I}_{1}+{D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})\right]}^{{a}^{d}({\xi}_{p})-1}\\ ({A}^{d}({\xi}_{p})\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}+{B}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){I}_{d})\end{array}$

**and for the viscous criterion:**

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=\\ \frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}-{a}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){I}_{1}+{D}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})\right]}^{{a}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{pp}})-1}\\ ({A}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}})\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}+{B}^{\text{vp}}({\xi}_{\text{vp}}){I}_{d})\end{array}$

with $\frac{\partial ({s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta ))}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}=$

$((\frac{{H}_{0}^{c}-{H}_{0}^{e}}{{h}_{0}^{c}-{h}_{0}^{e}})(\frac{\gamma \cos(\mathrm{3\theta })}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}}\frac{{\mathrm{3s}}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}^{2}}-\frac{\gamma \sqrt{54}}{\mathrm{6h}{(\theta )}^{5}{s}_{\text{II}}^{3}}(\frac{\partial \det(\underline{\underline{s}})}{\partial {s}_{kl}})){s}_{\text{II}}+H(\theta )\frac{{s}_{kl}}{{s}_{\text{II}}}).({\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl})$

#### 3.7.2 Computation of $\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}$

Expression of the threshold in stresses:

${f}^{d}(\sigma )={s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )-{\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){I}_{1}+{D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})\right]}^{{a}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}$

with ${A}^{d}({\xi}^{p})=-\frac{{m}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}{\sqrt{6}{\sigma}_{c}{h}_{c}^{0}}$ , ${B}^{d}({\xi}_{p})=\frac{{m}^{d}({\xi}_{p}){k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}{{\mathrm{3\sigma }}_{c}}$ , ${D}^{d}({\xi}_{p})={s}^{d}({\xi}_{p})k({\xi}_{p})$ , ${k}^{d}({\xi}_{p})={(\frac{2}{3})}^{\frac{1}{{\mathrm{2a}}^{d}({\xi}_{p})}}$

$\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}=\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {a}^{d}}.{\dot{a}}^{d}({\xi}_{p})+\frac{\partial f}{\partial {m}^{d}}.{\dot{m}}^{d}({\xi}_{p})+\frac{\partial f}{\partial {s}^{d}}.{\dot{s}}^{d}({\xi}_{p})$

$\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {s}^{d}}=-{a}^{d}{k}^{d}{\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\left[{A}^{d}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}{I}_{1}+{D}^{d}\right]}^{{a}^{d}-1}$

$\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {m}^{d}}=-{a}^{d}{\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}\left[\frac{{A}^{d}}{{m}^{d}}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+\frac{{B}^{d}}{{m}^{d}}{I}_{1}\right]{\left[{A}^{d}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}{I}_{1}+{D}^{d}\right]}^{{a}^{d}-1}$

$\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {a}^{d}}=$

${\sigma}_{c}{H}_{0}^{c}{\dot{a}}^{d}{\left[{A}^{d}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}{I}_{1}+{D}^{d}\right]}^{{a}^{d}}.\left[\ln\left[{A}^{d}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}{I}_{1}+{D}^{d}\right]-\frac{\frac{{s}^{d}}{{\mathrm{2a}}^{d}}\ln(\frac{2}{3}){(\frac{2}{3})}^{(\frac{1}{\mathrm{2a}})}}{\left[{A}^{d}{s}_{\text{II}}H(\theta )+{B}^{d}{I}_{1}+{D}^{d}\right]}\right]$

## 4 Integration in Code_Aster

The integration of the LETK model can be carried out according to two distinct integration schemes. The first integration scheme (“historical”) is described as SPECIFIQUE and corresponds to an explicit integration scheme. The second scheme is constructed on the basis of the implicit integration schemes. It is accessible under the keyword NEWTON_PERT .

- If ${\gamma}^{p}=0$ then the material is in domain 1;

### 4.2 Explicit integration scheme ( SPECIFIQUE )

#### 4.2.1 Update of the stresses

One expresses the stresses updated at the instant + relative to those computed at the instant -:

$\sigma ={\sigma}^{-}+{D}^{e}{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}^{e}$ ; $s={s}^{-}+\mathrm{2G\Delta }{\tilde{\epsilon}}^{e}$ ; ${I}_{1}={I}_{1}^{-}+{\mathrm{3K\Delta \epsilon }}_{v}^{e}$

${\sigma}_{ij}={s}_{ij}+\frac{{I}_{1}}{3}{\delta}_{ij}$ ; ${\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{ij}=\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}+\frac{\text{tr}(\mathit{\Delta \epsilon })}{3}{\delta}_{ij}=\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}+\frac{{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{v}}{3}{\delta}_{ij}$ ; ${I}_{1}=\text{tr}(\sigma )$ ; ${\epsilon}_{v}=\text{tr}(\mathit{\Delta \epsilon })$

Elastic prediction:

${\sigma}^{e}={\sigma}^{-}+{D}^{e}\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }$ ; ${s}^{e}={s}^{-}+\mathrm{2G\Delta }\tilde{\epsilon}$ ; ${I}_{1}^{e}={I}_{1}^{-}+{\mathrm{3K\Delta \epsilon }}_{v}$

$K={K}_{0}^{e}{\left[\frac{{I}_{1}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3P}}_{a}}\right]}^{{n}_{\text{elas}}}$ and $G={G}_{0}^{e}{\left[\frac{{I}_{1}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3P}}_{a}}\right]}^{{n}_{\text{elas}}}$

**Note:**  The bulk modulus $K$ and the shear modulus $G$ are considered at the instant -.

##### 4.2.1.1 Hypoelasticity

${\mathit{\Delta \sigma }}_{ij}={\mathit{\Delta s}}_{ij}+\frac{{\mathit{\Delta I}}_{1}}{3}{\delta}_{ij}$         ${\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{ij}=\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}+\frac{{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{v}}{3}{\delta}_{ij}$         ${\mathit{\Delta \sigma }}_{ij}={\mathrm{2G\Delta \epsilon }}_{ij}+(K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3})\text{tr}(\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }){\delta}_{ij}$

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\sigma}_{11}\\ \Delta {\sigma}_{22}\\ \Delta {\sigma}_{33}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\sigma}_{12}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\sigma}_{13}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\sigma}_{23}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\underset{{D}^{e}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}\frac{\mathrm{4G}}{3}+K& K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& 0& 0& 0\\ K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& \frac{\mathrm{4G}}{3}+K& K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& 0& 0& 0\\ K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& K-\frac{\mathrm{2G}}{3}& \frac{\mathrm{4G}}{3}+K& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{2G}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{2G}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& \mathrm{2G}\end{array}\right]}}.\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{11}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{22}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{33}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{12}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{13}\\ \sqrt{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{23}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

##### 4.2.1.2 Plasticity and viscoplasticity

One expresses the stress field at the instant $+$ :

${\sigma}_{ij}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{e}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}({\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{p}-{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}})$

which is written, upon replacing the increment of the plastic and viscous strains by their expressions, in the form:

${\sigma}_{ij}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}({\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{\mathrm{\Delta \lambda G}}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\langle \phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t})$

The principal unknown is the plastic multiplier $\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }$ .

One seeks $\mathit{\Delta \lambda }/{f}^{d}(\sigma ,{\xi}_{p})=0$

${f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}({\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{\mathrm{\Delta \lambda G}}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\langle \phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}),{\xi}_{p}^{-}+{\mathrm{\Delta \xi }}_{p})=0$

with ${\mathit{\Delta \gamma }}_{p}=\mathit{\Delta \lambda }\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\tilde{G}}_{ij}{\tilde{G}}_{ij}}=\mathit{\Delta \lambda }\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{G}_{\text{II}}$

One chooses to carry out an explicit solution with an Euler expansion:

${f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}{\mathrm{\Delta \xi }}_{p}=0$

One distinguishes the two cases:

- ${\mathrm{\Delta \xi }}_{p}={\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{p}+{\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}$ (dilatant case: the stress state exceeds the contractancy/dilatancy limit)

${f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\mathit{\Delta t},{\xi}_{p}^{-})$

$-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})\mathit{\Delta \lambda }+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}({\mathit{\Delta \gamma }}_{p}+{\mathit{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}})=0$

$\begin{array}{c}{f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}<\Phi >{G}_{kl}^{\mathit{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\Delta t,{\xi}_{p}^{-})=\\ (\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{G}_{\mathit{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})\Delta \lambda -\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\Delta {\gamma}_{\mathit{vp}})\end{array}$

$\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }=\frac{{f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}{\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$

- ${\mathrm{\Delta \xi }}_{p}={\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{p}$ (contractant case: the stress state is below the contractancy/dilatancy limit)

$\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }=\frac{{f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$

with $\Phi ={A}_{v}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})}{\text{Pa}})}^{{n}_{v}}$ , ${A}_{v}$ and ${n}_{v}$ are parameters of the model.

#### 4.2.2 Tangent operator

The stress at the state $+$ :

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{ij}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}^{e}={\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}(\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}-\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}^{p}-\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}^{\mathrm{vp}})=\\ {\sigma}_{ij}^{-}+{D}_{ijkl}^{e}(\Delta {\epsilon}_{kl}-\Delta \lambda {G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\mathrm{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{\mathrm{vp}}^{-})\Delta t)\end{array}$

$\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}={D}_{ijkl}^{e}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}^{-}\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{imn}}^{\text{visc}-}\frac{\partial \langle \phi \rangle }{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}\mathrm{\Delta t}$

One distinguishes the two cases:

- ${\mathrm{\Delta \xi }}_{p}={\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{p}$ (contractant case: the stress state is below the characteristic threshold)

$\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }=\frac{{f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$ and $\Phi ={A}_{v}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})}{\text{Pa}})}^{{n}_{v}}$

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}=\frac{\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}.\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{v}^{-})\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$

$\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}=\frac{{A}_{v}.{n}_{v}}{\text{Patm}}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})}{\text{Patm}})}^{{n}_{v}-1}.\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{e}}.\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{e}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}=\frac{{A}_{v}.{n}_{v}}{\text{Patm}}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})}{\text{Patm}})}^{{n}_{v}-1}.\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{e}}.{D}_{ijkl}^{e}$

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}={D}_{ijkl}^{e}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}.{G}_{\text{mn}}^{-}\frac{\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}.\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}-\\ {D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}.{G}_{\text{mn}}^{\text{visc}-}\frac{{A}_{v}.{n}_{v}}{\text{Patm}}{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})}{\text{Patm}})}^{{n}_{v}-1}.\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{e}}.{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\mathrm{\Delta t}\end{array}$

- $\Delta {\xi}_{p}=\Delta {\gamma}_{p}+\Delta {\gamma}_{v}$ (dilatant case: the stress state exceeds the characteristic threshold)

$\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }=\frac{{f}^{d}({\sigma}_{ij}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}{\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}-{D}_{ijkl}^{e}\langle \Phi \rangle {G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{ijkl}^{e}{G}_{kl}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$

$\frac{\partial \mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}=\frac{\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}.\left[{D}_{ijkl}^{e}-{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}^{\text{visc}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}}\mathrm{\Delta t}\right]+\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}.\frac{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}}}.\frac{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{ij}^{e}}.{D}_{ijkl}^{e}}{(\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{D}_{\text{ijmn}}^{e}{G}_{\text{mn}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-})-\frac{\partial {f}^{d}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\tilde{G}}_{\text{II}}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{p}^{-}))}$

where:

$\frac{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}}}=\frac{1}{2}{(\frac{2}{3}\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}}.\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}})}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.\frac{2}{3}.2.\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}}.\frac{\partial \Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}}}=\frac{2}{3}.\frac{\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{ij}^{\text{vp}}}{{\mathrm{\Delta \gamma }}_{\text{vp}}}.({\delta}_{\text{ik}}.{\delta}_{\text{jl}}-\frac{1}{3}{\delta}_{ij}.{\delta}_{kl})$

$\frac{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \epsilon }}_{kl}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{ij}^{e}}=\frac{{n}_{v}.{A}_{v}}{\text{Patm}}.{(\frac{{f}^{\text{vp}}({\sigma}^{e},{\xi}_{v}^{-})}{\text{Patm}})}^{{n}_{v}-1}.\frac{\partial {f}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{e}}.{G}_{kl}^{\text{visc}-}({\sigma}^{-},{\xi}_{\text{vp}}^{-})\mathrm{\Delta t}$

#### 4.2.3 Solution algorithm in Code_Aster

- Change of sign of the stresses at the state $-$ and of the strain increment:

${\sigma}_{L\wedge K}^{-}=-{\sigma}^{-}$

### 4.3 Implicit integration scheme

The integration of the LETK model according to the implicit integration scheme is carried out under the PLASTI environment. The integration of the LETK model under the implicit scheme is currently available only by computation of a perturbed local Jacobian matrix ( 'NEWTON_PERT' ).

The solution algorithm follows the following logic. It uses an elastic prediction then correction iterations if the viscous and/or plastic thresholds are activated. The scheme aims to produce the variation of the stresses and of the hardening variables under the effect of a strain increment.

The local subdivision of the model can be activated under this integration scheme by the keyword ITER_INTE_PAS of the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT , cf [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)).

#### 4.3.1 Elastic prediction phase

This phase is similar to that presented in section  4.2.1 .

The exceeding of the plasticity and viscosity thresholds is tested with respect to this stress state. The expression of the thresholds tested is made explicit in §  3.2 .

- If none of the thresholds is activated, the prediction is considered valid with respect to the models. An update of the internal variables is undertaken in order to display the activation state of the various thresholds.
- If one threshold among the two to be considered (plasticity and/or viscosity) is activated, the solution of a local system of non-linear equations must be initiated. The dissipation mechanisms defined as potentially active must lead to the exceeding of the associated thresholds (plasticity and/or viscosity)

#### 4.3.2 Correction phase: non-linear equations to be solved

This step consists in solving the system of local non-linear equations established on the basis of the viscous and/or plastic mechanisms. After convergence, the stresses and internal variables of the model are updated.

The unknowns of the system of non-linear equations are the stresses ${\sigma}_{n+1}$ , the plastic multiplier ${\lambda}_{n+1}^{p}$ , the plastic hardening variable ${\xi}_{n+1}^{p}$ and the viscous hardening variable ${\xi}_{n+1}^{\mathit{vp}}$ .

The vector of unknowns therefore comprises at most, for $\mathrm{3D}$ modellings, 9 unknowns.

The non-linear equations to be solved are the following:

- The incremental state equation, $\mathit{E1}$ :

${\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}-{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}-{C}^{e}({\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}):(\Delta \underline{\underline{\epsilon}}-\Delta \lambda {\underline{\underline{G}}}^{p}-\Phi ({f}^{\mathit{vp}})\cdot {\underline{\underline{G}}}^{\mathit{vp}})=0$

- The Kuhn–Tucker condition, $\mathit{E2}$ :

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{cccc}\text{Si}& {f}^{d}\le 0& \text{alors}& \Delta \lambda =0\\ \text{Si}& {f}^{d}=0& \text{alors}& \Delta \lambda >0\end{array}$

- The incremental evolution of the plastic hardening variable, $\mathit{E3}$ :

${\xi}_{n+1}^{p}-{\xi}_{n}^{p}-\Delta {\xi}^{p}=0$ , with $\Delta {\xi}^{p}$ evolving according to the conditions specified in §  3.4.2 .

- The incremental evolution of the visco-plastic hardening variable, $\mathit{E4}$ :

${\xi}_{n+1}^{\mathit{vp}}-{\xi}_{n}^{\mathit{vp}}-\Delta {\xi}^{\mathit{vp}}=0$ , with $\Delta {\xi}^{\mathit{vp}}$ evolving according to the conditions specified in §  3.4.1 .

These equations constitute a square system $R(\Delta Y)$ , where the unknowns are $\Delta Y=(\Delta \underline{\underline{\sigma}},\Delta \lambda ,\Delta {\xi}^{p},\Delta {\xi}^{\mathit{vp}})$ . At iteration $j$ of the local Newton correction loop, one solves the following matrix equation:

$\frac{dR(\Delta {Y}^{j})}{d(\Delta {Y}^{j})}\cdot \delta (\Delta {Y}^{j+1})=-R(\Delta {Y}^{j})$

The Jacobian matrix $\frac{dR(\Delta {Y}^{j})}{d(\Delta {Y}^{j})}$ , non-symmetric, is constructed in the following way:

$\frac{dR(\Delta {Y}^{j})}{d(\Delta {Y}^{j})}=\left[\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{\partial \mathit{E1}}{\partial {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{j}}& \frac{\partial \mathit{E1}}{\partial \Delta {\lambda}^{j}}& \frac{\partial \mathit{E1}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}^{j}}& \frac{\partial \mathit{E1}}{\partial {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}^{j}}\\ \frac{\mathit{E2}}{\partial {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E2}}{\partial \Delta {\lambda}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E2}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E2}}{\partial {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}^{j}}\\ \frac{\mathit{E3}}{\partial {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E3}}{\partial \Delta {\lambda}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E3}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E3}}{\partial {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}^{j}}\\ \frac{\mathit{E4}}{\partial {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E4}}{\partial \Delta {\lambda}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E4}}{\partial {\xi}_{p}^{j}}& \frac{\mathit{E4}}{\partial {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}^{j}}\end{array}\right]$

This matrix is today evaluated analytically or by perturbation (ALGO_INTE = 'NEWTON' or ALGO_INTE = 'NEWTON_PERT').

In order to make the scales uniform between the various equations to be solved, one chooses to scale to strains the equation E1 bearing on the incremental state equation. One applies for this the inverse of the non-linear elastic shear modulus. This choice makes it possible to ensure a more uniform convergence over the whole of the system.

Convergence is deemed to have been reached as soon as $∣∣R(\Delta {Y}^{j})∣∣<$ RESI_INTE_RELA. One also ensures, when the plasticity mechanism is active, that the plastic multiplier is strictly positive. If this is not the case, the local integration is restarted without taking the plasticity mechanism into account. Only the viscosity mechanism can then be considered.

#### 4.3.3 Update phase

The update of the solution vector is carried out according to the following operation:

$\Delta Y=\Delta {Y}^{j+1}=\Delta {Y}^{j}+\delta \Delta {Y}^{j+1}$

This update phase consists in reporting the evolution of the stresses, plastic strains, visco-plastic strains and plastic and visco-plastic hardening parameters.

#### 4.3.4 Rate tangent operator

The rate tangent operator has already been presented within the framework of the explicit integration scheme. This stiffness operator is used during the predictions at the global scale of Newton–Raphson with the tangent matrix ( PREDICTION='TANGENTE' ). #### 4.3.5 Consistent tangent operator

On the basis of the analytical developments specified in the document [R5.03.12](doc:r5.03.12), it is possible to determine the tangent operator ${M}_{c}=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }$ from the terms of the Jacobian matrix defined above, §  4.3.2 ( $J=\frac{dR}{dY}$ ).

Indeed, the system $\Phi (\Delta Y)=0$ is satisfied at the end of the increment and, for a small variation of $\Phi$ , considering this time $\epsilon$ as a variable, the system remains at equilibrium and one therefore verifies $d\Phi =0$ .

By differentiation, one obtains:

$\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \epsilon }d(\Delta \epsilon )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \sigma }d(\Delta \sigma )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \lambda }d(\Delta \lambda )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta {\xi}_{p}}d(\Delta {\xi}_{p})+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}}d(\Delta {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}})=0$

One rewrites the system by putting the terms in $\epsilon$ on the right-hand side:

$\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \epsilon }d(\Delta \epsilon )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \sigma }d(\Delta \sigma )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta \lambda }d(\Delta \lambda )+\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta {\xi}_{p}}d(\Delta {\xi}_{p})=-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \Delta {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}}}d(\Delta {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}})$

This system can then be written in the following form:

$J\cdot d(\Delta Y)=-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial (\Delta \epsilon )}d(\Delta \epsilon )$ with $\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial (\Delta \epsilon )}=\left\lbrace -{C}^{e}(\sigma ),0,0,0\right\rbrace$

Finally, one obtains: $J\cdot d(\Delta Y)=\left\lbrace {C}^{e}(\sigma ):\Delta \epsilon ,0,0,0\right\rbrace$

One then writes the system by blocks, separating $d(\Delta \sigma )$ from the other variables $Z=(\Delta \lambda ,\Delta {\xi}_{p},\Delta {\xi}_{\mathit{vp}})$ , which gives:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{\sigma \sigma }& {J}_{\sigma Z}\\ {J}_{Z\sigma }& {J}_{\mathit{ZZ}}\end{array}\right]\cdot (\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ Z\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}{C}^{e}(\sigma )d(\Delta \epsilon )\\ 0\end{array})$

The expression of the tangent operator becomes:

${M}_{c}=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }=\frac{d(\Delta \sigma )}{d(\Delta \epsilon )}={\left[{J}_{\sigma \sigma }-{J}_{\sigma Z}{({J}_{\mathit{ZZ}})}^{-1}{J}_{Z\sigma }\right]}^{-1}{C}^{e}(\sigma )$

**Note**  : The Jacobian matrix not being symmetric, the tangent operator ${M}_{c}$ is not either.
