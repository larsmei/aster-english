# r4.05.08 Method for taking into account the Floor–Equipment interaction

**Summary:**

In seismic studies, one is often led to produce floor spectra for buildings in which the equipment is modelled by masses added to the floor. These additional masses affect the floor spectra. The method proposed here makes it possible to modify the floor spectra by taking into account the interaction of the masses and of the floor.

## 1 Introduction of the IPM method

The problem of taking into account the dynamic coupling between the primary and secondary structures in the preparation of the vertical floor spectra is an important point of the dynamic study of the structure.

On certain floor strips, equipment is sometimes installed whose total mass relative to that of the floor strip may reach a few percent, or even a few tens of percent. Now, the interaction between this equipment and the floor strip will in general tend to attenuate the amplifications observed, especially when there is coincidence between the frequency of the floor and the natural frequencies of the equipment installed on it.

The investigations carried out in this respect have shown that the equipment/structure mass ratios were liable, in certain cases, to generate a dynamic coupling, described as Floor–Equipment Interaction, and having as its principal effect, when the natural frequencies of the equipment are close to those of the support, that of modifying the dynamic behaviour of the floor relative to the "rigid" case.

This document forms part of an intention to produce realistic floor spectra taking into account the effects of dynamic coupling, as prescribed by the ASN guides ([1]).

### 1.1 Method

The equipment–floor interaction rests on the principle that a heavy item of equipment placed on a floor modifies the behaviour of the floor. Simplifying assumptions are adopted for the modelling of the floor and of the equipment. Both will be represented by 1D oscillators in the vertical direction.

The procedure described in this note leads to the construction of floor spectra corrected by the IPM. These floor spectra are obtained from the raw vertical accelerograms (that is to say, not taking the IPM into account) coming from a prior transient seismic analysis.

### 1.2 Taking account of the equipment in the modelling

Before any modelling of a building, thought must be given to the taking into account of the equipment in the mass balance.

Three rules may be set out concerning the taking into account of the equipment (or secondary structures) in the modelling [1]:

- Small items of equipment (a few hundred kilograms) are not taken into account. They are in fact covered by the conventional live load on the floors;
- If the mass of the equipment is small (see Figure 1) relative to that of its support, its dynamic behaviour has little influence on the dynamic behaviour of the supporting structure. One is content to represent this equipment by added masses in the building model;
- For elements of significant mass having a sufficiently flexible support, the question then arises of the dynamic coupling between the structure and the equipment attached to it.

To summarise, when an item of equipment is assumed to be uncoupled from the floor, its mass is simply taken into account in the floor model as a distributed or localised added mass. On the other hand, when an item of equipment is assumed to be coupled, it is necessary to take the equipment into account in the global model in order to characterise the coupling phenomena. A modal analysis of the equipment placed on the floor panel must be carried out; the principal modes must be extracted from it. The items of equipment are then represented in the global model as simple oscillators .

However, it sometimes happens that certain items of equipment have been taken into account as an added mass without any stiffness being attached to them, because their relatively low mass, taken individually, did not justify it, or when the dynamic coupling of an item of equipment had been judged not significant by the modellers .

The purpose of this method is to correct this shortcoming in the upstream modelling by an a posteriori taking into account of the dynamic coupling between the primary structures and the equipment. The equipment concerned may consist of a sum of small items of equipment whose total mass and modal characteristics satisfy the criterion set out in Figure 1 (for example: a row of electrical cabinets), or of a single item of equipment satisfying this same criterion in mass and in frequencies

## 2 Development of the method

### 2.1 Principles of the method

The method proposed rests on the simple assumption that the response of the floor without Floor–Equipment Interaction (IPM) may be likened to the response of a 1D oscillator of frequency ${f}_{1}$ , of mass ${m}_{1}+{m}_{2}$ and of damping ${\xi}_{1}$ (see Figure 2), where we have defined ${m}_{1}$ as being the mass of the floor and ${m}_{2}$ the mass of the equipment.

It is the bending mode of the floor panels which is taken into account by the one-dimensional modelling of the floor. We furthermore consider that the modal mass mobilised by the bending mode is the total mass of the floor, which is a conservative assumption. The response of the floor without IPM amounts to considering that the equipment of mass ${m}_{2}$ is rigidly linked to the floor.

The equipment, for its part, is characterised by a set of 1D oscillators of variable frequencies and masses. We indeed assume that the heavy equipment placed on the floor possesses several natural frequencies in the vertical direction. The modal characteristics of the equipment (frequencies and participating masses) are to be determined by the user upstream of the analysis. These data may be found in the qualification notes or the design notes. A finite-element analysis may also supply this type of information. Each of the mechanical characteristics of the oscillators representing the equipment will bear the index $i$ in the remainder of this document.

From the mass ${m}_{2}$ of the equipment and the mass ${m}_{1}$ of the floor, one defines the mass ratio $\lambda ={m}_{2}/{m}_{1}$ .

The method used to obtain the corrected floor spectra rests on the following principle:

- The accelerogram coming from the response analyses of the building and of the floors characterises the motion of the mass ${m}_{1}+{m}_{2}$ in model A (see Figure 2);
- The corrected accelerogram taking the IPM into account is computed by considering that the equipment is characterised by a series of simple oscillators;
- From the corrected accelerogram, corrected floor spectra are computed for different values of the percentage of critical damping.

For the computation of the corrected accelerogram, we employ a frequency-domain method based on the use of transfer functions. This method therefore assumes the computation of the Fourier transform of the initial accelerogram (or raw accelerogram).

An inverse Fourier transform computation makes it possible to find the corrected accelerogram after having multiplied the FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) of the initial accelerogram by the transfer function between the motion of the mass ${m}_{1}$ in model A and in model B.

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.08/10000200000002F3000001181BEC62F76695410C.png)

Figure 2: Descriptions of the models

We define the following notations:

- ${\omega}_{1}=\sqrt{\frac{{k}_{1}}{{m}_{1}+{m}_{2}}}$
- ${f}_{1}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{{k}_{1}}{{m}_{1}+{m}_{2}}}$
- ${C}_{1}$ denotes the damping coefficient
- ${\xi}_{1}=\frac{{C}_{1}}{2({m}_{1}+{m}_{2}){\omega}_{1}}$
- ${f}_{1}^{\ast }={f}_{1}\sqrt{1+\lambda }$
- ${\xi}_{1}^{\mathrm{\ast }}={\xi}_{1}\sqrt{1+\lambda }$
- $\lambda =\frac{{m}_{2}}{{m}_{1}}$
- ${f}_{i}\in 0,40\mathit{Hz}$
- ${m}_{i}={\alpha}_{i}{m}_{2}$
- ${\Sigma}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}=1$
- ${\lambda}_{i}={\alpha}_{i}\lambda$

In these notations, we have denoted by ${\alpha}_{i}$ the unit effective masses associated with each mode of the heavy equipment and by ${\lambda}_{i}$ the mass ratio associated with each of these modes. We shall denote by $N$ the total number of oscillators constituting the equipment placed on the floor.

In model A, the oscillator has a mass ${m}_{2}+{m}_{1}$ because we consider that the raw accelerogram, computed during a prior transient analysis, takes the added masses into account. This therefore assumes that the finite-element model of the floor incorporates the added masses of the heavy equipment.

The analyses necessary for the modification of the floor spectra proceed in the following manner:

- Identification of the parameters (frequency ${f}_{1}$ and damping ${\xi}_{1}$ of the support; dampings ${\xi}_{i}$ of the heavy equipment, mass ratio $\lambda$ , unit effective masses ${\alpha}_{i}$ and frequencies ${\lambda}_{i}$ );
- Reading of the accelerogram at the point of the floor under study $\ddot{x}(t)$ ;
- Fourier transform of the accelerogram, $\stackrel{ˆ}{\ddot{x}}(\omega )$ ;
- Computation of the transfer function between the motion of the mass ${m}_{1}$ in model A and in model B (see Figure 2), $H(\omega )$ ;
- Product $\stackrel{ˆ}{\ddot{x}}(\omega )H(\omega )$ ;
- Inverse Fourier transform, $\ddot{y}(t)$ ;
- Computation of the spectrum associated with $\ddot{y}(t)$ at the desired damping $\xi$ .

We obtain, after the analyses, a series of spectra at the desired damping values. These spectra constitute the corrected floor spectra to be included in the collections of spectra. The analysis may be carried out for several points of the floor.

### 2.2 Computation of the transfer functions

In this section, we propose to develop the equations leading to the correction of the floor spectra by the IPM.

The transfer function between the points P of models A and B, see Figure 2 and Figure 3, is equal to the transfer function between the base and point P in model B divided by the transfer function between the base and point P in model A.

#### 2.2.1 Transfer function for model B

Below, the equations of motion for an excitation ${\ddot{x}}_{b}$ at the base of model B:

$$
{m}_{i}{\ddot{x}}_{i}+{C}_{i}({\dot{x}}_{i}-{\dot{x}}_{1})+{k}_{i}({x}_{i}-{x}_{1})=-{m}_{i}{\ddot{x}}_{b}
$$

And:

$$
{m}_{1}{\ddot{x}}_{1}+{C}_{1}{\dot{x}}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}{C}_{i}({\dot{x}}_{1}-{\dot{x}}_{i})+{k}_{1}{x}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}{k}_{i}({x}_{1}-{x}_{i})=-{m}_{1}{\ddot{x}}_{b}
$$

For a harmonic loading of angular frequency $p$ of amplitude ${\widehat{x}}_{b}$ , the equations are written:

$$
-{m}_{i}{p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{i}+{\mathit{ipC}}_{i}({\widehat{x}}_{i}-{\widehat{x}}_{1})+{k}_{i}({\widehat{x}}_{i}-{\widehat{x}}_{1})={m}_{i}{p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

And:

$$
-{m}_{1}{p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{1}+{\mathit{ipC}}_{1}{\widehat{x}}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}\mathit{ip}{C}_{i}({\widehat{x}}_{1}-{\widehat{x}}_{i})+{k}_{1}{x}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}{k}_{i}({\widehat{x}}_{1}-{\widehat{x}}_{i})={m}_{1}{p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

Which may be rewritten:

$$
(-{p}^{2}+2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{i}{\xi}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}){\widehat{x}}_{i}-2(2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}){x}_{1}={p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

And:

$$
\left(-{p}^{2}+2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{1}{\xi}_{1}+{\omega}_{1}^{2}\right){\widehat{x}}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}\left({\lambda}_{i}{\omega}_{i}{\xi}_{i}+{\lambda}_{i}{\omega}_{i}^{2}\right){\widehat{x}}_{1}-\lambda \left(2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{i}{\xi}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}\right){\widehat{x}}_{i}={p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

From the equation , we obtain:

$$
\frac{\widehat{{x}_{i}}}{{\widehat{x}}_{b}}=\frac{\left(2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}\right)\frac{{\widehat{x}}_{1}}{{\widehat{x}}_{b}}+{p}^{2}}{-{p}^{2}+2\mathit{ip}{\omega}_{i}{\xi}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}}
$$

One defines the following notations:

$$
{\Delta}_{i}=-{p}^{2}+\mathrm{2ip}{\omega}_{i}{\xi}_{i}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}
{\overline{x}}_{j}=\frac{{\widehat{x}}_{j}}{{\widehat{x}}_{b}}
\overline{{x}_{i}}=\frac{({\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2})}{{\Delta}_{i}}{x}_{1}+\frac{{p}^{2}}{{\Delta}_{i}}
$$

Using these notations in the equation , one obtains:

$$
{\Delta}_{1}{\overline{x}}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}[{\lambda}_{i}({\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2})]{\overline{x}}_{1}-{\lambda}_{i}[{\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2}]{\overline{x}}_{i}={p}^{2}
$$

By defining the absolute displacement by ${X}_{i}={\widehat{x}}_{1}+{\widehat{x}}_{b}$ , the transfer function at point 2 is equal to:

$$
{T}_{B}(p)=\frac{{X}_{1}}{{\widehat{x}}_{b}}=1+{\overline{x}}_{1}=1+{p}^{2}\left(\frac{1+\sum_{i=2,N}{\lambda}_{i}({\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2})}{{\Delta}_{1}+\sum_{i=2,N}{\lambda}_{i}({\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2})-{\lambda}_{i}\frac{{({\Delta}_{i}+{p}^{2})}^{2}}{{\Delta}_{i}}}\right)
$$

#### 2.2.2 Transfer function in model A

With the same notations, concerning only the simple oscillator representing the support in model A:

$$
({m}_{1}+{m}_{2}){\ddot{x}}_{1}+{C}_{1}{\dot{x}}_{1}+{k}_{1}{x}_{1}=-({m}_{1}+{m}_{2}){\ddot{x}}_{b}
$$

And:

$$
-({m}_{1}+{m}_{2}){p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{1}+{\mathit{ipC}}_{1}{\widehat{x}}_{1}+{k}_{1}{\widehat{x}}_{1}=({m}_{1}+{m}_{2}){p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

For a harmonic loading of angular frequency $p$ of amplitude ${\widehat{x}}_{b}$ , the equations are written:

$$
(-{p}^{2}+2i\omega {\xi}_{1p}+{\omega}_{1}){\widehat{x}}_{1}={p}^{2}{\widehat{x}}_{b}
$$

By defining the absolute displacement by ${X}_{1}={\widehat{x}}_{1}+{\widehat{x}}_{b}$ , the transfer function at point 2 is equal to:

$$
{T}_{A}(p)=\frac{{X}_{1}}{{\widehat{x}}_{b}}=1+{\overline{x}}_{1}=1+\frac{{p}^{2}}{{\Delta}_{1}}
$$

#### 2.2.3 Total transfer function

The total transfer function $H(\omega )$ is then computed by taking the simple ratio of ${T}_{A}$ and ${T}_{B}$ :

$$
H(\omega )=\frac{{T}_{B}(\omega )}{{T}_{A}(\omega )}
$$

One indeed checks that when $\lambda =0$ or ${f}_{i}\to \infty ` , for :math:`i\in [2;N]$ , then $H(\omega )=1$ ; there is therefore, in these two cases, no effect on the raw input spectrum.

#### 2.2.4 Initial conditions

The transformation of the acceleration of node P between model A and B assumes that the initial conditions are identical between these two models.

One checks that these conditions are zero by comparing the initial value with the maximum value of the signal:

| :math:frac{left | {ddot{x}}_{A}(0)right | }{max(left | {ddot{x}}_{A}(t)right | )}<mathit{tol}` |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

If this condition is not satisfied and the correction option is chosen, one modifies the initial value of the signal of model A to 0.
