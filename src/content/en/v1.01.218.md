# v1.01.218 ZZZZ218 – Validation of the MACR_ECREVISSE command

**Summary:**

This test case enables the validation of Code_Aster's chaining with Ecrevisse, done via MACR_ECREVISSE macro-command, to estimate fluid flows (air/water/vapor) that can pass through a crack.

Three modellings are proposed, the first and second of which are non-regression. In the first model, a horizontal crack is found in a structure; in the second one has two vertical cracks that interact; in the third one tests the macro-command in relation to a rotation-translation.

## 1 Reference problem

This test is based on the SIMIBE test performed at CEA.

Three different modellings are described. The material and flow characteristics are the same for the three modellings, and they are described in section §1. Geometry changes slightly for the three modellings. A geometry diagram for Modelling A is shown in §1.1; for the other modellings the differences will be described.

Crack Details:

- section: RECTANGLE
- flow direction: $X$ , positive
- solid roughness of the wall: $0,5{.10}^{-6}m$
- singular load loss coefficient at input: none (ZETA = 0)
- dimension of crack in normal direction at plane ($z$ ): $0.5m$ (LIST_VAL_BL = (0.5.0.05)
- Remanent opening fixed to $30\mu m$

Flow:

- Stabilizing pressure at inlet: ${10.10}^{5}\mathrm{Pa}$
- Stabilization pressure at outlet: ${10}^{5}\mathrm{Pa}$
- Inlet fluid condition: air alone (FLUIDE_ENTREE = 6)
- Inlet temperature: $140°C$

Model:

- SATURATION flow model
- friction calculated directly from the absolute roughness of the wall (FROTTEMENT = 1)
- heat transfer with a laminar monophase Sieder & Tate correlation and Mac Adams correlation in other regimes. In the case of gas/liquid mixture, calculation either in the gas or in the liquid or via Chen correlation as a function of gas mass titre and vacuum rate (TRANSFERT_CHAL = 1).

### 1.2 Material properties

The values chosen are representative of a concrete.

$E=35000\mathrm{MPa}$

$\nu =0.25$

$\alpha =10-5/°C$

$\lambda =2.3J/{m}^{2}s°C/m$

$\rho {C}_{p}=2500000J/{m}^{3}°C$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

The upper and lower parts are clamped.

It is assumed that the crack cannot be closed completely. Thus, the standing opening is fixed to $30\mu m$ to leave a minimum fluid flow not zero.

At l-extrados, the temperature is always ${T}_{\mathrm{ext}}=20°C$ and the pressure ${P}_{\mathrm{ext}}=1.105\mathrm{Pa}$.

On the internal and external sides, it is assumed that the exchanges with the ambient medium take place with the following exchange coefficients: ${h}_{int}=\mathrm{8W}/{m}^{2}$ and ${h}_{\mathit{ext}}=\mathrm{4W}/{m}^{2}$

Hot air is injected under pressure either ${T}_{int}=140°C$ and ${P}_{int}={10.10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$ .

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The concrete is at rest, at $20°C$, with a $140\mu m$ opening crack.

## 2 reference solution

Modellings A and B are non-regression tests.

Model C checks the macro-command behavior for an oblique crack, and the macro is robust compared to a rotation-translation. The structure's response to a reference position is compared with that of the same tow and translatate structure.

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the use of MACR_ECREVISSE macro-command is validated in relation to rotation and translation, so this is not a non-regression test.

The results of *Ecrevisse* depend only on the characteristics of the crack, including the direction of the crack. This is influenced by gravity. Thus, cracks with the same characteristics and having a symmetrical direction in relation to the vertical must give the same flows.

The mesh is also the same (material, crack flow) as in Modelling A (§ 1.1). The mesh is also the same, except that it is turned and translated. The mesh details are given in §5.2.

### 5.2 mesh et boundary conditions.

The two meshes used were obtained by rotation and translation of the mesh of model A, so they retain the same dimensions. They are presented in the and .

For the first mesh, the first curvilinear abscissors of the crack is the point of origin of the $(0;0)$ axes.

The second mesh results from a 120 degree rotation of the first mesh around the origin and a translation of the $(-10;20)$ vector.

The boundary conditions for both meshes are similar to model A:

1. The ratings named FIXE1 (or FIXE2) are clamped ($\mathit{DX}=0$ , $\mathit{DY}=0$ ).
2. A contact condition is defined between the lip BFISH1-BFISB1 (BFISH2-BFISB2) (DEFI_CONTACT).
3. The pressure and temperature on the INTRA1 (INTRA2) intrados are greater than on the EXTRA1 (EXTRA2) extrados: ($1.E6\mathrm{Pa}$ and $140°C$ versus ${P}_{\mathrm{atm}}$ and $20°C$ ). This causes the flow of the intrados to the extrados.
4. The material is considered, at the beginning of the calculation, at room temperature $20°C$ , which is the reference temperature.
