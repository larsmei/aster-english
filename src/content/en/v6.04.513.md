# v6.04.513 SSNV513 – Block cut by three interfaces sequentially plugging with X-FEM

**Summary:**

This test validates the approach to join with $\text{X-FEM}$. This is an test case where three cracks are introduced. The first crack completely breaks the domain. The second plugs in on the first via the JONCTION key word of the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator. The third plugs in on the second. The approach is tested with and without contact.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a healthy square in which three interfaces are inserted, in red in Figure 1.1-a. The first one is oblique and completely cuts the domain. The second one is vertical. The third one is horizontal. It connects to the second one. The dimensions of the structure and the position of the interfaces are given in Figure 1.1-a and are expressed in meters, $\text{[m]}$.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_1.png)

Figure 1.1-a: Géométrie de la structure et positionnement des interfaces.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material has an isotropic elastic behaviour defined by the following material parameters:

Young's modulus : $100\text{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : $0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In the case of contactless (modellings A to D), conditions are applied on the left and right edges of the structure, so that each of the 4 zones has a different displacement from the others according to $\text{X}$ . This load is shown in figure 1.3-a. The displacements in $\text{Y}$ (and in $\text{Z}$ for modellings 3D) are blocked on the same edges.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_2.png)

Figure 1.3-a : Illustration des conditions aux limites et des chargements, cas sans contact.

In the case of contact (modellings E to H), roll conditions are imposed on the left and lower edges and a homogeneous pressure is applied on the right and upper edges of $\text{1 MPa}$. This load is shown in Figure 1.3-b. Each block is then compressed uniformly according to $X$ and $Y$.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_33.png)

Figure 1.3-b : Illustration des conditions aux limites et des chargements, cas avec contact.

## 2 reference solution

Without contact, each area shall undergo a rigid body movement corresponding to the limit condition imposed on its edge (right or left).

With contact, the 4 blocks undergo a uniform compression following $\text{X}$ and $\text{Y}$. The structure can be expressed as follows:

> ${\mathrm{Depl}}_{X}(X)=-(5+X)\frac{\mathrm{Press}}{E}$                             éq 2.1-1

> ${\mathrm{Depl}}_{Y}(Y)=-(5+Y)\frac{\mathrm{Press}}{E}$                                 éq 2.1-2

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a $\text{X-FEM}$ model, in flat deformations (D_PLAN). Interfaces are defined by level functions (normal level sets rated $\text{LN}$).

The level function equations for slant, horizontal and vertical interfaces are as follows:

$\text{LN}1=Y-1-\frac{X}{2}$                                     éq 3.1-1

$\text{LN}2=Y$                                             éq 3.1-2

$\text{LN}3=X$                                         éq 3.1-3

The oblique interface is defined in a classic way using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator with the normal $\text{LN1}$ level set. To define the vertical interface, the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator is called with the normal $\text{LN2}$ level set, defining a connection with the oblique crack via the JONCTION key word, and choosing an point "below" the oblique interface in the POINT operand. This step allows you to define the crack 2 in the domain below the first (see Figure 3.1-a in the centre).

To define the horizontal interface, the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator is called with the normal $\text{LN3}$ level set , defining a connection with the vertical crack via the JONCTION keyword, and choosing a point "right" in the POINT operand. This step allows you to define the 3 crack in the domain to the right of the second crack (see Figure 3.1-a to the right).

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_8.png)

Figure 3.1-a : Étapes de construction de l'intersection.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh with 25 mesh cells of QUAD4 type is shown in Figure 3.2-a.

This figure shows that the central mesh cell is cut by the three interfaces. This test allows you to validate the multiple cutting. The nodes of this mesh cell is enriched 3 times: they therefore have the degrees of freedom DX, DY, H1X, H1Y, H2X, H2Y, H3X and H3Y.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_9.png)

Figure 3.2-a: Le maillage de la modélisation A.

The advantage of this test is to verify that the addition of 3 discontinuity functions to the standard form function for each node of the central mesh cell allows the displacement kinematics of the 4 domains generated by the two connections (see Figure 3.2-b).

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.513/Object_12.png)

Figure 3.2-b : nœuds multi-heaviside et domaines de discontinuité représentés.

### 3.3 Tested Features

The DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator is tested using the JONCTION key word, which allows the definition of crack connections in $\text{X-FEM}$. The MODI_MODELE_XFEM operator is also tested for mesh cells which is cut by several cracks. The multi-Heaviside and multi-stocking of the data structures ($\text{SD}$ ) $\text{X-FEM}$ is of course activated. The assignment of Heaviside functions is verified in the elements sub-integration of the horizontal crack support (in blue in Figure 3.2-b).

The assembly of Heaviside ddegrades of freedom at the matrix and second member level of elements connected to the intersection for COMPORTEMENT option in STAT_NON_LINE is tested.

The post-processing $\text{X-FEM}$ is also valid for multi-cutting, with POST_MAIL_XFEM and POST_CHAM_XFEM operators.

The lip displacements of cracks are tested after post-processings operations relative to $\text{X-FEM}$ (POST_MAIL_XFEM and POST_CHAM_XFEM). ## 11 Summary of the results

It is possible to represent the junction of a crack for a crack already from a junction. Both junctions can be close and it is not necessary to refine the mesh in the area that contains the 2 junctions.

The approach was validated in $\mathrm{2D}$ for modellings C_PLAN and D_PLAN and elements type QUAD4 and TRIA3. The $\mathrm{3D}$ approach was also validated for elements HEXA8 and TETRA4 with and without contact.
