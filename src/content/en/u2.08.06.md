# u2.08.06 User's guide to parallelism

**Summary:**

Any *Code_Aster* simulation can benefit from the **performance gains that parallelism provides** (HPC 1
in English). These gains can be of two kinds: on the analysis time and on the RAM/disk space required (per core allocated). Well understood and well used, the impact of HPC on studies can be major in terms of:

- **Feasibility:** unblocking a critical situation (model too fine, simulation too slow…);
- **Productivity:** its accelerations of up to X10 or even X100 [2]_ contribute to smoothing and to securing the conduct of the simulations;
- **Predictivity:** allowing finer models, more complex and better validated simulations (recalibration, sensitivity, parametric…);
- **Forward-looking studies:** allowing oneself completely different studies.

*Code_Aster* proposes different parallel strategies in order to adapt to the study and to the computing platform. Some are oriented rather towards computer-science aspects (distribution of complete analyses or of independent modal/MISS3D analyses, construction of linear systems, basic linear-algebra operations), others are more algorithmic (HPC linear solvers MUMPS and PETSc).

This document briefly describes the organisation of parallelism in the code. It then recalls a few fundamentals in order to help the user take advantage of these parallel strategies. One then details their implementations, their scopes of use and their potential gains. The chainings/combinations of different strategies (often natural and parameterised by default) are also addressed.

The user in a hurry can go straight to chapter 2 (“Parallelism in a few clicks!”). It summarises the operating procedure for implementing the parallel strategy recommended by default.

**Note:**

To use Code_Aster in parallel, (at least) three cases may arise:

- *One has access to the centralised Aster machine and one wishes to use the Astk access interface,*
- *One carries out analyses on a cluster or on a multi-core machine with Astk,*
- *The same as the previous case but without Astk.*

‘High Performance Computing’.

## 1 Parallelism in a few clicks!

### 1.1 Why?

Often a *Code_Aster* simulation can **benefit from significant performance gains** by distributing its analyses over several cores of a PC or over one or several nodes of a centralised machine.

One can **gain in time** (with MPI parallelism and with OpenMP parallelism) as well as in **memory** (only *via* MPI). These gains vary according to the functionalities called upon, their parameter settings, the data set and the software platform used: cf. figure 1.1.

### 1.2 How?

In *Code_Aster,* by default, the analysis is sequential. But one can activate **different parallelisation strategies**. These depend on the analysis step considered and on the parameter setting chosen. They can often be chained or combined. One can thus initiate parallel schemes comprising up to 3 nested levels of parallelism.

One has four major classes of parallelisable problems, the second being the most common.

1. Either the simulation can be organised into several independent **code_aster** **sub-analyses;** a single level of parallelism can be activated (MPI, cf. §4.1).

Or this is not the case but:

1. it remains **dominated by linear or non-linear analyses** (operators STAT/DYNA/THER_NON_LINE, MECA_STATIQUE…); two levels of parallelism can be activated (MPI and OpenMP, cf. §4.2/4.3/5 and figures 1.2a/b).
2. it remains **dominated by modal analyses** divisible into frequency sub-bands (INFO_MODE/CALC_MODES+'BANDE'); three levels of parallelism can be activated (MPI, MPI and threads, cf. §4.4 and 4.2/4.3/5 and figures 1.3a/b).
3. it remains **dominated by MISS3D analyses** (CALC_MISS), independent (option FICHIER_TEMPS) or not (other option) (cf. §4.5 and figures 1.4a/b). The first category admits up to two levels of parallelism (MPI and OpenMP), the second only one (OpenMP).

To have an **estimate of the time spent by an operator** and therefore of the predominant steps of an analysis, one can activate the keyword MESURE_TEMPS of the commands DEBUT/POURSUITE [U1.03.03] on a typical study (possibly shortened or stripped down).

In all cases, one will advise **dividing the largest analyses into different steps** in order to separate those which are purely **computational** [3]_
, from those concerning **displays**, **post-processings** and **manipulations of fields** [4]_
.

A code_aster simulation can moreover be **organised into several chained sub-analyses** (POURSUITE mode). Each of these sub-analyses can even belong to one of the previous categories. For example a simulation could comprise a first part dominated by a STAT_NON_LINE (case 2), followed by a restart centred on CALC_MODES (case 3), itself chained to a MISS3D analysis (case 4).

Each of **these “restarts” could be launched with a different parallel parameter setting** in order to take the best advantage of the machine resources available in order to accelerate them. Or, on the contrary, one will be able to choose a “median” parallel parameter setting, which will remain constant for each part of the study and which will produce interesting but reduced performances.

*In the first case*, one will refer to the dedicated use of the Astk tool (cf. §4.1 and [U2.08.07](doc:u2.08.07)).

*In the second case*, one must begin by locating the solutions of linear systems in the command file (keyword SOLVEUR) and modify their parameter settings in order to use an HPC linear solver [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01). To do this, one specifies the value 'MUMPS' or 'PETSC' for the keyword METHODE.

One then distributes, over the MPI processes, the steps of construction and those of solution of linear systems (cf. §4.2/5.2/5.3). This makes it possible to lower the level of memory required and to accelerate the simulation (cf. figures 1.2a/b).

One can add a second level of parallelism by using, for each MPI process, several OpenMP threads (cf. §4.3). This second level, however, accelerates only part of the solution of the linear systems and it does not make it possible to lower the peak in RAM.

### 1.3 In practice via Astk

**For the effective implementation of cases no. 2, 3 and no. 4**, one must pre-select a parallel version of *Code_Aster* (denoted ***_mpi), then specify the number of cores retained (Options menu of Astk) *via* the following fields:

- (optional) **ncpus=k**, number of threads allocated in OpenMP per MPI process; generally used in addition to MPI; value parameterised by default if one does not fill in the field and it remains empty.
- **mpi_nbcpu=m**, number of MPI processes allocated in total (sum over all the nodes).
- **mpi_nbnœud=p**, number of nodes over which these *m* x *k* parallel tasks will be distributed.

One advises in general **not to allocate all the cores of a node in MPI alone**. This can have the effect of **slowing down the simulation** because, even if part of the analyses is accelerated as a result of its distribution over more cores, since these share certain memory resources (caches, bus), the accesses to the data are, for their part, slowed down.

To use more efficiently and at 100% all the resources allocated, one rather advises **mixing and balancing** **the MPI and OpenMP parallelisms** (hybrid parallelism on 2 levels).

**To remain efficient**, one must of course take care **not to exceed the physical capacities of the platforms**:

- no more OpenMP threads (ncpus) than cores sharing a physical memory (24 on Aster5 and 28 on EOLE);
- no more MPI processes (mpi_nbcpu), possibly multiplied by the previous number of threads, than physical cores available: per node (24 on Aster5 and 28 on EOLE) and in total (size of the machine);
- respect the constraints of the batch manager and, in particular, the maximum resources allocatable by a single user.

For example, a model comprising* 0.6M *degrees of freedom will very probably be able to benefit from an efficient parallelism by allocating 12 MPI x 5* OpenMP *threads = 60 cores.*

For example, for a modal analysis comprising 8 sub-bands, one can set* mpi_nbcpu=32 *. Each of the sub-bands will then use* MUMPS *on 4 MPI processes. And on each of these independent solutions one can request the previous parallel scheme. That is, potentially, three nested levels of parallelism.*

- a factor of X2 the RAM peak (per MPI process, cf. field 'Total memory' of Astk, figure 1.6),

- a factor of X2 in execution time.

## 2 General points

**Any simulation** **Code_Aster can benefit from the performance gains that parallelism provides.** Provided that it carries out elementary computations/assemblies, solutions of linear systems, large modal analyses or independent/similar simulations. The gains can be of two kinds: on the analysis time and on the RAM/disk space required (per MPI process).

Like most general-purpose codes in structural mechanics, *Code_Aster* proposes different strategies in order to adapt to the study and to the computing platform. Once parameterised, most of them chain together and couple automatically. The following paragraphs give an overview of them.

### 2.1 Computer-science parallelisms

- **1a/Launching of batches of independent/similar analyses** (parametric analyses, tests...).

*Tool:* shell scripting.

*Gain*: in elapsed time.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk [U2.08.07].

*Chaining*: none.

*Combination*: possible with the other parallel schemes but only in advanced use (overloading of sources).

- **1b/Distribution of the elementary computations and of the assemblies**, matrix and vector, in the pre/post-processings and in the constructions of linear systems. Parallelism in distributed memory.

*Tool:* MPI language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time, or even in RAM peak with MUMPS+MATR_DISTRIBUEE.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Chaining*: useful with 2b or 2c; possible but of little use with 1c or 1d alone; possible but useless with 2a.

*Combination*: none.

- **1c/Distribution of the basic linear-algebra computations** (sub-steps of MUMPS, BLAS library) **.** Parallelism in shared memory activated only with the MUMPS package (cf. 2b) **.**

*Tool:* OpenMP language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time (but increase in the CPU time).

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (ncpus).

*Chaining*: possible but of little use with 1b alone.

*Combination*: counter-productive with 2a, useful with 2b, possible but of little use with 2c or 1d.

- **1d/Distribution of the modal analyses** (resp. of the modal calibrations) in the operator **CALC_MODES** (resp. **INFO_MODE**). Parallelism in distributed memory (without memory gain, acceleration only).

*Tool:* MPI language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time (but increase in the RAM peak, to be offset by the combination with 2b).

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Chaining*: possible but of little use with 1b alone.

*Combination*: useful with 2b (or even 2b/1c); possible but of little use with 2a; impossible with 2c.

- **1e/Distribution of the** **MISS3D** **analyses** in the operator **CALC_MISS**. Parallelism in distributed memory (if OPTION=FICHIER_TEMPS) and/or shared memory (without memory gain, acceleration only).

*Tool:* MPI and OpenMP languages.

*Gain*: in elapsed time.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (ncpus, mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Chaining*: none.

*Combination*: with FICHIER_TEMPS two possible levels of parallelism, MPI x OpenMP.

- **1f/Other distributions of analyses:** certain steps of the frictional-contact algorithm, operator REST_SPEC_TEMP. Parallelism in distributed memory (without memory gain, acceleration only).

*Tool:* MPI language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Combination*: possible but of little use with 1b (OpenMP).

### 2.2 Numerical parallelisms

- **2a/Direct solver** **MULT_FRONT**; parallelism in shared memory.

*Tool:* OpenMP language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time (but increase in the CPU time).

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (ncpus).

*Chaining*: possible but of little use with 1b, 2b or 2c.

*Combination*: counter-productive with 1c, possible but of little use with 1d.

- **2b/ MUMPS package** (either as a direct solver, *via* METHODE='MUMPS', or as a preconditioner of PETSC/GCPC *via* PRE_COND='LDLT_SP'). Parallelism in distributed memory.

*Tool:* MPI language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time and in RAM peak.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Chaining*: useful with 1b or 2c, possible but of little use with 2a.

*Combination*: useful with 1c or 1d.

- **2c/** **Iterative solver PETSC** (with possibly MUMPS as a preconditioner, cf. PRECOND='LDLT_SP'); parallelism in distributed memory.

*Tool:* MPI language.

*Gain*: in elapsed time and in RAM peak.

*Launching:* standard *via* Astk (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

*Chaining*: useful with 1b or 2b, possible but useless with 2a.

*Combination*: possible but of little use with 1c, out of scope with 1d.

The parallel schemes 1b+2b/1c or 1b+2b+2c are the most widely adopted. They support an “industrial” and “general-public” use. These general-purpose and reliable parallelisms provide notable gains in CPU and in RAM peak per core. Their parameterisation is simple, their implementation facilitated *via* Astk (cf. §1).

**For a standard use, the user no longer has to worry about the fine implementation of the parallelism**. By filling in the dedicated menus of Astk [5]_
, one fixes the number of cores required (for MPI and/or OpenMP) as well as the number of nodes over which they are distributed.

### 2.3 Parallelisation of linear systems

Once these portions of linear system have been constructed (parallel scheme 1b), two cases arise:

- either the **following treatment is naturally sequential** and therefore all the MPI processes must have access to the global information. To this end one gathers these pieces of linear systems and therefore the following step will be neither accelerated, nor will it see its memory consumptions fall. It is most often the end of an operator, a post-processing or a linear solver not parallelised in MPI (strategy 1b+2a).
- or the **following treatment accepts MPI parallelism**; it is then mainly the HPC linear solvers MUMPS (1b+2b), PETSC (1b+2c) or both at once (1b+2b+2c). The parallel flow of data constructed upstream is then transmitted to them (after a few adaptations). These linear-algebra packages then reorganise, internally, their own parallel schemes (with a more algebraic view). One then speaks of a parallel scheme of rather “numerical” order. This combination of “computer-science parallelism”, at the level of the assembly of the linear system, and of “numerical parallelism”, at the level of its solution, the two *via* MPI, is the most common combination.

**Notes:**

- *Let us note that at the end of the cycle “construction of the linear system – solution of it”, whatever the scenario implemented (sequential or MPI-parallel linear solver), the solution vector is then transmitted, in full, to all the MPI processes. The cycle can thus continue whatever the following configuration.*

In addition, one can **superimpose on, or substitute for, this MPI parallelism** (which works on all platforms) another level of parallelism managed this time by the **OpenMP language**. This is however limited to the fractions of the machine which physically share the same memory (multi-core PC or nodes of a computing server).

It does not make it possible to lower the memory consumptions but, on the other hand, it accelerates certain types of computation, and this with a finer granularity than that of MPI: it provides a better acceleration even if the flow of data/treatments is not very large. It is a parallel scheme of “computer-science” order which intervenes mainly in the basic operations of linear-algebra algorithms (*via*, for example, the BLAS library).

This parallelism can be:

![Figure](/figures/u2.08.06/100000000000045A00000226CB38132BE9FC7B16.png)

- either complementary to the MPI parallelism by accelerating the computations within each MPI process (in the linear-system solution part with MUMPS, strategy known as “2b/1c”).
- or a substitute for the MPI parallelism by accelerating the solution of the linear system with MULT_FRONT (strategy 2a).

### 2.4 Distribution of modal analyses

  - When the simulation cannot be decomposed into independent *Aster* analyses, but it remains nevertheless **dominated** **by generalised modal analyses** (operators INFO_MODE and CALC_MODES), one can organise a specific parallel scheme.

- **combination** with the MPI (or even OpenMP) parallelism in the solutions of linear systems (if MUMPS is used, strategy with 2 levels of parallelism, 1d/2b, or even three, 1d/2b/1c).

## 3 Some preliminary advice

One formulates here **some advice to help the user take advantage of the parallel computation strategies of the code**. But one must be well aware that, before anything else, one must first optimise and validate one's sequential analysis, taking into account the advice which abounds in the command documentations. To do this, one uses, if possible, a coarser mesh and/or one activates only a few time steps.

**The default parameter setting and the displays/alarms of the code propose a balanced and instrumented mode of operation**. One lists below, and in a non-exhaustive manner, several questions which it is interesting to ask oneself when one seeks to parallelise one's analysis. Of course, certain questions (and answers) are cumulative and can therefore apply simultaneously.

### 3.1 Preamble

It is interesting to **validate**, beforehand, **one's parallel analysis** by comparing a few iterations in sequential mode and in parallel mode. This procedure also makes it possible to **calibrate the maximum gains attainable** (theoretical speed-ups) and therefore to avoid “wasting processors” too much. Thus, if one denotes by $f$ the parallel portion of the code (determined for example *via* a prior sequential run), then the maximum theoretical speed-up **S** p accessible on *p* processors is computed according to Amdahl's formula:

${S}_{p}=\frac{1}{1-f+\frac{f}{p}}$

For example, if one uses the distributed MUMPS parallelism by default (scenario 1b+2b) and the steps of construction/solution of the linear system represent 90% of the sequential time (*f* =0.90), the theoretical speed-up is bounded by the value ${\mathrm{S}}_{\infty}=\frac{1}{1-0.9+0.9/\infty }=10$ ! And this, whatever the number of MPI processes allocated.

It is interesting **to evaluate the main items of consumption (time/RAM):** in quasi-static mechanics, these are generally the steps of **elementary computations/assemblies**, of **solution of the linear system** and the **frictional-contact** algorithms. But their proportions depend greatly on the case (characteristics of the contact, size of the problem, complexity of the material laws...). If the elementary computations are important, one must parallelise them *via* scenario 1b (default scenario). If, on the contrary, the linear systems concentrate the essential part of the costs, scenarios 2b or 2c may suffice. On the other hand, if it is the frictional contact which sizes the analysis, one must seek to optimise its parameter setting and/or parallelise its internal linear solver (cf. GCP+MUMPS method).

In dynamics, if one carries out an analysis by projection onto a modal basis, it may be this last step which proves the most costly. To gain time, one can then use the operator CALC_MODES with the option 'BANDE' cut into several sub-bands, in sequential mode and, above all, in parallel (scenario 1d). This operator exhibits a distribution of quasi-independent tasks which leads to good speed-ups.

To optimise one's parallel analysis, one must watch for possible **load imbalances** of the data flow (CPU and memory) and limit the extra costs due **to memory off-loading** (JEVEUX and MUMPS OOC) and to the **archiving of fields**. On this subject, one may consult the documentation [U1.03.03](doc:u1.03.03) “Performance indicator of an analysis (time/memory)”. It indicates the procedure to follow in order to establish the right diagnoses and it proposes solutions.

For CALC_MODES with the option 'BANDE' cut into several sub-bands, **respecting a good load balance is crucial for the efficiency of the parallel analysis**: all the sub-bands must comprise a similar number of modes. One therefore advises proceeding in three steps:

- Prior calibration of the spectral zone by calls to INFO_MODE (if possible in parallel),
- Examination of the results,
- Launching in POURSUITE mode of the CALC_MODES analysis, with the option 'BANDE' cut into several parallelised sub-bands.

For more details one may consult the user's documentation of the operator [U4.52.02].

### 3.2 Some empirical figures

One advises allocating at least 30 to 50.103 degrees of freedom per MPI process (scenarios 1b, 2b or 2c). This granularity can go down to a few thousand degrees of freedom per OpenMP thread while remaining efficient (scenarios 1c or 2a).

A standard thermo-mechanical analysis generally benefits, on 32 processors, from a gain of the order of ten in elapsed time and from a factor of 4 in RAM peak (per core).

For CALC_MODES, one advises decomposing one's analysis into sub-bands of a few tens of modes (for example 60) and, then, providing for 2, 4 or even 8 MUMPS processes per sub-band. One must of course make do with the number of processors available and the memory peak required by the problem [8]_
.

One can obtain gains of a factor of 30, in elapsed time, on about a hundred processors. The gains in memory are more modest (a few tens of percent).

The modal calibration step *via* INFO_MODE costs practically nothing in parallel: a few minutes, at most, for problems of the order of a million unknowns, parallelised over about a hundred processors. The gains in time are of a factor of X70 on about a hundred processors and up to x2 in RAM peak.

### 3.3 Independent analyses

When the simulation that one wishes to carry out decomposes naturally (parametric study, sensitivity analysis...) or artificially (particular thermo-mechanical chaining...), into similar but independent analyses, one can gain a great deal in analysis time thanks to the numerical parallelism 1a.

### 3.4 Gain in RAM

When the dimensioning memory factor concerns the solution of linear systems (which is often the case), the combination of the computer-science parallelisms 1b (elementary computations/assemblies) and the numerical parallelisms 2b (distributed linear solver MUMPS) is entirely indicated [9]_
(or even 1b+2b/1c).

Once one has distributed one's *Code_Aster*+MUMPS/PETSC analysis over enough processors, the RAM consumptions of JEVEUX can become predominant (compared with those of MUMPS, which one has spread over the processors). To re-establish the right hierarchy (the external solver must bear the peak RAM consumption!) one must activate, in addition, the option SOLVEUR/MATR_DISTRIBUEE [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01).

To solve frontier problems of very large sizes (> 5M degrees of freedom), one can also try the iterative solvers of PETSC (parallel strategy 2c or 2b+2c).

### 3.5 Gain in time

If the essential part of the costs concerns only the solutions of linear systems of small size (N<0.5M degrees of freedom) one can be content to use the linear solvers MUMPS in centralised mode (strategy 2b) or MULT_FRONT (2a). As soon as the construction of the systems becomes non-negligible (>5%) or these prove fairly large, it is essential to extend the parallel scope by activating the distribution of the elementary computations/assemblies (1b) and by switching to distributed MUMPS (default value).

On frontier problems of large sizes (N>3M degrees of freedom), once the right numerical parameters have been selected [10]_
(preconditioner, relaxation... cf. [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01)), the parallel iterative solvers (2c) can provide very appreciable gains in time compared with the generic direct solvers (2a/2b). Especially if the solutions are relaxed [11]_
because, subsequently, they are corrected by an encompassing process (Newton algorithm of THER/STAT_NON_LINE...).

For large modal problems (in problem size and/or in number of modes), one must of course think of using CALC_MODES with the option 'BANDE' cut into several sub-bands [12]_
.

Distributing the linear solver MUMPS over more processes makes it possible to reduce the memory peak. Other levers: Out-Of-Core operation and change of renumberer.

To lower the memory consumptions of MUMPS one can also act on other parameters: OOC, use as a preconditioner or relaxation of the solutions [U4.50.01].

There is, however, no universal rule; all the parameters must be adjusted case by case.

That is to say that one is going to be less demanding as regards the quality of the solution. This can take the form of a mediocre stopping criterion, the computation of a crude preconditioner and its sharing during several solutions of the linear system... The functionalities of the non-linear and linear solvers of Code_Aster make it easy to implement this type of scenario.

Balanced via prior modal calibrations carried out with INFO_MODE. If possible in parallel.

## 4 Computer-science parallelisms

### 4.1 Batches of independent analyses

#### 4.1.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk*.

**Scope of use:** independent analyses (parametric, sensitivity study...).

**Number of cores recommended:** limit of the machine/batch *manager*.

**Gain:** in CPU time.

**Speed-up:** proportional to the number of independent cases.

**Type of parallelism:** computer-science *via* shell scripts.

**Scenario:** 1a of §3. Combination with all the other parallelism strategies is licit but addressed to advanced users (outside the scope of *Astk*).

#### 4.1.2 Implementation

The tool **Astk makes it possible to carry out a whole series of similar but independent analyses** (in sequential mode and above all in MPI parallel). One can use an official version of the code or a private overload built beforehand. The command files setting out **the analyses are constructed dynamically from a “model” command file and from a “dictionary”-type mechanism:** different parameter sets for each study (keyword VALE of the .distr file), variable blocks of *Aster*/Python commands (PRE/POST_CALCUL)...

The launching of these parallel batches is carried out with the usual submission parameters of *Astk*. One can even reparameterise the hardware configuration of the analysis (list of the nodes, number of cores, total RAM per node...) *via* a classical .hostfile file.

For more information on the implementation of this parallelism, one may consult the documentations [U1.04.00](doc:u1.04.00)/[U2.08.07].

**Notes:**

- *Before launching such a batch of analyses, it is preferable to optimise beforehand, on a typical study, the various dimensioning parameters: memory management* JEVEUX *, non-linear/modal/linear solver aspects, keyword* ARCHIVAGE, frictional-contact algorithm... (cf. documentations [U1.03.03](doc:u1.03.03), [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01)...).
- *One can easily bring a machine down by launching too many analyses with respect to the resources available. It is advisable to proceed by stages and to enquire as to the possibilities of use of shared computing resources (batch class, large priority jobs...).*

### 4.2 Elementary computations and assemblies

#### 4.2.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk*.

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising costly elementary computations/assemblies (non-linear mechanics). Activated by default as soon as the number of MPI processes >1.

**Number of cores recommended:** alone, between 4 and 8. Chained with the distributed parallelism of MUMPS or of PETSC (default value), typically 16, 32 or even 64.

**Gain:** in time or even in memory with the linear solver MUMPS/PETSC (if MATR_DISTRIBUEE).

**Speed-up:** Gains variable according to the cases (parallel efficiency [13]_
>50%). One needs a fairly large granularity for this parallelism to remain efficient: 30 or 50.103 degrees of freedom per MPI process.

**Type of parallelism:** computer-science *via* the MPI language (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

**Scenario:** 1b of §2. Natively designed to chain to the numerical parallelisms 2b or 2c. Chaining possible with 1c, possible but of little use with 1d or 2a. No combination possible.

#### 4.2.2 Implementation

The implementation of this parallel scheme is carried out transparently for the user. *Via* Astk, it is initialised by default as soon as one has selected a parallel version of *Code_Aster* (denoted ***_mpi) as well as a number of MPI processes at least equal to 2.

Thus on the centralised *Aster* server, one must parameterise the following fields in the Options menu **:**

- **mpi_nbcpu=m**, number of MPI processes allocated.
- **mpi_nbnœud=p**, number of nodes over which these MPI processes will be distributed.

For example, on the centralised machine Aster5, the nodes are composed of 24 cores. To allocate 32 MPI processes at a rate of 8 processes per node, one must therefore set mpi_nbcpu to 32 and mpi_nbnœud to 4.

One advises, in general, **not to allocate all the cores of a node in MPI alone**. This can have the effect of **slowing down the simulation** because, even if part of the analyses is accelerated as a result of its distribution over more cores, since these share certain memory resources, the accesses to the data are, for their part, slowed down.

To use more efficiently and at 100% all the resources allocated, one rather advises **mixing MPI parallelism** and **OpenMP** (cf. scenarios 1b+2b/1c or 1b+2b/1c+2c).

Once this number of MPI processes has been fixed, one can launch one's analysis (in batch on the centralised machine) with the same parameter setting as in sequential mode. If this parallel scheme is chained with the numerical parallelism of MUMPS or that of PETSC (or both, cf. scenarios 2b and 2c), one can reduce one's RAM peak by activating the option MATR_DISTRIBUEE.

**As soon as several MPI processes are activated**, the assignment of the model in the *Aster* command file (operator AFFE_MODELE) **distributes its mesh cells among the processors**. *Code_Aster* being a finite-element code, this is a natural distribution of the data (and of the associated tasks). Subsequently, the *Aster* steps of elementary computations and of assemblies (matrix and vector) will be based on this distribution in order to “drain” the data/treatment flows local to each processor. Each processor will carry out only the analyses associated with the mesh-cell group for which it is responsible.

This **mesh-cell/processor distribution** comes in different forms and it is parameterisable in the operators AFFE_MODELE [U4.41.01]/MODI_MODELE [U4.41.02] *via* the values of the keyword DISTRIBUTION/METHODE=:

- 'CENTRALISE': **The mesh cells are not distributed** (as in sequential mode). Each processor knows all the mesh cells of the model. Parallelism 1b is therefore not implemented. This mode of use is useful for non-regression tests and for certain studies where parallelism 1b brings little or is even counter-productive (for example if one must gather the elementary data in order to feed a non-distributed linear system and the MPI communications required are too slow). In all the cases where the elementary computations represent a small part of the total time (for example in linear elasticity), this option may be sufficient.
- 'GROUP_ELEM' **/'** MAIL_DISPERSE' /'MAIL_CONTIGU' /'SOUS_DOMAINE' **(default)** /'SOUS_DOM.OLD': the mesh cells are distributed on the basis of different criteria: by type, by cyclic distribution, by packets of the same size or according to sub-domain strategies.

In the last two scenarios, the distribution is carried out *via* the partitioners METIS (default) or SCOTCH (cf. keyword PARTITIONNEUR). These must therefore be installed and linked to the version of *Code_Aster* used. This is obviously done by default on the centralised machine.

**Notes:**

- *Between each command, the user can even change the distribution of the mesh cells among the processors. It is sufficient to use the command* *MODI_MODELE. This mechanism remains licit when one chains different analyses (* *POURSUITE mode). The rule being of course that this new distribution must remain compatible with the parallel parameter setting of the analysis (number of nodes/processors...).*
- *Only MPI parallelism makes it possible to accelerate this step of the analysis. Now if one allocates too many MPI, the linear-system solution step risks being slowed down or being blocked for lack of memory. To remedy this problem, one can, with the linear solver MUMPS (cf. keyword* *SOLVEUR/REDUCTION_MPI), reduce the number of MPI processes used only* *in MUMPS.*

*The unoccupied cores are then entrusted to the other level of parallelism, OpenMP. The overall memory consumption* *of the analysis* *is thus reduced, and this without slowing down* *the solutions of the linear system.* *One thus greatly accelerates the elementary-computation/assembly step without affecting either the memory cost or the time cost of the solution step.*

#### 4.2.3 Distributed data structures

**The distribution of the data implied by this type of numerical parallelism does not necessarily reduce the** **JEVEUX** **memory consumptions**. For reasons of readability/maintainability, the usual *Code_Aster* objects are initialised with the same size as in sequential mode. Each MPI process merely “contents itself” with filling them partially with the data produced by the mesh cells for which the processor is responsible. It is then up to the parallel linear solver used in the remainder of the operator (MUMPS, PETSC or both) to assemble these incomplete and distributed data. One therefore does not generally resize these data structures; they contain many zero values.

This strategy is tenable only so long as the main JEVEUX objects involved in the elementary computations/assemblies (CHAM_ELEM, RESU_ELEM, MATR_ASSE and CHAM_NO) do not size the memory constraints of the analysis (cf. §5 of [U1.03.03](doc:u1.03.03)).

Normally their memory occupation is negligible compared with that of the linear solver. But when the latter (for example MUMPS) is itself Out-Of-Core [14]_
and parallelised in MPI (with the distribution of the data among processors that this implies), this hierarchy is no longer necessarily respected. Whence the introduction of **an option** (keyword MATR_DISTRIBUEE, cf. [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01)) **making it possible truly to resize, as tightly as possible, the block of the** **Aster matrix proper to each MPI process.**

**Note:**

- *In distributed mode, each MPI process manipulates only incomplete matrices (resized or not). On the other hand, in order to avoid numerous MPI communications (during the evaluation of the stopping criteria, residual computations...), this scenario has not been retained for the right-hand-side vectors. Their constructions are indeed parallelised, but, at the end of* *the assembly, the partial contributions of each process are gathered. Thus, every MPI process knows in full the vectors (CHAM_NO) involved in the analysis.*

### 4.3 Distribution of the basic linear-algebra computations

#### 4.3.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk.*

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising solutions of linear systems *via* MUMPS (use as a direct solver or as a preconditioner of PETSC/GCPC).

**Number of cores recommended:** on Aster5, between 2 and 12. No more than the number of physical cores sharing the same physical memory.

**Gain:** in elapsed time (but increase in the CPU time).

**Speed-up:** Gains variable according to the cases (parallel efficiency [15]_
>50%). A low granularity is sufficient for this parallelism to remain efficient: 10.103 degrees of freedom per OpenMP thread.

**Type of parallelism:** computer-science *via* the OpenMP language (ncpus).

**Scenario:** 1c of §2. A classical use consists in taking advantage of a hybrid MPI+OpenMP parallelism in order to accentuate the performances of MUMPS and to take advantage of 100% of the machine resources (2b/1c or (2b/1c)+2c).

Combination counter-productive with 2a, useful with 2b, possible but of little use with 2c (alone) or 1d.

#### 4.3.2 Implementation

The implementation of this parallel scheme is carried out transparently for the user. *Via* Astk, it is initialised by default as soon as one has selected a parallel version of *Code_Aster* (denoted ***_mpi) as well as a number of OpenMP threads at least equal to 2.

Thus on the centralised *Aster* server, one must parameterise the following fields in the Options menu **:**

- **ncpus=k**, number of OpenMP threads allocated (per MPI process if mpi_nbcpu>1).

This parallel scheme is generally used in conjunction with the MPI parallelism of MUMPS. Because one advises, in general, **not to allocate all the cores of a node in MPI alone**. This can have the effect of **slowing down the simulation** because, even if part of the analyses is accelerated as a result of its distribution over more cores, since these share certain memory resources, the accesses to the data are, for their part, slowed down. To use more efficiently and at 100% all the resources allocated, one rather advises **mixing** **MPI and OpenMP parallelism** (cf. scenarios 2b/1c or (2b/1c)+2c).

In this type of hybrid parallelism (MPI/OpenMP), the tool Astk [U1.04.00] automatically completes the number of threads according to the machine resources, as soon as the ncpus field is left empty.

For example, on the centralised machine *Aster5*, the nodes are composed of 24 cores. If one wishes to organise a hybrid parallelism of 12 MPI x 4 OpenMP, it is sufficient to set mpi_nbcpu to 12, mpi_nbnœud to 2 and ncpus=<empty> (or 4 explicitly).

**Note:**

- *The implementation of this parallelism depends on the computing context (hardware, software) and on the linear-algebra libraries used. On the centralised Aster machine, one uses the threaded MKL BLAS.*

### 4.4 Modal analyses of INFO_MODE/CALC_MODES

#### 4.4.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk.*

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising costly searches for eigenmodes.

**Number of cores recommended:** several tens (for example, number of frequency sub-bands x 2, 4 or 8).

**Gain:** in *elapsed* time or even in RAM (thanks to the second level of parallelism).

**Speed-up:** Gains variable according to the cases: efficiency of the order of 70% on the first level of parallelism (on the frequency sub-bands) complemented by the possible parallelism of the second level (if SOLVEUR=MUMPS, complementary efficiency of the order of 20%).

**Type of parallelism:** computer-science *via* the MPI language (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud).

**Scenario:** 1d of §2. Natively designed to couple to the parallelism 2b (or even 2b/1c).

Chaining possible but of little use with 1b. Combination possible but of little use with 2a and impossible with 2c (out of scope).

#### 4.4.2 Implementation

The use of CALC_MODES with the option 'BANDE' cut into several sub-bands is to be favoured when one treats modal problems **of medium or large sizes** (>0.5M degrees of freedom) and/or when one seeks a **good part of their spectra** (> 50 modes).

One then cuts the analysis into several frequency sub-bands. On each of these sub-bands, a modal solver carries out the associated mode search. To do this, this modal solver uses a linear solver intensively.

These two computation bricks (modal solver and linear solver) are the **dimensioning steps** of the analysis in terms of memory and time consumption. It is on them that one must place the emphasis if one wants to reduce significantly the analysis costs of this operator (cf. figures 1.3a/b).

Now, the organisation of the modal analysis over distinct sub-bands offers here an ideal framework for parallelism: **distribution of large, almost independent analyses**. Its parallelism makes it possible to gain a great deal in time but at the price of an extra cost in memory [16]_
.

If one has available a sufficient number of processors (> the number of non-empty sub-bands), one can then engage a **second level of parallelism** **via the linear solver** (if one has chosen METHODE='MUMPS'). This will make it possible to continue to gain in time but, above all, it will make it possible to compensate for the memory extra cost of the first level or even to reduce notably the sequential memory peak.

For an **optimal use** of CALC_MODES with the option 'BANDE' cut into several parallelised sub-bands, it is therefore advisable to:

- **Construct relatively balanced computation sub-bands**. To do this, one can therefore, beforehand, calibrate the spectrum studied *via* a call to INFO_MODE [U4.52.01](doc:u4.52.01) (if possible in parallel). Then launch the CALC_MODES analysis with the option 'BANDE' cut into several sub-bands parallelised according to the number of sub-bands chosen and the number of processors available.
- **Take sub-bands of between** **50 and** **100 modes**.
- **Select a number of processors** which is a multiple of the number of (non-empty) sub-bands. One thus reduces the load imbalances which harm the performances.

For more details one may consult the user's documentation of the operator [U4.52.02].

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk.*

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising MISS3D analyses.

**Number of cores recommended:** with the option FICHIER_TEMPS, several tens (for example, number of frequencies x4 or X8); other option, up to 24 cores.

**Type of parallelism:** computer-science *via* the MPI language (mpi_nbcpu/mpi_nbnœud) if FICHIER_TEMPS, possibly complemented by an OpenMP parallelism (all the other options of CALC_MISS, ncpus).

**Scenario:** 1e of §2.

#### 4.5.2 Implementation

If the CALC_MISS analyses are independent (option FICHIER_TEMPS), one can already distribute them over several nodes, reserving several MPI processes per node. Each one will treat a series of frequencies. One will not take all the cores of a node, in order to leave enough RAM for the MISS3D analysis.

Then, in order to make the best use of these unoccupied cores, it is then advisable to add a second level of parallelism by activating OpenMP threads within each MISS3D MPI process (cf. figures 1.4a/b).

If these CALC_MISS analyses are, on the other hand, not independent (option other than FICHIER_TEMPS), one will use only one node of the cluster and one will activate only the second level of parallelism *via* OpenMP.

For more details one may consult the user's documentation of the operator [U2.06.07].

## 5 Numerical parallelisms

### 5.1 Direct solver MULT_FRONT

#### 5.1.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk*.

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising costly solutions of linear systems (in general STAT/DYNA_NON_LINE, MECA_STATIQUE...).

**Number of cores recommended:** 2 or 4.

**Gain:** in elapsed time.

**Speed-up:** Gains variable according to the cases (parallel efficiency ≈50%). One needs a good granularity for this parallelism to remain efficient: at least 50.103 degrees of freedom per core.

**Type of parallelism:** numerical *via* the OpenMP language (ncpus).

**Scenario:** 2a of §2. Chaining possible but of little use with 1b, 2b or 2c. Combination counter-productive with 1c, possible but of little use with 1d.

#### 5.1.2 Implementation

This multifrontal method developed in-house (cf. [R6.02.02](doc:r6.02.02) or [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01) §3.5) is used *via* the keyword SOLVEUR/METHODE='MULT_FRONT'. It is the linear solver (historical and self-contained) recommended by default in sequential mode on models of small or medium size (<0.5M degrees of freedom).

The implementation of this parallelism is carried out transparently for the user. It is initialised by default as soon as one launches an analysis *via Astk* (Options menu) using several OpenMP threads.

Thus on the centralised *Aster* server, one must parameterise the following field:

- ncpus=n, number of OpenMP threads allocated.

Once this number of threads has been fixed one can launch one's analysis (in batch on the centralised machine) with the same parameter setting as in sequential mode. One can of course lower the time specifications of the analysis.

### 5.2 MUMPS package

#### 5.2.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk*.

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising costly solutions of linear systems (in general STAT/DYNA_NON_LINE, MECA_STATIQUE...).

**Number of cores recommended:** chained with the distributed parallelism of the elementary computations/assemblies, typically 16, 32 or even 64.

**Gain:** in CPU time and in RAM.

**Speed-up:** Gains variable according to the cases (parallel efficiency ≈30%). One needs a medium granularity for this parallelism to remain efficient: between 30 and 50.103 degrees of freedom per MPI process.

**Type of parallelism:** numerical *via* the MPI language.

**Scenario:** 2b of §3. Natively designed to chain to the parallelisms 1b or 2c. Chaining possible but of little use with 2a. Coupling very useful with 1c or 1d (or even both).

#### 5.2.2 Implementation

**This multifrontal method relies on the external product MUMPS** (cf. [R6.02.03](doc:r6.02.03) or [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01) §3.7) and is used either as a direct solver (keyword SOLVEUR/METHODE='MUMPS'), or as a preconditioner of the iterative solvers PETSC or GCPC (keyword SOLVEUR/PRE_COND='LDLT_SP').

**It is the HPC package recommended in order to exploit fully the CPU/RAM gains that parallelism can provide**. This type of parallelism is efficient (especially when it is chained with 1b and coupled with 1c) while remaining generic, robust and for the general user.

The implementation of this parallel scheme is carried out transparently for the user. *Via* Astk, it is initialised by default as soon as one has selected a parallel version of *Code_Aster* (denoted ***_mpi) as well as a number of MPI processes at least equal to 2.

Thus on the centralised *Aster* server, one must parameterise the following fields in the Options menu **:**

- **mpi_nbcpu=m**, number of MPI processes allocated.
- **mpi_nbnœud=p**, number of nodes over which these MPI processes will be distributed.

For example, on the centralised machine Aster5, the nodes are composed of 24 cores. To allocate 32 MPI processes at a rate of 8 processes per node, one must therefore set mpi_nbcpu to 32 and mpi_nbnœud to 4.

One advises, in general, **not to allocate all the cores of a node in MPI alone**. This can have the effect of **slowing down the simulation** because, even if part of the analyses is accelerated as a result of its distribution over more cores, since these share certain memory resources, the accesses to the data are, for their part, slowed down.

To use more efficiently and at 100% all the resources allocated, one rather advises **mixing MPI and OpenMP parallelism** (cf. scenarios 1b+2b/1c or 1b+2b/1c+2c).

Ideally, this HPC linear solver must be used in distributed parallel mode (DISTRIBUTION/METHODE='GROUP_ELEM'/'MAIL_DISPERSE'/'MAIL_CONTIGU'/'SOUS_DOMAINE'/'SOUS_DOM.OLD'). That is to say that one must have initiated, upstream of this linear solver, within the elementary-computation/assembly procedures, distributed data/treatment flows (parallel scenario 1b). MUMPS accepts as input these incomplete data and it gathers them internally. One thus does not lose time (as is the case for the other linear solvers) in completing the data coming from each processor. This mode of operation is activated by default in the commands AFFE/MODI_MODELE (cf. §4.2).

In centralised mode (CENTRALISE), the upstream phase of construction of the linear systems is not parallelised (each processor proceeds as in sequential mode). MUMPS then takes into account only the data coming from the master processor.

In the first case, the code is parallel from the construction of the linear system through to its solution (chaining of the parallelisms 1b+2b); in the second case, one exploits the MPI parallelism only on the solution part (parallelism 2b).

### 5.3 Iterative solver PETSC

#### 5.3.1 Description

**Use:** for the general user *via Astk*.

**Scope of use:** analyses comprising costly solutions of linear systems (in general STAT/DYNA_NON_LINE, MECA_STATIQUE...). Rather non-linear problems of large sizes.

**Number of cores recommended:** chained with the distributed parallelism of the elementary computations/assemblies (1b), or even that of the MUMPS preconditioner (2b), typically 16, 32 or even 64.

**Gain:** in CPU time and in RAM (according to the preconditioners).

**Speed-up:** gains variable according to the cases (parallel efficiency >50%). One needs a medium granularity for this parallelism to remain efficient: 50.103 degrees of freedom per MPI process.

**Type of parallelism:** numerical *via* the MPI language.

**Scenario:** 2c of §3. Natively designed to chain to the parallelisms 1b or 2b; chaining possible but of little use with 2a. Combination possible but of little use with 1c, out of scope with 1d.

#### 5.3.2 Implementation

This library of iterative solvers (cf. [R6.01.02](doc:r6.01.02) or [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01) §3.9) is used *via* the keyword SOLVEUR/METHODE='PETSC'. This type of linear solver is recommended for treating either frontier problems of very large size (>5M degrees of freedom), or, in non-linear analysis, for taking full advantage of the sharing of the preconditioner between different Newton steps **.**

The implementation of this parallelism is carried out as for the MUMPS package (cf. §5.2).

**Note:**

- *Unlike the direct parallel solvers (MUMPS,* *MULT_FRONT), the iterative ones are not universal (they cannot be used in modal analysis) and not always robust. They can be very competitive (in time and above all in memory), but one must find the operating point (algorithm, preconditioner...) suited to the problem. However, on this last point, the generalised use (and parameterised by default) of* *MUMPS in single precision as a preconditioner (PRE_COND='LDLT_SP') has considerably improved matters.*
