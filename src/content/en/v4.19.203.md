# v4.19.203 TTLA203 - Cylinder with temperature imposed with adiabatic crack

**Summary**

The objective of this test is to validate elements axisymmetric X-FEM thermal thermal transient.

A full cylinder, cracked at the section at mid-height, is considered. The crack is a crown of radius inside the half radius of the cylinder, and the radius of the cylinder is external. The load is transient and consists of a temperature gradient imposed by the application of Dirichlet conditions to the two ends of the cylinder. It is assumed that an adiabatic crack (zero flow on the lips of the crack and discontinuous temperature through the crack)

Four modellings are considered:

- $A$ Modelling: $\text{FEM AXIS}$ (mesh crack)
- $B$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM AXIS}$ , crack in the middle of elements
- $C$ Modelling: $\text{FEM 3D}$ (mesh crack)
- $D$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{3D}$ , crack in the middle of elements

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure, shown in Figure , is a $R=1m$ radius and $H=2m$ height cylinder with a flat crack on the section at mid height.(included in the $y=H/2$ equation plane ). The crack is opening, and has as geometric support the inner $R/2$ radius crown and external $R$ radius crown (red filled in Figure ).

The disk included in the $y=0$ equation plane is called "lower disc", and the disk included in the $y=H$ equation plane is called "upper disc".

![Figure](/figures/v4.19.203/10000000000003AB000001F330D03579359A18DD.png)

Figure 1.1-1: Géométrie du problème

Finally, ${P}^{+}(\theta )$ is noted with point coordinates (located on the upper lip), and ${P}^{-}(\theta )$ with $\left(R\cos\theta ,{H}^{-}/2,R\sin\theta \right)$ coordinates (located on the lower lip)

### 1.2 Material properties

Thermal conductivity: $\lambda =1{\mathit{W.m}}^{\text{-1}}.{K}^{\text{-1}}$

Volumetric heating capacity: $\rho {C}_{p}=2\mathit{J.m}-{\mathrm{3.K}}^{-1}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The problem is solved on the time interval $\left[0.s,1.s\right]$ discretized in 5 time step equal (of duration $\Delta t=0.2s$). The default value is taken in THER_LINEAIRE of the parameter of the theta-schema: $\theta =0.57$ .

The following temperature ramp shall be imposed on the nodes of the lower disc ( *cf.* paragraph 1.1. ):

à $t=0.s$ , $\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\mathit{AB}}=10°C$ ;            à $t=1.s$ , $\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}=20°C$

The following temperature ramp shall be imposed on the nodes of the upper disc ( *cf.* paragraph 1.1. ):

à $t=0.s$ , $\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}=20°C$ ;            à $t=1.s$ , $\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}=40°C$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The initial state is determined by solving the stationary problem at $t=0.s$ (with the boundary conditions given in paragraph 1.3.)

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

Reference solution is obtained by refining the mesh Modelling A (elements classic axisymmetric with mesh crack): mesh regular composed of $1000\times 2000$ QUAD8 (instead of $100\times 200$ QUAD8 for mesh A)

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The temperature at the end of the last time step ($t=1.s$) is tested at the ${P}^{+}(\theta )$ and ${P}^{-}(\theta )$ points (see Figure ).

The problem being axisymmetric, the values tested cannot vary with $\theta$. These values are then tested with:

- $\theta =\pi /4$ for modellings C and D (respectively $\text{FEM 3D}$ and $\text{X-FEM 3D}$)
