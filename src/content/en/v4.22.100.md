# v4.22.100 TTNL100 - Nonlinear thermal source, homogeneous solution in space

**Summary:**

This test checks the thermal calculation in the presence of a nonlinear source loading, depending on the temperature.

The reference solution is analytical. The part considered in the modellings consists of a single element whose dimensions do not matter to the solution.

The following mesh cellss are checked:

- Modelization A:

  - TRIA3, TRIA6, QUAD4, QUAD8 and QUAD9 for PLAN Modelling,
  - TRIA3,QUAD4 for PLAN_DIAG Modelling,
- Modelling B: PENTA6 for 3D modelling

Since the temperature is homogeneous in the element, the calculation can be considered as 0D.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The part considered in both modellings is a unique element.

Since the temperature is homogeneous in the element, the calculation can be considered as 0D.

### 1.2 Material properties

| $\lambda =0$ | thermal conductivity |
| --- | --- |
| $\rho C=2$ | volumetric heat |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Nonlinear volume source loading, temperature function:

$s(T)=2-2\times w\times T$ with $w=2$

Boundary conditions are adiabatic, which corresponds to the defect in *Code_Aster* .

The $[0.;1.]$ time range is discretized in 100 time step (duration of each time step equal to $0.01$).

### 1.4 Initial conditions

$\mathit{T0}=0$ in all the element.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

In this problem, the boundary conditions are adiabatic, the initial temperature is constant equal to ${T}_{0}$ and the load is reduced to the heat source function of the temperature $r(T)={r}_{0}-{r}_{1}T$ where ${r}_{1}$ is positive for questions of thermal stability. These conditions ensure a homogeneous solution in space. The heat equation is reduced to:

> $\rho {C}_{p}\dot{T}={r}_{0}-{r}_{1}T$ ; $T(0)={T}_{0}$     [éq1]

Standardization can be reduced without loss of generality to the following equation:

> $\dot{u}=1-\omega u$ ; $u(0)=0$     [éq2]

The solution of this differential equation of first order is then:

> $u(t)=\frac{1}{\omega}(1-{e}^{-\omega t})$

Rather than going back from $u$ solution from [eq2] to $T$ solution from [eq1], we prefer to adopt the following set of parameters, without paying attention to the units, which leads to $T=u$: ${T}_{0}=0$, ${r}_{0}=\rho C$ and ${r}_{1}=\omega {r}_{0}$.

### 2.2 Baseline
