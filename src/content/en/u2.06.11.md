# u2.06.11 Analysis of the seismic resistance of large metal tanks

**Summary:**

The aim of this document is to describe the different steps to be implemented in *Code_Aster* in order to carry out the numerical simulation of the seismic resistance of large metal tanks. These thin metal structures can then present two preferred failure modes: failure of the anchorages or buckling of the shell courses.

The first approach is based on a regulatory method of push-over type. One can, in addition, use a non-linear stability criterion (*cf.* documentation [U2.08.04](doc:u2.08.04) and test cases SSLL105 and SSNL126).

The second approach is direct transient modelling with the fluid domain fully taken into account through a coupled fluid-structure approach in large displacements (a modelling similar to that of test case FDNV100). One can also couple this transient analysis to a non-linear stability analysis, as test case FDNV100 also shows.

## 1 Introduction

This documentation presents the different numerical methodologies developed in order to simulate and analyse the seismic resistance of large metal tanks. The aim here is therefore to present the implementation and the chaining of various operators of *Code_Aster* in order to carry through the study of this type of component, according to different modelling hypotheses which are mainly dictated by regulatory considerations. These tanks, which are thin metal structures, can present two preferred failure modes: failure of the anchorages or buckling of the shell courses.

The first methodology is based on a regulatory method of push-over type ([bib1], [bib2], [bib3], [bib4]). The tank is subjected to an imposed internal pressure, spatially variable and increasing. The solution is obtained in quasi-statics and the fluid domain is not modelled directly: its influence on the wall is transcribed by a particular imposed pressure field. The non-linearities are of geometric and constitutive type (plasticity). In the course of the incremental analysis, this pressure is increased until the ultimate load is obtained, which corresponds to the buckling of the structure (one can use the keyword CRIT_STAB of STAT_NON_LINE for a non-linear stability analysis). In order to model the anchorages more finely, one can introduce the uplift at the bolted anchorages with the ground [bib5]. One can also increase the mechanical resistance to buckling by adding a carbon-fibre reinforcement on the shell courses. Its modelling is presented in this document.

The second approach is direct transient modelling with the fluid domain fully taken into account through a coupled fluid-structure approach in large displacements [bib6]. This modelling, finer than the previous one, complements the regulatory approaches, in particular by making it possible to identify more clearly the limits of their domain of validity, mainly with respect to the large global non-linearities such as the large displacements. However, the practical use of these transient approaches is limited by the extra computational cost which they induce (a ratio of the order of 10). It is possible to combine the transient approach with a non-linear stability analysis via the use of the keyword CRIT_STAB of DYNA_NON_LINE. Unlike the quasi-static analyses, the particular features of the fluid-structure model require a specific treatment in CRIT_STAB, which will be detailed in this document.

## 3 Quasi-static push-over approach

### 3.1 Imposed loadings

The imposed loading is of regulatory origin (EC-8), and quasi-static [bib1]. The effects of an earthquake on the structure are represented by a variable pressure field imposed on the inner face of the shell courses. The value at each point is a function of the current coordinate and increases linearly with time. This monotonic evolution in time is characteristic of the methods known as push-over, in the sense of EC-8, whose objective is to simulate by a quasi-static analysis the response to a seismic loading, and therefore of a physically transient dynamic nature. The dynamic effects, such as the inertia and the forces generated by the sloshing fluid, are replaced by this imposed pressure distribution. The problem to be treated involves only a modelling of the structure, without modelling of the fluid domain.

The push-over methods have of course been constructed and justified by making strong hypotheses of linearisation of the problem (small displacements, elastic constitutive behaviour, Euler buckling…). The definitions of the imposed pressure fields are given below [bib2]:

### 3.3 Notes on the analyses and the post-processings

In the example presented above a Newton algorithm is used: one solves with the global tangent operator (stiffness) updated at each iteration. In the case where the problem is well posed (sufficiently “regular”), it is known that this type of algorithm offers the best convergence. Therefore, in the present case, as long as the global tangent operator is “well” defined (far from being singular), the analysis will proceed well with rapid convergence. When the imposed load level approaches the ultimate load, the structure will then become unstable in the sense of buckling [R7.05.01](doc:r7.05.01), which is reflected by the tendency of the global tangent operator to become singular. The loss of stability by a limit point is in fact the loss of uniqueness of the solution, and hence the singularity of the solution operator. In the neighbourhood of the limit point the Newton algorithm will converge less well, whence the need to impose smaller time increments and an increase in the number of iterations on the equilibrium residual.

In a general manner, the closer one gets to the ultimate load, the smaller the time step will have to be. Despite this, the risks of the analysis stopping on non-convergence are high, whence the obligation to carry out the analysis over several successive restarts.

It is nevertheless possible to improve this convergence by changing algorithm in the course of the analysis and switching to a quasi-Newton method. To do this, it is sufficient to solve on the tangent operator which one updates only at each step (between two iterations one keeps the same one), and if this is insufficient, one can then solve with the elastic operator which, for its part, cannot become singular. This choice reinforces the robustness of the algorithm in terms of convergence, but it increases, sometimes considerably, the number of iterations (and/or of steps) necessary to obtain the solution.

For this type of study one can distinguish two types of quantities of interest for the post-processing. On the one hand a quantity able to reflect the buckling of the structure and therefore to bring out the ultimate load. For this one can plot the pressure level (multiplier coefficient) as a function of the displacement of a point located at the top of the tank (at the end of the generatrix most subjected to compression). On the other hand a more local indicator of the appearance of plasticity: the iso-value of the cumulative plastic strain, at each time instant of the analysis.

These two post-processings present no particular difficulty in *Code_Aster*.

The finite-element models implemented comprised between 55000 and 110000 degrees of freedom. These models were based on a simplified representation of the anchorages: the geometry of the reinforcements and gussets is finely meshed, but the bolting is not present and it is replaced by a clamped boundary condition on all the nodes at elevation $0m$ . A more realistic modelling of the anchorages, with lift-off, would therefore have led to a global problem size that was appreciably larger.

The analysis methodology presented here, whose objective is to study the quasi-static buckling response [R7.05.01](doc:r7.05.01) of a tank, is very close to the framework of the user's guide for the buckling analysis [U2.08.04](doc:u2.08.04). This documentation presents the linear stability analyses (in the Euler sense) and the setting up of a non-linear analysis of push-over type in order to obtain the ultimate load.

### 3.4 Fine modelling of the anchorages: uplift

The type of tanks studied here are bolted to the ground [bib5]. These bolts (or tie rods) pass through reinforced flanges welded at the base of the shell course. For a faster run, the method implemented is presented below on a simplified mesh, but with realistic anchorages: the tank is fixed to the ground by 18 tie rods.

In *Code_Aster*, several modellings of contact are available.

The modelling of the geometry of the contact zone can be surfacic (3D problem), lineic (2D problem) or made up of discrete elements (keywords DIS_CHOC in STAT_NON_LINE and DIS_CONTACT for the material law).

The contact itself can be treated either in a nodal manner by penalisation or by the Lagrangian method, with active constraints or not, or in a continuous manner by the augmented Lagrangian method.

The simplest method, in this study configuration, is that of penalisation, which in this case also has the advantage of being able to take into account the role of the sealing joint without having to mesh it separately. For a quasi-static analysis, penalisation is not numerically problematic, as it is in dynamics where this technique can generate high-frequency disturbances (linked to the penalisation stiffness). Moreover, for structural finite elements, one can adjust the penalisation stiffness so as to approximate as well as possible the contact stiffness of the part which is in reality massive.

The other methods are however more rigorous because they do not generate interpenetration.

For the study of the lift-off of the tank, a penalisation method will therefore be used, which presents the best ratio of modelling quality to computational cost on this particular case.

Given the option taken of forcing all the nodes to be able to move only vertically, one can take advantage of this complete pairing in order to be content with using discrete elements for the contact. Indeed, it is not necessary to carry out re-pairing, and therefore the general surfacic master-slave modellings of the contact zones are unnecessarily heavy. A carpet of discrete contact elements will therefore be placed under the lower hoop. There will then be one discrete element (DIS_T on a SEG2 mesh cell) under each node at elevation 0.

If the computed solution proves too far from the experimental reference solution, it will be necessary to quantify the influence of the condition of purely vertical displacement. It will be sufficient to re-run analyses without this kinematic hypothesis, with, if the horizontal displacements are large, a contact-management method with re-pairing of the nodes of the surfaces concerned.

**Notes**

*Strictly speaking, every node of the base shell course ought to be provided with a discrete contact element. However, if finite elements of type* *COQUE_3D are used for the hoops, one cannot place a contact element at the nodes at the centre of the finite elements. Indeed, this node has the particular feature of carrying only degrees of freedom of rotation type. The contact condition, which bears on the displacement normal to the face, therefore cannot be expressed at these nodes. The contact elements, as well as the kinematic condition of vertical displacement of the base nodes, must therefore be carried only by the vertex nodes or the mid-side nodes of the edges.*

The modelling of contact by discrete elements makes it possible, in *Code_Aster*, to define directly the preload of these elements. One can thus represent the prestress of the tie rods, without having to pre-deform these elements, as one would have to do if one had chosen another modelling of the contact based on the facing surfaces.

### 3.5 Use of the non-linear stability criterion

One can also use a stability criterion based on the tangent matrix: there is an instability if the tangent stiffness matrix becomes singular, that is if at least one of its eigenvalues vanishes. One then solves the following eigenvalue problem, written in large displacements (Lagrangian formulation with the Green-Lagrange strain tensor) [R7.05.01](doc:r7.05.01):

$({K}_{T}+\lambda {I}_{d})x=0\mathrm{\iff }{K}_{T}x=\lambda {I}_{d}x$ with:

| ${K}_{T}=K+{K}^{L}(\underline{u})+{K}^{Q}(\underline{u})+K(\pi )$ | Tangent stiffness matrix |
| --- | --- |
| ${K}^{L}(\underline{u})$ | Part of the matrix ${K}_{T}$ linear in $\underline{u}$ |
| ${K}^{Q}(\underline{u})$ | Part of the matrix ${K}_{T}$ quadratic in $\underline{u}$ |
| $K(\pi )$ | Geometric stiffness matrix |
| $\pi$ | Piola-Kirchhoff tensor $\mathrm{II}$ |
| ${I}_{d}$ | Identity matrix |
| $\lambda$ | Eigenvalue |

Documentation [R7.05.01](doc:r7.05.01) presents these stability analyses in more detail. Documentation [U2.08.04](doc:u2.08.04) presents their use.

**Notes**

*When the displacements are small, one simply has* $K(\pi )=K(\sigma )={K}_{G}$ *and the matrices* ${K}^{L}$ *and* ${K}^{Q}$ *can be neglected.*

*The linear analysis in the Euler sense in Code_Aster does not make it possible to take into account the follower character of the pressure field: one must necessarily switch to the approach presented here and based on a non-linear solution.*

If one wants to make use of the stability criterion, it is sufficient to add the following line among the arguments of STAT_NON_LINE([U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03), [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)):

CRIT_STAB=_F(NMAX_CHAR_CRIT=1,),

One then looks for the first eigenvalue of the global tangent operator of the system.

If in the course of the analysis one observes that this eigenvalue decreases, or even changes sign, this means that one has approached the first critical load and that one has then even gone past it.

The number of eigenvalues to be determined can be imposed by the keyword NMAX_CHAR_CRIT (3 by default).

It is also possible, by using the command CHAR_CRIT, to choose the band in which these eigenvalues are to be sought (from –10 to 10 by default).

**Note**

*The indication of a frequency band is useful above all for analyses in small perturbations, where a Sturm test is carried out for the frequency band supplied. One can thus save time by computing the eigenvalues only if there are some in the band indicated. The Sturm test is not done in large strains and the eigenvalues are computed at each time step.*

The buckling mode as well as the eigenvalues determined (critical load coefficient) can be recovered by using the command IMPR_RESU:

IMPR_RESU( MODELE = MODELE ,FORMAT = 'RESULTAT' ,RESU=(_F(RESULTAT=RESU,

NOM_PARA='CHAR_CRIT',’MODE_FLAMB’,),),)

Test case SSLL105 [V3.01.105](doc:v3.01.105) offers an example of the use of this stability criterion for a linear case and test case SSNL126 [V6.02.126](doc:v6.02.126) for a non-linear case (elastoplastic beam).

### 3.6 Piloting of the loading

In order to facilitate the convergence of the incremental analysis when one is close to the ultimate load level, or in order to be able to go past this critical point, it can be judicious to no longer place oneself under imposed loading and to favour a displacement piloting or an arc-length piloting (its use for the post-critical regime is briefly recalled in documentation [U2.08.04](doc:u2.08.04)). Piloting cannot be used with contact [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03).

## 4 Coupled transient approach

### 4.1 Reference problem

One leaves here the regulatory framework and one is going to exploit all the modelling possibilities offered by *Code_Aster*. The model of the tank itself remains unchanged (elastoplastic volumetric shells). On the other hand, the fluid domain will be represented by a solid mesh. Moreover, the solution will be carried out in transient dynamics with the operator DYNA_NON_LINE ([U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) , [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05)), the external loading being of seismic type.

The fluid domain is modelled in linear acoustics (barotropic, compressible, non-viscous and with a free surface). The coupled fluid-structure problem is solved in *Code_Aster* by a symmetric formulation $(u,p,\varphi )$ ([R4.02.02](doc:r4.02.02), [bib7]), in an updated Lagrangian formulation. The loading is of the type of an accelerogram imposed at the base of the tank.

The discretised problem then appears as follows [bib6]:

### 4.2 Coupled fluid-structure modelling in Code_Aster

In order to be able to carry out a coupled analysis by the formulation $(u,p,\varphi )$ [bib7] in *Code_Aster*, with free surfaces and slip at the interface (no adhesion between the non-viscous fluid and the inner wall of the tank), a certain construction of the mesh and of the corresponding models must be respected.

One must therefore define the fluid mesh, the structure mesh and the fluid-structure interface.

In the mesh generator one therefore generates two distinct meshes (different mesh cells but common nodes) for the fluid domain and the structure. For practical reasons, it can be simpler to generate the distinct mesh cells separately, therefore with duplicated nodes, and then to eliminate these duplicate nodes so as to have only single nodes.

Then, in *Code_Aster*, the support mesh of the interface will be generated as follows, from the mesh-cell groups SURF0, SURF1 and SURF2 which are the shell courses of the tank:

MAILLA01=CREA_MAILLAGE(MAILLAGE=MAILLA1,

CREA_GROUP_MA=_F(NOM='IFLUSTRU',

GROUP_MA=('SURF0','SURF1','SURF2',),

PREF_MAILLE='I',),);

One therefore creates a new mesh-cell group IFLUSTRU. The nodes of this mesh coincide with those of the structure, but the mesh cells are different.

**Notes**

*The structure is meshed with* *COQUE_3D finite elements, whose geometric support is a 9-node quadrilateral. The central node has the particular feature of carrying only rotation degrees of freedom.*

*If one wants to couple such a finite element with a solid fluid finite element, one therefore cannot write a kinematic coupling condition for this central node, since it does not have a translation degree of freedom, unlike the corresponding node which comes from the fluid domain and which, for its part, carries only translation degrees of freedom.*

*To get round this problem, the fluid-structure coupling will be written only on the nodes of the shell mesh which carry the translation degrees of freedom: the vertex nodes and the mid-side nodes of the edges of the finite elements.*

*One must therefore have in Code_Aster the structure mesh comprising only 8-node quadrilateral finite elements (these are indeed the vertex nodes and the mid-side nodes of the edges), on which the interface is defined. The structure mesh for the* *COQUE_3D is defined only afterwards, from this mesh, by adding the central nodes.*

*The fluid mesh, in order to be conforming with the interface, will for its part be composed of 20-node parallelepipeds.*

*Moreover, even if one used a structure mesh with shell finite elements whose geometric support was a 9-node quadrilateral, and where all the nodes, even the central one, carried translation degrees of freedom, the fluid-structure coupling could pose a problem. Indeed, the conforming solid fluid mesh would have to be built with 27-node parallelepiped solid finite elements. However, some mesh generators do not offer this type of complete finite element, which is rather little used in structural analysis, unlike the classical quadratic finite elements that are the 20-node parallelepipeds.*

It is imperative that the outward normal to the fluid domain always be oriented in the same direction. It is strongly advised to keep the convention of orientation from the structure towards the fluid for all the modellings of the fluid-structure interface, in particular FLUI_STRU.

Once the interface has been defined, which is therefore meshed with 8-node quadrilateral finite elements (which are equivalent to the faces of the solid finite elements employed for the fluid domain: 20-node parallelepiped finite elements), one can therefore carry out the modification of the structure mesh (mesh-cell group RESERVOI) into 9-node finite elements, the geometric support of the COQUE_3D:

MAILLA2=CREA_MAILLAGE(MAILLAGE=MAILLA01,

MODI_MAILLE=_F(GROUP_MA='RESERVOIR',

OPTION='QUAD8_9',

PREF_NOEUD='NSQ',

PREF_NUME=1,),);

One can then define the models necessary for the coupled analysis:

MODELE=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAILLA2, INFO=1, VERIF='MAILLE',

AFFE=(_F(GROUP_MA='SURFLIBR',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='2D_FLUI_PESA',),

_F(GROUP_MA=('FLUID0','FOND','PLANCENT',),

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='3D_FLUIDE',),

_F(GROUP_MA='IFLUSTRU',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='FLUI_STRU',),

_F(GROUP_MA='RESERVOI',

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='COQUE_3D',),),);

The mesh-cell group SURFLIBR (boundary mesh cells of the fluid domain, located on its upper face) carries a free-surface model [R4.02.04](doc:r4.02.04): 2D_FLUID_PESA.

The mesh-cell groups FLUID0 (solid fluid domain), FOND (boundary mesh cells of FLUID0 defining the bottom) and PLANCENT (boundary mesh cells of FLUID0 defining the plane of symmetry) define the total fluid domain: 3D_FLUIDE.

The fluid-structure interface (FLUI_STRU) is carried by the mesh-cell group IFLUSTRU.

Finally, the mesh of the tank (RESERVOI) is the support of the structural model in volumetric shells (COQUE_3D).

The compressible fluid material is also defined:

EAU=DEFI_MATERIAU(FLUIDE=_F(RHO=1000.0, CELE_R=1500.0,),);

which will be assigned to the fluid domain (solid model and its boundaries) as well as to the interface IFLUSTRU and to the free surface SURFLIBR:

CHMAT=AFFE_MATERIAU(MAILLAGE=MAILLA2,

AFFE=(_F(GROUP_MA=('FLUID0','FOND','PLANCENT','IFLUSTRU','SURFLIBR',),

MATER=EAU,),

…

);

### 4.3 Boundary conditions

The kinematic boundary conditions bear on the clamping at the base of the tank (SYMETRI2), on the generatrices in the vertical plane of symmetry (SYMETRIE) and on the non-penetration (zero normal velocity) at the bottom of the fluid domain (FOND), as well as in its vertical plane of symmetry (PLANCENT).

CONDLIM=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=MODELE,

DDL_IMPO=(_F(GROUP_NO='SYMETRI2',

DX=0.0, DY=0.0, DZ=0.0,

DRX=0.0, DRY=0.0, DRZ =0.0,),

_F(GROUP_NO='SYMETRIE',

DY=0.0, DRX=0.0, DRZ=0.0,),),

VITE_FACE=_F(GROUP_MA=('FOND','PLANCENT',), VNOR=0.0,),);

The effects of gravity are taken into account:

PESA=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(    MODELE=MODELE,

> PESANTEUR=_F(    GRAVITE=9.81,

> DIRECTION=(0.0,0.0,-1.0),),);

However this command is insufficient because, in the modelling $(u,p,\varphi )$ [bib7], the effects of gravity in the fluid domain cannot be taken into account. If nothing more were done, gravity would therefore truly be imposed only on the structure.

In order to approximate the effects of the gravity of the fluid on the wall, an equivalent hydrostatic pressure will be imposed (but one which cannot take into account the variation in the height of the fluid in the course of the analysis when the sloshing begins):

PHZ=DEFI_FONCTION(NOM_PARA='Z',

…

VERIF='CROISSANT',);

#

PH = FORMULE(NOM_PARA=('X','Y','Z'),VALE='PHZ(Z)',),

#

PRESSHYD=AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F(MODELE=MODELE,

FORCE_COQUE=_F(GROUP_MA='VIROLE', PRES= PH ,

PLAN='INF',),);

The earthquake is imposed as an accelerogram (function GASDM_X1) imposed at the base along the direction $X$ . It is therefore a loading of classical single-support type:

ACCELERX=CALC_FONCTION(COMB=(_F(FONCTION=GASDM_X1, COEF=0.5,),),);

#

MULT_X=CALC_CHAR_SEISME(MATR_MASS=MATMAS,

DIRECTION=(1.0,0.0,0.0,), _APPUI='OUI',);

#

CHARG_SE=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=MODELE, VECT_ASSE=MULT_X,);

The mass matrix used (MATMAS) is the mass matrix of the total coupled system.

### 4.4 Initial conditions

The initial state of the transient analysis must correspond to the equilibrium of the total system when it is not subjected to the earthquake. This static equilibrium state therefore corresponds to the gravity loading and to the hydrostatic effects.

If one started the dynamic analysis with an initial state which did not respect this equilibrium, then this would generate oscillations of the transient solution, since it would not initially be at equilibrium (the seismic level then being however zero). One can attenuate these oscillations by adding a “large” structural damping and by waiting for the solution to stabilise before imposing the earthquake, but this technique is rather inelegant…

In order to compute this statically equilibrated initial state, one can therefore solve a static equilibrium problem (which one assumes in addition to be linear) under the action of the gravity and hydrostatic forces.

To do this, one computes and assembles beforehand the global matrices $K$ and $M$ with the hydrostatic loading and the gravity:

ASSEMBLAGE( MODELE = MODELE, CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

CARA_ELEM=CARAELEM, CHARGE = (CONDLIM,PESA,PRESSHYD,),

NUME_DDL = CO('NUMSTA'),

SOLVEUR=_F(METHODE='MULT_FRONT',RENUM='METIS'),

MATR_ASSE=(

_F( MATRICE= CO('RIGSTA'), OPTION= 'RIGI_MECA'),

_F( MATRICE= CO('MASSTA'), OPTION= 'MASS_MECA'), ),);

The assembled stiffness matrix being singular because of the fluid domain (the formulation (u, p,) makes this matrix singular at zero frequency [bib8]), one slightly modifies the problem by considering the stiffness matrix ${K}_{\mathrm{cor}}=K+\varepsilon M$ $\varepsilon \ll 1$ which is no longer singular (it is named RIGICOMB).

One can, for example, take $\varepsilon =0,001$ as below:

RIGICOMB = COMB_MATR_ASSE(COMB_R=(_F( MATR_ASSE= RIGSTA, COEF_R= 1.),

_F( MATR_ASSE= MASSTA, COEF_R= -0.001),);

One then assembles the loading vector F_0 (right-hand side):

E_0 = CALC_VECT_ELEM(CARA_ELEM=CARAELEM, CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

OPTION='CHAR_MECA',CHARGE=(CONDLIM, PESA,PRESSHYD,),);

F_0 = ASSE_VECTEUR(VECT_ELEM= E_0, NUME_DDL= NUMSTA );

One can then solve the linear statics problem ${K}_{\mathrm{cor}}\underline{U}=\underline{{F}_{0}}$ , using, for example, a factorisation of type ${\mathrm{LDL}}^{T}$ :

RIGICOMB = FACTORISER(reuse=RIGICOMB, MATR_ASSE= RIGICOMB,

STOP_SINGULIER= 'NON');

DEP0 = RESOUDRE(MATR_FACT= RIGICOMB, CHAM_NO=F_0);

The solution field DEP0 will therefore be the initial state of the transient dynamic analysis which follows.

### 4.5 Transient solution

The operator DYNA_NON_LINE ([U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) , [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05)) is used as follows:

RESU=DYNA_NON_LINE(MODELE=MODELE,

CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

CARA_ELEM=CARAELEM,

EXCIT=(_F(CHARGE=CONDLIM,),

_F(CHARGE=PESA,),

_F(CHARGE=PRESSHYD,

FONC_MULT=FONCMUL0,

TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV',),

_F(CHARGE=CHARG_SE,

FONC_MULT=ACCELERX,),),

COMPORTEMENT=(_F(RELATION='ELAS',

DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC',

GROUP_MA=('FLUID0','FOND','PLANCENT',

'IFLUSTRU','SURFLIBR',),),

_F(RELATION='ELAS', DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC',

GROUP_MA='ANNEAU',),

_F(RELATION='VMIS_ISOT_TRAC',

DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC',

GROUP_MA=('SURF0','SURF1',

'SURF2','SURF3',),),),

ETAT_INIT=_F(INST_ETAT_INIT=0.0, DEPL=DEP0,),

INCREMENT=_F(LIST_INST=LINST,),

SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='HHT', ALPHA=-0.1,

FORMULATION='DEPLACEMENT',),

NEWTON=_F(REAC_INCR=1, MATRICE='TANGENTE',REAC_ITER=1,),

SOLVEUR=_F(STOP_SINGULIER='NON',),

CONVERGENCE=_F(RESI_GLOB_RELA=1.e-05, ITER_GLOB_MAXI=20,

ARRET='OUI',),

ARCHIVAGE=_F(LIST_INST=LARCH),);

The solution is carried out in updated Lagrangian (option DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC') because the fluid domain is in small perturbations on each step. One must therefore check that the time step is small enough for this hypothesis to be verified.

A time-integration scheme of modified average-acceleration type is used (SCHEMA='HHT', ALPHA=-0.1) with numerical damping in order to stabilise the solution and to facilitate convergence.

Test case FDNV100 [V8.03.100](doc:v8.03.100) presents the analysis of a rectangular vessel full of water with a flexible wall. The modelling implemented is very close to that used here for the large tanks.

### 4.6 Use of the non-linear stability criterion

One can also use a stability criterion just as in quasi-statics. The presence of the fluid however requires a few specific options. Indeed, the global assembled stiffness matrix of the coupled fluid-structure problem is intrinsically singular (cf. documentation [R4.02.02](doc:r4.02.02)) at the level of the fluid degrees of freedom. These degrees of freedom must therefore be excluded from the stability analysis, but the stiffness matrix must also be modified (as well as the geometric stiffness matrix when it is used). To do this, the following keywords must be supplied, under CRIT_STAB:

- MODI_RIGI = 'OUI',
- DDL_EXCLUS=('PHI','PRES','DH',).

The list of excluded degrees of freedom must include all the types of degrees of freedom linked to the fluid model: in the example of test case FDNV100 one therefore has the potential PHI, the pressure PRES and the vertical displacement at the free surface DH. If this treatment is not carried out, then the call to CRIT_STAB will abort because of a singular matrix and no shift strategy could overcome this.

In quasi-statics, this problem does not arise because the coupled fluid-structure modelling then has no meaning.
