# v3.03.144 SSLS144 - Internal pressure cylinder

**Summary:**

This test validates COQUE_AXIS Modelling (carried by mesh cells SEG3). Static calculation is performed on an internal pressure cylindrical tank. The model is axisymmetric. Pressure distribution on the inner wall of the tank is not uniform. Generalised displacements and forces obtained are compared to an analytical reference solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A cylinder of medium radius $5m$ , $t=0.25m$ thickness and $L=10m$ length is considered. The chosen geometric dimensions allow to ignore the free edge effects in $y=\pm L/2$ (in axisymmetric, $L$ must check: $\frac{1}{2}L>3\sqrt{\text{Rt}}=3m$ ).

![Figure](/figures/v3.03.144/Object_16.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

The isotropic elastic material is characterized by the following properties:

- $E=1\mathit{Pa}$
- $\nu =0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The vertical cylinder is subjected to constant internal $p=1\mathit{Pa}$ pressure on the $y\le 0$ part, and zero on $y>0$. ${u}_{y}=0$ vertical displacement is imposed on the point $A$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

We place ourselves in the theory of LOVE-KIRCHHOFF for the calculation of the analytical solution of this problem.

The deflection in the axisymmetric mark reads:

${u}_{x}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{P}{8{\alpha}^{4}D}(2-{\mathrm{e}}^{\alpha y}\cos(\alpha y))& \forall y\le 0\\ \frac{P}{8{\alpha}^{4}D}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha y}\cos(\alpha y)& \forall y\ge 0\end{array}$

with $D=\frac{E{t}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}$ and $4{\alpha}^{4}=\frac{\mathit{Et}}{{\mathit{DR}}^{2}}$ .

The rotation says:

${\beta}_{s}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{P}{8{\alpha}^{3}D}{\mathrm{e}}^{\alpha y}(\cos(\alpha y)-\sin(\alpha y))& \forall y\le 0\\ \frac{P}{8{\alpha}^{3}D}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha y}(\cos(\alpha y)+\sin(\alpha y))& \forall y\ge 0\end{array}$

The general efforts are:

${N}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{\mathit{Et}}{R}{u}_{x}\left(y\right)$ ,

${M}_{\mathit{ss}}={\mathit{Du}}_{x}^{''}\left(y\right)=\frac{p}{4{\alpha}^{2}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-|y|}\sin(\alpha y)$

The three-dimensional constraints are:

${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{N}_{\theta \theta }}{t}+12\frac{{M}_{\theta \theta }(x-R)}{{t}^{3}}$ ,

${\sigma}_{\mathit{ss}}=12\frac{{M}_{\mathit{ss}}(x-R)}{{t}^{3}}$ ,

either:

${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }(y,x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{\mathit{pR}}{t}\left(1-\frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{\alpha y}}{2}\left(\cos(\alpha y)+2\nu \frac{R-x}{t}\sqrt{\frac{3}{1-{\nu}^{2}}}\sin(\alpha y)\right)\right)& \forall y\le 0\\ \frac{\mathit{pR}}{t}\cdot \frac{{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha y}}{2}\left(\cos(\alpha y)-2\nu \frac{R-x}{t}\sqrt{\frac{3}{1-{\nu}^{2}}}\sin(\alpha y)\right)& \forall y\ge 0\end{array}$ ,

et:

${\sigma}_{\mathit{ss}}(y,x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{\mathit{pR}}{t}\cdot \frac{x-R}{t}\sqrt{\frac{3}{1-{\nu}^{2}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{\alpha y}\sin(\alpha y)& \forall y\le 0\\ \frac{\mathit{pR}}{t}\cdot \frac{x-R}{t}\sqrt{\frac{3}{1-{\nu}^{2}}}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\alpha y}\sin(\alpha y)& \forall y\ge 0\end{array}$ .

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The following values are tested:

- deflection $\mathit{DX}$ at $A$, $B$ and $C$ points,
- $\mathit{DRZ}$ rotation at $A$ and $B$ points,
- generalised $\mathit{NYY}$ effort at $B$ and ${B}_{1}$ points,
- $\mathit{MXX}$ generalised moment at point ${B}_{1}$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

COQUE_AXIS Modelling is used. COQUE_AXIS models are usable for both thick plates (HENCKY-MINDLIN-REISSNER) and thin plates (KIRCHOFF-LOVE) thanks to a penalization approach that allows for the neutralization of shear energy or not: this is the theory of HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI. To approximate numerically the solution of LOVE-KIRCHHOFF, you must take a shear coefficient large enough (A_CIS) to inhibit the ${\gamma}_{s}$ transverse shear kinematic.The larger this coefficient, the more the rigidity matrix is almost singular, thus causing numerical instability.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh contains 100 elements of type SEG3.

## 4 Summary of the results

The different curves show that the movements and calculated forces are very close to the analytical solution. This test is therefore a good way to validate the axisymmetric COQUE_AXIS shell formulation.
