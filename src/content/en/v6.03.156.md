# v6.03.156 SSNP156 – Contact Validation Benchmark NAFEMS 4: loaded pin

**Summary:**

This is the fourth test case in a NAFEMS benchmark for the validation of contact and frangling. Benchmark references are obtained with Abaqus and MARC codes.

This test modeled friction contact between a pin and a mesh bore with different finesses and therefore not compatible. However, the calculation with *Code_Aster* requires the use of compatible meshes.

This test includes 2 modellings for testing:

- linear and quadratic elementss,
- ‘CONTINUE
- contact pressures and tangential friction forces

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is modelled on flat deformations. Only half is meshed due to symmetry.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.156/100002010000026F0000013988EE2A30464E3001.png)

$F$ is the center of the pin, $A$ the point belonging to the console and located on the plane of symmetry.

### 1.2 Material properties

**Console**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $70000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

**Goupilla**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $210000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

The friction coefficient between the block and the cylinder is $\mu =0,1$ **.**

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The structure being symmetrical and subject to a load respecting symmetry, only half is represented. Therefore, $\mathit{DY}=0$ is applied on the plane of symmetry.

The console is clamped on its left side:

- $\mathit{DX}=0$
- $\mathit{DY}=0$

The pin is subjected to a point force in its $F$ centre:

- $\mathit{FX}=20000\mathit{kN}$ , i.e. $\mathit{FX}=10000N$ for the half-structure

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is derived from results obtained with Abaqus and MARC codes in a NAFEMS benchmark for the [bib1] contact-frost validation.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Horizontal displacement of point $A$ (according to $x$ ) (external reference).

Contact pressure at point $A$ (external reference). The raised contact pressure is that extrapolated from the stresses in the volume.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Important (average code).

Note that the mesh used is different from the *benchmark* . The reason is given in conclusion of the document.

### 2.4 Bibliographic reference

### 3.4 Remarks

The linear results obtained by the continuous friction formulation are very close to the results of MARC and Abaqus. It should be noted that the horizontal displacement of the nodes pin does not coincide with the reference results.

This difference is probably due to the fact that this mark is given in particular in relation to flat deformations, which are made difficult by conflicting information in NAFEMS (the thickness is taken into account in commercial codes even in flat deformations).

Although different from Abaqus and MARC references, the results obtained are nevertheless identical to those obtained by CAST3M on the same calculation.

### 4.4 Remarks

The quadratic results obtained by the continuous friction formulation are close to the reference results, whether at displacement or pressure, and are almost identical to those obtained by Modelling A (linear mesh).

The contact pressure on the edge of the pin, whether via the degree of freedom LAGS_C of the continuous formulation or by extrapolation from the stresses, is perfectly consistent with the reference.

The degree of freedom LAGS_C has oscillations: their amplitude decreases when using an integration scheme of type 'GAUSS' and increasing the number of points in the quadrature.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test validates the contact-frotting relative to references given by commercial calculation codes (Abaqus and MARC).

It is important to note, however, that the meshes used in Aster calculations are different from the *benchmark* meshes: the latter had the particularity of not being compatible (for cylindrical contact it means either initial interpenetration or an initial non-zero game). However, in the presence of curved contact, this type of meshes leads to strong oscillations (for example, on pressure due to alternating contact points and non-contact points) which can only be avoided by using advanced features ( *splines* , repositioning of nodes). These features are currently not available in *Code_Aster* .

In quadratic, the contact pressure obtained from the degree of freedom LAGS_C in continuous formulation has less oscillations when increasing the number of points in the integration scheme.
