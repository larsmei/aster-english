# v6.03.180 SSNP180 - SCIENCE test Modelling with ENDO_LOCA_TC

**Summary:**

The constitutive law ENDO_LOCA_TC is validated by SCIENCE experimental tests. Two tests are modelled: a reinforced concrete beam in 4-point bending (test #1) and the same 4-point bending beam after heating at 170°C of its lower face (test #4). The beams have 5 m × 0.4 m × 1 m dimensions. The Modelling is D_PLAN (2D) where half of the test piece is used thanks to symmetry conditions. The steels (longitudinal frames and pins) are represented by bars and the restraint steel-beton is perfect. For concrete, the ENDO_LOCA_TC damage law is coupled with viscous regularization to improve robustness and reduce calculation times. In fine, the simulations give close results of the difference tests at the moment of plasification of the steels. For more results, a R&D note is available: 6125-1723-2023-00259.

## 1 Introduction

ENDO_LOCA_TC was developed in 2022 as part of the NGC project. The SCIENCE test Modelling was done in 2022-2023 and the R&D note 6125-1723-2023-00259 summarizes the results obtained with different constitutive laws and configurations. The "High performance" case is given below. The objective of this validation case is to enrich the V&V file for this law. By agilers, it provides an example of the use of the law coupled with the viscous regularization to improve convergence and robustness of the calculation.

### 1.1 SCIENCE tests

The European SCIENCE project (2013-2017) was designed to produce a dimensioning guide for the Steel-Concrete Structure (SCS) construction. The partners have conducted numerous laboratory tests to characterize the behaviour of the SCS but also on conventional reinforced concrete beams, with a view to comparison. The originality of this campaign lies in the fact that beams have been solicited by not only mechanical but also thermomechanical loads.

This validation case focuses on the beam of 5 x 5 x 0.4 m. It consists of a concrete C33/37. It contains 2 sheets of 9 longitudinal frames of diameter 31.7 mm in B500B steel and pins of the same steel of diameter 11.9 mm.

## 2 Reference modelling

### 2.1 Mesh

A 2D Modelling of flat deformations was preferred to allow very fine spatial discretization without overly penalising performance. Due to the symmetry of geometry and loading, only half of the beam is modelled (therefore artificially a symmetrical distribution of damage is required).
For modellings A and B, the mesh of the concrete domain consists of 1,368 mesh cells rectangular 3 to 4 cm with quadratic functions and 9 point Gauss integration scheme, V6.03.180-fig-mesh 2D.
For C and D modelling, the mesh is linear, and the integration scheme is 4 Gauss points.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.180/maillage_2d.png)

*mesh of the beam in 2D*

The longitudinal (black) and pins (violet and yellow) are represented by elements bars of the same size as the adjacent but linearly shaped mesh cells 2D concrete. The steel density per rebar is 7,103 mm2/m beam depth corresponding to 9 bars of diameter 31.7 mm over a metre depth. In this 2D model, which is based on an mesh whose length is the metre, the (fictive) depth is one unit length, i.e. one metre. To obtain a density of stiffness in tension identical to that of the actual rebar, an equivalent bar of section 7 103 mm2 must therefore be introduced into the 2D model. For the pins (14 bars of diameter 11.9 mm for a beam depth of 1 m), the steel density is 1,557 mm2 / m. Following the same reasoning as previously, the equivalent bar to be introduced into the 2D Modelling, is shown to be a squared-section, and the 2D-section is shown to be equal to that of section 2D, thus the 5-section is shown to be equal to that of section 2D.

The only difference between the #1 test and the #4 test at the mesh level is the use of a linear mesh that includes only concrete for thermal calculation before performing a mechanical calculation chained to the previous mesh. The word "chained" indicates that the mechanical calculation includes at the input a thermal field from a previous calculation. The calculations are performed sequentially.

### 2.2 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 2.2.1 Test 1

Test 1 is a "simple" 4-point bending test. The difficulty lies in the definition of boundary conditions and the application of the load. It was decided to control the average displacement on a line, consisting here of two mesh cells, that is 5 nodes (because the finished elements are quadratic). The kinematic relationship is applied:

$$
\frac{1}{N}*\sum_{k=1}^NDY(k)=U_{imposé} ; N=5
$$

where DY(k) is the displacement in the y (vertical) direction of the node d-indice k. In the case of the supports, U_imp=0; for the cylinders, U_imp=v t. The test lasts TFIN= 1.85 hours and the displacement at the verin is 200mm. The simulation is conducted only at the moment of platification of the steels, up to the moment 1400s corresponds to a required displacement of 35 mm at the cylinder level.

We must not forget the symmetry condition on the right edge of the test piece where DX = 0 and the own weight of the beam. the V6.03.180-fig-CL_ test1 synthesizes the boundary conditions and loads of test number 1.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.180/CL_test1.png)

*Illustration of boundary conditions and the loading of the mechanical test (test n°1)*

#### 2.2.2 Test #4

The thermomechanical test simulation is performed in two stages: first a purely thermal calculation, followed by a mechanical calculation with the previously calculated temperature field (linking) input. The thermal calculation is performed with the load illustrated in V6.03.180-fig-CLTH_ test4:

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.180/CLTH_test4.png)

*Illustration of thermal loading during thermomechanical test (test No. 4)*

The initial temperature is set at 25°C. The thermal boundary conditions are as follows:
* On the plane of symmetry a zero flow is imposed.
* On the lower side outside the heating zone, on the left end of the beam and on the upper side, convection exchange is possible with ambient air remaining at a temperature of 25°C. The convection coefficient chosen is h = 4 W/m2/K.
* On the heated part, in the absence of a document detailing this load, the following ramp is imposed (V6.03.180-fig-comparison_temperature):

| Instant | Temperature in °C |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | 25 |
| 3600 | 120 |
| 7200 | 140 |
| 10800 | 150 |
| 21600 | 160 |
| 32400 | 170 |
| 36000 | 170 |

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.180/comparaison_temperature.svg)

*Comparison between measurement and applied thermal loading (test #4)*

It should be noted that a nonlinear thermal calculation is carried out to integrate energy consumption by changing the phase of water at 100°C. The impact on the temperatures inside the beam is not negligible even if the impact on the mechanics is very limited. Thus, enthalpy is defined by:

$$
\beta(T)= \begin{cases}\int_{0}^{T} \rho C_{p}(u)du &  \text { avec} & T<100°C\\\int_{0}^{T} \rho C_{p}(u)du+m_{eau}L_{eau} &  \text { avec} & T>100°C\end{cases}
$$

With $\rho$ the density of concrete, $\rho {C}_{p}$ the density heat at constant pressure, $L_{eau}$ the latent heat of water vaporization (2 257 kJ/kg) and $m_{eau}$ the weight of water per cubic metre of concrete (136 kg). On the practical level, the nonlinear thermal calculation is based on the β(T) enthalpy data to be reported under DEFI_MATERIAU THER_NL. After , $\beta(T)$ enthalpie has a discontinuity equal to the latent heat when $L_{eau}*m_{eau}$ β(T) = 100 °C. To facilitate convergence of the nonlinear thermal calculation, it was chosen to spread this discontinuity affine between 95°C and 105°C.

In terms of mechanical loading:
* Mechanical preload in bending 4 points of 21 kN per cylinder, at a uniform temperature of 25°C.
* Temperature rise in the lower wall for eight hours and then maintain the set temperature during the 4 point bending test. It should be noted that for 8 hours the displacement of the cylinders is blocked at its value at the end of preload.
* Flexion 4 points by imposing a constantly increasing displacement of the cylinder from the preload position. The test is conducted at a speed of 1.92 mm/min. The measured displacements at the support level are on average 230 mm at the end of the test (i.e. at t = 10 hours).
* Taking into account own weight

As in Test 1, the displacements imposed on the supports and cylinders follow. Furthermore, the symmetry condition on the right edge of the test piece (DX = 0) is applied.

### 3.1 Overall response

The amount of interest that is the subject of the trial / calculation comparison is the time of effort from which steels plasticize, i.e. 1,144 kN.m in the trial. The experimental and numerical responses are confronted V6.03.180-fig-test1_moment_deplacement:

*Test #1: Answer moment vs. Move*

The moment of plasification is very well predicted, with only 2% d-difference (1 163 kN.m vs. 1 144 kN.m). On the other hand, the numerical response is a little more rigid than the test, which results in a difference on the displacement of 30% (29.38 vs. 41.89 mm).
Some elements explanation has been considered, but not yet conclusive:
* Uncertainties on material parameters
* Restraint steel Modelling – concrete (perfect grip)
* Modelling of support conditions
* Displacement measurement uncertainties (stiffness of the support and test machine)

The reason that seems most relevant at the moment is the modelling of pins that drastically alter the overall response depending on their presence or their restraint with the frames.

### 3.2 Damage mapping

## 7 Summary of the results

Two tests carried out in the SCIENCE project were numerically simulated: a reinforced concrete beam subjected to a purely mechanical 4 point bending load and an identical beam, previously subjected to a heat transient heating and then bending 4 points. Different Modelling configurations were examined based on the choices of mesh, convergence criteria, resolution methods, constitutive law, load representation and boundary conditions, etc. The results are visible in note 6125-1723-2023-00259.
