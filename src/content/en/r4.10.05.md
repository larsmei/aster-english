# r4.10.05 Residual error indicator for the HM modellings

**Summary**

In this document, one presents the a posteriori error indicators developed for the HM modellings. The a posteriori estimation concerned is of explicit residual type. One first gives a working framework for the a posteriori study of these problems. One then presents the families of error indicators for the two types of problem and one states the theoretical results of reliability and of optimality which guarantee the validity of the indicators. The proofs of the results stated will not be given; the interested reader may consult [1] for that purpose.

> **Contents**

## 1 Working framework

In this paragraph, one recalls the equations of the HM problem. One then proposes a non-dimensionalisation of them which will be useful for the a posteriori error analysis. The reader is advised to have previously consulted documents [2,3,4] for more details on the establishment of the equations given in this section and on their solution in Code_Aster.

### 1.1 The continuous model problem

The working framework is that of linear poroelasticity. One considers a porous medium $\Omega$ , of boundary $\Gamma$ , saturated by liquid water, with a linear elastic mechanical behaviour. The behaviour of the fluid influences that of the skeleton and conversely. The spatial dimension is denoted $\text{dim}$ . One solves a mechanical equilibrium problem (momentum balance for the skeleton and the fluid) and a hydraulic evolution problem (fluid mass balance). One recalls that the treatment of these two problems is fully coupled in Code_Aster. The unknowns of the problem are the displacement $u$ and the hydraulic pressure $p$ .

Given a simulation time $T$ , the mechanical equilibrium is formulated

$-\nabla \cdot {\sigma}^{'}(u)+b\nabla p=f$ in $[\text{0,T}]\times \Omega$ **eq 1.1-1**

```text
où on a posé:math:`f={\rho}_{\text{ref}}{F}^{m}`et
```

| ${\sigma}^{'}$ | Effective stress tensor |
| --- | --- |
| $b$ | Biot coefficient |
| ${\rho}_{\text{ref}}$ | Homogenised density |
| ${F}^{m}$ | Gravity force |

In the framework of isotropic linear elasticity, the effective stress tensor admits the expression

${\sigma}^{'}(u)={\lambda}_{1}(\nabla \cdot u)\text{Id}+{\lambda}_{2}(\nabla u+\nabla {u}^{t})$ **eq 1.1-2**

where ${\lambda}_{1}$ and ${\lambda}_{2}$ are the Lamé coefficients. The hydraulic equation, for its part, comes from writing the conservation of the fluid mass in the course of time and from Darcy's law. Darcy's law supplies a proportionality relation between the hydraulic flux ${M}_{\text{lq}}$ and the pressure gradient. More precisely, it is written

> $\frac{{M}_{\text{lq}}}{\rho}=\kappa (-\nabla p+\rho {F}^{m})$ **eq 1.1-3**

The hydraulic equation is written

${\partial}_{t}(\frac{1}{M}p+b\nabla \cdot u)-\nabla \cdot (\kappa \nabla p)=g$ in $s[\text{0,T}]\times \Omega$ **eq 1.1-4**

where $1/M=(b-\varphi )/{K}_{s}$ and $g=-\rho \nabla \cdot (\kappa {F}^{m})$ .

| $\varphi$ | Lagrangian porosity |
| --- | --- |
| $\rho$ | Hydraulic density |
| $\kappa$ | Hydraulic conductivity |
| ${K}_{s}$ | Compressibility of the solid grains |

```text
L’équation [:ref:`1.1-1 <1.1-1>`] couplée avec [:ref:`1.1-4 <1.1-4>`] constitue la version transitoiredu problème HM.
```

The boundary conditions are of mixed Dirichlet/Neumann type on the displacement and on the pressure. One considers a partition of the boundary in the form $\Gamma ={\Gamma}_{D}^{H}\cup {\Gamma}_{N}^{H}={\Gamma}_{D}^{M}\cup {\Gamma}_{N}^{M}$ .

On ${\Gamma}_{D}^{H}$ , one imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions in hydraulics: $p={p}_{D}$ .

On ${\Gamma}_{N}^{H}$ , one imposes Neumann boundary conditions in hydraulics: ${M}_{\text{lq}}\cdot n={M}_{\text{lq},\text{nor}}$ .

On ${\Gamma}_{D}^{M}$ , one imposes Dirichlet boundary conditions in mechanics: $u={u}_{D}$ .

On ${\Gamma}_{N}^{M}$ , one imposes Neumann boundary conditions in mechanics: $({\sigma}^{'}(u)-\text{bp}{\chi }_{S}\text{Id})\cdot n={\sigma}_{\text{nor}}$ , where one has denoted ${\Gamma}_{S}={\Gamma}_{N}^{M}\cap {\Gamma}_{N}^{H}$ , with characteristic function ${\chi }_{S}$ , the portion of the boundary where the stresses and the hydraulic flux are imposed.

### 1.2 Non-dimensionalisation

The model problem involves two unknowns, the displacements and the liquid pressure, whose orders of magnitude may be very different. One therefore proposes a scaling of the equations constituting the model problem, which will serve as the starting point for the a posteriori error analysis. Attention is drawn to the fact that this scaling is not used in the numerical solution stage. The a posteriori error estimates of section 2 will therefore be obtained on the non-dimensionalised problem, then re-dimensionalised with a view to their use in Code_Aster.

For any variable $a$ , one denotes by ${a}^{\text{*}}$ the corresponding non-dimensional variable and by $A$ the corresponding characteristic quantity. For example, for the hydraulic pressure, one has the relation

$p={\text{Pp}}^{\text{*}}$ ,

where $P$ denotes a characteristic pressure. One uses the same symbolism for the mathematical operators. Thus, one has: ${\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {u}^{\text{*}}=\frac{L}{U}\nabla \cdot u$ , ${\varepsilon}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})=\frac{L}{U}\varepsilon (u)$ , ${\nabla}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}=\frac{L}{P}\nabla p$ .

The mechanical equation is rewritten

$-{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {\sigma}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={f}^{\text{*}}$ **eq 1.2-1**

with ${f}^{\text{*}}=\frac{L}{P}f$ . Denoting by $\nu$ Poisson's ratio, ${\sigma}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})$ is a non-dimensional version of the stress tensor given by

${\sigma}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})=\frac{\nu}{(1-2\nu )(1+2\nu )}({\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {u}^{\text{*}})\text{Id}+\frac{1}{1+\nu }{\varepsilon}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})$ **eq 1.2-2**

One proposes the following scaling strategy. The user chooses two quantities: the length scale $L$ and the pressure scale $P$ . The length scale $L$ is determined by the geometry of the model. The pressure scale is determined by the Dirichlet boundary conditions imposed on the pressure or, possibly, by the initial conditions. For the mechanical part, one imposes

$U=\frac{\text{LP}}{E}$ **eq 1.2-3**

Furthermore, the hydraulic equation is written, taking [1.2-3] into account,

${\partial}_{t}^{\text{*}}(\frac{E}{M}{p}^{\text{*}}+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {u}^{\text{*}})-\frac{E}{M}{\Delta}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={g}^{\text{*}}$ **eq 1.2-4**

```text
   avec :math:`{g}^{\text{*}}=\frac{E}{M}\frac{{L}^{2}}{\kappa P}g`.

La version adimensionnée du problème HM transitoire estdonc:
```

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}-{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {\sigma}^{\text{*}}({u}^{\text{*}})+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={f}^{\text{*}}\text{dans}[0,{T}^{\text{*}}]\times {\Omega}^{\text{*}}\\ {\partial}_{t}^{\text{*}}(\frac{E}{M}{p}^{\text{*}}+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {u}^{\text{*}})-\frac{E}{M}{\Delta}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={g}^{\text{*}}\text{dans}[0,{T}^{\text{*}}]\times {\Omega}^{\text{*}}\end{array}$ **eq 1.2-5**

### 1.3 Finite-element discretisation and notation

The HM problem is discretised by a finite-element method based on a mesh ${({T}_{h})}_{h>0}$ . One recalls that the displacements are discretised by polynomials of degree 2 and the pressure by polynomials of degree 1. One denotes the discrete displacements ${u}_{h}$ and the discrete pressure ${p}_{h}$ .

One denotes by ${F}_{h}^{i}$ the set of the interior faces of the mesh and by ${F}_{h}^{\partial}$ the set of the faces located on the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ . For a given mesh cell $K\in {T}_{h}$ , one denotes by ${F}_{K}$ the set of the faces of $K$ and one sets

${F}_{K}^{i}={F}_{h}^{i}\cap {F}_{K},{F}_{K}^{\partial}={F}_{h}^{\partial}\cap {F}_{K}$

Let $F\in {F}_{h}^{i}$ , that is to say such that there exist two mesh cells ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ in ${T}_{h}$ with $F={K}_{1}\cap {K}_{2}$ . One denotes by ${n}_{{K}_{1}}$ and ${n}_{{K}_{2}}$ the outward normals to ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ , respectively (see figure 1). One denotes, for almost every $x\in F$ ,

$\left[\Phi ({u}_{h})\cdot n\right](x)={(\Phi ({u}_{h}))}_{\mid {K}_{1}}(x)\cdot {n}_{{K}_{1}}+{(\Phi ({u}_{h}))}_{\mid {K}_{2}}(x)\cdot {n}_{{K}_{2}}$

the jump of the normal component of $\Phi ({u}_{h})$ across $F$ .

![Figure](/figures/r4.10.05/Object_82.svg)

*Figure 1: Example of an interior face of the mesh.*

One observes that, as the vectors ${n}_{{K}_{1}}$ and ${n}_{{K}_{2}}$ are opposite, the quantity above does indeed define this jump.

For any mesh cell $K$ , one denotes by ${\Delta}_{K}$ the set of the mesh cells sharing an edge with $K$ , including the mesh cell $K$ , as illustrated in figure 2.

![Figure](/figures/r4.10.05/Object_89.svg)

*Figure 2: Example of a mesh cell* $K$ *and of a macro-element* ${\Delta}_{K}$

For the time discretisation of the transient problem, one considers an implicit Euler scheme and one denotes by ${\left\lbrace {t}_{i}\right\rbrace }_{i=1}^{N}$ a discretisation of the interval $\left[0,T\right]$ . One denotes by ${u}_{h\tau }$ (respectively ${p}_{h\tau }$ ) the function, continuous and piecewise affine in time, such that for every $n\in \left\lbrace 0,...,N\right\rbrace$ , ${u}_{\mathrm{h\tau }}({t}_{n})={u}_{h}^{n}$ (respectively ${p}_{h\tau }({t}_{n})={p}_{h}^{n}$ ). One also needs to consider functions which are piecewise constant in time, namely ${\pi}^{0}{p}_{\mathit{h\tau }}$ equal to ${p}_{h}^{n}$ on $\left[{t}_{n-1},{t}_{n}\right]$ . For every $n\in \left\lbrace 1,...,N\right\rbrace$ , one sets ${\tau}_{n}={t}_{n}-{t}_{n-1}$ and ${I}_{n}=\left[{t}_{n-1},{t}_{n}\right]$ .

One also denotes the discrete “derivatives” of the displacements and of the pressure

$\begin{array}{c}{\delta}_{t}{u}_{h}^{n}={\tau}_{n}^{-1}({u}_{h}^{n}-{u}_{h}^{n-1})\\ {\delta}_{t}{p}_{h}^{n}={\tau}_{n}^{-1}({p}_{h}^{n}-{p}_{h}^{n-1})\end{array}$

The notation $x<y$ means that there exists a constant $c>0$ independent of the mesh such that $x\le \text{cy}$ . The notation ${\parallel \cdot \parallel }_{V}$ denotes a norm on a space $V$ . One introduces its version ${\parallel \cdot \parallel }_{V,K}$ , localised to the mesh cell $K$ , by ${\mathrm{\parallel }\cdot \mathrm{\parallel }}_{V}^{2}=\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}{\mathrm{\parallel }\cdot \mathrm{\parallel }}_{V,K}^{2}$ .

## 2 Study of the transient problem

One recalls the expression of the continuous problem of interest. Find $({u}^{\text{*}},{p}^{\text{*}})$ such that

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}-{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {\sigma}^{\text{'*}}({u}^{\text{*}})+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={f}^{\text{*}}\text{dans}[0,{T}^{\text{*}}]\times {\Omega}^{\text{*}}\\ {\partial}_{t}^{\text{*}}(\frac{E}{M}{p}^{\text{*}}+b{\nabla}^{\text{*}}\cdot {u}^{\text{*}})-\frac{E}{M}{\Delta}^{\text{*}}{p}^{\text{*}}={g}^{\text{*}}\text{dans}[0,{T}^{\text{*}}]\times {\Omega}^{\text{*}}\end{array}$ **eq 3.1-1**

In the thesis [1], 2 families of error indicators for this problem were proposed. Only one has been implemented in Code_Aster, making it possible to evaluate the error on the pressure efficiently.

One defines the error estimators in space:

- Estimator for the hydraulic equation: for every $m\in \left[1,N\right]$ ,

$\begin{array}{c}{\tau}_{m}{E}_{p,0}^{m}=\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}{\tau}_{m}{E}_{p,0,K}^{m}\\ =\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}({\tau}_{m}{h}_{K}^{2}\frac{{E}^{2}}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}\mathrm{\kappa M}}{\parallel \frac{1}{M}{\delta}_{t}{p}_{h}^{m}+b\nabla \cdot ({\delta}_{t}{u}_{h}^{m})\parallel }_{0,K}^{2}+{\tau}_{m}{h}_{K}\frac{{E}^{2}}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}{\text{κρ}}^{2}M}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{i}}{\parallel \left[{M}_{\text{lq},h}^{m}\cdot n\right]\parallel }_{0,F}^{2}\\ +{\tau}_{m}{h}_{K}\frac{{E}^{2}}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}{\text{κρ}}^{2}M}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{\partial}\cap {\Gamma}_{N}^{H}}{\parallel {M}_{\text{lq},\text{nor}}^{m}-{M}_{\text{lq},h}^{m}\cdot n\parallel }_{0,F}^{2})\end{array}$

- Estimators for the mechanical equation: for every $m\in \left[1,N\right]$ ,

$\begin{array}{c}{E}_{u}^{m}=\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}{E}_{u,K}^{m}\\ =\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}({h}_{K}^{2}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}{\parallel {f}^{m}+\nabla \cdot {\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m})\parallel }_{0,K}^{2}+{h}_{K}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{i}}{\parallel \left[{\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m})\cdot n\right]\parallel }_{0,F}^{2}\\ +{h}_{K}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{\partial}\cap {\Gamma}_{N}^{M}}{\parallel {\sigma}_{\text{nor}}^{m}-({\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m})\cdot n-{\text{bp}}_{h}^{m}n)\parallel }_{0,F}^{2})\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{c}{E}_{u}^{m}({\delta}_{t})=\sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}{E}_{u,K}^{m}({\delta}_{t})\\ \sum_{K\in {T}_{h}}({h}_{K}^{2}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}{\parallel {f}^{m}-{f}^{m-1}+\nabla \cdot {\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m}-{u}_{h}^{m-1})-b\nabla ({p}_{h}^{m}-{p}_{h}^{m-1})\parallel }_{0,K}^{2}\\ +{h}_{K}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{i}}{\parallel \left[{\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m}-{u}_{h}^{m-1})\cdot n\right]\parallel }_{0,F}^{2}\\ +{h}_{K}\frac{1}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}}\sum_{F\in {F}_{K}^{\partial}\cap {\Gamma}_{N}^{M}}{\parallel {\sigma}_{\text{nor}}^{m}-{\sigma}_{\text{nor}}^{m-1}-({\sigma}^{'}({u}_{h}^{m}-{u}_{h}^{m-1})\cdot n-b({p}_{h}^{m}-{p}_{h}^{m-1})n)\parallel }_{0,F}^{2}\end{array}$

One defines the estimator in time

${E}_{\text{tim}}^{m}={\tau}_{m}\frac{E}{{P}^{2}{L}^{\text{dim}}{\rho}^{2}\kappa }{\parallel {M}_{\text{lq},h}^{m}-{M}_{\text{lq},h}^{m-1}\parallel }_{0,\Omega }^{2}$

One has the following properties:

**Theorem 1**  (Reliability) For every $n\in \left[1,N\right]$ ,

${\int}_{0}^{{t}_{n}}{\parallel (p-{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{d}^{2}+{\int}_{0}^{{t}_{n}}{\parallel (p-{\pi}^{0}{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{d}^{2}<\sum_{m=1}^{N}{\tau}_{m}{E}_{p,0}^{m}+\underset{0\le m\le N}{\sup}{E}_{u}^{m}+{(\sum_{m=1}^{N}{({E}_{u}^{m}({\delta}_{t}))}^{1/2})}^{2}+\sum_{m=1}^{N}{E}_{\text{tim}}^{m}$

The operator ${\pi}^{0}$ denotes the projection operator onto the functions which are piecewise constant in time, namely ${\pi}^{0}{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }}$ equal to ${p}_{h}^{n}$ on ${I}_{n}$ for every $n\in \left\lbrace 1,\cdots ,N\right\rbrace$ .

**Theorem 2**  (Optimality of the time indicator)

One has the following estimate

${E}_{\text{tim}}=\sum_{m=1}^{N}{E}_{\text{tim}}^{m}<{\int}_{0}^{T}{\parallel (p-{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{d}^{2}\text{ds}+{\int}_{0}^{T}{\parallel (p-{\pi}^{0}{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{d}^{2}\text{ds}$

**Theorem 3**  (Optimality of the space indicators) For every $K\in {T}_{h}$ , one has

$\begin{array}{c}{E}_{u,K}^{m}<\sum_{{K}^{'}\in {\Delta}_{K}}\left[{h}_{K}^{2}{\parallel {f}^{m}-{f}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{0,{K}^{'}}+{\parallel {u}^{m}-{u}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{a,{K}^{'}}^{2}+{\parallel {p}^{m}-{p}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{0,{T}^{'}}^{2}\right]\\ {E}_{u,K}^{m}({\delta}_{t})<\sum_{{K}^{'}\in {\Delta}_{K}}{\tau}_{m}^{2}\left[{h}_{K}^{2}{\parallel {\delta}_{t}{f}^{m}-{\delta}_{t}{f}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{0,{K}^{'}}+{\parallel {\delta}_{t}{u}^{m}-{\delta}_{t}{u}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{a,{K}^{'}}^{2}+{\parallel {\delta}_{t}{p}^{m}-{\delta}_{t}{p}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{0,{T}^{'}}^{2}\right]\\ {\tau}_{m}{E}_{p,0,K}^{m}<\sum_{{K}^{'}\in {\Delta}_{K}}{h}_{K}^{2}{\int}_{{I}_{m}}[{\parallel (g-{\pi}^{0}{g}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{0,{K}^{'}}^{2}\text{ds}+{\tau}_{m}^{2}{\parallel {\delta}_{t}{u}^{m}-{\delta}_{t}{u}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{a,{K}^{'}}\\ +{\tau}_{m}^{2}{\parallel {\delta}_{t}{p}^{m}-{\delta}_{t}{p}_{h}^{m}\parallel }_{0,{K}^{'}}^{2}+{h}_{T}^{-2}{\parallel (p-{\pi}^{0}{p}_{\mathrm{h\tau }})(s)\parallel }_{d,{K}^{'}}^{2}]\text{ds}\end{array}$

## 3 Use in Code_Aster

The computation of the time indicators is triggered in STAT_NON_LINE by the keyword ERRE_TEMPS_THM=’OUI’ under the factor keyword CRIT_QUALITE. It makes it possible to compute the quantities ERRE_TPS_LOC, ${E}_{\text{tim}}^{m}$ , and ERRE_TPS_GLOB, $\sum_{m=1}^{N}{E}_{\text{tim}}^{m}$ .

The computation of the space indicators is triggered in CALC_ERREUR by the option 'ERME_ELEM'.

Example of an extract from the command file:

> RESU[k]=CALC_ERREUR(reuse =RESU[k],

RESULTAT=RESU[k],

LIST_INST=LINST,

OPTION=('ERME_ELEM','ERME_ELNO',),);

dico = RESU[k].LIST_PARA()

print dico['ERRE_MEC']

## 4 Conclusion – Prospects

The estimators for the HM modellings come to complete the substantial range of space estimators existing in Code_Aster. For the first time, time indicators make it possible to quantify the error in the time discretisation. The prospects of this work are of several kinds:

1. Extend the scope of use of the HM estimators to the non-linear modellings;
2. Develop estimators for the THHM modellings in general;
3. Set up a procedure for cutting the time steps on the basis of the time estimators. For the moment, only the numerical values of the time estimators are supplied in STAT_NON_LINE, without being connected to a mechanism for adapting the time discretisation.
