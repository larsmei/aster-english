# v7.01.100 HPLA100 - Hollow thermoelastic cylinder with uniform rotation

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to test the second members corresponding to the effects of gravity, thermal expansion and acceleration due to uniform rotation. For modellings $C$ and $D$ ($\mathrm{3D}$ hull), a chain thermo-elastic calculation and a thermoelastical calculation without plastic evolution were performed.

The results for modellings are presented:

- $\mathrm{2D}$ axisymmetric: elements isoparametric finished axisymmetric on mesh cells QUAD8,
- axisymmetric hulls: elements axisymmetric isoparametric finished on mesh cells SEG3 (mesh meridian section linear),
- $\mathrm{3D}$ hulls: elements finished MEC3QU9H, MEC3TR7H on mesh cells QUAD9 and TRIA7, respectively,
- $\mathrm{DKT}$ plates: elements finished DKQ planes, DKT on mesh cells QUAD4 and TRIA3 respectively. DEFI_COMPOSITE orthotropic shells are also tested for two layers of the same isotropic material and ELAS_COQU key word for the assignment of homogenized plate characteristics.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

- Displacement:

- $\Omega =1.0{s}^{-1}$ according to $\mathrm{OZ}$ axis

- Charge imposed:

- gravity, $g=10.0{\mathit{m.s}}^{-2}$ according to $\mathrm{OZ}$ axis
- traction force on the upper side: $-160.0{E}^{-4}N$ equivalent to a force distributed over $\mathrm{CD}$ of $-8.0{E}^{-4}{\mathrm{N.mm}}^{-1}$

- Thermal dilation: $T(\rho )-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}(\rho )=\frac{({T}_{s}+{T}_{i})}{2}+\frac{({T}_{s}-{T}_{i}).(r-R)}{h}$ with:

- case 1: ${T}_{s}=0.5°C,{T}_{i}=-0.5°C,{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.0°C$
- case 2: ${T}_{s}=0.1°C,{T}_{i}=0.1°C,{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}=0.0°C$

These temperature fields are calculated with THER_LINEAIRE, using a stationary calculation on the same mesh, but with a PLAN model to have a solution affine in thickness.

The boundary conditions on the move (and rotation) being different according to the model considered, they will be described later (in the paragraphs relating to modellings).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The calculation method used for reference solution was determined by F. Voldoire (EDF R&D / AMA) and is presented in the Appendix.

The analytical reference results are:

- movements and rotations,
- axial stress, generalized forces (in hull theory),

in internal and external skins on $\mathrm{AB}$ and $\mathrm{CD}$ sections.

In 2D axisymmetric, the complete solutions given in the annex are such that ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{rz}}=0$ where $r$ and $z$ are the radial and axial directions of the cylinder, respectively. For uniform rotation and thermal expansion loads, boundary conditions are chosen so that the solutions do not depend on $z$ (one has ${\varepsilon}_{zz}=0$ in particular).

For hulls, with equivalent boundary conditions, ${\theta}_{\theta}$ rotation around the orthoradial axis is zero for uniform rotation and thermal expansion loads, which is not the case for gravity loading where the rotation is constant (the cylinder then becomes conical). However, in all cases the transverse distortion is zero; thus Love-Kirchhoff's theories and Hencky-Mindlin's theories provide the same reference solution.

### 2.2 Reference quantity

> $\mathrm{DX}$ : $\mathrm{OX}$ axis displacement ,
> $\mathrm{DY}$ : $\mathrm{OY}$ axis displacement ,
> $\mathrm{DZ}$ : $\mathrm{OZ}$ axis displacement ,
> $\mathrm{NXX}$: normal force on $\mathrm{OX}$ axis,
> $\mathrm{NYY}$: normal force on $\mathrm{OY}$ axis,
> $\mathrm{MXX}$ : moment around the $\mathrm{OX}$ axis ,
> $\mathrm{MYY}$ : moment around the $\mathrm{OY}$ axis ,
> $\mathrm{SIXX}$: $\mathrm{OX}$ axis stress,
> $\mathrm{SIYY}$: stress on $\mathrm{OY}$ axis.

## 14 Summary of the results

The very good results for modellings $A$ and $B$ are due to the fact that the reference solutions belong to the space generated by the elements finished chosen. Only the numerical rounding errors remain.

The modellings of plates and hulls in $C,D,E,F,G,H,I,J$ and $K$ space were satisfactorily calculated. For $C,D,E,F,G,H,I,J$ and $K$ space, a chained thermo-elastic calculation was performed. The DKT ($E,F,G,H,I,J$) Modelling results show that elements quadrangles have a better behavior than elements triangles. It is necessary to have a sufficiently fine discretization with these elements planes so that they can properly model the circular geometry of the cylindrical hull. Indeed, discretizing the cylinder geometry by plane or parabolic facets is not consistent and induces a parasitic bending that decreases with the fineness of mesh. Thus, a multiplication of the number of d的elements by two on the height of the structure causes the relative maximum error to fall from 5.48% (case presented here) to 2.8%. The results with COQUE_3D ($C$ and $D$) modelling are very good except for gravity with the triangle gravity.

Calculations with DKTG($K$ ) Modelling give the same results as with DKT Modelling.
