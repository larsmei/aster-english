# v9.01.100 SZLZ100 - Fatigue on a Decent Cycle

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to calculate damage from a stress loading history and then from a deformation loading history.

From a simple loading history defined by DEFI_FONCTION, the elementary cycles are extracted by the RCCM [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01) cycle counting method, then the elementary damage associated with each cycle is calculated by interpolation on the Wöhler curve of the material (if load history in stress and interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material) (if load history in deformations).

The Wöhler curve is defined by DEFI_FONCTION. L-interpolation is logarithmic in number of cycles at $N$ rupture and on $\mathit{Salt}$ alternating stress.

The Manson-Coffin curve is also defined by DEFI_FONCTION.

Finally, the total damage to the piece is determined by cumulating all the elementary damage by the Miner linear rule.

In this test, the value of the average stress on the calculation of the elemental damage is also checked by the Wöhler method. A first calculation is performed without correction, a second with a correction by Gerber and the third with a correction by Goodman.

In this simple example, extraction of elementary cycles and calculation of damage can be done manually, applying the algorithms presented in reference document [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01).

The results provided by the POST_FATIGUE operator are therefore very satisfactory.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The analysis is to determine the damage to a piece in an point to which the loading history is supplied.

The stress loading history is considered to be the test of the Wöhler method and the elementary cycles are extracted by a cycle counting method, which is the RCCM method in this test. The elementary damage due to each elemental cycle is then calculated by interpolation on the Wöhler curve of the material.

The Wöhler curve is filled as a point function by point, which gives the value of the number of cycles at break as a function of the $\mathrm{Salt}=\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}$ alternating stress.

Interpolation is logarithmic on the abscisse and the abscisse and is allowed to extend linearly this function to the right and left.

Three different calls to the POST_FATIGUE operator allow for the average stress of each elemental cycle to be taken into account.

The correction adopted is that of the Haigh diagram, either to the right of Goodman or to the parable of Gerber [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01).

Total damage is determined by the Miner linear cumulation rule.

The Manson-Coffin method of calculating damage is tested by considering the deformation loading history and extracting the elementary cycles by a cycle counting method, which is the RCCM method in this test. The elementary damage due to each elemental cycle is then calculated by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

The Manson-Coffin curve is provided as an point function by point, which gives the value of the number of cycles at breakage as a function of $\frac{\Delta \varepsilon }{2}$.

Total damage is determined by the Miner linear cumulation rule.
