# v4.22.101 TTNL101 - Nonlinear thermal source in a bar

**Summary:**

This test checks the thermal calculation in the presence of a nonlinear source loading, depending on the temperature.

The reference solution is analytical and variable in time and space. The part considered in the three modellings is a symmetrical bar composed of molten elements.

The following mesh cellss are checked:

- Modelling A:

  - QUAD4, QUAD8, QUAD9 quadrangles for AXIS Modelling
  - QUAD4 quadrangles for AXIS_DIAG Modelling
- Modelling B:

  - HEXA8 hexahedrons for 3D_DIAG modelling
- Modelization C:

  - TRIA3 triangles for AXIS Modelling
  - TRIA3 triangles for AXIS_DIAG Modelling
- Modelling D:

  - QUAD9 quadrangles for linear AXIS_HHO Modelling
- Modelling E:

  - HEXA8 hexahedrons for quadratic 3D_HHO Modelling
- Modelling F:

  - QUAD9 quadrangles for constant AXIS_HHO Modelling
- Modelling G:

  - QUAD9 quadrangles for cubic AXIS_HHO Modelling
- Modelling H:

  - QUAD9 quadrangles for AXIS_HHO quartic Modelling

Both ends of the bar are subject to the default adabadicity conditions. The heat source is a linear function of temperature.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A one-dimensional structure (a bar whose side faces are subject to adiabatic conditions) of $\mathrm{2L}$ length occupying the $[-L;L]$ domain is considered.

Since the temperature is homogeneous in the normal directions at the bar, the calculation can be considered as 1D.

### 1.2 Material properties

| $\lambda =2$ | thermal conductivity |
| --- | --- |
| $\rho C=2$ | volumetric heat |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Nonlinear volume source loading, temperature function:

$s(T)=2-2\times w\times T$ with $w=2$

Boundary conditions are adiabatic on side faces and temperature-type imposed zero at the end of the bar; a symmetry condition is implemented on the symmetry plane (equivalent to an adiabatic boundary condition).

The $[0.;1.]$ time range is discretized in 100 time step (duration of each time step equal to $0.01$).

### 1.4 Initial conditions

An initial analytical state is provided. See developments in the paragraph detailing the reference solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The bar is subjected to a heat source $r(T)={r}_{0}-{r}_{1}T$ , where ${r}_{1}>0$ for thermal stability issues. Its initial temperature is ${T}_{0}(x)$ and the ends of the bar are kept at zero temperature. The temperature evolution follows the heat equation:

$\rho c\dot{T}=\lambda {\nabla}^{2}T+r(T)$ ; $T(x,0)={T}_{0}(x)$ ; $T(-L,t)=T(L,t)=0$

Standardization can be reduced without loss of generality to the following equation:

$\frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=\frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial {x}^{2}}+1-{\omega}^{2}u$ ; $u(x,0)={u}_{0}(x)$ ; $u(-1,t)=u(1,t)=0$

To solve this equation, we first look at the asymptotic solution ${u}_{\infty}(x)$ which checks:

$0=\frac{{\partial}^{2}{u}_{\infty}}{\partial {x}^{2}}+1-{\omega}^{2}{u}_{\infty}$ ; ${u}_{\infty}(-1)={u}_{\infty}(1)=0$

The solution of this linear differential equation of the second order is:

${u}_{\infty}(x)=\frac{1}{{\omega}^{2}}(1-\frac{\cosh\omega x}{\cosh\omega })$

The solution of the transient equation is then obtained by projection of $v=u-{u}_{\infty}$ on the Laplacian's own functions on $\left\rbrace -1,1\right\lbrace$. To simplify the analysis, an initial condition ${u}_{0}$ equal to the first mode of its own is adopted, namely:

${u}_{0}(x)={u}_{\infty}(x)-\cos\frac{\pi x}{2}$

Only the first mode is activated, so we are reduced to the resolution of a differential equation in first order, to finally obtain the solution:

$u(x,t)={u}_{\infty}(x)-\exp(-{\omega}^{2}t-\frac{{\pi}^{2}}{4}t)\cos\frac{\pi x}{2}$

Finally, as before, we go back from $u$ to $T$ by adopting a set of specific parameters, without taking into account the units, so that $T=u$. For this, we take $\lambda ={r}_{0}=\rho c$, ${r}_{1}={\omega}^{2}{r}_{0}$, $L=1$ and ${T}_{0}(x)={u}_{0}(x)$.
