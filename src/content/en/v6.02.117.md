# v6.02.117 SSNL117 - Elastoplastic flexion cover

**Summary:**

This test validates the Modelling of the ovalization phenomena in the pipework in the elastoplastic domain with elements TUYAU_3M: an elbow, extended by straight pipes is subjected to bending in its plane. The pipework is thick (similar to the elbows of the primary circuits). The reference solution is numerical: it is obtained with *Code_Aster* using mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ of the elbow (modelling C).

The first two modellingss are used to validate elements TUYAU_3M (with straight and 3 nodes straight and 3 nodes for A Modelling and 4 nodes straight and 4 nodes for B Modelling) in elastoplasticity.

In modellings B and C, a "global" rotation term, developed by EDF, CEA and FRAMATOME [bib2], for earthquake piping, is introduced via a Python macro-command.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Coved pipework in the $\mathrm{XY}$ plane . The straight parts are $L=1m$ length .

The elbow has a radius of curvature: ${R}_{c}=1.25m$

The tubular section has for medium ${r}_{\mathit{moy}}=395.5\mathit{mm}$ radius and for $e=\mathrm{77mm}$ thickness.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is elastoplastic with linear isotropic nutring.

$E=2.E11\mathrm{Pa}$

$\nu =0.3$

Limit of elasticity $\mathrm{SIGY}={200.10}^{6}\mathrm{Pa}$

$\text{D\_SIGM\_EPSI}={2.10}^{10}\mathrm{Pa}$ Hardening Module

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Clamped support in $A$ (degrees of freedom of beam blocked, but degrees of freedom of free ovalization).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Comparison with other numerical results obtained with *Code_Aster* (version 14.2 [bib1]) with an mesh $3D$ of the elbow and straight parts, linked to the extremities of straight beams (modelization C). This mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ has 1500 mesh cells HEXA20. Modelling of the elbow in elements COQUE_3D gave results comparable to the $\mathrm{3D}$ calculation (see [§2.2]).

Model B uses the **Plasticity** type MFront behavior instead of VMIS_ISOT_LINE behavior (reference 3D calculation).

Useful quantities for seismic maintenance are also calculated using the following formulae:

$$
\mathit{EN}={\epsilon}_{xx}
$$

$$
\mathit{ET}=\frac{{r}_{\mathit{moy}}\times {\gamma}_{\mathit{torsion}}}{2}
$$

$$
\mathit{EFY}={r}_{\mathit{moy}}\times {\kappa}_{y}
$$

$$
\mathit{ESTAR}=\sqrt{{\mathit{EN}}^{2}+{\mathit{ET}}^{2}+{\left(\frac{\pi \times \mathit{EFY}}{4}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{\pi \times \mathit{EFZ}}{4}\right)}^{2}}
$$

Calculation of the second type of quantities for the earthquake:

$$
\lambda =\frac{e\times {R}_{c}}{{r}_{\mathit{moy}}^{2}}
$$

et

$$
k2=max\left(1,\frac{1,65}{\lambda}\right)
$$

With:

$$
{\mathit{EFY}}_{2}=\frac{{r}_{\mathit{moy}}\times {\kappa}_{y}}{k2}
$$

Et:

$$
{\mathit{ESTAR}}_{2}=\sqrt{{\mathit{EN}}^{2}+{\mathit{ET}}^{2}+{\left(\frac{\pi \times \gamma \times \mathit{EFY}}{4}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{\pi \times \gamma \times \mathit{EFZ}}{4}\right)}^{2}}
$$

### 4.3 Calculation of the term "Global" Rotation

This "global" rotation term was developed as part of a tripartite action EDF‐CEA‐FRAMATOME [bib2], with a view to future integration into the RCC-M dimensioning code.

It is expressed from the rotations of two representative points of the elbow (inlet and outlet), by:

${R}_{G}=\sqrt{\Delta {R}_{x}^{2}+\Delta {R}_{y}^{2}+\Delta {R}_{z}^{2}}$

où

$\begin{array}{}\Delta {R}_{x}={\mathrm{DRX}}_{\mathrm{sortiecoude}}-{\mathrm{DRX}}_{\mathrm{entréecoude}}\\ \Delta {R}_{y}={\mathrm{DRY}}_{\mathrm{sortiecoude}}-{\mathrm{DRY}}_{\mathrm{entréecoude}}\\ \Delta {R}_{z}={\mathrm{DRZ}}_{\mathrm{sortiecoude}}-{\mathrm{DRZ}}_{\mathrm{entréecoude}}\end{array}$

This term is calculated by the Python MACR_ROTA_GLOBALE macro-command which is integrated into the body of the command file. The result of this macro-command is an Aster function of the overall rotation depending on the instant. A test of non-regression validates this function.

## 6 Summary of the results

Since reference solution is not an analytical but is numerical (obtained by a 3D model), the differences observed (from $\text{1\%}$ to $\text{3\%}$) can be considered reasonable. To obtain a better match between 3D and TUYAU solutions, the straight parts should be modeled for a longer length, and a finer mesh for each modellings should be adopted. This was not done in the context of this test, to keep the run times reasonable.
