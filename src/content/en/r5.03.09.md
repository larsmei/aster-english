# r5.03.09 Non-linear 1D constitutive relations

**Summary:**

This document describes the quantities computed by the operator STAT_NON_LINE which are necessary for the implementation of the quasi-static non-linear algorithm described in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) in the case of unidimensional elastoplastic or viscoplastic constitutive behaviours. These behaviours, unless otherwise stated, are applicable to BARRE elements, to beam elements and multifibre beams (axial direction only) and to concrete reinforcement elements (modelling GRILLE).

The constitutive behaviours described in this document are:

- the Von Mises behaviour with linear isotropic hardening: VMIS_ISOT_LINE, and arbitrary VMIS_ISOT_TRAC,
- the Von Mises behaviour with linear kinematic hardening: VMIS_CINE_LINE,
- the Von Mises behaviour with linear hardening, non-symmetric in tension and compression, with restoration of the centre of the elastic domain: VMIS_ASYM_LINE. The latter was developed in order to model the action of the soil on Gas-Insulated Cables,
- the viscoplastic behaviours with an irradiation effect: VISC_IRRA_LOG, GRAN_IRRA_LOG.
- the MAZARS behaviour in its $\mathrm{1D}$ version. The $\mathrm{1D}$ version of the Mazars model makes it possible to account for the restoration of stiffness in the event of crack closure.
- the behaviour for modelling the relaxation of prestressed cables.

The solution is carried out, case by case, by an implicit integration method, unless otherwise stated; from the previous analysis time instant, one computes the stress field resulting from a strain increment, and the tangent behaviour which makes it possible to construct the tangent matrices.

Finally, one describes a method, similar to the method due to R. de Borst [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03), making it possible to use all the behaviours available in 3D in the 1D elements.

The increments are denoted by $\Delta$ . One thus has:

### 1.3 Change of variables

Whatever the type of finite element referring to a 1D constitutive law, one must carry out a change of variables in order to pass from the elementary quantities (forces, displacements) to the stresses and strains.

#### 1.3.1 Computation of the strains (small strains)

For each of the finite elements of *Code_Aster*, in STAT_NON_LINE, the global algorithm (Newton) supplies to the elementary routine, which integrates the behaviour, an increment of the displacement field.

For the bar elements, one computes the strain (a single axial component) by:

$\varepsilon =\frac{u(l)-u(0)}{l}$ ,

and the strain increment by:

$\Delta \varepsilon =\frac{\Delta u(l)-\Delta u(0)}{l}$ ,

For the grid elements (modellings GRILLE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE), one computes the membrane strain as for the DKT shell elements. Simply, a single direction corresponds physically to the directions of the reinforcements. One is therefore in the presence of a 1D behaviour.

Moreover, in small strains, for all the models described in this document, one writes for every time instant the partition of the strains in the form of an elastic contribution, of thermal expansion, and of plastic strain:

$\varepsilon (t)={\varepsilon}^{e}(t)+{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}(t)+{\varepsilon}^{p}(t)$ with

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}^{e}(t)={A}^{-1}(T(t))\sigma (t)=\frac{1}{E(T)}\sigma (t)\\ {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}(t)=\alpha (T(t))(T(t)-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})\mathrm{Id}\end{array}$

#### 1.3.2 Computation of the generalised forces (integrated stresses)

After integration of the 1D behaviour, one must integrate the stress component obtained, in order to supply to the global algorithm (Newton) a vector containing the generalised forces.

For the bar elements, one computes the force (uniform in the element, assuming that the cross-section is constant) by: $N=S\sigma ,$

and the equivalent nodal force vector (as for the beam elements, [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01)) by:

$F=\left[\begin{array}{}-N\\ N\end{array}\right]$

For the GRILLE elements, one computes the forces as for the DKT shell elements (membrane forces) by integration of the stresses through the thickness (a single layer and a single integration point).

## 2 Von Mises behaviour with linear isotropic hardening: VMIS_ISOT_LINE or VMIS_ISOT_TRAC

### 2.1 Equations of the model VMIS_ISOT_LINE

They are the restriction of the 3D behaviour [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) to the uniaxial case:

$(\begin{array}{c}\bar{\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}}=\frac{3}{2}\bar{\dot{p}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}}=\bar{\dot{p}}\frac{\sigma}{\mid \sigma \mid }\\ \frac{\sigma}{E}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{p}-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}-R(p)=\mid \sigma \mid -R(p)\le 0\\ (\begin{array}{c}\bar{\dot{p}}=0\mathrm{si}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}-R(p)<0\\ \bar{\dot{p}}\ge 0\mathrm{si}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}-R(p)=0\end{array}\end{array}$

with:

| $\bar{\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}}$ | plastic strain rate, |
| --- | --- |
| $p$ | cumulative plastic strain, |
| ${\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})$ | thermal strain, |
| $R(p)=\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}p+{\sigma}_{y}$ | linear isotropic hardening function, or $R(p)$ indeed piecewise affine, deduced from the tensile curve. |

In the case VMIS_ISOT_LINE, the data of the material characteristics are those supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_LINE or ECRO_LINE_FO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

/ ECRO_LINE = ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

/ ECRO_LINE_FO = ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

In the case VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, the data of the material characteristics are supplied under the factor keyword TRACTION of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

TRACTION = _F (SIGM = courbe_traction)

courbe_traction represents the tensile curve, point by point. The first point makes it possible to define the elastic limit ${\sigma}_{y}$ and Young's modulus $E$ [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02).

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.09/100000000000013B0000009EDB36BE1E35CC4A3E.png)

ECRO_LINE_FO corresponds to the case where ${E}_{T}$ and ${\sigma}_{y}$ depend on the temperature and are then computed for the temperature of the current Gauss point. Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $v$ are those supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO. In this case the tensile curve is the following:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{cc}{\sigma}_{L}=E{\varepsilon}_{L}& \mathrm{si}{\varepsilon}_{L}<\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E}\\ {\sigma}_{L}={\sigma}_{y}+{E}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{L}-\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E})& \mathrm{si}{\varepsilon}_{L}\ge \frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{E}\end{array}\end{array}$

When the criterion is reached one has:

${\sigma}_{L}-R(p)=0$ , hence ${\sigma}_{L}-R({\varepsilon}_{L}-\frac{{\sigma}_{L}}{E})=0$ whence:

$R(p)=\frac{{E}_{T}E}{E-{E}_{T}}p+{\sigma}_{y}=Hp+{\sigma}_{y}$

In the case of a tensile curve, the approach is identical to [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01).

### 2.2 Integration of the relation VMIS_ISOT_LINE

By direct implicit discretisation of the constitutive relations, in a manner analogous to the 3D integration [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02), one obtains:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)\le 0\\ E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}=E\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma }{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣}\\ \Delta p\ge 0\mathrm{si}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)\\ \Delta p=0\mathrm{si}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣<R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)\end{array}$

Two cases arise:

- $∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣<R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)$

in this case $\Delta p=0$ , that is $\sigma =E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}$

hence $∣{\sigma}^{-}\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})∣<R({p}^{-})$

- $∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)$

in this case $\Delta p\ge 0$

hence $∣\frac{{\sigma}^{-}E}{{E}^{-}}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})∣\ge R({p}^{-})$ .

One deduces from this the solution algorithm:

let us set ${\sigma}^{e}=\frac{E{\sigma}^{-}}{{E}^{-}}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$

if $∣{\sigma}^{e}∣\le R({p}^{-})$ then $\Delta p=0$ and $\Delta \sigma =E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$

if $∣{\sigma}^{e}∣>R({p}^{-})$ then one must solve:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}^{e}={\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma +E\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma }{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣}\\ {\sigma}^{e}=(1+\frac{E\Delta p}{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣})({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )\end{array}$

hence, taking the absolute value:

$∣{\sigma}^{e}∣=(1+\frac{E\Delta p}{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣})({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )$

that is, using $\begin{array}{}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)\\ ∣{\sigma}^{e}∣=R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)+E\Delta p\end{array}$ .

One therefore deduces:

- in the case of a linear hardening: $\Delta p=\frac{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣-({\sigma}_{y}+{\mathrm{Hp}}^{-})}{E+H}$
- and in the case of an arbitrary hardening, the curve $R(p)$ being refined piecewise, one solves directly the equation in $\Delta p$ : in the same way $E\Delta p+R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)=∣{\sigma}^{e}∣$ as in 3D [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02).

Let us note in passing that: $\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}=\frac{\sigma}{R(p)}$

then $\sigma =({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )=\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}R(p)=\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{1+\frac{E\Delta p}{R(p)}}$

Moreover, the option FULL_MECA makes it possible to compute the tangent matrix ${K}_{i}^{n}$ at each iteration. The tangent operator which serves to construct it is computed directly on the previous discretised system. One obtains directly:

if $∣{\sigma}^{e}∣>R({p}^{-})$     $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{T}$

otherwise     $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

**Note:**

*The option* *RIGI_MECA_TANG, which makes it possible to compute the tangent matrix* ${K}_{i}^{0}$ *used in the prediction phase of the Newton algorithm, takes into account the plasticity indicator at the previous time instant:*

> *if* $\chi =1\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{T}$ *if* $\chi =0\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

### 2.3 Internal variables

The constitutive relation VMIS_ISOT_LINE produces two internal variables: $p$ and $\chi$

## 3 Von Mises behaviour, 1D linear kinematic hardening: VMIS_CINE_LINE

### 3.1 Equation of the model VMIS_CINE_LINE

For reasons of performance the relation is written in 1D. They are the restriction of the 3D behaviour ([R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) and [R5.03.16](doc:r5.03.16)) to the uniaxial case. The 3D behaviour is written:

$\sigma =K(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{p}-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$ with $K$ the elasticity operator

$X=C{\varepsilon}^{p}$

$F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{\sigma}_{y}$ with ${A}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}\cdot \tilde{A}}$

$\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}=\dot{p}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\mathrm{eq}}}}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\mathrm{si}F<0\dot{p}=0\\ \mathrm{si}F=0\dot{p}\ge 0\end{array}$

In the uniaxial case, the tensors are written:

$\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma DX=XD{\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}{\varepsilon}^{p}D$ with $D=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}2/3& \text{}& \text{}\\ \text{}& -1/3& \text{}\\ \text{}& \text{}& -1/3\end{array}\right]$

As long as the loading is monotonic, one immediately obtains the following relations:

$p={\varepsilon}^{p}X=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}\sigma =\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}+{\sigma}_{y}\sigma =F(\varepsilon )={\sigma}_{y}+\frac{E\cdot {E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}p$

C is determined by: $C=\frac{2}{3}\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}$ . One sets: $H=\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}=\frac{3}{2}C$

The 1D constitutive relation is then written:

$(\begin{array}{c}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣\\ E\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}\\ X=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}=H{\varepsilon}^{p}\\ ∣\sigma -X∣-{\sigma}_{y}\le 0\\ (\begin{array}{c}\dot{p}=0\mathrm{si}∣\sigma -X∣-{\sigma}_{y}<0\\ \dot{p}\ge 0\mathrm{si}∣\sigma -X∣-{\sigma}_{y}=0\end{array}\end{array}$

The data of the material characteristics are those supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_LINE or ECRO_LINE_FO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01):

/ ECRO_LINE = ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

/ ECRO_LINE_FO = ( D_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{T}$ , SY = ${\sigma}_{y}$ )

### 3.2 Integration of the relation VMIS_CINE_LINE

By direct implicit discretisation of the constitutive relations, in a manner analogous to the 3D integration ([R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) and [R5.03.16](doc:r5.03.16)), one obtains:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣-{\sigma}_{y}\le 0\\ E\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X}{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣}\\ \frac{X}{H}-\frac{{X}^{-}}{{H}^{-}}=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}\\ \Delta p\ge 0\mathrm{si}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣={\sigma}_{y}\\ \Delta p=0\mathrm{si}∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣<{\sigma}_{y}\end{array}$

with $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})-{\alpha}^{-}({T}^{-}-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})$

Two cases arise:

- $∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣<{\sigma}_{y}$  in this case $\Delta p=0$ , that is $\sigma =E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}-\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}{X}^{-}$ hence $∣{\sigma}^{-}\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}-{X}^{-}\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})∣<R({p}^{-})$ ,
- otherwise $\Delta p\ge 0$ .

In order to simplify the writing one will set: ${\sigma}^{e}=\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}-\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}{X}^{-}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$ .

One deduces from this the solution algorithm:

1. if $∣{\sigma}^{e}∣\le {\sigma}_{y}$ then $\Delta p=0,X={X}^{-}\frac{H}{{H}^{-}},\sigma =E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})+\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}$
2. otherwise one must solve:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}E\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})-\Delta \sigma ={\sigma}^{e}-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )+{X}^{-}\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X}{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣}=\Delta p\frac{\sigma -X}{∣\sigma -X∣}\\ X-\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}{X}^{-}=H\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}\\ ∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma -{X}^{-}-\Delta X∣-{\sigma}_{y}=0\end{array}$

Let us note that: $\frac{H}{{H}^{-}}{X}^{-}=X-H\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ .

One then deduces from the first equation: ${\sigma}^{e}=\sigma -X+(E+H)\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$

One therefore obtains, by eliminating $\sigma -X$ from the second equation:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}\frac{\Delta p}{(E+H)\Delta p+{\sigma}_{y}}$

By replacing $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ in the relation between ${\sigma}^{e}$ and $\sigma -X$ , one obtains:

$\sigma -X={\sigma}^{e}(\frac{{\sigma}_{y}}{(E+H)\Delta p+{\sigma}_{y}})$

By taking the absolute value of the two sides of the previous equation, one finds $\Delta p$ :

$(E+H)\Delta p+{\sigma}_{y}=∣{\sigma}^{e}∣$

Once $\Delta p$ has been determined, one can compute:

$\begin{array}{}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}\\ X={X}^{-}+\Delta X=\frac{H{X}^{-}}{{H}^{-}}+H\Delta p\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}\end{array}$

and using: $\frac{\sigma -X}{{\sigma}_{y}}=\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}$ , one obtains directly: $\sigma ={\sigma}_{y}\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}^{e}∣}+X$

Moreover, the option FULL_MECA makes it possible to compute the tangent matrix ${K}_{i}^{n}$ at each iteration. The tangent operator which serves to construct it is computed directly on the previous discretised system. One obtains directly:

if $∣{\sigma}^{e}∣>R({p}^{-})$     $\frac{\delta \partial }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{T}$

otherwise    $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

The option RIGI_MECA_TANG, which makes it possible to compute the tangent matrix ${K}_{i}^{0}$ used in the prediction phase of the Newton algorithm, is obtained with the aid of the plasticity indicator ${\chi }^{-}$ of the previous time instant:

- if ${\chi }^{-}=1$ then $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{T}$
- if ${\chi }^{-}=0$ then $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

### 3.3 Internal variables

The constitutive relation VMIS_CINE_LINE produces two internal variables: $X$ and $\chi$

## 4 Von Mises behaviour, 1D linear kinematic hardening: VMIS_CINE_GC

### 4.1 Equation of the model VMIS_CINE_GC

For reasons of performance the relation is also written in 1D for use with finite elements of multifibre-beam type. The equations come from the restriction of the 3D behaviour ([R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) and [R5.03.16](doc:r5.03.16)) to the uniaxial case.

The equations of the model are the same as those of §  3.1 .

The material data are those supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_LINE of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01]:

/ECRO_LINE=_F(

> ♦D_SIGM_EPSI    = ${E}_{T}$         [Real]
> ♦SY    = ${\sigma}_{y}$         [Real]
> ◊SIGM_LIM    =sigmlim        [Real]
> ◊EPSI_LIM    =epsilim        [Real]

)

The operands sigm_lim and espi_lim make it possible to define the bounds which correspond to the serviceability and ultimate limit states, classically used during a civil-engineering study.

◊SIGM_LIM=sigmlim

Definition of the limit stress.

◊EPSI_LIM=epslim

Definition of the limit strain.

These bounds are obligatory when one uses the behaviour VMIS_CINE_GC (Cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11) Non-linear behaviours, [U4.42.07](doc:u4.42.07) DEFI_MATER_GC). In the other cases they are not taken into account.

### 4.2 Integration of the relation VMIS_CINE_GC

The integration method is identical to that presented in § 3.2 .

### 4.3 Internal variables

The supported modelling is 1D; the number of internal variables is 6.

- $\mathit{V1}$ : This variable represents the stress divided by the limit stress sigmlim.
- $\mathit{V2}$ : This variable represents the total strain divided by the limit strain epslim.
- $\mathit{V3}$ : Kinematic hardening: XCINXX. In 1D only a scalar is necessary.
- $\mathit{V4}$ : Plastic indicator: INDIPLAS. Indicates whether the material has exceeded the elastic criterion.
- $\mathit{V5}$ : non-recoverable dissipation: DISSIP. During seismic analyses it may be useful to the user to know the non-recoverable dissipated energy. The variable dissip represents the cumulated non-recoverable energy. The increment of non-recoverable energy is written in the form:

$\Delta \mathit{Eg}=\frac{1}{2}({E}^{+}\Delta \varepsilon –({\sigma}^{+}-{\sigma}^{-}))\Delta \varepsilon$

- $\mathit{V6}$ : thermodynamic dissipation: DISSTHER. The increment of thermodynamic dissipation is written in the form: $\Delta \mathit{Eg}={\sigma}_{y}\dot{p}$ .

## 5 Von Mises behaviour with asymmetric linear hardening: VMIS_ASYM_LINE

### 5.1 Equations of the model VMIS_ASYM_LINE

#### 5.1.1 Asymmetric behaviour in tension and in compression

It is a behaviour decoupled in tension and compression, constructed from VMIS_ASYM_LINE, but with elastic limits and hardening moduli which differ in tension and in compression. We adopt an index $T$ for tension and $C$ for compression. The elastic behaviour in tension and compression is identical and characterised by the same Young's modulus. There are two isotropic-hardening domains defined by ${R}_{T}$ and ${R}_{C}$ . The two domains are independent of one another.

| $\mathrm{YT}$ | elastic limit in tension. In absolute value. |
| --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{YC}$ | elastic limit in compression. In absolute value. |
| ${p}_{T}$ | Internal variable in tension. Algebraic value. |
| ${p}_{C}$ | Internal variable in compression. Algebraic value. |
| ${E}_{\mathrm{TT}}$ | Hardening slope in tension. |
| ${E}_{\mathrm{TC}}$ | Hardening slope in compression. |

The equations of the constitutive model are:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\varepsilon}-{E}^{-1}\sigma -\dot{{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}}\\ {\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{C}^{p}+{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{T}^{p}\\ {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{C}^{p}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{C}\frac{\sigma}{∣\sigma ∣}\\ {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{C}^{p}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{T}\frac{\sigma}{∣\sigma ∣}\\ \sigma -{R}_{T}({p}_{T})\le 0\\ -\sigma -{R}_{C}({p}_{C})\le 0\end{array}\mathrm{avec}(\begin{array}{c}{\dot{p}}_{C}=0\mathrm{si}-\sigma -{R}_{C}({p}_{C})<0\\ {\dot{p}}_{C}\ge 0\mathrm{si}-\sigma ={R}_{C}({p}_{C})\\ {\dot{p}}_{T}=0\mathrm{si}\sigma -{R}_{T}({p}_{T})<0\\ {\dot{p}}_{T}\ge 0\mathrm{si}\sigma ={T}_{T}({p}_{T})\end{array}$

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{C}^{p}$ : plastic strain rate in compression,

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{T}^{p}$ : plastic strain rate in tension,

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{\mathrm{th}}$ : strain of thermal origin: ${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})$

One notes that one cannot have plastic flow simultaneously in tension and in compression: either ${\dot{p}}_{C}=0$ , or ${\dot{p}}_{C}=0$ , or both are zero.

The data of the material characteristics are those supplied under the factor keyword ECRO_ASYM_LINE of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

ECRO_ASYM_LINE = _F(

> DT_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{\mathrm{TT}}$ ,    SY_T= ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{yT}}$ ,
> DC_SIGM_EPSI = ${E}_{\mathrm{TC}}$ ,    SY_C= ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{yC}}$ ,)

Young's modulus E is supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO.

One computes the hardening functions by: $\begin{array}{}{R}_{T}(p)=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}E}{E-{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}{p}_{T}+{\sigma}_{\mathrm{yT}}={H}_{T}\cdot {p}_{T}+{\sigma}_{\mathrm{yT}}\\ {R}_{C}(p)=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{TC}}E}{E-{E}_{\mathrm{TC}}}{p}_{C}+{\sigma}_{\mathrm{yC}}={H}_{C}\cdot {p}_{C}+{\sigma}_{\mathrm{yC}}\end{array}$

### 5.2 Integration of the behaviour VMIS_ASYM_LINE

By direct implicit discretisation of the asymmetric constitutive relation, in a manner analogous to the previous one, one obtains:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta {\varepsilon}_{T}^{p}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{C}^{p}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}-\frac{\Delta \sigma }{E}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{T}^{p}=\Delta {p}_{T}\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma }{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣}\\ \Delta {p}_{T}\ge 0\mathrm{si}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{R}_{T}(\bar{{p}_{T}}+\Delta \bar{{p}_{T}})\le 0\\ \Delta {p}_{T}=0\mathrm{si}({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{R}_{T}(\bar{{p}_{T}}+\Delta \bar{{p}_{T}})<0\\ \text{}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{C}^{p}=\Delta {p}_{C}\frac{{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma }{∣{\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ∣}\\ -({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{R}_{C}(\bar{{p}_{C}}+\Delta \bar{{p}_{C}})\le 0\\ \Delta {p}_{C}\ge 0\mathrm{si}-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{R}_{C}(\bar{{p}_{C}}+\Delta \bar{{p}_{C}})=0\\ \Delta {p}_{C}=0\mathrm{si}-({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )-{R}_{C}(\bar{{p}_{C}}+\Delta \bar{{p}_{C}})<0\end{array}$

The integration is similar to that of VMIS_ISOT_LINE for each of the tension and compression directions. One must clearly see that the centres of the elasticity domains are data (computed explicitly at the previous step) for the incremental problem to be solved.

Four cases arise:

- $\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}>0$ one sets ${\sigma}_{T}^{e}={\sigma}^{-}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$

◦    if ${\sigma}_{T}^{e}<{R}_{T}(\bar{{p}_{T}})$ in this case $\Delta {p}_{T}=0$ hence $\sigma ={\sigma}_{T}^{e}$ and $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

◦    otherwise: $\Delta {p}_{T}=\frac{∣{\sigma}_{T}^{e}∣-({\sigma}_{\mathrm{yT}}+{H}_{T}{p}_{T}^{-})}{E+{H}_{T}}$ , $\Delta {p}_{C}=0$ $\begin{array}{}\sigma =\frac{{\sigma}_{T}^{e}}{1+\frac{E\Delta {p}_{T}}{{R}_{T}({p}_{T})}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{T}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}_{T}^{e}∣}{R}_{T}({p}_{T})\\ \frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{\mathrm{TT}}\end{array}$

- $\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}<0$  one sets ${\sigma}_{C}^{e}={\sigma}^{-}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$

◦    if $-{\sigma}_{C}^{e}<{R}_{C}(\bar{{p}_{C}})$ in this case $\Delta {p}_{C}=0$ hence $\sigma ={\sigma}_{C}^{e}$ and $\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=E$

◦    otherwise: $\Delta {p}_{C}=\frac{∣{\sigma}_{C}^{e}∣-({\sigma}_{\mathrm{yC}}+{H}_{C}{p}_{C}\bar )}{E+{H}_{C}}$ , $\Delta {p}_{T}=0$ $\begin{array}{}\sigma =\frac{{\sigma}_{C}^{e}}{1+\frac{E\Delta {p}_{C}}{{R}_{C}({p}_{C})}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{C}^{e}}{∣{\sigma}_{C}^{e}∣}{R}_{C}({p}_{C})\\ \frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }={E}_{\mathrm{TC}}\end{array}$

**Note:**

*The initial tangent matrix (option* *RIGI_MECA_TANG) is taken equal to the elastic matrix.*

## 6 Behaviours VISC_IRRA_LOG and GRAN_IRRA_LOG

The model presented in this chapter describes the 1D viscoplastic behaviours VISC_IRRA_LOG and GRAN_IRRA_LOG (creep and growth under irradiation of the alloys M5 and Zircaloy-4) for the modelling of the fuel assemblies, and applicable to bar elements and multifibre beams.

### 6.1 Formulation of the model

The equations are the following:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{vp}}=\dot{\epsilon}\frac{\sigma}{\left|\sigma \right|}\\ \dot{\epsilon}=\left|\sigma \right|.\left({e}^{\frac{-Q}{T}}\right).\dot{\Phi}\left(\frac{A\omega }{1+\omega \Phi }+B-C\omega {e}^{-\omega t}\right)\\ \frac{\dot{\sigma}}{E}=\dot{\epsilon}-{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{vp}}-{\dot{\epsilon}}^{g}-\dot{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}}\end{array}$

These relations are deduced from the creep tests FLETAN and REFLET [8] for various values of the neutron flux.

The coefficients are supplied under the keyword VISC_IRRA_LOG or GRAN_IRRA_LOG of DEFI_MATERIAU and $\Phi$ is the neutron fluence (integral of the flux with respect to time).

${\varepsilon}^{g}$ represents the growth strain under flux. It is taken into account only in the behaviour GRAN_IRRA_LOG and is expressed in the form:

${\varepsilon}^{g}(t)=f(T,{\Phi}_{t}(x,y,z))$

**Notes:**

1. *The neutron fluence* ${\Phi}_{t}(x,y,z)$ *is obligatorily expressed in* ${10}^{20}n/{\mathit{cm}}^{2}$ *. By convention in* DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), if the value supplied under the keyword FLUX_PHI *is equal to 1, it is the fluence field which is used for the behaviour. Otherwise, the value supplied in* DEFI_MATERIAU *is used as a constant neutron flux.*
2. *It is a field at the nodes defined as a command variable in the command* AFFE_MATERIAU *.*
3. *Warning* *: The irradiation field is incremental and corresponds to the irradiation history (stored as an internal variable – cf. below) to which one adds the increment of the fluence field coming from the command variable.*

### 6.2 Internal variables

Three internal variables:

- $\mathit{V1}$ : the cumulative viscoplastic strain: ${\varepsilon}_{p}$ ;
- $\mathit{V2}$ : storage of the irradiation history (fluence).
- $\mathit{V3}$ : the growth strain: ${\varepsilon}^{g}$ .

### 6.3 Implicit integration

By direct implicit discretisation of the constitutive relations, one obtains:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{vp}}=\Delta p\frac{\sigma \left({t}^{-}+\Delta t\right)}{\left|\sigma \left({t}^{-}+\Delta t\right)\right|}\\ \Delta p=\left|\sigma \left({t}^{-}+\Delta t\right)\right|\left({e}^{\frac{-Q}{T}}\right).\left(\frac{A\omega }{1+\omega \Phi ({t}^{-}+\Delta t)}+B-C\omega {e}^{-\omega t}\right)\Delta \Phi \\ \frac{\sigma}{E}-\frac{{\sigma}^{-}}{{E}^{-}}=\Delta \epsilon -\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{vp}}-\Delta {\epsilon}^{g}-\Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}\\ \text{avec}\\ \Delta {\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T)\left(T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}}\right)-\alpha ({T}^{-})\left(T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}}\right)\\ \Delta {\epsilon}^{g}=f({T}^{+},{\Phi}_{t}^{+})-f({T}^{-},{\Phi}_{t}^{-})\end{array}$

One can solve these equations explicitly by setting: ${\sigma}^{e}=\frac{E}{{E}^{-}}{\sigma}^{-}+E(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{g}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}})$

then the system reduces to: $\sigma ={\sigma}^{e}-E\sigma \left({e}^{\frac{-Q}{T}}\right).\left(\frac{A\omega }{1+\omega \Phi }+B-C\omega {e}^{-\omega t}\right)\Delta \Phi$

hence the solution is obtained immediately: $\sigma =\frac{{\sigma}^{e}}{1+E\left({e}^{\frac{-Q}{T}}\right).\left(\frac{A\omega }{1+\omega \Phi }+B-C\omega {e}^{-\omega t}\right)\Delta \Phi }$

and the tangent operator is written: $\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon }=\frac{E}{1+E\left({e}^{\frac{-Q}{T}}\right).\left(\frac{A\omega }{1+\omega \Phi }+B-C\omega {e}^{-\omega t}\right)\Delta \Phi }$

## 7 MAZARS model in 1D

### 7.1 Equations of the model

The aim of this modelling is to account for crack closure. This model is used only with multifibre beams. The equations presented in the document [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08) “Mazars damage model” are taken up and rewritten in $\mathrm{1D.}$

> $\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}=(1-{D}_{t})E{\langle {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}\rangle }_{+}\\ {\sigma}_{xx}=(1-{D}_{c})E{\langle {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}\rangle }_{-}\end{array}$

with:

- $E$     : Young's modulus,
- ${D}_{t}$     : the damage variable in tension.
- ${D}_{c}$     : the damage variable in compression.
- ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}$     : the elastic strain ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}}$

*•*     ${\varepsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})$ *:* the thermal expansion

The damage always remains driven by the extensions.

Tensile damage exists if ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}\ge {\epsilon}_{t0}$ , compressive damage exists if ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}\ge {\epsilon}_{c0}$ :

> ${D}_{c}=1-\frac{{\epsilon}_{c0}\left(1-{A}_{c}\right)}{{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}}-\frac{{A}_{c}}{\exp\left[{B}_{c}({\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}-{\epsilon}_{c0})\right]}\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}{D}_{c}\in [0,1[$
> ${D}_{t}=1-\frac{{\epsilon}_{t0}\left(1-{A}_{t}\right)}{{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}}-\frac{{A}_{t}}{\exp\left[{B}_{t}({\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}-{\epsilon}_{t0})\right]}\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}{D}_{t}\in [0,1[$

where ${A}_{c},{A}_{t},{B}_{c},{B}_{t},{\epsilon}_{c0},{\epsilon}_{t0}$ are material parameters to be identified.

The damage is driven by the equivalent strain ${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}$ . The extensions are essential in the phenomenon of cracking of concrete; the equivalent strain introduced is defined from the positive values of the strains, namely:

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\text{Si}{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}\ge 0\text{alors}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}=|{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}|\\ \text{Si}{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}\le 0\text{alors}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}=\sqrt{2}\nu |{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}|\end{array}$

**Remarque:**

In the case where ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}\le 0$ , in $\mathrm{1D}$ the principal strains in the other directions are ${\varepsilon}_{yy}^{e}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{e}=-\nu {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}$ . By using the formula ${\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{{\langle {\varepsilon}_{1}\rangle }_{+}^{2}+{\langle {\varepsilon}_{2}\rangle }_{+}^{2}+{\langle {\varepsilon}_{3}\rangle }_{+}^{2}}$ one does indeed obtain the previous expression.

The tangent matrix has as its expression: $\frac{d{\sigma}_{xx}}{d{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}}=(1-\tilde{D})E-\frac{d\tilde{D}}{d{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}}E{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{e}$ with:

$\begin{array}{ccc}\text{Si}{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}\ge 0\text{et}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}\ge {\epsilon}_{t0}& \frac{d\tilde{D}}{d{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}}=\frac{d{D}_{t}}{d{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}}=& \left(\frac{{\epsilon}_{t0}\left(1-{A}_{t}\right)}{{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{2}}+\frac{{A}_{t}{B}_{t}}{\exp\left[{B}_{t}({\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}-{\epsilon}_{t0})\right]}\right)\\ \text{Si}{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}<0\text{et}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}\ge {\epsilon}_{c0}& \frac{d\tilde{D}}{d{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}}=\frac{d{D}_{c}}{d{\epsilon}_{xx}^{e}}=& -\sqrt{2}\nu \left(\frac{{\epsilon}_{c0}\left(1-{A}_{c}\right)}{{\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{2}}+\frac{{A}_{c}{B}_{c}}{\exp\left[{B}_{c}({\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}-{\epsilon}_{c0})\right]}\right)\end{array}$

## 8 Constitutive law RELAX_ACIER

The phenomenon of relaxation of the steels used in prestressing is covered by regulations. The principal regulations are: BPEL83, NF-EN-1992-1-1 October 2005, AFCEN-ETCC-2010,

One wishes to be able to model strains which will vary slowly over time, in particular in order to take into account the creep of the concrete and the variations of temperature. One also wishes to take into account the influence of the temperature on the relaxation phenomenon.

From a regulatory point of view, it would be possible to take into account the effect of the creep of the concrete and of the thermal strain by making a linear combination of the various phenomena (Cf. the regulations for more details). This approach is incompatible with a finite-element analysis.

These regulations also make it possible to correct the relaxation after a “jump” in strain, which may be due to a variation of the stress arising from an external loading (for example during a ten-yearly test on a containment). In no case can this “jump” evolve in time; it can only be a Dirac. The method used in this case is that of the equivalent time which, by construction, is incompatible with a finite-element code.

### 8.1 Formulation of the model

In order for the relaxation law to be usable in a finite-element code for structural analyses with variations of loadings such as: creep of the concrete, retensioning of the cables, taking into account of the influence of temperature, … it must be incremental and thermodynamically correct.

Several formulations for the modelling of the strain linked to relaxation exist in the literature.

The formulation adopted is based on that proposed by J. Lemaitre:

$\sigma =E.{\epsilon}^{e}$     $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}$

${{\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{an}}=⟨\frac{\sigma –R\left({\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}\right)}{{f}_{\mathit{prg}}.k}⟩}^{n}$ with $R\left({\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}\right)=\frac{{f}_{\mathit{prg}}.c.{\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}}{{\left(1+{\left(b.{\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}\right)}^{\mathit{nr}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{\mathit{nr}}}}$     [eq9.1-1]

The constitutive law is 1D, and available only for modellings of bar type, which are used to model the prestressing cables in the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP.

In order to take into account the influence of the temperature on the relaxation, all the coefficients of the law may be functions of the temperature.

**Remarque:**

The parameters $c$ , $b$ , $n$ and $\mathit{nr}$ are dimensionless, and therefore independent of the units used during the study. $k$ is made dimensionless with respect to ${f}_{\mathit{prg}}$ , hence with respect to the stresses, but not with respect to time. Indeed ${\dot{\epsilon}}^{\mathit{an}}$ is homogeneous to ${[s]}^{-1}$ , if the unit of time is the second. Therefore, if one knows $k$ for a strain rate in one unit of time, it is necessary to convert its value with respect to the unit of time used during the study.

### 8.2 Internal variables

Two internal variables:

  - $V1$ : the cumulative anelastic strain: ${\epsilon}^{\mathit{an}}$ .

- $V2$ : storage of the tangent stiffness of the behaviour.

### 8.3 Explicit integration

The integration is carried out by a Runge–Kutta method of order five with variable steps.

### 8.4 Identification of the parameters

Several solutions are possible:

- An analytical piece of work makes it possible to determine relations between the coefficients present in the regulatory formulae and that of the constitutive law: tangent at the beginning of the relaxation, asymptote for an “infinite” time, …

For a given type of cable, which corresponds to a set of parameters (k1, k2, ${\rho}_{1000}$ , ${f}_{\mathit{prg}}$ ). Different values of $\mu$ will make it possible to obtain a bundle of “tests” on which an identification of the parameters can be carried out.
- A bibliographical study in order to recover the experimental and theoretical work carried out on the relaxation of prestressing cables, so as to use the tests directly and to identify the parameters.

## 9 Method for using in 1D all the 3D behaviours

As for the treatment of plane stresses [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03), it is possible to benefit, for the 1D modellings, from the behaviours available in 3D. One extends for this purpose the method due to R. de Borst to the 1D case, by treating this condition (unidimensional stress field) not at the level of the constitutive law but at the level of equilibrium. One thus obtains, in the course of the iterations of the algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE, stress fields which tend towards a unidirectional field. One verifies, at convergence of the global Newton iterations, that the stress fields are indeed unidirectional, to within a precision, otherwise one continues the iterations. The method consists in decomposing the strain and stress fields into a purely unidirectional part (direction x) and a part relative to the other directions, and in carrying out a static condensation by writing that the stress components relative to the other directions are zero. One considers in the tensors (of order 2) only the diagonal terms, written in the form of vectors with 3 components. The direction x corresponds to the direction of the element (bar, multifibre beam) or to the direction of the grid reinforcements. At any time instant of the solution of the incremental behaviour, the tangent operator $D$ relates the stress increment to the strain increment by:

$d\sigma =\left[\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }\right]d\varepsilon =Dd\varepsilon$ which one rewrites $\left[\begin{array}{}d{\sigma}_{x}\\ d{\sigma}_{y}\\ d{\sigma}_{z}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{D}_{11}& {D}_{12}& {D}_{13}\\ {D}_{21}& {D}_{22}& {D}_{23}\\ {D}_{31}& {D}_{32}& {D}_{33}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{}d{\varepsilon}_{x}\\ d{\varepsilon}_{y}\\ d{\varepsilon}_{z}\end{array}\right]$ .

By writing these increments as the difference between the Newton iterations $n$ and $n+1$ , one obtains:

$d\sigma ={\sigma}^{n+1}-{\sigma}^{n}=\Delta {\sigma}^{n+1}-\Delta {\sigma}^{n}$ , $d\varepsilon =\Delta {\varepsilon}^{n+1}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{n}$

At convergence, this difference must tend towards zero.

By introducing the conditions ${\sigma}_{y}^{n+1}=0$ and ${\sigma}_{z}^{n+1}$ (unidirectional behaviour), one obtains, for iteration $n+1$ :

$\left[\begin{array}{}d{\sigma}_{x}\\ d{\sigma}_{y}\\ d{\sigma}_{z}\end{array}\right]=(\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{x}^{n+1}-{\sigma}_{x}^{n}\\ {\sigma}_{y}^{n+1}-{\sigma}_{y}^{n}\\ {\sigma}_{z}^{n+1}-{\sigma}_{z}^{n}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{}{\sigma}_{x}^{n+1}-{\sigma}_{x}^{n}\\ -{\sigma}_{y}^{n}\\ -{\sigma}_{z}^{n}\end{array})=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{D}_{11}& {D}_{12}& {D}_{13}\\ {D}_{21}& {D}_{22}& {D}_{23}\\ {D}_{31}& {D}_{32}& {D}_{33}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{}d{\varepsilon}_{x}\\ d{\varepsilon}_{y}\\ d{\varepsilon}_{z}\end{array}\right]$

The last two equations make it possible to express $d{\varepsilon}_{y}$ and $d{\varepsilon}_{z}$ as a function of $d{\varepsilon}_{x}$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{\varepsilon}_{y}=\frac{1}{{D}_{22}}(-{\sigma}_{y}^{n}-{D}_{21}d{\varepsilon}_{x}-{D}_{23}d{\varepsilon}_{z})\\ d{\varepsilon}_{z}=\frac{1}{{D}_{33}}(-{\sigma}_{z}^{n}-{D}_{31}d{\varepsilon}_{x}-{D}_{32}d{\varepsilon}_{y})\end{array}$

that is

$\begin{array}{}d{\varepsilon}_{y}=\frac{1}{\Delta}(-{D}_{33}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}+{D}_{23}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+{D}_{y}d{\varepsilon}_{x})\\ d{\varepsilon}_{z}=\frac{1}{\Delta}(-{D}_{32}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}+{D}_{22}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+{D}_{z}d{\varepsilon}_{x})\end{array}$

with $\Delta ={D}_{33}{D}_{22}-{D}_{23}{D}_{32,}{D}_{y}={D}_{23}{D}_{31}-{D}_{21}{D}_{33,}{D}_{z}={D}_{32}{D}_{21}-{D}_{31}{D}_{22}$

by substituting these expressions into the first equation, one obtains:

${\sigma}_{x}^{n+1}={\sigma}_{x}^{n}+({D}_{11}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{y}+{D}_{13}{D}_{z}}{\Delta})d{\varepsilon}_{x}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{23}-{D}_{22}{D}_{13}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{32}-{D}_{12}{D}_{33}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}$

Equilibrium at iteration $n+1$ is written:

$\begin{array}{}\int{D}^{T}{\sigma}^{n+1}\mathrm{dv}=\int{B}^{T}{\sigma}_{x}^{n+1}\mathrm{dv}=\int{B}^{T}({D}_{11}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{y}+{D}_{13}{D}_{z}}{\Delta})d{\varepsilon}_{x}\\ +\int{B}^{T}({\sigma}_{x}^{n}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{23}-{D}_{22}{D}_{13}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{32}-{D}_{12}{D}_{33}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{y}^{n})\mathrm{dv}\\ ={K}^{n}{\mathrm{du}}^{n+1}+\int{B}^{T}({\sigma}_{x}^{n}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{23}-{D}_{22}{D}_{13}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{32}-{D}_{12}{D}_{33}}{\Delta}{\sigma}_{y}^{n})\mathrm{dv}\end{array}$

One observes that the taking into account of the unidimensional behaviour intervenes at two levels:

- in the tangent matrix, by the corrective term:

$\int{B}^{T}\frac{{D}_{12}{D}_{y}+{D}_{13}{D}_{z}}{\Delta}B\mathrm{dv}$

- in the writing of the right-hand side, by the corrective term:

$\frac{\int{B}^{T}}{\Delta}(({D}_{12}{D}_{23}-{D}_{22}{D}_{13}){\sigma}_{z}^{n}+({D}_{12}{D}_{32}-{D}_{12}{D}_{33}){\sigma}_{y}^{n})\mathrm{dv}$

In order to implement this method, it suffices to compute these corrective terms and to add them to the stresses and to the tangent matrix obtained from the 3D solution of the behaviour. For this it is necessary to store information from one Newton iteration to the next, by means of 4 additional internal variables. The stages of the solution are:

- at iteration $n+1$ , the data are: $\Delta {u}^{n+1},{\sigma}^{-},{\alpha}^{-}$ and the 4 internal variables (computed at iteration $n$ ):

$\begin{array}{}\mathrm{V1}=\Delta {\varepsilon}_{y}^{n}+\frac{1}{\Delta}({D}_{23}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}-{D}_{33}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}-{D}_{y}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{x}^{n}),\mathrm{V2}=\frac{{D}_{y}}{\Delta}\\ \\ \mathrm{V3}=\Delta {\varepsilon}_{z}^{n}+\frac{1}{\Delta}({D}_{32}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}-{D}_{22}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}-{D}_{z}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{x}^{n}),\mathrm{V4}=\frac{{D}_{z}}{\Delta}\end{array}$

- before carrying out the integration of the behaviour (carried out in axisymmetric conditions) one computes:

$\begin{array}{}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{y}^{n+1}=\Delta {\varepsilon}_{y}^{n}+\frac{1}{\Delta}(-{D}_{33}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}+{D}_{23}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+{D}_{y}d{\varepsilon}_{x})\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{z}^{n+1}=\Delta {\varepsilon}_{z}^{n}+\frac{1}{\Delta}(-{D}_{32}{\sigma}_{y}^{n}+{D}_{22}{\sigma}_{z}^{n}+{D}_{z}d{\varepsilon}_{x})\end{array}$

- the integration of the behaviour supplies the stresses ${\sigma}^{n+1}$ and the tangent operator $D$ ,
- one modifies the right-hand side and the tangent matrix as indicated above,
- one stores the new internal variables and one verifies whether $∣{\sigma}_{z}^{n+1}∣<\eta$ and $∣{\sigma}_{y}^{n+1}∣<\eta$ , with $\eta =\xi ∣{\sigma}_{x}^{n+1}∣$ , $\xi =$ RESI_INTE_RELA

**Note:**
