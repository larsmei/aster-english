# v6.04.164 SSNV164 - Tensioning of prestress cables in a 3D beam

**Summary**

A reinforced concrete beam of square cross section, consisting of two 10 metre long sections, having one and four square metres of cross section respectively, is considered. The beam is vertical, the lowest section at the bottom. It is clamped at its base, and contains 5 straight prestress cables.

The phase of prestressing, i.e. the successive tensioning of the various cables, is tested here.

The specific features to be tested are:

- DEFI_CABLE_BP operator: determination of kinematic relationships between degrees of freedom of nodes of a cable and degrees of freedom of nodes "neighbors" of a concrete structure modelled by elements $\mathrm{3D}$ and calculation of voltages in a cable under friction effect and recoil anchoring,
- AFFE_CHAR_MECA operator associated with keyword RELA_CINE_BP,
- CALC_PRECONT operator: powering prestress cables with the standard NEWTON method or with the IMPLEX method.
- CALC_PRECONT operator: tensioning prestress cables modelled by elements BARRE with elements CABLE_GAINE in the model and vice versa (modelling C and D).

The results obtained are validated by comparison with the CASTEM 2000 calculation code.

$B$ Modelling allows more specific validation of the use of keyword CONE in DEFI_CABLE_BP.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A reinforced concrete beam of square cross section, consisting of two 10 metre long sections, having one and four square metres of cross section respectively, is considered. The beam is vertical, the lowest section at the bottom. It is clamped at its base, and contains 5 straight prestress cables.

Characteristics concerning the tensioning of cables:

- anchoring back: $1\mathrm{mm}$
- linear friction coefficient: $0,0015{m}^{-1}$
- tension force at the end of a cable: $3,75{10}^{6}N$
- Age of de-offrage 150 days
- first cable voltageing age: 300 days

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The base of the beam is blocked in the $Z$ direction. The two translation movements relative to $\mathrm{OX}$ and $\mathrm{OY}$ are blocked as well as the rotational movement around $\mathrm{OZ}$.

The loading sequence is as follows:

- 300 days, tensioning of 2 cables (1 and 2) at the lower end,
- 450 days, tensioning of 2 additional cables (3 and 4) at the lower end,
- 600 days, the last cable (5) is energized by both ends.

The beam is obviously subject to gravity.

## 2 reference solution

The reference solution was obtained by the CEA with CASTEM 2000, with a mesh containing 2080 elements cubics with 20 nodes and 100 elements cable.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The concrete beam is represented by 2080 elements MECA_HEXA8, supported by as many mesh cells hexahedrons at 8 nodes. The 5 cables are represented with the help of 20 elements MECA_BARRE each supported by as many segments at 2 nodes. The figure below shows the mesh of the beam.

| ![Figure](/figures/v6.04.164/10016A500000073C00002ACA7792FC2321188A5B.svg) | ![Figure](/figures/v6.04.164/1008723000003F41000051DB5287DBCFB0ABC78F.svg) |
| --- | --- |

**Figure 3.1-a: Description of mesh**

Concrete material is defined by ELAS and BPEL_BETON behaviors: the characteristic parameters of this relationship are set to 0 because it is not intended to include voltage losses due to the removal and creeping of concrete.

The steel material for the cables is defined by the ELAS and BPEL_ACIER behaviors. The non-zero values for BPEL_ACIER concern friction ($\mathrm{FROT}\text{\_}\mathrm{LINE}=1,5{10}^{-3}{m}^{-1}$) and the yield limit since a zero value is unlawful ($\mathrm{SY}=1,94{10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$). The cable radius is $2,8209{10}^{-2}m$ .

The blocked degrees of freedom are:

$\mathrm{DZ}$ for the lower side

$\mathrm{DX}$ and $\mathrm{DY}$ for points on the beam symmetry axis

$\mathrm{DX}$ for point $(0.50.0.)$ and $\mathrm{DY}$ for point $(–0.50.0.)$

The ${F}_{0}=3,75{10}^{6}N$ voltage is applied to the lower nodes cables 1, 2, 3 and 4 and both ends for cable 5.

The load is made in 6 time step:

- $t=50\text{et}100s$ , the first two steps in elasticity with the IMPLEX method: this is used to test the method convergence in the elastic domain in one time step
- $t=150s$ , taking into account the gravity: STAT_NON_LINE with boundary conditions, gravity and kinematic relationships for all cables. Cables that do not contribute to the rigidity of the model, they are assigned a constitutive law ‘SANS
- $t=300s$ , voltage of cables 1 and 2: CALC_PRECONT with boundary conditions, cables 1 and 2 being the cables to be voltaged, the cables 3, 4 and 5 being inactive,
- $t=450s$ , voltage of cables 3 and 4: CALC_PRECONT with boundary conditions, kinematic relationships for cables 1 and 2, 3 and 4 being cables to be energized, cable 5 being inactive,
- $t=600s$ , cable powering 5: CALC_PRECONT with boundary conditions, kinematic relationships for cables 1, 2, 3 and 4, cable 5 being the cable to be energized.

### 3.2 Calculation steps

The main steps in calculating are those that you want to validate:

- DEFI_MATERIAU operator: definition of BPEL_BETON behavioral relationships with default values and BPEL_ACIER, in the case of linear friction loss,
- DEFI_CABLE_BP operator: determination of a voltage profile along the prestress cable, taking into account linear friction losses and loss by anchoring back; calculation of the coefficients of kinematic relationships between degrees of freedom of the nodes of the cable and degrees of freedom of the nodes "neighbors" of the concrete beam, in the case of a beam modelled by elements $\mathrm{3D}$ ,
- AFFE_CHAR_MECA operator: definition of a RELA_CINE_BP(RELA_CINE =
- STAT_NON_LINE operator, option COMPORTEMENT: calculation of the equilibrium state taking into account the RELA_CINE_BP type load, in the case of a beam modelled by elements $\mathrm{3D}$ ,
- constitutive law SANS,
- CALC_PRECONT macro-command with an initial non-virgin state, whether or not there are inactive cables, whether there is one or more cables to be energized.

In addition, the calculation is tested with the exact iterative method of Newton and the approach method IMPLEX. In this test case, we remain elastic; the results obtained by the two resolution methods must therefore be identical.

## 7 Summary of the results

The macro-command is found to provide the voltage given by the BPEL (guaranteeed by the CASTEM procedure) with very good accuracy since the difference is less than $\text{0.1\%}$ , both with the Newton method and with the IMPLEX method. Furthermore, the effect of the cable tensioning on the rest of the structure, and especially on the already tense cables, is quite satisfactory since the difference between the reference solution and the Aster calculation is less than $\text{1\%}$ , while the reference mesh was quadratic and the calculation here used linear mesh cells.

The results are identical whether the calculation is solved using the Newton algorithm or with the IMPLEX method. The introduction of a diffusion cone does not alter the quality of the calculations.

It is also noted that it is possible to make successive voltages with cables modelled with the available 2 modellings BARRE and CABLE_GAINE.
