# u4.84.46 Macro-command POST_ROCHE

## 1 Purpose and description

The objective of this macro-command is to carry out post-processings of linear elastic analyses, accounting for the elastoplastic behaviour of piping lines under earthquake and permanent loadings, according to the simplified Roche method.

It takes as input values of moments (components MT, MFY and MFZ) coming from previous analyses corresponding to different types of loading (inertial earthquake, self-weight, thermal expansion, imposed non-seismic displacement, seismic differential displacement DDS) on piping lines (POU_D_T elements only). These moments are supplied either in the form of fields at the nodes by element (ELNO) or else of results containing the field EFGE_ELNO. The internal pressure is taken into account directly, without a previous linear analysis (impossible with POU_D_T elements).

This macro-command handles the cases in which all the loadings are independent of time (factor keyword RESU_MECA).

The output concept is two fields at the nodes by element of quantity (grandeur) NEUT_R: a principal field ROC1_ELNO and a complementary field ROC2_ELNO.

Two variants are proposed: RCC_MRX, based on the codification, and ASNR, in accordance with the file supplied to the ASNR in 2024.

One distinguishes the loadings depending on the displacements (dynamic inertial earthquake, thermal expansion, imposed non-seismic displacement DINS, seismic differential displacement DDS) from the loadings depending on the forces (self-weight, quasi-static inertial earthquake).

The 17 components Xi of the principal field ROC1_ELNO relate to the reference linear elastic stresses, to the abatement coefficients, and to the abated quantities (equivalent stress and moment). They correspond to the following characteristics, quantities directly usable in an industrial framework:

| X1 | Reference elastic stress due to the displacements (DINS, DDS) and to the thermal expansion, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| --- | --- |
| X2 | Reference elastic stress due to the displacements and to the thermal expansion, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X3 | Reference elastic stress due to the inertial earthquake, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X4 | Reference elastic stress due to the inertial earthquake, for the abated-moment computation (B2=1) |
| X5 | Optimised monotonic abatement coefficient, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X6 | Optimised monotonic abatement coefficient, for the abated-moment computation (B2=1) |
| X7 | Optimised seismic abatement coefficient, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X8 | Optimised seismic abatement coefficient, for the abated-moment computation (B2=1) |
| X9 | Optimised abated equivalent stress |
| X10 | Optimised abated moment, permanent resultant: torsion component |
| X11 | Optimised abated moment, permanent resultant: bending component in the plane of the elbow |
| X12 | Optimised abated moment, permanent resultant: bending component out of the plane of the elbow |
| X13 | Optimised abated moment, permanent resultant: norm |
| X14 | Optimised abated moment, seismic resultant: torsion component |
| X15 | Optimised abated moment, seismic resultant: bending component in the plane of the elbow |
| X16 | Optimised abated moment, seismic resultant: bending component out of the plane of the elbow |
| X17 | Optimised abated moment, seismic resultant: norm |

The 28 components Xi of the complementary field ROC2_ELNO relate to intermediate quantities (local and total reversibilities, spring-effect factors, true stress and true strain) and to verification tests. They correspond to the following characteristics:

| X1 | Local reversibility due to the displacements and to the thermal expansion, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| --- | --- |
| X2 | Local reversibility due to the displacements and to the thermal expansion, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X3 | Local reversibility due to the inertial earthquake, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X4 | Local reversibility due to the inertial earthquake, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X5 | Total reversibility due to the displacements and to the thermal expansion, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X6 | Total reversibility due to the displacements and to the thermal expansion, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X7 | Total reversibility due to the inertial earthquake, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X8 | Total reversibility due to the inertial earthquake, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X9 | Monotonic spring-effect factor, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X10 | Monotonic spring-effect factor, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X11 | Seismic spring-effect factor, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X12 | Seismic spring-effect factor, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X13 | Maximum monotonic spring-effect factor, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X14 | Maximum monotonic spring-effect factor, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X15 | Maximum seismic spring-effect factor, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X16 | Maximum seismic spring-effect factor, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X17 | Criterion on the optimised monotonic true stress, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X18 | Criterion on the optimised monotonic true stress, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X19 | Criterion on the optimised seismic true stress, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X20 | Criterion on the optimised seismic true stress, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X21 | Optimised monotonic true stress, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X22 | Optimised monotonic true stress, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X23 | Optimised seismic true stress, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X24 | Optimised seismic true stress, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X25 | Optimised monotonic true strain, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X26 | Optimised monotonic true strain, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |
| X27 | Optimised seismic true strain, excluding the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2) |
| X28 | Optimised seismic true strain, for the abated-moment computation of ASNR form (B2=1) |

The components X21, X22, X23, X24 are verifications. These 4 components are equal to 0 or 1. The value 1 means that, during the analysis, one obtained a value for the true stress lower than the pressure stress. In this case, this induces an abatement coefficient equal to 1.

For the variant RCC_MRX, one specifies that, in the case where SIGM_LIM=’OUI’, the components X17 to X28 are not used and are left at 0.

The printout in the “result” file of the fields and results computed is carried out via the commands IMPR_RESU [U4.91.01](doc:u4.91.01) or POST_RELEVE_T [U4.81.21](doc:u4.81.21) (by specifying the appropriate order numbers in the case of result concepts).

## 2 Syntax

```text
evol_noli = POST_ROCHE(
    ◇ VARIANTE = / "ASNR",
                 / "RCC_MRX" (par défaut),
    # Si: equal_to("VARIANTE", "RCC_MRX")
        ◇ SIGM_LIM = / "NON",
                     / "OUI" (par défaut),
        ◇ SIGM_ABAT = / "CODE" (par défaut),
                      / "REFE_ELAS",
        # Si: equal_to("SIGM_LIM", "NON")
            ◇ TRAC_EPSI = fonction,
    # Si: equal_to("VARIANTE", "ASNR")
        ◇ TRAC_EPSI = fonction,
    ◆ ZONE_ANALYSE = _F(
         ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
           / GROUP_MA = list[grma],
         ◆ GROUP_NO_ORIG = grno,
      ),
    ◇ COUDE = _F(
         ◇ GROUP_MA = list[grma],
         ◆ ANGLE = float,
         ◆ RCOURB = float,
      ),
    ◇ MODELE = modele,
    ◇ CARA_ELEM = cara_elem,
    ◇ CHAM_MATER = cham_mater,
    ◇ INST_TEMP = float (défaut: 0.0),
    ◆ RESU_MECA = _F(
         ◆ TYPE_CHAR = / "DDS",
                       / "DILAT_THERM",
                       / "DINS",
                       / "POIDS",
                       / "SISM_INER_SPEC",
         # Si: equal_to("TYPE_CHAR", 'SISM_INER_SPEC')
             ◆ RESULTAT = mode_meca / mult_elas,
             ◇ DIRECTION = / "COMBI" (par défaut),
                           / "X",
                           / "Y",
                           / "Z",
             ◆ TYPE_RESU = / "DYN",
                           / "QS",
         # Si: not equal_to("TYPE_CHAR", 'SISM_INER_SPEC')
             ◆ / CHAM_GD = cham_elem,
               / RESULTAT = evol_elas,
             ◆ / NUME_ORDRE = int,
               / INST = float,
             # Si: exists("INST")
                 ◇ CRITERE = / "ABSOLU",
                             / "RELATIF" (par défaut),
                 ◇ PRECISION = float (défaut: 1e-06),
      ),
    ◇ PRESSION = _F(
         ◆ / TOUT = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
           / GROUP_MA = list[grma],
         ◆ VALE = float,
      ),
    ◇ TOUT_CHAM = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
    # Si: not exists("TOUT_CHAM")
        ◇ NOM_CHAM = / "ROC1_ELNO" (par défaut),
                     / "ROC2_ELNO",
)

◆ : obligatoire
◇ : optionnel
⟐ : présent par défaut
& : ensemble
/ : un seul parmi
| : plusieurs choix possibles
```

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operand VARIANTE

Makes it possible to define the variant of the Roche method: 'RCC_MRX' is for the most part in accordance with the RCC-MRx codification; 'ASNR' denotes the variant relating to the file accepted by the ASNR in 2024.

```text
◊   VARIANTE    =   / ‘RCC_MRX’ [DEFAUT]
                        / ‘ASNR’
```

### 3.2 Operand SIGM_LIM

```text
◊    SIGM_LIM    =  / ‘OUI’   [DEFAUT]
                            / ‘NON’
```

Operand to be supplied only if FORME= 'RCC_MRX'.
Makes it possible to define the method of computation of the true stress and of the true strain. If this parameter is equal to ‘OUI’ (by default), the true stress is computed from an allowable stress level, itself computed from the material data of the material POST_ROCHE (See R7.10.04), in accordance with the RCC-MRx codification. If this parameter is equal to ‘NON’, the true stress is obtained by solution of a system of non-linear equations, representing the projection of the reference linear elastic stress onto the tension curve of the material.
If VARIANTE= 'ASNR', the method of computation of the true stress corresponds to the case SIGM_LIM = 'NON'.

#### 3.2.1 Operand SIGM_ABAT

Operand to be supplied only if VARIANTE= 'RCC_MRX'. The keyword makes it possible to indicate which formulation is chosen for the computation of the optimised abated stress.

```text
◊   SIGM_ABAT       =       / ’CODE’ [DEFAUT]
                                / ‘REFE_ELAS ‘
```

The value 'CODE' refers to the formulation of the RCC-MRx.
The value 'REFE_ELAS' relates to a formulation based on the formulation of the reference elastic stress, and taking the pressure stress into account in addition.
It is in fact the formulation of the variant 'ASNR'.

### 3.3 Operand TRAC_EPSI

```text
♦ TRAC_EPSI         = traction,             [fonction]
```

Makes it possible to define the tension curve of the material (strain as a function of the stress) onto which the reference linear elastic stress is projected.

### 3.4 Operand INST_TEMP

```text
◊    INST_TEMP    =        inst
```

In order to define the various material parameters necessary for the operator, the user may use the keyword POST_ROCHE_FO of DEFI_MATERIAU instead of POST_ROCHE if he or she wishes these parameters to be functions depending on a temperature field. This temperature field is then to be supplied in AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC. To do this, the user is strongly encouraged to use the keyword CHAM_GD to declare the temperature field, rather than the keyword EVOL to which a result is to be supplied.

However, it may happen that the user is constrained to use EVOL because other previous analyses impose it. In this case, the operand INST_TEMP makes it possible to indicate which time instant of the result data structure supplied is to be taken into account for the computation of the material parameters.

### 3.5 Factor keyword ZONE_ANALYSE

```text
♦    ZONE_ANALYSE=_F(
    ♦   / TOUT            =    'OUI',               [DEFAUT]
        / GROUP_MA        =    gma1,                [groupma]
    ♦    GROUP_NO_ORIG    =    gno1,                [groupno]
```

This keyword makes it possible to define the different zones (or runs) to be analysed. Each zone must be a contiguous line. For example, for the analysis of a “T”, one must define at least two zones (the “branch” and the “run”).

#### 3.5.1 Keywords TOUT and GROUP_MA

Declaration of the mesh cells of the zone.

Only the modelling POU_D_T is accepted on the mesh cells declared.

#### 3.5.2 Keyword GROUP_NO_ORIG

Definition of a node placed at one of the ends of the run.

### 3.6 Factor keyword COUDE

```text
◊    COUDE=_F(
    ♦    GROUP_MA      =    gma1,           [groupma]
    ♦    ANGLE         =    ang,            [R]
    ♦    RCOURB        =    rc,             [R]
```

This keyword makes it possible to locate the elbows in the piping and to assign certain parameters to these mesh cells.

On the parts declared as an elbow, it is necessary to define in the properties of the material POST_ROCHE the optional parameter RP02_MIN.

Furthermore, in order to comply with the codification, the user must define in AFFE_CARA_ELEM/ORIENTATION the local reference frame in the bent parts so as to take the bending moments correctly into account (in the plane of the elbow and out of plane). An example is given in the test case sdll157a.

#### 3.6.1 Keywords GROUP_MA

Declaration of the mesh cells belonging to an elbow

#### 3.6.2 Keywords ANGLE, RCOURB

```text
♦    ANGLE
```

Defines the angle, in degrees, of the elbow to which the mesh cells belong.

```text
♦    RCOURB
```

Defines the radius of curvature of the elbow to which the mesh cells belong.

### 3.7 Operands MODELE, CARA_ELEM and CHAM_MATER

```text
◊    MODELE    =        modele
```

Makes it possible to define the model in the case where it cannot be recovered directly from the field by element or from the result supplied in the first occurrence of RESU_MECA.

```text
◊    CARA_ELEM    =        cara
```

Makes it possible to define the elementary beam characteristics in the case where they cannot be recovered directly because only fields by element have been supplied in the occurrences of RESU_MECA.

```text
◊    CHAM_MATER    =        chmat
```

Makes it possible to define the material field in the case where it cannot be recovered directly because only fields by element have been supplied in the occurrences of RESU_MECA.

### 3.8 Factor keyword PRESSION

```text
◊    PRESSION=_F(
    ♦   / TOUT        =    'OUI',        [DEFAUT]
        / GROUP_MA    =    gma1,         [groupma]
    ♦    VALE         =    press,        [R]
```

This keyword makes it possible to define the values of the internal pressure of the piping.

#### 3.8.1 Keywords GROUP_MA

Definition of the mesh cells to which a pressure value is to be assigned.

#### 3.8.2 Keyword VALE

```text
♦    VALE
```

Defines the value of the pressure.

### 3.9 Factor keyword RESU_MECA

```text
♦     RESU_MECA
```

This keyword makes it possible to define the different loadings to be taken into account for the analysis by the Roche method.

The loadings do not depend on time; the output concept is a field at the nodes by element (ELNO).

#### 3.9.1 Operand TYPE_CHAR

This operand makes it possible to indicate the type of loading declared so that it is processed correctly during the classification.

```text
♦ TYPE_CHAR    = ‘SISM_INER_SPEC’,
                 ‘DDS’,
                 ‘DINS’,
                 ‘DILAT_THERM’,
                 ‘POIDS’
```

There may be several occurrences with the same type of loading for the types 'DDS', ‘DINS’, ‘DILAT_THERM’ and ‘POIDS’.

The value ‘SISM_INER_SPEC’ makes it possible to declare a spectral modal inertial seismic response.

The value ‘DDS’ makes it possible to declare a loading of seismic differential displacements of the anchors.

The value ‘DINS’ makes it possible to declare a loading of imposed non-seismic displacements.

The value ‘DILAT_THERM’ makes it possible to declare a loading due to thermal expansion.

The value ‘POIDS’ makes it possible to declare a self-weight loading.

#### 3.9.2 Operands RESULTAT, DIRECTION and TYPE_RESU

For TYPE_CHAR = ‘SISM_INER_SPEC’, the loading is defined by the following keywords:

```text
♦    RESULTAT    =    resu    ,            [mode_meca / mult_elas]
```

The results supplied here must come from the command COMB_SISM_MODAL and possess the field EFGE_ELNO.

```text
◊    DIRECTION    =    ‘COMBI’,             [DEFAUT]
                           ‘X’,
                       ‘Y’,
                       ‘Z’,
```

This keyword makes it possible to indicate the excitation to be taken into account among the three directions of space and their combination.

```text
◊    TYPE_RESU    =    ‘DYN’,
                       ‘QS’,
```

In the case of an inertial seismic loading TYPE_CHAR = ‘SISM_INER_SPEC’, this keyword makes it possible to indicate whether the inertial seismic response is dynamic or quasi-static.
There must be exactly two occurrences, one with TYPE_RESU equal to ‘DYN’, the other with TYPE_RESU equal to ‘QS’.

The processing of two distinct previous spectral modal analyses relates to the so-called historical Roche method: the quasi-static part (‘QS’) is therein the result of a spectral modal inertial analysis at a damping ratio of 100%; the dynamic part ('DYN') is then defined as the difference between the spectral modal response at the desired damping ratio and this quasi-static part ('QS') thus determined. The computation of the part 'DYN' is described in the test case SDLL157a.

#### 3.9.3 Operands RESULTAT and CHAM_GD

One may supply a result coming from the command MECA_STATIQUE, from which one will extract the field EFGE_ELNO (computed beforehand) at the analysis time instant indicated, or directly a field at the nodes by element EFGE_ELNO containing the components MT, MFY and MFZ.

```text
♦   / RESULTAT   =    resu    ,            [evol_elas]
    / CHAM_GD    =    cham    ,            [cham_elem]
```

#### 3.9.4 Operands NUME_ORDRE, INST, CRITERE and PRECISION

These keywords serve to indicate the analysis time instants to be taken into account when one uses the keyword RESULTAT for all the types of loading except SISM_INER_SPEC, for which one uses DIRECTION and TYPE_RESU.

```text
   ♦    / NUME_ORDRE  =    ordre,     [I]
        / INST        =    inst,      [R]

**# Si INST :**
```

```text
◊   CRITERE         =   'RELATIF',        [DEFAUT]
                            'ABSOLU' ,
◊   PRECISION       =   prec,             [R]
                            1.E-6,            [DEFAUT]
```

The keywords CRITERE and PRECISION are used to parameterise the search for the analysis time instants in the data structure from the time instant supplied to INST.

#### 3.9.5 Operands TOUT_CHAM / NOM_CHAM

These keywords make it possible to indicate the type of output field (principal ROC1_ELNO and complementary ROC2_ELNO). By default, TOUT_CHAM = "OUI": the components of the two fields ROC1_ELNO and ROC2_ELNO are output.

The keyword NOM_CHAM makes it possible to indicate whether one wishes to output only the components of the field ROC1_ELNO or the components of the field ROC2_ELNO.

```text
◇ TOUT_CHAM = "OUI" (ou non renseigné),
    # Si: not exists("TOUT_CHAM")
        ◇ NOM_CHAM = / "ROC1_ELNO",         [DEFAUT]
                     / "ROC2_ELNO",
```
