# v7.31.139 WTNV139 – Modelling a well dug in a water saturated transverse isotropic formation

**Summary:**

This test case from the literature (Abousleiman et al. [1]) presents the case of a well sloping in a water saturated medium. This is a coupled hydromechanical model that has an analytical solution. This test case is intended to test the correct consideration of the transverse isotropy of the porous medium.

## 1 Reference problem

The objective of this test case is to compare the solution obtained with Code_Aster, using a elements Modelling with a analytical solution. This test case and its analytical solution have been developed and detailed by Abousleiman et al.[1]. Here we present the main lines and the permissible analogies with our model.

### 1.1 Geometry of the problem

A volume of $h=\mathrm{1m}$ high rock and $l=\mathrm{15m}$ square base is considered. In the centre, a cylindrical cavity of $R=\mathrm{0.1m}$ radius and infinite extension according to its $z$ axis is dug. Here, one can notice that one models a $\mathrm{1m}$ height domain, which is a restrictive hypothesis (ideally, it would require a much larger domain but would lead to long time of calculations). This cavity is used to model the well dug in the rock massif. This massif is also characterized by isotropy planes perpendicular to the axis of the cylindrical cavity.

The well is also characterised by two angles to the main stress marker:

- its azimut ${\varphi}_{y}=30°$
- its inclination ${\varphi}_{z}=60°$

Which corresponds to the nautical angles:

- $\alpha =30°$
- $\beta =-60°$

The Cartesian marker associated with the mass and stress marker is noted $(Ox'y'z')$ and the one associated with the cavity is noted $(\mathit{Oxyz})$. A $(r,\theta ,z)$ cylindrical coordinate system is also defined to characterize the cylindrical cavity.

It is recalled that the transverse isotropy assumptions imply that ${\nu}_{T}={\nu}_{L}$; ${\nu}_{N}={\nu}_{N}$; ${\nu}_{L}={\nu}_{T}$ and $\frac{{\nu}_{N}}{{\nu}_{L}}=\frac{{E}_{N}}{{E}_{L}}$ .

Also, writing the terms of the Hook matrix, such as:

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and model initials

In the next section we consider the initial and boundary conditions in the well-related benchmark.

#### 1.3.1 Travel cases

Given the symmetry of the model, 3 faces corresponding to the 3 planes $x=0;y=0$ and $z=0$ will be blocked.

#### 1.3.2 Pressure case

The initial pore pressure in the massif and for $r\to +\infty$ is fixed to ${p}_{0}=9,8\mathit{MPa}$. This pressure will therefore be applied to the 3 faces corresponding respectively to the 3 planes $x=l;y=l$ and $z=h$. Following the watering of the well, a uniform pressure is developed in the wall which is fixed to ${p}_{w}=12\mathit{MPa}$ on the edge of the cavity.

#### 1.3.3 Constraints

In the massif, the stress state *in situ* associated with the $(Ox'y'z')$ mark prior to the well digging is characterized by the following initial state:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}S{'}_{x}=-25\mathit{MPa}\\ S{'}_{y}=-22\mathit{MPa}\\ S{'}_{z}=-29\mathit{MPa}\end{array}$

In addition, the well dig involves a disturbance of the initial stress state *in situ* in the massif. In order to take into account this new stress state in the vicinity of the well, two successive rotations must be considered to pass from the $(Ox'y'z')$ marker associated with the massif to the $(O\mathit{xyz})$ marker associated with the well (see Figure ).

The transition matrices describing this change of reference shall be ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$:

${P}_{1}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\cos(\alpha )& -\sin(\alpha )& 0\\ \sin(\alpha )& \cos(\alpha )& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array})$ ${P}_{2}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\cos(\beta )& 0& -\sin(\beta )\\ 0& 1& 0\\ \sin(\beta )& 0& \cos(\beta )\end{array})$

After a change of reference, the initial stress state in the $(\mathit{Oxyz})$ mark and when $r\to +\infty$ to be applied to the model is as follows:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{S}_{xx}=-27,81\mathrm{MPa}\\ {S}_{yy}=-22,75\mathrm{MPa}\\ {S}_{zz}=-25,44\mathrm{MPa}\\ {S}_{xy}=0,65\mathrm{MPa}\\ {S}_{xz}=2,06\mathrm{MPa}\\ {S}_{yz}=1,125\mathrm{MPa}\end{array}$

For boundary conditions, shear is neglected and pressure conditions are applied to the 3 faces $x=l;y=l$ and $z=h$.

In the $(r=R)$ well wall and following , the radial stress is applied: ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}(r=R,t)=-{p}_{w}.H(t)$ where $H(t)$ is the Heaviside step function.

### 1.4 reference solution

In this section we recall the theoretical equations useful for the calculation of radial stress, and pore pressure.

The boundary conditions of the general problem is summarized:

- for $r\to +\infty$

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}=-{S}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}=-{S}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=-{S}_{zz}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}=-{S}_{xy}\\ {\sigma}_{xz}=-{S}_{xz}\\ {\sigma}_{yz}=-{S}_{yz}\end{array}$ et $p={p}_{0}$

- for $r=R$

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=-{p}_{w}.H(t)$

${\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}=0$

$p={p}_{w}.H(t)$

Given the linearity of the problem, this is in fact the result of the superimposition of three sub-problems which allow a three-dimensional study:

- problem 1: A poro-elastic problem is considered in plane deformations (only the main stresses according to $x$ and $y$ axes are taken into account, as well as the shearing in the $(xy)$ plane and the disturbances related to pore pressure). The boundary conditions of this problem is then written:
- for $r\to +\infty$

${\sigma}_{xx}=-{S}_{xx}$

${\sigma}_{yy}=-{S}_{yy}$

${\sigma}_{xy}=-{S}_{xy}$

${\sigma}_{zz}=-{\nu}_{\text{LN}}({S}_{xx}+{S}_{yy})-({\alpha}_{\text{LN}}-2{\nu}_{\text{LN}}{\alpha}_{\text{LT}}){p}_{0}-({\beta}_{3}^{s}-2{\nu}_{\text{LN}}{\beta}_{1}^{s}){T}_{0}$

${\sigma}_{yz}={\sigma}_{xz}=0$

$p={p}_{0}$

- for $r=R$

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=-{p}_{w}.H(t)$

$p={p}_{w}.H(t)$

- problem 2: The $z$-oriented vertical constraint is considered only. The boundary conditions of this problem is then written:
- for $r\to +\infty$

${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{xy}={\sigma}_{xz}={\sigma}_{yz}=p=0$

${\sigma}_{zz}=-{S}_{zz}+({\nu}_{\text{LN}}({S}_{xx}+{S}_{yy})+({\alpha}_{\text{LN}}-2{\nu}_{\text{LN}}{\alpha}_{\text{LT}}){p}_{0})$

${\beta}_{3}^{s}$ and ${\beta}_{1}^{s}$ are defined in the rest of this document.

- for $r=R$

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}={\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathrm{rz}}=p=0$

- Problem 3: The shears in the $(xz)$ and $(yz)$ planes are considered only. The boundary conditions of this problem are then written:
- for $r\to +\infty$

${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{zz}={\sigma}_{xy}=p=0$

${\sigma}_{xz}=-{S}_{xz}$

${\sigma}_{yz}=-{S}_{yz}$

- for $r=R$

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}={\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathrm{rz}}=p=0$

In the next part of this document we will develop the solution of problem 1.

#### 1.4.1 Presentation of the analytical problem

The expression of radial stress, pore pressure and temperature for the flat deformation problem (problem 1 above) is also the result of the superimposition of 3 loading modes, the boundary conditions of which is given below:

- Mode 1: account for the hydrostatic part of the stress at the limits (in $r=R$ )

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(1)}={\sigma}_{0}-{p}_{w}$

${p}^{(1)}=0$

- Mode 2: account for pore pressure and temperature disturbances ($r=R$)

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(2)}=0$

${p}^{(2)}={p}_{w}-{p}_{0}$

- Mode 3: account for the deviatoric part of the constraints at the limits (in $r=R$ )

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(3)}=-{S}_{0}\cos(2(\theta -{\theta}_{r}))$

${p}^{(3)}=0$

with:

${\sigma}_{0}=\frac{({S}_{xx}+{S}_{yy})}{2}$ the average stress, ${S}_{0}=0.5\sqrt{({({S}_{xx}-{S}_{yy})}^{2}+4{S}_{xy}^{2})}$ the deviatoric stress and

$\text{}{\theta}_{r}=0.5\arctan(\frac{2{S}_{xy}}{{S}_{xx}-{S}_{yy}})$

- The general solution for the calculation of the radial stress is therefore given by the following expression:

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=-{\sigma}_{0}+{S}_{0}\cos(2(\theta -{\theta}_{r}))+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(1)}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(2)}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(3)}$

with:

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(1)}=({\sigma}_{0}-{p}_{w})\frac{{R}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}$

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(2)}={L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s}({b}_{L}(1-\frac{{M}_{12}}{{M}_{11}})\left[{F}_{1}\psi (\xi )+{F}_{2}\psi (\omega )\right]+{\beta}_{1}^{s}(1-\frac{{M}_{12}}{{M}_{11}})\left[({T}_{w}-{T}_{0})\right]\psi (\omega ))\right]$

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{(3)}={L}^{-1}\left[\frac{{S}_{0}}{s}\cos(2(\theta -{\theta}_{r}))({A}_{1}{C}_{1}(\frac{1}{\xi r}{K}_{1}(\xi r)+\frac{1}{{(\xi r)}^{2}}{K}_{2}(\xi r))-{A}_{2}{C}_{2}\frac{{R}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}-3{A}_{3}{C}_{3}\frac{{R}^{4}}{{r}^{4}})\right]$

The quantities used are defined at the end of this section.

- The general solution for the calculation of pore pressure is therefore given by the following expression:

$p={p}_{0}+{p}^{(2)}+{p}^{(3)}$

with:

${p}^{(2)}={L}^{-1}\left[\frac{1}{s}\left[{F}_{1}\phi (\xi )+{F}_{2}\phi (\omega )\right]\right]$

${p}^{(3)}={L}^{-1}\left[\frac{{S}_{0}}{s}\cos(2(\theta -{\theta}_{r}))({A}_{1}{C}_{2}\frac{{R}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}+\frac{{c}_{f}}{2{G}_{\text{LT}}\kappa }{C}_{1}{K}_{2}(\xi r))\right]$

The quantities used are defined at the end of this section.

On the other hand, ${K}_{n}$ is defined as the modified functions of Bessel of second species of $n$ (where $n$ is an integer) such as:

${K}_{n}(x)=\begin{array}{c}\frac{1}{2}{(\frac{1}{2}x)}^{-n}{\sum}_{k=0}^{n-1}\left[\frac{(n-k-1)!}{k!}{(\frac{-1}{4}{x}^{2})}^{k}\right]+{(-1)}^{n+1}\ln\left[\frac{1}{2}x\right]{I}_{n}(x)\\ +{(-1)}^{n}\frac{1}{2}{(\frac{1}{2}x)}^{n}{\sum}_{k=0}^{\infty}[(\chi (k+1)+\chi (n+k+1))\frac{{\left[\frac{1}{4}{x}^{2}\right]}^{k}}{k!(n+k)!}]\end{array}$

where ${I}_{n}(x)=\frac{1}{\pi}{\int}_{0}^{\pi}{e}^{\mathit{xcos}\theta }\cos(n\theta )d\theta$ are the modified functions of Bessel of first kind of $n$ (where $n$ is an integer) and $\chi$ the digamma function.

The following quantities can therefore be defined:

$\phi (x)=\frac{{K}_{0}(\mathit{xr})}{{K}_{0}(\mathit{xR})}$

$\psi (x)=\frac{{K}_{1}(\mathit{xr})}{{\mathit{xrK}}_{0}(\mathit{xR})}-\frac{{\mathit{RK}}_{1}(\mathit{xr})}{{\mathit{xr}}^{2}{K}_{0}(\mathit{xR})}$

${F}_{1}=({p}_{w}-{p}_{0})-\frac{{c}_{\mathit{hf}}}{1-\frac{{c}_{f}}{{c}_{h}}}({T}_{w}-{T}_{0})$

${F}_{2}=\frac{{c}_{\mathit{hf}}}{1-\frac{{c}_{f}}{{c}_{h}}}({T}_{w}-{T}_{0})$

${c}_{f}=\frac{\kappa M{M}_{11}}{{M}_{11}+{b}_{L}^{2}M}$

$\kappa =\frac{{K}_{L}^{int}(\phi )}{{\mu}_{w}}$

${c}_{\mathit{hf}}=\frac{{c}_{f}}{\kappa}\left[{\beta}^{\mathit{sf}}-\frac{{b}_{L}{\beta}_{1}^{S}}{{M}_{11}}\right]$

${C}_{1}=\frac{4}{{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}({B}_{3}-{B}_{2})-{A}_{2}{B}_{1}}$

${C}_{1}=\frac{4}{{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}({B}_{3}-{B}_{2})-{A}_{2}{B}_{1}}$

${C}_{3}=\frac{{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}({B}_{3}+{B}_{2})+{\mathrm{3A}}_{2}{B}_{1}}{3({\mathrm{2A}}_{1}({B}_{3}-{B}_{2})-{A}_{2}{B}_{1})}$

${A}_{1}=\frac{{b}_{L}M}{{M}_{11}+{b}_{L}^{2}M}$

${A}_{2}=\frac{{M}_{11}+{M}_{12}+2{b}_{L}^{2}M}{{M}_{11}+{b}_{L}^{2}M}$

${B}_{1}=\frac{{M}_{11}}{2{G}_{\text{LT}}{b}_{L}}{K}_{2}(\xi R)$

${B}_{2}=\frac{1}{\xi R}{K}_{1}(\xi R)+\frac{6}{{(\xi R)}^{2}}{K}_{2}(\xi R)$

${B}_{3}=2(\frac{1}{\xi R}{K}_{1}(\xi R)+\frac{3}{{(\xi R)}^{2}}{K}_{2}(\xi R))$

$\xi =\sqrt{\frac{s}{{c}_{f}}}$ and $\omega =\sqrt{\frac{s}{{c}_{h}}}$ where $s$ represents the variable of Laplace.

#### 1.4.2 Analytical solution resolution method

As we have seen before, the solutions for modes 2 and 3 are expressed in the space of Laplace. Given the complexity of the inversion of the solution to time space, we use an inversion algorithm called *Stehfest-Graver.* This method is known for its numerical stability.

The method principle is to approximate the $\tilde{f}(s)$ reverse function using a delta series using the following formula:

$f(t)\mathrm{\simeq }\frac{\ln(2)}{t}{\sum}_{i=1}^{N}{V}_{i}\tilde{f}(i\frac{\ln(2)}{t})$

où ${V}_{i}={(-1)}^{i+(N/2)}\frac{{\sum}_{k=E((i+1)/2)}^{min(i,N/2)}{k}^{N/2}(\mathrm{2k})!}{(\frac{N}{2}-k)!k!(k-1)!(i-k)!(\mathrm{2k}-i)!}$

with $E(x)$ the entire part function and $N$ the number of terms in the series. It is usually between 10 and 20. In our case we chose $N=18$

### 2.2 Results

The radial stresses are represented for an $\theta =90°$ angle, i.e. according to the $y$ axis. The modelling and analytical results are represented. If the overall appearance is the same, however, there are differences which are logical: first for reasons of time calculations the mesh is here coarse especially in thickness $h$. Moreover, we are quite far from an infinite geometry and shear stresses at the edge are not applied. Finally, the actual/total stress formalism proposed by Abousleiman is slightly different from ours and the comparison requires some adjustments.

Given this, it can be considered that this agreement is entirely acceptable.
