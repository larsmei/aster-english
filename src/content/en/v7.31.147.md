# v7.31.147 WTNV147- Hydromechanical coupling in poro-elastic and fractured column: use of the XFEM method

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to ensure that the hydrodynamic coupling between the cohesive interface and the porous matrix is working properly for hydromechanical elements coupled with XFEM. This test is therefore purely hydrodynamic, all displacement degrees of freedom are blocked at zero. Modelling A is two-dimensional while Modelling B is three-dimensional.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of the problem

This column is $\mathit{LZ}=5m$ height, $\mathit{LX}=1m$ length and $\mathit{LY}=1m$ width. This column has an interface-type discontinuity in $Z=\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}$. The column is thus completely crossed by discontinuity.

### 1.3 boundary conditions

The following Dirichlet conditions shall be applied:

- throughout the domain, the displacements are blocked at zero (${u}_{\text{x}}=0$ , ${u}_{\text{y}}=0$ and ${u}_{\text{z}}=0$ in the three-dimensional case),
- on the [ABCD] and [EFGH] sides, the pore pressure is blocked at zero,
- In the cohesive interface, a fluid pressure is imposed ${p}_{\text{f}}=10\mathit{MPa}$ .

The initial pore pressure in the matrix is zero. The test is performed over a total duration of $t=10s$. The $\theta$ parameter is taken equal to $0,56999999$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The ${p}_{f}$ fluid pressure imposed in the cohesive interface is passed on to the pore pressure at the lips of the cohesive interface (see [R7.02.18](doc:r7.02.18) Documentation). Given the boundary conditions, everything happens as if a pore pressure step were imposed on the end of the two subcolumns formed by the cohesive interface. The problem is therefore unidirectional. These two subcolumns are frozen because the movements are blocked. By neglecting the gravity, the mass conservation equation in the porous matrix is written as follows:

$\frac{\partial {m}_{w}}{\partial t}+\nabla \cdot M=0$

With ${m}_{w}=\rho \varphi (1+{{\varepsilon}}_{v})$ the mass inputs in the porous matrix and $M=\frac{-\rho {K}^{int}}{{\mu}_{w}}\nabla p$ the Darcean fluid flux in the porous matrix. With the movements blocked, the ${{\varepsilon}}_{v}$ volume deformation is zero. $d\varphi =(b-\varphi )(d{{\varepsilon}}_{v}+\frac{\mathit{dp}}{{K}_{s}})$ with ${K}_{s}$ the compressibility module of the solid matrix. Now the solid matrix is assumed to be incompressible because we took $b=1$ (and $b=1-\frac{{K}_{m}}{{K}_{s}}$ with ${K}_{S}$ the compressibility module of the porous medium). So finally $d\varphi =0$. Finally $\frac{d\rho }{\rho}=\frac{\mathit{dp}}{{K}_{w}}$ . The mass conservation equation is rewritten therefore:

$\frac{\rho \varphi }{{K}_{w}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}-\frac{{K}^{int}}{{\mu}_{w}}[\nabla p\cdot \nabla \rho +\rho \Delta p]=0$

By neglecting the term of the second order $\nabla p\cdot \nabla \rho$, it comes:

$\frac{\varphi {\mu}_{w}}{{K}_{w}{K}^{int}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}=\Delta p$

This is a scatter equation. The $\frac{\varphi {\mu}_{w}}{{K}_{w}{K}^{int}}$ coefficient is a dimension of a time over a squared length. In our problem, the characteristic dimension for diffusion is $\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}$. This is derived from a characteristic diffusion time $\tau =\frac{\varphi {\mu}_{w}}{{K}_{w}{K}^{int}}\ast {(\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2})}^{2}$. This characteristic time is $\tau =0,459842s$. At the end of $t=10s$, the diffusion is completed, the pore pressure in the porous matrix then checks $\Delta p=0$.

Under the boundary conditions, below the interface:

- the pore pressure is $p(y)=\frac{2y}{\mathit{LZ}}{p}_{f}$ and the fluid flow in the matrix is $M=\frac{-2\rho {K}^{int}{p}_{f}}{\mathit{LZ}{\mu}_{w}}$.

And above the interface:

- the pore pressure is $p(y)=(1-\frac{y}{\mathit{LZ}})2{p}_{f}$ and the fluid flow in the matrix is $M=\frac{2\rho {K}^{int}{p}_{f}}{\mathit{LZ}{\mu}_{w}}$.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a D_PLAN_HM Modelling using quadratic elements HM-XFEM. The bar on which the Modelling is performed is divided into 5 QUAD8. The interface is unmeshed and cuts the central element. Thus, we have 3 elements HM-XFEM and 2 elements HM classic. As shown in Figure , the 3 elements XFEM undergo sub-division in triangles (to perform the integration of Gauss-Legendre on either side of the interface lips, but these triangular sub-elements are not elements mesh's).

The mesh consists of 5 mesh cells quadratic quadrangles (QUAD8).

## 5 Summary of the results

This test validates the hydrodynamic coupling between the cohesive interface and the porous matrix for HM-XFEM cohesive elements, especially via the output fluxes of the cohesive interface LAG_FLI and LAG_FLS. The correct consideration of enrichment in the pore pressure field approximation by the Heaviside function is also valid.
