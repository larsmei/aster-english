# u4.52.02 Operator CALC_MODES

## 1 Purpose

Compute the vibration eigenmodes or the Euler buckling modes of a mechanical structure.

The operator CALC_MODES solves, for this purpose, a modal problem which may be either generalised (GEP)
[[R5.01.01]](https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.01.pdf), or quadratic (QEP)
[[R5.01.02]](https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.02.pdf).

The user specifies the mode search criterion (for example: over a band; in the vicinity of given values; etc.) by means of the keyword OPTION. Depending on the value of this keyword, the general analysis method (simultaneous iterations or inverse iterations) is then determined automatically.

Within each of these two general methods, variants exist:

- case of simultaneous iterations: subspace-type method (Bathe & Wilson, Lanczos or Sorensen), global QR-type method (QZ for small problems);
- case of inverse iterations: direct method or method accelerated by the Rayleigh quotient.

These variants are accessed with a factor keyword SOLVEUR_MODAL.

In the case of an analysis of vibration eigenmodes, post-processing may also be carried out: normalisation of the modes according to a given criterion, filtering of the modes according to a given criterion, production of the modal parameters...

This operator produces a concept mode_meca_* (dynamic case) or mode_flamb (Euler buckling case, only in GEP) or mode_gene according to the value entered under the keyword TYPE_RESU and the type of the input matrices of the modal problem.

The concept mode_meca_*, or mode_flamb, contains the eigenvalues and the eigenvectors, and modal parameters, cf.
[[R5.01.03]](https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.03.pdf)
`  <[https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.03.pdf](https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.03.pdf)>`_.

As a first approach one may simply fill in the following parameters: OPTION to define the mode search criterion, TYPE_RESU, the input matrices of the modal problem MATR_*, CALC_FREQ (or CALC_CHAR_CRIT).

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Principles

This operator solves the following **generalised eigenvalue problem (GEP)** [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01):

Find $(\lambda ,\mathrm{x})$ such that $\mathrm{A}\mathrm{x}=\lambda \mathrm{B}\mathrm{x}$ , $\mathrm{x}\mathrm{\ne }0$ , where $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathrm{B}$ are real matrices, symmetric or not. To model hysteretic damping in the study of the free vibrations of a structure, the matrix $\mathrm{A}$ may be complex symmetric [U2.06.03](doc:u2.06.03) [R5.05.04](doc:r5.05.04).

This type of problem corresponds, in mechanics, in particular to:

- **The study of the free vibrations of a structure** which is undamped and non-rotating. For this structure, one looks for the smallest eigenvalues or those which lie in a given interval in order to know whether an exciting force can create a resonance. In this case, the matrix $\mathrm{A}$ is the material stiffness matrix, denoted $\mathrm{K}$ , real symmetric (possibly augmented by the geometric stiffness matrix denoted ${\mathrm{K}}_{g}$ , if the structure is prestressed), and $\mathrm{B}$ is the mass or inertia matrix denoted $\mathrm{M}$ (real symmetric). The eigenvalues obtained are the squares of the angular frequencies associated with the frequencies sought. The system to be solved may be written

$\underset{\mathrm{A}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{(\mathrm{K}+{\mathrm{K}}_{g})}}\mathrm{x}=\lambda \underset{\mathrm{B}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\mathrm{M}}}\mathrm{x}$

where $\lambda ={(2\pi f)}^{2}$ is the square of the angular frequency $\omega$ , $f$ the eigenfrequency and $\mathrm{x}$ the associated eigen displacement vector. The eigenmodes handled $(\lambda ,x)$ are real-valued. This type of problem is activated by the keyword TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE' and generates a data structure of type mode_meca, mode_acou or mode_gene (depending on the type of the input data).

- **The search for a linear buckling mode**. Within the framework of linearised theory, assuming *a priori* that the stability phenomena are adequately described by the system of equations obtained by assuming the linear dependence of the displacement with respect to the load level, the search for the buckling mode **x** associated with this critical load level $\mu =-\lambda$ . This reduces to a generalised eigenvalue problem of the form:

$(\mathrm{K}+\mu {\mathrm{K}}_{g})x=0\mathrm{\iff }\underset{\mathrm{A}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\mathrm{K}}}\mathrm{x}=\lambda \underset{\mathrm{B}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\mathrm{K}}_{g}}}\mathrm{x}$

with $\mathrm{K}$ the material stiffness matrix and ${\mathrm{K}}_{g}$ the geometric stiffness matrix. The eigenmodes handled $(\lambda ,x)$ are real-valued. This type of problem is activated by the keyword TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB' and generates a data structure of type mode_flamb.

*Caution:*

- *In Code_Aster, one* *produces the eigenvalues of the generalised problem,* *that is to say* *the variables* $\lambda$ *. To obtain the true critical loads, the variables* $\mu$ *, they must be multiplied by –1.*

*Caution:*

- *In GEP, to treat problems with complex modes (non-symmetric matrices and/or with complex values), one must use the* modal solver with METHODE='SORENSEN' *or* 'QZ' *.*

This operator also makes it possible to study the **dynamic stability of a structure in the presence of damping and/or gyroscopic effects**. This leads to the solution of a modal problem of higher order, called quadratic **(QEP)** [R5.01.02](doc:r5.01.02). One then looks for complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors $(\lambda ,\mathrm{x})$ .

- The problem consists in finding $(\lambda ,\mathrm{x})\in (\mathrm{ℂ},{\mathrm{ℂ}}^{N})$ such that:

$({\lambda}^{2}\mathrm{B}+\lambda \mathrm{C}+\mathrm{A})\mathrm{x}=0$

where typically, in linear mechanics, $\mathrm{A}$ will be the stiffness matrix, $\mathrm{B}$ the mass matrix and $\mathrm{C}$ the damping matrix. The matrices $\mathrm{A}$ , $\mathrm{B}$ and $\mathrm{C}$ are symmetric real matrices. The complex eigenvalue $\lambda$ is related to the eigenfrequency $f$ and to the reduced damping $\xi$ by $\lambda =\xi (2\pi f)\pm i(2\pi f)\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}$ . This type of problem is activated by the keyword TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE' and generates a data structure of type mode_meca_c. The complex angular frequency is therefore $\omega = i \lambda =\pm |\omega | \sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}} + i | \omega | \xi$

*Caution:*

- *In QEP, to treat problems with non-symmetric matrices and/or with complex values, one must use the* modal solver with METHODE='SORENSEN' or 'QZ' *.*
- *Buckling (* TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB' *) is not allowed in QEP.*
- *All the options work whatever the taking into account of the boundary conditions (* AFFE_CHAR_MECA or AFFE_CHAR_CINE *).*

***The Sturm test***

[R5.01.04] <https://code-aster.org/V2/doc/default/fr/man_r/r5/r5.01.04.pdf>_*is operative only in GEP with real symmetric matrices. Outside this framework (QEP, GEP with non-symmetric real matrices or with a complex symmetric matrix* $A$ *), the option ‘BANDE’ is prohibited and the post-verification based on* *the* *Sturm test is not activated (parameter* *VERI_MODE/STURM inoperative).*

**To capture a significant part of the spectrum,** it is preferable to use the values **'PLUS_PETITE'** **,** **'PLUS_GRANDE'** **,** **'CENTRE'** or **'BANDE'** of the keyword **OPTION,** which use a method of type **“simultaneous iterations”**: subspace methods (Lanczos, IRAM, Jacobi) or the global QZ method (very robust but costly method; to be reserved for small problems).

On the other hand, **when it is a matter of determining a few simple, well-separated eigenvalues or of refining a few estimates,** the values **'SEPARE',** **'AJUSTE' or** **'PROCHE'** of the keyword OPTION (which use a method of type “**inverse iterations”**) are often well suited.

It is moreover quite recommended to take advantage of the strong points of the two classes of method by refining the eigenvectors previously obtained by a simultaneous iteration method, *via* the inverse iteration method **.** This will make it possible to reduce the norm of the final residual. This is what the post-processing keyword 'AMELIORATION' makes it possible to do, cf. §3.13.

Thus, even if the options of the simultaneous iteration methods (**'PLUS_PETITE/GRANDE',** **'CENTRE'** and **'BANDE'**) are often preferable, one can compute up to about ten modes with the options of the power-type methods: 'SEPARE', 'AJUSTE' or 'PROCHE'. To compute tens or even hundreds of modes, one must favour, when it is possible, the option 'BANDE'. This improves the robustness, the quality and the performance of the analysis.

In the standard case of a real symmetric GEP, ideally one should organise the analysis into several sub-bands each containing between 20 and 60 modes. With, if possible, a splitting that is homogeneous in number of modes (an “imbalance” of less than 3 1
).

To do this, one may proceed in several stages:

- **Calibrate** the zones of interest by an initial call to INFO_MODE [U4.52.01] on a given list of frequencies 2 (resp. critical loads),
- **Look at** the numbers of eigenmodes printed in the message file (or in the generated sd_table),
- **Relaunch** one or more CALC_MODES analyses with OPTION='BANDE' while trying to **balance** the bands.

*Note* *:*

- *If one finally computes only a single band, in order to save time, one may even share part of the computational cost of the initial* INFO_MODE *by notifying* CALC_MODES *of the name of the generated* sd_table *(cf. keywords* TABLE_). This chaining can thus make the extra cost of* INFO_MODE *negligible and efficiently guide the modal analysis.*

On the other hand, as soon as one treats a QEP or an atypical GEP (complex and/or non-symmetric matrix), the spectrum becomes complex. The chaining INFO_MODE + CALC_MODES is then no longer possible. Certain keywords or values become irrelevant (OPTION='BANDE', VERI_MODE/STURM...).

*Note* **:**

- One strongly advises a prior reading of the reference documents [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) [R5.01.02](doc:r5.01.02) and [R5.01.04](doc:r5.01.04). It gives the user the properties and the limitations, theoretical and practical, of the modal methods discussed, while relating these considerations to a precise setting of the options.

### 3.2 Keyword TYPE_RESU

```text
◊    TYPE_RESU=        /‘DYNAMIQUE’            [DEFAUT]
/‘MODE_FLAMB’
/'GENERAL'
```

This keyword makes it possible to define the nature of the modal problem to be treated: search for vibration frequencies (classical dynamic case with or without damping and gyroscopic effects) or search for critical loads (case of linear buckling theory, only in GEP). Depending on this class, the results are displayed and stored differently in the data structure:

- **In dynamics** (TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE', the frequencies are ordered by increasing order of the modulus of their distance from the shift (cf. [R5.01.01/02], §3.8/2.5). This is the value of the access variable NUME_ORDRE of the data structure. The other access variable, NUME_MODE, is equal to the true modal position in the spectrum of the eigenvalue (determined by the Sturm test cf. [R5.01.04](doc:r5.01.04)). This Sturm test is allowed only in GEP with real modes (real symmetric matrices); in the other cases (GEP with complex modes and QEP), one sets NUME_MODE=NUME_ORDRE.
- **In buckling** (TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB'), the eigenvalues are stored in algebraically increasing order. The variables NUME_ORDRE and NUME_MODE take the same value equal to this order.
- General case (TYPE_RESU='GENERAL'): same as for the buckling case.

*Note* **:**

- *The* TYPE_RESU='GENERAL' *makes it possible to solve an eigenvalue problem in the case of a* **general matrix system**. *For the moment its scope is limited to standard GEPs (real symmetric matrices). Its only difference from* MODE_FLAMB *is therefore in the naming of the matrices:* MATR_A / MATR_B *rather than* MATR_RIGI / MATR_RIGI_GEOM *..*

Keyword making it possible to set the algorithms and the parameters of the modal solver.

#### 3.7.1 Keywords associated with the simultaneous iteration method

These keywords are usable only if the value of the keyword OPTION is among 'PLUS_PETITE', 'PLUS_GRANDE', 'BANDE', 'CENTRE', 'TOUT'.

##### 3.7.1.1 Keyword METHODE

Four solution methods are then available to solve the eigenvalue problem:

```text
◊    METHODE=/'SORENSEN'        [DEFAUT]
```

One uses the Sorensen method (external package ARPACK) to compute the eigenmodes of the GEP or of the QEP (cf. [R5.01.01/02] §7/4). Its scope covers real matrices, symmetric or not, or even a complex symmetric matrix $A$.

```text
/'TRI_DIAG'
```

One uses the Lanczos method (Newmann-Pipano variant in GEP, Parlett & Saad variant in QEP) to compute the eigenmodes of the GEP or of the QEP (cf. [R5.01.01/02] §6/4). Its scope is limited to real symmetric matrices.

```text
/'JACOBI'
```

One uses the Bathe & Wilson method (then the Jacobi method on the projected system) to compute the eigenmodes of the GEP (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §8). Its scope is limited to real symmetric matrices.

```text
/'QZ'
```

One uses the QZ method of the external library LAPACK to compute the eigenmodes of the GEP or of the QEP (cf. [R5.01.01/02] §9/5). Its scope covers real matrices, symmetric or not, or even a complex symmetric matrix $\mathrm{A}$. This very costly reference method is to be reserved for problems of small size (<103 degrees of freedom).

##### 3.7.1.2 Parameters related to the solution method

Each of these solution methods has different settings:

- if METHODE=‘SORENSEN’:

```text
◊    PREC_SOREN=pso            (0.)        [DEFAUT]
```

**Note:**

- *The method then considers that it must work with the smallest possible precision, the “machine zero”. To give an order of magnitude, in double precision on standard machines, this value is close to* $2.22{.10}^{-16}$ *.*

```text
◊    NMAX_ITER_SOREN=nso            (20)        [DEFAUT]

◊    PARA_ORTHO_SOREN=porso        (0.717)    [DEFAUT]
```

These are adjustment parameters for the accuracy required on the modes (by default, machine precision is chosen), for the number of restarts allowed for the Sorensen method (*cf.* [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §7) and for the orthogonalisation coefficient of the IGSM of Kahan-Parlett (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) appendix 2).

If the coefficient porso is negative, the reorthogonalisation is carried out on all the computed modes instead of targeting only the modes belonging to the same eigenspace. The analysis may then be two or three times more costly.

For more information on the mode of selection of the eigenspaces one may consult the parameters SEUIL_FREQ/CHAR_CRIT.

**Note:**

- *On the first passes, it is strongly advised not to modify these advanced parameters of the algorithm, which are initialised empirically to standard values.*

- if METHODE=‘TRI_DIAG' :

```text
◊    PREC_ORTHO    =po            (1.10-12)            [DEFAUT]

◊    NMAX_ITER_ORTHO=nio        (5)                    [DEFAUT]

◊    PREC_LANCZOS=pl            (1.10-8)            [DEFAUT]

◊    NMAX_ITER_QR=nim            (30)                [DEFAUT]

◊    MODE_RIGIDE=/‘OUI’

 /'NON'                            [DEFAUT]
```

The first two parameters make it possible, respectively, to adjust the orthogonalisation accuracy and the number of reorthogonalisations in the Lanczos method in order to obtain independent vectors spanning the subspace (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §6).

The third is an adjustment parameter for determining the nullity of a term on the superdiagonal of the tridiagonal matrix characterising the reduced problem obtained by the Lanczos method. It is merely a deflation criterion and not, contrary to what its name might suggest, a quality criterion for the modes.

The fourth sets the maximum number of iterations for the solution of the reduced system for the QR method ([R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) appendix 1).

```text
Le mot-clé MODE_RIGIDE permet de détecter et de calculer au préalable, par une méthode algébrique, les modes de corps rigide. Ils sont utilisés par la suite pour calculer les autres modes avec l’algorithme de Lanczos. Ils sont fournis à l’utilisateur seulement s’ils font partie des modes demandés. Si les modes de corps rigide sont calculés sans utiliser cette option, les valeurs propres calculées par l’algorithme de Lanczos ne sont pas nulles mais très voisines de zéro.
```

*Note* **:**

- *On the first passes, it is strongly advised not to modify these parameters, which rather concern the inner workings of the algorithm and which are initialised empirically to standard values.*

- if METHODE=‘JACOBI' :

```text
◊    PREC_BATHE    =pbat                (1.10-10)        [DEFAUT]

◊    NMAX_ITER_BATHE=nbat            (40)            [DEFAUT]

◊    PREC_JACOBI=pjaco                (1.10-2)        [DEFAUT]

◊    NMAX_ITER_JACOBI=njaco            (12)            [DEFAUT]
```

The first two parameters make it possible, respectively, to adjust the convergence accuracy and the maximum number of iterations allowed for the Bathe & Wilson method (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §8).

The other two adjust the convergence accuracy and the maximum number of iterations of the JACOBI method (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) appendix 3). This global modal solver is used to compute the eigenmodes of the matrix projected according to the Bathe & Wilson algorithm.

*Note* **:**

- *On the first passes, it is strongly advised not to modify these parameters, which rather concern the inner workings of the algorithm and which are initialised empirically to standard values.*

- if METHODE=‘QZ’:

```text
◊    TYPE_QZ =    /'QZ_SIMPLE'    [DEFAUT]
/'QZ_EQUI'
/'QZ_QR'
```

This parameter makes it possible to choose one of the variants of the QZ algorithm proposed by LAPACK. The first choice (‘QZ_SIMPLE’) designates the basic method, the second (‘QZ_EQUI’) adds to it a preprocessing of balancing of the terms of the matrix. This often improves the quality of the modes but, conversely, if the matrix has very small terms due to rounding errors, this phase then generates spurious modes.

As for the third choice (‘QZ_QR'), it is reserved for the symmetric positive definite case (real stiffness matrix, Dirichlet condition without Lagrange, no buckling and no damping). It is much faster than the previous options.

##### 3.7.1.4 Keywords DIM_SOUS_ESPACE and COEF_SOUS_ESPACE

> ◊    DIM_SOUS_ESPACE=des
> ◊    COEF_DIM_ESPACE=mse

> EXCLUS(‘DIM_SOUS_ESPACE’,’COEF_DIM_ESPACE’)

If the keyword DIM_SOUS_ESPACE is not filled in or is initialised to a value strictly less than the number of requested frequencies nf, the operator automatically computes an admissible dimension for the projection subspace using COEF_DIM_ESPACE (cf. § 5 of this document and [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §5.3).

Thanks to the datum of this multiplicative factor, mse, one can project onto a space whose size is proportional to the number of frequencies contained in the interval under study.

If one looks for eigenmodes over a band split into several sub-bands (OPTION='BANDE' and FREQ is a list of at least 3 values), one can therefore optimise the size of the subspaces, which remains proportional to the number of frequencies sought on each sub-band: the subspaces rich in eigenvalues thus do not penalise the poorer ones (in terms of CPU).

One may however set arbitrarily the size of this subspace, *via* the value taken by the keyword DIM_SOUS_ESPACE (which must be greater than nf in order to be taken into account).

In both cases, if the size of the projection subspace ndim is strictly greater than the number of “active degrees of freedom”, nactif (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §3.2), then one forces it to take this ceiling value.

*Notes* **:**

- *If one uses the Sorensen method (IRAM) and* ndim-nf *<* 2 *, numerical and software requirements force one to impose* ndim=nf+2 .*

*In quadratic, one works on a real problem of double size:* 2*nf, 2*ndim .*

- *These parameters are useless for the method ‘* QZ *’.*

### 3.10 Operands common to CALC_FREQ and CALC_CHAR_CRIT: SEUIL_FREQ / SEUIL_CHAR_CRIT, PREC_SHIFT, NMAX_ITER_SHIFT

These operands are located inside the keyword CALC_FREQ (case TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE') or CALC_CHAR_CRIT (case TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB' or 'GENERAL').

```text
# SI TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE'
◊    PREC_SHIFT        =    p_shift            (0.05)        [DEFAUT]
◊    SEUIL_FREQ        =    f_seuil            (0.01)        [DEFAUT]
◊     NMAX_ITER_SHIFT    =    n_shift            (3)            [DEFAUT]

# SI TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB'ou 'GENERAL'
◊    PREC_SHIFT        =    p_shift            (0.05)        [DEFAUT]
◊    SEUIL_CHAR_CRIT    =    c_seuil            (0.01)        [DEFAUT]
◊    NMAX_ITER_SHIFT    =    n_shift            (3)            [DEFAUT]
```

The course of a modal analysis in this operator requires the factorisation ${\mathit{LDL}}^{T}$ of dynamic matrices $\mathrm{Q}(\lambda )$ of the type (cf. [R5.01.01/02] §2.5/3.8):

$\mathrm{Q}(\lambda ):=\mathrm{A}-\lambda \mathrm{B}$ (in GEP)

$\mathrm{Q}(\lambda ):={\lambda}^{2}\mathrm{B}+\lambda \mathrm{C}+\mathrm{A}$ (in QEP)

These factorisations are subject to numerical instabilities when the shift $\lambda$ is close to an eigenvalue of the problem. This detection is carried out by comparing the loss of decimal places of the diagonal terms of this factorised matrix with respect to their initial values (in absolute value). If the maximum of this loss is greater than ndeci 7
, the matrix is assumed to be singular and one looks for a shifted value of the shift yielding an invertible matrix.

For the GEPs, the parameters SEUIL_* make it possible to define the “modal zero”, that is to say the value below which one considers that an eigenvalue is zero. As a corollary, in certain processing of the operator, if the gap between two eigenvalues is less than this value, one considers that they coincide. One must therefore adjust this value according to the average amplitude of the eigenvalues sought.

If one is in dynamics one transforms this value into an angular frequency:

$\mathit{omecor}={(2\pi \text{f\_seuil})}^{2}$

whereas in buckling one keeps it as it is:

$\mathit{omecor}=\text{c\_seuil}$.

For the QEPs, this value of the “modal zero” is used during the sorting carried out at the end of the modal analysis. During this sorting, one seeks to determine whether a mode is real (one does not keep it), complex conjugate (one keeps the one with positive imaginary part) or unpaired complex (one does not keep it). Two eigenvalues ${\lambda}_{1,}{\lambda}_{2}$ are considered to be conjugates if:

$|{{\lambda}_{1}-\overline{\lambda}}_{2}|<\mathit{omecor}$

*Notes* **:**

- *An* eigenvalue *is considered* *to be real* *if its imaginary part is less than* SEUILR=1E-7 *(hard-coded value initialised in the sorting routines).*
- *When* eigenvalues *have been sorted* *as purely* real *or unpaired complex, an information message or an alarm appears (* ALGELINE4_87/88 *) depending on the case.*
- *With the Sorensen and QZ methods, in standard GEP (real symmetric), the parameters* * _seuil are used to determine whether two modes must be orthogonalised or not (when selective orthogonalisation is activated, as is the case by default, cf. PARA_ORTHO_SOREN). Two modes are considered to be “multiple”, hence to be reorthogonalised, if their moduli are both less than 100* omecor or, otherwise, if their relative gap is less than omecor. This reorthogonalisation is costly but indispensable for the subsequent projections onto a modal basis, hence the need for balanced values for this criterion. Normally the values set by default are sufficient and they do not have to be modified often.

The other parameters, PREC_SHIFT and NMAX_ITER_SHIFT, are linked to the algorithm for shifting the bounds of the interval $[{f}_{min},{f}_{max}]`(cf. [:external:ref:`R5.01.04 <R5.01.04>$] §3.2), when one realises that these are very close to an eigenvalue. Roughly, these bounds ${f}_{\min}`(or :math:`{\lambda}_{\min}$ in buckling) or ${f}_{\max}`(resp. :math:`{\lambda}_{\max}$) are then shifted towards the outside of the segment by p_shift%. If the dynamic matrix thus rebuilt is still judged “numerically singular”, one shifts again after having emitted an ALARME. One attempts this shift n_shift times:

${{lambda}^{-}}_{min}={lambda}_{min}-max(mathit{omecor}{,2}^{(i-1)}text{x}{p}_{mathit{shift}}text{x}∣({lambda}_{min})∣)$(i-th attempt)

${{lambda}^{-}}_{max}={lambda}_{max}+max(mathit{omecor}{,2}^{(i-1)}text{x}{p}_{mathit{shift}}text{x}∣({lambda}_{max})∣)$(i-th attempt)

In fact, in dynamics as in buckling, the shift is carried out in the same way. Strictly speaking, in dynamics it is therefore not the frequencies that one shifts, but the angular frequencies.

Another clarification: the shift is in fact, for the sake of efficiency, dichotomic: p_shift% the first time, 2xp_shift% the second time, etc. This procedure must make it possible to move quickly away from the “singularity zone” at lower cost. Conversely, one must not increase the values of these parameters too much, because by dint of shifts, the resulting bounds may turn out to be very different from the initial bounds.

In addition, in order to remain consistent with the “modal zero” (denoted here omecor ):

- one does not shift by a value less than this minimum (hence the max in the formulae above);
- if from the outset, the bound considered is less than this “zero” $∣{\lambda}_{\text{*}}∣<\mathit{omecor}$ (in absolute value) one sets it to plus or minus this value (depending on whether this bound is positive or negative). One then no longer allows any shift.

*Notes* :

- *A bound of the interval* $\sigma$ *is close to an eigenvalue, when the LDLT factorisation of the dynamic matrix associated with this bound (for example that of a GEP is written* $\mathrm{Q}(\sigma ):=\mathrm{A}-\sigma \mathrm{B}$ *), leads to a loss of decimal places of more than* *NPREC digits (value set under the keyword* *SOLVEUR). By playing on the value of this parameter (NPREC=7, 8 or 9), one can then avoid the costly refactorisations implied by these shifts when this numerical singularity is not very pronounced.*
- *Likewise, by playing on the numerical parameters of the linear solvers (for example:* METHODE, RENUM, PRETRAITEMENTS *...), one can also influence this singularity criterion.*
- *One implements this shift technique in two situations: computation of a Sturm test (pre- and/or post-processing) and construction of the working dynamic matrix. In the event of failure of the shift algorithm: in the first case, one emits an* ALARME *, in the second, one stops in* ERREUR_FATALE *.*

Splitting into several sub-bands is to be favoured when one treats problems **of medium or large size** (> 0.5M degrees of freedom) and/or when one looks for a **good part of their spectra** (> 50 modes).

One then splits the analysis into several frequency sub-bands (cf. operand FREQ). On each of these sub-bands, a modal solver carries out the associated mode search. To do this, this modal solver makes intensive use of a linear solver.

These two computational building blocks (modal solver and linear solver) are the **stages which dominate** the analysis in terms of memory consumption and time. It is on them that one must concentrate if one wants to reduce significantly the computational costs of this operator.

Now, the organisation of the modal analysis over distinct sub-bands offers here an ideal framework for parallelism: **distribution of large, almost independent analyses** [11]_
. Its parallelism makes it possible to gain a great deal in time but at the price of an extra cost in memory [12]_
.

If one has a sufficient number of processors (greater than the number of non-empty sub-bands), one can then switch on a **second level of parallelism** **via the linear solver** (if one has chosen METHODE='MUMPS'). This will make it possible to continue to gain in time but above all, it will make it possible to compensate the memory extra cost of the first level or even to reduce notably the sequential memory peak.

This double level of parallelism (activated by default *via* the keyword NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='COMPLET') then makes it possible to take advantage, in the best way, of both aspects.

When one truly wishes to gain in memory peak because the analysis does not fit on the machine and one has tried, without success, all the other levers [13]_
, one may deliberately choose to limit the parallelism to the linear solver only [14]_
: NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='PARTIEL'. This works only with the parallel linear solver MUMPS.

**The functional rules** are the following, denoting by nbproc the number of processors set and by nb_sbande the number of non-empty sub-bands (=nb_freq-1):

- *With* *NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='COMPLET' (default):* very large gain in time / moderate improvement or deterioration of the RAM memory peak.

- *With* *NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='PARTIEL'* : moderate gain in time / large gain on the RAM memory peak.

![Figure](/figures/u4.52.02/Cadre2.gif)

For an **optimal use** of the parallelism, it is therefore advised to:

- Build relatively balanced computation sub-bands. To do this, one may therefore, beforehand, calibrate the spectrum under study *via* one or more calls to INFO_MODE[U4.52.01]. If possible in parallel mode. Then launch the CALC_MODES analysis in parallel according to the number of sub-bands chosen and the number of processors available.
- Take sub-bands finer than in sequential, between 10 and 20 modes instead of 40 to 80 modes in sequential. The quality of the modes and the robustness of the analysis will thereby be increased. The memory peak will be reduced. One must however have enough processors available (and with enough memory).
- Select a number of processors which is a multiple of the number of (non-empty) sub-bands. Thus, one reduces the load imbalances which harm performance.

To reduce the memory peak of an analysis, one has several levers: reduce the size of the sub-bands, use the linear solver MUMPS (possibly in OUT_OF_CORE[U4.50.01]) and/or parallelise only this computational building block (NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='PARTIEL').

To use CALC_MODES efficiently in parallel, one therefore proposes to proceed in three stages:

- Prior **modal pre-calibrations** *via* INFO_MODE. If possible, in parallel mode (Potential gains in time x70 on about a hundred processors. Gain in RAM memory peak up to x2).
- Examine the results produced and decompose the analysis into sub-bands of modest size (for example 20 modes) and balanced, according to the number of processors available.
- Launch, in POURSUITE mode, the CALC_MODES analysis proper in parallel.

### 3.15 Keyword AMELIORATION

```text
◊    AMELIORATION=        /'NON'        [DEFAUT]
/'OUI'
```

Keyword making it possible to improve automatically the quality of the computed modes: mainly the mode shapes (which results in a decrease of the error norm) and the eigenvalues. This improvement is carried out by a chaining of two modal analyses: a first one, preferably carried out by a simultaneous iteration method (keyword OPTION='BANDE' or 'PLUS_PETITE' or 'CENTRE' or 'TOUT'); the second is carried out in a way that is transparent to the user, with the inverse iteration method (OPTION='PROCHE') with, as input, the eigenvalues estimated thanks to the first analysis.

*Notes* **:**

- *The quality of the modes is generally already sufficient at the end of a first analysis. This option, despite its cost, may however prove very useful for complicated models.*
- *When one activates this improvement option, the post-verification tests, normally carried out at the end of the first stage (according to the setting of* *VERI_MODE), are carried out here only at the end of the second.*

## 5 Execution phase

- In the case where it is the simultaneous iteration method which is used (i.e. if OPTION='BANDE' or 'CENTRE' or 'PLUS_PETITE' or 'PLUS_GRANDE' or 'TOUT'):

if the keyword DIM_SOUS_ESPACE (under the factor keyword SOLVEUR_MODAL) is not filled in or is initialised to a value strictly less than the number of requested modes nf (operand NMAX_FREQ or NMAX_CHAR_CRIT), the operator automatically computes an admissible dimension for the projection subspace *via* the empirical formulae (cf § 3.7.1.4 ):

```text
METHODE='SORENSEN'

ndim=MIN(MAX(2+nf,mse*nf),nactif) avec mse=2 par défaut.
```

```text
METHODE='TRI_DIAG'

ndim=MIN(MAX(7+nf,mse*nf),nactif ) avec mse=4 par défaut.
```

```text
METHODE='JACOBI'

ndim=MIN(MAX(7+nf,mse*nf),nactif) avec mse=2 par défaut.
```

where nactif is the number of active degrees of freedom (that is to say the total number of degrees of freedom minus the number of LAGRANGE degrees of freedom and minus the number of linear relations which link degrees of freedom together, [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01) §3.2) and mse is the proportionality factor set by COEF_DIM_ESPACE.

If one solves a GEP, the dimension of the subspace is doubled. The values of these various parameters are printed in the MESSAGE file.

- For the option 'SEPARE': having obtained an interval bracketing an eigenvalue, one takes for the computation of the mode the middle of the interval. During the computation of the mode, the value of the eigenvalue is further refined. This is the result of the inverse iteration proper.
- For the option 'AJUSTE

': if the separation is not possible and if in a given interval there is more than one eigenvalue, one does not apply the adjustment method to this interval. On the other hand, one will carry out, during the computation of the modes, reorthogonalisations with respect to the previous modes contained in the interval (this makes it possible to compute modes associated with a multiple eigenvalue).

## 6 Modal parameters/ Norm of the modes/ Modal position

On output from this operator, the real or complex eigenmodes are normalised to the largest of the components which is not a LAGRANGE multiplier. To choose another norm, one must use the factor keyword NORM_MODE inside CALC_MODES, or the command NORM_MODE [U4.52.11](doc:u4.52.11) following CALC_MODES (one then has access to more functionalities).

In the case of a dynamic analysis, the data structure mode_meca_*, and mode_gene, contains, in addition to the vibration frequencies and the associated mode shapes, modal parameters (generalised mass, generalised stiffness, participation factor, effective mass and effective inertia for the mode_meca case only). The definition of these parameters will be found in [R5.01.03](doc:r5.01.03).

In the case of a linear buckling analysis, the data structure mode_flamb contains only the critical loads and the associated mode shapes.

In the case of a generalised dynamic analysis with real symmetric matrices, the modal position corresponds to the position of the mode in the whole of the spectrum defined by the initial matrices.

In all the other cases, the modal positions are assigned from 1 to nf (nf being the number of modes retained) by classifying them in algebraically increasing order. All the modal positions are therefore positive.

## 7 Optimisation of the CPU performance

### 7.1 Parallelism of the linear solver

The modal analysis calls on a linear solver. If one chooses the linear solver MUMPS with the factor keyword SOLVEUR, operand METHODE='MUMPS', one can activate its operation in parallel. For this one must use a parallel version of *Code_Aster*, and fill in the number of processors to be used. Empirically, the use of one processor per slice of 105 to 106 degrees of freedom that the model comprises gives good performance. In parallel, one also recommends the setting RENUM='QAMD'.

### 7.2 Computation of the modes by sub-bands

If the problem to be treated is a GEP and one is looking for many modes over a given band, or if the number of degrees of freedom of the model is high (> 105), it is advised to split the global search band into several sub-bands, of 40 to 80 modes, as balanced as possible. The analysis will thus be more robust and faster, even by calling a CALC_MODES sequentially for each sub-band.

For better ergonomics, the analyses on each sub-band can be carried out automatically and in a way that is transparent to the user, by giving the keyword FREQ a list of n>2 values. This moreover makes it possible to parallelise the processing of the various sub-bands, producing considerable gains on the CPU performance (a factor which may reach 10 to 20 on the computation time, and several tens of percent on the memory peak).

For more details, one will refer to the generic documents [U2.08.06](doc:u2.08.06) on parallelism, and [U2.06.01](doc:u2.06.01) on the computation of eigenmodes.
