# r4.07.05 Homogenisation of a network of beams immersed in a fluid

**Summary:**

This note describes a model obtained by a homogenisation method in order to characterise the vibratory behaviour of a periodic network of tubes immersed in an incompressible fluid. The development of a finite element associated with this homogenised model is then presented.

The tubes are modelled by Euler beams and the fluid by a potential model.

This modelling is accessible in the command AFFE_MODELE by choosing the modelling 3D_FAISCEAU.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

In the nuclear industry, certain structures consist of quasi-periodic networks of tubes immersed in fluids: the “fuel” assemblies, the steam generators, ... To determine the vibratory behaviour of such structures, the classical approach (each tube is modelled, the volume occupied by the fluid is meshed) is costly and tedious or even impracticable (in particular, the construction of a complicated mesh containing a large number of nodes). The structures studied presenting a quasi-periodic character, it seems interesting to use homogenisation methods.

Homogenisation techniques applied to a network of tubes immersed in a fluid have already been developed on various occasions [bib1], [bib5], [bib4]. The models obtained differ by the assumptions made on the fluid (compressibility, initial velocity of the flow, viscosity). Depending on the assumptions adopted, the action of the fluid on the network of tubes corresponds to an added mass (lowering of the vibration frequencies with respect to those determined in the absence of fluid), to an added damping or even to an added stiffness [bib5].

At the beginning, finite elements associated with two-dimensional models (network of masses immersed in a fluid) were developed [bib2]. To study the three-dimensional problems (network of tubes), one solution consisted in projecting the motion onto the first bending mode of the beams [bib4]. Subsequently, three-dimensional finite elements were developed [bib3], [bib8].

## 2 Initial physical problem

### 2.1 Description of the problem

One considers a set of identical beams, of axis $z$ , arranged periodically (let $\varepsilon$ be the spatial period). These beams are located inside an enclosure filled with fluid (see [fig 2.1-a]). One wishes to characterise the vibratory behaviour of such a medium, considering for the moment only the added-mass effect of the fluid, which is predominant [bib6].

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_2.svg)

**Figure** 2.1-a

### 2.2 Modelling assumptions

One considers that the fluid is a perfect fluid, initially at rest, incompressible. As the assumption of small displacements about the equilibrium position has been made (fluid initially at rest), the displacement field of the fluid particles is irrotational, so that there exists a fluid displacement potential denoted $\Phi$ . There is no fluid flux through the outer surface $\Gamma$ .

One considers that the beams are homogeneous and of constant cross-section along $z\in \left]0,L\right[$ . To model the beams, the Euler model is used and only the bending motions are taken into account. The beam cross-section is rigid and the displacement of any point of the cross-section is denoted:

${\mathrm{s}}^{l}$ the bending of beam no. $l$ $({\mathrm{s}}^{l}(z)=({\mathrm{s}}_{x}^{l}(z),{\mathrm{s}}_{y}^{l}(z)))$ .

The beams are clamped at their two ends.

The variational form of the vibro-acoustic fluid–structure problem (conservation of mass, dynamic equation of each tube) is written:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{F}}\nabla \Phi .\nabla {\Phi}^{\text{*}}=\sum_{l}{\int}_{{\gamma}_{l}}({\mathrm{s}}^{l}·\mathrm{n}){\Phi}^{\text{*}}\forall {\Phi}^{\text{*}}\in {V}_{\Phi}$                         eq 2.2-1

${\int}_{0}^{L}{\rho}_{S}S.\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}^{l}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\cdot {\mathrm{s}}^{l\text{*}}+{\int}_{0}^{L}E\mathrm{I}\cdot \frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}^{l}}{\partial {z}^{2}}\cdot \frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}^{l\text{*}}}{\partial {z}^{2}}=-{\int}_{0}^{L}({\int}_{{\gamma}_{l}}{\rho}_{F}.\frac{{\partial}^{2}\Phi }{\partial {t}^{2}}\cdot \mathrm{n}){\mathrm{s}}^{l\text{*}}\forall {\mathrm{s}}^{l\text{*}}\in {V}_{s}$     eq 2.2-2

with:

${V}_{s}={({H}_{0}^{2}(]0,L[))}^{2}$ and ${V}_{\Phi}={H}^{1}({\Omega}_{F})$

where:

- $n$ is the inward normal to beam no. $l$ ,
- ${\rho}_{F}$ is the density of the fluid, constant throughout the domain,
- ${\rho}_{S}$ is the density of the material constituting the beam,
- $S$ is the cross-section of the beam,
- $E$ is Young's modulus,
- $\mathrm{I}$ is the inertia tensor of the beam cross-section.

The right-hand side of equation [eq 2.2-2] represents the forces exerted on the beam by the fluid. The pressure $p$ of the fluid is related to the displacement potential by: $p=-{\rho}_{F}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\Phi }{\partial {t}^{2}}$ . In the same way, the right-hand side of equation [eq 2.2-1] represents the fluid flux induced by the motions of the beams. On the boundary of each beam $l$ one has: ${\mathrm{s}}^{l}\cdot \mathrm{n}=\nabla \Phi \cdot \mathrm{n}$ .

This formulation leads to a non-symmetric matrix system, which is not very convenient, in

particular when searching for vibration modes.

## 3 Homogenised problem

### 3.1 Homogenised problem obtained

In order to take into account the periodic character of the medium studied, one uses a homogenisation method based in this precise case on an asymptotic expansion of the variables intervening in the initial physical problem. As regards the operational approach, the reader is referred to the following references [bib2], [bib4], [bib5], [bib6]. One will content oneself here with stating the results obtained.

In the homogenised medium ${\Omega}_{0}$ (see [fig 3.1-a]), the following two homogenised variables are considered: ${s}_{0}$ (displacement of the beams) and ${\Phi}_{0}$ (fluid displacement potential). In variational form, these variables are related by the equations of the eigenvibrations:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}\mathrm{A}.\nabla {\Phi}_{0}.\nabla {\phi }^{\text{*}}=-{\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}\mathrm{D}.{\mathrm{s}}_{0}.{\phi }^{\text{*}}& \forall {\phi }^{\text{*}}\in {V}_{\Phi}^{\text{hom}}\\ {\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}\mathrm{M}.\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}{\mathrm{s}}^{\text{*}}+{\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}\mathrm{K}.\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}_{0}}{\partial {z}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\mathrm{s}}^{\text{*}}}{\partial {z}^{2}}={\rho}_{F}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}\mathrm{D}.\nabla \frac{{\partial}^{2}{\Phi}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}.{\mathrm{s}}^{\text{*}}& \forall {\mathrm{s}}^{\text{*}}\in {V}_{s}^{\text{hom}}\end{array}$     eq 3.1-1

where:

$\begin{array}{c}{V}_{s}^{\text{hom}}={L}^{2}{({\Omega}_{0})}^{2}\times {H}_{0}^{2}{({\Omega}_{0})}^{2}\\ \text{où}{\Omega}_{0}=S\times ]0,L[\\ {H}_{0}^{2}({\Omega}_{0})=\left\lbrace v;\forall (x,y)\in Sz\to v(x,y,z)\in {H}_{0}^{2}(]0,L[)\right\rbrace \end{array}$

The various tensors which intervene in [eq 3.1-1] are defined by means of two functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}(\alpha =1,2)$ in the following manner:

$B=({b}_{ij})=\frac{1}{∣Y∣}(\begin{array}{ccc}{\int}_{{Y}_{F}}\frac{\partial {\chi }_{1}}{\partial {y}_{1}}& {\int}_{{Y}_{F}}\frac{\partial {\chi }_{1}}{\partial {y}_{2}}& 0\\ {\int}_{{Y}_{F}}\frac{\partial {\chi }_{2}}{\partial {y}_{1}}& {\int}_{{Y}_{F}}\frac{\partial {\chi }_{2}}{\partial {y}_{2}}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})A=({a}_{ij})=(\frac{∣{Y}_{F}∣}{∣Y∣}{\delta}_{ij}-{b}_{ij})$             eq 3.1-2

$\begin{array}{}D=({d}_{ij})=B+\frac{1}{∣Y∣}(\begin{array}{ccc}{Y}_{S}& 0& 0\\ 0& {Y}_{S}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})M=({m}_{ij})={\rho}_{F}B+\frac{{\mu}^{2}}{∣Y∣}(\begin{array}{ccc}{\rho}_{S}S& 0& 0\\ 0& {\rho}_{S}S& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})\\ K=({k}_{ij})=\frac{E{\mu}^{2}}{∣Y∣}(\begin{array}{ccc}{I}_{xx}& {I}_{xy}& 0\\ {I}_{xy}& {I}_{yy}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})\end{array}$ eq 3.1-3

where $∣{Y}_{F}∣$ and $∣{Y}_{S}∣$ represent respectively the areas of the fluid and structure domains of the reference elementary cell (cf. [fig 3.1-b]). $∣Y∣$ represents the sum of the two preceding areas. The reference elementary cell is homothetic, with ratio $\mu$ , to the real periodicity cell of the heterogeneous medium.

The two functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}(\alpha =1,2)$ are solutions of a two-dimensional problem, called the cell problem. On the reference elementary cell, the functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}(\alpha =1,2)$ are defined by:

$\begin{array}{}{\int}_{{Y}_{F}}\nabla {\chi }_{\alpha}.\nabla v={\int}_{\gamma}{n}_{\alpha}.v\forall v\in V\\ {\int}_{{Y}_{F}}{\chi }_{\alpha}=0(\text{pour avoir une solution unique})\end{array}$                     eq 3.1-4

where:

$V=\lbrace v\in {H}_{1}({Y}_{F}),v(y)\text{périodique en}y\text{de période 1}\rbrace$

**Note:**

*One shows that the two-dimensional part of* $B$ *is symmetric and positive definite [bib5].*

**Note:**

*In the matrix* $M$ *, the term* ${\rho}_{F}\mathrm{B}$ *plays the role of an added-mass matrix specific to each beam in its cell.*

**Note:**

*For the various tensors, one can factor out the multiplicative term* $\frac{1}{∣Y∣}$ . *It has been added* *in order to obtain the “correct mass” of tubes in the absence of fluid. One then has* ${\int}_{{\Omega}_{0}}M\text{dV}$ *= mass of the tubes making up* ${\Omega}_{0}$ *.*

### 3.2 Matrix problem

By discretising the problem [eq 3.1-1] by finite elements, one arrives (with obvious notation) at the following problem:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}\hat{A}{\Phi}_{0}=-\hat{D}{s}_{0}\\ \hat{M}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{s}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}+\hat{K}{s}_{0}={\rho}_{F}{\hat{D}}^{T}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\Phi}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\end{array}$                                     eq 3.2-1

which can be put in the form (one pre-multiplies the first equation by ${\rho}_{F}$ ):

$\tilde{M}(\begin{array}{}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{s}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\\ \frac{{\partial}^{2}{\Phi}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\end{array})+\tilde{K}(\begin{array}{}{s}_{0}\\ {\Phi}_{0}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}\hat{M}& -{\rho}_{F}{\hat{D}}^{T}\\ -{\rho}_{F}\hat{D}& -{\rho}_{F}\hat{A}\end{array})(\begin{array}{}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{s}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\\ \frac{{\partial}^{2}{\Phi}_{0}}{\partial {t}^{2}}\end{array})+(\begin{array}{cc}\hat{K}& 0\\ 0& 0\end{array})(\begin{array}{}{s}_{0}\\ {\Phi}_{0}\end{array})=0$     eq 3.2-2

**Note:**

*The problem obtained is symmetric. If, instead of choosing the displacement potential to represent the fluid, one had chosen the velocity potential, one would have obtained a non-symmetric matrix problem also bringing out a damping matrix.*

**Note:**

*It is necessary to have* ${\rho}_{F}>0$ *in order that the mass matrix* $\tilde{\mathrm{M}}$ *be invertible. If one wishes to carry out an analysis in “air”, cf. §*  6.3 *, one must fix the* *degrees of freedom in* ${\Phi}_{0}$ *.*

## 4 Solution of the cell problem

### 4.1 Problem to be solved

On the two-dimensional elementary cell (see [fig 4.1-a]), one seeks to compute the functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}(\alpha =1,2)$ satisfying:

$\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla {\chi }_{\alpha}.\nabla v={\int}_{\gamma}{n}_{\alpha}v\forall v\in V\\ {\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{\chi }_{\alpha}=0(\text{pour avoir une solution unique})\end{array}$                         eq 4.1-1

where:

$V=\lbrace v\in {H}_{1}({Y}^{\text{*}}),v(y)\text{périodique en}y\text{de période 1}\rbrace$

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_47.svg)

**Figure** 4.1-a

After having determined the functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}(\alpha =1,2)$ , one computes the homogenised coefficients defined by ${b}_{\alpha \beta }(\alpha =1,2;\beta =1,2)$ the formula:

${b}_{\alpha \beta }={\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\frac{\partial {\chi }_{\alpha}}{\partial {y}_{B}}$                                     eq 4.1-2

By using Green's formula and the periodic character of ${\chi }_{\alpha}$ , one shows that the coefficients ${b}_{\alpha \beta }$ can be written otherwise:

${b}_{\alpha \beta }={\int}_{\gamma}{\chi }_{\alpha}{n}_{\beta}$                                     eq 4.1-3

To estimate this quantity, one must, during a discretisation by finite elements, determine for each element the outward normal, which can be tedious. One then proceeds in another way; by taking in equation [eq 4.1-1] $v={\chi }_{\beta}$ , one obtains:

${b}_{\alpha \beta }={\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla {\chi }_{\alpha}\nabla {\chi }_{\beta}$                                     eq 4.1-4

From the potential-energy function defined by the classical formula:

$W(v)=-\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla v.\nabla v$                                 eq 4.1-5

one can rewrite the homogenised coefficients in the form:

${b}_{\alpha \beta }=-(W({\chi }_{\alpha}+{\chi }_{\beta})-W({\chi }_{\alpha})-W({\chi }_{\beta}))$                     eq 4.1-6

In the general two-dimensional case, one must compute three coefficients of the homogenised problem (one knows that ${b}_{11,}{b}_{12}={b}_{21,}{b}_{22}$ the matrix $B=({b}_{\alpha \beta })$ is symmetric). One must solve the following two problems:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}\text{Calculer}{\chi }_{1}\in V/\underset{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{\int}\nabla {\chi }_{1}\nabla v=\underset{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{\int}{n}_{1}v\\ \text{Calculer}{\chi }_{2}\in V/\underset{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{\int}\nabla {\chi }_{2}\nabla v=\underset{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{\int}{n}_{2}v\\ \text{Calculer}{\chi }^{\text{*}}\in V/{\chi }^{\text{*}}={\chi }_{1}+{\chi }_{2}\end{array}$                     eq 4.1-7

One then has:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{b}_{11}=-2W({\chi }_{1})\\ {b}_{22}=-2W({\chi }_{2})\\ {b}_{12}={b}_{21}=-(W({\chi }^{\text{*}})-W({\chi }_{1})-W({\chi }_{2}))\end{array}$                     eq 44.1-8

**Note:**

*If the elementary cell possesses symmetries, this makes it possible to solve the problem on a part of the cell with well-suited boundary conditions and to compute only certain coefficients of the homogenised problem. For example, for the cell of figure no. 4.1-a one has:* ${b}_{11}={b}_{22,}{b}_{12}={b}_{21}=0$ *.*

### 4.2 Equivalent problem for defining ${\chi }_{\alpha}$

In equation [eq 4.1-1], the computation of the right-hand side requires the determination of the normal to the edge. To avoid a determination of the normal, one can write an equivalent problem, satisfied by the functions ${\chi }_{\alpha}$ .

Let the vectors ${G}_{1}=(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 0\end{array}),{G}_{2}=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 1\end{array})\text{et}{G}^{\text{*}}=(\begin{array}{c}1\\ 1\end{array})$ , one seeks the functions ${\chi }^{\text{*}},{\chi }_{1,}{\chi }_{2}\in V$ such that:

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla {\chi }_{1}.\nabla v={\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{G}_{1}.v\forall v\in V\\ {\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla {\chi }_{2}.\nabla v={\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{G}_{2}.v\forall v\in V\\ {\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}\nabla {\chi }^{\text{*}}.\nabla v={\int}_{{Y}^{\text{*}}}{G}^{\text{*}}.v\forall v\in V\end{array}$                     eq 4.2-1

By using Green's formula and the anti-periodicity of the normal $n$ , one shows that the problems [eq 4.1-1] and [eq 4.2-1] are equivalent.

### 4.3 Implementation in Code_Aster

In *Code_Aster* , to solve the problem [eq 4.2-1], the thermal analogy, by defining a material having a coefficient ${c}_{p}$ equal to zero and a coefficient $\lambda$ equal to one, is used. To impose the computation of the right-hand side involving the term in ${G}_{\alpha}$ , the keyword PRE_GRAD_TEMP in the command AFFE_CHAR_THER is selected. The thermal problem is solved by using the command THER_LINEAIRE. The computation of the potential energy $W$ is supplied by the command POST_ELEM with the option ENER_POT. In the general case, three analyses are carried out in order to determine the values $W({\chi }_{1}),W({\chi }_{2}),W({\chi }^{\text{*}})$ and then the values of the coefficients of the homogenised problem are deduced from them manually. To impose the periodic character of the space in which the solution is sought, the keyword LIAISON_GROUP in the command AFFE_CHAR_THER is used.

## 5 Choice of the finite element for the homogenised problem

### 5.1 Choice of the finite elements

In the model presented previously, the axis $z$ has a predominant role as the principal axis of the beams. The finite elements developed satisfy this particularity. The mesh cells are of cylindrical type: the quadrangular bases are contained in planes $z=\mathrm{Cte}$ and the axis of the cylinder is parallel to the axis $z$ (see [fig 5.1-a]).

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_77.svg)

**Figure** 5.1-a

According to the equations [eq 3.1-1], second derivatives with respect to the coordinate $z$ intervene in the model, which necessitates finite elements ${C}^{1}$ in the direction $z$ . Shape functions of Hermite type to represent the variations of $s$ along the axis $z$ are therefore used. At the discretisation points, the displacements ${s}_{x},{s}_{y}$ but also the derivatives $\frac{\partial {s}_{y}}{\partial z},\frac{\partial {s}_{x}}{\partial z}$ , which are linked to the rotational degrees of freedom ${\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y}$ by the formulae ${\theta}_{x}=\frac{\partial {s}_{y}}{\partial z},{\theta}_{y}=-\frac{\partial {s}_{x}}{\partial z}$ , must be known. As regards the variations along $x,y$ , one restricts oneself for the moment to shape functions ${Q}_{1}$ .

For the potential degree of freedom, shape functions ${Q}_{1}$ or ${Q}_{2}$ along the three directions $x,y,z$ of space are used.

The finite element therefore has as unknowns the following degrees of freedom: ${s}_{x},{s}_{y},{\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},\Phi$ .

**Note:**

*The order of the nodes of the support mesh cells is very important. Indeed, the edges parallel to the axis* $z$ *are not represented in the same way as the edges contained in the planes* $z=\mathrm{Cte}$ *. The nodes of the mesh cells are therefore arranged in a very precise order: list of the nodes of the lower base, then list of the opposite nodes of the upper base (or vice versa).*

As regards the geometry, the shape functions making it possible to pass from the reference element to the current element are ${Q}_{1}$ . The finite element is therefore subparametric.

Two finite elements have been developed:

- one associated with a HEXA8 mesh cell. At each node of the mesh cell, the unknowns are ${s}_{x},{s}_{y},{\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},\Phi$ . The shape functions associated with the potential $\Phi$ are ${Q}_{1}$ .
- another associated with a HEXA20 mesh cell. At each corner node of the mesh cell, the unknowns are ${s}_{x},{s}_{y},{\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},\Phi$ . At each mid-edge node, the unknown is $\Phi$ . The shape functions associated with the potential $\Phi$ are ${Q}_{2}$ .

### 5.2 Reference finite elements

#### 5.2.1 HEXA8 mesh cell

On the HEXA8 reference finite element (see [fig 5.2-a]), the following shape functions are defined:

${N}_{\pm 1,\pm 1,\pm 1}^{L}(\xi )={P}_{\pm 1}({\xi}_{1}){P}_{\pm 1}({\xi}_{2}){P}_{\pm 1}^{L}({\xi}_{3})\text{avec}L=\Phi \text{ou}D\text{ou}R$             eq 5.2.1-1

The indices $\pm 1$ represent the coordinates of the nodes of the reference support mesh cell.

The functions which make it possible to define the shape functions are written:

$\begin{array}{cc}{P}_{(-1)}(\varsigma )=\frac{1-\varsigma }{2}& {P}_{(+1)}(\varsigma )=\frac{1+\varsigma }{2}\\ {P}_{(-1)}^{\Phi}(\varsigma )=\frac{1-\varsigma }{2}& {P}_{(+1)}^{\Phi}(\varsigma )=\frac{1+\varsigma }{2}\\ {P}_{(-1)}^{D}(\varsigma )=\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{3}{2}\varsigma +\frac{1}{2}{\varsigma}^{3})& {P}_{(+1)}^{D}(\varsigma )=\frac{1}{2}(1+\frac{3}{2}\varsigma -\frac{1}{2}{\varsigma}^{3})\\ {P}_{(-1)}^{R}(\varsigma )=\frac{1}{4}(1-\varsigma -{\varsigma}^{2}+{\varsigma}^{3})& {P}_{(+1)}^{R}(\varsigma )=\frac{1}{4}(-1-\varsigma +{\varsigma}^{2}+{\varsigma}^{3})\end{array}$ $\varsigma \in [-1,1]$     eq 5.2.1-2

The functions ${P}^{D},{P}^{R}$ are the Hermite functions.

The unknowns of the homogenised problem, on a mesh cell, are decomposed in the following manner:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{s}_{x}(\xi )=\sum_{i=1}^{8}{\mathrm{DX}}_{i}{N}_{i}^{D}(\xi )+\frac{{L}_{S}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{8}{\mathrm{DRX}}_{i}{N}_{i}^{R}(\xi )\\ {s}_{y}(\xi )=\sum_{i=1}^{8}{\mathrm{DY}}_{i}{N}_{i}^{D}(\xi )+\frac{{L}_{S}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{8}{\mathrm{DRY}}_{i}{N}_{i}^{R}(\xi )\xi =({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2,}{\xi}_{3})\\ \Phi (\xi )=\sum_{i=1}^{8}{\Phi}_{j}{N}_{j}^{\Phi}(\xi )\end{array}$         eq 5.2.1-3

where ${\mathrm{DX}}_{i},{\mathrm{DY}}_{i},{\mathrm{DRX}}_{i},{\mathrm{DRY}}_{i},{\Phi}_{i}$ are the values of the displacement along $x$ , of the displacement along $y$ , of the rotation about the axis $x$ , of the rotation about the axis $y$ and of the displacement potential at the vertex $i$ of the mesh cell. In *Code_Aster* , for each node, the degrees of freedom are arranged in the order cited previously.

### 5.3 Choice of the Gauss points

Each integral which intervenes in the expressions of the elementary matrices is transformed into an integral on the reference element (one carries out a change of variable) which is then computed by using a quadrature formula of GAUSS type.

The Gauss points are chosen so as to integrate exactly the integrals on the reference element. Different families of integration points are used to compute the mass matrices and the stiffness matrices (the degrees of the polynomials to be integrated are different). But here, to compute the various contributions of the mass matrix, different families of Gauss points may still be used.

The reference element being a HEXA8 or a HEXA20, the integral over the volume can be separated into a product of three integrals which each correspond to one direction of the reference space. The number of integration points necessary is determined by direction.

Depending on the reference mesh cell, the number of integration points per direction is the following:

|  | **HEXA8 mesh cell** | **HEXA20 mesh cell** |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | **direction** $x$ **or** $y$ | **direction** $z$ | **direction** $x$ **or** $y$ | **direction** $z$ |
| matrix $\hat{K}$ | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| matrix $\hat{A}$ | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 |
| matrix $\hat{D}$ | 2 | 3 | 2 | 3 |
| matrix $\hat{M}$ | 2 | 4 | 2 | 4 |

Four families of Gauss points have been defined. Each family corresponds to one of the matrices of the problem to be solved.

On the segment [-1,1], the coordinates of the integration points and their weights are the following [bib7]:

| **Number of integration points** | **Coordinates** | **Weights** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | $\hat{M}$ | $1$ |
| 3 | $0$ $\pm \sqrt{3/5}$ | $8/9$ $5/9$ |
| 4 | $\pm \sqrt{\frac{3-2\sqrt{6/5}}{7}}$ $\pm \sqrt{\frac{3+2\sqrt{6/5}}{7}}$ | $\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{6\sqrt{6/5}}$ $\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{6\sqrt{6/5}}$ |

The weight of a Gauss point in the three-dimensional reference element is obtained by multiplying the three weights corresponding to each of the coordinates of the Gauss point.

### 5.4 Addition of the tension and torsion problems

To complete the homogenised bending problem described previously, the tension problem and the torsion problem are added in an uncoupled manner (these problems do not involve the fluid).

#### 5.4.1 Tension problem

The homogenised tension problem is written in the following form:

${\int}_{\Omega}\frac{ES{\mu}^{2}}{∣Y∣}\frac{\partial {s}_{z}}{\partial z}\frac{\partial v}{\partial z}+{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{{\mu}^{2}{\rho}_{S}S}{∣Y∣}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{s}_{z}}{\partial {t}^{2}}v=0\forall v\in V$ with $V={H}^{1}([0,L])$

$\mu$ being the side ratio of the reference elementary cell, of area $∣Y∣$ , with respect to the real periodicity cell of the heterogeneous medium.

The reference finite element is a HEXA8 having as unknown the displacement

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_144.svg)

at each node. The associated shape functions are ${Q}_{1}$ .

#### 5.4.2 Torsion problem

The homogenised torsion problem is written in the following form:

${\int}_{\Omega}\frac{E{J}_{z}{\mu}^{2}}{2(1+\nu )∣Y∣}\frac{\partial {\theta}_{z}}{\partial z}\frac{\partial v}{\partial z}+{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{{\mu}^{2}{\rho}_{S}{J}_{z}}{∣Y∣}\frac{{\partial}^{2}{\theta}_{z}}{\partial {t}^{2}}v=0\forall v\in V$ with $V={H}^{1}([0,L])$

where ${J}_{z}$ is the torsion constant.

The reference finite element is a HEXA8 having as unknown the displacement $\mathrm{DRZ}$ at each node. The associated shape functions are ${Q}_{1}$

### 5.5 Integration into Code_Aster of this finite element

The finite element is developed in *Code_Aster* in 3D. A modelling has been added to the catalogue of modellings:

- 'FAISCEAU_3D' for 3D.

In the catalogue of elements, the element can be applied on the following two mesh cells:

| **Mesh cell** | **Number of nodes** **in displacement and rotation** | **Number of nodes** **in fluid potential** | **Name of the element** **in the catalogue** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HEXA8 | 8 | 8 | meca_poho_hexa8 |
| HEXA20 | 8 | 20 | meca_poho_hexa20 |

In the initialisation routines of this element, one defines:

- two families of shape functions associated respectively with the displacements and rotation of the beams (shape function linear in $x,y$ and cubic in $z$ ) and with the fluid-potential terms (function linear in $x,y,z$ ),
- four families of Gauss points in order to compute the stiffness matrix and the various parts of the mass matrix.

During the computation of the elementary terms, the first or second derivatives of the shape functions on the current element are computed. Despite the simplified geometry of the finite element (the axis of the cylindrical mesh cell is parallel to the axis $z$ and the lower and upper cross-sections are in planes $z=\mathrm{Cte}$ ), a general subroutine to compute the second derivatives has been written [bib7]. Moreover, two new subroutines have been developed from the existing subroutines for the isoparametric elements in order to take into account the subparametric character of the element.

## 6 Use in Code_Aster

### 6.1 The necessary data

The characteristics of the beams (cross-section $S$ , inertia tensor $I$ , torsion constant ${J}_{z}$ ) are supplied directly under the factor keyword POUTRE of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM.

The characteristics of the homogenised coefficients and of the reference cell are supplied under the factor keyword POUTRE_FLUI of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM. For the simple keywords, the correspondence is the following:

B_T: ${b}_{11}$

B_N: ${b}_{22}$

B_TN: ${b}_{12}$

A_FLUI: ${Y}_{F}$

A_CELL: $Y={Y}_{F}+{Y}_{S}$

COEF_ECHELLE: $\mu$

The characteristics of the materials are supplied in the command DEFI_MATERIAU. For the tubes, the factor keyword ELAS is used to indicate Young's modulus (E: $E$ ), Poisson's ratio (NU: $\nu$ ) and the density (RHO: ${\rho}_{S}$ ). For the fluid, the factor keyword FLUIDE is used to indicate the density of the fluid (RHO: ${\rho}_{F}$ ).

### 6.2 Orientation of the axes of the beams

The generators of the cylindrical mesh cells are obligatorily parallel to the axis of the beams and the bases of the mesh cells perpendicular to this same axis. During the construction of the mesh, one must ensure that the order of the nodes (local numbering) of each cylindrical mesh cell is correct: the nodes of the lower base then the nodes of the upper base (or vice versa). The direction of the axis of the beams is supplied under the factor keyword ORIENTATION of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM.

The following assumption has been made: the reference frame is the same as the principal inertia frame of the characteristic tube representing the homogenised medium. This means that in the equations [eq 3.1-3], the term ${I}_{xy}$ is zero.

### 6.3 Modal analysis

The finite element developed makes it possible to characterise the vibratory behaviour of a network of beams immersed in a fluid. It is interesting to determine the vibration frequencies of such a network in air and in water.

To carry out a modal analysis in air (${\rho}_{F}=0$ ), one must fix all the degrees of freedom corresponding to the fluid displacement potential $\Phi$ , otherwise the stiffness matrix (and even the shifted matrix of the modal problem) is not invertible [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01).

To carry out a modal analysis in water (${\rho}_{F}\ne 0$ ), one must use in the command CALC_MODES with OPTION='CENTRE'. The shifted matrix $(\tilde{K}-\sigma \tilde{M})$ is then invertible if $\sigma$ is not an eigenvalue or if $\sigma$ is different from zero.

## 7 Characterisation of the spectrum of the homogenised model

### 7.1 Heterogeneous model

Let a square-pitch network of $n$ beams clamped at their lower ends and whose upper ends move in the same way (uniform motion) (cf. figure [fig 7.1-a]). Only the bending motions are considered.

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_175.svg)

**Figure** 7.1-a

The vibration spectrum in air of this network has the following form. For each order of bending vibration mode, the modal structure consists of a double frequency corresponding to a mode in $x$ and to a mode in $y$ in which the whole of the upper part moves (all the beams have the same deformed shape) and of a frequency of multiplicity $(2n-2)$ corresponding to modes in which the whole of the upper part of the beams is immobile and in which beams move in phase opposition.

In the presence of fluid, the spectrum is modified. For each order of bending vibration mode, the $2n$ vibration frequencies are lower than the vibration frequencies obtained in air. The effect of the incompressible fluid is comparable to an added mass. One still has a double frequency corresponding to a mode in $x$ and to a mode in $y$ in which the whole of the upper part moves (all the beams have the same deformed shape). On the other hand, one obtains $(n-1)$ different couples of double frequency (one in $x$ and one in $y$ ) corresponding to modes in which the whole of the upper part of the beams is immobile and in which beams move in phase opposition.

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_185.svg)

**Figure** 7.1-b

### 7.2 Homogeneous model

The heterogeneous medium has been replaced by a homogeneous medium.

#### 7.2.1 Continuous problem

Recent work, concerning a plane homogenisation problem of a network of masses retained by springs and immersed in a fluid, shows that the spectrum of the continuous homogeneous model consists of a continuous part and of two frequencies of infinite multiplicity [bib10]. The spectrum of the eigenfrequencies of the problem in water is also contained in a well-defined interval bounded above by the vibration frequency in air of a mass [bib5].

These results can be transposed for each bending order of the network of tubes.

#### 7.2.2 Discretised problem

Let the homogeneous domain be meshed by hexahedra. Let $p$ be the number of generators parallel to the axis $z$ of the network of beams.

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_187.svg)

**Figure 7.2.2-a**

One finds results analogous to those obtained for the heterogeneous model. It suffices to replace $n$ by $p$ . For a beam bending order, the number of frequencies corresponding to modes in which the beams do not all vibrate in the same direction depends on the discretisation used in the directions transverse to the axis of the beams.

Depending on the finite element used (HEXA8 mesh cell or HEXA20 mesh cell), the distribution of the last $(2p-2)$ frequencies is different. The first double frequency (the one corresponding to the mode in which the upper part moves) is the same for the two finite elements.

![Figure](/figures/r4.07.05/Object_191.svg)

**Figure 7.2.2-b**

Overall, the homogeneous model makes it possible to obtain easily the vibration frequencies corresponding to modes in which all the beams vibrate in the same direction. The other modes obtained supply only a partial view of the spectrum. In the discretised spectrum, one can recover one or both frequencies of infinite multiplicity present in the spectrum of the continuous model.

## 8 Conclusion

The use of the finite elements developed, associated with the homogenised model of a periodic bundle of tubes immersed in a fluid, makes it possible to characterise the overall vibratory motions (the whole structure moves in the same direction) of such a structure.
