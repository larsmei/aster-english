# r7.10.03 Post-processing of shaft-line analyses: Campbell diagram

**Summary:**

This document presents the commands CALC_MODE_ROTATION and IMPR_DIAG_CAMPBELL, which make it possible to follow the modes of rotating machines as a function of the rotational speed, together with the instability zones of these modes. The definition of this tool is first recalled, then its flow chart is detailed. Finally, the main steps of the corresponding algorithm are set out, as it is designed and implemented in *Code_Aster*.

## 1 Purpose

The purpose of this document is to describe the macro-commands required to plot the Campbell diagram in *Code_Aster*. The approach is based on that already developed in the ROTORINSA software [bib1].

The Campbell diagram is a graphical representation of the natural frequencies of a rotating system as a function of its rotational speed. The natural frequencies and the modes of a rotating system are obtained by solving the dynamic equilibrium equation of a system of rotating shafts, with no right-hand side and including the effects due to damping.

> $M\ddot{\delta}+C(\Omega )\dot{\delta}+K\delta =0$ **eq** **1-** 1-1

Where $M$ is the mass matrix of the system, $C(\Omega )$ is a non-symmetric matrix, a function of the rotational speed, including the gyroscopic effect (skew-symmetric) and the damping characteristics of the bearings, and $K$ is the stiffness matrix of the system.

The data required to plot the Campbell diagram are therefore the natural frequencies, together with the corresponding modes, as a function of the rotational speed, in the form of a table. The step allowing the computation of these modes is already developed in *Code_Aster* (cf. [R5.01.01](doc:r5.01.01)).

Two macro-commands are developed. The first macro-command, CALC_MODE_ROTATION, computes the frequencies and the modes on the complete system as a function of the rotational speeds. The second macro-command, IMPR_DIAG_CAMPBELL, classifies the modes into bending, torsion and tension/compression, normalises these modes, determines the sense of precession of the bending modes, sorts the frequencies according to various mode-tracking methods, then plots the Campbell diagram.

## 3 Algorithm

After the computation of the frequencies and of the complex modes of the system, the list ‘MODES’ of mode_meca_c is one of the input data of the macro. The algorithm is presented below:

- Classification of the modes ‘MODES’ into bending, torsion and tension/compression:

  - Extraction of the bending modes
  - Normalises the bending modes
  - Extraction of the torsion modes
  - Normalises the torsion modes
  - Extraction of the tension/compression modes
  - Normalises the tension/compression modes
- Extraction of the frequencies

  - Bending frequencies
  - Torsion frequencies
  - Tension/compression frequencies

  - Initialisation of the frequency-connection tables; the filling of these tables corresponds to the mode-tracking method ‘Pas de TRI’, following the order of the mode as it stands in the mode_meca_c concepts.
- Computation of the direct or reverse sense of precession 1

for the bending modes at each rotational speed, carried out in two different ways according to the choice of the type of computation of the precession:

- PREC_GOR: The identification of the precession is a function of the sign of the largest orbit in a mode (direct precession, reverse precession)
- PREC_MOY: The identification of the precession is performed according to the sign of the sum of the signs of all the orbits
- Filling of the precession-sense table SENS

- Mode tracking:
- If the type of mode tracking is TRI_PREC_MOD, that is, step-by-step sorting of the frequencies according to the sense of the precession. It requires the computation of the axes of the orbits and the evaluation of the sense in which the orbits are traversed. The identification of the precession is performed according to the sign of the largest orbit or the sign of the sum of the signs of all the orbits. The frequencies are then classified into two groups (direct precession and reverse precession). Within each group the frequencies are classified in increasing order for each rotational speed. Each curve of the Campbell diagram connects the points as a function of the rotational speed, respecting the classification order in each group (the $i$-th frequency of group $p$ (speed ${\Omega}_{p}$) with the $i$-th frequency of group $p+1$ (speed ${\Omega}_{p+1}$), and so on).

  - Bending connection table updated
- If the type of mode tracking is TRI_FORM_MOD, that is, sorting by mode shape. Sorting the frequencies according to the mode shape requires the computation of the MAC correlation matrix of the modes. The modes are then compared as follows: each mode (starting from the highest rotational speed) is compared with the other modes of the previous rotational speed by taking the maximum correlation. This comparison makes it possible to plot, for each mode, the curve of evolution of its frequency as a function of the rotational speed in order to build the Campbell diagram.

  - Bending connection table updated
  - Torsion connection table updated
  - Tension/compression connection table updated.
- Plotting of the Campbell diagram

  - Plotting of the Campbell diagram of the bending modes
  - Plotting of the Campbell diagram of the torsion modes
  - Plotting of the Campbell diagram of the tension/compression modes.
- Plotting of the straight lines of slopes $S$ and determination of the intersection points (critical speeds).

### 3.1 Classification of the modes

The rotation of the rotor takes place about the axis $Z$ (this is the condition for correct use of IMPR_DIAG_CAMPBELL).

After the computation of the modes in rotation at various speeds, the following results are obtained:

- bending modes characterised by the translational displacements $\mathit{DX}$, $\mathit{DY}$ and the rotational displacements $\mathit{DRX}$ and $\mathit{DRY}$
- torsion modes characterised by the rotational displacements $\mathit{DRZ}$
- tension/compression modes characterised by the translational displacements $\mathit{DZ}$.

As a first step, the modes are classified according to these three categories.

For each mode, the following computations are performed:

1. Total norm $\mathit{Ntot}$

$\mathrm{Ntot}=\sqrt{\sum{\mid \mathrm{DX}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathrm{DY}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathrm{DZ}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathrm{DRX}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathrm{DRY}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathrm{DRZ}\mid }^{2}}$

1. Bending ratio $\mathit{Nflexion}$

$\mathit{Nflexion}=\frac{\sqrt{\sum{\mid \mathit{DX}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathit{DY}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathit{DRX}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathit{DRY}\mid }^{2}}}{\mathit{Ntot}}$

1. Torsion ratio $\mathit{Ntorsion}$

$\mathit{Ntorsion}=\frac{\sqrt{\sum{\mid \mathit{DRZ}\mid }^{2}}}{\mathit{Ntot}}$

1. Tension/compression ratio $\mathit{Nlongi}$

$\mathit{Nlongi}=\frac{\sqrt{\sum{\mid \mathit{DZ}\mid }^{2}}}{\mathit{Ntot}}$

The modes are classified as follows:

1. For the bending modes: $\mathit{Nflexion}>\mathit{Ntorsion}\mathit{et}\mathit{Nflexion}>\mathit{Nlongi}$

1. For the torsion modes: $\mathit{Ntorsion}>\mathit{Nflexion}\mathit{et}\mathit{Ntorsion}>\mathit{Nlongi}$

1. For the tension/compression modes: $\mathit{Nlongi}>\mathit{Nflexion}\mathit{et}\mathit{Nlongi}>\mathit{Ntorsion}$

If the system includes a foundation block (supporting member), mixed modes may exist (all three at once). In that case, the same method as above is applied.

From the initial modal basis $\mathit{MODES}$, three modal bases are therefore extracted:

1. $\mathit{MODEf}$: containing the bending modes
2. $\mathit{MODEt}$: containing the torsion modes
3. $\mathit{MODEl}$: containing the tension/compression modes

For the modal bases $\mathit{MODEf}$ and $\mathit{MODEl}$, normalisation is performed with respect to the largest of the translational components $(\mathit{DX},\mathit{DY},\mathit{DZ})$.

For the modal basis $\mathit{MODEf}$, normalisation is performed with respect to the largest of the rotational components $(\mathit{DRX},\mathit{DRY},\mathit{DRZ})$.

Extraction and normalisation of the modes of the structure are performed by means of the operators EXTR_MODE and NORM_MODE, respectively.

### 3.2 Computation of the sense of precession for the bending modes

#### 3.2.1 Concepts

The modes in rotation are defined by the ellipses at each node.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.03/Object_11.svg)

**Figure 3.2-a: Ellipses defining the modes**

For a given mode and for each node, one has the translational displacements $\mathit{DX}$, $\mathit{DY}$ with:

$\mathit{DX}=({X}_{R},{X}_{I}),{\mid \mathit{DX}\mid }^{2}={\mid {X}_{R}\mid }^{2}+{\mid {X}_{I}\mid }^{2}$

$\mathrm{DY}=({Y}_{R},{Y}_{I}),{\mid \mathrm{DY}\mid }^{2}={\mid {Y}_{R}\mid }^{2}+{\mid {Y}_{I}\mid }^{2}$

4 cases are possible:

1. If ${\mid \mathit{DX}\mid }^{2}+{\mid \mathit{DY}\mid }^{2}\approx 0$ there is no ellipse, then $a=0,b=0,\alpha =0,S=0$
2. If ${\mid \mathit{DX}\mid }^{2}\approx 0$ there is no ellipse, the trajectory is along the axis $Y$, then $a=0,b=\mid \mathit{DY}\mid ,\alpha =0,S=0$
3. If ${\mid \mathit{DY}\mid }^{2}\approx 0$ there is no ellipse, the trajectory is along the axis $X$, then $a=\mid \mathit{DX}\mid ,b=0,\alpha =90°,S=0$
4. If ${\mid \mathit{DX}\mid }^{2}\mathrm{\ne }0$ and ${\mid \mathit{DY}\mid }^{2}\mathrm{\ne }0$ then the precession $S$ can be computed for each node.

The displacements $u$ along $X$ and $v$ along $Y$ of this node will be of the following form:

$u={X}_{R}\cos(\omega t)-{X}_{I}\sin(\omega t)$ **,** $v={Y}_{R}\cos(\omega t)-{Y}_{I}\sin(\omega t)$

#### 3.2.2 Determination of the sense of traversal

During a period of vibration, the ellipse may be traversed in the positive or the negative sense (direct or reverse precession).

1. at $\omega t=0$ the axis of the rotor is at the point $P$
2. at $\omega t=\frac{\pi}{2}$ the axis of the rotor is at the point $Q$

The previous relations make it possible to compute the components of the vectors $\overrightarrow{\mathit{OP}}=(\begin{array}{c}{X}_{R}\\ {Y}_{R}\\ 0\end{array})$ and $\overrightarrow{\mathit{OQ}}=(\begin{array}{c}-{X}_{I}\\ -{Y}_{I}\\ 0\end{array})$. The sign of the vector product $\overrightarrow{\mathit{OP}\mathrm{\wedge }\mathit{OQ}}=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ {Y}_{R}{X}_{I}-{Y}_{I}{X}_{R}\end{array})$ indicates the sense of traversal, or precession (positive or negative).

For each node, the results are available at maximum amplitude, i.e.:

$u:\mathit{XMAX}=\sum{X}_{R}^{2}+{X}_{I}^{2}$ and $v:\mathit{YMAX}=\sum{Y}_{R}^{2}+{Y}_{I}^{2}$

For a mode, there are two ways of determining the sense of precession:

- PREC_GOR: The identification of the precession is performed according to the sign of the largest orbit in a mode (direct precession, reverse precession).
- PREC_MOY: The identification of the precession is performed according to the sign of the sum of the signs of all the orbits.

On output, the table SENS is filled for the senses of precession of all the modes at all the rotational speeds. This table makes it possible to track the bending modes with the method TRI_PREC_MOD. It also makes it possible to determine the sense of precession on the Campbell diagram according to the colour code defined in the User's manual of the command IMPR_DIAG_CAMPBELL.

### 3.3 Computation of the MAC matrices between modal bases at two rotational speeds

For the sorting of the modes by shape comparison, the correlation coefficients (MAC matrix) between the modes of the two rotational speeds of successive indices are computed. The operator MAC_MODES is used to

- compute the MAC matrix between two successive bending modal bases.
- compute the MAC matrix between two successive torsion modal bases.
- compute the MAC matrix between two successive tension/compression modal bases.

### 3.4 Sorting according to the sense of the precession

This is a mode-tracking method by step-by-step sorting of the frequencies with a check of the sense of precession. The modes are classified into two groups according to the sense of precession (direct-precession mode, reverse-precession mode). Tracking is then performed within each group according to the order of the modes. Tracking starts from the last rotational speed, of number $\mathit{NBV}$, and the last mode $\mathit{NFREQf}$ of that speed (cf. [Figure 3.4-a]).

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.03/Object_44.svg)

**Figure** 3.4-a **.4-a: Sorting according to the sense of precession**

### 3.5 Sorting according to the mode shape (MAC)

This mode-tracking method by shape comparison requires the computation of the MAC correlation matrix of the modes of two successive rotational speeds; see paragraph 3.3.

Tracking starts from the last rotational speed, of number $\mathit{NBV}$. For each mode of a given rotational speed $\mathit{IVI}$, one looks for a mode of the previous rotational speed $\mathit{IVI}-1$ which gives the maximum correlation coefficient. The connection table is filled with the value of the number of the selected mode (cf. [Figure 3.5-a]).

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.03/Object_45.svg)

**Figure 3.5-a: Sorting according to the mode shape**

This tracking is performed separately for the three categories of bending, torsion and tension/compression modes.

Direct precession (respectively reverse) corresponds to the case in which the orbit of the rotor is described in the same sense as (respectively the sense opposite to) the rotation of the shaft.
