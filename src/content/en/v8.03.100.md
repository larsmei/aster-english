# v8.03.100 FDNV100 - Balking a water tank with elastic deformable wall

**Summary:**

This test, from the fluid-structure domain, proposes the implementation of a transient dynamic calculation (operator DYNA_NON_LINE) with consideration of a free surface. Given the absence of suitable reference values, it is test case non-regression.

The stability analysis (with the keyword CRIT_STAB) is also valid for this fluid-structure problem.

## 1 Reference problem

This test case, based on the model of the article [bib1], aims to test the correct consideration of a free surface in a fluid-structure calculation coupled with the DYNA_NON_LINE operator.

### 1.1 Geometry

A parallelepipedic tank, filled with water, with external walls that are unformable, is considered. This rigid tank has a deformable inner plate, called $\Gamma$. It is clamped at its base at the bottom of the tank, with its vertical sides free. This flexible wall exceeds the free surface of a height of $12,9\mathrm{cm}$:

![Figure](/figures/v8.03.100/10000000000004C8000002BBF6DC3AC11006265D.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

The fluid (water) contained in the tank has the following characteristics:

| density: | ${\rho}_{f}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| --- | --- |
| speed of sound: | $c=1500m/s$ |

The deformable wall is linear elastic (durumin):

| density: | ${\rho}_{s}=2787\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Young's modulus : | $E=62,43\mathrm{GPa}$ |
| Poisson's ratio : | $\nu =0,35$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

#### 1.3.1 Dirichlet conditions

The load defined here is of the type of displacement imposed on a surface. More precisely, the bottom of the container is considered to be only able to move according to the direction $x$ .

In this direction $x$, the system will be requested by imposing a time-based sinusoidal displacement of $1,7704\mathrm{Hz}$ frequency and $0,001m$ amplitude at the bottom of the tank.

This displacement may be considered as a single-support type of stress applied by the base of the tank (seismic application).

#### 1.3.2 Neumann conditions

In superposition to the above-defined dirichlet surface condition, the model is also submitted to the gravity field (forced volume).

Finally, the upper surface of the fluid domain is characterized by a free surface condition.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The only results of the literature [bib1] are modal types: eigenfrequencies and appearances of certain modes.

Given the need to test the DYNA_NON_LINE operator, and given the relative complexity of the 3D model, it is not possible to find eigenfrequencies by transient analysis in a reasonable CPU time. Linear research is also used.

For information, this type of analysis conducted with a random loading corresponding to a white noise requires, for probabilistic convergence reasons, a calculation for a physical loading time of $250s$ , which corresponds to a time CPU of a few hours.

In order to have a calculation time of the order of a few minutes, it is mandatory to calculate the evolution over a short time (a few seconds). This restrictive framework does not allow to find precisely and in a way compatible with an automated post-processing the modal analysis results.

The validation provided by this test can therefore only be of the non-regression type of the digital solution.

Since the fluid-structure coupled computing features are already being tested for validation, this limitation to non-regression for this particular test case is not re-defining.

As a further validation, the complete $250s$ signal calculation was performed. The spectra at the observation points showed a good match with the modal analysis results of [bib1].

To validate the stability analysis on this fluid-structure problem, we will use keyword CRIT_STAB from DYNA_NON_LINE.

### 2.2 Baseline

The displacement values are tested at different times, depending on the $x$ direction, for two points of the mesh: $\mathrm{N145}$ and $\mathrm{N3119}$ . These points are on the free surface, on either side of the deformable wall, as can be seen from the diagram in paragraph [§1.1].

As for stability, since the matrix of geometric stiffness is not to be used (which is unavailable for the elements DKT used here), the analysis can only be of the type looking for singularities of the stiffness matrix (so a clean value that tends to 0).

The problem being linear elastic, one expects no instability and on the other, one should find the value of critical own (or critical load) as for the same model but without the fluid and worth -2,47726. This value of own will be the same in each step of calculation because the problem remains linear.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v8.03.100/10000000000004AE0000031090491E2344399B1A.png)

1. The total mesh has 8163 nodes, or approximately 125000 degrees of freedom,
2. L-element punctual $Q$ (DIS_TR model) allows to simply represent an accelerometer present in the model of article [bib1],
3. The deformable plate is modelled by 5120 elements of solid solid (3D Modelling) pentaedrics at 6 nodes (10 layers in thickness for a good approximation of bending behavior despite the linearity of the elements),
4. the free surface is modelled by 512 elements MEFP_FACE3 (modelling 2D_FLUI_PESA) triangles to 3 nodes,
5. the fluid volume is modelled by 24576 elements of the fluid (3D_FLUIDE Modelling) tetraedric to 4 nodes.

### 3.2 Writing boundary conditions

The bottom of the container can only move in the direction $x$:

```text
CONDLIM=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(    MODELE=MODELE,
DDL_IMPO=(_F(
GROUP_NO=('FONDS','FONDP',),
DY=0.0, DZ=0.0,),),);
```

In this direction $x$ , the bottom of the tank is required to move sinusoidally in time, in frequency $1,7704\mathrm{Hz}$ and in amplitude $0,001m$ :

```text
FREQ = 1.7704;
LFONC=DEFI_LIST_REEL(DEBUT=0.0, INTERVALLE=_F( JUSQU_A=10.0,
PAS=0.01,),);
FONC = FORMULE(    REEL = '''(REEL:INST) =
(0.001)*SIN(2*PI*FREQ*INST)''');
DEPLX=CALC_FONC_INTERP(        FONCTION=FONC,
NOM_PARA='INST',
LIST_PARA=LFONC,);
CHARG_SE=AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F(    MODELE=MODELE,
DDL_IMPO=_F(
GROUP_NO=('FONDS','FONDP',),DX=DEPLX,),);
```

The volume loading of gravity is defined as follows:

```text
PESA=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(    MODELE=MODELE,
PESANTEUR=_F(    GRAVITE=9.81,
DIRECTION=(0.,0.,-1.,),),);
```
