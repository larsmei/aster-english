# u2.03.08 Carrying out a civil-engineering study with prestressing cables in fast dynamics via the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS

> **Summary.**

The aim of this document is to give advice for carrying out civil-engineering studies in fast dynamics with prestressing cables and the concrete modelled as a shell (Q4GG element). The fast-dynamics analyses are carried out with *Europlexus* via the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS. The prestressing cables may be of three types: bonded, sliding or frictional.

One recalls the three types of cable modelling available in *Europlexus* and one illustrates their use on the example of a prestressed containment subjected to an impact. The four stages for the solution of the problem will be presented: creation of the mesh, data setup in *Code_Aster*, solution of the numerical problem and post-processing.

## 1 Introduction

In the context of the study of the resistance of civil-engineering buildings, the numerical simulations aim to gain a better understanding of their non-linear behaviour. This document presents the tools available in *Code_Aster* in order to model slender reinforced-concrete structures (Q4GG shell modelling) with prestressing cables and to carry out a fast-dynamics analysis calling on the *Europlexus* software. For more technical details on the data setup of an impact analysis with the law GLRC_DAMAGE, one may consult reference [8].

The aim of this document is to provide the user with advice on the data setup of the study with prestressing cables which may be of bonded, sliding or frictional type.

## 2 The EUROPLEXUS software

The *Europlexus* software [1] is a simulation software dedicated to the fast-dynamics analysis of structures and fluids. EDF co-develops this code in partnership with the owners: the French Alternative Energies and Atomic Energy Commission (CEA) and the Joint Research Centre of the European Commission located at Ispra (Italy).

The *Europlexus* software is based on a discretisation of space by means of finite elements. The time integration is carried out by means of a conditionally stable explicit scheme. The solution algorithm is non-linear both at the geometric level (large displacements, large strains) and at the material level (non-linear constitutive laws).

The *Europlexus* code can be called by the *Code_Aster* software via the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS, which makes it possible to drive the execution of a *Europlexus* study from a *Code_Aster* command file. The command CALC_EUROPLEXUS thus makes it possible to define and execute a *Europlexus* study while remaining entirely within the *Code_Aster* environment. The solution is carried out in the background by *Europlexus*, without the user having to worry about the preparation of the *Europlexus* input files.

CALC_EUROPLEXUS takes the Aster concepts as arguments, builds the *Europlexus* command file, launches and drives the execution of *Europlexus*, then rebuilds, from a *Europlexus* result file in MED format, an Aster result concept which can then be analysed with Aster in post-processing.

The scope of application of the command CALC_EUROPLEXUS is detailed in the documentation [U7.03.10](doc:u7.03.10).

## 3 Type of modelling of the prestressing cables

### 3.1 Introduction

In *Code_Aster* and *Europlexus*, the prestressing cables are modelled by 1D elements (bar with 2 nodes). In *Code_Aster*, there also exists a CABLE-GAINE element using 3 nodes. Their tensioning consists in applying a non-zero tension in these cables.

One distinguishes three cable modellings [5] and [6]:

- bonded cables,
- sliding cables,
- frictional cables.

The difference between these three types of cable rests on their connection with the concrete that surrounds them (see [2] for the theoretical aspects and the validation).

### 3.2 Bonded cables

A bonded cable is a cable entirely linked to the concrete by kinematic relations. All the displacements of the nodes of the cables are therefore constrained by the displacements of the nodes of the concrete.

This modelling corresponds to the technology of prestressing with bond. In this case, the cables or strands are placed in metal or plastic ducts, are tensioned, then the ducts are grouted with cement grout so as to ensure the bonding between the cables and the concrete.

### 3.3 Sliding cables

A sliding cable is a cable which can move freely along its trajectory (and only in this direction) relative to the concrete in which it is embedded. A sliding cable is therefore kinematically linked to the concrete only along the two directions orthogonal to its trajectory at each of its nodes.

In order for the cable to move along its trajectory in the case of large strains and large slips, these kinematic relations must be recomputed at each time step. One must both recompute the projection of the cable nodes onto the concrete and the trajectory of the cable in order to obtain the new tangent. The figure illustrates this procedure.

![Figure](/figures/u2.03.08/10000201000001DE0000012FA547520A215AA2AD.png)

**Figure** 3.3-a **: Sliding of the cable along its duct**

This modelling corresponds to the technology of prestressing without bond: this is the case, for example, of greased sheathed strands (TGG). In these cases, the cables or strands are placed in ducts filled with grease.

### 3.4 Frictional cables

A frictional cable is an intermediate case between a bonded cable and a sliding cable. The nodes of the cable can move relative to the concrete in which it is embedded according to a friction law in the direction of the trajectory of the cable. The friction law used in *Code_Aster and Europlexus* is a Coulomb law in which a friction threshold separates the bonded zone and the sliding zone (). The threshold of the law is defined by the linear (straight) and curved (curvilinear) friction coefficients (cf. 4.2.4).

![Figure](/figures/u2.03.08/100002010000021A000001C688D66EB54ABE682F.png)

**Figure** 3.4-a **: Friction model**

In this figure Flim denotes the threshold force of the law and dVr is the relative velocity of the cables with respect to the concrete.

This modelling may be used to reproduce the tension profiles of the BPEL [3] or ETCC [4] standards. From a practical point of view, frictional spring-type elements, called discrete elements in *Code_Aster*, must be defined in the model in order to take into account the friction constitutive law linking the nodes of the cable and the nodes of the concrete ().

![Figure](/figures/u2.03.08/10000201000001B2000001712699C80C65D70080.png)

**Figure** 3.4-b **: Modelling of the frictional concrete–steel connections**

## 4 Typical study of a prestressed containment

### 4.1 First stage: Creation of the mesh

#### 4.1.1 Concrete structure

The containment modelled for the typical study consists of a cylindrical barrel surmounted by a dome. The reinforced-concrete wall is meshed with 4-node surface elements (QUA4). The passive reinforcement is modelled via the homogenised reinforced-concrete constitutive law GLRC_DAMAGE; it is therefore not meshed. One can define a limited zone for taking into account the non-linear behaviour of the material.

The ribs, located on either side of the containment for $theta =0°$ and $theta =180°$, are reinforced zones in which the horizontal prestressing cables are anchored. They are modelled by shell elements () representing the extra thicknesses of concrete.

The mesh-cell groups allowing the assignment of the materials and of the loading are defined.

![Figure](/figures/u2.03.08/100002010000039A000002B958023D3B7B64F183.png)

Figure 4.1.1-a: Mesh of the concrete structure of the containment

| Figure 4.1.1-b: Close-up view of the anchorage theta =0° | Figure 4.1.1-c: View of the cylinder of the containment and of the frame |
| --- | --- |

**Note**

One will take care to produce a regular mesh with a uniform mesh-cell size.

The minimum mesh-cell size is taken into account in the computation of the time step for the explicit solution scheme. The stability of the scheme is therefore conditioned by the mesh.

Moreover, in the case of the use of a non-linear constitutive law, the presence of local refinements can lead to a localisation of the stresses and to unrealistic damage.

#### 4.1.2 Prestressing cables

Each cable is meshed by two-node segment elements. The choice of mesh-cell size must be close to the mesh-cell size chosen for the plate elements. One thus ensures that there is not a large number of cable nodes in one and the same concrete mesh cell, which would lead to a model with numerous kinematic relations leading to a significant run time. Conversely, if the prestressing cables are not meshed finely enough, the prestressing forces are poorly distributed and certain cable segments may “come out” of the concrete mesh during the loading.

It is not necessary to make the nodes of the cable coincide with the nodes of the concrete. The segment of the cables is not necessarily in the plane of the shell modelling the concrete, because the thickness of the shell will be taken into account in the pairing of the nodes of the cables and of the concrete. One must therefore ensure that the position of the nodes of the cable is indeed at a distance less than half the thickness of the corresponding concrete plate.

For each cable, one must define the anchorage nodes, which are the two ends of the cable. One must create a node group (containing a single node) for each of these anchorage nodes.

In this study, one distinguishes three groups of cables: vertical cables, horizontal cables and those of the dome. For the vertical cables and those of the dome, one can easily identify the ends in the figure. For the horizontal cables, a close-up view of the anchorage at the ends is proposed: the two terminations of the cables cross but are disconnected. The horizontal cables may be anchored on one of the two anchorage zones. It is advisable to alternate the horizontal cables anchored in the zones $theta =0°$ and $theta =180°$, in order to obtain a uniform prestress of the containment. This advice is particularly necessary in the case where friction is considered during prestressing, because the tension is not uniform along the cable during prestressing.

**Figure** 4.1.2-a **: Mesh of the prestressing cables**

![Figure](/figures/u2.03.08/100002010000029000000379723142B2A5B2A9A1.png)

**Figure** 4.1.2-b **: Close-up view of the anchorage of the horizontal cables**

**Specific case of the frictional cables**

For the case of the frictional cables, discrete elements must be built in the mesh all along the cables. To do this, one duplicates each cable node in the Salomé mesh-generation script, except the ends, and one creates a SEG2 mesh cell of zero length. One can create a single mesh-cell group DISFROT grouping all the discrete elements created.

### 4.2 Second stage: Data setup of the problem

#### 4.2.1 Reading and enrichments of the mesh

One must first check that the anchorage nodes are indeed accessible (individually) by a GROUP_NO.

One must also orient correctly the mesh-cell groups on which one imposes loadings of pressure or flux type (command ORIE_NORM_COQUE).

**Specific case of the frictional cables**

One must make sure that one has built a group of discrete mesh cells along the cables without including the ends.

The components modelled are:

1. the prestressing cables modelled by BARRE elements (supported by SEG2),
2. the concrete modelled by Q4GG shells (these are the only shell elements accepted by the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS).
3. **Specific case of the frictional cables**

#### 4.2.4 Definition of the materials

The constitutive laws available for the concrete in the context of this modelling are:

- ELAS
- GLRC_DAMAGE

The constitutive law GLRC_DAMAGE is a homogenised damage law whose parameters depend on the geometry of the section (thickness and cross-section of passive reinforcement), on the parameters of the response of the concrete and of the steel. The material field is created by means of the command DEFI_GLRC.

For the concrete and the steel, one must first create the materials with the elastic parameters (E, NU and RHO) and the damage parameters (SYC, SYT, D_SIGM_EPSI).

For taking the prestressing cables into account, one must add the keyword BETON_BPEL for the concrete.

| BETON = DEFI_MATERIAU( ELAS=_F(E = e_b, NU = nu_b, RHO = rho,), BETON_ECRO_LINE=_F( D_SIGM_EPSI=d_sigm_epsi, SYT=syt, SYC=syc,), BPEL_BETON=_F() ); | ← Keyword to be added |
| --- | --- |

If one wishes to take friction into account during prestressing (which can be done for all types of cable), one must add the friction parameters in the keyword BPEL_ACIER. The keywords are in this case FROT_COURB (curved friction coefficient) and FROT_LINE (linear friction coefficient).

| ACIER = DEFI_MATERIAU( ELAS=_F(E = e_a, NU = nu_a, RHO = rho,), ECRO_LINE=_F( D_SIGM_EPSI=d_sigm_epsi, SYT=syt, SYC=syc,), BPEL_ACIER=_F( FROT_COURB= frot_cb, FROT_LINE = frot_li, ) ); | ← Keywords to be added with the friction parameters (compulsory in the case of the frictional cables, optional for the other cables) |
| --- | --- |

The material fields are associated with the mesh via the command AFFE_MATERIAU.

**Note**

The material field created is assigned to the mesh. For the command CALC_EUROPLEXUS all the mesh-cell groups of the model must be associated with a material.

#### 4.2.5 Definition of the cables

The cable-definition stage goes through the command DEFI_CABLE_BP. This makes it possible to define what the tension in the cables must be according to the rules of the BPEL, as a function of the initial tension, of the anchorage draw-in (which applies only for the active anchorages), of the relaxation of the steel and of the delayed strains of the concrete (creep and shrinkage).

Let us point out that a single DEFI_CABLE_BP can group several cables provided that they have the same input parameters for the computation of the tension, and that one wishes to tension all these cables simultaneously.

In the command DEFI_CABLE_BP, one also indicates the concrete mesh cells surrounding the cables for the pairing.

| L_CAB =DEFI_CABLE_BP(MODELE = MODELE, CHAM_MATER = CHMATER, CARA_ELEM = CARAELE, GROUP_MA_BETON = l_beton, TYPE_ANCRAGE = ('ACTIF','ACTIF'), TENSION_INIT = tension_val, RECUL_ANCRAGE = recul, DEFI_CABLE = (_F(GROUP_MA = cable_1, GROUP_NO_ANCRAGE=(n1_debut,n1_fin)), _F(GROUP_MA = cable_2, GROUP_NO_ANCRAGE=(n2_debut,n2_fin)), ...)) | ← One occurrence per cable |

### 4.3 Third stage: Solution of the numerical problem

#### 4.3.1 Tensioning of the cables

The initial tension profiles of the cables are defined by the macro-command DEFI_CABLE_BP described previously. The effective tensioning of the cables is carried out by means of the macro-command CALC_PRECONT.

| --- | --- |

In the case presented, one tensions simultaneously all the prestressing cables as well as an instantaneous loading, without other loadings beforehand. In this case, it is sufficient to call the macro-command CALC_PRECONT only once. The loading is composed of the boundary conditions and of any instantaneous loadings. The loading CINE_CAB must not be included in the loadings. Under the keyword CABLE_BP, one will include all the DEFI_CABLE_BP concepts.

One will refer to the documentation [U2.03.06](doc:u2.03.06) [6] for the methodology on the application of the prestress in several stages.

#### 4.3.3 Limits of use

For the sliding and frictional cables, at each time step, one allows the cable nodes to move along the tangent to the trajectory of the cable at the given time instant. However, the tangent is an approximation of the trajectory of the cable at the node considered. Thus, when the cable node slides, it moves away by a small distance from the trajectory and always towards the outside of it. At each time step this deviation is all the greater as the displacement is large. With the repetition of the time steps, the nodes may end up moving away in a non-negligible manner from the theoretical trajectory of the cable.

In these cases, the analysis will stop with a message indicating that a cable node has left the thickness of the concrete mesh cell in which it should be included. In this case, one can use a smaller time step in order to remedy the problem.

### 4.4 Fourth stage: Post-processing

Once the analysis has been executed by Europlexus, the results are stored in the *evol_noli* concept returned by the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS. The post-processing can be carried out in the classical manner from this concept. The macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS also offers the possibility, from the keywords OBSERVATION or COURBE, of building a table with the data of interest for the post-processing.

One illustrates, for the study considered, certain post-processing operations that can be carried out. In what follows, one considers three different configurations with a modelling of bonded, sliding and frictional cables.

### 5.1 State at the application of the prestress

Following the application of the prestress in Code_Aster, via the macro-command CALC_PRECONT, the structure is deformed by the tensioning of the cables. The deformed shapes obtained for the three study configurations are presented with an amplification factor of 5000.

In all three cases, one observes a reduction in the diameter of the cylinder and a sagging of the dome. One notes that the deformed shape for the bonded-cable and sliding-cable configurations is identical.

| Bonded cables | Sliding cables | Frictional cables |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |

**Figure** 5.1.1-a **: Deformed shape following prestressing (amplification factor 5000)**

The tension of the cables, resulting from the prestressing, is defined by the macro-command CALC_PRECONT, which follows the rules of the BPEL [3]. The tension profiles therefore depend on the friction parameters and on the anchorage draw-in. It is possible to apply friction coefficients on all types of cable in order to represent the tensioning in a realistic manner. In the case of this study, no friction coefficient is imposed, nor any anchorage draw-in, for the bonded and sliding cables. The comparison of the tensions along the horizontal and vertical cables (see Figure) confirms that the imposed prestress is identical in these two cases. The tension of the cables is constant along the cables, equal to $3,75mathit{MN}$.

In the case of the configuration with frictional cables, one considers a linear friction, a curved friction and an anchorage draw-in for the horizontal cables. This is manifested by tension profiles that vary along the cable. For the vertical cables, one notes a loss of tension between the centre of the cable and the ends due to the linear friction coefficient. For the horizontal cables, the loss of tension between the centre of the cables and the ends is more pronounced because the curved friction is greater than the linear friction. The tension varies between $1,8mathit{MN}$ and $3,2mathit{MN}$. The tension peak is not measured at the ends of the cables because of the anchorage draw-in considered. The appreciable drop in the effective tension of the horizontal cables leads to a deformed shape of the structure that is less pronounced than for the bonded-cable and sliding-cable case. The variation of the tension along the frictional cables can also lead to an asymmetry of the behaviour of the structure. It is therefore important to model correctly the position of the anchorages of the cables, *i.e.* an alternation of the anchorages on either side of the structure. This ensures a uniform prestress.

The tension of the cables is reduced to $mathrm{3MN}$ locally for the computation time instant $0,1s$.

For the sliding cables, the tension profile along the cables remains constant throughout the analysis, around $3,75mathit{MN}$. The slip of the cable allowed in its duct makes it possible to accommodate the large strains of the concrete shell at the impact zone.

For the frictional cables, the tension profile along the cables is not constant because of the friction. The effect of the impact has little influence on the tension profile.

## 6 Conclusion

This document illustrates the methodology for taking the prestress into account in a *Europlexus* analysis carried out in the *Code_Aster* environment via the macro-command CALC_EUROPLEXUS on a simplified study of an impact on a containment.

The three types of cable modelling possible in *Europlexus* are considered, namely the bonded, sliding and frictional cables. The specific features of the data setup of each case are presented, as well as the results of the three analyses.
