# v6.03.144 SSNP144 – Using a cohesive zone model with the X-FEM method

**Summary**

This test is designed to validate the constitutive law cohesive that is used during cracking of elements $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ . This approach, described in [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12), allows you to model the opening of an interface taking into account a cohesion force between the lips of the interface.

Modellings $A$ at $K$ involves a parallelepipedic bar cracked over its entire section (i.e., interface), subject to a forced displacement, resulting in the separation of both parts of the structure in $I$ mode as well as in $\mathrm{II}$ mode. The influence of the refinement of the mesh and the type of elements used is studied.

$L$ Modelling tests the case of an interface that opens and closes in $I$ mode. It is used simultaneously with penalised contact and control.

Validation is done by comparing the Lagrangian contact and friction values with an analytical reference solution.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.4.1 Degrees of freedom enriched and boundary conditions

Remember that moving under $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ is the sum of a continuous displacement and a discontinuous displacement. In the case of an interface, without crack bottom, the approximation of the displacement is written as follows:

${u}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i\in {N}_{n}(x)}{a}_{i}{\phi }_{i}(x)+\sum_{j\in {N}_{n}(x)\cap K}{b}_{j}{\phi }_{j}(x)H(\mathrm{lsn}(x))$

Où:

> ${a}_{i}$ and ${b}_{i}$ are the degrees of freedom moving node $i$ ,
> ${\phi }_{i}$ the form functions associated with node $i$ ,
> ${N}_{n}(x)$ is all nodes with point $x$ in its support,
> $K$ is all nodes with a fully cracked support,
> $H(x)$ is the generalized Heaviside function defined by $H(x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{}-1\text{si}x<0\\ +1\text{si}x\ge 0\end{array}$,
> $\mathrm{lsn}(x)$ is the normal level-set value at point $x$ .

For more details, refer to the $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) reference documentation.

It is therefore possible to impose boundary conditions on total displacement (or physical displacement), on its continuous component or on its discontinuous component. We will first give different useful relationships between this physical displacement and the degrees of freedom existing in Code_Aster.

Example: a edge of an mesh $\mathrm{2D}$ any interface crossing:

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.144/10000000000001860000016CCFF7C3A185887905.png)

Figure 1.4.1-1: Arête intersectée par une interface

Call ${P}^{+}$ and ${P}^{-}$ the geometric points that belong to the segment and are located just below and above the level set, respectively, and consider without loss of generality vertical displacements. We have:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\mathit{DY}({N}^{+})={a}_{y}^{+}+{b}_{y}^{+}\\ \mathit{DY}({N}^{-})={a}_{y}^{-}-{b}_{y}^{-}\\ \mathit{DY}({P}^{+})=\alpha ({a}_{y}^{+}+{b}_{y}^{+})+(1-\alpha )({a}_{y}^{-}+{b}_{y}^{-})\\ \mathit{DY}({P}^{-})=\alpha ({a}_{y}^{+}-{b}_{y}^{+})+(1-\alpha )({a}_{y}^{-}-{b}_{y}^{-})\end{array}$

The expression of the displacement jump for the ridge can be drawn from this:

$\mathrm{〚}\mathit{DY}\mathrm{〛}=\mathit{DY}({P}^{+})-\mathit{DY}({P}^{-})=\alpha {b}_{y}^{+}+(1-\alpha ){b}_{y}^{-}$

#### 1.4.2 Loading

In $I$ mode, the nodes on the lower side of the bar is clamped and vertical displacement is imposed on the upper side, as explained in Figure .

In $\mathrm{II}$ opening mode, the nodes located below the level set are clamped, and a vertical $\mathit{DY}$ ($\mathit{DZ}$ in $\mathrm{3D}$) shift is imposed from $\mathrm{1,0E-13}$ to all nodes located above the level set. This ensures the nullity of the contact indicator at the interface level. We also impose pure shear at the cohesive area which, taking into account the notations of the figure, is imposed for each intersected edge:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\mathit{DX}({P}^{-})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{-})=0\\ \mathit{DX}({P}^{+})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{+})\end{array}$

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.144/Cadre21.gif)

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.144/Cadre21-1.gif)

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.144/Cadre21-2.gif)

Figure 1.4.2-1: Chargement appliqué, en mode $I$ et en mode $\mathrm{II}$

We see that this requires imposing restraints between degrees of freedom on both sides of the $i$ edge that depend on the $\alpha$ coefficient. In order not to unnecessarily weigh the command file, we will only test in $\mathrm{II}$ mode meshes for which:

- or all intersect edges are in their middle, i.e. for each $\alpha =0,5$
- either the interface is compliant, i.e. $\alpha =1$

In the first case, where all edges are intersected in their middle, we ensure the nullity of the contact indicator by imposing $\mathit{H1Y}=5.E-14$ on all nodes ends of the intersected edges by the interface. As far as shearing is concerned, we must impose the relationships:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\mathit{DX}({P}^{-})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{-})\mathrm{\iff }\frac{{a}_{x}^{+}-{b}_{x}^{+}}{2}+\frac{{a}_{x}^{-}-{b}_{x}^{-}}{2}={a}_{x}^{-}-{b}_{x}^{-}=0\\ \mathit{DX}({P}^{+})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{+})\mathrm{\iff }\frac{{a}_{x}^{+}+{b}_{x}^{+}}{2}+\frac{{a}_{x}^{-}+{b}_{x}^{-}}{2}={a}_{x}^{+}+{b}_{x}^{+}\end{array}$

We see then that if we impose the ${a}_{x}^{+}={b}_{x}^{+}$ relationship then $\mathit{DX}({P}^{-})=0$ .

If we then impose the ${b}_{x}^{+}={b}_{x}^{-}$ relationship, we have more $\mathit{DX}({P}^{+})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{+})$.

In the second case, with mesh compliant interface, the jump is represented by the degree of freedom Heaviside at point confused with the interface and we must therefore impose $\mathit{H1Y}=1.E-13$ on nodes confused with the interface. As far as shearing is concerned, if ${N}^{-}$ corresponds to the point confused with the ${P}^{-}$ interface, we must impose:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\mathit{DX}({P}^{-})={a}_{x}^{-}-{b}_{x}^{-}=0\\ \mathit{DX}({P}^{+})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{+})\mathrm{\iff }{a}_{x}^{-}+{b}_{x}^{-}={a}_{x}^{+}\end{array}$

We see then that if we impose the ${a}_{x}^{-}={b}_{x}^{-}$ relationship then $\mathit{DX}({N}^{-})=\mathit{DX}({P}^{-})=0$ .

If we then impose the ${a}_{x}^{-}=\frac{{a}_{x}^{+}}{2}$ relationship, we have more $\mathit{DX}({P}^{+})=\mathit{DX}({N}^{+})$.

These two relationships are imposed for all nodes vis-à-vis mesh cells crossed by the interface.

Note that in the command file, the syntax to impose these different degrees of freedoms differs depending on the operators. The DDL_IMPO factor keyword of the AFFE_CHAR_MECA operator allows to impose total displacement (or physical displacement) thanks to the DX, DY, and DZ keywords, as well as degrees of freedom discontinuous ${b}_{i}$ keywords thanks to H1X, H1Y, and H1Z. On the other hand, the LIAISON_GROUP factor keyword allows to control degrees of freedom continuous ${a}_{i}$ keywords through DX, DY, and DZ (not total displacement) as well as degrees of freedom discontinuous ${b}_{i}$ keywords through H1X, H1Y, and H1Z.

## 2 reference solution

The reference solution for this test case is provided by the cohesive behavior relationship defined in [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12):

${t}_{c}=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha )({〚u〛}_{n}+{\beta}^{2}{〚u〛}_{\tau})$

Où :

- $〚u〛$ is the displacement jump
- ${〚u〛}_{n}=n\otimes n.〚u〛$ is the projection of the shift jump as normal at the interface
- ${〚u〛}_{\tau}=(\text{Id}-n\otimes n).〚u〛$ is the projection of the displacement jump according to the tangential plane at the interface
- $\beta$ is an experimentally obtained quantity representing the ratio of the opening forces in $I$ mode and $\mathrm{II}$ mode. For this test, this parameter is selected unitary.
- ${〚u〛}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{{∥{〚u〛}_{n}∥}^{2}+{\beta}^{2}{∥{〚u〛}_{\tau}∥}^{2}}$
- $\alpha$ is an internal variable corresponding to the highest value of ${〚u〛}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ reached during opening. This internal variable has as its initial value ${\alpha}_{0}=\frac{{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}\text{PENA\_ADHERENCE}$. If the material comes out of its elasticity range, we have $\alpha ={〚u〛}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ .

The cohesion force is therefore explicitly known 1
depending on the displacement jump.

*Mode* $I$ *:*

For reasons of invariance, consider a one-dimensional problem. We will also assume that the structure is meshed with only three elements, which does not change the validity of the results subsequently stated. The interface is present in the middle of the central element as shown in []. Since we are driving by DDL_IMPO the degree of freedom $\mathrm{H1Y}$ of node $\text{N2}$ (also noted ${b}_{\mathrm{y2}}$), let's solve the problem according to this parameter.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.144/1000000000000113000002762AA6AE8AD5014A80.png)

**Figure** 2-a **: One-dimensional representation of Reference problem**

Let us express the movements of the different points of the structure:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{u}^{1}=0\\ {u}^{2}=L\epsilon \\ {u}^{+}=(L+\frac{a}{2})\epsilon \\ {u}^{-}=(L+\frac{a}{2})\epsilon +{[[u]]}_{n}\\ {u}^{3}=(L+a)\epsilon +{[[u]]}_{n}\\ {u}^{4}=(\mathrm{2L}+a)\epsilon +{[[u]]}_{n}\end{array}$

Now let's express the displacements of $\text{N2}$, $\text{M+}$, $\text{M-}$ and $\text{N3}$ points based on degrees of freedom $X-\mathrm{FEM}$.

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{u}^{2}={a}_{\mathit{y2}}-{b}_{\mathit{y2}}\\ {u}^{3}={a}_{\mathit{y2}}+{b}_{\mathit{y3}}\\ {u}^{+}=\frac{{a}_{\mathit{y2}}+{b}_{\mathit{y2}}}{2}+\frac{{a}_{\mathit{y3}}+{b}_{\mathit{y3}}}{2}\\ {u}^{-}=\frac{{a}_{\mathit{y2}}-{b}_{\mathit{y2}}}{2}+\frac{{a}_{\mathit{y3}}-{b}_{\mathit{y3}}}{2}\end{array}$

By reversing this system, we obtain:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{a}_{\mathit{y2}}=\frac{{u}_{2}}{2}-\frac{{u}_{3}}{2}+{u}^{+}\\ {b}_{\mathit{y2}}=-\frac{{u}_{2}}{2}-\frac{{u}_{3}}{2}+{u}^{+}\\ {a}_{\mathit{y3}}=-\frac{{u}_{2}}{2}+\frac{{u}_{3}}{2}+{u}^{-}\\ {b}_{\mathit{y3}}=\frac{{u}_{2}}{2}+\frac{{u}_{3}}{2}-{u}^{-}\end{array}$

The elastic law of the structure gives: $\sigma =E\epsilon$ .

The system balance gives $\sigma ={t}_{c}$ .

Finally, the cohesive constitutive law provides a relationship between stress and displacement jump. This relationship is different depending on whether the joint remains in its elastic domain or if it damages itself.

If the joint remains in the elastic domain, the internal variable $\alpha$ remains constant. We have ${t}_{c}=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha ){〚u〛}_{n}$ then .

The movements of the different points of the structure are then written:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{u}^{1}=0\\ {u}^{2}=\frac{L}{E}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha ){\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n}\\ {u}^{+}=\frac{L+\frac{a}{2}}{E}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha ){\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n}\\ {u}^{-}=(1+\frac{L+\frac{a}{2}}{E}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha )){\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n}\\ {u}^{3}=(1+\frac{L+a}{E}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha )){\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n}\\ {u}^{4}=(1+\frac{\mathrm{2L}+a}{E}\frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{\alpha}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}\alpha )){\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n}\end{array}$

By injecting these expressions into the formulas giving ${b}_{\mathrm{y2}}$ and ${b}_{\mathrm{y3}}$, we find that ${b}_{\mathrm{y2}}={b}_{\mathrm{y3}}$.

So we have ${[[u]]}_{n}={u}^{+}-{u}^{-}={b}_{\mathrm{y2}}+{b}_{\mathrm{y3}}={\mathrm{2.b}}_{\mathrm{y2}}$. So the problem is completely determined for the degree of freedom ${b}_{\mathrm{y2}}$, corresponding to the half jump, and known at any moment thanks to the steering.

If the seal remains damaged, the internal variable $\alpha$ becomes equal to the ${[[u]]}_{n}$ displacement jump. We have ${t}_{c}={\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})$ then.

The movements of the different points of the structure are then written:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{u}^{1}=0\\ {u}^{2}=\frac{L}{E}{\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})\\ {u}^{+}=\frac{L+\frac{a}{2}}{E}{\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})\\ {u}^{-}=\frac{(L+\frac{a}{2})}{E}{\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})+{[[u]]}_{n}\\ {u}^{3}=\frac{(L+a)}{E}{\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})+{[[u]]}_{n}\\ {u}^{4}=\frac{(\mathrm{2L}+a)}{E}{\sigma}_{c}\exp(\frac{-{\sigma}_{c}}{{G}_{c}}{\mathrm{〚}u\mathrm{〛}}_{n})+{[[u]]}_{n}\end{array}$

By injecting these expressions into the formulas giving ${b}_{\mathit{y2}}$ and ${b}_{\mathit{y3}}$, we find that ${b}_{\mathit{y2}}={b}_{\mathit{y3}}$.

So we have ${[[u]]}_{n}={u}^{+}-{u}^{-}={b}_{\mathit{y2}}+{b}_{\mathit{y3}}={\mathrm{2.b}}_{\mathit{y2}}$. So the problem is completely determined for the degree of freedom ${b}_{\mathit{y2}}$, corresponding to the half jump, and known at any moment thanks to the steering.

*Mode* $\mathrm{II}$ *:*

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a XFEM model, with flat constraints, with the discontinuity interface defined by a level function (level set rated $\text{LN}$ for the normal level set) directly in the command file using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator.

The level function equation for the interface is as follows:

$\text{LN}=Y-2.5$

No tangential level is required since the TYPE_DISCONTINUITE='INTERFACE' key word is used, which allows the structure to be completely cut into two parts.

The cohesive law is introduced via the DEFI_CONT operator, specifying ALGO_CONT='CZM', and the cohesive constitutive law is enabled with the RELATION='CZM_EXP_REG' keyword.

Here we test the $I$ and $\mathrm{II}$ opening modes.

## 14 Modelling L

### 14.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ model, in flat constraints, with contact definition on the discontinuity interface defined by a level function (level set rated $\text{LN}$ for the normal level set) directly in the command file using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator.

The slave/master status for a contact surface $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ is given by the sign of the normal level function $\text{LN}$: the slave surface is on the negative side while the master surface is on the positive side.

The level function equation for the interface is as follows:

$\text{LN}=Y-2.5$

No tangential level is required since the TYPE_DISCONTINUITE='INTERFACE' key word is used, which allows the structure to be completely cut into two parts.

This test models an interface in opening and closing for which the displacement jump is controlled by a SAUT_IMPO control.

The cohesive law is introduced via the DEFI_CONT operator, specifying ALGO_CONT='CZM', and the cohesive constitutive law is activated with the RELATION='CZM_EXP_REG' keyword. When closed, the contact is managed by a penalty term included in the cohesive law.

### 14.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh is the same as the $A$ Modelling. The interface is therefore present in the central element via the level sets.

## 15 Summary of the results

The numerical results are in line with the analytical solution. These tests validate the cohesive contact law implemented for the $X-\mathrm{FEM}$ method in $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$ in the various opening modes.

$L$ Modelling also helps to remove the incompatibility between contact in continuous method and control using a term of penalization in cohesive law.
