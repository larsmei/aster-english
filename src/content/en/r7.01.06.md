# r7.01.06 Constitutive relation BETON_UMLV for the creep of concrete

**Summary:**

This document presents the UMLV basic-creep model (constitutive behaviour BETON_UMLV), which is a way of modelling the creep of concrete (basic creep and desiccation creep).

The writing and the numerical treatment of the model are also detailed there. The integration of the model (that is, the update of the stresses) is carried out according to an incremental scheme from the increment of total strains supplied by the global solution scheme.

The description of the coupling between basic creep and the MAZARS model is added. The integration of the model (that is, the update of the stresses) is carried out according to a “total” scheme from the total strains accumulated since the initial state before loading.

## 1 Introduction

Within the framework of studies of the long-term behaviour of concrete structures, a predominant part of the strains measured on the structure concerns the delayed strains that appear in the concrete during its life. They comprise the shrinkages at early age, the desiccation shrinkage, the basic creep and the desiccation creep.

The model presented here is dedicated to the modelling of the delayed strain associated with creep, both basic and of desiccation. Basic creep is, in addition to desiccation creep, the part of the creep of concrete that one would observe during a test without exchange of water with the exterior. Experimentally, concrete in creep presents an ageing viscoelastic behaviour. The creep strain observed is proportional to the loading stress, and depends on the temperature and on the hygrometry. The ageing aspect and the dependence on temperature are not taken into account by this law.

**Basic creep.**  The first creep model for concretes introduced previously into Code_Aster (see for example [R7.01.01](doc:r7.01.01) and [bib4]) was developed with a view to predicting the longitudinal creep strains under uniaxial stresses. The generalisation of this model, in order to take into account a multiaxial stress state, is then carried out by means of an arbitrary creep Poisson's ratio, constant and equal to, or close to, the elastic Poisson's ratio. Now, the *a posteriori* determination of the effective creep Poisson's ratio shows its dependence on the loading path. Moreover, the concrete of certain structures of the EDF fleet, such as the containment buildings of nuclear reactors, is subjected to a biaxial stress state. This observation led to the development of the UMLV basic-creep strain law (Université de Marne-la-Vallée, a partner in the development of this model), for which the creep Poisson's ratio is a direct consequence of the computation of the principal strains.

> **Desiccation creep.** The model proposed here is that of Bazant [bib6]. It is a purely viscous law.

In *Code_Aster*, the creep model presented here is used under the name BETON_UMLV.

## 2 Assumptions

**Assumption 1 (small-perturbation assumption)**

The law is written within the framework of small perturbations.

**Assumption 2 (partition of the strains)**

In small strains, the total strain tensor is decomposed into several terms relating to the processes considered. As regards the description of the various mechanisms of delayed strains of concretes, one admits that the total strain is written:

$\epsilon =\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{déformation}\\ \text{élastique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{e}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{fluage}\\ \text{propre}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fp}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{fluage de}\\ \text{dessiccation}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fdess}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{retrait}\\ \text{endogène}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\Re }}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{retrait de}\\ \text{dessiccation}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{rd}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{déformation}\\ \text{thermique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}}}$                         eq 2-1

In this document, one does not describe the taking into account of the various types of shrinkage (for that, see the *Code_Aster* documentation [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12)), so that [eq 2-1] reduces to:

$\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fp}}+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{fdess}}$                                                             eq 2-2

**Assumption 3 (decomposition of the creep components)**

In general, basic creep may be modelled by combining the elastic behaviour of the solid and the viscous behaviour of the fluid. For the law presented, basic creep is described as the combination of the elastic behaviour of the hydrates and of the aggregates and of the viscous behaviour of the water.

In the case of the model BETON_BURGER, one makes the assumption that basic creep may be decomposed into a process decoupling a spherical part and a deviatoric part. The tensor of the total basic-creep strains is then written:

${\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{\mathit{fp}}=\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{partie}\\ \text{sphérique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\epsilon}^{\text{fs}}\cdot \underline{\underline{1}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{partie}\\ \text{déviatorique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{\text{fd}}}}$ with ${\epsilon}^{\text{fs}}=\frac{1}{3}\cdot \text{tr}{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{\mathit{fp}}$                                         eq 2-3

The stress tensor may be expanded in a similar form:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}=\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{partie}\\ \text{sphérique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\sigma}^{s}\cdot \underline{\underline{1}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{c}\text{partie}\\ \text{déviatorique}\end{array}}{\underset{⏟}{{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}^{d}}}$                                                             eq 2-4

The model BETON_BURGER assumes a total decoupling between the spherical and deviatoric components of basic creep: the strains induced by the spherical stresses are purely spherical and the strains induced by the deviatoric stresses are purely deviatoric. On the other hand, the accumulated viscous strains have an effect on the viscous properties of the fluid, whatever its origin (spherical or deviatoric). In order to take into account the effect of the internal humidity, the strains are multiplied by the internal relative humidity:

${\epsilon}^{s}=h\cdot f({\sigma}^{s})$ et ${\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{d}=h\cdot f({\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}^{d})$                                                 eq 2-5

where $h$ denotes the internal relative humidity.

Condition [eq 2-5] makes it possible to verify a posteriori that the basic-creep strains are proportional to the relative humidity.

## 3 Description of the model

### 3.1 Basic creep

#### 3.1.1 Description of the spherical part of basic creep

The spherical stresses are at the origin of the migration of the water adsorbed at the interfaces between the hydrates at the level of the macro-porosity and absorbed within the micro-porosity into the capillary porosity. The diffusion of the inter-lamellar water from the hydrate pores towards the capillary porosity takes place in an irreversible manner. The total spherical creep strain is therefore written as the sum of a reversible part and of an irreversible part:

${\epsilon}^{\text{fs}}=\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{partie}\\ \text{réversible}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{partie}\\ \text{irréversible}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}}}$                                                         eq 3.1.1-1

> The process of spherical creep strain is governed by the following system of coupled equations (equations [eq 3.1.1-2] and [eq 3.1.1-3]):

${\dot{\epsilon}}^{\text{fs}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}\cdot \left[h\cdot {\sigma}^{s}-{k}_{r}^{s}\cdot {\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}\right]-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}$                                                 eq 3.1.1-2

where ${k}_{r}^{s}$ denotes the apparent stiffness associated with the skeleton formed by blocks of hydrates at the mesoscopic scale;

and ${\eta}_{r}^{s}$ the apparent viscosity associated with the diffusion mechanism within the capillary porosity.

${\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}{\langle \left[{k}_{r}^{s}\cdot {\epsilon}^{\text{fs}}-({k}_{r}^{s}+{k}_{i}^{s})\cdot {\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}\right]-\left[{\mathrm{h\sigma }}^{s}-{k}_{r}^{s}\cdot {\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}\right]\rangle }^{+}$                                 eq 3.1.1-3

where ${k}_{i}^{s}$ denotes the apparent stiffness associated intrinsically with the hydrates at the microscopic scale;

and ${\eta}_{i}^{s}$ the apparent viscosity associated with the inter-layer diffusion mechanism.

In [eq 3.1.1-3], the brackets ${\langle \rangle }^{+}$ denote the Mac Cauley operator: ${\langle x\rangle }^{+}=\frac{1}{2}(x+\mid x\mid )$

Figure 3.1.1-1: Phenomenological model associated with the spherical part of basic creep

#### 3.1.2 Description of the deviatoric part

The deviatoric stresses are at the origin of a sliding mechanism (or quasi-dislocation mechanism) of the CSH sheets in the nano-porosity. Under deviatoric stress, creep takes place at constant volume. Moreover, the UMLV creep law assumes the isotropy of the deviatoric creep. Phenomenologically, the sliding mechanism comprises a reversible viscoelastic contribution of the water strongly adsorbed on the CSH sheets and an irreversible viscous contribution of the free water:

$\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{déformation}\\ \text{déviatorique}\\ \text{totale}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{\text{fd}}}}=\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{contribution}\\ \text{eau}\\ \text{absobée}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{r}^{\text{fd}}}}+\underset{\begin{array}{}\text{contribution}\\ \text{eau}\\ \text{libre}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{i}^{\text{fd}}}}$                                                         eq 3.1.2-1

The *j*-th principal component of the total deviatoric strain is governed by equations [eq. 3.1.2-2] and [eq 3.1.2-3]:

- ${\eta}_{r}^{d}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{d,j}+{k}_{r}^{d}{\epsilon}_{r}^{d,j}=h\cdot {\sigma}^{d,j}$                                                 eq 3.1.2-2

- ${\eta}_{i}^{d}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{d,j}=h\cdot {\sigma}^{d,j}$                                                         eq 3.1.2-3

where ${k}_{r}^{d}$ denotes the stiffness associated with the capacity of the adsorbed water to transmit loads (load bearing water), ${\eta}_{r}^{d}$ the viscosity associated with the water adsorbed by the hydrate sheets and ${\eta}_{i}^{d}$ denotes the viscosity of the free water.

### 3.2 Desiccation creep

One assumes that one can decompose the desiccation creep ${\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}^{\mathit{fdess}}$ into two parts called intrinsic and structural [bib4]:

${\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}^{\mathit{fdess}}={\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{int}^{\text{fdess}}+{\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{\text{struct}}^{\text{fdess}}$                                                                 eq 3.2-1

It is agreed that the structural strain is not a strain component in itself; therefore, in this document, the only component of desiccation creep concerns the intrinsic part:

${\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}^{\text{fdess}}={\mathit{\Delta \epsilon }}_{int}^{\text{fdess}}$                                                                     eq 3.2-2

Bazant et al. [bib10] suggest that drying and the application of a compressive loading simultaneously are responsible for the micro-diffusion of the molecules between the macro-pores and the micro-pores. The micro-diffusion of the water molecules would favour the breaking of the bonds between the gel particles, inducing the desiccation-creep strain. It is one of the most complicated physico-chemical phenomena to model, resulting from a coupling between the stress, the basic creep and the drying. They propose the following equation (equation of a dashpot) in order to take into account the (intrinsic) desiccation creep at the elementary level:

${\dot{\epsilon}}^{\text{fdess}}=\frac{\left|\dot{h}\right|\sigma }{{\eta}^{\mathit{fd}}}$                                                                     eq 3.2-3

with:

- ${\epsilon}^{\text{fdess}},$ the desiccation-creep strain,
- ${\eta}^{\mathit{fd}}$ a material parameter (in $\left[\mathit{Pa}\cdot \mathit{sec}\right]$ in SI units),
- $h,$ the relative humidity, which evolves with time, a datum of the evolution problem.

## 4 Discretisation of the constitutive equations of the model

### 4.1 Discretisation of the constitutive equations of spherical creep

One carries out a first-order linearisation of the product of the stresses and of the humidity:

$\sigma (t)\cdot h(t)\approx {\sigma}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+\frac{t-{t}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}({\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n})$                                 eq 4.1-1

After discretisation of the stresses and of the relative humidity by affine functions, the spherical basic-creep strain is discretised by the following equation:

${\text{Δε}}_{n}^{\text{fs}}={a}_{n}^{s}+{b}_{n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n}^{s}+{c}_{n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n+1}^{s}\iff \Delta \left(\text{tr}{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{f}\right)={\mathrm{3a}}_{n}^{s}+{b}_{n}^{s}\cdot \text{tr}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}+{c}_{n}^{s}\cdot \text{tr}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}$                 eq 4.1-2

where ${\sigma}_{n}^{s}$ and ${\sigma}_{n+1}^{s}$ are the spherical stresses at the beginning and at the end of the current time step.

One must distinguish two cases according to whether the irreversible strain must be taken into account or not.

**1st case:** the irreversible spherical creep strain is not taken into account; equation [eq4.1‑2] may be put in the form (simple Kelvin chain):

${\eta}_{r}^{s}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)=h(t){\sigma}_{r}^{s}(t)$                                                 eq 4.1-3

After discretisation, the previous equation may be put in the form:

${\text{Δε}}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}={a}_{r,n}^{s}+{b}_{r,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n}^{s}+{c}_{r,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n+1}^{s}$                                             eq 4.1-4

With:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{a}_{r,n}^{s}=\left[\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}})-1\right]\cdot {\epsilon}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}\\ {b}_{r,n}^{s}=\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left\lbrace \left[-(\frac{{\mathrm{2\tau }}_{r}^{s}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+1){h}_{n}+\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n+1}\right]\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}})+\left[(\frac{{\mathrm{2\tau }}_{r}^{s}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-1){h}_{n}-\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{s}-{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n+1}\right]\right\rbrace \\ {c}_{r,n}^{s}=\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\left[\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{s}}){h}_{n}-\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{s}-{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n}\right]\end{array}$     eq 4.1-5

The irreversible strain, for its part, does not vary:

${\text{Δε}}_{i,n}^{\text{fs}}=0\Rightarrow \lbrace \begin{array}{c}{a}_{i,n}^{s}=0\\ {b}_{i,n}^{s}=0\\ {c}_{i,n}^{s}=0\end{array}$                                                         eq 4.1-6

**2nd case:** the irreversible spherical creep strain must be taken into account.

With the aid of the linearisation [eq 4.1-1], ], the system of coupled equations is written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+2{\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}(t)=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}\left[{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+\frac{t-{t}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}({\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n})-{k}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)\right]\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}(t)=-\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}(-{\mathrm{2k}}_{r}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+{k}_{i}^{s}{\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}(t)+{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+\frac{t-{t}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}({\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}))\end{array}$                 eq 4.1-7

This system may be put in the form:

${\underline{\dot{\epsilon}}}^{\text{fs}}(t)=\left[\begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}(t)\end{array}\right]=\underline{\underline{A}}:{\underline{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+\underline{b}+(t-{t}_{n})\underline{c}\iff \lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)={a}_{\text{rr}}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+{a}_{\text{ri}}^{s}{\epsilon}_{i}^{s}(t)+{b}_{r}^{s}+{c}_{r}^{s}(t-{t}_{n})\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{i}^{\text{fs}}(t)={a}_{\text{ir}}^{s}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}(t)+{a}_{ii}^{s}{\epsilon}_{i}^{s}(t)+{b}_{i}^{s}+{c}_{i}^{s}(t-{t}_{n})\end{array}$         eq 4.1-8

$\underline{\underline{A}}$ , $\underline{b}$ and $\underline{c}$ are defined as follows:

$\begin{array}{}\underline{\underline{A}}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{a}_{\text{rr}}^{s}& {a}_{\text{ri}}^{s}\\ {a}_{\text{ir}}^{s}& {a}_{ii}^{s}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}-\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}-4\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}& 2\frac{{k}_{i}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\\ 2\frac{{k}_{r}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}& -\frac{{k}_{i}^{s}}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\end{array}\right]\\ \underline{b}=\left[\begin{array}{c}{b}_{r}^{s}\\ {b}_{i}^{s}\end{array}\right]={\sigma}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}\left[\begin{array}{c}\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}+\frac{2}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\\ -\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\end{array}\right]\\ \underline{c}=\left[\begin{array}{c}{c}_{r}^{s}\\ {c}_{i}^{s}\end{array}\right]=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}\left[\begin{array}{c}\frac{1}{{\eta}_{r}^{s}}+\frac{2}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\\ -\frac{1}{{\eta}_{i}^{s}}\end{array}\right]\end{array}$                             eq 4.1-9

The previous system of equations may be decoupled and solved in the space of the eigenvectors. The system of equations is in fact written:

${\dot{\epsilon}}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t)={\lambda}_{k}{\epsilon}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t)+{b}_{k}^{\text{*}}+{c}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t-{t}_{n})\text{avec}{\underline{\dot{\epsilon}}}^{\text{*}}=\left[\begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{1}^{\text{*}}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{2}^{\text{*}}\end{array}\right]={\underline{\underline{P}}}^{-1}\cdot \underline{\dot{\epsilon}}$                                 eq 4.1-10

Thus, in the space of the eigenvectors, the creep model becomes equivalent to a double Kelvin chain. It is necessary to know the solution of the homogeneous equation (without a right-hand side), as well as a particular solution, in order to solve the previous differential equation. The homogeneous solution of each of the two equations is the following:

${\epsilon}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t)={\mu}_{k}{e}^{{\lambda}_{k}\cdot t}$                                                                     eq 4.1-11

where ${\mu}_{k}$ is a parameter depending on the initial condition. A particular solution is obtained by the method of variation of the constant (${\mu}_{k}={\mu}_{k}(t)$ ). One then obtains the following solutions:

${\epsilon}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t)={\mu}_{k}{e}^{{\lambda}_{k}\cdot t}-\frac{1}{{\lambda}_{k}}\left[{b}_{k}^{\text{*}}+{c}_{k}^{\text{*}}(t-{t}_{n}+\frac{1}{{\lambda}_{k}})\right]$                                             eq 4.1-12

The reversible and irreversible spherical creep strains are then equal to:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n+1})=\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}+({x}_{1}{\mu}_{1}{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{t}_{n+1}}+{\mu}_{2}{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{t}_{n+1}})\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n+1})=\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}}+({\mu}_{1}{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{t}_{n+1}}+{x}_{2}{\mu}_{2}{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{t}_{n+1}})\end{array}$                                 eq 4.1-13

After simplification, one then obtains the following expressions for the values of ${\mu}_{k}$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\mu}_{1}=\frac{1}{({x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1){e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{t}_{n}}}\left[{x}_{2}({\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n})-\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}})-({\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n})-\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}})\right]\\ {\mu}_{2}=\frac{1}{({x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1){e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{t}_{n}}}\left[-({\epsilon}_{r}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n})-\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}})+{x}_{1}({\epsilon}_{i}^{\text{fs}}({t}_{n})-\frac{{\sigma}_{n}\cdot {\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}+{\sigma}_{n+1}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}})\right]\end{array}$     eq 4.1-14

Equation [eq 4.1-2] may therefore be put in the form, after discretisation:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{Δε}}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}={a}_{r,n}^{s}+{b}_{r,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n}^{s}+{c}_{i,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n+1}^{s}\\ {\text{Δε}}_{i,n}^{\text{fs}}={a}_{i,n}^{s}+{b}_{i,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n}^{s}+{c}_{i,n}^{s}\cdot {\sigma}_{n+1}^{s}\end{array}$                                         eq 4.1-15

With:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{a}_{r,n}^{s}=\left[\frac{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}-1\right]\cdot {\epsilon}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}-{x}_{1}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]\cdot \underset{k\le n}{\max}({\epsilon}_{i,k}^{\text{fs}})\\ {b}_{r,n}^{s}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\cdot \left[\frac{-{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}+1\right]+\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}}\cdot {x}_{1}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]\\ {c}_{r,n}^{s}=\frac{{h}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\cdot \left[\frac{-{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}+1\right]+\frac{{h}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}}\cdot {x}_{1}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]\end{array}$                 eq 4.1-16

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{a}_{i,n}^{s}={x}_{2}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]\cdot {\epsilon}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}+\left[\frac{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}-1\right]\cdot \underset{k\le n}{\max}({\epsilon}_{i,k}^{\text{fs}})\\ {b}_{i,n}^{s}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]+\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta h}}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}}\cdot \left[\frac{-{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}+1\right]\\ {c}_{i,n}^{s}=\frac{{h}_{n}}{{k}_{r}^{s}}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot \left[\frac{{e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}\right]+\frac{{h}_{n}}{{k}_{i}^{s}}\cdot \left[\frac{-{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}\cdot {e}^{{\lambda}_{2}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+{e}^{{\lambda}_{1}{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}}{{x}_{1}\cdot {x}_{2}-1}+1\right]\end{array}$                 eq 4.1-17

In equations [eq 4.1-16] and [eq 4.1-17] the parameters ${\lambda}_{1}$ , ${\lambda}_{2}$ , ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ are functions of the intrinsic parameters of the material. At each analysis step, it is necessary to save **two internal variables** ${\epsilon}_{r,n}^{\text{fs}}$ , the last reversible spherical strain obtained, and $\underset{k\le n}{\max}({\epsilon}_{i,k}^{\text{fs}})$ , that is ${\epsilon}_{i,n}^{\text{fs}}$ , the largest reversible spherical strain obtained in the history of the element. The choice of retaining expressions [eq 4.1-5] and [eq 4.1-6] (no irreversible strain), or expressions [eq4.1‑16] and [eq 4.1-17] (existence of irreversible strains) in order to determine the increment of total spherical strain is made *a posteriori* as a function of the sign of ${\text{Δε}}_{i,n}^{\text{fs}}$ in [eq 4.1-15].

### 4.2 Discretisation of the constitutive equations of deviatoric creep

After discretisation of the stresses and of the relative humidity by affine functions, the deviator tensor of the basic-creep strains is discretised by the following equation:

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{n}^{\text{fd}}={\underline{\underline{a}}}_{n}^{d}+{b}_{n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}^{d}+{c}_{n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{d}$                                             eq 4.2-1

where ${\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}^{d}$ and ${\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{d}$ are the deviatoric stress tensors at the beginning and at the end of the current time step.

The steps carried out are:

- One computes the parameters with respect to the *reversible* deviatoric basic-creep strain, whose model is:

${\eta}_{r}^{d}{\underline{\underline{\dot{\epsilon}}}}_{r}^{\text{fd}}(t)+{k}_{r}^{d}{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{r}^{\text{fd}}(t)=h(t){\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}^{d}(t)$                                         eq 4.2-2

After discretisation, the previous equation may be put in the form:

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{r,n}^{\text{fd}}={\underline{\underline{a}}}_{r,n}^{d}+{b}_{r,n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}^{d}+{c}_{r,n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{d}$                                             eq 4.2-3

With:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{a}}}_{r,n}^{d}=\left[\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{d}})-1\right]\cdot {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{r,n}^{f,d}\\ {b}_{r,n}^{d}=\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{d}}\left\lbrace \left[-(\frac{{\mathrm{2\tau }}_{r}^{d}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}+1){h}_{n}+\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{d}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n+1}\right]\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{d}})+\left[(\frac{{\mathrm{2\tau }}_{r}^{d}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}-1){h}_{n}-\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{d}-{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n+1}\right]\right\rbrace \\ {c}_{r,n}^{d}=\frac{1}{{k}_{r}^{d}}\left[\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{d}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}\exp(-\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\tau}_{r}^{d}}){h}_{n}-\frac{{\tau}_{r}^{d}-{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}}{h}_{n}\right]\end{array}$     eq 4.2-4

**Note:**

Equation [eq 4.2-4] (reversible part of the deviatoric creep) is analogous to equation [eq4.1-5] (reversible part of the creep in the absence of irreversible strains). They correspond to the discretisation of a single Kelvin chain.

One computes the parameters with respect to the deviatoric basic-creep strain, whose model is:

${\eta}_{i}^{d}{\underline{\underline{\dot{\epsilon}}}}_{i}^{f,d}(t)=h(t){\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}^{d}(t)$                                                     eq 4.2-5

After discretisation, the previous equation may be put in the form:

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{i,n+1}^{f,d}={\underline{\underline{a}}}_{i,n}^{d}+{b}_{i,n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}^{d}+{c}_{i,n}^{d}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{d}$                                         eq 4.2-6

With:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{a}}}_{i,n}^{d}=0\\ {b}_{r,n}^{d}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n+1}}{{\mathrm{2\eta }}_{i}^{d}}\\ {c}_{r,n}^{d}=\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta t}}_{n}\cdot {h}_{n}}{{\mathrm{2\eta }}_{i}^{d}}\end{array}$                                                         eq 4.2-7

### 4.3 Discretisation of the equations of desiccation creep

The terms linked to the taking into account of desiccation creep decompose according to the same concept as the reversible basic-creep terms [bib3]:

## 5 Tangent matrix

Introducing the elastic shear modulus $\mu$ , the stress deviator at time instant $n+1$ is written as a function of the deviator of the elastic strains:

${\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}^{d}=\mathrm{2\mu }{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{n+1}^{\text{ed}}={\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}^{d}+\mathrm{2\mu \Delta }{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{n}^{d}-\mathrm{2\mu \Delta }{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}_{n}^{f,d}$                                     eq 5-1

## 7 Coupling between BETON_UMLV and MAZARS

The model BETON_UMLV is a linear viscoelastic constitutive model. In order to be able to represent the failure of concrete by tertiary creep, one proposes in Code_Aster to couple the creep model to a damage model, namely the MAZARS model (cf. [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08)). The coupling is carried out by assuming, on the one hand, that the creep strains are generated by the effective stresses (that is, those really seen by the material) and, on the other hand, that only a part of the creep strain (assumed constant) contributes to the evolution of the damage. The scheme is carried out while maintaining the direct link between the state of elastic strains and the state of stresses.

### 7.1 Implementation

#### 7.1.1 Update of the internal variables of creep

As for the rest of the document, one restricts oneself here to the description of the coupling between damage and basic creep. One therefore assumes, as previously, that the strain is written according to equation [eq 2-2]:

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}^{e}+{\varepsilon}^{f}$

where ${\epsilon}^{e}$ , the elastic strain, also contains the contribution of the damage (micro-cracking) generated by the MAZARS model. Considering the spherical [eq 4.1-2] and deviatoric [eq 4.2-1] contributions of basic creep as well as that of desiccation creep, one obtains the increment of the creep strains $\Delta {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{f}$ between the time instants *n* and *n+1:*

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}_{n}^{f}={\underline{\underline{a}}}_{n}+{b}_{n}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n}+{c}_{n}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{n+1}$                                             eq 6.1-1

with ${\underline{\underline{a}}}_{n},{b}_{n}{c}_{n}$ the coefficients of the total basic creep in linear viscoelasticity.

In order to introduce damage into the model, one assumes that the creep strains are generated by the effective stresses, denoted $\underline{\underline{\sigma}}'$ in the remainder of the document. This makes it possible to associate the delayed strains with the sound part of the material, as proposed in [bib1].

One recalls that the effective stresses may be written as a function of the damage variable $D$ or (see also the documentation[R7.01.08-B]) as an elasticity law:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}'=\frac{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}{1-D}=\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{E}}}}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}^{e}$                                                         eq 6.1-2

Consequently, equation [eq 6.1-1] becomes:

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}_{n}^{f}={\underline{\underline{a}}}_{n}+{b}_{n}\underline{\underline{\sigma}}{'}_{n}+{c}_{n}\underline{\underline{\sigma}}{'}_{n+1}$                                                 eq 6.1-3

By using the elasticity law [eq 6.1-2] with equation [eq 6.1-3], one obtains the new relation for the creep-strain increment:

$\Delta {\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}_{n}^{f}={(1+c\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{E}}}})}^{\text{-1}}\left[{\underline{\underline{a}}}_{n}+{b}_{n}\underline{\underline{\sigma}}{'}_{n}+{c}_{n}\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{E}}}}({\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}_{n+1}-{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}_{n}^{f})\right]$                             eq 6.1-4

Thus, from all the quantities known at time instant $n$ , it is possible to compute with relation [eq 6.1-4] the creep strain at time instant $n+1$ . Then, one easily obtains the effective stresses at time instant $n+1$ thanks to relation [eq 6.1-2], and the internal variables of creep with equations [eq 4.1-2] and [eq 4.2-1].

#### 7.1.2 Evolution of the damage

One does not detail the MAZARS model here; the reader may refer to the *Code_Aster* reference documentation of the MAZARS model [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08).

The basic assumption is that the damage is driven by the elastic strain and a share of the creep strain. The strain tensor which drives the evolution of *D* is given by the following relation:

$\stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}={\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}^{e}+\chi {\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}^{f}$                                                                         eq 6.2-1

*with* $0\le \chi \le 1$ *, coupling coefficient.*

The level of coupling increases with increasing $\chi$ . There therefore exist two limiting cases: $\chi =0$ and $\chi =1$ .

- If , $\chi =0$ there is an absence of coupling; the evolution of the damage depends only on the elastic strain and therefore one cannot have tertiary creep.
- If $\chi =1$ the coupling is maximal; the damage depends on the total strain. This case generally leads to the premature ruin of the structure.

Note that equation [eq 6.2-1] is equivalent to averaging the total and elastic strains, using the coefficient $\chi$ as a weight:

$\stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}=\chi \underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}+(1-\chi ){\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}^{e}$                                                                 eq 6.2-2a

or to subtracting $1-\chi$ times the creep strains from the total strains:

$\stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}=\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}-(1-\chi ){\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}^{f}$                                                                 eq 6.2-2b

The equivalent strain ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}$ which drives the damage in the coupled law is no longer evaluated from the elastic strains but from the coupling strains $\stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}$ :

${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{{\langle \stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}\rangle }_{+}:{\langle \stackrel{˘}{\underline{\underline{\varepsilon}}}\rangle }_{+}}$                                                                     eq 6.2-3

${\langle \rangle }_{+}$ corresponding to the positive part of the tensor.

The damage in tension ${D}_{t}$ and in compression ${D}_{c}$ are computed as in the uncoupled case but with the equivalent strain defined in equation [eq 6.2-3]:

${D}_{c}=1-\frac{{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{d0}}(1-{A}_{c})}{{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}}-\frac{{A}_{c}}{\exp({B}_{c}({\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{d0}}))}$                                             eq 6.2-4a

${D}_{t}=1-\frac{{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{d0}}(1-{A}_{t})}{{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}}-\frac{{A}_{t}}{\exp({B}_{t}({\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{d0}}))}$                                             eq 6.2-4b

*(* ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{d0}},{A}_{c},{B}_{c},{A}_{t},{B}_{t}$ *) being the material parameters of the Mazars law.*
