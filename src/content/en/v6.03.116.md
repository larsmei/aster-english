# v6.03.116 SSNP116 - Fluid/crack coupling - Uniaxial Traction

**Summary:**

This validation case is intended to verify the model of coupling of the Granger creep laws with the plasticity/cracking laws. The coupling, initially restricted to some laws of the nonlinear environment of *Code_Aster* , can be extended subsequently to more laws. The parameters of the plasticity/cracking models are chosen in a particular way to model an almost perfect elasto-plastic behavior, and to reduce to a problem with a relatively simple analytical solution.

The geometry consists of three linear elements ($\mathrm{3D}$ cubics and prisms, squares and $\mathrm{2D}$ triangles), and three quadratic elements, linked to the previous ones by linear relationships. The modellings tested here are the modellings 3D, C_PLAN, and D_PLAN.

The load is a uniaxial traction while moving imposed.

The Granger creep model is tested for BETON_DOUBLE_DP and VMIS_ISOT_LINE. analytical solution in 3D and C_PLAN is available when there is no variation in water content.

In 3D and D_PLAN cases, solutions obtained when there is variation in water content and temperature with activation of corresponding withdrawals (with opposite sign) are also tested. These are non-regression tests.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

To calculate a simple analytical solution, the following choices were made, the aim being to validate the coupling and not the plasticity/cracking or creeping laws:

- a Granger creep law with a single Kelvin model in series,
- a law of plasticity / cracking Modelling a perfect elastoplastic law,
- a single-axial traction charge.

The reference solution is calculated analytically, knowing that in traction only the tensile criterion is activated. The model equations are reduced to scalar equations allowing the calculation of the analytical solution. The only difficulty is the determination of the beginning of plasticity (speech and creep deformation) which requires a numerical method to solve a nonlinear equation to an unknown.

In the case where the moisture content is not constant, the creep is more complex to solve, the analytical solution has not been calculated, so it is non-regression tests. However, in the 3D and D_PLAN cases, it can be verified that the same results are obtained with both models.

The imposed deformation (moving one end of the structure) is a linear function of time allowing to play creep and plasticity.

### 2.2 Calculation of the reference solution

$\varepsilon$ , the $xx$ component of the total ${\varepsilon}_{e}$ deformation , the $xx$ component of the elastic deformation, ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{fl}}$ the $xx$ component of the Granger creep deformation, and ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{pl}}$ the $xx$ component of the plastic deformation, $\sigma$ the $xx$ component of the stress, and $E$ the Young's modulus.

The chosen creep model consists of only one Kelvin model in series and the plasticity/cracking model is a virtually perfect elastoplastic law (nearly zero hardening slope), which allows the analytical solution of the creep/plasticity coupling to be easily calculated, in the case of single-axial single-axile traction. The almost perfect elastoplastic law can be obtained from the laws of *Code_Aster* BETON_DOUBLE_DP or VMIS_ISOT_LINE , choosing the appropriate set of parameters (nearly zero hardening). The load is a uniaxial tensile while moving imposed. Therefore, a total deformation proportional to the elapsed time, of the ${\varepsilon}_{xx}={\lambda}_{0}.t$ form, is imposed. Since there is no stress exerted in the other directions, the stress field is uniaxial. Therefore, one can reduce to a $\mathrm{1D}$ problem for resolution, which allows to calculate deformations in the transverse directions to the load ($yy$ and $zz$ ).

$\sigma =({\sigma}_{xx},0,0,0,0,0)$ et $\epsilon =({\varepsilon}_{xx},{\varepsilon}_{yy},{\varepsilon}_{zz},0,0,0)$

The creep model and plasticity model equations are confused with the following scalar equations, omitting the $xx$ index corresponding to the first tensor component:

$\varepsilon ={\lambda}_{0}.t$ (imposed withdrawal)

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}$

$\sigma =\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ with $\mu =\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{{J}_{s}}$ and $K=\frac{1}{{J}_{s}}$

$\sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{e}$ $\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]$

**Resolution in linear elasticity**

Before reaching the plasticity threshold, plastic deformation is zero, leading to:

$\varepsilon ={\lambda}_{0}.t$ (imposed withdrawal)

${\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}=0$

$\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

$\sigma =\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ with $\mu =\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{{J}_{s}}$ and $K=\frac{1}{{J}_{s}}$

$\sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{e}$ $\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]$

The differential equation for calculating the creep deformation is obtained:

$\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ with $\varepsilon ={\lambda}_{0}.t$

The creep deformation is thus expressed as the sum of a linear function of time and an exponential function of the type:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)=a.t+b+\alpha {e}^{-\beta .t}$

which gives in the differential equation:

$0=\mu .{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+K.{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)+E.{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)-E.{\lambda}_{0}.t$

Either:

$0=\left[(K+E)b+\mu .a\right]+\left[(K+E)a-E.{\lambda}_{0}\right]t+\left[(K+E)\alpha -\mu .\beta .\alpha \right]{e}^{-\beta .t}$

d’où:

$a=\frac{E.{\lambda}_{0}}{K+E}$ $b\text{=-}\frac{\mu}{K+E}\frac{E.{\lambda}_{0}}{K+E}$ $\beta =\frac{K+E}{\mu}$

At the initial moment, a zero creep deformation is initiated, leading to:

$\alpha =\frac{m}{K+E}\frac{E.{\lambda}_{0}}{K+E}$

Finally, the expression of creep deformation is obtained by time:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}^{xx}(t)={\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)=\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.E}{K+E}\left[t-\frac{\mu}{K+E}(1-{e}^{-\frac{K+E}{\mu}t})\right]$

The $xx$ component of the elastic deformation is: ${\varepsilon}_{e}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ . Either:

${\varepsilon}_{e}^{xx}(t)={\varepsilon}_{e}(t)=\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.K}{K+E}t+\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.E.\mu }{{(K+E)}^{2}}(1-{e}^{-\frac{K+E}{\mu}t})$

$yy$ and $zz$ components of elastic and creep deformations are obtained by multiplication of the $xx$ component by the Poisson's ratio.

The $xx$ component of the constraint is: $\sigma =E.{\varepsilon}_{e}=E(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}})$ . Either:

${\sigma}_{xx}(t)=\sigma (t)=\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.K.E}{K+E}t+\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.{E}^{2}.\mu }{{(K+E)}^{2}}(1-{e}^{-\frac{K+E}{\mu}t})$

**Elasticity threshold**

The behaviour remains elastic until the limited elasticity is reached. In the case of uniaxial traction, the equivalent stress is equal to the non-zero component of the stress. Plasticity therefore occurs when ${\sigma}_{xx}(t)={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={f}_{t}$ (tension resistance), i.e.:

$\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.K.E}{K+E}t+\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.{E}^{2}.\mu }{{(K+E)}^{2}}(1-{e}^{-\frac{K+E}{\mu}t})={f}_{t}$

This equation, solved by a numerical method, allows to obtain the moment of the start of ${t}_{\text{plas}}$ plasticization and the creep deformation at this moment:

${\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}={\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}({t}_{\text{plas}})=\frac{{\lambda}_{0}.E}{K+E}\left[{t}_{\text{plas}}-\frac{\mu}{K+E}(1-{e}^{-\frac{K+E}{\mu}{t}_{\text{plas}}})\right]$

**Plasticity resolution**

The plasticity model was chosen to obtain a simple analytical resolution. It is a quasi-perfect plasticity law, obtained by taking a particular set of parameters for the behavior model leading to a nearly zero-nut-setting slope. So, in the plastic phase, the stress (component $xx$), equal to the equivalent stress is the tensile strength. The equations of the model are then:

$\varepsilon ={\lambda}_{0}.t$ (imposed draw) and $\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{e}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}+{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}$

$\sigma =m{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{e}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}\right]$ with $\mu =\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{{J}_{s}}$ and $K=\frac{1}{{J}_{s}}$

$\sigma =E{\varepsilon}_{e}$ $\sigma =E\left[\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}\right]={f}_{t}$

with as initial conditions:

$t={t}_{\text{plas}}$ ${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}({t}_{\text{plas}})={\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}$

which leads to the differential equation for calculating the creep deformation:

$\sigma ={f}_{t}=\mu {\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}+K{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$

The creep deformation is thus expressed in the form:

${\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)=a+\alpha {e}^{-\beta .t}$

which gives in the differential equation:

$0=\mu .{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{\text{fl}}(t)+K.{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}(t)-{f}_{t}=\left[K.a-{f}_{t}\right]+\left[K.\alpha -\mu .\beta .\alpha \right]{e}^{-\beta .t}$ d’où:

$a=\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}$ $\beta =\frac{K}{\mu}$

At the moment ${t}_{\text{plas}}$, the creep deformation is ${\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}$, which leads to:

$\alpha =({\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}-\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}){e}^{\beta .t}$

Finally, the expression of creep deformation is obtained by time:

${\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{xx}}(t)=\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}+({\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}-\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}){e}^{-\frac{K}{\mu}(t-{t}_{\text{plas}})}$ with $\varepsilon ={\lambda}_{0}.t$

The $xx$ component of the elastic deformation is: ${\varepsilon}_{e}=\frac{\sigma}{E}=\frac{{f}_{t}}{E}$

The $xx$ component of the plastic deformation is: ${\varepsilon}_{\text{pl}}=\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{e}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}={\lambda}_{0}.t-{\varepsilon}_{e}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{fl}}$ . Either:

${\varepsilon}_{{\text{plas}}^{xx}}(t)={\lambda}_{0}.t-\frac{{f}_{t}}{E}-\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}-({\varepsilon}_{{\text{fl}}^{\text{plas}}}-\frac{{f}_{t}}{K}){e}^{-\frac{K}{m}(t-{t}_{\text{plas}})}$

$yy$ and $zz$ components of elastic and creep deformations are obtained by multiplication of the $xx$ component by the Poisson's ratio.

The $xx$ component of the constraint is: $\sigma ={f}_{t}$

## 5 Summary of the results

In the case where analytical solution (non-variation of drying) is known, this test case offers very satisfactory results with a difference below $\text{0.02\%}$ for all calculation cases. The number of iterations for the plastic phase is usually around ten; this is explained by the choice of the almost perfect plasticity law, obtained with the VMIS_ISOT_LINE model, with particular sets of parameters. In fact, the same model used **without** the creep/crack coupling, under the same loading conditions and with the same parameters, presents the same convergence difficulties.

Finally, in 3D and C_PLAN, it is verified that the 2 models that have been degenerated give almost similar results. However, in D_PLAN, the BETON_DOUBLE_DP model is not equivalent to the VMIS_ISOT_LINE model because of the writing of the criterion which depends on the trace of the strain tensor and is therefore not equivalent to the perfectly plastic model.
