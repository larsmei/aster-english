# v2.04.126 SDLV126 – Viscoelastic damping Modelling with RIGI_MECA_HYST

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the extension of RIGI_MECA_HYST option of the CALC_MATR_ELEM method for linear viscoelastic materials in harmonic regime. In this test, it is checked that the RIGI_MECA option is not impacted, that the degraded case of hysterical damping is found and a cross validation with the numerical results of Lucie Rouleau's thesis was performed.

The modellings H, I and J validate the use of non-homogeneous kinematic loads (c-a-d from AFFE_CHAR_CINE) with DYNA_VIBRA for harmonic calculations on a physical basis.

## 1 A-D modelling

### 1.1 Purposes

The objective of these modellings is to check on a element the behavior of the RIGI_MECA and RIGI_MECA_HYST options by testing the real and imaginary parts of the produced stiffness matrix.

In isotropic elasticity, the constitutive law sss: $\sigma =2G(ϵ-\frac{1}{3}\mathit{Tr}(ϵ))-K\mathit{Tr}(ϵ)\mathit{Id}$

If the material has complex modules, the real/imaginary part separation simply writes:

${\sigma}_{r}=2\mathrm{\Re }(G)(ϵ-\frac{1}{3}\mathit{Tr}(ϵ))-\mathrm{\Re }(K)\mathit{Tr}(ϵ)\mathit{Id},{\sigma}_{i}=2\mathrm{\Im }(G)(ϵ-\frac{1}{3}\mathit{Tr}(ϵ))-\mathrm{\Im }(K)\mathit{Tr}(ϵ)\mathit{Id}$

### 1.2 Geometry

For rigidity matrix construction test, geometry corresponds to a cube for the elements 3D and a square plane for the elements 2D.

### 1.3 Material properties

Three fictitious isotope materials are declared:

- elastic, material 1: ${G}_{1}=15\mathit{Pa},{K}_{1}=20\mathit{Pa}$
- elastic, material 2: ${G}_{2}=10\mathit{Pa},{K}_{2}=25\mathit{Pa}$
- viscoelastic material v: $G=15+10j\mathit{Pa},K=10+25j\mathit{Pa}$

Thus the matrix calculated on material 1 shall be the actual part of the matrix calculated on material v and the matrix calculated on material 2 shall be the imaginary part of the matrix calculated on material v.

### 1.5 Quantities tested

For each modellings, the following stiffening matrices are constructed:

- ${K}_{i}$ , the actual part of the rigidity matrix on the material i or i=1,2 (built with RIGI_MECA)
- ${K}_{i}\text{*}$ the rigidity matrix on the material i or i=1,2 (built with RIGI_MECA_HYST)
- ${K}_{v}$ , the complex rigidity matrix on the material v (built with RIGI_MECA_HYST)

Then we check: ${K}_{v}={K}_{1}+\mathit{jK}2$ and ${K}_{1}\text{*}={K}_{1}$

## 2 modellings E et F

### 2.1 Purposes

The objective of these modellings is to verify on an element that the Modelling of a viscoelastic material with the Poisson's ratio is constant/real and the complex Young's modulus is equivalent to the Modelling of an elastic material with a hysteretic damping coefficient.

In isotropic elasticity, the constitutive law writes:

$\sigma =\frac{E}{1+\nu }(ϵ+\frac{\nu}{1-2\nu }\mathit{Tr}(ϵ)\mathit{Id})$

So if $E={E}_{r}+j{E}_{i}$ then

$\sigma =(1+j\frac{{E}_{i}}{{E}_{r}})\frac{{E}_{r}}{1+\nu }(ϵ+\frac{\nu}{1-2\nu }\mathit{Tr}(ϵ)\mathit{Id})$

This corresponds to Young's modulus ${E}_{r}$ Poisson $\nu$ isotropic elastic material and hysteretic damping $\eta =\frac{{E}_{i}}{{E}_{r}}$

### 2.2 Geometry

For rigidity matrix construction test, geometry corresponds to a cube for the elements 3D and a square plane for the elements 2D.

### 2.3 Material properties

The two declared materials are:

- viscoelastic material 1: $G=\mathrm{0,53e9}+\mathrm{9,3e6}j\mathit{Pa},\nu =0,3$
- elastic, material 2: $E=\mathrm{1,3e9}\mathit{Pa},\nu =\mathrm{0,3,}\eta =\mathrm{1,8e-2}$

### 2.4 Features of modellings

| Modelling | element | Mesh |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E | 3D | 1 HEXA8 |
| F | C_PLAN | 1 QUAD4 |

### 2.5 Quantities tested

For each modellings, complex stiffener matrices are constructed:

- ${K}_{1}\text{*}$ of material 1
- ${K}_{2}\text{*}$ material 2

then check ${K}_{1}\text{*}={K}_{2}\text{*}$

## 3 Modelling G

### 3.1 Description of Reference problem

#### 3.1.1 Geometry

A slender structure is considered a square section sandwich made up of two skins and a heart. This is the same case of calculation as that presented by Lucie Rouleau in chapter 4 of his thesis [bib1].

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.126/10000201000002C30000024B43968A4756A7B573.png)

x=1m

#### 3.1.2 Material properties

The skins are composed of a material which is elastic isotropic, the properties of which are:

- Young's modulus $E=2.1{10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.3$
- Density $\rho =7800\mathit{kg}/m\mathrm{³}$

The core is composed of a linear viscoelastic material whose shear coefficient is modelled by a fractional Zener model, i.e.: $G(\omega )=\frac{{G}_{0}+{G}_{\infty}{(j\omega )}^{\alpha}}{1+{(j\omega )}^{\alpha}}$ , the compressibility coefficient is when it is taken constant. The numerical values are:

- ${G}_{0}=\mathrm{1,4e6}\mathit{Pa}$
- ${G}_{\infty}=\mathrm{0,54e9}\mathit{Pa}$
- $\tau =\mathrm{0,52e-6}s$
- $\alpha =0,59$
- Density $\rho =1460\mathit{kg}/m\mathrm{³}$
- Compressibility coefficient $K=\mathrm{2,2e9}\mathit{Pa}$

#### 3.1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

This structure is excited punctually by a unitary harmonic effort at point $(1m,0,5m,0,5m)$, we will be interested in moving according to $z$ in this same point. The structure is clamped in x=0.

## 4 Modelling H

This Modelling is the same as G Modelling.But it uses dualised boundary conditions (d-AFFE_CHAR_MECA) and not kinematic.

In addition, a non-nul blocking point Zau displacement is required to treat the case of non-homogeneous boundary conditions.

This test is used as a reference to modellings I and J. It does not really have physical significance.

## 5 Modelling I

This Modelling is the same as H Modelling with kinematic boundary conditions.

It validates the use of boundary conditions kinematic (d-AFFE_CHAR_CINE) and non-homogeneous by comparison with the same calculation with dual and non-homogeneous boundary conditions. It also validates the keyword COEF_MULT with a kinematic charge.

This test has no real physical significance.

## 6 Modelling J

This Modelling is the same as the Javez Modelling combination of a frequency dependent kinematic charge and a frequency-dependent multiplicative function so as to fall back on the same load as the H and I models.

It validates the use of boundary conditions kinematics depending on frequency (d-extract d-AFFE_CHAR_CINE_F) and non-homogeneous as well as the correct consideration of keyword FONC_MULT throughout the calculation.

This test has no real physical significance.

## 7 Summary of the results

This test case has been used to verify the methods of calculating complex stiffness matrix on linear viscoelastic material. It performs test case matrix construction and cross validation with third-party code.
