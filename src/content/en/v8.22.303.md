# v8.22.303 AHLV303 - linear elasticity hollow sphere immersed in infinite fluid

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the IFS formulations with consideration of the Sommerfeld condition. This is an test case solution which is (semi) analytical

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A hollow sphere of ${R}_{\mathit{ext}}=1,4m$ external radius and ${R}_{int}=1,0m$ internal radius immersed in water is considered to be infinite. But for the modelling of the problem, the fluid domain is truncated into a hollow sphere of ${R}_{\mathit{bgt}}=3,0m$ external radius and ${R}_{\mathit{ext}}=1,4m$ internal radius.

### 1.2 Material properties

The structure is elastic isotropic with properties: $E=210000\mathit{MPa}$ , $\nu =0.3$ and ${\rho}_{s}=7850\mathit{kg}.{m}^{-3}$

The fluid is water which has ${\rho}_{f}=1000\mathit{kg}.{m}^{-3}$ density. The speed of sound in this fluid is ${c}_{f}=1500m.{s}^{-1}$.

The BGT condition is imposed (IMPE_FACE) with the following acoustic impedance: ${Z}_{c}=3\times {\rho}_{f}+{\rho}_{f}{c}_{f}i$ . This quantity is, of course, complex.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The stress considered in the problem is a strictly radial ${p}_{\mathit{imp}}=1,5\mathit{MPa}$ distributed pressure imposed on the ${\Gamma}_{int}$ surface.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

We start from a zero state (contraints, speeds, displacements and accelerations)

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The problem is spherical symmetry. So we place ourselves in the $(r,\theta ,\varphi )$ marker. We consider the case where ${p}_{\mathit{imp}}$ does not depend on $\theta$, nor $\varphi$. This hypothesis allows us to express the analytical solution for the problem in harmonic and semi-analytical regime for the problem in transient by exploiting the symmetry of the problem. Indeed, by symmetry in the problem, the structure displacement s.

$$
{u}_{s}(r,\theta ,\varphi ,t)=u(r,t){e}_{r}
$$

And the sound pressure in the fluid:

$$
{p}_{f}(r,\theta ,\varphi ,t)=p(r,t)
$$

The deformation tensor of Green-Lagrange linearized is therefore given by:

$$
\epsilon (r,t)=\frac{\partial u(r,t)}{\partial r}(r,t){e}_{r}\otimes {e}_{r}+\frac{u(r,t)}{r}\left({e}_{\theta}\otimes {e}_{\theta}+{e}_{\varphi}\otimes {e}_{\varphi}\right)
$$

With an isotropic elastic material, the Cauchy stress is then:

$$
\sigma (r,t)=\left((\lambda +2\mu )\frac{\partial u(r,t)}{\partial r}+2\lambda \frac{u(r,t)}{r}\right){e}_{r}\otimes {e}_{r}+\left(\lambda \frac{\partial u(r,t)}{\partial r}+(\lambda +2\mu )\frac{u(r,t)}{r}\right)\left({e}_{\theta}\otimes {e}_{\theta}+{e}_{\varphi}\otimes {e}_{\varphi}\right)
$$

$\lambda$ and $\mu$ being the coefficients of Lamé.

The expression of the preservation of the amount of movement allows to write:

$$
\frac{{r}^{2}}{{c}_{s}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}u(r,t)}{\partial {t}^{2}}-{r}^{2}\frac{{\partial}^{2}u(r,t)}{\partial {r}^{2}}-2r\frac{\partial u(r,t)}{\partial r}+2u(r,t)=0
$$

With the speed of propagation of sound in the ${c}_{s}$ structure which is:

$$
{c}_{s}^{2}=\frac{\lambda +2\mu }{{\rho}_{s}}
$$

$\lambda$ and $\mu$ being the coefficients of Lamé.

$$
\frac{1}{{c}_{f}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {t}^{2}}(t,x)-\Delta p(t,x)=0
$$

In spherical coordinates, the acoustic pressure propagation equation is written:

$$
\frac{1}{{c}_{f}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {t}^{2}}(r,t)-\frac{{\partial}^{2}p}{\partial {r}^{2}}(r,t)-\frac{2}{r}\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}(r,t)=0
$$

The condition of continuity of the normal stress on the surface ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{ext}}$ is written as a scalar equation:

$$
(\lambda + 2\mu) \frac{\partial u(t)}{\partial r}_{r=R_{\text{ext}}} + 2\lambda \frac{u(t)}{R_{\text{ext}}} = -p(t)_{r=R_{\text{ext}}}
$$

The condition of the normal acceleration continuity on the ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{ext}}$ surface is written:

$$
\frac{\partial^2 u(t)}{\partial t^2}_{R_{\text{ext}}} = -\frac{1}{\rho_f} \frac{\partial p(t)}{\partial r}_{R_{\text{ext}}}
$$

Pressure is imposed on the ${\Gamma}_{int}$ surface and is written:

$$
(\lambda + 2\mu) \frac{\partial u(t)}{\partial r}_{r=R_{\text{int}}} + 2\lambda \frac{u(t)}{R_{\text{ext}}} = -p_{\text{imp}}(t)
$$

Finally, the Sommerfeld condition (non-reflection of the waves) is applied to the outer surface ${\Gamma}_{\mathit{bgt}}$:

$$
\underset{r\to \infty }{\lim}\left(\frac{1}{{c}_{f}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial t}(r,t)+\frac{\partial p}{\partial r}(r,t)\right)=0
$$

### 2.2 Harmonic resolution

The ${p}_{\mathit{imp}}(t)={P}_{\mathit{imp}}{e}^{i\omega t}$ form is used here and the solution of the $u(r,t)=U(r){e}^{i\omega t}$ and $p(r,t)=P(r){e}^{i\omega t}$ form is sought. In this form, the partial derivatives in time are trivial. For example:

$$
u(r,t)=U(r){e}^{i\omega t}\to \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}=i\omega U(r){e}^{i\omega t}\to \frac{{\partial}^{2}u}{\partial {t}^{2}}=-i{\omega}^{2}U(r){e}^{i\omega t}
$$

The problem is finding $U$ and $P$ that are solutions to the following problem:

$$
{r}^{2}\frac{{d}^{2}U}{{\mathit{dr}}^{2}}+2r\frac{dU}{\mathit{dr}}+\left(\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{c}_{s}^{2}}{r}^{2}-2\right)U=0
$$

Et:

$$
\frac{{d}^{2}P}{{\mathit{dr}}^{2}}+\frac{2}{r}\frac{\mathit{dP}}{\mathit{dr}}+\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{c}_{f}^{2}}P=0
$$

The solution to the problem () is written:

$$
U(r)=A(\omega ){j}_{1}\left(\frac{\omega}{{c}_{s}}r\right)+B(\omega ){y}_{1}\left(\frac{\omega}{{c}_{s}}r\right)
$$

Where ${j}_{1}$ and ${y}_{1}$ are, respectively, the spherical Bessel function of order 1 of the first type and the spherical Bessel function of order 1 of the second type.

The solution to the problem () is written:

$$
P(r)=C(\omega )\frac{{e}^{-i\omega r/{c}_{f}}}{r}+D(\omega )\frac{{e}^{i\omega r/{c}_{f}}}{r}
$$

To find the values $A(\omega )$ , $B(\omega )$ , $C(\omega )$ and $D(\omega )$ . Limit conditions are used.

In equation (), the term $\frac{{e}^{-i\omega r/{c}_{f}}}{r}$ corresponds to the wave radially propagating towards infinity while the other term $\frac{{e}^{i\omega r/{c}_{f}}}{r}$ is the reflection of the wave (to the source). As one imposes a condition of non-reflection, on the outer edge, this second contribution is null and so one has $D(\omega )=0$ .
