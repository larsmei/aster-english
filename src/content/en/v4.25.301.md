# v4.25.301 TTLV301 - Parallelepiped under a temperature imposed on its faces

**Summary:**

This test is based on independent validation of version 3 in linear transient thermal analysis.

This is a volume problem represented by a single model (3D).

The functions tested are as follows:

- element thermal volumetric,
- transient thermal analysis algorithm,
- limit conditions: temperature imposed.

The results are compared to an analytical solution.

### 1.1 Geometry

### 1.2 Material properties

| --- | --- |
| ${c}_{p}=1.J/\mathrm{kg}°C$ | specific heat |
| $\rho =1.\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | density |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

### 1.4 Initial conditions

$T(t=0)=1°C={T}_{0}$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

${T}_{(x,y,z,t)}={T}_{w}+\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{l=1}^{\infty}{a}_{\mathrm{nml}}\exp(-{\kappa}_{\mathrm{mnl}}^{2}\alpha .t){\mathrm{Tcos}}_{(x,y,z,m,n,l)}$

with ${\mathrm{Tcos}}_{(x,y,z,m,n,l)}=\cos(\frac{(2m-1)\pi x}{2{L}_{1}})\cos(\frac{(2n-1)\pi y}{2{L}_{2}})\cos(\frac{(2l-1)\pi z}{2{L}_{3}})$

${a}_{\mathrm{mnl}}=\frac{64({T}_{0}-{T}_{w})}{{\pi}^{3}(2m-1)(2n-1)(2l-1)}\sin(\frac{(2m-1)\pi }{2})\sin(\frac{(2n-1)\pi }{2})\sin(\frac{(2l-1)\pi }{2})$

${\kappa}_{\mathrm{mnl}}={(\frac{(2m-1)\pi }{2{L}_{1}})}^{2}+{(\frac{(2n-1)\pi }{2{L}_{2}})}^{2}+{(\frac{(2l-1)\pi }{2{L}_{3}})}^{2}$

$\alpha =\frac{\lambda}{\rho {c}_{p}}$

Reference values are obtained with $m=n=l=100.$

### 2.2 Baseline

Point temperature: $O(0,0,0)$ and $H(0.5,0.8,1.)$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

- M.J Chang, L.C Chow, W.S Chang, "Improved alternating direction imperative for solving three dimensional heat diffusion problems", Numerical Heat Transfer, vol 19, pp. 69-84, 1991.

## 6 Summary of the results

The results obtained are satisfactory. the difference maximum obtained (0.816%), is located at point $H$ mid-distance between the outer surface and the centre of the parallelepiped. At the end of $\mathrm{1.2s}$, this difference decreases, the maximum obtained is then 0.059% (point $O$: center of the parallelepiped).

This test allowed testing in transient linear 3D Modelling with mesh cells HEXA27.
