# v2.01.031 SDLD31 - Basic validation of dynamic time schemas

**Summary:**

This test case validates the programming of the time-input schemas in DYNA_NON_LINE and DYNA_VIBRA.

More specifically, the following implicit diagrams are tested for DYNA_NON_LINE:

1. Average acceleration (keyword NEWMARK) with resolution in motion and acceleration;
2. modified average acceleration (keyword HHT with MODI_EQUI='NON');
3. HHT complete (keyword HHT with MODI_EQUI='OUI');

With the complete HHT schema, we also test the pursuits because this schema requires a particular initialization. Similarly, we also validate the pursuits for the average acceleration with acceleration resolution because it is not tested in other cases.

DYNA_VIBRA is tested for constant time step schemas, i.e.:

1. explicit order scheme 1 says EULER;
2. NEWMARK implicit order 2 schema;
3. explicit order 4 scheme says DEVOGELAERE

The aim is to study the behaviour of the patterns in time, so the problem chosen is deliberately very simple: it is a linear system 1 degree of freedom spring mass which is subjected to sinusoidal force.

The reference solution is obtained by reprogramming the integration diagrams in Matlab and by calculating the analytical solution.

### 1.2 Material properties

Stiffness spring $K$ : $36.{\pi}^{2}N/m$

Point mass $M$ : $1\mathit{kg}$

The values shall be selected so as to have a specific pulse of the ${\omega}_{0}$ mass-spring system such as:

${\omega}_{0}=6.\pi \mathit{rad}/s$ because ${{\omega}_{0}}^{2}=K/M$ .

### 1.3 Geometrical characteristics

The displacement is done in vertical direction $z$ .

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

The spring base is clamped, so the only degree of freedom is the following $z$ displacement of the point mass M which is fixed to the other end of the spring.

The load is a vertical $F(t)$ sinusoidal force imposed on the point mass M:

$F(t)=\sin(1,1.{\omega}_{0}.t)$ .

### 1.5 Initial conditions

The system is initially at rest.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

With this test case we want to study the behavior of the various implicit time integration schemes of the DYNA_NON_LINE operator. Therefore, it is not a question of trying to reproduce as closely as possible an analytical solution.

So we choose a time step $\mathit{dt}={10}^{-2}s$ , small enough compared to the system's own pulse and we will solve the linear transient problem with the DYNA_NON_LINE operator.

For the non-dissipative scheme of the average acceleration (keyword NEWMARK) it would be possible to calculate the analytical solution to compare it. The resolution in motion or acceleration is tested which must obviously give the same results.

The HHT scheme uses an ALPHA=-0.3 coefficient.

For other schemes that are wanted to be tested and dissipative, obtaining an analytical solution is not easy.

So we chose to compare all calculations with a numerical solution obtained with the Matlab code. To do this, the different schemas were programmed in Matlab.

The result is several transient calculations in one step: with the average acceleration diagram, the modified average acceleration diagram, the complete HHT diagram and the Krenk diagram. For this latter dissipative diagram (with the selected parameter values), the displacement resolution and speed are validated.

Then we test the calculation resume with the complete HHT schema, to validate the tracking mechanism with this schema (we do two pursuits, the first at $0,2s$ and the second at $0,35s$).

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Comparisons will be made on the displacement and acceleration of the $M$ point mass at the following moments: $0,5s$ , $0,7s$ and $1s$ .

## 4 modellings B et C - DYNA_VIBRA

In the tests on the DYNA_VIBRA diagrams a slight viscous damping of one per thousand is introduced. This validates the correct treatment of the damping. It is also an opportunity to validate the quadratic modal calculation.

Modelling B is done with elements DIS_T and Modelling C with elements 2D_DIS_T (the Z axis therefore corresponds to Y for this Modelling).

### 4.1 analytical solution

William Weaver Jr. Stephen P. Timoshenko and Donovan H. Young provide in Chapter 1.9 of "Vibration Problems in Engineering" the solution to the problem of a mass/spring system with viscous damping subject to harmonic excitation.

The equation to be solved is a second order equation in time on a single degree of freedom space:

$\ddot{x}+2\eta \dot{x}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}x=\frac{F}{m}\sin({\omega}_{e}t)$

where $x$ is the shift of the mass, $\dot{x}$ its speed and $\ddot{x}$ its acceleration.

${\omega}_{0}^{2}=\frac{k}{m}$ is the system's own pulse, $m$ being its mass and $k$ its stiffness.

$\eta$ is the reduced depreciation.

Finally $F$ is the amplitude of the excitation force while ${\omega}_{e}$ is its pulsation.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case allows the validation, in linear form, of the implicit time diagrams (average acceleration, modified average acceleration and complete HHT) of the DYNA_NON_LINE operator, in the case of a variable load, for resolutions on the move, speed or acceleration depending on the case.

In this context we also validate the continuation with the complete HHT schema.

The same model also validates the EULER, NEWMARK and DEVOGELAERE operator schemas.
