# v6.04.143 SSNV143 - Biaxial Traction with constitutive law BETON_DOUBLE_DP

**Summary:**

This validation case is intended to verify the $\mathrm{3D}$ BETON_DOUBLE_DP model of behaviour formulated in the thermoplasticity framework, for the description of the nonlinear behaviour of concrete, in traction, and in compression, with the consideration of irreversible variations in the thermal and mechanical characteristics of concrete, particularly sensitive at high temperature.

The description of cracking is treated in the plasticity framework, using energy equivalence, by identifying the density of cracking energy in $I$ mode, with the plastic work of an equivalent homogeneous medium, where plastic deformation is evenly distributed, in an "elementary" zone. This approach preserves the continuity of model formulation, over the whole of its behavior, and helps to avoid the possible numerical difficulties when changing the material's condition.

The pathological sensitivity of the digital solution to spatial discretization (mesh), generated by the introduction of a softening behaviour of concrete in traction and compression, is partially resolved by introducing a cracking or breaking energy, depending on a characteristic length ${l}_{c}$, related to the size of the elements.

The test case consists of two modellings 3D, the load consists of a load followed by a discharge.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is calculated semi-analytically, knowing that in traction only the tensile criterion is activated. Therefore, a system must be solved from an equation to an unknown one, which allows for the resulting plastic deformation by dichotomy, for example, accumulated in tensile tension. This can then be calculated by deformation and stress. This is possible, knowing the displacement, and therefore the deformation in both directions imposed. The displacement in the third direction is then an unknown problem.

The reference solution is calculated only in traction. The solution is determined by a dichotomy resolution program in independent fortran. In compression, in discharge, the exact solution has not been recalculated, and is a non-regression solution of the code, linked to version 5.02.14.

For model B, the results are derived by rotation of the stress tensor of model A from the intrinsic cub mark to the user mark, the stress field of both configurations being identical in the intrinsic cub mark.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case has satisfactory results compared to the reference results, lower than $\text{0.06\%}$ for the first two loading cases, more important for the third, which is due to a relatively low stress level (the end of the tensile nut-cut curve is reached).

The landfill test (fourth load) allows you to check the non-regression of the code.

The number of iterations is relatively large in the first step, in the order of 13, then decreases to 7, 4 and 1, which is explained by the change from the plastic threshold to the first step of calculation, to achieve a quasi-linear behavior thereafter (linear post-pic curves).

A larger number of iterations are also obtained at step 31 (early in the fourth loading case), and then a number of iterations decreasing to 1, due to the passage into landfill, with a change in behaviour, followed by a quasi-linear behavior thereafter (linear post-pic curves).
