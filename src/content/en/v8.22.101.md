# v8.22.101 AHLV101 - Anechoic output waveguide

**Summary:**

A straight wave guide with anechoic output, rigid walls, whose propagation medium is "normal" air, is excited by a harmonic incident wave, normal at the inlet face. The acoustic pressure field of the harmonic response is calculated using the elasto-acoustic formulation of displacement-potential displacement pressure.

The tests concern six different modellings (elements three-dimensional, two-dimensional, and axisymmetric elasto-acoustic finished, and axisymmetric), they validate the stiffness, mass, impedance, and source vector matrices for the six modellings. The first three are for FORMULATION='U_P_PHI', the following three for FORMULATION='U_P' and the last three for FORMULATION='U_PSI'.

The reference result is derived from an analytical calculation.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The frequencies of the excitation are quite low and the waveguide is long enough in relation to its lateral dimensions to limit itself to the flat waves: the phenomenon is then identical at all points of a waveplane, i.e. does not depend on the coordinates describing the points of this plane, $y$ and $z$ for example.

This hypothesis gives the well-known general solution of acoustic equations for both quantities **pressure** $p$ and **acoustic speed** $v$:

$$
v=f(t-\frac{x}{c})+g(t+\frac{x}{c})
$$

Et:

$$
p=\rho c\left[f(t-\frac{x}{c})-g(t+\frac{x}{c})\right]
$$

The guide is supposed to be closed at the $L$ abscisse end on an ${Z}_{L}$ impedance; there is reflection at the impedance level, which gives a $g$ return wave.

In each point of the guide, there is then superposition of the two $f$ and $g$ functions; by definition even the ${Z}_{L}$ terminal impedance imposes on the point of $L$ abscisse , between $p$ and $v$ the following relationship:

$$
\frac{{p}_{L}}{{v}_{L}}={Z}_{L}
$$

In the harmonic case $f$ and $g$ are written:

$$
\begin{array}{}f(t-\frac{x}{c})=I{e}^{i\omega (t-\frac{x}{c})}\\ g(t+\frac{x}{c})=R{e}^{i\omega (t+\frac{x}{c})}\end{array}
$$

where $I$ and $R$ are determined by the boundary conditions.

In the calculation of the $Z=\frac{p}{v}$ impedance in total point $x$ the time variable this time disappears, according to the very calculation of impedances and is written:

$$
Z(x)={Z}_{0}\frac{I{e}^{-i\omega \frac{x}{c}}-R{e}^{i\omega \frac{x}{c}}}{I{e}^{-i\omega \frac{x}{c}}+R{e}^{i\omega \frac{x}{c}}}
$$

Terminal impedance becomes:

$$
{Z}_{L}={Z}_{0}\frac{I{e}^{-i\omega \frac{L}{c}}-R{e}^{i\omega \frac{L}{c}}}{I{e}^{-i\omega \frac{L}{c}}+R{e}^{i\omega \frac{L}{c}}}
$$

${Z}_{0}=\rho c$ is called iterative impedance.

On the fluid border at the entrance of the guide the boundary condition of incident wave type imposed on ${P}_{i}={P}_{0}{e}^{i\omega t}$ , obtains by writing at the border the following linear relation:

$$
p-\rho c{v}_{n}={P}_{i}
$$

where ${v}_{n}=\mathrm{v.n}$ is the speed according to the $n$ **unit normal** fluid.

A final ${Z}_{L}={Z}_{0}$ impedance value is also required at the end of the guide, making it an anechoic end.

The terminal impedance is equal to the iterative impedance ${Z}_{0}$ when $R=0$ , that is when there is no return wave ; then one has a pure *progressive** wave in the direction of the incident wave, that is:

$$
\begin{array}{}v=I{e}^{i\omega (t-\frac{x}{c})}\\ p=\rho cI{e}^{i\omega (t-\frac{x}{c})}\end{array}
$$

Thus the imposed incident wave relation () is written:

$$
p-\rho c{v}_{n}=p(x=0)+\rho cv(x=0)=2\rho cI{e}^{i\omega t}
$$

From where $2\rho cI{e}^{i\omega t}={P}_{i}$ is identified, the expression of the *progressive** pressure wave in the guide is derived when ${P}_{i}$ is applied at the guide entrance:

$$
p=\frac{{P}_{i}}{2}{e}^{-i\omega \frac{x}{c}}=\frac{{P}_{0}}{2}{e}^{i\omega (t-\frac{x}{c})}
$$

## 12 Summary of the results

The discretization is strong, since it is about 45 nodes per wavelength. That's why we get results of high accuracy: the pressure calculated at the least favourable point differs from the theoretical value of less than 1%, for the three formulations: $(u,p)$ , $(u,p,\phi )$ and $(u,\psi )$ .

It should also be noted that all modellings used give identical results.
