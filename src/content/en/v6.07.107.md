# v6.07.107 COMP007 – Thermomechanical validation of nonlinear elastic laws (COMPORTEMENT)

**Summary**

This test validates the thermo-elastic constitutive laws defined in COMPORTEMENT. It allows you to check the following points:

- Thermal expansion is well calculated (with consideration of the variation of thermal expansion with temperature)
- The variation of the material coefficients with temperature is correct.

The 4 validated laws of behaviour are as follows:

- $A$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the ELAS model,
- $B$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the ELAS_VMIS_LINE model,
- $C$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the ELAS_VMIS_PUIS model,
- $D$ Modelling: This Modelling allows to validate the ELAS_VMIS_TRAC model.

## 1 Methodology

It is a double simulation, the first in thermomechanical, the second in pure mechanics. The first will be validated in comparison with the second, assuming of course that the behaviour tested provides a correct solution in pure mechanics.

The first simulation (solution that is being validated) is to apply a temperature variation to a material point, for example by blocking the following $x$ deformations: ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=0$ . The temperature imposed is increasing linearly with time.

The second simulation (which must be equivalent to the first) consists of applying to the same material point a deformation imposed following $x$: ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=-\alpha (T)(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})$ , in pure mechanics. Indeed, for any behavior (assuming additive decomposition of deformations):

${\sigma}_{xx}=E(T)({\varepsilon}_{xx}-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}-{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p})$

in the first case, ${\sigma}_{xx}=E(T)(0-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}-{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p})$ , and in the second: ${\sigma}_{xx}=E(T)(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p})$ .

Therefore, at every moment, you can apply ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=-\alpha (T)(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})$ for mechanical calculation.

In addition, to obtain the same results in both cases, it is necessary, at each time step of the second simulation, to perform the pure mechanical calculation with coefficients whose values are interpolated according to the temperature at the current moment. This interpolation is performed in the command file of the test, in a loop in outside time at STAT_NON_LINE.

## 2 Interpretation of results

The purpose is to verify with TEST_TABLE that the result obtained at each moment of the thermomechanical transient of the first simulation is identical to the result obtained with the second simulation.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Simulation 1

This is a thermomechanical test with a zero-imposed deformation on the $x$ axis. The test is performed on a material point with the SIMU_POINT_MAT control. The temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=20°C$ to ${T}_{\max}=500°C$. The material is elastic. The transient is made of NCAL not. The reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathrm{Ref}}=20°C$.

### 3.2 Simulation 2

The purpose is to perform a loop on NCAL mechanical calculations. Each $i$ calculation, the load imposed is made up of the ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=-{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{th}}=-\alpha (T)({T}_{i}-{T}_{\mathrm{Ref}})$ thermal deformation. The initial load consists of the deformations, stresses and internal variables of the previous mechanical calculation.

### 3.3 Constitutive law and material parameters

The constitutive law tested is 'ELAS' documented in the [R4.01.02](doc:r4.01.02) documentation.

The elastic parameters are as follows:

$E(T)$ , $\nu (T)$ et $\alpha (T)$

Values of parameters used:

| Parameter | $T=20°C$ | $T=500°C$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $E(T)$ | $200000.$ MPa | $100000.$ MPa |
| $\nu (T)$ | $0.$ | $0.$ |
| $\alpha (T)$ | $1.\times {10}^{-5}$ ${K}^{-1}$ | $2.\times {10}^{-5}$ ${K}^{-1}$ |

### 3.4 Tested quantities and results

Validation is done by comparing the fields calculated at each step of the transition on the one hand and the result of a mechanical calculation on the other.

The command used is TEST_TABLE which tests the reference value against the calculated value.

The reference value being the component of the field extracted at a given moment $i$ from the first thermomechanical simulation performed on NCAL moments. The calculated value is that obtained at the end of the mechanical calculation $i+1$ of the loop on the NCAL.

| Result at serial number i | Name of parameter tested | Reference type | Reference value | Tolerance |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RESU_ i | NOM_PARA |  | VALE_REF | TOLE |
| RESU_4 | VMIS | NON_REGRESSION |  | 0.1% |
| RESU_4 | TRACE | NON_REGRESSION | -960. | 0.1% |

## 7 General summary of results

For each of the constitutive laws studied, the results of the thermomechanical transient of the first simulation are compared with those obtained with the second simulation in pure mechanics. The results are consistent, showing the correct consideration of thermal expansion by these constitutive laws, as well as the good dependence of material parameters on temperature.
