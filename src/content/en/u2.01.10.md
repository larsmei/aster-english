# u2.01.10 User's guide on the choice of finite elements

> **Summary.**

This document aims to give some information on the choice of finite elements and of their associated modelling in the case of thermal, thermomechanical or non-linear mechanical studies. It amounts, in a sense, to proposing to the user an a priori choice, making it possible to avoid certain common errors. In the event of particular difficulties, other choices may be made.

## 1 Introduction

This document gives the a priori choices which may be made concerning the finite elements. The case considered is that of a thermomechanical chaining, but the advice remains valid for thermal or mechanical analyses which are not chained (linear or otherwise). A brief justification is given. For more details on the justification of these choices, the user may refer to the reference documents of *Code_Aster* as well as to the note [bib1].

## 2 A priori choices

### 2.1 The mesh

The elements may equally well be:

- triangular elements or quadrangles in 2D,
- tetrahedra or hexahedra in 3D.

Indeed, contrary to a widespread idea, elements of triangle or tetrahedron type give good results, even in plasticity, **provided of course that one does not use a mesh that is too coarse**. One may also use the HOMARD software, which carries out the adaptation of 2D/3D meshes for finite elements of triangular, quadrangular, tetrahedral or hexahedral type by refinement and unrefinement. One can thus obtain the optimum mesh as a function of an error indicator (cf. [R4.10.01](doc:r4.10.01), [R4.10.02](doc:r4.10.02), [R4.10.03](doc:r4.10.03), or the test case TPLL01j [V4.02.01](doc:v4.02.01) for a demonstration) by calling the command MACR_ADAP_MAIL in the *Code_Aster* command file.

On the other hand, it is recommended to use:

- linear elements in thermal analysis for chained analyses and for rapid transient thermal analyses. For the other cases, one may also opt for quadratic elements,
- quadratic elements in mechanics.

This choice is all the more important when one carries out chained thermal then mechanical analyses. One must then use two different meshes for the thermal analysis and for the mechanical analysis. Two strategies are then possible:

- either mesh the structure independently for the thermal analysis and for the mechanical analysis
- or build a mesh with linear elements and then transform it into a quadratic mesh by means of the command CREA_MAILLAGE, factor keyword LINE_QUAD.

Whatever method is chosen, one can optimise each mesh separately with *Homard* by means of the thermal and mechanical error indicators available in Aster (cf. test case forma05b [V6.03.120](doc:v6.03.120)).

> **Note.**

| It is recalled here that all quantities of stress or strain type are computed at the Gauss points, and that any transfer to the nodes introduces a bias. This is all the more true when one then seeks to compute norms; we have thus observed that the tetrahedra were more sensitive than the hexahedra to the method of computation of the equivalent stresses, for example. One must therefore look even more critically at the results computed at the nodes. |
| --- |

### 2.2 The modelling

Whether for the solution of thermal or mechanical problems, several modellings are available in *Code_Aster*. These different modellings may be distinguished by the number or the type of degrees of freedom, the number of integration points, particular treatments… Depending on the analysis carried out, some are of course better suited than others.

#### 2.2.1 In thermal analysis

To carry out a thermal analysis with *Code_Aster*, two types of modelling are available ([U3.23.01](doc:u3.23.01), [U3.24.01](doc:u3.24.01), [R3.06.02](doc:r3.06.02), [R3.06.07](doc:r3.06.07)):

- classical finite elements: modelling 3D, AXIS or PLAN
- lumped or diagonalised finite elements: modelling 3D_DIAG, AXIS_DIAG or PLAN_DIAG

We propose as the default choice:

**modelling** **XXXX_DIAG with linear elements**

**Justification**

In thermal analysis, the time step $\Delta t$ cannot be arbitrary; it must satisfy a condition $\Delta {t}_{\min}<\Delta t<\Delta {t}_{\max}$ , $\Delta {t}_{\min}$ and $\Delta {t}_{\max}$ depending on the material properties, the size of the finite elements and the time-integration parameters (cf. [R3.06.07](doc:r3.06.07)).

In the case of rapid transient thermal problems, one may be led to use a time step that is too small. One may then observe oscillations of the solution and non-physical temperatures due to the violation of the maximum principle (a temperature higher than the initial temperature of a part that one is cooling). The DIAG modelling, which consists in diagonalising the mass matrix, makes it possible to dispense with the condition on $\Delta {t}_{min}$ and to avoid the associated problems.

Note, however, that this diagonalisation is not sufficient to remove the oscillations in every configuration (cf. [R3.06.07](doc:r3.06.07)). It does not guarantee the absence of oscillation with quadratic elements, for example. This is why linear elements are recommended.

#### 2.2.2 In mechanics

Four types of modelling are available to solve non-linear mechanical problems using "classical" constitutive laws (of the elastoplasticity type):

- classical isoparametric finite elements: 3D, D_PLAN, C_PLAN, AXIS ([U3.14.01](doc:u3.14.01), [U3.13.01](doc:u3.13.01)),
- under-integrated elements: 3D_SI, D_PLAN_SI, C_PLAN_SI, AXIS_SI ([U3.14.01](doc:u3.14.01), [U3.13.05](doc:u3.13.05)),
- elements based on a quasi-incompressible formulation with 3 fields (displacement, swelling, pressure): 3D_INCO_UPG, D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG ([U3.14.06](doc:u3.14.06), [U3.13.07](doc:u3.13.07), [R3.06.08](doc:r3.06.08)),
- elements based on an incompressible formulation with 2 fields (displacement, pressure): 3D_INCO_UP , D_PLAN_INCO_UP and AXIS_INCO_UP for small strains and large strains (GDEF_LOG).

We propose, as an a priori choice, to use:

**quadratic elements**

As regards the choice of the modelling, it depends on the type of elements and on the need to treat the incompressibility condition. These considerations are summarised in the table below.

|  | normal | quasi-incompressible (high plasticity or nu >0.45 ) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| triangles/tetrahedra | standard | INCO |
| quadrilaterals/hexahedra | SI | SI or INCO |

**Justifications and precautions:**

- If the material is quasi-incompressible ($\nu >0,45$ ), it is preferable to use one of the INCO formulations, because the standard displacement formulation does not give good results.
- Plastic flow takes place at constant volume. This incompressibility condition may cause difficulties with the classical modelling, namely an overly stiff behaviour and, above all, the appearance of oscillations in the stresses. Under-integration makes it possible to improve these problems, because one then checks the incompressibility condition at fewer Gauss points. However, **only the QUAD8 and HEXA20 elements are truly under-integrated;** for the other mesh cells, it is the classical integration which is retained. Consequently, when oscillation phenomena are observed for a mesh composed of triangles or tetrahedra, it is preferable to use one of the INCO formulations. This markedly improves the results, but the analyses will take longer.
- In the general case, the under-integrated modelling gives results as good as those of the classical finite elements, and this for a shorter run time, since fewer Gauss points are used. Furthermore, in the case of thermomechanical analyses, this makes it possible to limit the difficulties when passing from the strain of thermal origin to the mechanical analysis when the refinements of the thermal and mechanical meshes differ. However, under-integration may sometimes lead to the appearance of spurious modes. If, at the end of the analysis, the deformed shape exhibits this kind of non-physical deformation mode, it is better to carry out the analysis with the classical modelling, or with the quasi-incompressible modelling if the levels of plasticity are very high.

## 3 Implementation in Code_Aster

The main stages of the *Aster* analysis are recalled here in the case of a plane-strain analysis, stating explicitly where the specifications discussed above come into play. For the mechanical part, what is specific to the case of a thermomechanical analysis has been written in bold.

### 3.1 Thermal study

- Reading of the thermal mesh

| MA=LIRE_MAILLAGE(UNITE=20,) |
| --- |

- Choice of the thermal model

```text
MOTH2D=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MA,

VERIF='MAILLE',

AFFE=_F(GROUP_MA= (‘GMA1’,’GMA2’,…),

PHENOMENE='THERMIQUE',

MODELISATION='PLAN_DIAG',),)
```

- Thermal properties of the material
- Thermal loading
- THER_LINEAIRE or THER_NON_LINE

```text
THER = …
```

- Possible post-processings

### 3.2 Mechanical study

- Reading of the mechanical mesh

| MAME=LIRE_MAILLAGE() | MAME=CREA_MAILLAGE( MAILLAGE = MA, LINE_QUAD=_F(TOUT=’OUI’)) |
| --- | --- |

- Definition of the mechanical model

```text
MOME=AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE=MAME,

VERIF='MAILLE',

AFFE=_F(GROUP_MA= (‘GMA1’,’GMA2’,…),

PHENOMENE='MECANIQUE',

MODELISATION='D_PLAN_SI',),);
```

- Projection of the thermal analysis if the analysis is chained onto 2 different meshes

```text
CHTHER=PROJ_CHAMP(    METHODE='COLLOCATION',
RESULTAT=THER,
MODELE_1=MOTH2D,
MODELE_2=MOME,);
```

- Characteristics of the material

```text
CHMAT = AFFE_MATERIAU(MAILLAGE = MAME,
AFFE_VARC = _F(    NOM_VARC='TEMP',
TEMP_REF = 20.,
EVOL=CHTER ou THER si pas de projection …)
```

- Mechanical loadings
- STAT_NON_LINE
- Post-processings
