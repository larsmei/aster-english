# v3.03.137 SSLS137 - Prestressed concrete plate with a flexion cable

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the CALC_PRECONT macro-command for elements hulls. The reference solution is based on beam theory. The first Modelling is done with elements $\mathrm{3D}$ and is designed to validate the use of beam theory as a reference. Two modellings hulls are then proposed (DKT and Q4GG) as well as two different mesh cells types (TRIA3, QUAD4) for each Modelling. Finally, the F Modelling resumes the D Modelling by replacing the constitutive law concrete elastic with the constitutive law nonlinear GLRC_DAMAGE.

## 1 Description

### 1.1 Geometry

The design of the study is a rectangular plate of length $L=\mathrm{4m}$ , width $l=\mathrm{0.5m}$ and thickness $t=\mathrm{0.2m}$ . A bending rail composed of a single frame, crosses the plate at its half and is eccentricated by a distance ${e}_{z}=\mathrm{0.075m}$ from the median plane of the plate following the $z$ axis . D-un point geometrically visible, only the area of the right section of the $A={\mathrm{0.00015m}}^{2}$ bar is known . The figure below illustrates the position of the cable for the study model . The median plane of the plate is defined by the $\mathit{ABCD}$ rectangle and the cable by the $\mathit{EF}$ segment .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The AB segment of the plate is clamped, all degrees of freedom on this side are blocked and the other edges are left free to allow it a beam-type configuration. The order of application of the loads is defined as follows.

#### 1.3.1 Loading 1:

It corresponds to the cable voltage setting. We charge a tension in the cable equal to $3.75\times {10}^{5}N$, and only the node $F$ of the cable located at the free edge of the plate is active. Voltage losses due to steel relaxation and back anchoring are neglected.

#### 1.3.2 Loading 2:

The second load applies a pressure ${P}_{0}={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$ , carried by the $-z$ axis , to the entire plate.

### 1.4 Key steps in testing

DEFI_CABLE_BP macro-command is used to obtain kinematic relationships between the plate and cable as well as the voltage-related load in the cable. Only the node $F$ of the cable is considered active.

The CALC_PRECONT macro-command is then launched to prestress the plate from the voltage of the given cable.

The STAT_NON_LINE control is also used to apply the pressure load to the plate. The maximum value of the displacement on the free edge of the plate is then extracted at node $D$ , to compare them with the reference values and thus validate the voltage setting of the prestress cables for elements hulls.

## 2 reference solution

For each model, the objective is to recover the correct initial prestress voltage of the cable after the application of CALC_PRECONT, but also to determine the maximum displacement of the plate to node $D$ following the $z$ axis after the application of the pressure.

### 2.1 Baseline

For determining maximum displacement, the theory of bending beams is used. For one clamped beam at one end and free of the other under distributed load, the maximum displacement at the free end called deflection is given by:

$f=-\frac{{\mathit{qL}}^{4}}{\mathrm{8EI}}$

$q$: the force distributed in $N/m$.

$L$: the length of the beam in $m$.

$E$: the Young's modulus of the plate, so concrete in $\mathit{Pa}$.

$I=\frac{{t}^{3}l}{12}$: the quadratic moment of the beam relative to the y axis in ${m}^{4}$.

*For pressure force:*

Thanks to its excentre, the cable contributes to the rigidity of the model:

${(\mathit{EI})}_{\mathit{eq}}={E}_{b}\frac{{t}^{3}l}{12}+{E}_{a}{a}_{x}l\times {e}_{z}^{2}$

${a}_{x}=\frac{A}{\mathit{dx}}$: is the rate of railing.

${(\mathit{EI})}_{\mathit{eq}}=13.50{\mathit{MN.m}}^{2}$

The deflection under pressure loading is calculated as follows:

${f}_{p}=\frac{-{P}_{0}l\times {L}^{4}}{8{(\mathit{EI})}_{\mathit{eq}}}$

Then:

${f}_{p}=-\mathrm{0.118552m}$

*For the voltage setting of the prestress cable:*

The energized cable then applies an $-{F}_{0}$ compression force and an bending moment $-{e}_{z}{F}_{0}$ to the free end of the plate.

With the principle of superposition, the expression of normal stress is:

${\sigma}_{x}=\frac{-{F}_{0}}{\mathit{tl}}(1+\frac{12{e}_{z}z}{{t}^{2}})$

If the effects of Poisson's ratio are ignored, the displacement field is given by:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u(x,y,z)=\frac{-{F}_{0}}{{E}_{b}\mathit{tl}}(1+\frac{12{e}_{z}z}{{t}^{2}})x\\ v(x,y,z)=0\\ w(x,y,z)=\frac{{F}_{0}}{{E}_{b}\mathit{tl}}(\frac{6{e}_{z}}{{t}^{2}}{x}^{2})\end{array}$

The movements are given at the free end of the beam to node $D$ , i.e. in $(4,0.5,0)$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u(x,y,z)=-\mathrm{0.375mm}\\ v(x,y,z)=0\\ w(x,y,z)=\mathrm{16.875mm}\end{array}$

By superimposition, the theoretical maximum displacement of the prestressed plate under the pressure force is:

${f}_{\mathit{tot}}={f}_{p}+w$

${f}_{\mathit{tot}}=-\mathrm{0.101677m}$

## 9 Executive summary

The results obtained with Modelling A correspond well to the analytical solution proposed from the beam theory. This therefore validates the choice of beam model as a reference.

In modellings B to E (DKT and Q4GG), the stress values in the CALC_PRECONT output cable are well expected. On the other hand, the deflection values obtained are almost identical to the analytical solution. It is noted that the error percentage is less important with the DKT Modelling for this study case, but above all that the error on the deflection is much less than with the $\mathrm{3D}$ Modelling.

Modelling F validates that the prestress calculation is correct when the concrete is modelled by the GLRC_DAMAGE law.

This validates the use of CALC_PRECONT macro-command for hulls.
