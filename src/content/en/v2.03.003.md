# v2.03.003 SDLS03 - Rectangular thin plate simply pressed on the edges

**Summary:**

This three-dimensional problem consists in searching for vibration frequencies of a mechanical structure of a plate type. Two different configurations allow testing vibration modes in the plane of the plate (membrane behaviour) with elastic supports on two opposite edges, and the bending vibration modes of a plate pressed on its contour.

This structural mechanical test corresponds to a dynamic analysis of a surface model with linear behavior. It has two modellings (mesh in triangle or quadrangle).

This problem allows testing the elements plate in transverse bending and membrane and the calculation of vibration frequencies by the Lanczos method or the Bathe and Wilson method.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 Flexion problem

The plate is in single support on all sides: for all point $P$ of the edge one has: $w=0$ .

#### 1.3.2 Membrane problem

For all points of the plate, the $z$ displacement and the three degrees of rotation are blocked, i.e.:

$w=0.$ ${\theta}_{x}={\theta}_{y}={\theta}_{z}=0.$

On the sides $\mathrm{AD}$ and $\mathrm{BC}$ the displacement in $y$ is blocked: for $y=0.$ or $y=a$ we have $v=0$.

At $A,B,C,D$ points, stiffness $k$ springs are attached. The axis of these springs is the $x$ direction.

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.003/10000712000018CE00000B0F086A97A8D3B0E151.svg)

The numerical value of $k$ is as follows: $k=25N/m$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

#### 2.1.1 Flexion problem

The reference solution of the bending problem is the one given in the SDLS03/89 form of the VPCS guide which presents the calculation method as follows.

The formulation of M.V. BARTON for a rectangular plate, placed on its four sides leads for bending modes to:

${f}_{ij}=\frac{\pi}{2}\left[{\left[\frac{i}{a}\right]}^{2}+{\left[\frac{j}{b}\right]}^{2}\right]\sqrt{\frac{E{t}^{2}}{12\rho (1-{\nu}^{2})}}$

with:

$i$ = number of half wave length according to $y$ (dimension $a$),

$j$ = number of half wave length according to $x$ (size $b$).

#### 2.1.2 Membrane problem

The membrane problem is equivalent for the first vibration frequency to the following one-dimensional problem:

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.003/100002A000001389000003D3590F44B9BA27389B.svg)

où:

$k$ is the stiffness of the springs,

$m$ is the mass of the plate.

The frequency searched is: $f=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{4k}}{m}}$

### 2.2 Baseline

For the bending problem, the first six vibration frequencies are calculated and for the membrane calculation the first frequency is calculated.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

The solutions are analytical, so there is no uncertainty.
