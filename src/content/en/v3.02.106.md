# v3.02.106 SSLP106 – Rectangular pure bending massif (test of elements QUAD4 embedded)

**Summary:**

The elements finished under integrated point of Gauss stabilized by the *assumed train* method is tested on a pure bending calculation in flat deformation.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Geometry is a square on the $L=100\mathrm{mm}$ side.

| ![Figure](/figures/v3.02.106/Shape1.gif) |
| --- |

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is elastic incompressible and has for properties:

$E=100\mathrm{MPa}$

$\nu =0.4999$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Given the antisymmetric nature of the problem, only half of the massif is modelled with the following boundary conditions:

On $\mathrm{OE}$:

$\mathrm{DX}(\mathrm{OE})=0$

On $\mathrm{OD}$:

$\mathrm{DX}(O)=\mathrm{DY}(O)=0$

$\mathrm{DX}(D)=0$

$\mathrm{fsx}=\frac{\mathrm{8y}}{L}.{\sigma}_{d}$

$\mathrm{fsy}=-(1-\frac{{\mathrm{4y}}^{2}}{\mathrm{L²}}).{\sigma}_{d}$

On $\mathrm{BC}$:

$\mathrm{fsy}=+(1-\frac{{\mathrm{4y}}^{2}}{\mathrm{L²}}).{\sigma}_{d}$

With ${\sigma}_{d}$ a given constraint, which will be taken equal to 1 in the test. ${\sigma}_{d}=1\mathrm{MPa}$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution comes from an analytical solution from [Bib1]:

$$
{u}_{x}(x,y)=\frac{4(1-{\nu}^{2})}{{\mathrm{EL}}^{2}}.(\mathrm{y.}({x}^{2}-\mathrm{2.Lx})+\frac{2+\nu (1-\nu )}{3}.y.(\frac{{L}^{2}}{4}-{y}^{2})).{\sigma}_{d}
$$

And then $y$:

$$
{u}_{y}(x,y)=\frac{4(1-{\nu}^{2})}{{\mathrm{EL}}^{2}}({\mathrm{Lx}}^{2}-\frac{{x}^{3}}{3}-\nu .(1-\nu ).{y}^{2}.(x-L)+\frac{4+5\nu .(1-\nu )}{12}.{\mathrm{xL}}^{2})).{\sigma}_{d}
$$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Applying , you can find the following $x$ displacement at point $C$: ${u}_{x}(L,L/2)=-1.5\mathrm{mm}$

And by applying , we find the following $y$ displacement at point $C$: ${u}_{y}(L,L/2)=4.25\mathrm{mm}$

### 2.3 Bibliographical references

[Bib1] Timoshenko & Woinowsky-Krieger, Theory of plates and shells, McGrawHill, 1964.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Modelling in flat deformations on the following mesh $\mathrm{2D}$:

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.106/Shape2.gif)

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes: 25

Mesh cells Numbers and Types: 16 SEG2, 16 QUAD4 with elements D_PLAN_SI.

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

| **Value tested** | **Reference** | **Type** | **difference** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{DX}$ moving to $C$ | -1,25 | Analysis | 1.7% |
| $\mathrm{DY}$ moving to $C$ | 4,25 | Analysis | 0,3% |

### 3.4 Remarks

The stress is said to be at dominant bending. Through this calculation, the difficulty for the QUAD4, even under-integrated, to represent the bending modes in flat deformation and for an $\nu$ coefficient close to 0.5 is shown. This results in excessive rigidity of the element due to the shearing of the discretized gradient operator: it is a numerical blockage.

### 4.4 Remarks

These elementss are suitable for incompressible problems and give the same result as the analytical solution.

## 5 Summary of the results

The poor quality of the result of the elements QUAD4 sub-integrated is explained by the digital blocking phenomenon which makes the element very rigid. Moreover, its convergence towards the analytical solution is very slow. This phenomenon appears of course also for the fully integrated element. The calculation using the incompressible quadratic d-elements allows to obtain an exact solution.
