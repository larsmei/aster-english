# v2.08.012 FORMA12 – Practical work of the training "Introduction to linear & nonlinear dynamic analysis"

**Summary:**

This case is an introduction to the use of Code_Aster for dynamic transient analysis (linear and nonlinear). It deals with a thick plate of steel, subject to sinusoidal excitation on a vibrating table.

The plate is modeled in elements 3D. The transient analysis on modal basis is compared with a direct transient analysis. In the last part the plasticity of the steel is taken into account by a nonlinear law.

The different modellings addressed are:

- Modelling A: modal analysis;
- modelling B: direct transition;
- Model C: modal-based transition with static correction a priori;
- Modelling D: modal transition with post-static correction;
- Modelling E: nonlinear transition;
- Modelling F: linear transient with non-linear contact;
- Model G: nonlinear transient by dynamic substructure.

The first chapter presents the geometry and data common to all modellings.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Geometry

The study concerns a thick plate of steel. Its geometry is simple. It is a simple flattened cube with the following dimensions [cf. geometrie-vue-3D]:

- length = $2,0$ metres;
- width = $2,0$ metres;
- thickness = $0,003$ meters.

The plate is firmly clamped on one of its sides (ENCAS) and fixed to a vibrating table.

The displacement of an point on the upper side of a plate corner is calculated, in contrast to the clamped support (point P).

![Figure](/figures/v2.08.012/geometrie_vue_3D.png)

*Geometry (view 3D)*

![Figure](/figures/v2.08.012/geometrie_vue_dessus.png)

*Geometry (view from above)*

### 1.2 Materials

Steel is considered to be a linear isotropic material, which is elastic:

- Young's modulus : $E=200000.{10}^{+6}\mathit{Pa}$;
- Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0,3$;
- density: $\rho =8000\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$.

Post-elastic (plastic) behavior is idealized by a linearly hardening elastoplastic law whose plastic module (plastic slope) is 1% of the elastic Young's modulus. The stress threshold of elasticity is 200 MPa.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

The plate is clamped on one side.

A distracted engineer (or somewhat facetious) made an error in the vibrating table parameters. Instead of the expected stress (a synthetic accelerogram), he sent a simple sinusoidal signal but of very high amplitude. The plate was not designed for such a shaking and the movement that the plate could undergo during this unfortunate test was determined.

The input signal is a simple $15\mathit{Hz}$ frequency sinus.

The amplitude is $\mathrm{30g}$ horizontally and $\mathrm{30g}$ vertically downwards ($g=10m/{s}^{2}$ ).

The signal duration is $0.5s$ .

## 3 Modelling B – Direct transient analysis

### 3.1 Problem and outline of calculation

We now want to calculate the movement of the plate on its vibrating table. We start with the most natural calculation, the direct calculation on physical basis. We make two calculations: without and with a damping type Rayleigh.

A key question is the Modelling of the load on the plate. Here we choose to use a force of gravity of intensity and direction corresponding to the settings of the vibrating table.

## 4 Modelling C – transient analysis on a modal basis

### 4.1 Problem and indices

The transitional analysis can also be done more effectively by projecting the problem on a modal basis. It is proposed to compare this method with the direct transient calculation.

The first step is to resolve the transition to the family of modes determined in model A. To do so, a modal base is constructed with the previous modes, matrices and vectors are projected into this new base and the transition is resolved. These steps are specified below:

- calculation of modes as in Modelling A
- construction of a vector containing the second member by *CALC_VECT_ELEM and ASSE_VECTEUR*
- definition of a modal base by *DEFI_BASE_MODALE*
- creation of a generalised numbering by *NUME_DDL_GENE*

  - If the base is orthonormized, it can be indicated by the keyword *STOCKAGE=
- projection of mass matrices, stiffness and load vector: *PROJ_VECT_BASE* and *PROJ_MATR_BASE*

  - as the vector is a load, we specify *TYPE_VECT='FORC'*
- modal-based transitional calculation: *DYNA_VIBRA(TYPE_CALCUL='TRANS', BASE_CALCUL='GENE')*

  - calculate the response up to $0.5s$ with time step of $0.002s$
- recovery of point P displacement by *RECU_FONCTION(RESU_GENE)*

  - It is appropriate to ask what basis this displacement is expressed on.
- P/time displacement curve printing: *IMPR_FONCTION*

It is emphasized that the *DYNA_LINE* command ensures all these steps in a very synthetic way. Attention: by default, this command determines static enrichment. To not do so, you must specify *ENRI_STAT='NON'*.

### 4.3 Modal calculations with static correction

A method is much more effective, however, and is the static correction method, which allows for the "static" contribution of the structure to the movement. It can be performed in two ways:

- a priori: a new mode is incorporated into the modal base on which to solve the transient;
- a posteriori: the initial modal base is retained but a correction is added to the solution obtained.

We'll look at these two possibilities.

First of all, the static correction a priori (modelization C). The steps of the transitional calculation on a modal basis with static correction a priori are as follows:

- The mode of the plate is calculated up to 50 Hz via *CALC_MODES*.
- The pseudomode is calculated via *MODE_STATIQUE/SPEUDO_MODE*.
- A new modal base is defined by adding the static mode to the "dynamic" modes by *DEFI_BASE_MODALE / RITZ*.
- Since these modes are not orthogonal in principle, *ORTHO='OUI'* is specified to perform orthonormalization. It is also necessary to specify which matrix will be used for this operation (orthogonality in the standard matrix).
- Creating a generalized numbering by *NUME_DDL_GENE*
- If the base is orthonormized, it can be indicated by the keyword *STOCKAGE=
- projection of mass matrices, stiffness and load vector: *PROJ_VECT_BASE* and *PROJ_MATR_BASE*
- Since the vector is a load, we indicate *TYPE_VECT='FORC'*
- Transitional calculation on modal basis: *DYNA_VIBRA(TYPE_CALCUL='TRANS', BASE_CALCUL='GENE')*

  - The answer to $0.5s$ is calculated with time step of $0.002s$
  - The Devogelaere-Fu time scheme is used by *SCHEMA_TEMPS / SCHEMA='DEVOGE'*
- construction of the overall response of the structure on a physical basis by *REST_GENE_PHYS*
- recovery of displacement of point P *RECU_FONCTION(RESULTAT)*
- P/time displacement curve printing: *IMPR_FONCTION*

It is emphasized that the *DYNA_LINE* command ensures all these steps in a very synthetic way.

The static post-retirement correction will be treated in model D.

## 6 Modelling E – nonlinear dynamics

The main objective of this TP is to set up a transitional nonlinear calculation with plasticity taken into account. We will use the operator *DYNA_NON_LINE*.

### 6.1 Calculation procedure

The general-purpose operator *DYNA_NON_LINE* is used to calculate dynamic nonlinear behaviour. It is close to *STAT_NON_LINE* and its use in the vicinity. The main difference is the time integration pattern and the influence of inertia and damping.

#### 6.1.1 General data

Compared to the previous calculations in *DYNA_VIBRA* and *DYNA_LINE*, the fundamental difference is that the matrices (of rigidity, damping and mass) and the second members (loading) are not to be calculated before the operator. Indeed, the command *DYNA_NON_LINE* is called "monolithic" because the rigidity matrix varies during the transient since it solves a nonlinear problem (which depends on the displacement). Therefore, you have to give it the information as you do in the ASSEMBLAGE macro-command to evaluate its operators itself:

- *CHAM_MATER*
- *MODELE*
- *CARA_ELEM* if you have elements structure or discrete (which is not the case here)

The loads are the same as in previous exercises. They are given by the key word factor *EXCIT* as in *DYNA_VIBRA*. Do not forget to apply the sinus in *FONC_MULT* for gravity.

#### 6.1.2 Choice of nonlinear behaviour

The choice of nonlinear behavior is made in the *COMPORTEMENT* key word factor of *DYNA_NON_LINE*. If you do not provide information, we will consider the calculation to be elastic in small deformations.

Of course, if you have a nonlinear behavior, you have to define the appropriate material characteristics in *DEFI_MATERIAU*.

In this exercise, we propose the plasticity of von Mises with linear kinematic hardening:

- *DEFI_MATERIAU* must be selected *ECRO_CINE*. *D_SIGM_EPSI = 2GPa* (Young's modulus divided by 100) and an elastic limit SY = 200MPa. It is highly recommended to always inform the keyword *ELAS* even when plasticizing.
- in *DYNA_NON_LINE/COMPORTEMENT*, select *RELATION = 'VMIS_CINE_LINE' * and *DEFORMATION =

#### 6.1.3 Time selection and temporal discretization

The choice of the time pattern is fundamental in dynamic. Moreover, a nonlinear problem adds an additional constraint on temporal discretization to have sufficient precision for the integration of nonlinearity at the level of the Gauss point (plasticity). In code_aster, the majority of patterns of discretization of behavioral equations are implicit and precise, but this precision may depend on this discretization, especially because it makes some assumptions about the nature of the problem (see advanced code_aster formation). It is therefore an additional numerical parameter on which to be very careful.

A time discretization which has three characteristics should be chosen:

1. The application of the Shannon rule is a good basis: at least a frequency of discretization twice as high as the most penalizing load.
2. time discretization must also be chosen according to the characteristics of the pattern in time: stability condition (for diagrams with one) and accuracy
3. Temporal discretization must be chosen to accurately integrate the nonlinear part

It is proposed to take a 0.002 s time step. Since nonlinear calculation is potentially very long, it is recommended to stop at tfin time = 0.1s:

*LISTR=DEFI_LIST_REEL(DEBUT=0.,INTERVALLE=_F(JUSQU_A=0.1,PAS=0.002,),)*

The additional constraint of nonlinear behavior requires that *DYNA_NON_LINE* be allowed to cut the time step when it fails in integration. To do this, we use the *DEFI_LIST_INST* prepator:

*LIS=DEFI_LIST_INST(METHODE='MANUEL',DEFI_LIST=_F(LIST_INST=LISTR,),)*

This command says that the calculation will be speeded up on the basis of the list of moments *LISTR* (and, for example, all the data of the calculation will be saved at these times),

But if there is a problem (e.g. failure to integrate nonlinear), it is allowed to cut the time step in 5, via * ECHEC=_F(SUBD_NIVEAU=5,SUBD_PAS_MINI=1e-5,SUBD_METHODE='MANUEL',*. The cut is recursive: it cuts in 5, if it fails, it cuts in 5, etc. It will stop permanently (fail) as soon as it reaches a minimum step given by the parameter *SUBD_PAS_MINI=1E-5*.

The result of this command is to be entered directly in the key word *INCREMENT/LIST_INST of DYNA_NON_LINE*. **Please choose LIS and not LISTR, otherwise the time step cut in case of failure will not be enabled.**

In the key word *SCHEMA_TEMPS*, you can select your schema in time. By default, it is a moving Newmark schema. This schema is unconditionally stable and retains energy. Other schematic features are available, such as the dissipative HHT schema, which helps to mitigate the digital disturbances generated by high frequencies. Here, the HHT schema is adopted with the parameter *ALPHA=-0.1*.

## 7 Modelization F – linear dynamics with localized nonlinearities

### 7.1 Purpose

This case is an introduction to the use of Code_Aster for dynamic transient analysis with localized nonlinearities. The shock behavior considered is of elastic type. Nonlinearity is introduced by a displacement/speed/force relationship to nodes bearing nonlinearities. The calculation is performed by relying on the linear dynamic operator of Code_Aster *DYNA_VIBRA*. Resolution s-based on an explicit integration scheme, nonlinearity is treated to the second member of the equations of movement:
* At moment i, the displacements are calculated to wait for equilibrium with the forces calculated at moment i-1;
* the forces at moment i are calculated from the displacements thus calculated and are applied at moment i+1 to obtain the displacements at i+1.
The plate response is calculated up to a final time of 1 second, a normal stiffness of the impact equal to *KN=1E8N/m* is considered.

### 7.2 Calculation framework

A thick plate of steel is deformed before being dropped. Two contact points then limit the movement of the plate. Both contact points are arranged under the plate, opposite the ends of the free edge (Figure 7.1). When the plate is horizontal, the game is zero between the plate and the contact points. The geometry of the plate and the material are the same as those already used for modellings a,b,c and d.

![Figure](/figures/v2.08.012/plaque-acier-point-contact.png)

*Position of the impact points*

Specifically, the plate is pre-deformed by 0.25m in direction Z and then left free to vibrate. Contact will occur whenever, during the vibrational movement, the nodes d-extremity of the plate on the lower surface is in their initial position before deformation.

### 7.3 Mesh

The mesh of the plate is the same as the one already used for modellings a,b,c,d and e. However, you must create:

- A group of mesh cell CHOC_ a2 and CHOC_ b2 containing the nodes d-end of the plate positioned on the lower surface opposite the contact points;
- LIBRE: it is a mesh-cell group (*SEG2*) at the free end of the plate, connecting the nodes CHOC_ a2 and CHOC_ b2 groups.
- The assignment of the static problem load is done via the *MECA_STATIQUE* operator.
- Boundary conditions and loading are imposed via the keyword *EXCIT*.

The *CREA_CHAMP* command is then used to create the displacement field which will then be the initial condition of the dynamic calculation. This is the displacement field calculated at the last moment of static calculation. Attention, it is important to use the same numbering of the degrees of freedom that was used during assembly without the required displacement load in the field construction.

### 7.4 Dynamic calculation

The common steps are as follows:

- calculate the modal base using the *CALC_MODES* command;
- Static modes are calculated by the *MODE_STATIQUE* command. A unit stress is imposed on the impact nodes b2 and a2 to calculate the static distortion of the plate under these conditions. The displacement field thus obtained is used to enrich the modal base of the plate to take into account its static response. The enrichment is done by the *DEFI_BASE_MODALE* command;
- the built base is not made up of orthogonal modes. It is then necessary to orthogonalize. To do this we again rely on the command *DEFI_MODALE*;
- The mass and stiffness matrices are then projected on the new base via *PROJ_BASE* operator, but the displacement vector resulting from the static calculation must also be projected.
- The Aster command that defines the geometry of the shock places is *DEFI_OBSTACLE* (U4.44.21 user documentation). The key word *TYPE* allows for a more precise definition of the shock physics. For example, the key words *PLAN_Y*, *PLAN_Z*, *CERCLE* and *DISCRET* define the geometry of the contacts between a mobile structure and an unformable obstacle. The types *BI_PLAN_Y*, *BI_PLAN_Z*, *BI_CERCLE* and *BI_CERC_INT* allow you to define the possible contact points between two mobile nodes. In the present case, a plate that comes into contact with fixed points in space, the type *PLAN_Z* is chosen.
- The parameterization of *DYNA_VIBRA* is defined by the definition of the impact behaviour. The key word behaviour must be present as many times as the number of contact points, so twice in the present case. For each occurrence of the key word behavior, then you must specify:

  - which is the node group of the structure to which the behavior will be affected (CHOC_ a2 at the first occurrence of the behavior keyword, CHOC_ b2 at the second)
  - if the impact occurs with friction or not: keyword *FROTTEMENT*
  - CHOC(*DIS_CHOC* relation for elastic contacts)
  - stiffness in normal direction at impact: *RIGI_NORM*
  - the geometry of the impact site;
  - normal at the impact direction *NORM_OBST*;
  - The obstacle defined by the command *DEFI_OBSTACLE*, keyword *OBSTACLE*;
  - The origin of the obstacle via the keyword *ORIG_OBST*. This is the origin of the impact marker;
  - normal at the impact direction *NORM_OBST*;
  - the game, or the position of the crash plane in the impact marker;
  - mass and stiffness matrices (projected on the enriched and orthogonized basis)
  - Initial state (*ETAT_INIT*) including projected static displacement on modal basis
  - the integration scheme (a Runge-Kutta type scheme, keyword *RUNGE_KUTTA_32*), and time increment (final moment and not. A step equal to 0.0001s was chosen for the study)
  - mass and stiffness matrices (projected on the enriched and orthogonized basis)
  - *In-fine:* results obtained on a physical basis are reported using the *REST_GENE_PHYS* command. This is done by selecting a 0.001s time step. The time-determining definition for *REST_GENE_PHYS* is done via *LIST_DEFI_REEL*.
  - The results are printed in a .med *via* file with the *IMPR_RESU* command.

## 8 Modelization G – nonlinear dynamics with substructure

### 8.1 Purpose

This TP follows the Modelling E (nonlinear dynamics). The objective of this TP is to implement a transitional nonlinear calculation with DYNA_NON_LINE taking into account dynamic substructure.

Dynamic substructure consists of partitioning the structure into one or more substructures to reduce the size of the numerical model and to be able to independently study each substructure. The overall model is subsequently obtained by assembling the models of the different substructures.

Each substructure is projected on a judiciously chosen vector basis, which reduces the size of the model of each substructure and therefore the size of the model of the assembled structure.

The behaviour of the substructure must be linear. The portion of the plate in which the nonlinear (plasticity) behaviour was observed in model E will remain represented as a physical basis. Therefore, the choice of the region of the model to be applied the substructure method must be judicious.

The following figure shows the elements which have plasticized (red) along the E model transient, corresponding to the near area of the plate:

![Figure](/figures/v2.08.012/deux-zones-plastification_modelisation-G.png)

*Result of Modelling E with the red plasticized zone.*

Half of the model remaining linear (side opposite to l-clamped support) will therefore be subject to the substructure procedure:

![Figure](/figures/v2.08.012/mesh-deux-zones-modelisation-G.png)

*mesh with two *GROUP_MA* for substructure*

For the purpose of this study, the mesh forma12g.med was enriched with the creation of a d-group elements (VOL_1) representing the part that will be modelled in physical basis (clamped support side), a d-group elements (VOL_2) representing the part that will be represented in a reduced base, and an node group (No_Int) at the interface of these two zones of the model.

### 8.2 Calculation procedure

#### 8.2.1 Creating dynamic macro-element

The first step is to create the dynamic macro-element that will represent the behavior of the condensed part of the structure in the final model. To do this, the Craig-Bampton method will be applied. This step is only done on the d-group d

Craig-Bampton's technique consists of selecting normal and forced modes as the projection base. Normal modes are the modes of the substructure where all nodes interfaces with adjacent substructures are blocked. A constrained mode is defined by the static distortion obtained by imposing a unit displacement on a degree of freedom restraint, while the other degrees of freedom restraint is blocked. The projection base contains normal modes and all the forced modes associated with all degrees of freedom restraint restraint degrees of freedom.

To calculate normal modes, we must therefore incorporate the nodes group of the interface *No_Int* with the *AFFE_CHAR_MECA* operator. Before assembling the mass and rigidity matrices necessary for the calculation of normal modes, we use the opportunity to create the load applied to the group *VOL_2*, which will also compose the dynamic macro-element. We then run the *ASSEMBLAGE* operator with the *RIGI_MECA* options *MASS_MECA*, *AMOR_MECA* and *CHAR_MECA*.

Normal modes are calculated with *CALC_MODES*. Attention, the number of modes selected must be multiple of the DDL number of the finished elements that make up the condensed domain (3 in this case), keyword *CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=30)*.

Forced modes are calculated with the *MODE_STATIQUE* operator, key words *MODE_STAT=(_F(GROUP_NO="No_Int", AVEC_CMP=("DX", "DY", "DZ")*.

Then the dynamic interface must be defined with *DEFI_INTERF_DYNA*, keyword *TYPE=的CRAIGB* in order to define the modal base containing normal modes, forced modes and dynamic interface from *DEFI_BASE_MODALE* operator.

The new database is re-numbered using *NUME_DDL_GENE*, and the load is projected on this basis with *PROJ_VECT_BASE*.

The macro-element is built by the *MACR_ELEM_DYNA* operator with the key words *BASE_MODALE* and *CAS_CHARGE*. Next, the *DEFI_MAILLAGE* key word *DEFI_SUPER_MAILLE* is used to define a new mesh from the dynamic macro-element. This new mesh must then be assembled at the basic mesh of the TP, forma12g.med, using the *ASSE_MAILLAGE* operator.

#### 8.2.2 Construction of the model

Once the macro-element is generated and integrated into the initial mesh, the second step of this TP consists of building the plate model, which will have a half represented in physical base, and the other half by the dynamic macro-element. The macro-element is taken into account in the model from the *AFFE_SOUS_STRUC* key word of the *AFFE_MODELE* operator.

Once the elasto-plastic material has been assigned to the physical mesh-cell group ‘VOL_1' and the boundary conditions d-clamped support applied to the edge of the plate, it is important to calculate the model's eigenmodes for verification of whether the macro-element has been properly constructed and integrated into the model that will be used thereafter.

The calculation continues as in Modelling E, the only difference being that in the *DYNA_NON_LINE* operator the key word *SOUS_STRUC* with the *CAS_CHARGE* argument must be added to allow the load applied to the part of the model that has been condensed to be taken into account.

Once the calculation is complete, the *REST_COND_TRAN* operator allows the fields to be recovered in the condensed part of the model. The post-processing suite is identical to the E Modelling suite.
