# v2.01.325 SDLD325 - Transient dynamic response of a 2 degrees of freedom damped spring mass system

**Summary:**

This problem consists in analysing the dynamic response of a system composed of a set of 2 degrees of freedom shock absorbers with very different spring stiffeners under excitation of a niche type in 1 degree of freedom.

Through this problem, the sensitivity of integration patterns to physical space or modal space to rigidity ratio is tested.

The results on the move and speed are compared with an average of results from industrial codes and an improved Newmark digital integration method.

### 1.1 Geometry

Restraint Riders: $k={28.10}^{3}{\mathit{N.m}}^{-1}$

et ${F}_{0}=\mathrm{5N}$ .

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The system is at rest at $t=0$: $u(0)=0$ and $\frac{\mathrm{du}}{\mathrm{dt}}(0)=0$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The search for a transitional response to this non-proportional damping problem can be carried out by digital integration into real space:

$[M]\lbrace \ddot{{u}_{n}}\rbrace +[C]\lbrace \dot{{u}_{n}}\rbrace +[K]\lbrace {u}_{n}\rbrace =\lbrace F\rbrace$

For this, the answer was calculated with two industrial codes:

- PERMAS : Newmark integration scheme ($\alpha =0,25$ and $\delta =0,5$ ) $\Delta t={10}^{-\mathrm{4s}}$ ;
- ABAQUS : Hilbert-Hugues-Taylor integration scheme [bib1] ($\alpha =–0,05$ ) $\Delta t={10}^{-\mathrm{4s}}$ ;

and the $\beta$ -Newmark Enhanced Integration Method [bib2]:

$\left[\frac{[M]}{\Delta {t}^{2}}+\frac{[C]}{2\Delta t}+\frac{[K]}{3}\right]\left\lbrace {u}_{n+2}\right\rbrace =\left[\frac{\lbrace  {F}_{n+2}\rbrace  +\lbrace  {F}_{n+1}\rbrace  +\lbrace  {F}_{n}\rbrace  }{3}\right]+\left[\frac{2[M]}{\Delta {t}^{2}}-\frac{[K]}{3}\right]\left\lbrace {u}_{n+1}\right\rbrace +\left[\frac{[M]}{\Delta {t}^{2}}+\frac{[C]}{2\Delta t}-\frac{[K]}{3}\right]\left\lbrace {u}_{n}\right\rbrace$

where $n$ , $n+1$ , $n+2$ refer respectively to the calculations performed at the times ${t}_{n}$ , ${t}_{n+1}={t}_{n}+\Delta t$ and ${t}_{n+2}={t}_{n}+2\Delta t$ where $\Delta t$ is the time increment retained.

To start, we take:

- ${u}_{0}$ et ${u}_{-1}={u}_{0}-\Delta t\dot{{u}_{0}}$
- ${F}_{-1}=2{F}_{0}-{F}_{1}$

The adopted time step is $\Delta t={10}^{-\mathrm{5s}}$ .

### 2.2 Baseline

Moving and speed of point end $B$ .

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

Average number of digital solutions.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

1. H.M. HILBERT, T.J.R HUGUES and R.L. TAYLOR "Improved numerical dissipation for time integration algorithms in structural dynamics" Earthquake Engineering and Structural Dynamics, Vol.5, 1977, pp. 283-292
2. N.M. NEWMARK "A method of computation for structural dynamics" Processing ASCE J.Eng.Mech. DIV E-3, July 1959, pp. 67-94

Names of nodes : $A=\mathrm{N1}$ , $C=\mathrm{N2}$ , $B=\mathrm{N3}$ .

- Integration on physical space with Newmark ($\alpha =0,25$ , $\delta =0,5$ )

- Integration on the full modal basis with Euler

Time step $\Delta t={10}^{-3}s$ then modal recombination

- Integration on full modal basis with $\Delta t$ Adaptive Order 2

Time step initial $\Delta t={10}^{-3}s$ and then modal recombination

- Integration on the full modal basis with $\Delta t$ adaptive by the Runge-Kutta-type method of order (54). The relative error tolerance is ${10}^{-6}$ .

Observation time: 3 seconds.

## 5 Summary of the results

For both modellings, the results are accurate with an error of less than 1%.

Modal integration with an adaptive step diagram of order 2 gives the best results for a limited calculation time.
