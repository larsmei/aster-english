# v4.25.300 TTLV300 - Parallelepiped subjected to flux density on its faces

**Summary:**

This test is based on independent validation of version 3 in linear transient thermal analysis.

This is a volume problem represented by a 3D model.

The functions tested are as follows:

- element thermal volumetric,
- transient thermal analysis algorithm,
- limit conditions: flow imposed.

The results are compared to a three-dimensional analytical solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v4.25.300/Object_1.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

| $\lambda =1.W/m°C$ | thermal conductivity |
| --- | --- |
| ${c}_{p}=1.J/\mathrm{kg}°C$ | specific heat |
| $\rho =1.\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | density |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Flux imposed on the 6 faces $q=0.5W/{m}^{2}={q}_{w}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

$T(t=0)=1°C={T}_{0}$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

$T(x,y,z,t)={T}_{0}+{\mathrm{2q}}_{w}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha t}}{\lambda}(A+B+C)$ with:

$A=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{1}+x}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]+\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{1}-x}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]\right]$

$B=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{2}+y}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]+\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{2}-y}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]\right]$

$C=\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{3}+z}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]+\mathrm{i.erfc}\left[\frac{(\mathrm{2m}-1){L}_{3}-z}{2\sqrt{\alpha t}}\right]\right]$

$\alpha =\frac{\lambda}{\rho {C}_{p}}$

Reference values are obtained with $m=1000.$

### 2.2 Baseline

Point temperature: $O(0,0,0)$ , $H(0.5,0.8,1.)$ and $C(1.,1.6,2.)$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

- M.J Chang, L.C Chow, W.S Chang, "Improved alternating direction imperative for solving three dimensional heat diffusion problems", Numerical Heat Transfer, vol 19, pp. 69-84, 1991.

## 5 Summary of the results

The results are satisfactory. the difference maximum (0.43%) is located on the outer surface of the parallelepiped (Point $C$ ) at the lowest $t$ moment. At the end of $10s$, this difference decreases, the maximum is then 0.054% (point $O$ : center of the parallelepiped).

This test allowed testing in linear transient 3D modelling with mesh cells HEXA8 and PENTA6.
