# v6.01.112 SSNA112 – axisymmetric tear-out test for restraint steel-concrete study: JOINT_BA law

**Summary:**

In this test case of mechanics the tear test carried out by La Borderie & Pijaudier- Cabot [bib1] was modelled for the purpose of studying the influence of stress state in the matrix on the mechanical properties of the interface. Geometric data and material characteristics are derived from their ratio, and the numerical results will be compared with the experimental results.

Axisymmetric Modelling uses elements QUAD4 for concrete and steel, in combination with elements joint for the interface (see doc.[R3.06.09]). Concrete and steel are considered elastic to test only the nonlinearity of constitutive law of restraint steel – concrete, JOINT_BA (see document [R7.01.21](doc:r7.01.21)). Monotonous calculation is performed with forced displacements at the end of the steel bar.

## 2 reference solution

The work of La Borderie & Pijaudier-Cabot [bib1] consisted of a field of tests of extractions carried out on fibers and reinforcements for reinforced concrete. The purpose of these tests was to study the influence of stress state in the matrix on the mechanical properties of the interface.

Each specimen was a $80\times 80\times 80\mathit{mm}$ cube with a concrete paste of $\mathit{Grade}\mathit{C30}/37$ ($16\mathrm{mm}$ gravier) with resistance at the time of testing (three days after casting and with a 4-hour heat treatment) of $14.5\mathrm{MPa}$ , an Young's modulus of $15500\mathit{MPa}$ and an Poisson's ratio of $0,17$ . For the reinforcements, they used $8\mathrm{mm}$ high-adhesive bars of diameter, with a steel-beton contact length of $45\mathrm{mm}$ , plus $10\mathrm{mm}$ free at the edge of the concrete, which eliminated the singularities in the field of concrete stresses in the clamped surface of the cube. The test was performed with controlled displacements ($\dot{u}=8.3\times 10E-3\mathrm{mm}/s$ ) and four containment levels: $0,5$ , $10$ and $15\mathrm{MPa}$ , constant during each experiment.

## 4 Summary of the results

With differences below $\text{5.0 \%}$ compared to experimental results obtained by La Borderie & Pijaudier-Cabot [bib1], the axisymmetric simulation of the removal test is considered satisfactory. However, in order to test the stability of the constitutive law in combination with other laws (e.g. the MAZARS law for concrete), the size of the time step will have to be reduced by considering that other laws are normally made for small deformation formulations.
