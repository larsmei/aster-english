# r7.02.09 Identification of the Weibull model

**Summary**

This document addresses the problem of identifying the parameters of the WEIBULL model on a sample of tests representative of the fracture behaviour of a brittle material (typically, a ferritic steel at low temperature). The linear-regression method and the maximum-likelihood method are the two methods retained. Their principle is set out in detail, together with the associated solution methods, both of which rely on an iterative procedure. Finally, their extension is shown to the case in which one of the two parameters of this model (the cleavage stress) depends on the temperature.

> Contents

## 1 1    Introduction

When they call upon the WEIBULL model (cf. POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22)), studies of the modelling of the brittle fracture of steels generally require a prior identification of the parameters of this model. In order to avoid a laborious identification "by hand" of these parameters, which would require the POST_ELEM operation with the WEIBULL option to be relaunched iteratively, an automatic calibration procedure has been implemented in *Code_Aster* .

In this document, the equations of the WEIBULL model are briefly recalled, then the identification problem posed is defined. The principle of the two solution methods retained (linear regression and maximum likelihood) is then described, including the case in which one of the two parameters of the model depends on the temperature.

## 2 2    Reminders

### 2.1 2.1    The WEIBULL model

Consider a structure of elastoplastic constitutive behaviour subjected to a thermomechanical loading. It is assumed that the cumulative failure probability of this structure follows the following two-parameter WEIBULL law [bib1]:

${P}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})=1-\exp\left[-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{w}}{{\sigma}_{u}})}^{m}\right]$                                     eq 2.1-1

an expression in which the WEIBULL modulus $m>1$ describes the tail of the statistical distribution of the sizes of the defects giving rise to cleavage, ${\sigma}_{u}$ is the cleavage stress and ${\sigma}_{w}$ is the WEIBULL stress, which depends on the history of the maximum principal stress field in the plastified zone of the structure. For example, in the case of a monotonic loading path, it is written:

${\sigma}_{w}=\sqrt[m]{\sum_{p}{({\sigma}_{I}^{p})}^{m}\frac{{V}_{p}}{{V}_{0}}}$ .                                    eq 2.1-2

The summation is carried over the volumes of material ${V}_{p}$ which have plastified, ${\sigma}_{I}^{p}$ denoting the maximum principal stress in each of these volumes (${V}_{0}$  is a characteristic volume of the material).

### 2.2 2.2    Identification of the parameters

In a very general manner, one considers an experimental database made up of tests of different kinds (type 1, 2, ..., n), each type of test being carried out ${n}_{j}$ times, so that the total number of tests amounts to:

$N=\sum_{j=1}^{j=n}{n}_{j}$ .

This experimental database could, for example, be made up of tests on notched axisymmetric specimens of different notch radii, carried out at different temperatures. Given the random nature of the fracture properties of the material considered, this database constitutes only a sample. The larger the number of these samples, the more representative it will be of the behaviour of the material considered.

Among the various identification methods proposed in the literature (see for example [bib2]), two are retained: the linear-regression method, often used, and the maximum-likelihood method recommended by the "European Structural Integrity Society (ESIS)" [bib3].

**Note** :

A systematic comparative study of the results given by these two methods [bib2] as a function of the number of samples drawn at random from a theoretical distribution has shown that the maximum-likelihood method leads to a better estimate of the parameters of the WEIBULL model. The linear-regression method nevertheless remaining very widely used, it has been incorporated into the developments.

In both calibration methods retained, a first computation of the WEIBULL stresses is carried out with a given set of parameters (typically, $m=20$ , ${s}_{u}=3000\text{MPa}$ ). These N tests are ranked by means of their WEIBULL stress reached at the moment of failure. One therefore has an increasing list of WEIBULL stresses $({\sigma}_{w}^{1}\text{,...,}{\sigma}_{w}^{i}\text{,...,}{\sigma}_{w}^{N})$ , such that for each $(i)$ , the number of specimens broken with a WEIBULL stress less than or equal to ${\sigma}_{w}^{i}$ is ${n}_{w}^{i}$ (in general ${n}_{w}^{i}=i$ ). Among the various possible estimators of the cumulative failure probability ${P}_{f}^{i}$ corresponding to ${\sigma}_{w}^{i}$ [bib2], the one generally recommended is chosen: ${P}_{f}^{i}=\frac{i}{N+1}$ .

**Note** :

*In the particular case in which the WEIBULL stress depends on the temperature, the preceding ranking must be carried out temperature by temperature, each temperature corresponding to a different statistical law. The preceding estimator of the failure probability therefore becomes:* ${P}_{f}^{i}=\frac{i}{{N}_{T}+1}$ *, if specimen* $(i)$ *was broken at temperature* $T$ *, for which there were* ${N}_{T}$ *tests.*

The two calibration methods retained are valid so long as [eq 2.1-1] remains true. If the identification is carried out on results of non-isothermal tests whereas the cleavage stress is assumed to depend on the temperature, this condition is no longer satisfied (cf. POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22)). In this particular case, one will therefore not be able to apply the developments that follow.

## 3 3    Linear-regression method

### 3.1 3.1.1    Principle

The theory–experiment discrepancy is measured by the expression:

$\sum_{i}{(\text{LogLog}(\frac{1}{1-{P}_{f}^{i}})-\text{LogLog}(\frac{1}{1-{P}_{f}({\sigma}_{W}^{i})}))}^{2}$                 eq 3.1.1-1

("Log" denotes the natural logarithm). One wishes to minimise this discrepancy with respect to (m, ${\sigma}_{u}$ ).

### 3.2 3.1.2    Solution

The calibration method usually used relies on successive linear regressions: at iteration $(k)$ , the values (${m}_{k},{\sigma}_{u(k)}$ ) of the modulus and of the cleavage stress are known. It is therefore possible, with these values, to compute the WEIBULL stresses ${\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}$ at the various failure time instants by means of [eq 2.1-2]. These new WEIBULL stresses are then ranked by increasing amplitude and the new estimates of the failure probability ${P}_{f(k)}^{i}$ at iteration $(k)$ are deduced from them. For these fixed values of the WEIBULL stresses, the minimisation of [eq 3.1.1-1] reduces to a simple linear regression on the cloud of points ($\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})$ , $\text{LogLog}(\frac{1}{1-{P}_{f(k)}^{i}})$ ), since if one plots $\text{LogLog}(\frac{1}{1-{P}_{f}})$ as a function of $\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W})$ , one obtains a straight line of slope m which intersects the abscissa axis at ($\text{Log}({\sigma}_{u})$ ). The new values (${m}_{k+1},{\sigma}_{u(k+1)}$ ) of these parameters are therefore given by (vanishing of the partial derivatives of [eq 3.1.1-1] with respect to each parameter):

${m}_{k+1}=\frac{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}{X}_{i(k)}{Y}_{j(k)}-\sum_{i}{Y}_{i(k)}{X}_{i(k)}}{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i,j}{X}_{i(k)}{X}_{j(k)}-\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k{)}^{2}}}$                     eq 3.1.2-1

${\sigma}_{u(k+1)}=\exp(\frac{1}{N}(\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k)}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i}{Y}_{i(k)}))$ ,                eq 3.1.2-2

with ${X}_{i(k)}=\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})$ and ${Y}_{i(k)}=\text{LogLog}(\frac{1}{1-{P}_{f(k)}^{i}})$ .

These iterations are repeated so long as the difference between the sets of parameters obtained at iterations (k) and (k+1) is significant (typically, five iterations). The measure of this discrepancy is given by: $\text{Max}\left[∣\frac{{m}_{k+1}-{m}_{k}}{{m}_{k}}∣,∣\frac{{\sigma}_{u(k+1)}-{\sigma}_{u(k)}}{{\sigma}_{u(k)}}∣\right]$ .

**Note** :

*If* $m$ *is fixed,* ${\sigma}_{u(k+1)}$ is still given by [eq 3.1.2-2]. On the other hand, if ${\sigma}_{u}$ *is fixed,* ${m}_{k+1}$ is no longer given by [eq 3.1.2-1] but: ${m}_{k+1}=\frac{\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k)}{Y}_{i(k)}}{\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k{)}^{2}}-\log({\sigma}_{u})\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k)}}$ *.*

## 4 4    Maximum-likelihood method

### 4.1 4.1    Principle

Let ${p}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})$ denote the probability density associated with the cumulative failure probability ${P}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})$ :

${p}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})=\frac{m}{{\sigma}_{u}}{(\frac{{s}_{w}}{{\sigma}_{u}})}^{m-1}\exp\left[-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{w}}{{\sigma}_{u}})}^{m}\right]$

The quantity ${p}_{f}({\sigma}_{w})d{\sigma}_{W}$ is equal to the probability of breaking a specimen subjected to a loading corresponding to a WEIBULL stress lying in the interval $\left[{\sigma}_{W},{\sigma}_{W}+d{\sigma}_{W}\right]$ . The probability that all the specimens of the database have broken therefore amounts to:

$p(m,{\sigma}_{u})d{\sigma}_{w}=\prod_{i}{p}_{f}({\sigma}_{W}^{i})d{\sigma}_{w}$ ,                            eq 4.1-1

p being the likelihood function. The maximum-likelihood method then consists in choosing the parameters of the WEIBULL model so that the likelihood function defined by [eq 4.1-1] (in practice rather its natural logarithm) is maximal.

### 4.2 4.2    Solution

An iterative procedure is again used. Here again, at iteration $(k)$ , (${m}_{k},{\sigma}_{u(k)}$ ) as well as the ${\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}$ are known. For these fixed values of the WEIBULL stresses, the maximisation of $\text{Log}(p)$ leads to a new pair (${m}_{k+1},{\sigma}_{u(k+1)}$ ) given by:

$f({m}_{k+1})=\frac{N}{{m}_{k+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})-N\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}{)}^{{m}_{k+1}}\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}{({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})}^{{m}_{k+1}}}=0$             eq 4.2-1

${\sigma}_{(k+1)}=\sqrt[{m}_{k+1}]{\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}{)}^{{m}_{k+1}}}$ .                            eq 4.2-2

At each step, the solution of [eq 4.2-1] may be carried out by means of Newton's method, the gradient of $f(m)$ being given by:

$\frac{\text{df}}{\text{dm}}(m)\text{=-}N\left(\frac{1}{{m}^{2}}+\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W}^{i}{)}^{m}{\text{Log}}^{2}({\sigma}_{W}^{i})\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W}^{i}{)}^{m}\right)-{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W}^{i}{)}^{m}\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W}^{i})\right)}^{2}}{{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}({\sigma}_{W}^{i}{)}^{m}\right)}^{2}}\right)$ .

**Note** :

*If* $m$ *is fixed,* ${\sigma}_{u(k+1)}$ is given by [4.2-2]. On the other hand, if ${\sigma}_{u}$ *is fixed,* ${m}_{k+1}$ is no longer the solution of [4.2-1] but of:

$f({m}_{k+1})=\frac{N}{{m}_{k+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}\text{Log}(\frac{{\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}}{{\sigma}_{u}})(1-(\frac{{\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}}{{\sigma}_{u}}{)}^{{m}_{k+1}})=0$ *.*

*This equation may again be solved by means of Newton's method, the gradient now being given by:*

$\frac{\text{df}}{\text{dm}}(m)\text{=-}\frac{N}{{m}^{2}}-\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}(\frac{{\sigma}_{W}^{i}}{{\sigma}_{u}}{)}^{m}{\text{Log}}^{2}(\frac{{\sigma}_{W}^{i}}{{\sigma}_{u}})$ *.*

## 5 5    Dependence of the parameters on the temperature

If one wishes to calibrate the two parameters independently, temperature by temperature, it is sufficient to split the test database into as many sub-databases by temperature and to apply the preceding methods to each of these sub-databases. If, on the other hand, one wishes only to make the cleavage stress ${\sigma}_{u}$ vary with the temperature, one proceeds as follows.

### 5.1 5.1    Linear regression

The estimation of the failure probabilities now being carried out temperature by temperature (cf. note of [§2.2]), it is sufficient to calibrate the cleavage stress on each cloud of points associated with the different temperatures (T). Equation [eq 3.1.2-2] therefore becomes:

${\sigma}_{u(k+1)}=\exp(\frac{1}{{N}_{T}}(\sum_{i\in T}{X}_{i(k)}-\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i\in T}{Y}_{i(k)}))$

(${N}_{T}$  denoting the number of tests for the sub-database corresponding to temperature (T)), the WEIBULL modulus being given by:

${m}_{k+1}=\frac{\sum_{T}(\frac{1}{{N}_{T}}\sum_{i\in T,j\in T}{X}_{i(k)}{Y}_{j(k)})-\sum_{i}{Y}_{i(k)}{X}_{i(k)}}{\sum_{T}(\frac{1}{{N}_{T}}\sum_{i\in T,j\in T}{X}_{i(k)}{X}_{j(k)})-\sum_{i}{X}_{i(k{)}^{2}}}$ .

### 5.2 5.2    Maximum likelihood

The cleavage stress is given for each temperature (T) considered by:

${\sigma}_{u(k+1)}(T)=\sqrt[{m}_{k+1}]{\frac{1}{{N}_{T}}\sum_{i\in T}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}(T){)}^{{m}_{k+1}}}$ ,

${m}_{k+1}$ being the solution of:

$f({m}_{k+1})=\frac{N}{{m}_{k+1}}+\sum_{i=1}^{i=N}\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})-\sum_{T}{N}_{T}\frac{\sum_{i\in T}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}{)}^{{m}_{k+1}}\text{Log}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i})}{\sum_{i\in T}({\sigma}_{W(k)}^{i}{)}^{{m}_{k+1}}}=0$ .

## 6 6    Conclusion

The command RECA_WEIBULL of *Code_Aster*  makes it possible to carry out the calibration of the parameters of the WEIBULL model [U4.82.06](doc:u4.82.06).

The user gives as input to this command the result concepts associated with the various non-linear analyses carried out. The possible dependence of the cleavage stress on the temperature is implicitly specified when different temperatures are associated with each of these result concepts (if all these temperatures are identical or if they are not specified, there is no dependence on the temperature of this parameter).

The user may carry out this calibration by the maximum-likelihood method (METHODE: ‘MAXI_VRAI’) or that of linear regression (METHODE: ‘REGR_LIN’).

The quantities determined by the command RECA_WEIBULL are reported in a table in which one finds the value of the identified parameters, the failure probabilities estimated from the experimental results, as well as the theoretical failure probabilities computed with the identified parameters.
