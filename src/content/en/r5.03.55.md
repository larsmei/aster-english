# 1 **r5.03.55** LAC method – Local Average Contact

**Summary:**

This document describes the way of treating contact between solids (2D or 3D) in large transformations by a method of «mortar» type. The formulation is very accurate for the contact pressures obtained, in particular when the meshes are not matching or when the surfaces are curved.

This formulation is available in the command DEFI_CONTACT under the name ALGO_CONT='LAC'.

## 1 Introduction

The finite-element method is currently used on a massive scale in computational mechanics to simulate problems of unilateral contact between two deformable objects. These are problems possessing a non-linear boundary condition, namely satisfying a non-interpenetration condition for the displacement field, a sign condition for the normal pressure on the contact zone and a complementarity condition between these two fields. In the general case, such problems lead to the handling of incompatible meshes (i.e. whose nodes of the contact zones of the objects considered are non-matching).

The most widely used methods are based on algorithms of node-segment type in $2D$ and of node-facet type in $3D$ (in code_aster, these are the formulations DISCRETE and CONTINUE in mode INTEGRATION='NOEUD'). These methods are known to fail the patch tests in the general case of incompatible meshes and to lead to suboptimal mathematical analyses. On the other hand, the academic community has been very productive in this field over the last fifteen years. The most accomplished methods are the **mortar**  methods adapted to contact.

These methods consist in using a simple projection ${L}^{2}$ onto a mortar space, i.e., a space of finite elements defined between the two objects. The various choices of mortar spaces generate a whole family of methods of mortar type. However these methods have important disadvantages when one considers their implementation in a generic way in an industrial code.

The method proposed here gives numerical and mathematical results of as good quality as those obtained by the mortar methods while keeping the aspects (locality, analytical definition of the mortar spaces, etc...) that make the implementation in *code_aster*  easier while preserving the current architecture of the code

To do this, one will define a simple contact condition which consists in satisfying the contact conditions on average on each macro-element of a macro-mesh defined in a suitable way (which will have to be prepared by means of the option 'DECOUPE_LAC' of the operator CREA_MAILLAGE).

#### 2.1.2 Weak formulation

Starting from the equations () to (), one can introduce a weak formulation of the contact problem in the form of the following variational inequality:

$$
\textrm{Trouver}

u

\textrm{tel que :math:`\forall v\in K` ,}

{\sum}_{l=1}^{2}{\int}_{{\Omega}^{l}}{A}_{l}\epsilon ({u}_{l}):\epsilon ({v}_{l}-{u}_{l})d{\Omega}^{l}⩾{\sum}_{l=1}^{2}{\int}_{{\Omega}^{l}}{f}_{l}\cdot ({v}_{l}-{u}_{l})d{\Omega}^{l}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{N}^{l}}{F}_{l}\cdot {({v}_{l}-{u}_{l})}_{N}d{\Gamma}^{l}
$$

where $K\subset V$ is the convex cone of the admissible displacements ($K$  contains the non-interpenetration condition). One therefore uses the following spaces:

$$
{V}_{l}=\lbrace {v}_{l}\in {({H}^{1}({\Omega}^{l}))}^{d}:v=0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{D}^{l}\rbrace

V={V}_{1}\times {V}_{2}

K=\lbrace v\in V:[{v}_{N}]\le 0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{C}\rbrace
$$

One will denote subsequently:

$$
a(u,v)={\sum}_{l=1}^{2}{\int}_{{\Omega}^{l}}{A}_{l}\epsilon ({u}_{l}):\epsilon ({v}_{l})d{\Omega}^{l}
$$

And:

$$
l(v)=\sum_{l=1}^{2}{\int}_{{\Omega}^{l}}{f}_{l}\cdot {v}_{l}d{\Omega}^{l}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{N}^{l}}{F}_{l}\cdot {v}_{l}d{\Gamma}^{l}
$$

One introduces the equivalent mixed problem:

$$
\textrm{Trouver}

u\in V

\textrm{et :math:`\lambda \in M` tels que:}

a(u,v)-b(\lambda ,v)=l(v)\text{}\forall v\in V

b(\mu -\lambda ,u)\ge 0\text{}\forall \mu \in M
$$

With:

$$
b(\mu ,v)={\int}_{{\Gamma}_{C}}\mu [{v}_{N}]d\Gamma
$$

And:

$$
M=\left\lbrace \mu \in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{C}):{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{C}}\mu \Psi d\Gamma \le 0\forall \Psi \in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{C}),\Psi \ge 0\right\rbrace
$$

**Note:** *The problems () and () are well posed. One therefore has existence and uniqueness of the solutions* $(u,\lambda )$ . *Furthermore, the solutions* $u$ *of the problem ()  and* $u$ *of the problem () are identical.*

### 2.2 Discrete problems

#### 2.2.1 Discrete variational inequality

One considers the discrete contact problem from the point of view of the variational inequality (discretisation of the problem):

$$
\textrm{Trouver}

{u}^{h}\in {K}^{h}

\textrm{tel que}

\forall {v}^{h}\in {K}^{h}

a({u}^{h},{v}^{h}-{u}^{h})⩾l({v}^{h}-{u}^{h})
$$

where ${K}^{h}\subset {V}^{h}$ is the convex cone of the admissible displacements, $a$ and $l$ the bilinear and linear forms defined previously.

To define a method, one must choose the discrete spaces ${V}_{l}^{h}$ and the set ${K}^{h}$ . Let ${T}_{l}^{h}$ be a regular triangulation of ${\Omega}^{l}\subset {\mathrm{ℝ}}^{d}$ , where $d=2,3$ . One then defines:

$$
{V}_{l}^{h}=\lbrace {v}_{l}^{h}\in {(C(\overline{{\Omega}^{l}}))}^{d}:{v}_{l}^{h}{\text{|}}_{T}\in {P}_{k}(T),\forall T\in {T}_{l}^{h},{v}_{l}^{h}=0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{D}^{l}\rbrace
$$

where $k=1,2$ , ${V}^{h}={V}_{1}^{h}\times {V}_{2}^{h}$ .

One proposes to use the following contact condition:

$$
{K}^{h}=\left\lbrace {v}^{h}\in {V}^{h}:{\int}_{{T}^{m}}[{v}_{N}^{h}]d\Gamma \le 0\text{}\forall {T}^{m}\in {T}^{M}\right\rbrace
$$

where ${T}^{M}$ is a macro-mesh of elements ${T}^{m}$ to be defined according to the choice of ${V}^{h}$ .

This contact condition is inspired by methods of mortar type while keeping a local aspect. Indeed, the mortar projection matrix is no longer a full matrix because the inverse of the basis matrix of the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ is diagonal. This property ensures that the mortar projection matrices (matrix product between the inverse basis matrix of the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ and the coupling matrices between the mortar space and the approximation spaces) are local. However, one knows that the simple condition of type:

$$
{\int}_{{T}^{h}\cap {\Gamma}_{C}}[{v}_{N}^{h}]d\Gamma \le 0
$$

is not stable for all the elements commonly used in engineering studies (in particular in 3D, where only the hexahedron with 27 nodes is naturally stable), and therefore does not lead to optimal mathematical analyses. However, by slightly widening the integration support, according to the cases, one manages to prove results of optimal convergence and of stability.

#### 2.2.2 Discrete mixed formulation

One introduces the equivalent mixed problem:

$$
\textrm{Trouver}

{u}^{h}\in {V}^{h}

\textrm{et :math:`{\lambda}^{h}\in {M}^{h}` tel que}

a({u}^{h},{v}^{h})-b({\lambda}^{h},{v}^{h})=l({v}^{h})\text{}\forall {v}^{h}\in {V}^{h}

b({\mu}^{h}-{\lambda}^{h},{u}^{h})\ge 0\text{}\forall {\mu}^{h}\in {M}^{h}
$$

with:

$$
{M}^{h}=\lbrace {\mu}^{h}\in {X}_{1}^{h}:{\mu}^{h}\le 0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{C}\rbrace
$$

Where:

$$
{X}_{1}^{h}=\lbrace {\mu}^{h}\in {L}^{2}({\Gamma}_{C}):{\mu}^{h}{\text{|}}_{{T}^{m}}\in {P}^{0}({T}^{m}),\forall {T}^{m}\in {T}^{M}\rbrace
$$

One therefore obtains a «mortar ${P}^{0}$ » method which will be automatically stabilised by the definition of the macro-mesh ${T}^{M}$ .

**Note:** *The contact condition in* ${K}^{h}$ *being fixed, the space of the Lagrange multipliers will always be a subspace of* ${P}^{0}({T}^{m})$ *whatever the type of finite elements used in the approximation space of the displacements* ${V}^{h}$ *. One therefore notes that the order of the contact condition remains fixed* *for every approximation space considered (linear or quadratic); only the definition of the macro-mesh varies in order to ensure the good mathematical properties of the method.*

### 2.3 Mathematical results

We recall here the main mathematical results of the LAC method. The fundamental assumption of the method defines the way of constructing the *macro-mesh*  is the assumption of the internal degree of freedom:

**Assumption of the** internal **degree of freedom:**

For every macro-element ${T}^{m}\in {T}^{M}$ , there exists a degree of freedom ${x}_{i}$ of ${V}_{1}^{h}$ such that $\mathit{supp}({\varphi}_{i})\subset {T}^{m}$ , where ${\phi }_{i}$ is the basis function associated with ${x}_{i}$ .

**Note:** *One notes that there exists a minimum size for the macro mesh cells making it possible both to satisfy the* previous *assumption and to guarantee the locality of the method. One also notes that the choice of the trace mesh used to define the macro-mesh is free; here, one has chosen mesh 1.*

One can now recall the two main convergence results of the LAC method.

**Theorem 1:** *Let* $u$ *and* ${u}^{h}$ *be the solutions of the continuous and discrete contact problems . One assumes that* $u\in ({H}^{s}({\Omega}^{1}){)}^{d}\times ({H}^{s}({\Omega}^{2}){)}^{d}$ *with* $d=2,3$ *and* $3/2<s\le 2$ *(* $3/2\le s<5/2$ *if* $k=2$ *). If the* internal degree-of-freedom *assumption is satisfied, then there exists a constant* $C>0$ *independent of* ${h}_{1}$ *,* ${h}_{2}$ *(where* ${h}_{1}$ *and* ${h}_{2}$ *are the discretisation parameters of the solid* $1$ *and of the solid* $2$ *respectively) and* $u$ *, such that:*

$$
{\Vert u-{u}^{h}\Vert }_{1,{\Omega}^{1},{\Omega}^{2}}\le C({h}_{1}^{s-1}+{h}_{2}^{s-1}){\Vert u\Vert }_{s,{\Omega}^{1},{\Omega}^{2}}
$$

**Theorem 2:** *Let* $(u,\lambda )$ *and* $({u}^{h},{\lambda}^{h})$ *be the solutions of the continuous and discrete contact problems . One assumes that* $u\in ({H}^{s}({\Omega}^{1}){)}^{d}\times ({H}^{s}({\Omega}^{2}){)}^{d}$ *with* $d=2,3$ *and* $3/2<s\le 2$ *(* $3/2<s\le 5/2$ *if* $k=2$ *). If the* internal d.o.f. *assumption is satisfied, then there exists a constant* $C>0$ *independent of* ${h}_{1}$ *,* ${h}_{2}$ *and* $u$ *, such that:*

$$
{\Vert \lambda -{\lambda}^{h}\Vert }_{1/2,\ast ,{\Gamma}_{C}}+{\Vert u-{u}^{h}\Vert }_{1,{\Omega}^{1},{\Omega}^{2}}\le C({h}_{1}^{s-1}+{h}_{2}^{s-1}){\Vert u\Vert }_{s,{\Omega}^{1},{\Omega}^{2}}
$$

*where* ${\Vert .\Vert }_{1/2,\ast ,{\Gamma}_{C}}$ *is the dual norm of* ${\Vert .\Vert }_{1/2,{\Gamma}_{C}}$ *.*

**Note:** *In the remainder of this document one will adopt the following convention: one will call slave surface the surface on which one will have defined the macro mesh cells, that is the surface carrying the contact Lagrange multipliers in the case of the mixed formulation . The surface facing this last one will be the master surface.*

### 2.4 Matrix formulation

One considers the following matrices ${C}^{E}\in {M}_{e,k}(\mathrm{ℝ})$ , ${C}^{M}\in {M}_{m,k}(\mathrm{ℝ})$ and ${M}^{\mathit{LAC}}\in {M}_{k}(\mathrm{ℝ})$ , where ${M}_{i,j}$ corresponds to the space of rectangular matrices of size $i\times j$ and ${M}_{i}={M}_{i,i}$ corresponds to the space of square matrices of size $i$ , defined by:

$$
{C}_{ij}^{E}={\int}_{{T}_{j}}{\left({\varphi}_{N}^{E}\right)}_{i}d\Gamma

{C}_{ij}^{M}={\int}_{{T}_{j}}{\left({\varphi}_{N}^{M}\right)}_{i}d\Gamma

{M}_{ij}^{\mathit{LAC}}={\delta}_{ij}\text{|}{T}^{i}\text{|}
$$

where the ${T}^{i}$ are the supports of the basis functions of ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ , the ${\varphi}_{N}^{E}$ are the products of the normal with the basis functions associated with the slave surface and the ${\varphi}_{N}^{M}$ are the products of the normal with the basis functions associated with the master surface.

Let $U$ be the displacement field and $\Lambda$ the Lagrange multiplier; the matrix formulation of the problem is the following:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{K}_{\mathit{NN}}& {K}_{\mathit{NM}}& {K}_{\mathit{NE}}& 0\\ {K}_{\mathit{MN}}& {K}_{\mathit{MM}}& {K}_{\mathit{ME}}& {C}^{M}\\ {K}_{\mathit{EN}}& {K}_{\mathit{EM}}& {K}_{\mathit{EE}}& {C}^{E}\end{array}\right]\times \left[\begin{array}{c}{U}_{N}\\ {U}_{M}\\ {U}_{E}\\ \Lambda \end{array}\right]=F
$$

With the following conditions:

$$
{M}^{{\mathit{LAC}}^{-1}}({\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{E}{U}_{E}+{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{M}{U}_{M})⩽0

\textrm{dans :math:`{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}` ,}

{M}^{\mathit{LAC}}\Lambda ⩽0

\textrm{dans :math:`{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}` ,}

{M}^{{\mathit{LAC}}^{-1}}({\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{E}{U}_{E}+{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{M}{U}_{M})\cdot {M}^{\mathit{LAC}}\Lambda =0

\textrm{dans :math:`\mathrm{ℝ}` .}
$$

Noting that ${M}^{\mathit{LAC}}$ is diagonal positive definite, one has equivalence with the following formulation:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{K}_{\mathit{NN}}& {K}_{\mathit{NM}}& {K}_{\mathit{NE}}& 0\\ {K}_{\mathit{MN}}& {K}_{\mathit{MM}}& {K}_{\mathit{ME}}& {C}^{M}\\ {K}_{\mathit{EN}}& {K}_{\mathit{EM}}& {K}_{\mathit{EE}}& {C}^{E}\end{array}\right]\times \left[\begin{array}{c}{U}_{N}\\ {U}_{M}\\ {U}_{E}\\ \Lambda \end{array}\right]=F
$$

With the following conditions:

$$
{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{E}{U}_{E}+{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{M}{U}_{M}⩽0

\textrm{dans :math:`{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}` ,}

\Lambda ⩽0

\textrm{dans :math:`{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}` ,}

{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{E}{U}_{E}+{\rbrace ^{t}{C}^{M}{U}_{M}\cdot {M}^{\mathit{LAC}}\Lambda =0

\textrm{dans :math:`\mathrm{ℝ}` .}
$$

One solves the problem ()-() using an active-set strategy (active set) coupled with a Newton algorithm.

## 3 Implementation of the method

This chapter presents the implementation of the «LAC» method (Local Average Contact) in *code_aster* . One will discuss more particularly the implementation choices linked to the new contact condition, avoiding the creation of new mesh-cell types in *code_aster*  and fitting into the already existing formalism for the management of contact. It is therefore appropriate, for this first version of the implementation of the method, to adopt the meshed-contact strategy (one will define late contact elements making it possible to carry out the elementary computations of the contact contributions) and a generalised Newton type approach.

The operators affected by the method are:

- CREA_MAILLAGE;
- DEFI_CONTACT;
- STAT_NON_LINE.

The proposed method implies the following developments:

- A modification of the mesh on the slave contact zone (non-invasive in the volume) in the operator CREA_MAILLAGE;
- A redefinition of the contact Lagrange multipliers, implementation of the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ in the operator DEFI_CONTACT;
- A new pairing of «segment-segment» type in 2D and of «face-face» type in 3D, a new evaluation of the contact statuses and a new elementary computation, with an impact in the operator STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE.

### 3.1 Preparation of the mesh

#### 3.1.1 Principles of the cutting

The theoretical basis imposes that we satisfy the assumption of the internal degree of freedom (see § 2.3 ). For the sake of simplicity, of consistency between 2D and 3D, and in order not to «coarsen» the contact condition, one will use the local refinement method in the 2D and 3D cases. The factor keyword DECOUPE_LAC of the operator makes it possible to prepare this macro-mesh. In certain situations, the keyword will cut the surface mesh cells; in others, this will not be necessary. When one cuts a surface element one therefore arranges not to *propagate*  this cut into the volume. This strategy makes it possible to limit the creation of too many mesh cells but can produce mesh cells of poor quality.

Likewise, in order not to modify the user's initial choices, one will always propose a **conforming** subdivision: a triangle will be cut into triangles, a hexahedron into hexahedra, etc.

#### 3.1.2 The 2D case

In the 2D case, the slave contact interface is «1D». The latter is meshed either in SEG2 or in SEG3. First, one notes that the SEG3 associated with itself (macro mesh cell) satisfies the internal d.o.f. assumption; it will therefore not be necessary to cut the mesh cells having SEG3 on the skin (TRIA6, QUAD8 and QUAD9). In the SEG2 case, there exist two cuttings according to the type of the associated body mesh cell. In the TRIA3 case, one adds a node at the centre of the SEG2 mesh cell and one reconstructs the two new skin elements and the two new body elements. In the QUAD4 case. Given that one wishes to proceed to a conforming subdivision, one adds two nodes in the SEG2 mesh cell, and two nodes in the QUAD4 mesh cell. One can then reconstruct the three new skin elements and the four new body elements while supplying the information characterising the macro mesh cell. #### 3.1.4 Some remarks on the cutting

The DECOUPE_LAC feature of the operator is capable of treating:

- different types of mesh cell inside the same contact zone;
- the multi-zone contact.

Concerning the complexity of the computations on the new mesh one notes that one adds a certain number of nodes which will carry d.o.f. and potentially contact Lagrange multipliers. Let M be the number of mesh cells of the slave surface of the contact zone considered; then for (body) meshes in:

- TRIA3 one adds $M$ nodes and $2M$ mesh cells,
- QUAD4 one adds $4M$ nodes and $5M$ mesh cells,
- TETRA4 one adds $M$ nodes and $4M$ mesh cells,
- TETRA10 one adds $5M$ nodes and $4M$ mesh cells,
- HEXA8 (version 1) one adds $8M$ nodes and $9M$ mesh cells,
- HEXA20 (version 1) one adds $28M$ nodes and $9M$ mesh cells.

Furthermore the aspect ratio and the configuration of the generated mesh cells may possibly disturb the post-processing of certain data on the slave surface (skin elements and body elements in contact).

### 3.2 Preparation of the contact

The preparation of the contact data is ensured by the operator DEFI_CONTACT. This operator must take into account the new definition of the contact Lagrange multipliers, i.e. the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ . One must add for each macro mesh cell of the LAC surface a contact Lagrange multiplier (see figure ) knowing that it is not possible for **code_aster** to assign a degree of freedom to a physical entity other than a node. One will present here a device making it possible to define the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ locally on each computation mesh cell provided that one has an intelligent assembly of the matrix system.

Each degree of freedom $\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C$ is indeed common only to three elements, the three elements making up the macro mesh cell. Given that the basis function ${P}^{0}$ associated with the contact Lagrange multiplier does not depend on the geometric vertices of the macro mesh cell, one can split the elementary computation on the macro mesh cell into an elementary computation on each mesh cell composing it. During assembly, one sums the contributions of each mesh cell underlying the macro mesh cell. Therefore by assigning to each degree of freedom $\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C$ a basis function that is locally constant on the element, one indeed obtains that the degrees of freedom $\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C$ correspond to the space ${P}^{0}({T}^{M})$ .

### 3.3 Solution of the contact problem

There are three main impacts:

- One must have a pairing of «segment-segment» type in 2D and of «face-face» type in 3D;
- One must compute the contact matrices ;
- One must develop an intelligent assembly of the global system (handling of the second difficulty linked to the device in DEFI_CONTACT, see § 3.2.2 ).

The first two points require an efficient and robust tool for the intersection of mesh cells in the case of straight mesh cells (linear and quadratic) and in the case of curved mesh cells (quadratic).

#### 3.3.1 Algorithm for the solution of the non-linearities linked to contact

One opts for the use of an algorithm of generalised Newton type, handling the geometric non-linearity, the active-set type strategy for the contact non-linearity and the constitutive non-linearity inside the same Newton loop. This algorithmic framework is already present in *code_aster*  (see [R5.03.52](doc:r5.03.52)). It will therefore be sufficient to replace all the internal building blocks of the algorithm in order to implement the new method. One sets:

$$
U=\left[\begin{array}{c}{U}_{N}\\ {U}_{M}\\ {U}_{E}\end{array}\right]

\textrm{;}

K=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{NN}& {K}_{NM}& {K}_{NE}\\ {K}_{MN}& {K}_{MM}& {K}_{ME}\\ {K}_{EN}& {K}_{EM}& {K}_{EE}\end{array}\right]` ; :math:`C=\left[\begin{array}{c}0\\ {C}^{M}\\ {C}^{E}\end{array}\right]
$$

Taking into account the initial «integrated gap» $\overline{{G}_{0}}$ (see the definition in §), the problem is equivalent to:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\left[\begin{array}{cc}K& C\end{array}\right]\times \left[\begin{array}{c}U\\ \Lambda \end{array}\right]=F\\ {\rbrace ^{t}\mathit{CU}⩽\overline{{G}_{0}}\text{dans}{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}\\ \Lambda ⩽0\text{dans}{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{k}\\ {\rbrace ^{t}\mathit{CU}\cdot \Lambda =0\text{dans}\mathrm{ℝ}\end{array}
$$

One introduces the following function:

$$
A(U,\Lambda )=\Lambda +\max(0,\rho (CU-\overline{{G}_{0}})-\Lambda )
$$

One can then show that is equivalent to:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}K& C\end{array}\right]\times \left[\begin{array}{c}U\\ \Lambda \end{array}\right]=F

\textrm{et}

A(U,\Lambda )=0
$$

The solution of the contact non-linearity then amounts to the search for the active constraints. Contact is active when the status of the point is active, which one will denote $S{\Lambda}_{i}=1$ . One obtains the following global algorithm:

- Initial pairing: computation of $\overline{{G}_{0}}$
- Loop over the Lagrange multipliers $i=1..\mathit{dim}(\Lambda )$

  - If $\overline{{{G}_{0}}_{i}}⩽0$ then the point is not in contact, hence ${\Lambda}_{i}^{0}=0$
  - Otherwise, the point is in contact, hence ${\Lambda}_{i}^{0}=1$
- Newton loop $k=1$

  - While { $S{\Lambda}^{k}\ne S{\Lambda}^{k+1},\forall k$  and ${\Vert r\Vert }_{2}⩾\mathit{tole}$ (Newton equilibrium residual)

    - Computation of the elementary stiffness matrices and right-hand sides
    - Computation of the elementary contact matrices and right-hand sides
    - Loop over the Lagrange multipliers $i=1..\mathit{dim}(\Lambda )$

      - If $S{\Lambda}_{i}^{k}=1$ then $\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& {C}_{k}^{E}& 0\\ {\rbrace ^{t}{C}_{k}^{E}& 0& {\rbrace ^{t}{C}_{k}^{M}\\ 0& {C}_{k}^{M}& 0\end{array}\right]$
      - Otherwise $\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& \mathit{Id}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$
  - Assembly of the global system and solution:

    - $\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{J}_{k}^{K}& {C}_{k}& 0\\ {\rbrace ^{t}{C}_{k}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \mathit{Id}\end{array}\right]\times \left[\begin{array}{c}\Delta {U}_{k}\\ \Delta {\Lambda}_{k}^{a}\\ \Delta {\Lambda}_{k}^{\mathit{na}}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{c}{r}_{k}\\ \overline{{G}_{k}}\\ -\Delta {\Lambda}_{k-1}\end{array}\right]$ .
  - Update of the unknowns:

    - ${U}_{k+1}={U}_{k}+\Delta {U}_{k}$ and ${\Lambda}_{k+1}={\Lambda}_{k}+\Delta {\Lambda}_{k}$
  - Pairing on the updated geometry, computation of $\overline{{G}_{k+1}}$
  - Evaluation of the new statuses:

    - Loop over the Lagrange multipliers $i=1..\mathit{dim}(\Lambda )$

      - If ${{\Lambda}_{k+1}}_{i}+\rho {{G}_{k+1}}_{i}⩽0$ then the point is in contact, hence $S{\Lambda}_{i}^{k+1}=1$
      - Otherwise, the point is not in contact, hence $S{\Lambda}_{i}^{k+1}=0$
  - Computation of the Newton residual ${r}_{k+1}$

#### 3.3.2 Pairing

The aim of this phase is to handle the geometric non-linearity of the problem and that arising from possible large displacements. For each slave contact mesh cell, one seeks the master mesh cells likely to communicate with the slave mesh cell. One must also, during this operation, compute the mean «gap» between the two surfaces for each macro mesh cell. To do this, one will use four tools:

- A search tool of linear complexity using the neighbourhood information at the level of the edges;
- A tool for the projection of two mesh cells onto the same reference plane (reference parametric space of the slave mesh cell);
- A tool for the intersection of plane mesh cells;
- A tool for the computation of the mean distance between a triangle and a surface (3D case) or between two segments (2D case).

##### 3.3.2.1 Search algorithm of linear complexity

One adapts to the contact case the PANG algorithm for the computation of the mortar projection matrix for domain decomposition. This algorithm is of linear complexity once a starting pair of mesh cells has been found. It uses a restricted search on the neighbouring master mesh cells of the master mesh cells intersecting a slave mesh cell. Furthermore it uses the neighbourhood information at the level of the slave mesh cells to update the list of starting pairs of mesh cells.

This algorithm allows an important gain in time with respect to a pairing of brute-force type (i.e. a double master–slave pairing loop). However it lacks robustness. Indeed, in the case of a pairing zone that does not possess a single connected component, it fails. One then detects only one of the pairing zones and the contact algorithm is consequently put in failure. One proposes a more robust but more costly version of the initial algorithm by associating with it a recursive search for a starting pair of mesh cells by brute force.

##### 3.3.2.2 Projection tool

This tool is already available in **code_aster** . Indeed, one of the tools of the pairing of the CONTINUE method (see [R5.03.52](doc:r5.03.52)) performs the orthogonal projection of a point of real space into the parametric space of a given mesh cell. One will therefore be able to project any master mesh cell into the parametric space of the slave mesh cell in order to carry out the intersection test. One notes two important points:

- One restricts oneself to the case where the projection of the master mesh cell into the slave parametric space is **convex** ;
- Given that one performs the projection into the parametric space of the master mesh cell, one will carry out only a purely $1D$ or $2D$ intersection.

Furthermore, another routine is available in **code_aster** : the projection along an imposed direction. One notes that this projection algorithm will be useful for the mean «gap» tool.

$$
{\overline{G}}_{\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C}={\overline{G}}_{\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C}+\sum_{i=1}^{N}{d}_{i}{\omega}_{i}\Vert ({\partial}_{1}\sigma \times {\partial}_{2}\sigma )({\mathit{Pg}}_{i}){\Vert }_{2}
$$

where ${d}_{i}$ is the signed gap at the i-th Gauss point (${\mathit{Pg}}_{i}$ ), ${\omega}_{i}$ is the weight associated with the i-th Gauss point in the reference space of the master mesh cell of the contact mesh cell, $\sigma ` est la transformation de passage élément de référence élément réel et :math:`N$ is the number of Gauss points defined on the intersection.

By iteration over the contact mesh cells, one obtains the vector $\overline{G}$ whose dimension corresponds to the number of degrees of freedom $\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C$ ; in our example there is a single component in the integrated «gap» vector.

In the three-dimensional case, a tool for the triangulation of a convex polygon is necessary. Indeed, it is easier to compute the $\overline{G}$ piecewise on a set of triangles (simpler definition of the quadrature formulae than on an arbitrary polygon).

To finish the operation it suffices to divide each component of the integrated «gap» vector by the intersection weight found on the associated macro mesh cell.

##### 3.3.2.4 Handling of quadratic meshes

In the curved quadratic case, one cannot approximate the geometry of the master mesh cell in the slave parametric space. To simplify the algorithm, one will use a simple linearisation in place of the decomposition of the mesh cell into linear elements commonly used in the literature of mortar methods for contact. The slave geometry is for its part approximated exactly by its parametric space. Unlike the current approach consisting in using an auxiliary intersection plane (defined from the slave surface), one has chosen to carry out all the projection and intersection operations in the slave parametric space. This choice allows both the use of a pairing tolerance criterion independent of the modelling and the carrying out of only purely $2D$ or $1D$ intersections. By using this approach and by controlling the quality of the mesh, one prevents the appearance of the severe pathological cases that lead to fatal errors or to a non-convergence of the generalised Newton algorithm. Furthermore, unlike the classical mortar methods $P2/P2$ the LAC method is not confronted with the sign problem with respect to the basis functions of the space of the Lagrange multipliers. Attention must therefore be paid mainly at the level of the quality of the mesh, and more particularly of the mesh of the slave surface. Indeed, the algorithmic choices implemented in this first version of the implementation of the method do not take into account the case of non-convex intersections. If the slave mesh cells do not satisfy this constraint, one obtains irrelevant intersections; one then observes aberrant intersection coefficients (greater than one), which leads to a failure of convergence of the algorithm for the solution of the problem.

#### 3.3.3 Computation of the contact matrices

The computation of the elementary contact matrices is driven by the status field $\mathit{S\Lambda }$ . If the constraint associated with the macro-element $T$ is active, then one must compute the following contributions:

$$
{C}_{i}^{{M}_{\mathit{elem}}}={\int}_{T}{\phi }_{i}^{M}{\vec{n}}_{m}d\Gamma =\sum_{{M}_{c}}{\int}_{{M}_{m}\cap {M}_{e}}{\phi }_{i}^{M}{\vec{n}}_{m}d\Gamma

{C}_{i}^{{E}_{\mathit{elem}}}={\int}_{T}{\phi }_{i}^{E}{\vec{n}}_{e}d\Gamma =\sum_{{M}_{c}}{\int}_{{M}_{m}\cap {M}_{e}}{\phi }_{i}^{E}{\vec{n}}_{e}d\Gamma
$$

where ${\phi }^{E}$ and ${\phi }^{M}$ are the slave and master basis functions, ${\vec{n}}_{e}$ and ${\vec{n}}_{m}$ the respective normals, ${M}_{c}$ the set of contact mesh cells, and ${M}_{E}$ the set of slave mesh cells. If the contact constraint associated with the macro-element $T$ is inactive then there are no contact conditions to be satisfied on this macro-element and one sets to zero the associated degree of freedom $\mathit{LAGS}\text{\_}C$ . Given that the macro mesh cell $T$ does not exist as an element, one will compute «sub-elementary» matrices at the level of the contact mesh cells ${M}_{c}$ (association of a slave and a master contact mesh cell).

##### 3.3.3.1 Computation of the elementary contact matrices

One in fact computes the elementary contact matrices ${C}^{{M}_{\mathit{elem}}}$ corresponding to the integrals under the loop over the contact mesh cells (see equations). For each contact mesh cell, one must compute the following matrices:

$$
{C}_{i}^{{M}_{\mathit{elem}}}={\int}_{{M}_{m}\cap {M}_{e}}{\phi }_{i}^{M}{\vec{n}}_{m}\mathit{d\Gamma }

{C}_{i}^{{E}_{\mathit{elem}}}={\int}_{{M}_{m}\cap {M}_{e}}{\phi }_{i}^{E}{\vec{n}}_{e}\mathit{d\Gamma }
$$

where ${M}_{e}$ is the slave mesh cell of the contact mesh cell and ${M}_{m}$ is the master mesh cell of the contact mesh cell. Given that the chosen contact condition corresponds to a zero gap only on average, one must proceed to a projection-intersection in order to define the integration support. To do this, one reuses the tools presented in the previous section. Following the scheme of the integrated-gap tool, one manages to obtain the coordinates and weights of a quadrature formula in the parametric space of the slave mesh cell of the contact mesh cell. By looping over the degrees of freedom of the master displacements, one can then compute the coefficients of the matrix ${C}_{i}^{{E}_{\mathit{elem}}}$ . Then by using the projection onto the parametric space of the master mesh cell of the contact mesh cell one then obtains the integration support in the reference space of the master mesh cell, which makes it possible to compute (by defining an integration scheme) the coefficients of the matrix ${C}_{i}^{{M}_{\mathit{elem}}}$ .

##### 3.3.3.2 Choice of the normal

The question of the choice of the normal has not yet been addressed. This choice not being unique, one proposes to use in our case two types of normal. Either one evaluates the normal at each master and slave Gauss point, or one uses a field of smoothed normals (keyword LISSAGE='OUI') computed on each deformed configuration (master and slave). These two choices make it possible to avoid the problem of the multiple definition of the normal at the vertices of the elements when one considers curved geometries. In the quadratic case, these two choices are relatively similar; on the contrary in the case of linear meshes, the smoothed normal field makes it possible to regularise the field of the normals and to approximate curved geometries better, see figure .
