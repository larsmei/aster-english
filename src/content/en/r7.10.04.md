# r7.10.04 Post-processing according to the Roche method

**Summary:**

The operator [POST_ROCHE](doc:u4.84.46) is a post-processing of purely linear elastic analyses, which uses the Roche method to evaluate the response of a piping line under an elastoplastic constitutive behaviour.

## 1 Introduction

The Roche method makes it possible to evaluate the response relating to an elastoplastic constitutive behaviour of a pressurised piping line, from the results of linear elastic analyses to which abatement coefficients are applied. The mechanical loadings considered are: the inertial earthquake, the displacements imposed at the anchors, the thermal expansion and the weight. The objective of this approach is to reduce significantly the run times compared with a classical non-linear solution approach for the seismic analyses.

The abatement coefficients are computed from spring-effect factors. The linear elastic response, reduced following the application of the abatement coefficients, is deemed to provide a conservative estimate of the non-linear response. The abatement coefficients are applicable only to the moments depending on the displacements, according to the CMS2 classification of the RCC-MRx; they are not applicable to the moments depending on the forces.

Two variants of the macro-command [POST_ROCHE](doc:u4.84.46) are proposed:

- The RCC-MRX variant, in accordance with the codification, for which the determination of the abatement coefficients of the moments coming from the linear elastic flexibility analyses of the piping lines follows the specifications of chapters RB 3651.1132 and RB 3651.1142 of the RCC-MRx (post-spectral Roche method),
- The ASNR variant, in accordance with the validation file of the Roche method supplied to the ASNR in 2024.

The output quantities (grandeurs), functions of the curvilinear abscissa, are:

- the optimised monotonic and seismic abatement coefficients,
- the components of the abated moment, and its norm, relative to the set of the loadings considered,
- the abated equivalent stress, taking into account the abated moments and the non-abated moments, relative to the set of the loadings considered.

The geometries concerned are:

- the pipes of constant diameter and of variable diameter (reducers),
- the single-run lines and the branched lines (including tees).

## 2 Input data

The input data of the operator [POST_ROCHE](doc:u4.84.46) are:

The data recovered from the results of the previous analyses:

- the geometry of the piping line,
- the field of characteristics of the beam elements,
- the elastic characteristics of the material,
- the flexibility coefficients of the elbows,

The specific data:

- the material field `CHAM_MATER`: it is the map of the materials assigned to the mesh-cell groups of the mesh by [AFFE_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.03), whose parameters of the Ramberg-Osgood curve ($K$ and $n$ ) as well as other parameters in the case LIMITE_ADM=’OUI’, these data are supplied in [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01) (keyword `POST_ROCHE` or `POST_ROCHE_FO`); if `POST_ROCHE_FO`, declaration of a temperature field via the keyword `AFFE_VARC` of the operator [AFFE_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.03),
- the tension curve of the material, a function defined under `TRAC_EPSI` describing the strain as a function of the stress,
- the orientations of the local frames (in [AFFE_CARA_ELEM](doc:u4.42.01), keyword `ORIENTATION`) for the bent parts, making it possible to separate the in-plane and out-of-plane bending moments (see sdll157a),
- the angles and radii of curvature of the elbows,
- the internal pressure $P$ imposed on the mesh-cell groups,
- the list of the mechanical loadings among: non-seismic imposed displacements DINS, seismic differential displacements DDS, thermal expansion, self-weight, inertial spectral earthquake,
- the results of the analyses for each mechanical loading: field by elements at the nodes of generalised forces (`EFGE_ELNO`).

## 3 Analysis stages

In order to determine the reversibilities and the spring-effect factors, one uses a plastic constitutive law according to Ramberg-Osgood, previously fitted to the monotonic tension law. The projection of the linear elastic stress is carried out onto the real monotonic tension curve of the material.

### 3.1 Establishment of the curve according to Ramberg-Osgood, representative of the monotonic tension curve of the material

A Ramberg-Osgood curve must previously be fitted to the real tension curve of the material. With the aim of minimising the exponent ${1/n}$, it is advised to determine it in the domain of small strains (plastic strain ${\strainCmp}_{M,p}$ lower than 0.2%) (RB 3643.31).

The Ramberg-Osgood curve is characterised by three parameters $E$, $K$ and $n$ . The strain is then written as a function of the stress:

$$
\strainCmp
=
\frac{\stressCmp}{E}
+
\varepsilon_{M,p}(\stressCmp)
$$

where $E$ is Young's modulus and ${\strainCmp}_{M,p}$ the plastic strain of the mean monotonic tension curve, defined by the power law:

$$
{\strainCmp}_{M,p}
=
K{ \left( \frac{\stressCmp}{E} \right)}^{1/n}
$$

The material parameters of this law are given by using the keyword `POST_ROCHE` (or `POST_ROCHE_FO`) of left lbrace:ref:DEFI_MATERIAU <U4.43.01>right rbrace. $K$ corresponds to the keyword `RAMB_OSGO_FACT` and $n$ to `RAMB_OSGO_EXPO`. They are used in the computation of the spring-effect factors.

### 3.2 Recovery and classification of the moments

One classifies the moments according to the curvilinear abscissa, for each segment. In the RCC-MRX variant, the combination of the moments is carried out in accordance with the RCC-MRx CMS2 (RB 3645.82), for the computation of the abated equivalent stress, formula CODE. Another combination is proposed for the computation of the abated moment and of the abated equivalent stress (RCC-MRX variant, formula REFE_ELAS, and ASNR variant).

#### 3.2.1 Classification of the moments according to RCC-MRx CSM2

The moments depending on the forces (RB 3623.3) are due:

- to the weight; they are denoted ${M}_{\text{poids}}$.
- to the earthquake, quasi-static inertial part, if the second method of classification of the seismic moments CMS2 is applied; they are denoted ${M}_{\text{SI}}$ .
- to the applied mechanical loadings (including the pressure).

The moments depending on the displacements (RB 3623.4) are due:

- to the thermal expansions; they are denoted ${m}_{\text{ther}}$.
- to the displacements imposed at the anchors (signed permanent imposed displacements DINS and unsigned seismic differential displacements DDS); they are denoted respectively ${m}_{\text{DINS}}$ and ${m}_{\text{S}}$.
- to the earthquake, inertial part complementary to the quasi-static part, if the 2nd method of classification of the seismic moments CMS2 is applied. They are denoted ${m}_{\text{SI}}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Combination of the moments for the computation of the abated equivalent stress according to RCC-MRx CMS2 (RB 3645.82) (RCC-MRX variant, formula CODE)

The combination of the moments, for the computation of the abated equivalent stress, is the following, in accordance with the RCC-MRx:

The moments ${M}_{\text{SI}}$ are combined with the other moments “depending on the forces” by adding the absolute values and by assigning to each component the sign of the component of the moment due to the permanent mechanical loadings. The result is denoted $M$.

$$
M
=
{M}_\text{poids}
+
\sgn \left( {M}_\text{poids} \right)
\vert {M}_{\text{SI}} \vert
$$

The moments ${m}_{\text{S}}$ are combined with the other moments “depending on the displacements” by assigning to them, for each component, the sign of the sum of the latter. The result is denoted $m$.

$$
m
=
{m}_{\text{ther}}
+
{m}_{\text{DINS}}
+
\sgn \left( {M}_{\text{ther}}+{m}_{\text{DINS}} \right) \vert {m}_{S} \vert
$$

The signed moments are summed together and the absolute values of the unsigned moments are summed together; the sign of the sum of the signed moments is assigned to the sum of the absolute values of the unsigned moments. These two sums are then added.

### 3.3 Computation of the reference stresses

The reference stress is computed for each section. One distinguishes the reference stress due to the imposed displacements and thermal expansions and the inertial seismic reference stress. For each of them, one value is determined for the computation of the abated moment (RCC-MRX variants) and the computation of the abated equivalent stress (RCC-MRX and ASNR variants), and another value is determined specifically for the computation of the abated moment (ASNR form).

For the computation of the abated moment (RCC-MRX variant) and the computation of the abated equivalent stress (RCC-MRX and ASNR variants), the stress coefficient ${B}_{2}$, appearing in the formula of the reference stresses, is defined according to:

- ${B}_{2}=1$ stress coefficient (RB 3680) for the segments of straight part (straight pipes, reducers, tee and branch connections),
- ${B}_{2}=\max \left( \frac{1.30}{{f}^{2/3}},1.0 \right)$ stress coefficient (RB 3680) for the elbows and the bends, with $f={h}_{c}\frac{{R}_{c}}{{r}_{m}^{2}}$ where ${r}_{m}=\frac{{D}_{e}-{h}_{c}}{2}$. ${R}_{c}$ is the radius of curvature, ${D}_{e}$ and ${h}_{c}$ correspond respectively to the outside diameter and to the thickness of the pipe,

For the computation of the abated moment (ASNR form), the stress coefficient ${B}_{2}$, appearing in the formula of the reference stresses, is defined according to:

- ${B}_{2}=1$ everywhere

#### 3.3.1 Reference stresses due to the imposed displacements and to the thermal expansions

The reference stress is computed in accordance with §RB 3643.31 (Combination according to the 2nd method of Classification of the Moments depending on the displacements CMS2):

$$
{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}^{2}
=
{\left(0.79{B}_{2} \frac{{m}_{R}^{\prime}}{Z}\right)}^{2}+{\left(0.87\frac{{m}_{1}}{Z}\right)}^{2}
$$

where:

- ${m}_{R}^{\prime}=\sqrt{{m}_{2}^{2}+{m}_{3}^{2}}$ is the norm of the resulting bending moment due to the imposed displacements and to the thermal expansions (RB 3623.4),
- ${m}_{1}$ is the torsional moment due to the imposed displacements (RB 3623.4),
- $Z$ is the moment of inertia, which is written for circular beams $Z=\frac{{I}_{y}}{R}$ , for the reducers one takes the minimum of the ratio over the pipe element $Z=\min \left( \frac{{I}_{y}}{R} \right)$ .

#### 3.3.2 Inertial seismic reference stresses

The inertial seismic reference stress is computed in accordance with §RB 3643.31 (Combination according to the 2nd method of Classification of the Seismic Moments CMS2):

$$
{\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}}^{2}
=
{\left(0.79{B}_{2}\frac{{({M}_{\text{S2}})}_{R}^{\prime}}{Z}\right)}^{2}
+
{\left(0.87\frac{{M}_{S21}}{Z}\right)}^{2}
$$

where:

- ${({M}_{\text{S2}})}_{R}^{\prime}$ is the resulting inertial seismic bending moment (RB 3645.7 and RB 3645.82),
- ${M}_{S21}$ is the inertial seismic torsional moment (RB 3645.7 and RB 3645.82),
- the index $\text{S2}$ relates to the CMS2 combination,
- ${B}_{2}$ is defined as in r7.10.04-sections-contraintesReferences.

### 3.4 Computation of the local and total reversibilities

The local reversibility is computed for each section and the total reversibility for each segment of the line. For each of them, one distinguishes the reversibilities due to the imposed displacements and thermal expansions and the inertial seismic reversibilities. For each of them, one value is determined for the computation of the abated moment (RCC-MRX forms) and the computation of the abated equivalent stress (RCC-MRX and ASNR variants), and another value is determined specifically for the computation of the abated moment (ASNR form).

#### 3.4.1 Local reversibilities $t$ and ${t}_{S}$

The local reversibility is defined by the ratio of the elastic strain to the plastic strain, corresponding to the reference stress.

At each cross-section, the local reversibility linked to the reference stresses due to the imposed displacements and thermal expansions is written:

$$
\frac{1}{t}
=
\frac{{\strainCmp}_{M,p}({\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}})}{\frac{{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}}{E}}
$$

The local reversibility linked to the seismic reference stresses is written:

$$
\frac{1}{{t}_{S}}
=
\frac{{\strainCmp}_{M,p}({\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}})}{\frac{{\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}}}{E}}
$$

#### 3.4.2 Total reversibilities $T$ and ${T}_{S}$

The total reversibility is computed on each segment of the piping system presenting two fixed points.

The total reversibility linked to the reference stresses due to the imposed displacements and thermal expansions is written:

$$
\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{l}{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}^{2}\, A \, ds
=
\int_{0}^{l}\frac{1}{t}{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}^{2}\, A \, ds
$$

and the total reversibility linked to the seismic reference stresses is written:

$$
\frac{1}{{T}_{S}}\int_{0}^{l}{\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}}^{2}\, A \, ds
=
\int_{0}^{l}\frac{1}{{t}_{S}}{\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}}^{2}\, A \, ds
$$

where:

- $l$ is the length of the segment of the curvilinear abscissa,
- $A$ is the area of the cross-section along the curvilinear abscissa.

### 3.5 Computation of the spring-effect factors

The spring-effect factor is computed for each section; their maxima are computed on each segment. One distinguishes the spring-effect factor due to the imposed displacements and thermal expansions and the inertial seismic spring-effect factor. For each of them, one value is determined for the computation of the abated moment (RCC-MRX variant) and the computation of the abated equivalent stress (RCC-MRX and ASNR variants), and another value is determined specifically for the computation of the abated moment (ASNR variant).

#### 3.5.1 Monotonic spring-effect factor

The monotonic spring-effect factor ${r}_{M}$ quantifies the monotonic spring effect. It is computed on each smallest part of the piping system presenting two fixed points.

This factor characterises the difference between the stresses and strains ${\stressCmp}_{\text{élas}}$ and ${\strainCmp}_{\text{élas}}$ computed by assuming an elastic constitutive behaviour and the true values $\stressCmp$ and $\strainCmp$, related by the monotonic tension curve:

$$
{r}_{M}
=
-E \left( \frac{\strainCmp-{\strainCmp}_{\text{élas}}}{\stressCmp-{\stressCmp}_{\text{élas}}} \right)
$$

This factor ${r}_{M}$ is:

- zero when the spring effect is zero and the stress is purely secondary (the strain is that computed elastically)
- infinite when the stress is purely primary (the stress is that computed elastically)

There is a factor ${r}_{M}$ at each piping cross-section:

$$
{r}_{M}
=
\max \left( \frac{T}{t}-1;0 \right)
$$

#### 3.5.2 Seismic spring-effect factor

The seismic spring-effect factor ${r}_{s}$ quantifies the seismic spring effect:

$$
{r}_{s}
=
\frac{{T}_{s}}{{t}_{s}}-1
$$

### 3.6 Computation of the abatement coefficients for each segment

The elastic stress is assumed to be the linear sum of the reference stress ${\stressCmp}_{R}$ and of the pressure stress ${\stressCmp}_{P}$

$$
{\stressCmp}_{\text{élas}}
=
{\stressCmp}_{R}+{\stressCmp}_{P}
$$

For the monotonic case, one has ${\stressCmp}_{R}={\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}$ and for the seismic case, one has ${\stressCmp}_{R}={\stressCmp}_{\text{S2},\text{ref}}$.

The abatement coefficients $g$ and ${g}_{S}$ relate the reference stress computed linearly to the “true” stress, coming from the projection of the elastic stress onto the monotonic tension curve along the straight line of slope $-\frac{E}{r}$. They are computed for each section of each piping segment (option `SIGM_LIM ='NON'`).

Depending on the monotonic or seismic cases, one has $r={r}_{M}$ or $r={r}_{S}$ respectively.

![Figure](/figures/r7.10.04/thumbnail_image001.png)

*Abatement of the linear elastic stress ${\stressCmp}_{\text{élas}}$ onto the monotonic tension curve*

Two formulations of the abatement coefficient exist, according to the RCC-MRX and ASNR variants, respectively:

- For the RCC-MRX variant:

$$
{g}_{\text{opti}}
=
\frac{\stressCmp -{\stressCmp}_{P}}{{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}}
$$

- For the ASNR variant:

$$
{g}_{\text{opti}}
=
\frac\stressCmp{{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}+{\stressCmp}_{P}}
$$

#### 3.6.1 Optimised monotonic abatement coefficient

The optimised monotonic abatement coefficient ${g}_{\text{opti}}$ is computed from the maximum of the monotonic spring-effect factor along the curvilinear abscissa ${\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right)$, depending on the segment.

The true stress $\stressCmp$ and the true strain $\strainCmp$ are then solutions of the following system of equations:

$$
{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right)
=
-E \left(
    \frac{
        \strainCmp-\frac{{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}}{E}
        -
        {\strainCmp}_{trac}({\stressCmp}_{P})
    }
    {\stressCmp -{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}-{\stressCmp}_{P}}
\right)
$$

and

$$
\strainCmp={\strainCmp}_{trac}(\stressCmp)
$$

where ${\stressCmp}_{P}$ corresponds to the stress induced by the internal pressure of the pipe, of expression:

$$
{\stressCmp}_{P}
=
0,87 \, P
\frac{{D}_{i}}{2{h}_{c}}
$$

with $P$ the internal pressure, ${D}_{i}$ the inside diameter and ${h}_{c}$ the thickness of the pipe.

The inequality ${\stressCmp}_{P}<\stressCmp$ must be satisfied in order to guarantee the correct computation of ${g}_{\text{opti}}$.

#### 3.6.2 Optimised seismic abatement coefficient

The optimised seismic abatement coefficient ${g}_{\text{Sopti}}$ is computed from the maximum of the seismic spring-effect factor along the curvilinear abscissa ${\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right)$, depending on the segment.

The true stress $\stressCmp$ and the true strain $\strainCmp$ are then solutions of the following system of equations:

$$
{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right)
=
-E \left(
    \frac{\strainCmp-\frac{{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}}{E}-{\strainCmp}_{trac}({\stressCmp}_{P})}
    {\stressCmp-{\stressCmp}_{\text{ref}}-{\stressCmp}_{P}}
\right)
$$

and

$$
\strainCmp={\strainCmp}_{trac}(\stressCmp)
$$

The inequality ${\stressCmp}_{P}<\stressCmp$ must be satisfied in order to guarantee the correct computation of ${g}_{\text{Sopti}}$.

### 3.7 Computation of the abatement coefficients for each segment for the RCC-MRX variant

The abatement coefficients are computed for each section of each piping segment (option `SIGM_LIM ='OUI'`).

In this case, one considers that the true stress is no longer an unknown of the system of two equations, but a constant depending on an allowable stress defined from the datum of the minimum rupture stress and of the minimum stress at 0.2% strain of the material; the abatement coefficient can then be computed directly without solution of the system, by an analytical formula codified in the RCC-MRx.

#### 3.7.1 Computation of the true stress from an allowable stress

The allowable stress ${S}^{\star}$ is defined by:

$$
{S}^{\star}
=
\alpha \left \lbrace
    0,426{({R}_{p0,2})}_{\text{min}}({\theta}_{m})
    +
    0,032{({R}_{m})}_{\text{min}}({\theta}_{m})
\right \rbrace
$$

where:

- $\alpha$ is a dimensionless coefficient greater than or equal to 1; it corresponds to the keyword `COEF` of the material `POST_ROCHE`
- ${({R}_{p0,2})}_{\text{min}}({\theta}_{m})$ is the minimum conventional yield strength at 0.2% strain, for the mean temperature ${\theta}_{m}$ in the thickness during the loading studied; it corresponds to the operand `RP02_MIN` of the material `POST_ROCHE`
- ${({R}_{m})}_{\text{min}}({\theta}_{m})$ is the minimum tensile strength, for the mean temperature ${\theta}_{m}$ in the thickness during the loading studied; it corresponds to the operand `RM_MIN` of the material `POST_ROCHE`

The true stress $\stressCmp$ is then defined by:

$$
\stressCmp
=
2{S}^{\star}
\frac{{({R}_{p0,2})}_{\text{moy}}({\theta}_{m})}
{{({R}_{p0,2})}_{min}({\theta}_{m})}
$$

with:

- ${({R}_{p0,2})}_{\text{moy}}({\theta}_{m})$ the mean conventional yield strength at 0.2% strain, for the mean temperature ${\theta}_{m}$ in the thickness during the loading studied

#### 3.7.2 Computation of the monotonic and seismic abatement coefficients

According to the RCC-MRx codification (RB 3651.1132 and RB 3651.1142) the monotonic abatement coefficients ${g}_{\text{RX}}$ and seismic abatement coefficients ${g}_{\text{SRX}}$ are then the following:

$$
{g}_{\text{RX}}
=
\frac{(\stressCmp-{\stressCmp}_{P}) \left( 1+{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right) \right )}
{
    E \left \lbrace
        {\strainCmp}_{M,p}(\stressCmp)-{\strainCmp}_{M,p}({\stressCmp}_{P})
    \right \rbrace
    +
    \left ( \stressCmp -{\stressCmp}_{P} \right) \left( 1+{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{M} \right) \right)
}
$$

and

$$
{g}_{\text{SRX}}
=
\frac{(\stressCmp -{\stressCmp}_{P})  \left( 1+{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{S} \right) \right )}
{
    E\left \lbrace
        {\strainCmp}_{M,p}(\stressCmp)-{\strainCmp}_{M,p}({\stressCmp}_{P})
    \right \rbrace
    +
    \left ( \stressCmp -{\stressCmp}_{P} \right) \left( 1+{\underset{s}{\max}} \left( {r}_{S} \right) \right)
}
$$

Note that these monotonic and seismic coefficients are computed from the maximum monotonic and seismic spring-effect factors along the curvilinear abscissae, respectively.

In what follows, the processing is the same whatever the value of `SIGM_LIM`. ${g}_{\text{RX}}$ will therefore correspond to ${g}_{\text{opti}}$ and ${g}_{\text{SRX}}$ to ${g}_{\text{Sopti}}$ .

### 3.8 Computation of the abated moments (components and norm)

The computation of the abated moments is not explicitly provided for in the RCC-MRX; it is nevertheless available in the RCC-MRX variant. It is available in the ASNR variant.
The abated moments fall into two categories: permanent and seismic (or non-permanent). They are differentiated because the former are signed (relative) whereas the latter are unsigned (absolute).
They are also added in absolute value, component by component, in order to form the complete abated moments (used for the computation of the abated equivalent stresses).

#### 3.8.1 Abated permanent moments

$$
\begin{split}{M}_{\text{abat\_perm}, k} = &{M}_{\text{poids}, k}
                        +{g}_{\text{opti}}{m}_{{\text{ther},k}}
                        +{g}_{\text{opti}}{m}_{{\text{DINS},k}} \\\end{split}
$$

With $k$ which is worth 1 torsion for the torsion, 2 for the bending in the plane of the elbow and 3 for the bending out of the plane of the elbow.

The resultant is written

$$
{M}_{\text{abat\_perm}}
=
\sqrt{
    {M}_{\text{abat\_perm},1}^{2}
    +
    {M}_{\text{abat\_perm},2}^{2}
    +
    {M}_{\text{abat\_perm},3}^{2}
}
$$

These 4 values are supplied to the user in the principal output field.

#### 3.8.2 Abated seismic moments

$$
{M}_{\text{abat\_sism}, k} =
                            \left \lvert M_{SI,k} \right \rvert
                            +
                            {g}_{\text{opti}} \left \lvert m_{S,k} \right \rvert
                            +
                            {g}_{\text{Sopti}}\left \lvert m_{SI,k} \right \rvert
$$

The resultant is written in the same way:

$$
{M}_{\text{abat\_sism}}
=
\sqrt{
    {M}_{\text{abat\_sism},1}^{2}
    +
    {M}_{\text{abat\_sism},2}^{2}
    +
    {M}_{\text{abat\_sism},3}^{2}
}
$$

These 4 values are also supplied to the user in the principal output field.

#### 3.8.3 Abated moments

$$
{M}_{\text{abat}, k} = \left \lvert {M}_{\text{abat\_perm}, k}\right \rvert + \left \lvert{M}_{\text{abat\_sism}, k}\right \rvert
$$

These three values are used in the computation of the abated equivalent stresses in the case `VARIANTE = 'ASNR'` as well as in the case `SIGM_ABAT = 'REFE_ELAS'` of `VARIANTE = 'RCC_MRX'`.

### 3.9 Computation of the equivalent abated stresses

#### 3.9.1 Codified formula

The codified formula corresponds to `SIGM_ABAT = 'CODE'` and is valid only for the RCC-MRX variant.

##### 3.9.1.1 General case

The optimised equivalent abated stress is obtained by combination of the moments obtained in §:ref:r7.10.04-sections-classementMomentsRCCMRX weighted by the abatement coefficients.

One then uses the coefficients ${g}_{\text{opti}}$ and ${g}_{\text{Sopti}}$ in order to obtain the optimised equivalent stress (this value is not computed in the case `RCCM_RX=’OUI’`)

$$
\stressCmp_{\text{equi,opti}}
=
\sqrt{
     \left( D_1 \frac{P \, D_i}{2 h_c} \right)^2
     +
     \left(
         \left \langle
             \frac{D_2}{Z} ,\, \vert M \vert
             +
             g_{\text{opti}} \vert m \vert
             +
             g_{\text{Sopti}} \vert m_{\text{SI}} \vert
         \right \rangle
     \right)^2
}
$$

where:

- $\left \lvert M \right \rvert = \left(\begin{array}{c}\left \lvert {M}_{1} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {M}_{2} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {M}_{3} \right \rvert\end{array}\right)$ is the signed sum ${M}_{\text{forc}}$ of the moments depending on the forces, including the contribution of the primary loadings (weight…), plus the absolute value of the quasi-static part of the inertial earthquake (pseudo-mode), assigned the sign of ${M}_{\text{forc}}$,
- $\left \lvert m \right \rvert = \left(\begin{array}{c}\left \lvert {m}_{1} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {m}_{2} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {m}_{3} \right \rvert\end{array}\right)$ is the signed sum ${M}_{\text{depl}}$ of the moments depending on the displacements (thermal expansion, non-seismic imposed displacements), plus the absolute value of the unsigned moments due to the seismic differential displacements, assigned the sign of ${M}_{\text{depl}}$
- $\left \lvert {m}_{\text{SI}} \right \rvert = \left(\begin{array}{c}\left \lvert {{m}_{\text{SI}}}_{1} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {{m}_{\text{SI}}}_{2} \right \rvert\\ \left \lvert {{m}_{\text{SI}}}_{3} \right \rvert\end{array}\right)$ is the moment corresponding to the dynamic part of the inertial earthquake, classified as depending on the displacements, taken in absolute value,
- $P$ is the pressure imposed inside the pipe,
- ${D}_{i}$ and ${h}_{c}$ correspond respectively to the inside diameter and to the thickness of the pipe,
- $Z$ is the moment of inertia (which is written for circular beams $Z=\frac{{I}_{y}}{R}$ ),
- ${D}_{1}$ is the stress coefficient associated with the internal pressure,
- ${D}_{2}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{D}_{21}\\ {D}_{22}\\ {D}_{23}\end{array}\right)$ are the stress coefficients associated with the moments in torsion (${D}_{21}$) and in bending (${D}_{22}$ and ${D}_{23}$),
- $\left \langle \cdot , \cdot \right \rangle$ is the scalar product.

##### 3.9.1.2 Case of the straight pipes

In the case of the straight pipes, one sets:

- ${D}_{1}=0$ ,
- ${D}_{21}=0,712$ ,
- ${D}_{22}={D}_{23}=0,429 \left( \frac{{D}_{e}}{{h}_{c}} \right)^{0,16}$ ,

where ${D}_{e}$ is the outside diameter of the pipe.

##### 3.9.1.3 Case of the reducers

In the case of the reducers, one sets:

- $Z=\min \left( \frac{{I}_{y}}{R} \right)$
- ${D}_{1}=0$ ,
- ${D}_{21}=0,712$ ,
- ${D}_{22}={D}_{23}=0,429 \left( \max \left( \frac{{D}_{e}}{{h}_{c}} \right) \right)^{0,16}$ ,

where $\min \left( \frac{{I}_{y}}{R} \right)$ is the minimum of the ratio and $\max \left( \frac{{D}_{e}}{{h}_{c}} \right)$ is the maximum of the ratio over the pipe element.

##### 3.9.1.4 Case of the bent pipes and of the elbows

In the case of the elbows, one sets:

- ${D}_{1}=0,87$ ,
- ${D}_{21}=0,712$ ,
- ${D}_{22}=\max \left( 1,07 \left(\frac{\pi}{{\psi}_{c}}\right)^{-0.4} \, {f}^{-2/3} \left( 1+0,142 \frac{P \, D_{i}} {2 \, {h}_{c} \, {S}_{y} \, {f}^{1,45}} \right)^{-1} , 1,02 \right )$ ,
- ${D}_{23}=\max \left( 0,809 \, {f}^{-0,44} \left( 1 + 0,142\frac{ P \, {D}_{i}}{2 \, {h}_{c} \, {S}_{y} \, {f}^{1,45}} \right)^{-1}, 1,02 \right)$ ,
- $f={h}_{c}\frac{{R}_{c}}{{r}_{m}^{2}}$ where ${r}_{m}=\frac{{D}_{e}-{h}_{c}}{2}$ ,
- ${D}_{i}$, ${D}_{e}$ and ${h}_{c}$ correspond respectively to the inside and outside diameters and to the thickness of the pipe.
- ${S}_{y}$ is the minimum conventional yield strength at 0.2% strain (given by the user),
- ${R}_{c}$ is the radius of curvature (given by the user),
- ${\psi}_{c}$ is the opening angle of the elbow (given by the user).

#### 3.9.2 Modified formula

Another formulation of the optimised equivalent abated stress is available, based on the formula of the reference elastic stress.
It is selected by `SIGM_ABAT = 'REFE_ELAS'`. It is proposed as a choice in the RCC-MRX variant; it is integrated, with no possible choice, into the ASNR variant.

$$
\stressCmp_{\text{equi,opti}}
=
\left(
    D_1 \frac{P \, D_i}{2 \, h_c}
\right)
+
\sqrt{
    \left(
        0,79 \, {B}_{2} \frac{{M}_{\text{abat},R}^{\prime}}{Z}
    \right)^2
    +
    \left(
        0,87 \, \frac{{M}_{\text{abat},1}}{Z}
    \right)^{2}
}
$$

where ${M}_{\text{abat},R}^{\prime}$ is the norm of the optimised abated bending moment:

$$
{M}_{\text{abat},R}^{\prime}
=
\sqrt{
    {M}_{\text{abat},2}^{2}
    +
    {M}_{\text{abat},3}^{2}
}
$$

Note that the cumulation of the pressure stress with the stress relative to the other loadings is algebraic, and not quadratic as in the RCC-MRX formula, for reasons of conservatism.
