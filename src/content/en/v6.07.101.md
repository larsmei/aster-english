# v6.07.101 COMP001 – Test for elasto-plastic behavior. Simulation in one point hardware

**Summary:**

This test uses a simulation of a loading path in stress or deformation in a material point, i.e. on a model such that the stress and deformation states are homogeneous at any time. It thus allows testing a number of models of elastoplastic behaviour, with the aim of verifying the robustness of their numerical integration, their insensitivity to a change in units, the correct consideration of the control variables on which the coefficients of the model depend, the invariance with an overall rotation applied to the problem, the accuracy of the tangential matrix.

Modelling A: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_ISOT_LINE model in 3D and C_PLAN.

Modelling B: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_ISOT_TRAC model in 3D and C_PLAN.

Modelling C: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_CINE_LINE model in 3D.

Modelling D: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_ECMI_LINE model in 3D and C_PLAN.

Modelling E: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_ECMI_TRAC model in 3D and C_PLAN.

Modelling F: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_CIN1_CHAB model in 3D.

Modelling G: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_CIN2_CHAB model in 3D.

H Modelling: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_ISOT_PUIS model in 3D.

Modelling J: This Modelling allows to validate the MOHR_COULOMB model in 3D.

Modelling K: This model allows to validate the MohrCoulombAS model in 3D.

Modelling L: This Modelling allows to validate the NLH_CSRM model in 3D.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 Characteristics of loading routes

Two loading routes have been defined to handle 3D and 2D plan cases. They are common to all constitutive laws. Each of them meets the following criteria:

- a combined plastic deformation, $p$ , from 4 to 5 % throughout the journey,
- a 1% increase in $p$ during a portion of the journey,

This calibration was conducted on the VMIS_ISOT_LINE law and then postponed on the other laws.

The proposed loading causes a decoupled variation of each component of the tensor of the deformations by successive step. A cyclic discharge path is proposed covering the tensile and compression states and an inversion of the shear signs in order to test a wide range of values.

Schematically, it follows a course on 8 segments $[O-A-B-C-O-C’-B’-A’-O]$ where the second part of the journey [O-C-B-A-O] is symmetrical with the origin of the first $[O-A-B-C-O]$.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

## 2 reference solution

This test performs, for each model, an intercomparison between the reference solution (obtained with a very fine time step), the solution with medium coarse discretization, the solution with temperature effect (or another control variable), the solution by changing the unit system ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{MPa}$), and the solution obtained after rotation or symmetry.

### 2.1 Definition of robustness test cases

We propose 3 angles of analysis to test the robustness of integration of constitutive laws:

- equivalent problem studies
- verification of the tangent matrix
- study of time step discretization

For each of these, the relative differencess are studied in terms of evolution between several calculations using the same law but with different parameters or calculation options. The exploitation is concerned with the invariants of the stress tensor: tensor trace, Von-Mises stress and internal variables of a scalar nature: usually it is the cumulative plasticity.

The overall convergence criteria are the default values for ASTER. (RESI_GLOB_RELA=10-6, ITER_GLOB_MAXI=10) A common Newton schema for the re-update of the tangent matrix was adopted: with each converged increment (REAC_INC=1) and with every 1 iteration (REAC_ITER=1).

### 2.2 Equivalent problem studies

For rough discretization of the journeys: 1 time step for each segment of the journey, the solution obtained for each law is compared to 3 strictly equivalent problems for the condition of the material point:

- $\mathit{Tpa}$ , same route with a change of unit, substitutes $\mathrm{Pa}$ for $\mathrm{MPa}$ in material data and possible law parameters,
- $\mathit{Trot}$ , route by imposing the same $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after rotation: ${\rbrace ^{t}R\cdot \stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}\cdot R$ where $R$ is a rotation matrix. For the 2D case, the rotation angle will be $\alpha =0.9\mathit{rad}$ , for the 3D configuration, the angles d-Euler were chosen with arbitrary values { $\Psi =0.9\mathit{rad}$ , $\theta =0.7\mathit{rad}$ , and $\mathrm{\phi }=0.4\mathit{rad}$ },
- $\mathit{Tsym}$ , path by imposing the $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after symmetry: $x$ and $y$ axis permutation to 2D, $x$ to $y$ , $y$ to $z$ and $z$ to $x$ to 3D.

For each of these problems, the solution (invariants in stresses, cumulative equivalent plastic deformation) must be identical to the basic solution, obtained with the same discretization in time. The reference value of the difference is therefore 0. This means in practice that the difference found must be of the order of machine precision is about $1.E-15$.

### 2.3 Test of the tangent matrix

The tangential matrix is also tested for each behavior, by difference with the matrix obtained by disturbance. Here again, the reference value is 0.

### 2.4 Study of time step discretization (A2)

The behavior of the integration of laws in relation to discretization is studied. For the same Modelling, therefore a given behavior, several discretizations are studied in different times, multiplying by 5 the number of steps of the loading path. In the reference [1], the discretization is pushed up to 3125 increments per segment on the same principle. Here, to limit the duration of the tests, we limit ourselves to three successive refinements. This leads to the following discretization:

| Number of intervals per loading segment | 1 | 5 | 25 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Total number of steps on the entire journey | 8 | 40 | 200 |
| Calculation | $\mathit{T0}$ | $\mathit{T1}$ | $\mathit{Tréf}$ reference solution |

The reference solution, $\mathit{Tréf}$ , is the $N=25$ , 200 steps for the entire route. These different solutions allow to judge the sensitivity to the large time step and the robustness of integration. To show the convergence speed according to the time step, the solutions presented in [1] are reported here up to 3125 time step for each of the 8 segments of the load path.

## 13 Executive summary

For all the elastoplastic behaviors tested in modellings A to L, the results are satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathit{MPa}$), or following rotation or symmetry of the load
- the results converge correctly with the time step, and the integration schemes (implicit for the elastoplastic behaviors studied) are robust, since they allow the use of large time step
- the tangent matrices are correct because they are similar to the tangent matrices calculated by disturbance.
