# r5.03.14 Implicit and explicit integration of non-linear constitutive relations

**Summary**

This document describes two integration methods (one implicit and one explicit) for the solution of problems with non-linear constitutive behaviours in the operators STAT_NON_LINE [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) and DYNA_NON_LINE [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05).

The implicit numerical method presented is that of Newton, with or without line search.

The explicit numerical method is that of Runge Kutta of order 2 with adaptive step.

## 1 Generalities on the integration of constitutive laws

### 1.1 Algorithm for the solution of the quasi-static problem

When the constitutive behaviour of the materials composing the structure is not linear, the solution of the global equilibrium of the structure at each time instant is a non-linear problem whose variational formulation in the case of small strains can be written, in the quasi-static case, in the following form, deduced from the expression of the principle of virtual work (cf. [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)):

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{v}^{T}.{L}^{int}(u,t)={v}^{T}.{L}^{\text{ext}}(t)\text{}\forall v\text{tel que}B.v=0\\ B.u={u}^{d}(t)\end{array}
$$

Where:

- $t$ represents the time-instant variable
- $u$ is the displacement field taken from a reference configuration
- $v$ is the kinematically admissible virtual displacement field
- the relation $B.u={u}^{d}(t)$ corresponds to the boundary conditions imposed in displacements
- ${L}^{\text{ext}}(t)$ is the external mechanical loading to which the structure is subjected
- ${L}^{int}$ represents the internal forces of the non-linear quasi-static mechanics problem. ${L}^{int}(u,t)$ is related to the stress field $\sigma$ by the operator of the work of the virtual strains ${Q}^{T}$ according to the following relation:

$$
{L}^{int}(u,t)={Q}^{T}(u).\sigma
$$

The dualisation of the Dirichlet boundary conditions $B.u={u}^{d}(t)$ leads to the following problem [R3.03.01]:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{L}^{int}(u,t)+{B}^{T}.\lambda ={L}^{\text{ext}}(t)\\ B.u={u}^{d}(t)\end{array}
$$

The formulation of the discretised quasi-static problem consists in expressing the equilibrium of the structure for a sequence of computation time instants ${\left\lbrace {t}_{i}\right\rbrace }_{1\le i\le I}$ which parameterise the loading; the quantities at the time instant ${t}_{i}$ will be denoted by the index $i$ (for example ${L}^{int}({u}_{i},{t}_{i})={{L}_{i}}^{int}$ ):

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{L}_{i}^{int}+{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{i}={L}_{i}^{\text{ext}}\\ B.{u}_{i}={u}_{i}^{d}\end{array}
$$

This non-linear problem is solved by a Newton method (or quasi-Newton), cf. [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)).

The system to be solved is then written, for each iteration $n$ of the correction phase:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{K}_{i}^{n}.\delta {u}_{i}^{n+1}+{B}^{T}.\delta {\lambda}_{i}^{n+1}={L}_{i}^{\text{méca}}-{L}_{i}^{int,n}-{B}^{T}.{\lambda}_{i}^{n}\\ B.\delta {u}_{i}^{n+1}=0\end{array}
$$

The vector of the internal forces ${L}_{i}^{int,n}$ is computed from the stresses ${\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ , the latter being computed from the displacements ${u}_{i}^{n}$ by means of the constitutive relation of the material. In fact, in the case of incremental constitutive behaviours, ${\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ is computed from $({\sigma}_{i-1},{\alpha}_{i-1})$ and from the strain increment $\varepsilon (\Delta {u}_{i}^{n})$ induced by the displacement increment since the beginning of the iterative process.

### 1.2 Purpose of the integration

This scheme requires, starting from the estimate of the displacements at iteration $n$ , computing at each Gauss point:

- the stress tensor ${\sigma}_{i}^{n}$ which satisfies the constitutive law, and which makes it possible to compute ${L}_{i}^{int,n}$ and the internal variables specific to each constitutive behaviour,
- the consistent tangent constitutive operator ${(\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon })}_{i}^{n}$ , in order to obtain the tangent matrix:

${K}_{i}^{n}={\sum}_{e}{K}_{e,ì}^{n}$ with ${K}_{e,i}^{n}={\int}_{{\Omega}_{e}}{Q}^{T}{(\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \epsilon })}_{i}^{n}Qd{\Omega}_{e}$

### 1.3 Solution methods

For each finite element, at each integration point, the constitutive behaviour must therefore be integrated, that is to say the stress tensor and the internal variables that are the solution of the problem must be computed, as well as the tangent operator.

In the following paragraphs we present two general algorithms making it possible to compute the solution: either the solution by a Newton method, based on an implicit discretisation of the equations, or by a Runge-Kutta method of order 2 with error control, based on an explicit discretisation of these equations.

Other solution methods, specific to each constitutive behaviour, are also employed. They are based on an optimisation of the system of non-linear equations, making it possible to reduce the number of equations to be solved, very often to a single equation. The method used then is a simple search for a zero of a function (see for example [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02), [R5.03.04](doc:r5.03.04), [R5.03.16](doc:r5.03.16)).

For all these algorithms, it is possible, in the event of non-convergence, to proceed to a local subdivision of the time step. ; for this purpose, a linear interpolation of the displacement and of the command variables (for example the temperature) over the time interval is adopted, which leads, for every time increment, to:

$\forall \tau \in [:ref:`{t}_{\text{i-1}},{t}_{i} <{t}_{\text{i-1}},{t}_{i}>$]T(tau )={T}_{text{i-1}}+frac{tau -{t}_{text{i-1}}}{Delta t}[T-{T}_{text{i-1}}]` and ${\varepsilon}_{ij}^{k}(\tau )={\varepsilon}_{ij}({u}_{\text{i-1}})+\frac{\tau -{t}_{\text{i-1}}}{\Delta t}[{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{k}({u}_{i}^{n})-{\varepsilon}_{ij}({u}_{\text{i-1}})]$

## 2 Explicit integration of an incremental constitutive relation

In order to solve the non-linear system relative to the constitutive law, one can use an explicit integration method of Runge Kutta type of order 2 (option ‘RUNGE_KUTTA’ of the operand ALGO_INTE of the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT) for incremental problems of non-linear constitutive behaviour.

The relations currently concerned are:

| VISCOCHAB | Elasto-viscoplastic constitutive behaviour of Chaboche, with two centres, with memory and recovery effect |
| --- | --- |
| VENDOCHAB | Viscoplastic constitutive behaviour with Chaboche damage |
| POLYCRISTAL | Homogenised polycrystalline elasto-viscoplastic constitutive behaviour |
| MONOCRISTAL | Single-crystal viscoplastic constitutive behaviours of metals |
| HAYHURST | Viscoplastic constitutive behaviour with Hayhurst damage |

The relation POLYCRISTAL is accessible only by the explicit method presented below.

This type of integration makes it possible to implement very easily a new constitutive model [bib2]. The computation of the stress field from a given strain increment is described by following the evolution of the internal variables.

### 2.1 Use of an explicit method

It is assumed that the system to be solved can be expressed in the form:

$\frac{dY}{dt}=F(Y,t;\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n},{\sigma}_{i-1})$

where $Y$ represents the set of the internal variables of the model, including the plastic-strain variation $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ .

Their time derivatives are assumed to depend only on the internal variables and on the stresses at the previous time instant, and on the total-strain increment supplied by the global Newton algorithm $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}$ .

This assumption rests in particular on the linear relation (Hooke's law) between the stress tensor at the current time instant and the elastic part of the strain tensor: $\Delta \sigma =A(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})$ , which makes it possible to express $\sigma$ as a function of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ in the differential equations $\frac{dY}{dt}=F(Y,t;\sigma (\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n},{\sigma}_{i-1},Y))$

One of the simplest techniques to implement in order to solve these differential equations is the use of explicit methods. For them to remain numerically efficient, it is indispensable to associate with them an automatic step control in order to retain a good cost-precision compromise.

The explicit and embedded Runge and Kutta methods [bib1], [bib2] are undoubtedly the simplest integration schemes respecting these criteria. Their principle is to associate two integration schemes of different order in order to control the time step according to a required precision. Depending on the integration order chosen, several algorithms are available and the simplest scheme is a method of order two.

One integrates according to the following scheme:

- ${Y}_{t+h}={Y}^{(2)}$ if the precision criterion is satisfied;
- ${Y}^{(2)}=Y+\frac{h}{2}[F(Y,t)+F({Y}^{(1)},t+h)]$ with ${Y}^{(1)}=Y+hF(Y,t)$

The difference between ${Y}^{(2)}$ (scheme of order 2) and ${Y}^{(1)}$ (scheme of order 1, Euler) supplies an estimate of the integration error and makes it possible to control the size of the time step $h$, which is initialised to $\Delta {t}_{i}$ for the first attempt. Thus, the method remains efficient if the constitutive behaviour remains elastic during the increment, and one naturally has local sub-steps independent of the global increment in order to integrate, with a better precision, the evolution of the internal variables at the Gauss points where the non-linearity of the constitutive behaviour is the most significant.

### 2.2 Control of the time step

The strategy of the step control is defined from a norm of the discrepancy between the two integration methods: $\parallel {Y}^{(2)}-{Y}^{(1)}\parallel$ and from the precision required by the user $\eta$ (keyword: RESI_INTE_RELA). The criterion retained is the following, where one denotes $Y=({y}_{1,}{y}_{2,}\mathrm{...}{y}_{N})$ :

$\deltay (t)=\underset{j=\mathrm{1...N}}{\sup}\left\lbrace \frac{\mid {y}_{j}^{(2)}-{y}_{j}^{(1)}\mid }{\max[\varepsilon ,∣{y}_{j}(t)∣]}\right\rbrace <\eta$

The parameter $\eta$ is fixed at 0.001. The desired integration precision $\eta$ must be consistent with the level of precision required for the global stage. The default value $\eta ={10}^{-6}$ often proves penalising in terms of computation time. It can often be increased by one or two orders of magnitude, in particular for the elasto-visco-plastic models, without any real loss of precision (to be seen case by case).

If the criterion is not satisfied, the time step is subdivided according to a heuristic method. When the time step becomes too small ($h<\text{}$ machine precision), a return code is issued, and the global subdivision of the time step is activated, if requested by the user.

### 2.3 Influence on the global stage

In its current version, the method does not supply a tangent matrix; its evaluation is nevertheless possible (but costly) by a perturbation technique (activated under COMPORTEMENT by the keyword TYPE_MATR_TANG='PERTURBATION'). If the elastic matrix is used for the global equilibrium, the number of Newton iterations required to converge can become very large, which often leads to a larger number of increments so that the global solution is facilitated.

In order to improve convergence, it is possible to initialise the Newton iterations from the previously computed solution: instead of considering the displacement increment for the first iteration at the current time instant (obtained via the elastic matrix), the sought displacement increment for the first iteration $\Delta {U}_{1}$ is initialised from the value $\Delta {U}_{n}^{-}$ , the solution at the previous increment (cf. figure on the following page). The keyword PREDICTION=‘EXTRAPOLE’ (under NEWTON) [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) makes it possible to initialise with the converged increment of the previous step (weighted by the ratio of the time steps). This estimate is projected onto the field of the kinematically admissible displacements so that the final solution does indeed satisfy the Dirichlet boundary conditions.

### 3.1 Implicit integration by Newton's method

The PLASTI environment makes it possible in *Code_Aster* to integrate, by a Newton method, in a systematic manner, non-linear constitutive models. The approach for solving the equations of the model can be schematised in the following manner: knowing all the variables (stresses, internal variables) at the previous time instant ${t}_{i-1}$ , and the total-strain increment supplied at the point considered by the global Newton algorithm $\varepsilon (\Delta {u}_{i}^{n})$ , also denoted $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}$ , the solution of the system must be found:

$f(\Delta Y)=0=\left[\begin{array}{c}g(\Delta y)\\ l(\Delta y)\\ i(\Delta y)\\ j(\Delta y)\\ k(\Delta y)\end{array}\right]$ with $\Delta y=(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}\\ \Delta {X}_{1}\\ \Delta {X}_{2}\\ \Delta p\end{array})={y}_{i}-{y}_{i-1}$ .

The first equation represents for example the elastic stress-strain relation $g(\Delta y)=\Delta \sigma -A(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})$ , the second expressing the plastic flow, and the following ones correspond to the evolution laws of the various internal variables, vectorial or scalar, denoted here $\Delta {X}_{1}$ , $\Delta {X}_{2}$ and $\Delta p$ .

The number of equations to be solved obviously varies according to the constitutive behaviour (see for example the constitutive behaviours MONOCRISTAL [R5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11), VISCOCHAB [R5.03.12](doc:r5.03.12), IRRAD3M [R5.03.23](doc:r5.03.23)).

This system is solved by the Newton method proposed in the PLASTI environment, that is:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}^{l}}{\partial \Delta {y}_{k}}d(\Delta {y}_{k})\text{= -}{f}^{l}(\Delta {y}_{k})\\ \Delta {y}_{k+1}=\Delta {y}_{k}+d(\Delta {y}_{k})\end{array}$ iterating in $k$ until convergence.

It remains to compute the Jacobian matrix of the system: $\frac{\text{df}}{{\text{dy}}_{k}}$ . It has the following form:

$J=\left[\begin{array}{ccccc}\frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta \sigma }& \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}& \frac{\partial g}{\partial \Delta p}\\ \frac{\partial l}{\partial \Delta \sigma }& \frac{\partial l}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}& \frac{\partial l}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}& \frac{\partial l}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}& \frac{\partial l}{\partial \Delta p}\\ \frac{\partial i}{\partial \Delta \sigma }& \frac{\partial i}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}& \frac{\partial i}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}& \frac{\partial i}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}& \frac{\partial i}{\partial \Delta p}\\ \frac{\partial j}{\partial \Delta \sigma }& \frac{\partial j}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}& \frac{\partial j}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}& \frac{\partial j}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}& \frac{\partial j}{\partial \Delta p}\\ \frac{\partial k}{\partial \Delta \sigma }& \frac{\partial k}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}& \frac{\partial k}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}& \frac{\partial k}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}& \frac{\partial k}{\partial \Delta p}\end{array}\right]$

The stopping criterion of the iterations bears, according to the constitutive behaviours, on the vanishing of the residual: $\frac{f({y}_{k})}{f({y}_{0})}<\epsilon$ or the stationarity of the solution: $\mid {y}_{k+1}-{y}_{k}\mid <\epsilon$ .

The initial values ${y}_{0}$ are chosen zero by default, but can take particular values according to the constitutive behaviours.

More precisely, the stopping tests and the initial values are:

| Constitutive behaviours | Stopping criterion | Initialisation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| VISCOCHAB | $\mid {y}_{k+1}-{y}_{k}\mid <\epsilon \mid {y}_{k+1}\mid$ | Explicit solution and modification of the initialisation in the event of poor convergence |
| MONOCRISTAL | $∣f({y}_{k})∣<\epsilon ∣f({y}_{0})∣$ | Zero solution |
| IRRAD3M | $∣f({y}_{k})∣<\epsilon ∣f({y}_{0})∣$ | Elastic solution |

The method used allows a local subdivision of the time step, either systematic, or in the event of non-convergence.

### 3.2 Tangent operator

The tangent operator can be obtained directly from the previous system. Indeed, the system formed by the equations of the model at convergence, $(f(\Delta {y}_{\mathrm{CV}})=0)$ is satisfied at the end of the increment. For a small variation of $f$ , considering this time the total-strain increment $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}$ as a variable and not as a parameter, the system remains at equilibrium and one verifies $\mathrm{df}=0$ , that is:

$\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta \sigma }\delta \Delta \sigma +\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}}\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta {X}_{1}}\delta \Delta {X}_{1}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta {X}_{2}}\delta \Delta {X}_{2}+\frac{\partial f}{\partial \Delta p}\delta \Delta p=0$

This system can also be written:

$\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}\delta (y)=X,\text{avec}Y=\left[\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta {Z}_{s}\end{array}\right]\text{et}X=\left[\begin{array}{c}\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}\\ 0\end{array}\right]$

where $\Delta Z$ represents the complementary unknowns of $\Delta \sigma$ in $y$ .

Writing the Jacobian matrix in the form:

$J.\mathrm{\delta Y}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{Y}_{0}& {Y}_{1}\\ {Y}_{2}& {Y}_{3}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta Z\end{array}\right]$

then operating by successive eliminations and substitutions, the tangent operator sought can be written directly:

${(\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon })}_{i}^{n}={({Y}_{0}-{Y}_{1}{Y}_{3}^{-1}{Y}_{2})}^{-1}$

The previous equations show that one is led to reuse the same Jacobian matrix $J$ as previously in order to evaluate the tangent operator.

### 3.3 Implicit integration by Newton's method with line search

In order to improve the robustness of Newton's method, the possibility is offered of activating the line search.

#### 3.3.1 Presentation of the general line-search algorithm

Let a system of non-linear equations to be solved: $f(x)=0$

A functional $F$ is associated with $f$ .

Newton's method with line search consists in finding at each iteration an advance step along the descent direction that minimises (in the strong sense or in the weak sense) the functional. This reduces to a succession of the following stages:

- computation of the descent direction: ${d}_{k}=-{[\nabla f({x}_{k})]}^{-1}.f({x}_{k})$
- computation of the advance step: $\rho$ such that $F({x}_{k}+\rho .{d}_{k})<F({x}_{k})$ , the best being the $\rho$ that minimises the functional $\rho =\mathrm{argmin}[F({x}_{k}+\rho .{d}_{k})]$
- updating of the unknowns: ${x}_{k+1}={x}_{k}+\rho {d}_{k}$

The functional $F(\rho )=\frac{1}{2}{\parallel f({x}_{k}+\rho .{d}_{k})\parallel }^{2}$ is associated with $f$ .

Unlike the line search of global Newton, in which $\rho$ is determined by the solution of $F'(\rho )=0$ , one seeks here a $\rho \in [0,1],\rho \ne 0$ that decreases the functional $F$ sufficiently. One will not seek an advance step greater than the Newton step ($\rho =1$ ). The principle is described in [1].

**Confidence interval:**

In order to know whether a $\rho$ decreases the functional $F$ sufficiently, Armijo's rule is used. The latter makes it possible to characterise a confidence interval by saying that $\rho$ is suitable if $F(\rho )\le F(0)+\omega F'(0)\rho$ where $\omega$ is a fixed real number less than 1. This expression can be represented graphically by the: the curve in blue dashes is the graph of the functional $h(\rho )={(7\rho -4)}^{4}+{(7\rho -5)}^{3}$ , the one in violet is the tangent at the origin, of slope $F'(0)$ and the one in red dashes is the straight line of slope $\omega F'(0)$ with $\omega =0,1$ . In this example, $\rho$ decreases the functional sufficiently if $\rho \in [0;0,871]$ .

**Principle of the computation of** $\rho$ **:**

One begins first by trying the Newton step ($\rho =1$ ). If this step satisfied Armijo's rule, it is retained and the Newton iterations are continued. Otherwise, a quadratic interpolation is made knowing $F(0)$ , $F'(0)$ and $F(1)$ . A new $\rho ={\rho}_{q}$ is then determined. If this step satisfied Armijo's rule, it is retained and the Newton iterations are continued. Otherwise, a cubic interpolation is carried out knowing $F(0)$ , $F'(0)$ , $F(1)$ and $F({\rho}_{q})$ . A new $\rho ={\rho}_{c}$ is then determined. If this step satisfied Armijo's rule, it is retained and the Newton iterations are continued. Otherwise, the cubic interpolations between 0 and the last 2 $\rho$ are continued. The paragraph below details the computation of $\rho$ .

**Details of the computation of** $\rho$ **:**

One begins first by trying the Newton step ($\rho =1$ ). At this stage, one knows $F(0)=\frac{1}{2}{\parallel f({x}_{k})\parallel }^{2}$ and $F'(0)=-{\parallel f({x}_{k})\parallel }^{2}$ .

One computes $F(1)=\frac{1}{2}{\parallel f({x}_{k}+{d}_{k})\parallel }^{2}$ .

If Armijo's rule is not satisfied, that is if $F(1)>F(0)+\omega F'(0)$ , then one models $F(\rho )$ by a polynomial of order 2 (quadratic interpolation):

$q(\rho )=[F(1)-F(0)-F'(0)]{\rho}^{2}+F'(0)\rho +F(0)$

The minimum of this polynomial is obtained for ${\rho}_{q}=\frac{-F'(0)}{2[F(1)-F(0)-F'(0)]}$ . Note that if $F(1)$ is much larger than $F(0)$ , then the minimum of the polynomial is reached at 0. Now one does not wish to have an advance step that is too small, on pain of having to carry out a very large number of Newton iterations. For this purpose, a ${\rho}_{\min}>0$ is imposed (for example ${\rho}_{\min}=0,1$ ). An upper bound ${\rho}_{\max}$ can also be imposed (for example ${\rho}_{\max}=0,6$ ). Thus, if ${\rho}_{q}<{\rho}_{\min}$ then ${\rho}_{q}={\rho}_{\min}$ and if ${\rho}_{q}>{\rho}_{\max}$ then ${\rho}_{q}={\rho}_{\max}$ .

Then one computes $F({\rho}_{q})=\frac{1}{2}{\parallel f({x}_{k}+{\rho}_{q}.{d}_{k})\parallel }^{2}$ .

If Armijo's rule is not satisfied, that is if $F({\rho}_{q})>F(0)+\omega F'(0){\rho}_{q}$ , then one models $F(\rho )$ by a polynomial of order 3 (cubic interpolation):

$c(\rho )=a{\rho}^{3}+b{\rho}^{2}+F'(0)\rho +F(0)$

with

$\left[\begin{array}{}a\end{array}\right]=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{1}-{\rho}_{2}}\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{1}{{\rho}_{1}^{2}}& \frac{-1}{{\rho}_{2}^{2}}\\ \frac{-{\rho}_{2}}{{\rho}_{1}^{2}}& \frac{{\rho}_{1}}{{\rho}_{2}^{2}}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{}F({\rho}_{1})-F(0)-F'(0){\rho}_{1}\\ F({\rho}_{2})-F(0)-F'(0){\rho}_{2}\end{array}\right]$

where ${\rho}_{1}$ and ${\rho}_{2}$ are the last two failed values of $\rho$ .

The minimal point of $c(\rho )$ is:

${\rho}_{c}=\frac{-b+\sqrt{{b}^{2}-\mathrm{3a}F'(0)}}{\mathrm{3a}}$ .

The cubic interpolations are continued as long as Armijo's rule is not satisfied. In practice, the number of cubic interpolations is limited to 2.

#### 3.3.2 Implementation of the line search in PLASTI

The system to be solved is:

$f(\Delta y)=0$

The following Newton iterations are carried out:

- updating of the variables at the time instant «+»
- computation of the residual $f(\Delta {y}_{i})=0$
- computation of the Jacobian $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(\Delta {y}_{i})$
- solution of the linear system $\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}(\Delta {y}_{i}).\delta \Delta {y}_{i}=-f(\Delta {y}_{i})$
- line search: computation of $\rho$
- updating of the solution: $\Delta {y}_{i+1}=\Delta {y}_{i}+\rho \delta \Delta {y}_{i}$

For the line-search stage:

The functional $F(\rho )=\frac{1}{2}{\parallel f({y}_{i}+\rho .\delta \Delta {y}_{i})\parallel }^{2}$ is introduced.

The parameters of the line search are fixed in the code:

| Parameter of Armijo's rule | $\omega =0,1$ |
| --- | --- |
| Minimum bound for the relaxation | ${\rho}_{\min}=0,1$ |
| Maximum bound for the relaxation | ${\rho}_{\max}=0,5$ |
| Number of cubic-interpolation iterations | ${\mathrm{it}}_{\max}=2$ |

**Note:**

*Some authors take even smaller values for* $\omega$ *(for example* ${10}^{-4}$ *).*

## 4 Implementation of a new constitutive model

### 4.1 Possible methods

Using the previous architectures, a new constitutive model can be developed relatively easily:

- the simplest method is the explicit integration, by the RUNGE_KUTTA method (see § 2);
- the complete implicit integration by the NEWTON method in the PLASTI environment (see § 3) requires a little more development, in particular the computation of the Jacobian matrix;
- it is nevertheless possible to use directly the development carried out for the explicit integration (2 routines), without an additional routine, in order to carry out analyses with implicit integration, the Jacobian matrix then being computed by perturbation.

### 4.2 Implementation of a new constitutive model by the explicit method

In order to implement a constitutive model XXX, usable with ALGO_INTE_='RUNGE_KUTTA', it suffices to write 2 routines:

- a routine for recovery of the material data, called by the dispatching routine LCMATE
- a routine computing the time derivatives of the internal variables, called by the dispatching routine LCDVIN.

The updating of the catalogues concerns only DEFI_MATERIAU and C_COMPORTEMENT, as well as the catalogue of the constitutive law (cf. [D5.04.01](doc:d5.04.01)).

### 4.3 Complete implementation of a new constitutive behaviour in PLASTI

Without giving a precise computer description of the developments (see on this subject [D5.04.01](doc:d5.04.01)), one can nevertheless describe the architecture of PLASTI and the places where one must intervene in order to integrate a new constitutive behaviour.

General architecture of PLASTI:

  - Call to the routines recovering the material parameters (at the time instant ${t}_{i}$ because possibly depending on the command variables): LCMATE => specific routines for recovery of the material, common with RUNGE_KUTTA (§3)
- Elastic integration over $\Delta t$ : LCELAS=> by default, isotropic or anisotropic linear elasticity; if necessary, call to specific routines for the elastic computation XXXELA.
- Evaluation of the threshold for the elastic integration: LCCNVX => call to the routines for evaluation of the threshold XXXCVX
- If $\mathrm{seuil}>0$ , then solution by Newton: LCPLAS which calls LCPLNL, which carries out the Newton loop. Its structure is the following:

  - Initialisation: LCINIT => by default initialisation to zero: ${y}^{0}=0$ ; if necessary, call to the specific initialisation routines XXXINI
  - Newton iterations:
  - Incrementation of the solution ${y}^{k}={y}_{i-1}+\Delta {y}^{k}$

  - computation of the residual $-f(\Delta {y}^{k})$ LCRESI => call to the routines computing the residual XXXRES

  - computation of the Jacobian matrix $\frac{\text{df}}{{\text{dy}}_{k}}$ : LCJACB => call to the specific routines XXXJAC
  - solution of the linear system $\frac{\partial {f}^{l}}{\partial \Delta {y}_{k}}d(\Delta {y}_{k})\text{= -}{f}^{l}(\Delta {y}_{k})$ by the Gauss method

  - incrementation of $\Delta {y}_{k+1}=\Delta {y}_{k}+d(\Delta {y}_{k})$

  - convergence test LCCONV => by default, test on the relative error on the solution $\mid {y}_{k+1}-{y}_{k}\mid <\epsilon \mid {y}_{k+1}\mid$ ; if necessary, call to the specific routines XXXCVG

  - if non-convergence, continuation of the iterations, except if the maximum number is reached, in which case one leaves PLASTI with a return code, in order to activate the local or global subdivision of the time step (according to the user data: ITER_INTE_PAS for the local subdivision and DEFI_LIST_INST for the global subdivision).

  - If convergence, incrementation ${y}^{k}={y}_{i-1}+\Delta {y}^{k}$ and exit.

In order to implement a constitutive model XXX, one must therefore write at least the routines:

- XXXMAT: recovery of the material data, called by LCMATE
- XXXCVX for the evaluation of the initial threshold
- XXXRES: computation of the residual, called by LCRESI
- XXXJAC: computation of the Jacobian matrix, called by LCJACB.
- One can also modify the following routines, if need be, in order to define in a particular manner:
- the elasticity XXXELA called by LCELAS
- the initialisation XXXINI called by LCINIT
- the convergence test XXXCVG, called by LCCONV.

The updating of the catalogues concerns only DEFI_MATERIAU and C_COMPORTEMENT, as well as the catalogue of the constitutive law (see [D5.04.01](doc:d5.04.01)).

### 4.4 Easy implicit implementation of a new constitutive model in the PLASTI environment

It is possible to use directly the 2 routines (recovery of the material data, derivatives of the internal variables) used with ALGO_INTE_='RUNGE_KUTTA' in order to carry out an implicit integration. Indeed, the system of differential equations solved by RUNGE_KUTTA can be written:

$\sigma ={\sigma}_{i-1}+\Delta \sigma ={\sigma}_{i-1}+A(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}^{n}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}(Y))=G(Y)$

$\frac{dY}{dt}=F(Y,t;\sigma )$

where $Y$ represents the set of the internal variables of the model. It is assumed here that the first values of $Y$ are the components of the plastic-strain variation $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ . The relation between the stress tensor and the elastic part of the strain tensor is generally linear, but can be evaluated in a non-linear manner by a specific expression.

Once the routine making it possible to compute $\frac{dY}{dt}=F(Y,t;\sigma )$ has been programmed, it is possible to use it for an implicit integration, which consists in solving (cf. 3.1):

$R(\Delta Z)=0=\left[\begin{array}{c}{R}_{1}(\Delta Z)\\ {R}_{2}(\Delta Z)\end{array}\right]$ with $\Delta Z=(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \sigma \\ \Delta Y\end{array})={Z}_{i}-{Z}_{i-1}$ .

- The first system of equations represents the elastic stress-strain relation: ${R}_{1}(\Delta Z)=\sigma -G(Y)=0$ ;
- The second expresses the evolution laws of the various internal variables: ${R}_{2}(\Delta Z)=\Delta Y-\Delta t.F(Y,t;\sigma )=0$ , , where $G$ and $F$ are computed by the explicit routine.

In order to solve this system by the Newton method proposed in the PLASTI environment, that is:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}^{l}}{\partial \Delta {Z}_{k}}d(\Delta {Z}_{k})\text{= -}{f}^{l}(\Delta {Z}_{k})\\ \Delta {Z}_{k+1}=\Delta {Z}_{k}+d(\Delta {Z}_{k})\end{array}$ , one must have the Jacobian matrix $\frac{\partial {f}^{l}}{\partial \Delta {Z}_{k}}$ .

Its computation can be carried out automatically by perturbation: it suffices to use ALGO_INTE='NEWTON_PERT'.

Moreover, the tangent matrix is then computed directly from the Jacobian matrix according to the method described in § 3.2.

In summary, this procedure makes it possible, with the only two routines necessary for the explicit integration, to use an implicit integration, and to benefit from a tangent matrix.

The updating of the catalogues concerns only DEFI_MATERIAU and C_COMPORTEMENT, as well as the catalogue of the constitutive law (cf. [D5.04.01](doc:d5.04.01)).

The implementation of this method is illustrated in the test SSNV225, for a creep law.
