# u4.53.03 Operator DYNA_VIBRA

## 1 Purpose

DYNA_VIBRA makes it possible to carry out a transient or harmonic response analysis on a linear model, on a physical basis or on a modal basis. It can also treat localised non-linearities for transient response analyses on a modal basis.

The concept produced depends on the type of analysis carried out (tran_gene, dyna_trans, harm_gene, dyna_harmo or acou_harmo).

## 5 Transient response analysis on a modal basis

Let us consider the finite-element model of a linear structure, presenting internal dissipation mechanisms, subjected to external forces, which may equally well be known a priori (loadings) as have to be evaluated as the analysis proceeds (shock forces, contact, etc.). In the time domain, the equilibrium relation is written:

$$
\left[{\mathrm{K}}_{e}\right] \mathrm{x}(t) + \left[{\mathrm{D}}_{v}\right] \dot{\mathrm{x}}(t) + \left[\mathrm{M}\right] \ddot{\mathrm{x}}(t) = \left[\mathrm{B}\right]\mathrm{u}(t)
$$

where ${\mathrm{K}}_{e}$ is the stiffness matrix of the structure, ${\mathrm{D}}_{v}$ the matrix associated with the viscous dissipation, and $\mathrm{M}$ the mass matrix. One also denotes by $\mathrm{x}$ the vector of the degrees of freedom. The external-force term is defined by the product of a force-localisation matrix $\mathrm{B}$ and of a vector $\mathrm{u}$ specifying the time evolution of the excitation. This formulation makes it possible to separate the spatial and temporal components of a force often denoted $\mathrm{f}(t)$.
One therefore has, in this case:

$$
\mathrm{f}(t)=\left[\mathrm{B}\right]\mathrm{u}(t)
$$

The solution of the problem on the basis of the complete model presented in the relation above can be prohibitive for models of industrial size. To get round this difficulty, one proposes to build a reduced model possessing the same spectral properties as the complete problem. One therefore postulates the existence of a reduction basis ${\mathrm{T}}_{r}$ which allows a reasonable representation of the behaviour of the structure over the frequency band of interest. Under these conditions, one therefore has:

$$
\mathrm{x}\approx \left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]{\mathrm{q}}_{r}
$$

The terms ${\mathrm{q}}_{r}$ of the linear combination correspond to the generalised amplitudes of the complete problem projected onto the basis ${\mathrm{T}}_{r}$. These amplitudes therefore satisfy, in the general case:

$$
\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}{\mathrm{K}}_{e}{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]{\mathrm{q}}_{r}(t)+\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}{\mathrm{D}}_{v}{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]\dot{{\mathrm{q}}_{r}}(t)+\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}\mathrm{M}{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]\ddot{{\mathrm{q}}_{r}}(t)={\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}\mathrm{B}\mathrm{u}(t)
$$

One therefore solves here a system of smaller size whose unknowns are the generalised amplitudes ${\mathrm{q}}_{r}$ .

$\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}{\mathrm{K}}_{e}{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]$ denotes the generalised stiffness matrix.

$\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}{\mathrm{D}}_{v}{\mathrm{R}}_{r}\right]$ denotes the generalised damping matrix.

$\left[{\mathrm{T}}_{r}^{T}\mathrm{M}{\mathrm{T}}_{r}\right]$ denotes the generalised mass matrix.

The basis ${\mathrm{T}}_{r}$ is composed, in the majority of cases, of eigenmodes of the structure under study. This selection of modes may be enriched with particular responses, in order to represent the dynamics of the response better, while avoiding recourse to too large a number of eigenmodes [U2.06.04](doc:u2.06.04). One may cite, for the usual cases:

- The static corrections associated with a point of application of a force (external, or shock), computed by imposing either a unit force or a unit displacement;
- The modes with imposed accelerations, in order to represent the seismic response, or the entrainment of an embedded substructure in translation through its interfaces;
- Any other term necessary for a good representation of the quasi-static response of the structure or of each substructure.

These corrective terms may be computed via the operator MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14) for the generic cases, or by direct analysis, via MECA_STATIQUE [U4.51.01](doc:u4.51.01), or MACR_ELAS_MULT [U4.51.02](doc:u4.51.02). All these vectors are then concatenated via the operator DEFI_BASE_MODALE [U4.64.02](doc:u4.64.02). By an abuse of language, this family of vectors will hereafter be called the “modal basis”.

##### 5.3.1.4 SCHEMA = 'ADAPT_ORDRE2': explicit scheme of order 2

This scheme (called 'ADAPT' in earlier versions of the code) supports the analysis taking into account all the localised non-linearities available. This method uses the centred-difference scheme; the time-step adaptation algorithm relies on the computation of an “apparent frequency”:

$$
{f}_{\mathit{APt}}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{∣\frac{\ddot{{x}_{t}}-\ddot{{x}_{t-1}}}{{x}_{t}-{x}_{t-1}}∣}
$$

The operands specific to the method of integration by adaptive time steps are specified below. They are the following operands of the factor keyword INCREMENT:

```text
◊    NB_POIN_PERIODE = N
```

Number of points per apparent period. It is this parameter which sets the accuracy of the analysis. It must be at least equal to 20; its default value (50) guarantees a satisfactory accuracy (of the order of 1%) in most cases.

```text
◊    VITE_MIN =
```

Method of computation of the reference velocity used to evaluate the apparent frequency.

When the denominator of the apparent frequency (${x}_{n}-{x}_{n-1}$ ) becomes small, the latter may become very high, which leads to an unjustified refinement of the time step. To remedy this, the algorithm uses the following criterion:

$$
\frac{\mid {x}_{n}-{x}_{n-1}\mid }{\Delta t}\le {V}_{min}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }{f}_{\mathit{APn}}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{∣\frac{\ddot{{x}_{n}}-\ddot{{x}_{n-1}}}{{V}_{min}\Delta t}∣}
$$

${V}_{min}$ may be computed in two different ways according to the value of VITE_MIN.

If VITE_MIN = 'NORM': then ${V}_{min}({t}_{n})=\frac{\parallel V({t}_{n})\parallel }{100}$ for all the degrees of freedom. This case may be used:

- if the system has several degrees of freedom,
- if the order of magnitude of the displacement is not too different from one degree of freedom to another.

If VITE_MIN = 'MAXI': ${V}_{min}^{i}({t}_{n})=\frac{\underset{0<{t}_{p}<{t}_{n}}{\mathit{Max}}(\mid {V}^{i}({t}_{p})\mid )}{100}$ for the degree of freedom $i$ . This case may be used:

- if the system has a small number of degrees of freedom (from 1 to 3),
- for a system with several degrees of freedom, in the case where the order of magnitude of the displacement is very different from one degree of freedom to another (for example in the presence of Lagrange degrees of freedom in substructuring),
- if the order of magnitude of the velocity does not vary too much over time.

```text
◊    NMAX_ITER_PAS = N
```

Maximum number of reductions of the time step per analysis step. It is equal to 16 by default, which limits the reduction factor of the step to ${0,75}^{16}={10}^{-2}$ per iteration (when the time step is too large, the analysis is resumed with a smaller step: $\Delta {t}_{n}'=0,75\Delta {t}_{n}$ ).

NMAX_ITER_PAS may be:

- increased in order to allow the time step to drop more sharply,
- decreased if the time step seems excessively refined, for example in the presence of discontinuities (dry friction, discontinuous excitation, ...).

If, at a given time instant, this maximum number of successive reductions of the time step is reached, then the code will nevertheless consider that the final step is correct and move on to the next step. An alarm message is then issued, which signals a possible risk of loss of accuracy and which advises the user to relaunch the analysis with modified parameters (by acting on PAS, NMAX_ITER_PAS and/or COEF_DIVI_PAS) in order to get past the difficulty with a smaller time step.

```text
◊    COEF_MULT_PAS = cmp
```

Coefficient of increase of the step when the error is sufficiently small:

$\Delta {t}_{n}<\frac{0,75}{{\mathit{Nf}}_{\mathit{APn}}}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\Delta {t}_{n+1}=\text{cmp}\Delta {t}_{n}$ .

Its default value ($\text{cmp}=1.1$ ) guarantees stability and accuracy, but it may in general be increased (at most up to $1.3$ ) in order to speed up the integration.

```text
◊    COEF_DIVI_PAS = cdp
```

Coefficient of refinement of the time step ($>1$ ) when the error is greater than 1, the maximum number of iterations (N_MAX_ITER_PAS) has not been reached and the minimum time step has not been reached:

$\Delta {t}_{n}<\frac{1}{{\mathit{Nf}}_{\mathit{APn}}}$ , ${N}_{\mathit{iter}}<{N}_{\mathit{iter}\text{\_}max}$ and $\Delta {t}_{n}>\text{plr}\Delta {t}_{\mathit{initial}}$

$\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\Delta {t}_{n}=\frac{\Delta {t}_{n}}{\text{cdp}}$

The default value is $1.33333334$ , that is a reduction by a factor $0.75$ .

```text
◊    PAS_LIMI_RELA = plr
```

Coefficient applied to the initial time step in order to define the refinement limit and therefore the minimum time step:

The default value is $1.33333334$ , that is a reduction by a factor $0.75$ .

$\Delta {T}_{min}=\text{plr}\Delta {t}_{\mathit{initial}}$

##### 5.3.1.5 SCHEMA = 'RUNGE_KUTTA_54': explicit scheme with adaptive step.

This scheme belongs to the family of integration schemes of Runge-Kutta type. In particular, it is the explicit integration scheme of Dormand-Prince(54) [R5.06.04](doc:r5.06.04) with adaptive time step.

The scheme 'RUNGE_KUTTA_54' supports account being taken of all the non-linearities available in the operator.

The computation of the optimal time step is carried out by controlling the error between the approximations of order 5 and 4 of the prediction of the state vector (concatenation of the displacement and velocity vectors).

This scheme relies on the following relative-error control condition:

$$
\mathit{err}\le \mathit{tol}
$$

With:

$$
\mathit{err}=\frac{1}{n}{\sum}_{i}\sqrt{\text{}(\frac{{y}_{\mathit{i1}}-{\hat{y}}_{\mathit{i1}}}{{\mathit{sc}}_{i}})\mathrm{²}}
\textrm{et}
{\mathit{sc}}_{i}=\mathit{MAX}(\mid {y}_{\mathit{i0}}\mid ,\mid {y}_{\mathit{i1}}\mid )+\alpha
$$

Where:

- ${y}_{\mathit{i1}}$ is the value of the order-5 prediction of component $i$ of the state vector $y$
- ${\hat{y}}_{\mathrm{1i}}$ is the value of the order-4 prediction of component $i$ of the state vector $y$
- $n$ is the size of the state vector $y$
- ${y}_{\mathrm{0i}}$ is the value of component $i$ of the state vector $y$ at the current state

```text
◊    TOLERANCE = tol
```

Value of the relative-error control given by the user. By default it is equal to 1.E-3.

```text
◊    ALPHA = alpha
```

Value of the regularisation given by the user, entering into the expression for ${\mathit{sc}}_{i}$ . By default it is equal to 1.E-3.

##### 5.3.1.6 SCHEMA = 'RUNGE_KUTTA_32': explicit scheme with adaptive step.

This is the default scheme for the transient analysis on a modal basis.

Like the scheme 'RUNGE_KUTTA_54', the scheme 'RUNGE_KUTTA_32' belongs to the family of integration schemes of Runge-Kutta type. In this case, it is the explicit integration scheme of Bogacki-Shampine(32) [R5.06.04](doc:r5.06.04) with adaptive time step.

Like the previous scheme, it supports account being taken of all the non-linearities available in the operator.

For this scheme, the computation of the optimal time step is carried out by controlling the error between the approximations of order 3 and 2 of the prediction of the state vector. The computation of the optimal time step itself is carried out in a manner analogous to the previous scheme.

##### 5.3.1.7 SCHEMA = 'ADAPT_ORDRE1': explicit scheme of order 1

This scheme is a variant of the previous scheme 'ADAPT_ORDRE2'. It is in fact a version of the Euler scheme with adaptive time step. Apart from this difference, this scheme is used in the same way as the adaptive scheme of order 2: the syntax of the keywords is the same, and so are the methods for driving the time step.

##### 5.3.1.8 SCHEMA = 'ITMI': integral scheme for the analysis of the response of very lightly damped mechanical systems

This integration scheme by the integral method makes it possible, for lightly damped systems, to obtain an exact response provided that the dynamic matrices of mass, stiffness and damping are diagonal.

##### 5.3.1.9 SCHEMA = 'TRBDF2': implicit scheme of order 2

This dissipative predictor-corrector scheme supports the analysis taking into account all the localised non-linearities available. It has very good stability properties, which make it a good candidate for stiff problems.

#### 5.8.1 Account taken of the neglected modes by static correction: keywords CORR_STAT, MODE_CORR and D_FONC_*

During the seismic analysis of a singly excited structure, it is possible to take into account, a posteriori, the static effect of the neglected modes. In this case, on return to the physical basis, the relative displacements computed (respectively the relative velocities and accelerations) are corrected by a pseudo-mode.

The details of this type of correction will be found in [R4.05.01](doc:r4.05.01).

Inside the factor keyword EXCIT, CORR_STAT='OUI' allows the neglected modes to be taken into account by static correction; it is then obligatory to supply the keywords MODE_CORR, D_FONC_DT and D_FONC_DT2.

```text
◊    MODE_CORR = modcor
```

Concept of type mult_elas produced by the macro-command MACRO_ELAS_MULT [U4.51.02](doc:u4.51.02), or mode_meca, which corresponds to the linear static response of the structure to a unit loading of imposed-force type (uniform acceleration) in the earthquake direction considered.

One notes that there are as many load cases as earthquake directions.

```text
◊    EXCIT = _F( CORR_STAT )
```

If MODE_CORR is present, CORR_STAT='OUI' makes it possible to take into account the contribution of the modal correction a posteriori for each occurrence of the keyword EXCIT.

```text
◊     EXCIT = _F( D_FONC_DTet D_FONC_DT2)
```

D_FONC_DT and D_FONC_DT2 are respectively the first derivatives and the second derivatives with respect to time of the accelerogram defined, in each seismic direction considered, by the operand FONC_MULT. They weight the contribution of the modal correction a posteriori for each occurrence of the keyword EXCIT in order to obtain respectively the velocity and acceleration corrections on the physical basis.

**Notes:**

- *Taking the static correction into account excludes that of multi-support excitation.*
- *The concept* mult_elas *must be based on a numbering of the equations consistent (same profile and same renumbering option) with that of the system solved in the operator* DYNA_VIBRA*.*
- *To the* $i$ *th occurrence of the keyword* EXCIT *there corresponds the* $i$ *th elastic solution of* MODCOR*.*
- *For the static correction to be effectively taken into account on return to the physical coordinates in the operator* REST_GENE_PHYS *or* RECU_FONCTION*, one must specify* CORR_STAT_='OUI'.

#### 5.8.2 Account taken of multi-support excitation: keywords MODE_STAT, MULTI_APPUI and ACCE, VITE, DEPL

In the case of a multi-supported structure, in order to recover the computed quantities in the absolute frame or to take localised non-linearities into account, one must compute the generalised response taking the entrainment component into account.

### 5.10 Keyword VERI_CHOC

Keyword which makes it possible to evaluate a posteriori the ability of the modal basis to represent the impacts correctly.

If VERI_CHOC is present, one computes at each shock node and for each mode the rate of reconstitution of the static solution: ${t}_{s}={K}_{\mathit{statique}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{{({}^{T}{\Phi}_{i}.{F}_{\mathit{impo}})}^{2}}{{k}_{i}}$ and, for information, the rate of reconstitution of the shear force: ${t}_{N}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{{}^{T}{\Phi}_{i}.{F}_{\mathit{impo}}}{{k}_{i}}.({}^{T}{F}_{\mathit{impo}}.K.{\Phi}_{i})$ . One then computes the values cumulated over all the modes which constitute the modal basis used.

One checks that the ratio of the neglected flexibility (static flexibility minus reconstituted static flexibility) to the shock flexibility remains lower than the value given by the operand SEUIL (SEUIL is equal to 0.5 by default); otherwise:

- if STOP_CRITERE= ‘OUI’ one stops the execution of the program (this is the default case);
- if STOP_CRITERE= ‘NON’ one continues the execution of the program with the issue of an alarm.

**Notes:**

- *This* *functionality is available only for obstacles of type* *plan or* *bi_plan.*
- *If the rate of reconstitution of the static solution is lower than the value of the threshold, the user is advised to complete the modal basis with the local modes at the shock points which have a large local flexibility.*
- *The formula is not applicable in the case of static modes (stiffness matrix not invertible). The analysis then continues without checking of the shock criteria, and the user is warned of this.*

##### 5.11.1.7 Operand ANGL_VRIL

```text
◊    ANGL_VRIL= gamma
```

$\gamma$ , angle in degrees completing the definition of the angular position of the local frame in the global frame.

By convention, the normal to the cutting plane of the obstacle, NORM_OBST, defines the axis ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ of the local frame. For obstacles of type PLAN or BI_PLAN, this normal is not unique. Any vector lying in the plane of the obstacle is a vector normal to one of the cutting planes of the obstacle. For all the other obstacles, this orientation is unique.

One passes from the global frame $(X,Y,Z)$ to the local frame of the plane of the obstacle $({X}_{\mathit{loc}}, {Y}_{\mathit{loc}}, {Z}_{\mathit{loc}})$ by a product of three rotations following the Euler angles:

- A first rotation $R_1(\alpha, Z)$ of angle $\alpha$ about the axis $Z$ of the global frame;
- A second rotation $R_2(\beta, Y_1)$ of angle $\beta$ about the vector ${Y}_{1}$, the image of $Y$ under $R_1(\alpha, Z)$;
- A third rotation $R_3(\gamma, X_2)$ of angle $\gamma$ about the vector ${X}_{2}$, the image of $X_1$ under $R_2(\beta, Y_1)$.

The first figure below gives the position of the plane of an obstacle of type PLAN_Z (or BI_PLAN_Z) in the global frame $(X, Y, Z)$, and the associated local frame $({X}_{\mathit{loc}}, {Y}_{\mathit{loc}}, {Z}_{\mathit{loc}})$. The second figure below describes the set of rotations, with their parameters, allowing the passage from the global frame to the local frame.

![Figure](/figures/u4.53.03/Composition_full_V2.png)

*Position of the obstacle and of its local frame in the global frame, and view of the complete composition of the rotations.*

![Figure](/figures/u4.53.03/Angles_Euler_V1.png)

*Decomposition of the complete motion into elementary rotations parameterised by the Euler angles.*

The angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are determined automatically from the normal to the obstacle ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$. $\alpha$ corresponds to the angle formed by $X$ and $X_1$, where $X_1$ is the projection of ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ onto the plane $(X, Y)$. $\beta$ is the angle formed by $X_1$ and $X_{\mathit{loc}}$. If $X_{\mathit{loc}}$ is collinear with $Z$, then $\alpha$ is taken equal to 0.

The sole datum $X_{\mathit{loc}}$ does not make it possible to position the local frame completely; one must then define $\gamma$, called the twist angle, in order to position the obstacle completely:

- For all types of obstacles, with the exception of obstacles of type BI_PLAN, the default value of $\gamma$ is zero. **It is then essential to check that this value is indeed the one expected**, or, otherwise, to indicate the desired value;
- In the case of shocks between moving structures with obstacles of type BI_PLAN, **if the user specifies nothing, the twist angle is computed by the code**. The procedure for computing the twist angle for the case of obstacles of type BI_PLAN is detailed below.

If one denotes by ${N_1}{N_2}$ the vector defined by the nodes of GROUP_NO_1 and GROUP_NO_2, the twist angle is computed from the directions of the vectors ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ and ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$, where ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ is obtained by projection:

- of the vector ${N_1}{N_2}$ onto the plane normal to ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ for the case of an obstacle BI_PLAN_Y;
- of the vector ${N_1}{N_2} \wedge {X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ onto the plane normal to ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ for the case of an obstacle BI_PLAN_Z,

and ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$ completes the triad, so that (${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$, ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$, ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$) is right-handed.

If one does not pay attention to this point, and if one does not specify the operand ANGL_VRIL, it is possible to obtain results which are surprising at first sight. One solution making it possible to guard against the results of this computation consists in arranging that the direction normal to the contact (${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ for an obstacle of type BI_PLAN_Y, or ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$ for an obstacle of type BI_PLAN_Z) lies in the plane formed by the vectors ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ and ${N}_{1}{N}_{2}$. This amounts to choosing ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ such that:

- ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ lies in the plane of the obstacle;
- ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ lies in the plane formed by the vector ${N_1}{N_2}$ and the vector normal to the contact direction (${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ or ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$, according to the type of obstacle chosen).

This choice of ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$, given the procedure for computing the twist angle, guarantees the correct orientation of the vectors ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ and ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$, whatever the type of obstacle chosen.

In all the other cases of choice of ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$, it is strongly recommended to specify the twist angle, in order to avoid unexpected behaviours. This need is illustrated by means of the following simple case.

The model consists of two beams, oriented by the axis $Y$ of the global frame, which may strike one another at the nodes ${N}_{1}$ and ${N}_{2}$ along the direction carried by the axis $Z$ of the global frame. These two beams lie in a plane parallel to the plane $(Y, Z)$, but the vector ${N_1}{N_2}$ is not collinear with $Z$. The definition of an associated obstacle of type BI_PLAN is represented in the figure below.

![Figure](/figures/u4.53.03/Cas_souhaite_bi_plan.png)

*Representation of the desired contact case.*

To obtain this result, one wishes to use an obstacle of type BI_PLAN_Z. By definition, the direction ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ must be normal to the cutting plane of the obstacle (and therefore, here, contained in the planes defining the obstacles). It is possible to choose ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ as being the direction $X$ of the global frame (case (a) in the figure below, with NORM_OBST = (1, 0, 0)), but also as being the direction ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$ of the global frame (case (b) in this same figure, with NORM_OBST = (0, 1, 0)). Any other choice of vector in the plane (${X}$, ${Y}$) is possible. On the other hand, only case (b) corresponds to the recommendation formulated.

![Figure](/figures/u4.53.03/Choix_possibles_pour_Xloc_Bi_plan_Z.png)

*Representation of 2 possible choices for ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ (operand NORM_OBST), and of the desired contact direction ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$.*

The figure below represents the planes associated with this obstacle in cases (a) and (b) **when the operand ANGL_VRIL is not supplied**, and when it is the code which computes it. It appears that case (a) does not correspond to what is desired (as in all the cases where ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ is not collinear with $Y$), since the vector ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$ obtained is not collinear with the vector $Z$, which is desired in order to define the contact direction. ${Y}_{\mathit{loc}}$, on the other hand, does indeed correspond to the direction obtained by projection of the vector ${N_1}{N_2} \wedge {X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ onto the plane normal to ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$, and ${Z}_{\mathit{loc}}$ completes the triad. To obtain the desired result, in this case, one must specify ANGL_VRIL = 0.

In case (b), on the other hand, since the vector ${X}_{\mathit{loc}}$ does indeed lie in the plane formed by the vector ${N_1}{N_2}$ and the desired contact direction, one does obtain the desired result without needing to specify the operand ANGL_VRIL.

#### 5.11.2 Localised non-linearities of a cracked rotor: ROTOR_FISS

The following operands are specific to the transient analysis with a localised non-linearity of “cracked rotor” type for analyses of a shaft line modelled in 1D (beam). The crack is considered to be wholly contained in a cross-section of the rotor. It is bounded by two distinct nodes but with coincident coordinates, one connected to the left-hand part of the shaft line, the other to the right-hand part. They represent respectively the left lip and the right lip of the crack.

The behaviour of the crack is given by a crack-stiffness law and its derivative. This law is determined elsewhere by 3D analyses in quasi-statics. It does not depend on the geometry of the rotor but only on the shape of the crack and on a dimensional coefficient.

◊    RELATION = 'ROTOR_FISS'

> /    ♦    GROUP_NO_G    =    node group naming the left lip of the crack

> ♦    GROUP_NO_D    =    node group naming the right lip of the crack
> ♦    ANGL_INIT        =    initial angle of the crack front with respect to its definition in the constitutive law of the crack [by default 0.0]
> ◊    ANGL_ROTA        =    function giving the imposed law of angular position of the crack front with respect to its definition in the constitutive law of the crack (speed transients)
> ♦    K_PHI             =    constitutive law in stiffness of the crack
> ♦     DK_DPHI         =    derivative of the constitutive law in stiffness

**Orientation of the axis of the rotor:**

In order to respect the trigonometric sense of rotation, it is important to orient the rotor correctly: the axis of the rotor is automatically oriented by the crack, going from the left edge towards the right edge of the crack.

##### 5.11.4.3 Operands related to the constitutive behaviour

The constitutive behaviour DIS_VISC is a non-linear viscoelastic rheological constitutive behaviour, of extended Zener type, making it possible to represent schematically the behaviour of a uniaxial damper, between two nodes.

#### 5.11.8 Non-linearity: FLAMBAGE

```text
◊    RELATION = 'FLAMBAGE'
```

This relation is used for the detection of possible buckling and for the evaluation of the residual strain of an element during a shock between two moving structures or between a moving structure and a fixed wall. The reaction force during a shock with account taken of buckling is given by three functions of the penetration (defined by the user), namely the threshold force, the buckling stiffness and the buckling damping.

One considers that there is buckling if the reaction force $F$ reaches the limit value $F_{\mathrm{seuil}}$ defined by the user. At each time instant, if the shock is detected, the force is given by:

$$
\begin{split}F = \left\{\begin{array}{ll} K(u_{\max})\times u + C(u_{\max})\times v   \ \ \text{si} \ K(u_{\max})\times u < F_{\mathrm{seuil}}(u_{\max})  \\
                                F_{\mathrm{seuil}}(u_{\max}) + C(u_{\max}) \times v \ \ \ \text{sinon} \end{array}\right.,\end{split}
$$

where $u$ denotes the compression between the two nodes, $v$ the relative velocity of the nodes, and $u_{\max}$ the maximum compression reached in the course of the analysis, defined by $u_{\max}(t) = \max_{\tau \le t}u(\tau)$ for every time instant $t$. $F_{\mathrm{seuil}}$, $K$ and $C$ denote respectively the threshold force, the buckling stiffness and the buckling damping.

Only the operands specific to the keyword FLAMBAGE are detailed. The other keywords make it possible to define the locations of the shock and are identical to the operands for the relation DIS_CHOC (cf. § 5.11.1 ).

```text
♦ FX = fx,                [fonction]
♦ RIGI_NOR = rigi_nor,    [fonction]
♦ AMOR_NOR = amor_nor,    [fonction]
```

The description of the non-linear constitutive behaviour is given by three functions, namely the threshold force (FX), the buckling stiffness (RIGI_NOR) and the buckling damping (AMOR_NOR). Each function must satisfy the following criteria:

- It is a function in the sense of code_aster: defined with the operator DEFI_FONCTION,
- The interpolations on the axes of the abscissae and of the ordinates are linear,
- The name of the abscissa when defining the function is DX.
- The function must be defined by **at least** 2 points,
- The abscissae of the function must be strictly increasing.

The units of the abscissae and of the ordinates must be consistent with those of the problem:

- The unit of the abscissae must be homogeneous with displacements,
- The unit of the function must be homogeneous with forces for FX, with stiffnesses for RIGI_NOR, and with dampings for AMOR_NOR.

```text
◊     CRIT_AMOR=| 'INCLUS', [DEFAUT]
                | 'EXCLUS'
```

The keyword CRIT_AMOR makes it possible to specify whether the buckling criterion is compared with the total shock force, or only with the part linked to the shock stiffness. If CRIT_AMOR='INCLUS', the buckling criterion $F_{\mathrm{seuil}}$ is compared with the total shock force $K(u_{\max})\times u+C(u_{\max})\times v$ . In this case the total shock force can never exceed the buckling criterion. If CRIT_AMOR='EXCLUS' the viscous force is not included when comparing with the criterion. The buckling criterion is compared with the force generated by the stiffness and the penetration $K(u_{\max})\times u$ . In this case the total shock force may be greater than the buckling criterion.

### 5.15 Checking phase

#### 5.15.1 Check on the matrices

In the case of an analysis by modal recombination, one checks that the generalised matrices do indeed come from a projection onto a common basis and with the same number of basis vectors. In the case of an analysis by dynamic substructuring, one checks that the generalised matrices do indeed come from the same generalised numbering.

#### 5.15.2 Check and advice on the choice of the time step for the schemes DIFF_CENTRE, DEVOGE and NEWMARK:

One makes sure that the time step chosen satisfies the stability conditions of the numerical scheme (CFL criterion):

- in the case of NEWMARK, stability is always ensured, but exceeding the criterion may induce a lack of accuracy on the result and is signalled by a message; the analysis continues (at the risk of producing a result of poor accuracy or a wrong result).
- in the case of the schemes DIFF_CENTRE and DEVOGE, if the operand VERI_PAS is equal to 'OUI' (default value), the execution is stopped, and a minimum time step is proposed. If the operand VERI_PAS is equal to 'NON' or if it is an adaptive scheme, an alarm message is issued and the analysis continues (at the risk of producing a result of poor accuracy or a wrong result).

In a transient analysis without non-linearity, one must take care that the time step is such that:

$\mathit{dt}<0,1/{f}_{n}$ for NEWMARK and DEVOGE

$\mathit{dt}<0,05/{f}_{n}$ for DIFF_CENTRE

${f}_{n}$ being the highest frequency of the modes of the modal basis considered.

**Note:**

*One mentions that, with localised non-linearities, the time step chosen must sometimes be very much smaller than this recommended value.*

## 6 Transient response analysis on a physical basis

The operator carries out the direct time integration of a linear transient mechanical problem of the form:

$$
\mathrm{M}\ddot{\mathrm{x}}+\mathrm{C}\dot{\mathrm{x}}+\mathrm{K}\mathrm{x}=\sum_{i}{\alpha}_{i}(t){\mathrm{F}}_{i}(\mathrm{x})
$$

where the matrices $\mathrm{M},\mathrm{C},\mathrm{K}$ are the real assembled matrices of the finite-element problem, respectively of mass, of damping and of stiffness of the system.

The ${\alpha}_{i}$ are functions of time (cf. DEFI_FONCTION [U4.31.02](doc:u4.31.02)) and the ${\mathrm{F}}_{i}$ are assembled vectors coming from loadings in imposed force (cf. AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01)); they may be supplied directly in the form of assembled vectors or in the form of loads which will be assembled in the algorithm.

The solution $(\mathrm{X},\dot{\mathrm{X}},\ddot{\mathrm{X}})$ is computed on a time discretisation ${t}_{i}$ of the interval of study specified by the user.

#### 6.11.3 Operands VITE_MIN / COEF_MULT_PAS / COEF_DIV_PAS / PAS_LIMI_RELA / NB_POIN_PERIODE / NMAX_ITER_PAS / PAS_MINI

These operands concern only the scheme with adaptive time step.

```text
◊    VITE_MIN =    /    'NORM'            [DEFAUT]
/    'MAXI'
```

Method of computation of the reference velocity used to evaluate the apparent frequency.

When the denominator of the apparent frequency $({x}_{n}-{x}_{n-1})$ becomes small, the apparent frequency may become very high, which leads to an unjustified refinement of the time step. To remedy this, the algorithm uses the following criterion for each degree of freedom $i$ :

$$
\frac{∣{x}_{n}^{i}-{x}_{n-1}^{i}∣}{\Delta t}\le {v}_{min}^{i}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }{f}_{{\mathit{AP}}_{n}}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{∣\frac{{\ddot{x}}_{n}^{i}-{\ddot{x}}_{n-1}^{i}}{{v}_{min}^{i}\Delta t}∣}
$$

${v}_{min}^{i}$ may be computed in two different ways according to the value of VITE_MIN:

**'NORM'**  : ${v}_{\min}^{i}({t}_{n})=\mathrm{Max}(\frac{\mathrm{Max}({\dot{x}}_{n+1/2}^{k},{\dot{x}}_{n+1/2}^{l})}{100},{10}^{-15}{\mathrm{ms}}^{-1})$ where $k$ and $l$ are the degrees of freedom of the same nature as the degree of freedom $i$ which are closest to $i$ in the numbering ($\mathit{DX}$  or $\mathit{DY}$ or $\mathit{DZ}$ ...).

**'MAXI'** : ${v}_{\min}^{i}({t}_{n})=\underset{0<{t}_{p}<{t}_{n}}{\mathrm{Max}}(\frac{\mid {v}^{i}({t}_{p})\mid }{100},{10}^{-15}{\mathrm{ms}}^{-1})$ for the degree of freedom $i$ .

It may be used if the order of magnitude of the velocity does not vary too much over time.

```text
◊    COEF_MULT_PAS = cmp
```

**Coefficient of coarsening of the time step**  ($>1$ ) when the error is sufficiently small:

$\Delta {t}_{n}<\frac{0.75}{{\mathit{Nf}}_{{\mathit{AP}}_{n}}}$ for more than 5 consecutive steps $\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\Delta {t}_{n+1}=min(\mathit{cmp}\Delta {t}_{n},\Delta {t}_{max})$

with $\Delta {t}_{max}=\Delta {t}_{\mathit{initial}}$

Its default value ($\mathit{cmp}=1.1$ ) guarantees stability and accuracy, but it may in general be increased (at most up to $1.3$ ) in order to speed up the integration.

```text
◊    COEF_DIVI_PAS = cdp
```

**Coefficient of refinement of the time step**  ($>1$ ) when the error is greater than 1, the maximum number of iterations (NMAX_ITER_PAS) has not been reached and the minimum time step has not been reached:

$\Delta {t}_{n}>\frac{1}{{\mathit{Nf}}_{{\mathit{AP}}_{n}}},\mathit{Niter}<{\mathit{Niter}}_{max}$ and $\Delta {t}_{n}>\mathit{plr}\mathrm{\ast }\Delta {t}_{\mathit{initial}}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\Delta {t}_{n}=\frac{\Delta {t}_{n}}{\mathit{cdp}}$

Its default value is $1.3334$ , that is a reduction by a factor $0,75$ .

```text
◊    PAS_LIMI_RELA = plr
```

Coefficient applied to the initial time step in order to define the refinement limit and therefore the minimum time step:

$\Delta {t}_{min}=\mathit{plr}\mathrm{\ast }\Delta {t}_{\mathit{initial}}$

```text
◊    NB_POIN_PERIODE = N
```

Number of points per apparent period. It is this parameter which sets the accuracy of the analysis. It must be at least equal to 20; its default value (50) guarantees a satisfactory accuracy (of the order of 1 to 2%) in most cases.

```text
◊    NMAX_ITER_PAS
```

Maximum number of reductions of the time step per analysis step:

if $\mathit{err}>1$ and ${N}_{\mathit{iter}}<{N}_{\mathit{iter}}max$ : $\Delta {t}_{n}=\mathit{cdp}\mathrm{\ast }\Delta {t}_{n}$

It is equal to 16 by default, which limits the reduction factor of the step to ${(1/1,33)}^{16}={10}^{-2}$ per iteration. NMAX_ITER_PAS may be:

- increased in order to allow the time step to drop more sharply,
- decreased if the time step seems excessively refined.

```text
◊     PAS_MINI = dtmin
```

Minimum value of the time step. If the conditions for decreasing the time step are satisfied, the current time step may then decrease down to this limiting value.

If the user does not give a value to this optional parameter, then the code will compute the minimum time step from PAS_LIMI_RELA.

### 6.14 Checking phase

The use of the centred-difference and adaptive schemes imposes certain restrictions of use:

- these two schemes require the use of a diagonal mass matrix. A test checks that the mass matrix was created with the option 'MASS_MECA_DIAG' of CALC_MATR_ELEM. Moreover, the mass matrix must be stored in skyline form,
- there must be no boundary conditions other than blocked degrees of freedom.

A test checks that there are no boundary conditions of the type of links between degrees of freedom.

It is not possible either to impose non-zero displacements by means of an assembled vector,

- for the centred-difference scheme, one makes sure that the time step chosen satisfies the stability conditions:

$\mathit{dt}<0,05/{f}_{max}$ with ${f}_{max}=\underset{1\le i\le \mathit{nddl}}{max}(\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{{k}_{ii}}{{m}_{ii}}})$ and ${k}_{ii}$ and ${m}_{ii}$ the diagonal terms of the stiffness and mass matrices.

## 7 Harmonic response analysis

One solves the following equation in the frequency domain:

$$
(-j\omega \mathrm{³}.\mathrm{Q}-\omega \mathrm{²}.\mathrm{M}+j\omega .\mathrm{C}+\mathrm{K})\cdot \mathrm{x}=\left\lbrace \sum_{i=1}^{k}{h}_{i}(f).{\omega}^{{n}_{i}}.{\mathrm{e}}^{j\pi \frac{{\phi }_{j}}{180}}.{\mathrm{g}}_{i}(P)\right\rbrace
$$

Where:

$\mathrm{K}$ represents a real or complex stiffness matrix (case of hysteretic damping)

$\mathrm{M}$ represents a mass matrix

$\mathrm{C}$ represents a viscous damping matrix

$\mathrm{Q}$ represents an acoustic impedance matrix coming from a displacement-pressure-potential formulation $(\mathrm{u},p,\varphi )$ , cf. [R4.02.02](doc:r4.02.02).

$P$ is a current point of the structure.

$\omega =2\pi f$ : excitation angular frequency

${\mathrm{g}}_{i}(P)$ : spatial description of the excitation

${h}_{i}(f)$ : frequency description of the excitation

$\mathrm{x}$ : complex response

The damping of the structure may be viscous or hysteretic [U2.06.03](doc:u2.06.03) [R5.05.04](doc:r5.05.04). In the case of hysteretic damping, one has a complex stiffness matrix and one solves the following equation:

$$
(\mathrm{K}-\omega \mathrm{²}.\mathrm{M})\cdot \mathrm{x}=\left\lbrace \sum_{i=1}^{k}{h}_{i}(f).{\omega}^{{n}_{i}}.{\mathrm{e}}^{j\pi \frac{{\phi }_{j}}{180}}.{\mathrm{g}}_{i}(P)\right\rbrace
$$

with $\mathrm{K}$ : complex stiffness matrix.

This operator may be used with imposed force or with imposed displacement (solution in the absolute frame) or with imposed entrainment inertia force (solution in the relative frame).

Choice of the fields to be computed in order to represent the response: displacement, velocity, acceleration, or all three.

### 7.12 Keyword EXCIT

```text
♦    EXCIT
```

Factor keyword making it possible to define several types of excitations. Either by indicating an assembled vector corresponding to a loading, or loads which will lead to the computation and to the assembly of a right-hand side. For each occurrence of the factor keyword, one defines a component of the excitation in the form $(h(f),\mathrm{g}(P),\phi )$ , which one may also multiply by multiplying functions. This keyword must be repeated as many times as there are loading vectors.

#### 7.12.1 Operands VECT_ASSE/VECT_ASSE_GENE/CHARGE

Make it possible to define ${\mathrm{g}}_{i}(P)$, the spatial discretisation of the loading, in the form of a field at the nodes corresponding to one or several loads of imposed force or of imposed motion.

```text
♦    /    VECT_ASSE = vecti
```

Name of the concept produced by:

- the operator ASSE_VECTEUR, in imposed force or in entrainment inertia force under imposed acceleration in an absolute frame. The amplitudes of the excitation may be defined in the corresponding concepts of load type. The field expected is a field at the nodes of quantity DEPL_R, DEPL_C or PRES_C.

```text
/    VECT_ASSE_GENE = vect_gene
```

Name of the concept produced by:

- the operator PROJ_VECT_BASE, which makes it possible to project an assembled vector (imposed force or entrainment force under imposed acceleration in an absolute frame) onto a modal basis or a Ritz basis.
- the operator ASSE_VECT_GENE, which makes it possible to project a loading onto a basis defined on a generalised model for dynamic substructuring analyses.

```text
/    CHARGE = chi
```

chi, name of the loading concept created by a call to the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01).

The keyword MODELE must be supplied if one uses the keyword CHARGE.

#### 7.12.2 Operands FONC_MULT_C / COEF_MULT_C / FONC_MULT / COEF_MULT

Make it possible to define ${h}_{i}(f)$, a complex or real function defining the dependence of the excitation as a function of the frequency, applied to all the components of the field at the node associated with this occurrence. Several possibilities are offered:

```text
♦    /    FONC_MULT_C    =    hci
```

Name of the concept of type fonction_C or formule_C defining a complex function ${h}_{i}(f)$ of the frequency $f$ ,

```text
/    COEF_MULT_C = aci
```

Complex multiplying coefficient of the loading, independent of the loading,

```text
/    FONC_MULT = hi
```

Concept of type fonction, formule or nappe defining a real function ${h}_{i}(f)$ of the frequency $f$ ,

```text
/    COEF_MULT = ai
```

Real multiplying coefficient of the loading, independent of the loading.

#### 7.12.3 Operand PUIS_PULS

```text
◊    PUIS_PULS = ni
```

Makes it possible to define the power of the angular frequency, cf. eq.(), when the loading is a function of the frequency; by default ${n}_{i}=0$ . For example, in order to take into account the time derivative of a loading; example: imposed displacement versus imposed velocity...

#### 7.12.4 Operand PHAS_DEG

```text
◊    PHAS_DEG = phi
```

Makes it possible to define the phase of each component of the excitation in degrees with respect to a single phase reference for all the excitations ${h}_{i}(f)$ ; by default ${\varphi}_{i}=0$ .

#### 7.12.5 Note

For a problem with motion imposed in displacement, one defines the blocked degrees of freedom (kinematic conditions prior to the construction of the cham_no); one may then choose an excitation:

- in imposed displacement $n=0$ , $\varphi =0$ degree,
- in imposed velocity $n=1$ , $\varphi =90$ degrees,
- in imposed acceleration $n=2$ , $\varphi =180$ degrees.

### 7.13 Operand EXCIT_RESU

```text
◊    EXCIT_RESU
```

This factor keyword makes it possible to define several loadings in the form of a harmonic evolution of type dyna_harmo of assembled right-hand-side vectors, computed on the physical basis. This possibility is available on a physical basis and on a reduced modal basis or a Ritz basis.

### 7.14 Keyword CHAR_GENE

```text
◊    CHAR_GENE
```

This keyword makes it possible to define a loading of type char_gene (see [U4.44.06](doc:u4.44.06))
which imposes linear relations between generalised degrees of freedom.

The problem to be solved is of the following form:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Biggl( \begin{bmatrix} K & B^T \\ B & 0 \end{bmatrix} -\omega^2 \begin{bmatrix} M & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix} \Biggr)
\cdot \begin{bmatrix} x \\ \lambda \end{bmatrix}
= \cdot \begin{bmatrix} 0 \\ \beta \end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

Where:

$- \lambda$ represents the additional Lagrange degrees of freedom,

$- B$ represents the matrix of the coefficients to be imposed between the modes,

$- \beta$ represents the values of the linear relations to be applied.

#### 7.14.1 Operand RESULTAT

This keyword makes it possible to define the right-hand sides to be extracted for each analysis frequency from an already computed result containing nodal force fields.

```text
♦    RESULTAT = resuforc
```

Name of the concept of harmonic evolution of right-hand sides produced by the chaining of the operator CALC_FORC_NONL [U4.84.21](doc:u4.84.21) and then of the operator REST_SPEC_TEMP [U4.63.34](doc:u4.63.34). The operator CALC_FORC_NONL makes it possible to produce a transient evolution of right-hand sides. The operator REST_SPEC_TEMP transforms this transient evolution into a harmonic evolution. An example of use is provided in the test case SDLS119A.

#### 7.14.2 Operand COEF_MULT_C

```text
♦    COEF_MULT_C = aci
```

Complex multiplying coefficient of the right-hand-side vector extracted from the result resuforc for each analysis frequency.

### 7.15 Operand INFO

```text
◊    INFO    =    inf
```

Makes it possible to carry out, in the message file, various intermediate printouts making it possible to follow the progress of the analysis.

By default, if INFO=1, one prints the progress of the harmonic analysis with a step which corresponds at most to 5% of the total number of frequencies. The general rule is to print systematically the first and last frequencies as well as a maximum number of 20 frequencies in between.

If INFO=2, a printout is made for each frequency and makes it possible to follow the progress of the analysis more precisely.
