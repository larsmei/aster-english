# r3.06.02 Linear modelling of continuum elements in thermal analysis

**Summary:**

One describes the expression of the elementary terms entering into the linear modelling of the heat equation and into the post-processings. One gives the mathematical expression of the integral to be evaluated, and for each element one supplies the number of integration points used.

## 1 The options of linear modelling

In this document, one considers only the linear modelling of the physical phenomenon of evolution of the temperature in a continuum. All the coefficients entering into the heat equation will be constants or else functions which may depend on time or on space. The boundary conditions can only be linear functions of the temperature.

By default the material is assumed to be isotropic; Fourier's law relating the heat flux to the temperature gradient involves a scalar coefficient $\lambda$, the thermal conductivity:

$q=-\lambda \nabla T$

In the general case, an arbitrary medium, this relation is expressed with a thermal-conductivity tensor. The associated matrix being positive definite, it is always possible to reduce it to a diagonal matrix in the frame associated with the principal directions. The treatment of thermal anisotropy (see [bib 1] is therefore carried out in *Code_Aster* by supplying the values of the thermal conductivity for each principal direction and the principal frame. The evaluation of the elementary terms is then carried out by recovering the various coefficients and by changing frame. Two types of anisotropy are treated in *Code_Aster*, namely:

- Cartesian anisotropy, in which the privileged directions remain fixed in a Cartesian frame; the data of the three nautical angles $\alpha$ , $\beta$ and $\gamma$ make it possible to pass from the global frame to the principal frame of anisotropy,
- cylindrical anisotropy, in which the privileged directions remain fixed in a cylindrical frame; the data of the two nautical angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ defining the direction of the axis and of the three coordinates of a point of this axis make it possible to pass from the global frame to the principal frame of anisotropy.

The variational formulation of the linear heat equation (cf. [R5.02.01](doc:r5.02.01)) leads to the evaluation of a certain number of expressions in the form of integrals which finally constitute a matrix system. The matrix and the right-hand side are built from various building blocks: the computation options, which group one or more integrals. The options described here are common to the set of the isoparametric finite elements. Their evaluation depends on the type of element: degree of the shape functions, number and family of integration points used.

One may refer to the documents [U3.23.01](doc:u3.23.01), [U3.23.02](doc:u3.23.02) and [U3.24.01](doc:u3.24.01) concerning the various modellings (type of mesh cell supporting the finite elements).

## 2 Expression of the elementary terms for the various computation options

### 2.1 General notations

We denote by:

| $O$ | an open set of ${\mathrm{\Re }}^{3}$ or of ${\mathrm{\Re }}^{2}$ of boundary $\Gamma$ , |
| --- | --- |
| $t$ | the variable representing time, |
| $\Delta t$ | the time step used, |
| $r$ | the space variable, |
| $T$ | the temperature (unknown of the problem), |
| ${T}^{n}$ | the temperature at the previous time instant (known), |
| ${T}^{\ast }$ | the test function, |
| $\rho$ | the density, |
| ${C}_{p}$ | the specific heat at constant pressure, |
| ${c}_{p}=\rho {C}_{p}$ | the heat capacity at constant pressure per unit volume, |
| $\theta$ | the parameter of the $\theta$-method for the transient thermal analysis. |

### 2.2 Elementary terms making a contribution

#### 2.2.1 Thermal stiffness

Term involving the temperature gradients and the conduction coefficient $\lambda$ in the case of isotropic media (name used by analogy with the stiffness term entering into the equation of the modelling of the mechanical phenomenon of elasticity). The coefficient $\lambda$ may depend on time.

- mathematical expression: ${\int}_{\Omega}\theta \lambda (t)\nabla T.\nabla {T}^{\ast }d\Omega$ ,

when the medium is anisotropic, the evaluation of the flux $\lambda (t)\nabla T$ is carried out in the principal frame of anisotropy after a first change of frame (the thermal-conductivity tensor is diagonal there), then by an inverse change of frame one returns to the global frame,

- name of the option in the catalogues: RIGI_THER,
- number of integration points used: (first family of integration points, cf. [R3.01.01]).

| **support mesh cell** | **number of nodes** | **number of points** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| triangle | 3 6 | 1 3 |
| quadrangle | 4 8 or 9 | 4 9 |
| tetrahedron | 4 10 | 4 15 |
| pentahedron | 6 15 | 6 21 |
| hexahedron | 8 20 27 | 8 27 27 |

Table 2.2.1-1.

#### 2.2.2 Thermal mass

Term involving the coefficient of heat capacity at constant pressure ${c}_{p}=\rho {C}_{p}$ (name used by analogy with the mass term entering into the equation of the modelling of the equations of dynamics). The coefficient ${C}_{p}$ may depend on time.

- mathematical expression: ${\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\rho {C}_{p}(t)T.{T}^{\mathrm{\ast }}d\Omega$
- name of the option in the catalogues: MASS_THER
- number of integration points used: (second family of integration points)

| **support mesh cell** | **number of nodes** | **number of points** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| triangle | 3 6 | 3 6 |
| quadrangle | 4 8 or 9 | 4 9 |
| tetrahedron | 4 10 | 4 15 |
| pentahedron | 6 15 | 6 21 |
| hexahedron | 8 20 27 | 8 27 27 |

Table 2.2.2-1.

#### 2.2.3 Stiffness due to the exchange boundary conditions

Term involving the exchange coefficient $h$ originating from a boundary condition modelling the convective exchanges with the edge of the domain. The coefficient $h$ may depend on time and on space.

- mathematical expression: ${\int}_{G}\theta h(r,t)T.{T}^{\mathrm{\ast }}\mathit{dG}$
- name of the option in the catalogues: RIGI_THER_COEF_R or RIGI_THER_COEF_F
- number of integration points used:

| **support mesh cell** | **number of nodes** | **number of points** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| segment | 2 3 | 4 4 |
| triangle | 3 6 | 3 4 |
| quadrangle | 4 8 or 9 | 4 9 |

Table 2.2.3-1.

#### 2.2.4 Stiffness due to the exchange conditions between walls

Term due to the Neumann-type boundary condition involving two facing sub-parts of the boundary and involving a single exchange coefficient $h$ . This type of boundary condition creates new relations between the degrees of freedom of the boundary.

In this case, one uses a particular finite element whose support mesh cell is obtained by associating two identical edge or face mesh cells; the shape functions used and the integration points are those of the original mesh cell.

In three dimensions, the support mesh cells of the face elements are of the type TRIA3-TRIA3, QUAD4-QUAD4, TRIA6-TRIA6, QUAD8-QUAD8 or QUAD9-QUAD9.

In two dimensions, they are of the type SEG2-SEG2 or SEG3-SEG3.

One may refer to [U4.25.02 § 3.1.3] for the description of the algorithm for searching for facing mesh cells.

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.02/100007600000124B00000E783869307A123E709C.svg)

- mathematical expression:

${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{1}}(h(r,t+\Delta t)\theta ({T}_{2}-{T}_{1})).{T}^{\ast }d{\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}(h(r,t+\Delta t)\theta ({T}_{1}-{T}_{2})).{T}^{\ast }d{\Gamma}_{2}$

where ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$ are two facing sub-parts of the boundary.

- name of the option in the catalogues: RIGI_THER_PARO_R or RIGI_THER_PARO_F
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.3-1].

### 2.3 Elementary terms making a contribution to the right-hand side

#### 2.3.1 Time discretisation

Term due:

- to the discretisation of the time derivative, involving a part of the mass term with the heat-capacity coefficient $\rho {C}_{p}$ ,
- to the $\theta$-method, involving a part of the stiffness in the right-hand side with the conduction coefficient $\lambda$ ,
- mathematical expression in the case of isotropic media:

${\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{\Delta t}\rho {C}_{p}{T}^{n}.{T}^{\ast }d\Omega -{\int}_{\Omega}(1-\theta )\lambda \nabla {T}^{n}.\nabla {T}^{\ast }d\Omega$

when the medium is anisotropic, the evaluation of the flux $\lambda (t)\nabla T$ is carried out in the principal frame of anisotropy after a first change of frame (the thermal-conductivity tensor is diagonal there), then by an inverse change of frame one returns to the global frame,

- name of the option in the catalogues: CHAR_THER_EVOL,
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.2-1].

#### 2.3.2 Volumetric source term

Term due to the volumetric heat source:

- mathematical expression: ${\int}_{\Omega}(\theta s(r,t+\Delta t)+(1-\theta )s(r,t)).{T}^{\ast }d\Omega$ ,
- name of the option in the catalogues: CHAR_THER_SOUR_R or CHAR_THER_SOUR_F,
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.1-1].

#### 2.3.3 Convective-exchange term

Term due to the convective-exchange boundary condition involving the exchange coefficient $h$ and the temperature of the “external” medium ${T}_{\text{ex}}$ .

- mathematical expression:

${\int}_{\Gamma}(\theta h(r,t+\Delta t){T}_{\text{ex}}(r,t+\Delta t)+(1-\theta )h(r,t)({T}_{\text{ex}}(r,t)-{T}^{n})).{T}^{\ast }d\Gamma$

- name of the option in the catalogues: CHAR_THER_R or CHAR_THER_F,
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.3-1].

#### 2.3.4 Imposed normal-flux term

Term due to the boundary condition of imposed flux along the normal to the boundary, involving a function which may depend on the variables $r$ and $t$ .

- mathematical expression: ${\int}_{\Gamma}(\theta f(r,t+\Delta t)+(1-\theta )f(r,t)).{T}^{\mathrm{\ast }}d\Gamma$ ,
- name of the option in the catalogues: CHAR_THER_FLUN_R or CHAR_THER_FLUN_F,
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.3-1].

#### 2.3.5 Term of exchange between walls

Term due to the Neumann-type boundary condition involving two facing sub-parts of the boundary and involving a single exchange coefficient $h$ .

- mathematical expression:

${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{1}}(h(r,t)(1-\theta )({T}_{2}^{n}-{T}_{1}^{n})).{T}^{\mathrm{\ast }}d{\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{2}}(h(r,t)(1-\theta )({T}_{1}^{n}-{T}_{2}^{n})).{T}^{\ast }d{\Gamma}_{2}$

where ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$ are two facing sub-parts of the boundary;

- name of the option in the catalogues: CHAR_THER_PARO_R or CHAR_THER_PARO_F,
- number of integration points used: cf. [Table 2.2.3-1].
