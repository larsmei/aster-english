# r3.03.04 External pressure forces in large displacements

**Summary:**

A pressure loading in large displacements is a follower loading.
By employing skin elements, one is led to compute, on the one hand, a right-hand side whose computation is
close to that in small displacements and, on the other hand, an additional stiffness term which is, in general,
not symmetric.

## 1 Introduction

Taking into account loadings of pressure type (keyword PRES_REP in the command  [AFFE_CHAR_MECA](doc:u4.44.01))
raises a certain number of difficulties in the absence of the small-displacement assumption.
Indeed, unlike the dead loads mentioned in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01),
the pressure depends on the displacements, since it is a force whose direction is normal to the domain;
one then speaks of follower forces, activated by the keyword TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV' in the command  [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03).
Nevertheless, the choice of the current configuration as the reference configuration (updated Lagrangian) leads to simple expressions (given a few notions of differential geometry) of the work of the pressure forces and of its first variation with respect to the displacement, the latter being a non-symmetric bilinear form.

## 2 Continuous statement of the problem

### 2.1 Elements of kinematics in large transformations

One considers a solid $\domain$ subjected to large strains (see figure).
Let $\gradTransfor$ be the gradient tensor of the transformation, $\phi$ taking the initial configuration $\domainRefe$
into the current deformed configuration $\domainCurr$. One denotes by $\posiRefe$ the position of a point in $\domainRefe$
and by $\posiCurr$ the position of this same point after deformation in $\domainCurr$.
$\disp$ is then the displacement between the two configurations. One therefore has:

$$
\posiCurr = \posiRefe + \disp
$$

The gradient tensor of the transformation is written:

$$
\gradTransfor
=
\frac{\partial \posiCurr}{\partial \posiRefe}
=
\tensTwoUnit + \gradVector{\disp}
$$

*Solid in large transformations*

### 2.2 Virtual work of the external pressure forces

One considers a pressure $\presRefe$ normal to the surface in the reference configuration.
This pressure is written $\presCurr$ in the current configuration.

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.04/Shape2.gif)

*Reference configuration and current configuration*

In the current configuration, the virtual work of the external pressure forces $\work{\pres}$
is written simply (see r3_03_04_Configurations):

$$
\work{\pres}(\disp)
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {-\pres \, \normalCurr \cdot \dispVirt \measBound }
$$

Furthermore, one assumes henceforth that the value of the pressure does not depend explicitly
on the displacement but only on the material point of application:

$$
\presCurr(\posiCurr)
=
\presCurr(\funcTransfor (\posiRefe))
=
\presRefe(\posiRefe)
$$

The follower character of the force comes from the dependence of the *normal* on the displacement.
In this case, one may then express the virtual work of the pressure forces
in the reference configuration (change of variable in the integral):

$$
\work{\pres}(\disp)
=
{\int}_{\boundNRefe(\disp)}
{-\presRefe \, \det \gradTransfor \,  \left[ \inverseTranspose{\gradTransfor} \cdot \normalRefe \right] \cdot \dispVirt \left(\funcTransfor \left(\posiRefe\right)\right)\measBound }
$$

On the practical level, formula  will be used to compute the work of the pressure forces.
However, formula  is the best adapted to a derivation with respect to the displacement,
the necessity of which will be seen in the following paragraph.

### 2.3 Variation of the virtual work of the external pressure forces

With a view to solving the equilibrium problem of the structure by a Newton method,
one is led to express the variation of the virtual work of the external pressure forces with
respect to the displacement, in a manner similar to what was done for the virtual work of the internal forces
in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01). The domain of integration being fixed in the expression ,
differentiation under the summation sign is legitimate (cf.  ):

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}{\partial \disp}
=
{\int}_{\boundNRefe(\disp)} {-\presRefe \, \frac{\partial}{\partial \disp} \left[\det \gradTransfor \,  \inverseTranspose{\gradTransfor} \right] \cdot \delta \disp \cdot \normalRefe \cdot \dispVirt\measBound}
$$

We decide to choose as reference configuration the current configuration, for which $\gradTransfor=\tensTwoUnit$.
This choice leads to a simple expression of the derivative of the term in brackets:

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial \disp}\left[ \det  \gradTransfor  \, \inverseTranspose{\gradTransfor} \right] \cdot \delta \disp
=
\divVector{\delta \disp} \, \tensTwoUnit - \gradVector{ \delta \disp}
$$

Finally, the variation of the virtual work of the external pressure forces is written in the current configuration:

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {-\presCurr \, \left[ \divVector{\delta \disp} \, \tensTwoUnit - \gradVector{ \delta \disp} \right] \cdot \normalCurr \cdot \dispVirt \measBound}
$$

In the expression  a difficulty remains. Indeed, one expects to obtain an essentially surface quantity
whereas the integrand brings out terms of differentiation normal to the surface.
In other words, one must know the expression of the virtual displacements not only on the surface of the domain
but also in its interior (in a neighbourhood of the surface, in order to be able to express the normal derivatives).
This drawback is not trivial, since in order to compute the elementary terms due to the surface forces,
one employs skin elements for which a normal variation has no meaning.

### 2.4 Adoption of a curvilinear parametrisation of the surface

In order to remedy the problem mentioned previously, one must seek to express the relation 
by means of surface quantities only. For this, one has recourse to elements of differential geometry,
, whose notation one adopts (in particular, one adopts the summation convention on repeated indices, where the Greek indices take the values $1$ and $2$ while the Latin indices take the values $1$ to $3$ ).

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.04/Shape3.gif)

*Curvilinear parametrisation of the neighbourhood of the surface subjected to the pressure*

Let $({\theta}^{1},{\theta}^{2})$ be an admissible parametrisation of the surface. In order to describe the volume constituted by a neighbourhood of this surface, one adjoins to it a third variable, ${\theta}^{3}$, which measures the progression along the unit normal $n$ at $({\theta}^{1},{\theta}^{2})$. One thus has (see r3_03_04_ParametrageCurviligne):

$$
\vector{OM}\left({\theta}^{1},{\theta}^{2},{\theta}^{3}\right)
=
\vector{OS}\left({\theta}^{1},{\theta}^{2})+{\theta}^{3} \,\normalCurr({\theta}^{1},{\theta}^{2}\right)
$$

With this choice of parametrisation, the covariant natural basis $(\vectorBaseCV{1},\vectorBaseCV{2},\vectorBaseCV{3})$ is written:

$$
\vectorBaseCV{i}
=
\frac{\partial \vector{OM}}{\partial {\theta}^{i}}
$$

While the metric tensor $\metric$ is equal to:

$$
\metric
=
\vectorBaseCV{i} \times \vectorBaseCV{j}
=
\left[ \begin{array}{ccc}
\tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{1}{1}& \tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{1}{2}& 0\\
\tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{2}{1}& \tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{2}{2}& 0\\
0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]
$$

In this curvilinear parametrisation, the integrand  has the expression:

$$
{-\pres \, \tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{i}{j} \, \vectorCmpCT{\normal}{i} \,
 \left[
    \derivee{\vectorCmpCT{\delta \disp}{k}}{k} \, \vectorCmpCT{\dispVirt}{j}
    -
    \derivee{\vectorCmpCT{\delta \disp}{j}}{k} \, \vectorCmpCT{\dispVirt}{k}
\right] }
$$

This term simplifies considerably. Indeed, one may already note that when $j=k$ , the term in brackets is zero. Furthermore, in the curvilinear system adopted, the contravariant components of $\normal$ are:

$$
\vectorCmpCT{\normal}{1}=0, \quad \vectorCmpCT{\normal}{2}=0 \quad \vectorCmpCT{\normal}{3}=1
$$

Finally, taking into account the particular form of $\metric$ (that is $\tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{1}{3}=0$ , $\tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{2}{3}=0$ and $\tensTwoCmpCO{\metric}{3}{3}=1$ ), the variation of the work  is written simply:

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp \cdot \dispVirt
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {
    -\presCurr \,
    \left[
        \derivee{\vectorCmpCT{\delta \disp}{\alpha}}{\alpha} \, \vectorCmpCT{ \dispVirt}{3}
        -
        \derivee{\vectorCmpCT{\delta \disp}{3}}{\alpha} \, \vectorCmpCT{\dispVirt}{\alpha}
    \right]
\measBound}
$$

On this expression, one observes that only surface differential operators intervene (covariant differentiation with respect to ${\theta}^{1}$ and ${\theta}^{2}$ only), which is indeed the aim sought. Introducing the contravariant basis $(\vectorBaseCT{1},\vectorBaseCT{2},\vectorBaseCT{3}=\normal)$, also called the dual basis, which is expressed from the covariant basis by:

$$
vectorBaseCT{i}
=
{\left[\metric^{ij}\right]}^{-1} \, vectorBaseCV{j}
$$

One may dispense with the curvilinear components:

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {
-\presCurr \,
\left[
    \left( \frac{\partial \delta \disp}{\partial {\theta}^{\alpha}} \cdot \vectorBaseCT{\alpha} \right)
    \,
    \left(\dispVirt \cdot \normal \right)
    -
    \left( \frac{\partial \delta \disp}{\partial {\theta}^{\alpha}} \cdot \normal \right)
    \,
    \left( \dispVirt \cdot \vectorBaseCT{\alpha} \right)
\right]  \measBound}
$$

It is henceforth the expression  which will be used to compute the variation of the virtual work of the pressure forces.

### 2.5 Particular case of a structure subjected to a constant internal or external pressure

In the particular case of a constant pressure in a cavity (see r3_03_04_Pression), one shows that the pressure forces derive from a potential $\Xi$ which is none other than the product of the pressure by the volume of the cavity. This result extends to the case of a structure immersed in a fluid at constant pressure.

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.04/Shape4.gif)

*Structure under constant internal or external pressure*

One writes this potential:

$$
\Xi
=
\pres \, {\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {   \measDomain }
=
\pres \, {\int}_{\boundNRefe(\disp)} {  \det \gradTransfor   \measDomain }
$$

Once again, one chooses as reference configuration the current configuration. The variation of $\Xi$ then indeed leads to the virtual work of the external pressure forces:

$$
\frac{\partial \Xi }{\partial \disp}\cdot\dispVirt
=
\pres \, {\int}_{\domainCurr(\disp)} { \divVector(\dispVirt) \,  \measDomain }
=
- {\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {   \pres \, \dispVirt \cdot \normal  \measBound}
=
\work{\pres} \, \dispVirt
$$

In this particular case, the variation of the virtual work is also the second variation of the potential $\Xi$ , that is a **symmetric** bilinear form:

$$
\frac{\partial {W}^{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt=\frac{{\partial}^{2}\Xi (\disp)}{\partial {\disp}^{2}}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt
$$

## 3 Discretisation

### 3.1 Options computed

Skin finite elements (surface elements immersed in a three-dimensional space) are employed to discretise the real and virtual displacements intervening in surface expressions such as  and . The latter make it possible to express respectively the right-hand-side vector and the stiffness matrix due to the pressure, whose use by the algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE is specified in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)

One therefore develops four options:

1. RIGI_MECA_PRSU_R : stiffness matrix for a follower pressure as a real constant
2. RIGI_MECA_PRSU_F : stiffness matrix for a follower pressure as a real function
3. CHAR_MECA_PRSU_R : right-hand-side vector for a follower pressure as a real constant
4. CHAR_MECA_PRSU_F : right-hand-side vector for a follower pressure as a real function

These options are developed for the elements 3D, D_PLAN and AXIS. One may apply a normal follower pressure but not a follower tangential shear. The pressure may be a real function of time or a real constant.

### 3.2 Discretisation of the differential-geometry terms

One denotes by  $\discVect{G_{\alpha}}$ the discretised version of the vectors of the covariant basis with ${\alpha={1,2}}$.
The first two vectors of the covariant basis $\discVect{G_{\alpha={1,2}}}$ are computed from the vector of the displacements discretised at the nodes $\discVect{U}$  and from the derivatives of the shape functions $\discMatr{B_{\alpha}}$ :

$$
\discVect{G_{\alpha}}
=
\discMatr{B_{\alpha}} \, \discVect{U}
$$

The normal $\normal$ is computed as the vector product of these first two vectors $\vector{g_{\alpha}}$ :

$$
\normal
=
\frac{{\vector{g}}_{1} {\wedge } {\vector{g}}_{2}}{\Vert {\vector{g}}_{1} {\wedge }{\vector{g}}_{2}\Vert}
$$

One may also compute the discretised metric tensor in covariant components $\discMatr{G_{\alpha \beta}}$ :

$$
\discMatr{G_{\alpha \beta}}
=
\discVect{G_\alpha} \discVectLigne{{G}_{\beta}}
$$

And its Jacobian:

$$
\jacobTransfor
=
\det \discMatr{G_{\alpha \beta}}
$$

One may compute the contravariant metric matrix, which will be denoted $\discMatr{G^{\delta \gamma}}$

$$
\discMatr{G^{\delta \gamma}}
=
\inverse{\discMatr{ G_{\alpha \beta}}}
$$

And finally extract the contravariant basis $\discVectCmp{G}{\delta}$ :

$$
\discVect{G^\delta}
=
\discMatr{G^{\delta \gamma}} \, \discVect{G_\alpha}
$$

### 3.3 Vector of the follower forces

The computation of the virtual work of the pressure forces  is in fact identical to that carried out in small displacements, subject to a prior updating of the geometry. One starts from the expression of the virtual works:

$$
\work{\pres}(\disp)
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {-\pres \, \normalCurr \cdot \dispVirt \measBound }
$$

In discretised form:

$$
\work{\pres}(\disp)
=
\discVectLigne{\delta V} \discVect{{F}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}
$$

The variation of the displacements is written from the shape functions:

$$
\dispVirt
=
\discMatr{ \shapeFunc } \,  \left\lbrace \delta V\right\rbrace
$$

All the differential-geometry terms have been discretised in the preceding paragraph; it only remains to discretise the integral by using a Gauss scheme with the weights $\quadWeight{\quadPointIndex}$. On an arbitrary quantity $A$, one will have

$$
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {A \measBound }
=
\sum_{\quadPointIndex} {\onQuadPoint{A}{\quadPointIndex} \, \quadWeight{\quadPointIndex} }
$$

The pressure $\pres$ given by AFFE_CHAR_MECA is located at the nodes,
one must therefore interpolate the pressure $\pres$ from its values on the nodes, which one denotes $\onNode{\pres}{\nodeIndex}$
towards the Gauss point of coordinates $\onQuadPoint{\xi}{\quadPointIndex}$ :

$$
\onQuadPoint{\pres}{\quadPointIndex}
=
\sum_{\nodeIndex}{\onNode{\shapeFunc(\onQuadPoint{\xi}{\quadPointIndex})}{\nodeIndex} \,  \onNode{\pres}{\nodeIndex} }
$$

One denotes by  $\onNode{\shapeFunc(\onQuadPoint{\xi}{\quadPointIndex})}{\nodeIndex}$ the value of the shape function of the node $\nodeIndex$ at the Gauss point of coordinates $\onQuadPoint{\xi}{\quadPointIndex}$. Finally:

$$
\discVect{{F}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}
= -
\sum_{{\quadPointIndex}}{
        \onQuadPoint{\pres}{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \quadWeight{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \onQuadPoint{\jacobTransfor}{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \transpose{\discMatr{\onQuadPoint{ \shapeFunc} {\quadPointIndex} }}
        \,
        \discVect{\onQuadPoint{\normal} {\quadPointIndex} }
    }
$$

### 3.4 Matrix of the follower forces

The variation of the virtual work of the pressure forces is expressed by the expression

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt
=
{\int}_{\boundNCurr(\disp)} {
-\presCurr \,
\left[
    \left( \frac{\partial \delta \disp}{\partial {\theta}^{\alpha}} \cdot \vectorBaseCT{\alpha} \right)
    \,
    \left(\dispVirt \cdot \normal \right)
    -
    \left( \frac{\partial \delta \disp}{\partial {\theta}^{\alpha}} \cdot \normal \right)
    \,
    \left( \dispVirt \cdot \vectorBaseCT{\alpha} \right)
\right]  \measBound}
$$

It makes it possible to extract the tangent matrix $\discMatr{{K}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}$ of the follower forces:

$$
\frac{\partial \work{\pres}(\disp)}{\partial \disp}\cdot\delta \disp\cdot\dispVirt
=
\discVectLigne{\delta V} \discMatr{{-K}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)} \discVect{\delta U}
$$

The minus sign comes from the fact that the contribution of the matrix is on the left-hand side.

Finally:

$$
\discMatr{{K}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}
=
\sum_{{\quadPointIndex}}{
        \onQuadPoint{\pres}{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \quadWeight{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \onQuadPoint{\jacobTransfor}{\quadPointIndex}
        \,
        \transpose{\discMatr{\onQuadPoint{ \shapeDFunc} {\quadPointIndex} }}
        \left(
        \discVect{\onQuadPoint{G^\delta}{\quadPointIndex} }
        \,
        \discVectLigne{\onQuadPoint{\normal} {\quadPointIndex} }
        -
        \discVect{\onQuadPoint{\normal} {\quadPointIndex} }
        \,
        \discVectLigne{\onQuadPoint{G^\delta}{\quadPointIndex} }
        \right )
        \,
        {\discMatr{\onQuadPoint{ \shapeFunc} {\quadPointIndex} }}
    }
$$

with $\discMatr{\onQuadPoint{ \shapeDFunc} {\quadPointIndex} }$ the matrix of the derivatives of the shape functions.

### 3.5 Choice of the matrix

In general, the matrix $\discMatr{{K}^{\pres}\left(\disp\right)}$ is **not symmetric**  (except for the particular case of a structure subjected to a constant internal or external pressure). One also observes in practice that, for strong variations of the geometry (using a hyperelastic constitutive behaviour in large strains such as ELAS_HYPER), symmetrising this matrix is not a good strategy (failure of convergence). One therefore decides to keep this non-symmetric matrix, despite the (slight) extra cost induced by the factorisation of such a matrix.
