# 1 **v6.04.130** SSNV130 –Flexible-plasticized straight pipe

**Summary:**

The problem is a straight cracked tube with semi-elliptic circumferential defect in internal skin. The right tube is subjected to an bending moment. This test allows to calculate the rate of local energy restitution in crack bottom by the G-theta method (command CALC_G). The local energy restitution rate is calculated according to theLINE interpolation. The integrating crowns are defined by the number of layers (NB_COUCHE_INF and NB_COUCHE_SUP).

The value of G at the bottom of the crack is compared with a note used for the validation of simplified methods in the mechanical of the fracture.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

It is a semi-elliptic circumferential crack that leads to a shallow internal dive in a straight pipe. Only a quarter of the pipe is modelled:

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.130/100002010000032800000224C719561A7006B63A.png)

Figure 1.1-2: Géométrie du défaut

The tube has for length 1,500 mm, for thickness 60 mm, and for internal radius 270 mm.

The defect is 15 mm deep and 45 mm long.

### 1.2 Material properties

> The austenitic steel 316L at 350°C- is used for all modellings.

$E=177000\mathit{MPa}$

$\nu =0.3$

${\sigma}_{\gamma}=120\mathit{MPa}$

> The material constitutive laws varies for each model.
> Linear elastic ( *‘ELAS* , $A$ Modelling )
> Non-linear elastic ( *‘ELAS_VMIS_TRAC
> Elasto-plastic ( *‘VMIS_ISOT_TRAC
> Below is the traction curve used.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.130/10000201000008F20000053D8A21AC68115FA9F6.png)

Figure 1.2-1: courbe de traction acier austénitique 316L à 350°C fournie par [bib1].

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Symmetrical to the 2 main plans:

${U}_{Y}=0.$ in the $Y=0$ plane.

${U}_{Z}=0.$ in $Z=0.$ plane out of crack

> Rigid mode on $X$ axis.
> ${U}_{X}$ average in nodes NODE_CENTRE_FISS_T and NODE_CENTRE_FISS_TM is null.

The loading conditions are:

A next bending moment ($-Y$ ) soliciting the semi-elliptic defect in the most penalizing way.

$M=-5216\mathit{kNm}$ where $M$ is the time on the $Y$ axis.

It is applied to the end of the tube via a linear distribution following $x$ of ${\sigma}_{zz}$ axial stresses

${\sigma}_{zz}=\frac{Mx}{I}$ in the $Z=1500\mathit{mm}$ plane

where $I$ is the quadratic bending moment. $I=\pi \frac{({R}_{\text{ext}}^{4}-{R}_{int}^{4})}{4}$

> For Modelling C and F, in the absence of a reference using an elasto-plastic constitutive law, the nonlinear elastic solution of the reference is compared, so a much larger difference is observed.
