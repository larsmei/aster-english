# r3.06.08 Finite elements treating quasi-incompressibility

**Summary:**

In certain situations, the mechanical behaviour of the material requires that the volumetric dilatation remain zero,
in other words that the strain take place at constant volume: isotropic elasticity with a Poisson's ratio
equal to $0.5$, perfect plastic flows in limit analysis …

One proposes here to treat this condition of “incompressibility” or of “quasi-incompressibility” by using a
formulation valid both in the compressible case and in the quasi-incompressible case. For this, one uses
a 3-field variational formulation in which the unknowns are the displacement, the volumetric strain and
the associated Lagrange multiplier (which would correspond to the pressure in the incompressible case).
One proposes two versions of this formulation: one for small strains, the other valid in the presence
of large strains. In the situation of a one-to-one relation between the pressure and the swelling,
the case of Von Mises plasticity, it is possible to eliminate the swelling unknown.
One then has a two-field displacement/pressure formulation.

After a few reminders of the difficulties posed by the solution of incompressible problems, one describes
the mixed finite elements implemented (in 3D and in 2D, plane and axisymmetric, in small and in large strains),
and one also presents the main lines of the integration into *code_aster*  (modellings `INCO_UP`, `INCO_UPG`, `INCO_UPO`).

This modelling is necessary in order to carry out limit analyses and in order to model elastic behaviours
for Poisson's ratios close to $0.5$ . It may also be useful in the case of modellings
giving rise to large plastic strains and for which the traditional modellings may be insufficient
and give rise to stress oscillations.

## 1 Difficulties linked to the treatment of incompressibility

In certain situations, the mechanical behaviour of the material requires that the strain take place at constant volume.
Materials possessing this property of non-dilatancy are often described as “incompressible” materials.
We shall see that these problems pose two types of difficulty. The first difficulty is linked to the writing of
the incompressibility condition, the second is linked to the numerical problems which this constraint gives rise to.
These difficulties are found again when the material is quasi-incompressible.

One reasons here in small perturbations, but the problem remains the same in the framework of finite transformations.

### 1.1 “Incompressible” and “quasi-incompressible” behaviours

In the framework of continuum mechanics, a strain of isochoric type is characterised by
the fact that the transformation gradient $F$ is such that $J=\det(F)=1$.

If one places oneself in the framework of small perturbations, the preceding condition reduces to:

$$
\text{tr}(\varepsilon )=divu=0
$$

The tensor $\varepsilon$ is therefore purely deviatoric: $\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}^{D}$ .

It follows that, in the case of isotropic materials, the invariant $\text{tr}(\varepsilon )$ (or $\det(F)$ )
does not intervene in the expression of the free-energy density $\varphi$ ;
thus, in the case of incompressible elasticity in HPP, one simply has:

$$
\varphi (\varepsilon )=\mu {\varepsilon}^{D}:{\varepsilon}^{D}
$$

This density makes it possible to express only the deviatoric part of the stress tensor:

$$
{\sigma}^{D}=2\mu {\varepsilon}^{D}
$$

In fact, the stress is defined up to a constant $p$ which is the opposite of the mean pressure:

$$
\sigma =2\mu {\varepsilon}^{D}+pI
$$

**Note:**

*Incompressible isotropic elasticity is of course a limiting case of isotropic elasticity with a Poisson's ratio*
$\nu =\frac{E}{2\nu }-1$ *tending towards* $0.5$ .

*It is not only the elastic materials whose Poisson's ratio is equal to or slightly less than* $0.5$
*which involve the incompressibility condition. Thus, it also intervenes in the case of a rigid-plastic material*
$(\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \text{tr}\sigma }=0)$ .
Indeed, one has in this case:
$\dot{\varepsilon}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \sigma };\ \ \dot{\lambda}\ge 0;\ \ \Phi \le 0;\dot{\lambda}\Phi =0$
*which leads to the incompressibility condition* $\text{tr}(\dot{\varepsilon})=0$ *.*

*Moreover, in the case of elastoplasticity, when the plastic strains become largely greater than the elastic strains,
one finds oneself in a virtually incompressible case with* $\text{tr}(\varepsilon )\mathrm{\simeq }0$ *.*

*Finally, the materials satisfying a constitutive relation of NORTON-HOFF type (law used for the limit analyses
[:external:ref:`R7.07.01 <R7.07.01>`]) also present the characteristic of incompressibility:*
$\varepsilon (v)=\alpha {({\sigma}_{\text{eq}})}^{n-1}{\sigma}^{D}\ \ \text{avec} \ \ n\ge 1 \ \ \text{et}\ \ \alpha >0$
*where* ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\sigma}^{D}:{\sigma}^{D}}$ *is the Von Mises equivalent stress.*

### 1.2 Some possible numerical solutions

If one wishes to treat the incompressibility condition exactly, as we have seen, the stress is not completely determined
from the strain (cf. ). It is therefore necessary to use a mixed formulation,
that is to say to introduce (at least) one other unknown of the problem which will make it possible to determine the stress tensor completely.
Several variants are possible, the simplest consisting in imposing the incompressibility condition by means of a
Lagrange multiplier which is then the pressure $p$ .

**Note:**
*If one opts for a penalisation procedure, one is brought back to the quasi-incompressible case and therefore to the difficulties mentioned below.*

One may also, in particular in the case of linear elasticity, choose to make the material slightly compressible.
In this way, the stress is entirely defined from the displacement and the use of a mixed formulation is no longer indispensable.
On the other hand, the solution of these problems with the classical displacement finite elements poses numerical difficulties.
Indeed, the kinematic constraint represented by a constant-volume strain is very strong, even too strong if the degrees of freedom of
the element are not numerous enough. Thus, the 3-node triangle may present locking phenomena, that is to say that the “mesh”
cannot deform. In a less extreme way, most classical elements, in particular linear ones, behave in an abnormally stiff manner.
New elements must therefore be used in order to “relax” the system. These elements may rest on different types of formulation:

- in displacement only
- mixed: displacements / stresses, displacements / pressures, strains / stresses, displacements / pressures / volumetric dilatations, …

In all cases, if one is not careful, one may have numerical difficulties. Several approaches are used in order to facilitate
the deformation of the elements:

- using under-integration makes it possible to improve the results but it presents a drawback: it may lead to the appearance of spurious

or hourglass modes. In order to solve this problem, one may either enrich the stiffness matrix by means of stabilisation matrices which
neutralise the hourglass modes, or use projection methods which consist in projecting, into a smaller space,
the incompressibility condition so as to eliminate the locking phenomena. The best known is the B-Bar method ,

- enriching the element by means of additional degrees of freedom: one then speaks of enhanced-strain methods, incompatible modes,...

### 1.3 Option adopted and fields of application

We have chosen here to opt for a formulation which covers the quasi-incompressible (up to the incompressible) as well as the compressible.
For this, the term $\text{tr}(\varepsilon )$ is treated as an independent variable. With the associated Lagrange multiplier,
this leads to a 3- or 2-field formulation. A large-strain version has also been developed on the same principle.
In this case, the independent variable linked to the incompressibility condition is no longer $\text{tr}(\varepsilon )$ but $J=\det(F)$ .

The advantage of the 3-field formulation with respect to the 2-field version is that it makes it possible to use, in a transparent manner, all the
elasto-plastic constitutive laws available in Aster (no need to separate the deviatoric part and the spherical part of the stress
tensor). It is therefore not limited to elasticity or to Von Mises elastoplasticity. On the other hand, it introduces a large number
of additional degrees of freedom. Whatever formulation is adopted, one will not be able to treat the case in which the Poisson's ratio is
strictly equal to $0.5$ , because one uses, for the computation of the elastic stress,
the term $\frac{E\nu }{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}\text{tr}(\varepsilon )$ , whose denominator is zero when $\nu =0.5$ .

Consequently, the incompressible formulations **must be used**:

- in order to treat limit-analysis problems for which one assumes that the flow takes place at constant volume [R7.07.01](doc:r7.07.01),
- in order to treat elastic problems whose Poisson's ratio is greater than $0.45$ .

They may also be used:

- in order to treat problems in which the plastic strains are large, which gives rise to oscillations at the level of the stresses (example:

in the case of analyses on notched specimens). Of course, this formulation being more costly than the classical displacement formulation,
it is to be reserved for the cases which pose a problem and in which one is interested in the values of the stresses (one may first try to use
under-integrated quadratic elements, which already improve the solution).

## 2 Mixed variational formulation of the problem

### 2.1 Formulation in the framework of small strains

Let a solid $\Omega$ be subjected to:

- an imposed displacement field $u={u}_{0}$ on ${\Gamma}_{u}$
- an imposed traction field $t=\sigma .n={t}_{0}$ on ${\Gamma}_{t}$
- a body-force field $f$ on $\Omega$

In the classical case of the displacement finite elements (modelling `3D` or `D_PLAN` or `AXIS` in *code_aster* ), when the problem derives from an energy, the problem solved is the following:

find $u\in V$ with $\sigma$ satisfying the constitutive relation, which minimises the potential energy:

As we have explained in section [r3.06.08-difficulte-inco], this formulation is not suitable when one seeks to approach the incompressible solution, that is to say the condition $div(u)=0$ or $\text{tr}(\varepsilon )=0$ . In order to circumvent this difficulty, one solution is to treat separately the spherical part of the strain tensor (the part which poses numerical problems) and its deviatoric part. One will therefore have:

$$
\varepsilon (u,g)={\varepsilon}^{D}(u)+\frac{g}{3}I
$$

The preceding problem therefore reduces to the solution of a problem with 2 variables, $u$ and $g$ , under the constraint $g=\text{tr}(\varepsilon (u))$ . It may be reduced to the solution of a problem without constraint by introducing a Lagrange multiplier $p$ ; it is written:

find $u\in V$ , $p$ and $g$ solutions of the saddle-point problem for the Lagrangian:

$$
ℒ(u,p,g)=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[\sigma :({\varepsilon}^{D}(u)+\frac{g}{3}I)+p(div(u)-g)\right]d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}f.ud\Omega -\underset{{\Gamma}_{t}}{\int}t.ud\Gamma
$$

This problem may be solved by writing the optimality conditions:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial u}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}({\sigma}^{D}+pI):\delta \varepsilon d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}f.\delta ud\Omega -\underset{{\Gamma}_{t}}{\int}t.\delta ud\Gamma =0\\ \frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial p}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}(div(u)-g)\delta pd\Omega =0\\ \frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial g}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}(\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\sigma )-p)\delta gd\Omega =0\end{array}\right.
$$

- *the first equation corresponds to the equilibrium equation,*
- *the second equation expresses the kinematic relation linking* $g$ *to* $u$,
- *the third equation gives the expression of the Lagrange multiplier* $p$ *,*

when the problem does not derive from an energy, one may directly use the system of equations .

In the case where there exists a one-to-one relation between the pressure and the swelling, as for example for an elastoplastic material with
a plasticity criterion of von Mises type, it is possible to express the swelling explicitly and therefore to remove the third equation of the
system .
One then obtains the following system of two equations in two unknowns:

$$
\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\underset{\Omega}{\int}({\sigma}^{D}+pI):\delta \varepsilon d\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}f.\delta ud\Omega -\underset{{\Gamma}_{t}}{\int}t.\delta ud\Gamma =0\\ \underset{\Omega}{\int}(div(u)-\frac{p}{\kappa})\delta pd\Omega =0\end{array}\right.
$$

Where $\kappa$ is the bulk modulus.

### 2.2 Formulation in large strains

As for the small strains, it is possible to propose a variational formulation valid for large strains.
The principle is identical, but one relies in this case on the decomposition of the transformation-gradient
tensor $F=I+\frac{\partial u}{\partial X}$ proposed by Flory  :

$$
F={F}^{s}\bar{F} \ \text{avec} \ {F}^{s}={J}^{1/3}I \ \ \text{et} \ \ \bar{F}={J}^{-1/3}F \ \ \text{et} \ \ J=\det(F)
$$

The idea, once again, is to enrich the kinematics by means of a swelling variable $g$ , a priori independent of the displacements, and weakly linked to the volume change by a weak relation:

$$
B(J)\underset{\mathit{faible}}{\approx}B\mathrm{°}A(g)=B(A(g))
$$

Several relations have been tested:

$$
\begin{split}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}J=1+g\\ {J}^{2}=1+g\\ \ln(J)=g\\ J=\exp(g)\end{array} \right.\end{split}
$$

For certain simulations, small differences have been observed. For the `INCO_UPG` elements with the SIMO_MIEHE strain,
it is finally the linear relation which has been implemented in the code: hence $B(J)=J$ and $A(g)=1+g$ .
For the `INCO_UPG` elements with the GDEF_LOG strain, it is the logarithmic relation which has been adopted.

Nevertheless, this choice not necessarily being final, one proposes to write the problem in the general case. One therefore introduces an enriched
deformation gradient:

$$
\tilde{F}={(\frac{A(g)}{J})}^{\frac{1}{3}}F
$$

The weak formulation of the problem rests on the search for the saddle point of the Lagrangian $ℒ$ , in
which the Lagrange multiplier $p$ and a third field $g$ , independent of the other two,
ensure in a weak manner that the relation between $J$ and $g$ is satisfied:

$$
\mathrm{ℒ}(u,g,p)=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\psi (\tilde{F})d{\Omega}_{0}-{W}_{\mathit{ext}}(u)+\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}p\left[B(J)-B\mathrm{°}A(g)\right]d{\Omega}_{0}
$$

where ${W}_{\mathrm{ext}}$ represents the potential of the external forces and $\psi (\tilde{F})$ the strain energy.

This problem may be solved as in small strains by writing the optimality conditions. The variation of the Lagrangian is written:

$$
\delta ℒ=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\left[P:\delta \tilde{F}+p(\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}J\frac{\delta J}{J}-\frac{\partial B°A(g)}{\partial g}\delta g)+\delta p(B(J)-B°A(g))\right]d{\Omega}_{0}-\delta {W}_{\mathrm{ext}}(u)
$$

with $P$ the first Piola-Kirchhoff stress tensor:

By injecting the variation of the enriched transformation and the expression of the Kirchhoff stress $\tau =P\tilde{{F}^{T}}$  1,
one obtains the following form for the variation of the Lagrangian:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\delta \mathrm{ℒ}& =& \underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}({\tau}^{d}+p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}JI):\delta Ld{\Omega}_{0}\\ & +& \underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}(\frac{\text{tr}(\tau )}{3}\frac{\partial A(g)/\partial g}{A(g)}-p\frac{\partial B\mathrm{°}A(g)}{\partial g})\delta gd{\Omega}_{0}+\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}(B(J)-B\mathrm{°}A(g))\delta pd{\Omega}_{0}\\ & -& \delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}(u)\end{array}
$$

where one has introduced the Eulerian displacement gradient ($x$  represents the position vector at the end of the increment):
$\delta L=\frac{\partial \delta u}{\partial x}=\delta F.{F}^{-1}$

In summary, the system to be solved is the following:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial u}=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\delta L:({\tau}^{d}+p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}JI)d{\Omega}_{0}-\delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}=0\\ \frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial g}=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\delta g(\frac{\text{tr}(\tau )}{3}\frac{\partial A(g)/\partial g}{A(g)}-p\frac{\partial B\mathrm{°}A(g)}{\partial g})d{\Omega}_{0}=0\\ \frac{\partial \mathrm{ℒ}}{\partial p}=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\delta p(B(J)-B\mathrm{°}A(g))d{\Omega}_{0}=0\end{array}
$$

**Note:**
*The Kirchhoff stress coming from the constitutive law is therefore written*
$\tau ={\tau}^{D}+p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}JI$

As regards the derivation of the tangent matrix, it of course requires a little more calculation than in small strains,
and has the particularity of not being symmetric in the general case. It is found in the code in the following form:

$$
\begin{split}K=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{\text{UU}}& {K}_{\text{UG}}& {K}_{\text{UP}}\\ {K}_{\text{GU}}& {K}_{\text{GG}}& {K}_{\text{GP}}\\ {K}_{\text{PU}}& {K}_{\text{PG}}& {K}_{\text{PP}}\end{array}\right]\end{split}
$$

The calculations are not detailed here. The reader may refer to the reading of [bib8].

**Note:**
*This formulation makes it possible to regularise, at low cost, the ductile damage models in which the damage variable is
directly linked to the volume change.*
*Indeed, in order to control the localisation of the damage and of the strain, the idea is to penalise the strong damage gradients.
As in this 3-field formulation the local swelling is treated as a nodal variable, its gradient is easily accessible
numerically (subject to an at least linear interpolation).*

In the spirit of the second-gradient displacement formulations (, ), it is enriched by a quadratic term in the swelling
gradient.
The variation of the Lagrangian is then written:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\delta ℒ& =& \underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\left({\tau}^{d}+p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}JI\right):\delta L+\left(\frac{\text{tr}(\tau )}{3}\frac{\partial A(g)/\partial g}{A(g)}-p\frac{\partial B\mathrm{°}A(g)}{\partial g}\right)\delta gd{\Omega}_{0}\\ & +& \underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\left[\left(B(J)-B\mathrm{°} A(g)\right)\delta p+c\nabla g\cdot \nabla \delta g\right]d{\Omega}_{0}-\delta {W}_{\mathit{ext}}(u)\end{array}
$$

$c$ is a parameter to be determined and homogeneous to a force.
This parameter introduces, in a way, an internal coupling length between the material points.
The term added here is isotropic: one considers that the internal length to be introduced is identical in all directions.
For the application to the ductile damage of steels, this assumption seems entirely acceptable. This formulation
is usable for the Rousselier model, [R5.03.07](doc:r5.03.07), subject to the
definition of the keyword `C_CARA` under the operand `NON_LOCAL` of `DEFI_MATERIAU` (see test ssnp122a)

## 3 Discretisation by mixed finite elements

### 3.1 Choice of the discretisation

When one uses a mixed formulation, it is necessary to discretise both the space of the displacements,
of the Lagrange multiplier p and of the “swelling” $g$ . The experience acquired on mixed elements,
in particular with 2 fields for the incompressible elements, makes it possible to know that the discretisation of these fields
cannot be arbitrary, on pain of obtaining oscillation phenomena (in particular at the level of the pressures)
or locking phenomena (elements which cannot deform, or which are too stiff). Thus it is necessary
to have a number of pressure Gauss points large enough to verify the incompressibility condition
almost everywhere and a number of pressure Gauss points small enough to have more degrees of freedom to
compute than constraints to verify. One of the conditions necessary in order to obtain satisfactory results is
the verification, by the finite element considered, of the LBB condition (LADYJENSKAIA, BREZZI, BABUSKA).
One may find in  and  examples of elements satisfying the LBB condition.

Here the problem is a little different when the 3-field formulation is adopted.

In the present state, the discretisations used are not the same in the HPP version and in the large-strain version.

#### 3.1.1 Small strains

For the small strains, we have drawn inspiration from the classical practice of mixed formulations (e.g. ),
by using an element of type $\mathit{P2}/\mathit{P1}/\mathit{P1}$ for the 3-field formulation.
In other words, the displacement is quadratic, and the pressure and the swelling are both linear. The finite elements
used for the 3-field formulation are therefore the following:

| in 2D: | $u$ | 6-node triangle | 8-node quadrilateral |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $p,g$ | 3-node triangle | 4-node quadrilateral |  |  |
| in 3D: | $u$ | 10-node tetrahedron | 20-node hexahedron | 15-node pentahedron |
| $p,g$ | 4-node tetrahedron | 8-node hexahedron | 6-node pentahedron |  |

For each type of element, one uses a single family of Gauss points:

- 3 points for the triangles
- 4 points for the quadrilaterals
- 4 points for the tetrahedra
- 8 points for the hexahedra
- 21 points for the pentahedra

For the two-field formulation, an element of type $\mathit{P2}/\mathit{P1}$ has been introduced.
The displacement therefore has a quadratic interpolation, while the pressure is interpolated linearly.
In the case of the use of a discretisation into linear triangles or tetrahedra, two stabilisation methods have
been introduced. The first corresponds to the stabilised finite element $\mathit{P1}\text{+/}\mathit{P1}$ .
The $+$ corresponds to the introduction of an additional degree of freedom at the centre of the element in
the interpolation of the displacements. This additional degree is commonly called a “bubble”. This stabilisation method
works only on simplex elements (triangle in 2D and tetrahedron in 3D). It has the advantage of using
very few degrees of freedom. The second stabilisation method corresponds to the Orthogonal Sub-Grid Scale (OSGS) method
. The advantage of this method is that it works for all element topologies.
Its principal drawback is that it introduces a third unknown (and therefore additional degrees of freedom)
corresponding to the projected pressure field $\pi$ on the space orthogonal to the displacement fields.

The finite elements used for the 2-field formulation are therefore the following:

| Interpolation |  | P1+/P1 | P1/P1 OSGS | P2/P1 | P1/P1 OSGS | P2/P1 | P1/P1 OSGS | P2/P1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| in 2D: | $u$ | 3-node triangle | 3-node triangle | 6-node triangle | 4-node quadrilateral | 8-node quadrilateral |  |  |
| $p$ | 3-node triangle | 3-node triangle | 3-node triangle | 4-node quadrilateral | 4-node quadrilateral |  |  |  |
| $\pi$ |  | 3-node triangle |  | 4-node quadrilateral |  |  |  |  |
| in 3D: | $u$ | 4-node tetrahedron + bubble | 4-node tetrahedron | 10-node tetrahedron | 8-node cube | 20-node cube | 6-node pentahedron | 15-node pentahedron |
| $p$ | 4-node tetrahedron | 4-node tetrahedron | 4-node tetrahedron | 8-node cube | 8-node cube | 6-node pentahedron | 6-node pentahedron |  |
| $\pi$ |  | 4-node tetrahedron |  | 8-node cube |  | 6-node pentahedron |  |  |

The families of Gauss points used are the same as those of the 3-field formulation. One will note that for
the elements $\mathit{P1}\text{+/}\mathit{P1}$ , one uses only a single Gauss point for the integration.

#### 3.1.2 Large strains

From version 11, the choice of the interpolations in large strains is identical to that of the small strains. The elements are of type $\mathit{P2}/\mathit{P1}/\mathit{P1}$ for the 3-field formulations and $\mathit{P2}/\mathit{P1}$ for the 2-field formulation. The finite elements used for the 3-field formulation are therefore the following:

| in 2D: | $u$ | 6-node triangle | 8-node quadrilateral |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $p,g$ | 3-node triangle | 4-node quadrilateral |  |  |
| in 3D: | $u$ | 10-node tetrahedron | 20-node hexahedron | 15-node pentahedron |
| $p,g$ | 4-node tetrahedron | 8-node hexahedron | 6-node pentahedron |  |

It is the same families of Gauss points as those of the small strains which have been used.

### 3.2 Writing of the discrete problem

One approaches here, in a first stage, the writing of the discrete problem in the framework of the 3-field formulation. Let ${u}^{e}$ , ${p}^{e}$ and ${g}^{e}$ be the vectors of the elementary nodal unknowns (respectively displacement, pressure and swelling). If ${N}^{u}$ , ${N}^{p}$ and ${N}^{g}$ are the shape functions (respectively of the interpolations of the displacement, of the pressure and of the swelling) associated with the finite element considered:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{array}{c} u={N}^{u}{u}^{e} \\ p={N}^{p}{p}^{e} \\ g={N}^{g}{g}^{e} \end{array}\end{split}
$$

#### 3.2.1 Writing in small strains

$B$ is the classical derivation matrix making it possible to pass from ${u}^{e}$ to $\varepsilon$ :

$$
\varepsilon =B{u}^{e}
$$

In the formulation, one distinguishes ${e}_{\text{dev}}$ and ${e}_{\text{dil}}$ , which leads us to define the operators ${B}_{\text{dev}}$ and ${B}_{\text{dil}}$ such that: ${\epsilon}^{D}={B}_{\text{dev}}{U}^{e}$ and $\frac{\text{tr}\epsilon }{3}={B}_{\text{dil}}{U}^{e}$

The discretised form of the equations of the 3-field problem [eq. ] is written:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{array}{l}
{F}_{u}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}^{T}({\sigma}^{D}+pI)d\Omega ={F}_{\mathrm{ext}}\\
{F}_{p}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{p})}^{T}({B}_{\mathrm{dil}}u-g)d\Omega =0\\
{F}_{g}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{g})}^{T}(\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\sigma )-p)d\Omega =0\end{array}\end{split}
$$

The tangent matrix of the problem is symmetric and rests on the following terms:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{array}{l}
{K}_{\mathrm{uu}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{u}}{\partial {u}^{e}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}_{\mathrm{dev}}^{T}D{B}_{\mathrm{dev}}d\Omega \\
{K}_{\mathrm{up}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{u}}{\partial {p}^{e}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}_{\mathrm{dil}}^{T}{N}^{p}d\Omega \\
{K}_{\mathrm{ug}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{u}}{\partial {g}^{e}}=\frac{1}{3}\underset{\Omega}{\int}\text{tr}({B}_{\mathrm{dev}}^{T}D){N}^{g}d\Omega \\
{K}_{\mathrm{pp}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{p}}{\partial {p}^{e}}=0 \\
{K}_{\mathrm{pg}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{p}}{\partial {g}^{e}}=-\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{p})}^{T}{N}^{g}d\Omega \\
{K}_{gg}=\frac{\partial {F}_{g}}{\partial {g}^{e}}=\frac{1}{9}\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{g})}^{T}\text{tr}(D){N}^{g}d\Omega\end{array}\end{split}
$$

As regards the 2-field formulation, it is easily deduced from the preceding one. The discretised forms of the equations give us:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{array}{l}
{F}_{u}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}^{T}({\sigma}^{D}+pI)d\Omega ={F}_{\mathit{ext}}\\
{F}_{p}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{p})}^{T}({B}_{\mathrm{dil}}u-\frac{{N}^{p}}{\kappa})d\Omega =0\end{array}\end{split}
$$

The tangent matrix of the problem is symmetric and rests on the following terms:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{array}{l}
{K}_{\mathrm{uu}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{u}}{\partial {u}^{e}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}_{\mathrm{dev}}^{T}D{B}_{\mathrm{dev}}d\Omega\\
{K}_{\mathrm{up}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{u}}{\partial {p}^{e}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{B}_{\mathrm{dil}}^{T}{N}^{p}d\Omega\\
{K}_{\mathit{pp}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{p}}{\partial {p}^{e}}=-\frac{1}{\kappa}\underset{\Omega}{\int}{({N}^{p})}^{T}{N}^{p}d\Omega\end{array}\end{split}
$$

## 4 Integration into Code_Aster of the incompressible finite elements

### 4.1 General presentation of the incompressible element in small strains

The finite elements are integrated into *Code_Aster*  in 2D plane strain, in 2D axisymmetric and in 3D. The 3 modellings
are accessible by using the following options of AFFE_MODELE:

- `3D_INCO_UPG`, `3D_INCO_UP` or `3D_INCO_UPO` for 3D and respectively for the 3-field formulation, the 2-field formulation and the

2-field formulation stabilised with the OSGS method,

- `D_PLAN_INCO_UPG`, `D_PLAN_INCO_UP` or `D_PLAN_INCO_UPO` for 2D in plane strain and respectively for

the 3-field formulation, the 2-field formulation and the 2-field formulation stabilised with the OSGS method,

- `AXIS_INCO_UPG`, `AXIS_INCO_UP` or `AXIS_INCO_UPO` for 2D axisymmetric and respectively for the 3-field formulation,

the 2-field formulation and the 2-field formulation stabilised with the OSGS method.

In the element catalogue, the incompressible elements may be applied on the mesh cells:

| **Mesh cells** | **Formulation** | **Number of displacement nodes** | **Number of pressure or swelling nodes** | **Number of nodes of the projected pressure gradient** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TRIA3 | 2 fields | 3 | 3 |  |
| TRIA3 | 2 fields OSGS | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| TRIA6 | 2 and 3 fields | 6 | 3 |  |
| QUAD4 | 2 fields OSGS | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| QUAD8 | 2 and 3 fields | 8 | 4 |  |
| HEXA20 | 2 and 3 fields | 20 | 8 |  |
| TETRA4 | 2 fields | 4 | 4 |  |
| TETRA4 | 2 fields OSGS | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| TETRA10 | 2 and 3 fields | 10 | 4 |  |
| PENTA6 | 2 fields OSGS | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| PENTA15 | 2 and 3 fields | 15 | 6 |  |

In the initialisation routines of the incompressible elements, one defines:

- 1 single family of Gauss points (cf. § r3.06.08-choixdisc ),
- 2 families of shape functions associated respectively with the displacements (shape functions of degree 2) and with the pressure

and swelling terms (of degree 1) if one is in the 3-field formulation .

Let us take as an example the 10-node tetrahedral element: the displacement degrees of freedom are carried by all the nodes,
whereas only the 4 vertex nodes possess the degrees of freedom $p$ and $g$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.08/100000000000090600000415F70C2876088841FF.png)

The components accessible for the field DEPL are therefore:

- the displacements: DX, DY and DZ in 3D at all the nodes,
- the pressure: PRES for the vertex nodes,
- the swelling (3-field formulation): GONF for the vertex nodes.

### 4.2 General presentation of the incompressible element in large strains

The finite elements are integrated into code_aster in 2D plane strain, in 2D axisymmetric and in 3D. The 3 modellings
resting on a 3-field formulation are accessible by using the following options of AFFE_MODELE:

- `3D_INCO_UPG` for 3D,
- `D_PLAN_INCO_UPG` for 2D in plane strain,
- `AXIS_INCO_UPG` for 2D axisymmetric.

The 3 modellings using the large-strain formalism of GDEF_LOG and resting on a 2-field formulation
are accessible by using the following options of AFFE_MODELE:

- `3D_INCO_UP` for 3D,
- `D_PLAN_INCO_UP` for 2D in plane strain,
- `AXIS_INCO_UP` for 2D axisymmetric.

In the element catalogue, the incompressible elements may be applied on the mesh cells:

| **Mesh cells** | **Number of displacement nodes** | **Number of pressure (and swelling) nodes** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| TRIA6 | 6 | 3 |
| QUAD8 | 8 | 4 |
| HEXA20 | 20 | 8 |
| TETRA10 | 10 | 4 |
| PENTA15 | 15 | 6 |

In the initialisation routines of the incompressible elements, one defines:

- 1 single family of Gauss points (cf. § r3.06.08-choixdisc ),
- 2 families of shape functions associated respectively with the displacements and with the pressure (shape functions of degree 2)

and with the swelling terms (shape functions of degree 1).

Let us take as an example the 10-node tetrahedral element: the degrees of freedom in displacement and in pressure are carried by all the
nodes, whereas only the 4 vertex nodes possess the swelling degrees of freedom.

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.08/100000000000090600000415F70C2876088841FF.png)

The components accessible for the field DEPL are therefore

- the displacements: DX, DY and DZ in 3D at all the nodes,
- the pressure: PRES for the vertex nodes,
- the swelling: GONF for the vertex nodes.

### 4.3 Use of the modelling

The modellings INCO_UP, INCO_UPG and INCO_UPO may be used with the non-linear mechanics operators
STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE. It is also possible to use the linear mechanics operator MECA_STATIQUE; however
this is strongly discouraged because the results obtained may be of mediocre quality. The small-strain version
is accessible by using DEFORMATION=’PETIT’ under COMPORTEMENT, the large-strain version by using DEFORMATION=’SIMO_MIEHE’
or DEFORMATION=’GDEF_LOG’. The usable constitutive relations are those available respectively in small strains
and in large strains SIMO_MIEHE or GDEF_LOG for the INCO_UPG modellings. The modellings INCO_UP and INCO_UPO are
at present limited to the relations ELAS and VMIS_ISOT_XXX.

It is therefore not possible to use the modellings with the commands: CALC_MATR_ELEM/CALC_VECT_ELEM/ASSE_MATRICE/ASSE_VECTEUR/RESOUDRE

Given the form of the tangent matrix for the 3-field formulations (INCO_UPG), it is often necessary to use
the MUMPS solver in order to solve the linear systems.

It is recommended to use the reference-stress convergence criterion RESI_REFE_RELA except for the INCO_UPO elements
because the option is not available.

### 4.4 Formulation of the elementary right-hand side terms

The loads may be gravity, distributed surface forces, pressures. The elementary terms are computed
in the classical manner for the displacement degrees of freedom and one assigns the zero value for the pressure and swelling degrees of freedom.

### 4.5 Computation of the strains and of the stresses

In this formulation, one should distinguish the stress field coming from the constitutive law ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ldc}}$,
from the stress field which satisfies equilibrium and which is defined by the relation $\sigma ={\sigma}_{\mathit{ldc}}^{D}+pI$ .
It is this latter field which is stored in SIEF_ELGA, together with the relation linking the multiplier $p$ and ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ldc}}$.

In small strains, the components of SIEF_ELGA are:

- SIXX, SIYY, SIZZ, SIXY in 2D as well as SIXZ and SIYZ in 3D: components of the tensor $\sigma ={\sigma}_{\mathit{ldc}}^{D}+pI$ ,
- SIP, which is equal to $\left(\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}({\sigma}_{\mathit{ldc}})-p\right)$ ,

In large strains, the components of SIEF_ELGA are:

- SIXX, SIYY, SIZZ, SIXY in 2D as well as SIXZ and SIYZ in 3D: components of the tensor $\sigma =\left(\frac{{\tau}^{d}}{J}+p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}I\right)$ ,
- SIP, which is equal to $\left(\frac{\text{tr}(\tau )}{\mathrm{3J}}-p\frac{\partial B(J)}{\partial J}\right)$ ,

It is also possible to recompute EPSI_ELGA, which is the strain field in the classical sense.

One may also carry out a limit-load computation with POST_ELEM.

## 5 Validation

### 5.1 Incompressible elastic case

The test SSLV130 (cf. [V3.04.130](doc:v3.04.130)) makes it possible to verify the validity of the modelling in
the case of an incompressible elastic cylinder subjected to an internal pressure. Its large-strain equivalent
also exists: test SSNV112 (cf. [V6.04.112](doc:v6.04.112)).
