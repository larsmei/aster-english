# r7.02.10 Simplified harmfulness analysis of a defect by the Kcp method, β correction

**Summary:**

The analysis method presented (Kcp method, β correction) is applied to the harmfulness analysis of a defect located under the cladding of pressurised-water reactor vessels, implemented by the command POST_KCP.

The reference Kcp method is codified in the RSE-M and has the aim of evaluating, from a study carried out within the framework of linear elasticity and in which the defect is not explicitly meshed, the elastoplastic stress intensity factor (also described as "plastically corrected" in the context of the present methodology) at each of the various tips of the defect, that is to say at the tip of the defect located on the cladding side (named tip A) and at the tip of the defect located in the base metal (named tip B).

To this end, one first computes the elastic stress intensity factor at the two tips of the defect, with the aid of the stresses at the nodes coming from the mechanical solution and of the residual stresses given by the user. Then, in a second stage, one computes the plastically corrected stress intensity factor from its elastic equivalent via the method known as the "β correction". At each time instant of the analysis and at each of the tips of the defect, the ratio obtained of the critical toughness to the plastically corrected stress intensity factor determines the instantaneous margin factor and then makes it possible to deduce from it the minimum margin factor with respect to fracture.

The theoretical aspects of the method and of its software implementation are the subjects of the following paragraphs.

## 1 General description of the method

### 1.1 Principle of the analyses carried out

In a first stage, from the processing of the stresses coming from the numerical analysis in linear elasticity on the sound mesh (without a meshed defect) on the one hand, and from the residual stresses supplied by the user on the other hand, the elastic stress intensity factor is computed on the basis of an analytical formula. This analytical formula corresponds to the method of influence coefficients for a semi-elliptical defect, which is codified in the RSE-M.

In a second stage, the application of the method known as the "β correction" to the elastic stress intensity factor makes it possible to deduce the elastoplastic stress intensity factor (consequently also described as "plastically corrected").

### 1.2 Domain of validity of the Kcp method, β correction

The method proposed applies to an underclad defect located in the regular region of a ferritic-steel vessel clad on the internal wall with austenitic stainless steel, subjected either:

- to a thermal transient applied on the internal surface, possibly combined with a limited pressure loading
- to a pressure loading alone.

The method known as the "β correction" correction is valid for an underclad defect whose tip located on the cladding side lies strictly at the interface between the cladding and the base metal.

### 2.4 Change of frame

In the linear elastic study, the frame considered for the vessel is the global Cartesian basis. Now, with a view to identifying the stress associated with each fracture mode, given the cylindrical geometry of the vessel and the two types of defect orientation studied, it is practical and appropriate to be able to place oneself in the cylindrical basis centred on the middle of the defect.

Thus, a change of frame is carried out in **POST_KCP** before the application of the analytical formula described in §2.2.

## 3 Step no. 2: computation of the elastoplastic stress intensity factor by the Kcp method, β correction

### 3.1 Plastic correction factor over a loading phase considered in isolation

The expression of the stress intensity factor corresponds to that of a semi-elliptical underclad defect under the assumption of a linear elastic constitutive behaviour of the cladding and of the base metal .

The method known as the "β correction" is specific to underclad defects and applies only within the framework of an increasing loading phase considered in isolation.

It consists in applying a corrective factor, denoted βA for the tip on the cladding side (respectively βB for the tip on the base-metal side), to the elastic stress intensity factor equivalent at tips A and B, in order to take account of the influence on the defect of the plastification phenomenon occurring at the tip on the cladding side and at the tip on the base-metal side.

The radius of the plastic zone $r_y$ is defined at point A by the formula:

$$
r_{yA} = \frac{1}{6 \pi} \left( \frac{K_{\text{eqA}}}{S_{yA}} \right)^2
$$

where $S_{yA}$ is the elastic limit of the cladding at A.

In the increasing phase of $K_{\text{eq}}$ , the factor $K_{\text{cp}}$ is obtained by the expressions:

- **At point A**:

$$
K_{\text{cpA}} = \beta_A \cdot K_{\text{eqA}}
$$

  with:

$$
\beta_A = 1 + C_A \cdot \tanh\left( \frac{36 \cdot r_{yA}}{t_r} \right)
$$

- **At point B**:

$$
K_{\text{cpB}} =
\begin{cases}
\beta_B^{3D} \cdot K_{\text{eqB}}, & \text{pour les défauts semi-elliptiques} \\
\beta_B \cdot K_{\text{eqB}}, & \text{pour les défauts bandes}
\end{cases}
$$

  with:

$$
\beta_B^{3D} = \max\left( 1, \beta_B \cdot \sqrt{\frac{1 + 1.464 \cdot \left( \frac{h}{2c} \right)^{1.65}}{1 + 1.464 \cdot (\beta_B^2) \cdot \left( \frac{h}{2c} \right)^{1.65}}} \right)
$$

  and:

$$
\beta_B = 1 + C_B \cdot \tanh\left( \frac{36 \cdot r_{yA}}{t_r} \right)
$$

- **At point C**:

$$
K_{\text{cpC}} = \beta_C \cdot K_{\text{eqC}}
$$

  with:

$$
\beta_C = \beta_B
$$

The coefficients $C_A$ and $C_B$ are given in the following table:

|  | Longitudinal defect | Circumferential defect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tip A (interface) | $C_A = 0.165 \ln(h)$ | $C_A = 0.5$ |
| Tip B (base metal) | $C_B = 0.465 (1 + h / 100)$ | $C_B = 0.5$ |

With the depth $h$ of the defect expressed in millimetres.

### 3.2 Plastic correction in the course of a transient

In the course of a transient, the vessel generally passes through a succession of **increasing loading phases** and of **unloading phases**. Consequently, a transient lies outside the theoretical domain of application of the method known as the "β correction".

However, the notion of plastic correction has been extended to the case of a real transient by retaining, at a given time instant, the maximum plastic correction obtained over all the preceding increasing loading phases. The principle of this extension is the same whether one considers the tip on the cladding side or the tip on the base-metal side. For the sake of simplicity, the explicit mention of the tip considered is omitted in the notations used in the remainder of this section.

**Determination of the plastic correction:**

At each new increasing loading phase, a plastic correction, denoted $\Delta K$ , is re-evaluated as follows:

$$
\Delta K = K_{\beta} - K_{I} = (\beta - 1) \times K_{I}
$$

where:

- $K_{I}$ is the elastic stress intensity factor determined in §2.
- $\beta$ is the plastic correction factor described in §3.1.

If this new plastic correction is greater than the preceding maximum plastic correction, denoted $\Delta K_{\text{max}}$ , then $\Delta K_{\text{max}}$ is updated.

**Expression of the plastic correction applied:**

The plastic correction finally applied, denoted $K_{\text{cp}}$ , is written:

$$
K_{\text{cp}} = K_{I} + \Delta K_{\text{max}}
$$

**Case of the unloading phases:**

During an unloading phase, the plastic correction applied consists in adding $\Delta K_{\text{max}}$ (computed during the preceding increasing loading phases) to the elastic stress intensity factor $K_{I}$ .

Indeed, the absence of plastification during an unloading phase implies that the plastic correction corresponds only to the plastified residue of the preceding loading phases.

**Algorithm**

- One initialises $K_{\text{max}} = 0$ .

- at the first time instant, one will be in an unloading phase by comparison with $K_{I_{\text{last}}}$ ,
- no plastification at the first time instant.

**One initialises $K_{I_{\text{last}}}$ to an arbitrarily high value:**

**Loop over the time instants of the loading history**:

1. **If** $K_I(t_n) \leq K_{I_{\text{last}}}$ **then** (unloading phase):

$$
K_{\text{cp}}(t_n) = K_I(t_n) + \Delta K_{\text{max}}
$$

2. **Else** (loading phase):

  - **If** $\beta(t_n) \times K_I(t_n) > K_I(t_n) + \Delta K_{\text{max}}$ **then**:

$$
K_{\text{cp}}(t_n) = \beta(t_n) \times K_I(t_n)
$$

$$
\Delta K_{\text{max}} = K_{\text{cp}}(t_n) - K_I(t_n)
$$

  - **Else**:

$$
K_{\text{cp}}(t_n) = K_I(t_n) + \Delta K_{\text{max}}
$$

  **End If**

**End If**

1. One updates $K_{I_{\text{last}}} = K_I(t_n)$ .

**End Loop**

The same algorithm described above is implemented for the plastic corrections of the stress intensity factors at the two tips $A$ and $B$ of the crack as the loading history proceeds.
