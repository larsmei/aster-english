# u2.08.04 User's guide to the analysis of critical loads and of Euler buckling modes, and to buckling analysis

**Summary:**

The aim of this documentation is to present a methodological guide for the analysis of critical loads and of Euler buckling modes on the one hand, and for the non-linear buckling analysis (instabilities) of a structure on the other. It deals mainly with the two functionalities of *Code_Aster*:

- the linear buckling analysis, known as the Euler analysis, through CALC_MODES (with the keyword TYPE_RESU=‘MODE_FLAMB’),
- the analysis of the quasi-static evolution (operator STAT_NON_LINE) of the structure, which presents geometric and constitutive non-linearities, for which one seeks the instabilities (option CRIT_STAB), a limit point, or even the post-critical response.

The first step is, generally, an Euler buckling analysis, which will make it possible to know the buckling modes and the corresponding critical loads. From the designer's point of view, knowledge of the first mode and of its critical load is often sufficient, in order to define an operating margin with respect to the imposed loading: the multiplier coefficient between the imposed loading and the lowest critical load gives the safety margin.

**Notes**

- *Knowledge of the first Euler buckling mode can also serve as an indication for optimising the management of the incremental non-linear analysis carried out afterwards. Indeed, as the critical load is approached, one can then decide to modify the piloting or to reduce the time step, or even to increase the number of iterations for checking equilibrium in the residual method, at each load step.*
- *The shape of the Euler buckling mode can also be used to impose an initial geometric imperfection on the structure, in order to ensure, among other things, that the incremental non-linear buckling analysis will indeed bifurcate onto this mode.*

The Euler analysis being linear by definition, it does not make it possible to take into account inelastic constitutive relations, unilateral contact or the follower character of the forces applied. It is then necessary to carry out an incremental non-linear analysis, which in quasi-statics will rely on the STAT_NON_LINE command of *Code_Aster*. This is the classical incremental method by equilibrium residual.

The particular points of its use will be dealt with later, in particular the use of the non-linear stability analysis with the keyword CRIT_STAB (which is also available in DYNA_NON_LINE for dynamic studies).

A table of the test cases available for this type of simulation will be found in § u2.08.04-_tude_non_lin_aire_quasi-statique_de_la_structure of this document.

## 1 Euler buckling analysis

The analysis of the buckling modes in the sense of Euler [bib5] is carried out by the operator for solving eigenvalue problems CALC_MODES (with the keyword TYPE_RESU = 'MODE_FLAMB'). In the context of buckling, one has the following typical syntax:

MODP1 = CALC_MODES ( MATR_RIGI = RAMEP1 ,

MATR_RIGI_GEOM = RAGEP1 ,

TYPE_RESU = 'MODE_FLAMB' ,

OPTION = 'BANDE' ,

CALC_CHAR_CRIT = _F( CHAR_CRIT = ( -2.4 ,-2.2 , ) ,),

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F( METHODE= 'SORENSEN',

DIM_SOUS_ESPACE = 80 ,

NMAX_ITER_SOREN = 80 , ) , )

The argument of the keyword MATR_RIGI must be the so-called material stiffness matrix, whereas the keyword MATR_RIGI_GEOM expects the geometric stiffness matrix.

As a reminder, the buckling modes are the eigenmodes of the following eigenvalue problem:

$(K+\mu {K}_{g})x=0\iff Kx=\lambda {K}_{g}x$

with:

| --- | --- | --- |
| ${K}_{g}$ | <string>:31: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | geometric stiffness matrix |
| $\lambda$ | <string>:31: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | eigenvalue ($\lambda =-\mu$ with $\mu$ the multiplier coefficient of the loading) |

**Note:**

It is indeed the critical load coefficient $\mu$ and not $\lambda$ which is stored in the result concept.

The material (or elastic) stiffness is computed with the option 'RIGI_MECA’ of CALC_MATR_ELEM.

The geometric stiffness is computed from the stress field which is the solution of the linear problem (option 'RIGI_GEOM’ of CALC_MATR_ELEM). One must therefore have carried out a linear static analysis before using CALC_MODES for buckling.

If the loading is composed of a fixed part (not piloted) and of a variable part, the multiplier coefficient of the loading must, of course, apply only to the variable part. The contribution of the other part of the loading is found in the first member. Let us denote by ${f}_{c}$ the fixed loading and by ${f}_{v}$ the piloted loading (proportional to $\mu$ ). The eigenvalue problem becomes:

$(K+{K}_{g}({f}_{c}+\mu {f}_{v}))x=0\iff (K+{K}_{g}({f}_{c}))x=\lambda {K}_{g}({f}_{v})x$

with:

| --- | --- | --- |
| ${K}_{g}({f}_{c})$ | <string>:52: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | geometric stiffness matrix for the non-piloted loading |
| ${K}_{g}({f}_{v})$ | <string>:52: (INFO/1) Unexpected possible title overline or transition. Treating it as ordinary text because it's so short.: | geometric stiffness matrix for the variable loading |

In this case, one must therefore solve two prior linear elastic problems, in order to be able to compute the two different geometric stiffness matrices. In order to be exhaustive, the presentation will concern a structure subjected to imposed displacements as well as to forces, which will be the combination of a fixed loading and of a variable loading that one will pilot with an increasing coefficient which may lead to buckling.

### 1.1 Step 1: Preliminary linear analysis (analyses)

One will use MECA_STATIQUE. The structure, meshed with shell-type elements (solid-shell type elements [bib3]), is subjected to Dirichlet (CONDLIM) and Neumann boundary conditions. The latter are decomposed into:

- PESA: gravity field,
- PRESPH: imposed non-piloted pressure field,
- PRESPS1: imposed variable pressure field.

For the buckling analysis, one must separate the constant forces from those which are variable (piloted by a coefficient). One will therefore carry out two linear static analyses. The first will be the case of the structure subjected to the imposed displacements and to the constant forces, the second will see the structure subjected to the imposed displacements and to the variable forces.

**Piloted loading:**

RESC11P1 = MECA_STATIQUE ( MODELE = MODELE ,

CHAM_MATER = CHMAT ,

CARA_ELEM = CARAELEM ,

EXCIT = ( _F( CHARGE = CONDLIM , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PRESPS1 , ) , ) ,

OPTION = 'SIEF_ELGA' ,

)

**Non-piloted loading:**

RESC12P1 = MECA_STATIQUE ( MODELE = MODELE ,

CHAM_MATER = CHMAT ,

CARA_ELEM = CARAELEM,

EXCIT = ( _F( CHARGE = CONDLIM , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PESA , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PRESPH , ) , ) ,

OPTION = 'SIEF_ELGA' ,

)

One will use the stress field to compute the associated geometric stiffness matrices, for the two loadings:

SIGC11P1 = CREA_CHAMP ( TYPE_CHAM = 'ELGA_SIEF_R' ,

OPERATION = 'EXTR' ,

RESULTAT = RESC11P1 ,

NOM_CHAM = 'SIEF_ELGA' ,

TYPE_MAXI = 'MINI' ,

TYPE_RESU = 'VALE' , )

#

REGC11P1 = CALC_MATR_ELEM ( OPTION = 'RIGI_GEOM' ,

MODELE = MODELE ,

CARA_ELEM = CARAELEM ,

SIEF_ELGA = SIGC11P1 , )

REGC11P1 is therefore the geometric stiffness matrix associated with the variable loading case (PRESPS1).

One likewise computes the geometric stiffness matrix for the constant loading (PESA and PRESPH), from RESC12P1:

SIGC12P1 = CREA_CHAMP ( TYPE_CHAM = 'ELGA_SIEF_R' ,

OPERATION = 'EXTR' ,

RESULTAT = RESC12P1 ,

NOM_CHAM = 'SIEF_ELGA' ,

TYPE_MAXI = 'MINI' ,

TYPE_RESU = 'VALE' , )

#

REGC12P1 = CALC_MATR_ELEM ( OPTION = 'RIGI_GEOM' ,

MODELE = MODELE ,

CARA_ELEM = CARAELEM ,

SIEF_ELGA = SIGC12P1 , )

It remains to compute the material stiffness matrix for the total loading:

REMEP1 = CALC_MATR_ELEM ( OPTION = 'RIGI_MECA' ,

MODELE = MODELE ,

CHAM_MATER = CHMAT ,

CARA_ELEM = CARAELEM ,

CHARGE = ( CONDLIM , PESA ,

PRESPH , PRESPS1 , ) , )

All the elementary matrices have been computed; the next step is therefore their assembly:

NUP1 = NUME_DDL ( MATR_RIGI = REMEP1 , )

#

RAMC1P1 = ASSE_MATRICE ( MATR_ELEM = REMEP1 ,

NUME_DDL = NUP1 , )

#

RAGEP1 = ASSE_MATRICE ( MATR_ELEM = REGC11P1 ,

NUME_DDL = NUP1 , )

#

RAGC12P1 = ASSE_MATRICE ( MATR_ELEM = REGC12P1 ,

NUME_DDL = NUP1 , )

One then sums the material stiffness matrix (RAMC1P1) and the geometric stiffness matrix (RAGC12P1) corresponding to the constant loading case:

RAMEP1 = COMB_MATR_ASSE ( COMB_R = ( _F( MATR_ASSE = RAMC1P1 ,

COEF_R = 1.0 , ) ,

_F( MATR_ASSE = RAGC12P1 ,

COEF_R = 1.0 , ) , ) , )

The two matrices required for the analysis of the buckling modes are therefore constructed.

### 1.2 Step 2: Analysis of the Euler modes

It can be useful to carry out STURM tests (operator INFO_MODE) on the search interval over which one wants to find the buckling cases. This will make it possible to optimise the size of the interval and to check the correct progress of the subsequent modal analysis, since one will know in advance the number of existing modes. The syntax is:

INFO_MODE ( MATR_RIGI = RAMEP1 ,

MATR_RIGI_GEOM = RAGEP1 ,

TYPE_MODE = 'MODE_FLAMB' ,

CHAR_CRIT = (-2.4 ,-2.2) )

Once the search interval for the critical buckling load has been chosen, one can then implement CALC_MODES as follows:

MODP1 = CALC_MODES ( MATR_RIGI = RAMEP1 ,

MATR_RIGI_GEOM = RAGEP1 ,

TYPE_RESU = 'MODE_FLAMB' ,

OPTION = 'BANDE' ,

CALC_ CHAR_CRIT =_F( CHAR_CRIT = ( -2.4 ,-2.2 , ) , ),

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F( DIM_SOUS_ESPACE = 80 ,

NMAX_ITER_SOREN = 80 , ) , )

**Note**

*If the algorithm does not converge or if the number of modes is not that predicted by* *INFO_MODE, it may be useful to increase the values of* *DIM_SOUS_ESPACE and* *NMAX_ITER_SOREN.*

One normalises the modes [bib6], using only the translational degrees of freedom:

MODP1 = NORM_MODE ( reuse = MODP1

MODE = MODP1 ,

NORME = 'TRAN' , )

The modes can then be post-processed.

**Notes**

- *It is indispensable to check that the geometric stiffness of the chosen model is indeed an option available in Code_Aster.*
- *A finer discretisation normally leads to a decrease in the critical loads.*
- *The discretisation must be able to capture the buckling modes, bearing in mind that these modes may generate localised strains (folds). The prior analysis of the dynamic modes can constitute a first indication of the quality of the mesh, although these modes may be very different from the buckling modes.*
- *The critical loads of the various modes are proportional to Young's modulus* $E$ *.*
- *With this method, one cannot take into account the follower character of the forces. Indeed, the geometric stiffness analysis carried out with the option* RIGI_GEOM *of* CALC_MATR_ELEM *is based on the assumption that all the forces imposed on the mechanical problem are of dead-force type. If one wants to take into account the follower character of certain loads, one must necessarily use the non-linear operators* STAT_NON_LINE *or* DYNA_NON_LINE *, with the keyword* CRIT_STAB, as will be seen in the remainder of this document.

## 2 Quasi-static non-linear study of the structure

This step is justified if the structure presents strong non-linearities, which the Euler buckling analysis cannot take into account. The operator for solving non-linear problems in quasi-statics is called STAT_NON_LINE [bib7].

These non-linearities may be linked to the material, which may have an elastoplastic constitutive behaviour [bib8], as in the example which follows. Taking contact into account, or even friction, is another source of non-linearities. One may also cite the case of follower loadings, such as pressure ([bib1] and [bib2] for solid-shell type elements), which require a non-linear approach in *Code_Aster*.

One can distinguish two types of non-linear stability analysis, which may be combined.

On the one hand, the generalisation of the Euler buckling analysis presented previously: one will follow the loading path in order to reach *limit points* or *critical loads* with the associated modes in the course of the incremental non-linear analysis. This takes the form of a stability analysis on the updated stiffness matrices. This type of analysis is often carried out on a structure without an initial imperfection.

On the other hand, one can take into account imperfections introduced on the perfect model (by inserting initial geometric imperfections), in order to “force” the bifurcation of the solution branch and to follow the stable branch in order to analyse the post-critical response. The piloting of the quasi-static loading path must be treated in an appropriate manner (operator STAT_NON_LINE, *cf.* [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)).

Obviously, this following of the post-critical solution can be initiated by the eigenvalue analysis of the updated stiffness matrices, in particular in order to detect the bifurcation properly and to define the imperfection then introduced on the basis of the buckling mode observed.

### 2.1 Stability analysis on updated stiffness matrices

Whether in quasi-statics (operator STAT_NON_LINE) or in dynamics (operator DYNA_NON_LINE), *Code_Aster* makes it possible to carry out incremental stability analyses in the sense of buckling on the current stiffness matrices. These analysis steps are managed by the factor keyword CRIT_STAB with the option TYPE = 'FLAMBEMENT' (*cf.* [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) and [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01)).

**Notes for the stability analysis**
In *Code_Aster*, one restricts oneself today to the case where the updated stiffness matrix is symmetric, because one calls the modal solver of SORENSEN type, *cf.* [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02). One therefore treats bifurcations of the equilibrium branches, that is to say a loss of uniqueness. This therefore restricts the families of constitutive models that one can treat (the so-called “associated” flow rule), as well as to cases of follower loadings such that a potential exists: for example for a closed enclosure under pressure. For a follower loading which removes the conservative character of the system and therefore leads to a non-symmetric stiffness matrix, the instability becomes dynamic.

**Notes for the non-linear transient dynamic analysis**

The stability analysis of trajectories is based on the study of the linearised system of the equations of motion and of the associated eigenvalue problem. Dynamic instability can occur if one exhibits a complex eigenvalue:
* with a strictly positive real part and with no imaginary part: the system is said to be unstable by *divergence*, the trajectory then having a non-oscillatory exponentially growing evolution;
* complex with a strictly positive real part: the system is said to be unstable by *flutter*, the trajectory then having an exponentially growing and oscillatory evolution. This is, for example, the case of the squeal produced by one solid sliding and rubbing on another;
* for irreversible constitutive laws with a non-associated flow rule, the loss of hyperbolicity may occur at the appearance of a pair of complex conjugate eigenvalues with a negative imaginary part linked to the non-symmetry of the tangent tensor: this is a *flutter* instability. If the eigenvalues become negative, it is a divergence instability. In fluid–structure interaction, one also identifies these two types of instability: a *flutter* instability when the damping of the system vanishes and then becomes negative — a fluid flow is necessary for this — a buckling instability when a natural circular frequency of the system vanishes.

The option CRIT_STAB acts in non-linear transient dynamics in *Code_Aster* as for the quasi-static case: one always carries out a stability analysis (therefore based only on the study of the symmetric stiffness matrices), not a dynamic instability analysis (damping becoming negative).

*With the coupled fluid–structure modelling* $(u,p,\phi )$ [R4.02.02](doc:r4.02.02), one must modify the assembled stiffness matrix (as well as the geometric stiffness when it is used). *To do this, one must supply the following keywords, under* *CRIT_STAB:*

- MODI_RIGI = 'OUI',
- DDL_EXCLUS=('PHI','PRES','DH',).

*The list of excluded degrees of freedom must include all the types of degrees of freedom linked to the fluid model: in the example of the test case FDNV100 one therefore has the potential* PHI *, the pressure* PRES *and the vertical displacement at the free surface* DH *. If one does not carry out this treatment, then the call to* CRIT_STAB *will fail because of a singular matrix and no shift strategy could overcome this.*

*In quasi-statics, this problem does not arise because the coupled fluid–structure modelling then has no meaning.*

This keyword makes it possible to trigger, at the end of each time increment, the analysis of a stability criterion. This criterion is useful for detecting, in the course of the loading, the point from which one loses stability (by buckling, for example).

This criterion is computed in the following way: at the end of a time step, in small perturbations, one solves $det\left({K}^{T}+\mu .{K}^{g}\right)=0$ . ${K}^{T}$ is the consistent tangent matrix at this time instant. ${K}^{g}$ is the geometric stiffness matrix, computed from the stress field at this time instant, and $\mu$ the critical load coefficient.

In practice, the loading is unstable if $|\mu |<1$ (in fact $0<\mu <1$ ). One computes the eigenvalues by the Sorensen method (*cf.* CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02)). This can be quite costly for large problems.

The keyword CHAR_CRIT makes it possible to save time by carrying out only a Sturm test in the frequency band supplied. If one finds at least one frequency, then one actually computes the values of the critical loads in this interval.

For large displacements and large strains, one solves $det\left({K}^{T}+\mu .I\right)=0$ because ${K}^{T}$ then contains ${K}^{g}$ .

The criterion is then an instability criterion: when $\mu$ changes sign (and therefore passes through 0) the loading is unstable.

**Notes concerning the geometric stiffness matrix**

*The stability analysis will be more accurate if one has the geometric stiffness matrix available: indeed, in its absence, certain instabilities cannot be detected, as, for example, in the purely elastic cases. Between sufficiently realistic models, one must therefore favour the use, for accurate stability analyses, of models which allow the computation of this geometric stiffness.*

The keyword NB_MODE designates the number of critical loads to be computed. Often the first is sufficient, but there may be multiple modes.

One stores the eigenmode corresponding to the smallest critical load (in absolute value) in the result object, under the name MODE_FLAMB. This eigenmode can be extracted and visualised (like a displacement field or a classical eigenmode). It is normalised to 1 on the largest displacement component. All the critical loads computed are displayed in the .mess file.

In practice, in order to limit the extra analysis cost, one advises optimising the calls to CRIT_STAB. One can for this purpose use the keywords LIST_INST/INST/PAS_CALCUL in the factor keyword CRIT_STAB. One can thus specify at which time steps the buckling modes will be computed.

In addition, it is judicious to use CRIT_STAB only on the time intervals where one suspects the possibility of instabilities.

Finally, if one wants a very good evaluation of the critical loads, it is advisable to refine the time step well as this zone is approached. This advice is all the more relevant in quasi-statics because the user then often resorts to time steps larger than in dynamics.

It is possible to stop cleanly (database reusable in restart) an analysis with STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE upon detection of an instability. This is not the default behaviour, in which *Code_Aster* will attempt to continue solving the problem, and if one has convergence, then this means that one has succeeded in following one of the solution branches.

To manage this stop (*cf.* test case ERREU10 [V1.01.308](doc:v1.01.308)), one must have previously used DEFI_LIST_INST with the following arguments:

> ECHEC=_F(EVENEMENT='INSTABILITE',ACTION='ARRET',),

Which means that, in the event of an instability-type event, the action triggered will be the stop.

Under CRIT_STAB, the following optional options make it possible to control this stopping criterion:

- PREC_INSTAB to define the (dimensionless) precision of the stopping criterion,
- SIGNE to specify the critical values to be considered.

The second keyword is used only when the geometric stiffness matrix is used. By default one considers the solution as being unstable if the critical load comes to lie between 1 and -1, but one can, if necessary, take into account only the positive or the negative part of this interval.

Without a geometric stiffness matrix, the instability will be detected when an eigenvalue of the global assembled stiffness matrix either:

- tends towards 0 (with a relative precision given by PREC_INSTAB),
- changes sign.

For example, in the case of a tank filled with water under earthquake, one can start the incremental or transient analysis with a critical load equal to 0.8 (analysis with geometric stiffness): which means that the tank would buckle if one imposed a depressurisation equal to 0.8 times the imposed hydrostatic pressure (the positive value of the critical load corresponds to a reversal of the direction of the loading considered). Therefore, if one specifies nothing, the analysis would be considered unstable and would stop. Since, in this case, one makes the assumption that there will be no depressurisation (for example by draining), one then excludes the interval $[0,1]$ from the stability analysis. The problem will therefore become unstable if the critical load reaches the interval $[-1,0]$ .

For analyses with monotonic evolution this type of reasoning is readily conceived, which is indeed the case for the static part of the loading, but for the dynamic part, the loading being cyclic, and unless one has specific information, it is safer and more conservative to keep the default option and therefore to consider the structure unstable if the critical load becomes less than 1 in absolute value.

Upon a stop on instability, the analysis will stop by closing the database cleanly: the user will be able to exploit it in restart.

### 2.2 Following of an unstable solution

For the study of a structure which is potentially unstable or liable to encounter a *limit point*, and which therefore risks encountering a bifurcation in solution in the course of the evolution of the loading, it is often useful to be able to choose a particular solution branch (often the physical solution when it is defined *a priori* without ambiguities). To this end, the user may have to introduce an initial imperfection which will “force” the structure to bifurcate onto the particular solution branch.

Several methods exist for defining this imperfection.

- One of the most suitable is to pre-deform the structure slightly according to the shape of the buckling mode corresponding to the branch that one wants to follow. The amplitude of this pre-deformation must be small, for example less than 1/10th of the thickness for a thin structure. The ideal is to find the minimal imperfection which is compatible with a satisfactory performance of the equilibrium residual algorithm. Indeed, too small an imperfection can lead to a difficulty of convergence of the residual, mainly in the case of force piloting. This initial imperfection can very judiciously be constructed from the instability mode computed with the option CRIT_STAB of STAT_NON_LINE / DYNA_NON_LINE (*cf.* previous paragraph). This mode then takes into account all the non-linearities introduced in the complete model. The more economical alternative is to use the Euler buckling mode, but this corresponds to the linear case.
- The geometric imperfection can also be defined by experimental measurements of the real part, whose geometry cannot be perfect.
- The imperfection can also take the form of a perturbation of the loading (misalignment, addition of a localised loading, ...) or of the mechanical characteristics of the material (local weakening of Young's modulus, for example). It may nevertheless then be more difficult to adapt the imperfection to the desired buckling mode, especially if the structure presents relatively close modes.

**Note**

*In certain cases, even on the unperturbed problem, the loading is such that it causes the desired bifurcation.*

One of the other particular points, linked to the instability, is the choice of the piloting technique of the STAT_NON_LINE algorithm, *cf.* [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03). Indeed, classical force piloting is no longer suitable because it cannot capture an unstable solution branch. Likewise, as a limit point is approached, convergence with force piloting will become more and more difficult, the tangent stiffness matrix becoming singular. It is then necessary to reduce the load increment and to increase the maximum number of iterations in order to continue the analysis.

One can also make use of the possibility of stopping cleanly in the event of instability (*cf.* previous paragraph) in order to manage, in restart, the bifurcation onto the chosen solution branch by initiating the continuation of the analysis with a perturbation along this instability mode.

There exist piloting techniques [bib9] which make it possible to get round these numerical difficulties. Among the methods proposed by *Code_Aster*, the so-called arc-length method [bib12] (option TYPE=’LONG_ARC’ of the keyword PILOTAGE in STAT_NON_LINE), which is the most suitable for buckling-type instabilities, in the case of possible “gentle” *snap-backs* [bib13]. For more brutal *snap-backs* (snap-through), M.A. Crisfield proposes a variant [bib13], not available in *Code_Aster*.

Other methods exist, such as that of E. Riks [bib14] (also not available in *Code_Aster*), which also treats the dynamic case.

If one only wants to obtain the *limit point*, including with good precision, loading piloting may suffice, provided that one manages well the parameters of the load-increment step (remember to use the command DEFI_LIST_INST) and of the maximum number of iterations allowed (ITER_GLOB_MAXI of CONVERGENCE). It may also be useful, as the limit point is approached, no longer to use the updated tangent matrix for the solver, since it is quasi-singular. One can then be content not to update this matrix at each analysis (parameters REAC_INCR and REAC_ITER) or, in the worst case, adopt the basic elastic matrix (PREDICTION=’ELASTIQUE’ and MATRICE=’ELASTIQUE’ of the keyword NEWTON).

Here is an example of the use of STAT_NON_LINE for an elastoplastic analysis in large displacements ([bib4] for the elements used, which are of solid-shell type), with force piloting:

RESU = STAT_NON_LINE ( ...

EXCIT = ( _F( CHARGE = CONDLIM ,

TYPE_CHARGE = 'FIXE_CSTE' , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PESA ,

TYPE_CHARGE = 'FIXE_CSTE' , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PRESPH ,

FONC_MULT = FONCMUL2 ,

TYPE_CHARGE = 'SUIV' , ) ,

_F( CHARGE = PRESPS1 ,

FONC_MULT = FONCMUL ,

TYPE_CHARGE = 'SUIV' , ) , ) ,

... )

**Notes**

- *One uses the tangent matrix updated at each analysis, allowing the cutting of the load step.*
- *The imposed pressures are follower forces (TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV').*
- *In the case of a modelling with solid elements, the strain tensor recommended in large displacements is ‘SIMO_MIEHE’.*

If one wants to replace force piloting by an arc-length method, it is sufficient to add:

RESU = STAT_NON_LINE ( ...

PILOTAGE = _F( GROUP_NO = 'G' ,

TYPE = 'LONG_ARC' ,

NOM_CMP = ( 'DY' , ) ,

COEF_MULT = 7. ) ,

...)

**Notes**

- *In Code_Aster, one cannot pilot follower forces.*
- *For arc-length piloting, it is, in general, recommended that* *GROUP_NO* *contain the whole structure.*

To finish, let us cite two articles by Crisfield which give a good general view of the problems and methods linked to non-linear analyses which may present various types of instability ([bib15] and [bib11]).

The documentation [U2.06.11](doc:u2.06.11) shows an example of the use of CRIT_STAB for the study of the behaviour of a metallic tank.
