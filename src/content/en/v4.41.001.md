# v4.41.001 TPNA01 - Steady axisymmetric problem with radiation

**Summary:**

This basic test allows to handle axisymmetric steady-state thermal analysis with a boundary condition radiation type. The solution is analytical. The problem is processed in axisymmetric and volume.

For the modellings presented here, the differences of the results obtained by *Code_Aster* are between 1 and 2% of the analytically calculated reference.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v4.41.001/100007B20000181500001AC5F6817B68E575AC1B.svg)

The hollow cylinder is supposed to be infinitely long.

### 1.2 Material properties

Only the conductivity coefficient is used. *Code_Aster* makes it mandatory to provide a function representing the volume enthalpy determined from the volume heat coefficient.

volumetric heat $\rho {C}_{P}=1.00J/{m}^{3}°C$

$k=40W/{m}^{3}°C$ thermal conductivity

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Radiation-like condition on the inner surface of the cylinder, convection-like condition (exchange with the outside medium) on the outer surface.

No boundary condition on the ends of the cylinder (which amounts to imposing a zero flow).

Internal surface $k\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}=\varepsilon \sigma [{(T+273.15)}^{4}-{({T}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{i}+273.15)}^{4}]$

> with $\varepsilon =0.6$ , $\sigma =5.73{10}^{-8}W/{m}^{2}{K}^{4}$ and ${T}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{i}=500.°C$ , $T$ in Celsius

External surface $k\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}={h}_{e}[{T}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{e}-T]$

> with ${h}_{e}=142.W/{m}^{2}°C$ and ${T}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{e}=20.°C$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

One analytical solution is available in the case of a cylinder of infinite length:

$T(r)=\frac{{T}_{e}-{T}_{i}}{\log(\frac{{R}_{e}}{{R}_{i}})}\log(r)+\frac{{T}_{i}\log({R}_{e})-{T}_{e}\log({R}_{i})}{\log({R}_{e})-\log({R}_{i})}$

## 5 Summary of results

The consideration of radiation conditions is quite correct in this stationary case. Note that this test involves a constant coefficient of thermal conductivity, so the only nonlinearity is the boundary conditions.

The errors are higher in the flow calculation, which can be explained by the use of nodes smoothings, based on the values calculated at the integration points (GAUSS points).
