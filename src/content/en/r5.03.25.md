# r5.03.25 Regularised damage law ENDO_SCALAIRE

**Summary:**

This document describes the elastic brittle constitutive model ENDO_SCALAIRE, available only for the non-local modelling with a damage gradient GRAD_VARI. The damage is modelled in a scalar manner; the compressive and tensile loadings are not distinguished. Unlike the other damage laws introduced previously, this one behaves in a regular manner (no snap-back, finite elongation at fracture) at least in the one-dimensional cases.

## 1 Field of application

The law ENDO_SCALAIRE belongs to a broad family of damage laws. It aims in particular to model an elastic brittle constitutive behaviour in a non-local version (GRAD_VARI [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01) ) so that its behaviour, at least in the one-dimensional cases, is regular. The parameters of the law have been chosen in order to ensure both the absence of snap-back in the force-displacement response and the finite elongation of the 1D bar at fracture. The local version of the law is not implemented. The modelled material is isotropic elastic. Its stiffness may decrease irreversibly when the strain energy becomes large, without distinguishing tension from compression. The width of the localisation bands is controlled by a material parameter, entered in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU under the keyword C_GRAD_VARI of the factor keyword NON_LOCAL [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

Piloting of type PRED_ELAS [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80) appears as the most appropriate mode of control of the loading level.

## 2 Variational formulation of the damage problem

### 2.1 Case of a generic law

Two equivalent approaches may be used to describe the damage process of a brittle isotropic material. On the one hand, it is possible to derive the damage law within the framework of the generalised standard description. In this case it is necessary to define a free energy of the system, as well as the dissipation potential. The flow rule then establishes the evolution of the internal variables.

As, for the description of damage, one needs only a scalar variable, the preceding description is simplified and may be reduced to a variational problem under the constraint of an increase of the damage [bib2].

In order to define a constitutive law with a damage gradient [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01) it is therefore sufficient to express the total free-energy density (elastic+dissipation) as a function of the strain tensor $\varepsilon$ and of the damage variable $0\le a\le 1$ . The spatial distribution of the damage is then given by a field $a(x)$ . The free-energy density is in general presented in the following form:

$\Phi (\varepsilon ,a)=A(a)w(\varepsilon )+\omega (a)+c/2{(\nabla a)}^{2}$ **eq 2.1-1**

Here $c$ is the non-locality parameter (C_GRAD_VARI), $w(\varepsilon )$ the elastic strain energy, $\omega (a)$ the dissipation energy and $A(a)$ the stiffness function. $a=0$ corresponds to the sound material and $a=1$ corresponds to the fully damaged material: $A(1)=0,A(0)=1$ . The evolution problem is henceforth a simple problem of minimisation of the Helmholtz free energy $F\equiv \int\Phi (\varepsilon ,a)d\Omega$ under the constraint $\dot{a}\ge 0$  1
.

${\min}_{(\varepsilon ,a)}F(\varepsilon ,a),\text{où}F(\varepsilon ,a)=\int\left[A(a)\varepsilon :E:\varepsilon +\omega (a)+c/2{(\nabla a)}^{2}\right]d\Omega$

where one has replaced $w(\varepsilon )=\varepsilon :E:\varepsilon /2$ by using the definition of the Hooke tensor. Two equations derive from the variational minimisation problem: $\delta F(\varepsilon ,a)/\delta \varepsilon =0$  2
and $\delta F(\varepsilon ,a)/\delta a\ge 0$ . The inequality in the second equation is linked to the presence of an imposed constraint. These two equations must be satisfied everywhere in the domain of integration $\Omega$ . They are supplemented by a Kuhn-Tucker consistency equation $\dot{a}\cdot \delta F(\varepsilon ,a)/\delta a=0$ . On the boundaries $\partial \Omega$ we obtain an additional normality condition $\nabla a\cdot n=0$ , where $n$ is a normal vector. Finally, the damage variable and its gradient must be continuous inside the domain of integration in order to realise the minimum of the functional in question (see [bib2,4] for more details).

### 2.2 Constitutive relations

The link between the variational formulation and the usual evolution laws is direct. The state of the material is characterised by the strain $\varepsilon$ and the damage $a$ , lying between 0 and 1. One defines the stress-strain relation, which remains elastic, and the stiffness is affected by the damage:

$\sigma =\delta F(\varepsilon ,a)/\delta \varepsilon =A(a)E:\varepsilon$             eq 2.2-1

with $E$ the Hooke tensor. The evolution of the damage, always increasing, is governed by the following threshold function:

$f(\varepsilon ,a)=-\delta \Phi (\varepsilon ,a)/\delta a=-\frac{1}{2}A'(a)\varepsilon :E:\varepsilon -\omega '(a)+c\Delta a$         eq 2.2-2

The consistency condition then takes its usual form:

$f(\varepsilon ,a)\le 0\dot{a}\ge 0\dot{a}f(\varepsilon ,a)=0$         eq 2.2-3

One notes two particular features of this formulation. Firstly, the threshold function is non-local because of the presence of the Laplacian of the damage. Next, the absence of a flow condition is justified by the dual role of the damage $a$ : on the one hand it appears as an internal evolution variable, on the other hand it fulfils the role of the Lagrange parameter $\lambda \equiv a$ .

One also sees the advantage of presenting the damage laws in their variational form. It is sufficient to describe the total free-energy density (eq. 2.1-1), which includes the dissipation, in order to define the evolution law completely.

### 2.3 Identification of the parameters for the law ENDO_SCALAIRE

In the law ENDO_SCALAIRE the stiffness and dissipation functions are chosen as follows:

$\omega (a)=\mathrm{ka},A(a)={(\frac{1-a}{1+\gamma a})}^{2}$

The parameters of this constitutive law are then five in number. On the one hand, Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ , which determine the Hooke tensor by:

${E}^{-1}\cdot \sigma =\frac{1+\nu }{E}\sigma -\frac{\nu}{E}(\text{tr}\sigma )\text{Id}$             eq 2.2-1

On the other hand, $k,\gamma ,c$ , which define the softening constitutive behaviour, as well as the characteristic width of the damage band. The latter may be calibrated to the macroscopic parameters from the one-dimensional model, which admits a semi-analytical solution (bib 6,7). Denoting the peak stress by ${\sigma}_{y}$ , the Griffith fracture energy by ${G}_{f}$ and the size of the damaged zone at fracture by $D$ , one obtains:

$k=\frac{3{G}_{f}}{4D},c=\frac{3}{8}D{G}_{f},\gamma =\frac{3E{G}_{f}}{4{\sigma}_{y}^{2}D}-1$

The numerical tests have shown that, in order to avoid the presence of snap-back in the force-displacement response in 1D, one should have $\gamma \ge 2.8$ . For this reason the choice has been made to simplify the data entry of the model: one enters not the complete set of macroscopic parameters ${\sigma}_{y},{G}_{f},D$ , but directly the parameters of the model $\gamma ,c$ and the peak stress ${\sigma}_{y}$ , given under the factor keywords ENDO_SCALAIRE (GAMMA, SY) and NON_LOCAL (C_GRAD_VARI) of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU. As for $E$ and $\nu$ , they are given classically under the factor keyword ELAS or ELAS_FO. The reasoning which follows is strictly valid only for the 1D modelling, but may prove useful for inexperienced users. If the parameters $E,\nu ,{G}_{f},{\sigma}_{y}$ are defined a priori, the user may vary the parameter $D$ in order to satisfy the condition of absence of local snap-backs $\gamma \ge 2.8$ . He or she must subsequently ensure that the size of the system considered is greater than the width of the damage band $D$ .

| **Example of** **concrete in tension** | $\begin{array}{}E=30\text{GPa,}\nu =0.2\\ {G}_{f}=100\text{N/m}\\ {\sigma}_{y}=3\text{MPa}\end{array}$ | $\begin{array}{}\text{ELAS}(\text{E}=\mathrm{3e10,}\text{NU}=0.2)\\ \text{ENDO\_SCALAIRE}(\text{GAMMA}=1/(\mathrm{4D})-1,\text{SY}=3e6)\\ \text{NON\_LOCAL}(\text{C\_GRAD\_VARI}=\mathrm{37.5D})\end{array}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |

The width of the damage band is to be chosen while respecting $\gamma \ge 2.8$ <=> $D\le 66\text{mm}$

### 2.4 Integration of the constitutive law in the local case

We present here the method of integration of the law ENDO_SCALAIRE in its local version ($c=0$ ), so that the user may make a generalisation to the non-local case, which is generic and relies entirely on the algorithm presented in the document [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01).

The time discretisation of the equations [eq 2.2-1] to [eq 2.1-3] over a time step

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.25/Object_26.svg)

is carried out by an implicit Euler scheme. Integrating the constitutive law in time consists in determining the stress state and the damage state of the solution of the following non-linear system:

$\sigma =A(a)E:\varepsilon$                 eq 2.4-1

${f}_{\text{loc}}(\epsilon ,a)\le 0a-{a}^{-}\ge 0(a-{a}^{-})\cdot {f}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\epsilon ,a)=0$         eq 2.4-2

where the variables without indices correspond to the final time step $t$ , as for example the strain $\varepsilon$ ; the state of the material at the beginning of the time step $({\varepsilon}^{-},{a}^{-})$ is indicated by the index "-". The local threshold function is given by (eq. 2.2-2): ${f}_{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon ,a)=(1+\gamma )\frac{(1-a)}{{(1+\gamma a)}^{3}}\varepsilon :E:\varepsilon -k$

A solution method has been proposed by [bib3]. It begins by examining the solution without evolution of the damage (also called the elastic trial) then, if necessary, proceeds to a correction in order to check the consistency condition. In the present case, the existence and the uniqueness of the solution guarantee the proper functioning of the method. Consider the elastic trial:

$a={a}^{-}$ solution if ${f}^{\text{el}}(\varepsilon )\equiv {f}_{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon ,{a}^{-})\le 0$         eq 2.4-3

In the contrary case, the damage is obtained by solving ${f}_{\text{loc}}(\varepsilon ,a)=0$ (polynomial of order 3).

$(1-a)(1+\gamma )\varepsilon :E:\varepsilon /k={(1+\gamma a)}^{3}$     eq 2.4-4

It is the largest root which is chosen among the three which exist.

It remains to ensure that the damage does not exceed the value 1. In fact, when $a=1$ , the stiffness of the material point considered vanishes $A(1)=0$ . Insofar as no technique for removing the "broken" finite elements is implemented (a technique which is possibly delicate when the finite elements possess several Gauss points), zero pivots may appear in the stiffness matrix. This is why one introduces a numerical threshold of residual elastic stiffness for the tangent matrix, which may be entered under the factor keyword COEF_RIGI_MINI of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU. This dimensionless value is a multiplier coefficient of the elastic modulus of an isotropic linear model. In order to preserve a reasonable conditioning of the stiffness matrix, one chooses the default value $\minA(a)={10}^{-5}$ .

An indicator $\chi$ , stored in the second internal variable, then specifies the constitutive behaviour during the current time step:

### 2.5 Integration of the constitutive law in the non-local case

We present here only the method of integration of the law ENDO_SCALAIRE in its local version ($c=0$ ), because the generalisation to the non-local case is generic and relies entirely on the algorithm presented in the document [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01). One notes that for the non-local version the threshold function is shifted, and one therefore obtains a polynomial of order 4 to be solved. As for the stress, it is given by [eq 2.4-1] in every case.

### 2.6 Description of the internal variables

The internal variables are three in number:

- $\mathrm{VI}(1)$ damage $a$
- $\mathrm{VI}(2)$ indicator $\chi$
- $\mathrm{VI}(3)$ residual stiffness $1-A(a)$

One denotes by $\nabla a$ the spatial derivative of the damage field and by $\dot{a}$ that linked to the time evolution

$\delta F(\varepsilon ,a)/\delta \varepsilon$ is the partial variational derivative in the direction of the spatial field $\varepsilon (x)$ , the field $a(x)$ remaining fixed.

## 3 Piloting by elastic prediction

The standard piloting of type PRED_ELAS controls the intensity of the loading in order to satisfy a certain equation linked to the value of the threshold function ${f}^{\mathrm{el}}$ during the elastic trial [bib5]. Consequently, only the points where the damage is not saturated are taken into account. The algorithm which takes charge of this piloting mode, cf. [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80), requires the solution, at each of these Gauss points, of the following scalar equation in which $\Delta \tau$ is a datum and $\eta$ the unknown:

> ${f}^{\mathrm{el}}({\varepsilon}_{\text{impo}}+\eta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pilo}},{a}^{\text{\_}})=\Delta \tau$ **eq 3-1**

Note that this equation is modified for the PRED_ELAS piloting in ENDO_SCALAIRE so that the parameter $\Delta \tau$ corresponds to the damage increment which one seeks to obtain for at least one point of the structure. One then no longer seeks a piloting parameter $\eta$ which makes the criterion depart by a value $\Delta \tau$ with the damage coming from the preceding time step (cf. Eq 3-1), but a parameter $\eta$ which brings us back onto the criterion with a damage increased by $\Delta \tau$ :

${f}^{\mathrm{el}}({\varepsilon}_{\text{impo}}+\eta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pilo}},{a}^{\text{\_}})=\Delta \tau \text{}\Rightarrow \text{}{f}^{\mathrm{el}}({\varepsilon}_{\text{impo}}+\eta {\varepsilon}_{\text{pilo}},{a}^{\text{\_}}+\Delta \tau )=0$ **eq 3-2**
