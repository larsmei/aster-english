# v7.31.152 WTNV152 - Dispersion of water vapour in a gas mixture

**Summary:**

The problem is considered to be constant temperature and saturation, and permeability is negligible to a purely diffuse problem.
By appropriate boundary conditions, constant gas pressure and liquid water pressure are imposed by imposing a capillary pressure on one edge of the domain (no flows on the other side). Only the water vapour pressure is changing.
This problem is reduced to a water vapour pressure equation of the "heat equation" type. The reference solution will then be a thermal calculation ASTER.

**Model A:**

- Modellings D_PLAN_HH2MD, D_PLAN_THH2MD, _PLAN_HH2D, (modellings flat and flambed). Mechanical and thermal are blocked everywhere.
- Matching: LIQU_AD_GAZ_VAPE law

**Model B:**

- Modellings AXIS_HH2MD, AXIS_THH2MD, _AXIS_HH2D, (modellings axisymmetric and molten). Mechanical and thermal are blocked everywhere.
- Matching: LIQU_AD_GAZ_VAPE law

**Model C:**

- Modellings 3D_HH2®, 3D_THH2®, _3D_HH2D, (modellings 3D and flambed). Mechanical and thermal are blocked everywhere.
- Matching: LIQU_AD_GAZ_VAPE law

**Model D:**

- Modellings D_PLAN_HHMD, D_PLAN_THHMD, _PLAN_HHD, (modellings flat and Dumping)

The mechanics are stuck here everywhere.

- Matching: LIQU_VAPE_GAZ law

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

On the whole domain, we take it like boundary conditions:

- a liquid water pressure ${p}_{w} = {p^0}_{w}=cte$
- a gas pressure ${p}_{gz} ={p^0}_{gz}=cte$
- ${S}(P_{c}) = cte = 0.4$ sorption isotherm
- a temperature ${T} = {T^{0}}=cte = 300 K$
- ${\phi}=  1$ porosity (problem without mechanical deformation)

On all edges: hydraulic and thermal flows are zero.

On the left edge AD, a capillary pressure is imposed which corresponds to a steam pressure $\mathit{p}_{\text{v}}$ of 500 Pa. This capillary pressure is calculated by Kelvin's law:

$$
\mathit{P_{c}}=\frac{{{\rho}_{w}}RT}{{M}_{\text{v}}^{\text{mol}}}ln(\frac{{p}_{\text{v}}}{{p}_{\text{v,sat}}})
$$

With ${{M}_{\text{v}}^{\text{mol}}}$ the molar mass of the water vapour and $\mathit{p}_{\text{v,sat}}$ the saturated vapour pressure at 300 K (equal to 3450 Pa)

## 2 reference solution

**Calculation of water body conservation**

The conservation of the ${\mathit{m}}_{\text{eau}}$ water body writes:

$$
\frac{dm_{\text{eau}}}{dt} + Div (M_{w} + M_{}v) = 0
$$

With ${\mathit{M}}_{\text{w}}$ and ${\mathit{M}}_{\text{v}}$ the liquid water and water vapour flows respectively.

The total water mass is written to be equal to the water vapour mass (due to water incompressibility) and is obtained:

$$
dm_{\text{eau}} = (1-\mathit{S}){\phi}d{\rho}_{\text{v}} =(1-\mathit{S})\frac{{M}_{\text{v}}^{\text{ol}}}{RT}{\phi}{d{p}_{\text{v}}}
$$

Since permeability is negligible, we have:

$$
M_{w}=0
$$

As regards the flow of water vapour, it is written as:

$$
M_{v}= {{\rho}_{\text{v}}}{{\lambda}_{\text{gz}}}(-\nabla{{p}_{\text{gz}}})-{{\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}}{{\rho}_{\text{gz}}}{\nabla{\frac{{\rho}_{\text{v}}}{{\rho}_{\text{gz}}}}}
$$

With ${\lambda}_{\text{gz}}$ gas conductivity, ${\rho}_{\text{gz}}$ gas density, ${\rho}_{\text{v}}$ water vapour density and ${\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}$ Fick diffusion coefficient for water vapour

Since the gas pressure (and its density because the two components have the same molar mass) is constant, the equation can be written as:

$$
M_{v}= -{{\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}}{\nabla{{\rho}_{\text{v}}}} = -{{\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}}\frac{{M}_{\text{v}}^{\text{ol}}}{RT}(\nabla{{p}_{\text{v}}})
$$

The equation can then be written in the form:

$$
(1-\mathit{S}){\phi}\frac{d{p}_{\text{v}}}{dt}= {{\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}}(\nabla{{p}_{\text{v}}})
$$

The equation can then be simplified and written as follows:

$$
{C}\frac{d{p}_{\text{v}}}{dt}=  {L}(\nabla{{p}_{\text{v}}})
$$

Heat equation which is treated by a thermal calculation Aster with $C = (1-\mathit{S}){\phi} = 0.6$ (with ${\phi} = 1$ and ${\mathit{S}} = 0.4$)
et $L={{\mathit{F}}_{\text{v}}}= FV = 10^{-12}{m}^{2}.{s}^{-1}$
