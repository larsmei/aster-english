# u2.11.01 User's guide to 1D–3D connections

**Summary:**

This guide aims to help the user in the choice of a Beam–3D connection in dynamics in Code_Aster.

The connection methodologies proposed are of two types:

- Non-intrusive surface connection called 1D–3D switchover (option 3D_POU of LIAISON_ELEM)
- Intrusive volume connection within the Arlequin framework (option 3D_POU_ARLEQUIN of LIAISON_ELEM)

## 1 Introduction

For the analysis of effects localised in space and in time, a beam model makes it possible to save analysis time in the absence of non-linear effects, and a mixed beam–3D model makes it possible to take into account the non-linear effects limited in space.

## 2 Non-intrusive 1D–3D switchover

A switchover from a beam model to a mixed beam–3D model makes it possible to save considerably in analysis time for an accuracy equivalent to a full 3D model [bib2].

![Figure](/figures/u2.11.01/10000200000003E9000001631D5D91C74FB7804B.png)

**Image 1.1-a weighting functions in the Arlequin framework**

*Code_Aster* makes it possible to initialise a dynamic analysis with non-zero displacement, velocity and acceleration fields (ETAT_INIT in the dynamic analysis operators). These initialisation fields can be computed beforehand, then transformed into a field in *Code_Aster*.

### 2.1 Theoretical principle of the switchover

The switchover consists in passing from a Beam model (**Up** is the beam solution of the system ${M}_{p}\ddot{{U}_{p}}+{C}_{p}\dot{{U}_{p}}+{K}_{p}{U}_{p}={f}_{p}$) to a 3D model (solution **U3D** to be initialised) by means of a relevant initialisation of the 3D solution. The basic principle is the following:

1. from the beam solution **Up**, create a 3D solution **PUp** for the rigid-section assumption
2. write the 3D displacement field as being the sum of the extruded field of Beam type **Pup** and of a corrective displacement field denoted **U3Dc**, taking into account the deformation of the section**:**

${U}_{\mathrm{3D}}={\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}$

1. The dynamics of the 3D model will then be written as follows:

${M}_{\mathrm{3D}}(\ddot{{\mathit{PU}}_{p}}+\ddot{{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}})+{C}_{\mathrm{3D}}(\dot{{\mathit{PU}}_{p}}+\dot{{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}})+{K}_{\mathrm{3D}}({\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}})={f}_{\mathrm{3D}}$

1. correct the displacements, **in statics**, under the effect of a force vector f**3Dc** computed as follows:

${f}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}={K}_{\mathrm{3D}}{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}={f}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}(t={t}_{b})-{M}_{\mathrm{3D}}\ddot{{\mathit{PU}}_{p}}-{C}_{\mathrm{3D}}\dot{{\mathit{PU}}_{p}}-{K}_{\mathrm{3D}}{\mathit{PU}}_{p}$

The static correction is carried out over 3 successive time steps: the switchover time instant tb as well as the one before (tb-1 = tb- Δt) and the one after (tb+1 = tb+ Δt) where Δt is the time step of the analysis. One deduces from this a velocity correction which is written as follows:

$\dot{{U}_{\mathrm{3D}}}=\frac{{\left[{\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}\right]}_{({t}_{b+1})}-{\left[{\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}\right]}_{({t}_{b-1})}}{2\Delta t}$ (Centred finite differences)

The accelerations, for their part, are either computed in the same way according to a scheme of Centred finite differences type,

$\ddot{{U}_{\mathrm{3D}}}=\frac{{\left[{\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}\right]}_{({t}_{b-1})}+{\left[{\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}\right]}_{({t}_{b+1})}-2{\left[{\mathit{PU}}_{p}+{U}_{\mathrm{3Dc}}\right]}_{({t}_{b})}}{{\Delta t}^{2}}$

or determined automatically by *Code_Aster* in the case of the use of an implicit time-integration scheme.

The correction comprises a correction of the deflection as well as a correction in order to take into account the deformation of the section.

## 3 3D–Beam connection within the Arlequin framework

The Arlequin framework (option 3D_POU_ARLEQUIN of the keyword LIAISON_ELEM of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA) is a flexible method for connecting 1D and 3D models with overlap [bib2]. It therefore also makes it possible to save considerably in analysis time for an accuracy equivalent to a full 3D model.

### 3.1 Main Arlequin ingredients

The Arlequin method rests on the following principles:

- The connection of the sub-domains by means of a weak formulation: the introduction of the Lagrange multipliers in the gluing zone guarantees the coupling of the models, the continuity of the kinematic quantities, as well as the control of the discrepancies of the stresses and of the strains between the coupled zones;
- The distribution of the energy between domains and models: in order not to count twice the energy of the global system in the overlap zone, the virtual works associated with the two models are distributed between the coupled sub-domains through the gluing zone by means of weighting functions $(\gamma ,1-\gamma )$ which form a partition of unity (the sum of the two functions is equal to 1) over the whole of the domain of the study.

The weighting makes it possible to avoid taking the energy into account several times in the same zone and thus allows the user a certain freedom in the choice of the predominant model. Indeed, it makes it possible to put the weight on the model that one wishes to have express itself.

The coupling matrices obtained are transformed into kinematic relations. This connection is therefore expressed by linear relations linking the displacements of the set of 3D nodes (3 degrees of freedom per node) linked with the set of the beam nodes (6 degrees of freedom per node).

### 3.2 Example of implementation

It is recalled that the Arlequin 3D–Beam connection, as developed today in Code_Aster, must satisfy a very important requirement. Indeed, the 1D and 3D meshes must be “hierarchically compatible”, in the sense that all the 3D elements are included in the cylindrical space of the facing 1D element (no 3D element straddling two beam elements).

The mesh associated with the mixed 1D–3D model is read by LIRE_MAILLAGE:
