# u3.13.07 Modellings AXIS_INCO_UPG, D_PLAN_INCO_UPG

**Summary:**

This document describes, for the modellings AXIS_INCO_UPG, D_PLAN_INCO_UPG:

- the degrees of freedom carried by the finite elements which support the modelling,
- the associated support mesh cells,
- the supported loadings,
- the non-linear capabilities,
- the test cases implementing the modellings.

This modelling is based on finite elements adapted to the treatment of quasi-incompressible problems. It is indispensable in order to carry out limit analyses with the Norton-Hoff law and is also useful for studies presenting large plastic strains for which the classical displacement formulation proves insufficient (stress oscillation). The formulation used is a 3-field formulation: displacement-pressure-swelling [R6.03.05](doc:r6.03.05), usable with all constitutive behaviours written in incremental form. The modellings are supported by plane mesh cells of degree 2 (TRIA6 and QUAD8).

## 1 Discretisation

### 1.1 Degrees of freedom

| **Modelling** | **Degrees of freedom at all the nodes** | **Degrees of freedom only at the vertex nodes** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| D_PLAN_INCO_UPG | DX, DY | PRES(*), GONF |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AXIS_INCO_UPG | DX, DY | PRES(*), GONF |

- no kinematic condition can be imposed on the degree of freedom PRES.

### 1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness matrices

The support mesh cells of the finite elements may be triangles, or quadrangles (degree 2), for the modellings D_PLAN_INCO_UPG and AXIS_INCO_UPG.

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Interpolation** **in displacement** | **Interpolation** **in pressure and swelling** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| D_PLAN_INCO_UPG | TRIA6 | Quadratic | Linear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | QUAD8 | Quadratic | Linear |
| AXIS_INCO_UPG | TRIA6 | Quadratic | Linear |
|  | QUAD8 | Quadratic | Linear |

### 1.3 Support mesh cell of the surface loadings

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Interpolation in displacements** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| D_PLAN_INCO_UPG and AXIS_INCO_UPG | SEG3 | Quadratic |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 2 Supported loadings

The available loadings are the following:

- **'CONTACT'**

Makes it possible to define the zones subjected to contact conditions.

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

- **'FORCE_CONTOUR'**

Makes it possible to apply line forces on the edge of a 2D domain.

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

- **'FORCE_INTERNE'**

Makes it possible to apply volume forces.

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

- **'PESANTEUR'**

Makes it possible to apply a gravity-type loading.

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

- **'PRES_REP'**

Makes it possible to apply a pressure to a continuum domain.

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

## 3 Non-linear capabilities

Note that this modelling is accessible only from STAT_NON_LINE. It cannot be used with MECA_STATIQUE or by a manual assembly.

### 3.1 Constitutive laws

All the constitutive laws usable on continuum mesh cells have a physical meaning for these modellings and can be assigned provided that they are accessible from COMPORTEMENT in STAT_NON_LINE (see [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)).

One constitutive law is specific to this modelling (dedicated to the limit-load analysis, cf. [R7.07.01]):

```text
/    'NORTON_HOFF'
```

Supported modellings: D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, AXIS_INCO_UPG

### 3.2 Strains

The available strains, used in the constitutive relations under the keyword DEFORMATION for the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE, are (see [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

```text
/    'PETIT'
```

The strains used for the constitutive relation are the linearised strains.

```text
/    'SIMO_MIEHE'

/     'GDEF_LOG'
```

Makes it possible to carry out analyses in large plastic strains.

### 3.3 Newton method

For the solution of the problem by the Newton-Raphson method, the elastic matrix is not available. One must therefore use, under the keyword NEWTON for the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE (see [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

```text
/    PREDICTION = ‘TANGENTE’
```

The prediction phase is carried out with the tangent matrix.

```text
/    MATRICE = ‘TANGENTE’
```

The matrix used for the global iterations is the tangent matrix.

**Note:**

The formulation used leads to non-positive matrices and the current solvers cannot always solve the linear systems associated with them satisfactorily. In the event of a convergence difficulty, it may therefore be useful to test the other solvers available in the code or the other renumbering methods (cf. [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01)).
