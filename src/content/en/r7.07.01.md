# r7.07.01 Calculation of the limit load by the Norton-Hoff-Friaâ method, constitutive behaviour NORTON_HOFF

**Summary:**

Limit analysis makes it possible to determine the admissible loadings of a structure, of given fixed geometry, constituted of a material having a strength criterion. One considers the case of loadings constituted of the sum of a permanent load and of another parameterised by the load factor, whose extreme supportable value one seeks.

After a reminder of the theoretical formulation, one presents the regularised kinematic approach applied to the Von Mises strength criterion (Norton-Hoff-Friaâ method) and implemented in *Code_Aster* . One may refer to [bib4] for the various possible regularisation methods proposed in the literature. One then sets out the calculation of the solutions of this non-linear problem and the post-processing providing an estimate of the limit load (a value from above in every case, and when there is no permanent loading, a value from below).

> **Contents**

## 1 Theoretical formulation of limit analysis

### 1.1 Definition of the limit load

One considers a solid occupying a bounded domain $\Omega$  subjected to surface loadings $\lambda F+{F}_{0}$ on the boundary ${\Gamma}_{f}$  and to volume loadings $\lambda f+{f}_{0}$ on $\Omega$ . One distinguishes the loading $(F,f)$ , parameterised by the positive real $\lambda$ , and the “permanent” loading $({F}_{0},{f}_{0})$ . The homogeneous Dirichlet conditions which are applied on the complementary boundary ${\Gamma}_{u}$ of $\partial \Omega$  (an imposed displacement or an initial anelastic strain – thermal, plastic… – have no effect on the domain of the admissible loads). One may find in [bib5] several other useful properties.

The constitutive material of the solid possesses a strength criterion expressed by a scalar function of the stresses, negative for the admissible stresses. The criterion used for a material of the perfect elastoplastic type with a von Mises threshold and retained here is:

$g(\sigma )=J(\sigma )-{\sigma}_{y}=\sqrt{}.\sqrt{{\sigma}^{D}.{\sigma}^{D}}-{\sigma}_{y}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}.\sqrt{{({\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{2})}^{2}+{({\sigma}_{2}-{\sigma}_{3})}^{2}+{({\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3})}^{2}}-{\sigma}_{y}$

| ${\sigma}^{D}$ | is the deviator of the stress tensor, |
| --- | --- |
| ${\sigma}_{y}$ | is the strength threshold in simple tension (like a yield limit), possibly variable according to the zones of the solid considered. |
| ${\sigma}_{i}$ | being the principal stresses of $\sigma$ . |

Given this strength criterion one seeks to calculate the limit value of $\lambda$ , called the limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ , for which the structure can support the loadings ${\lambda}_{\lim}F+{F}_{0}$ and ${\lambda}_{\lim}f+{f}_{0}$ .

Strictly speaking, the value ${\lambda}_{\lim}$  denotes the limit of the supportable loadings, but for the materials obeying the Principle of Maximum Plastic Work, this value is the limit of the supported loadings.

### 1.2 Calculation of the limit load by a kinematic approach

In yield design two approaches are possible: the static approach (in stress variables) and the kinematic approach (in velocity variables). These approaches provide bounds of the limit load: a lower bound for the static approach and an upper bound for the kinematic approach. When the two provide the same result, the limit load obtained is exact.

The kinematic approach is the one used in *Code_Aster*  by means of displacement finite elements *.* For the given loading

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_21.svg)

, one defines the space of the kinematically admissible and normalised velocities by:

${V}_{a}^{1}=\left\lbrace v\text{admissible,}v=0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{u},L(v)={\int}_{\Omega}f.vd\Omega +{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{f}}F.v\text{ds}=1\right\rbrace$

This normalisation forces the work of the loading

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_23.svg)

to be unity. The power of the “permanent” loading $({F}_{0},{f}_{0})$ is denoted: ${L}_{0}(v)$ .

From the strength criterion in stresses $g(\sigma )$ , one defines:

- the set of admissible stresses by: ${G}_{(x)}=\left\lbrace \sigma (x),g(\sigma (x))\le 0\text{}\right\rbrace$

(${G}_{(x)}$  is convex for the criterion $g$ )

- the indicator function: ${\Psi}_{G}(\sigma (x))=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0,\text{si}\sigma (x)\in {G}_{(x)}\\ +\infty ,\text{si}\sigma (x)\notin {G}_{(x)}\end{array}$
- the support function: $\pi (\varepsilon )=\underset{\sigma \in {\mathrm{IR}}^{6}}{\text{Sup}}\left[\sigma .\varepsilon -{\Psi}_{G}(\sigma )\right]$

The Sup in $\pi (\varepsilon )$ can be attained only if $\sigma$  is chosen in ${G}_{(x)}$ , such that: $\sigma =\lambda {\varepsilon}^{D}+\mu \text{Id}$ (which ensures $\sigma \text{//}{\varepsilon}^{D}$ ). The optimum corresponds to $g(\bar{\sigma})=0\Rightarrow \bar{\lambda}={\sigma}_{y}\sqrt{}.{({\varepsilon}^{D}.{\varepsilon}^{D})}^{-1/2}$

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_38.svg)

Figure 1.2-a: Optimum $\bar{\sigma}$  and graph of the function $\pi (\varepsilon )$ in 1D

Hence the support function:

$\pi (\varepsilon (v))={\sigma}_{y}.\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{\epsilon (v).\epsilon (v)}+\underset{\mu \in \text{IR}}{\text{Sup}}(\mu .divv)={\pi}_{R}(\varepsilon (v))+\underset{\mu \in \text{IR}}{\text{Sup}}(\mu .divv)$

One observes that the function $\pi (\varepsilon )$ , which is interpreted as the density of dissipatable power at the material point, is not differentiable at $0$ .

One does not treat to date in *Code_Aster*  the possible internal surfaces of discontinuity within the solid $\Omega$  [bib 4].

The kinematic approach is defined by means of the convex functional ${S}_{e}(v)$ , positively homogeneous of degree one, for $v\in {V}_{a}^{1}$ defined on the whole domain:

$\begin{array}{cc}{S}_{e}(v)& ={\int}_{\Omega}\pi (\varepsilon (v))d\Omega \end{array}-{L}_{0}(v)$

This functional is the integral over the domain of the support function $\pi$  of the convex ${G}_{(x)}$ , calculated at $\varepsilon (v)$  and is interpreted as the maximum resisting power in the velocity field

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_51.svg)

(the contribution of interface resistance on discontinuity surfaces is assumed zero). The support function $\pi$  is positively homogeneous of degree 1, and therefore the functional ${S}_{e}(v)$  is too as a consequence.

With the Von Mises criterion the power functional ${S}_{e}(v)$ is:

${S}_{e}(v)={\int}_{\Omega}\left[{\sigma}_{y}.\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\sqrt{\varepsilon (v).\varepsilon (v)}+\underset{q\in \text{IR}}{\text{Sup}}(q.divv)\right]d\Omega -{\text{L}}_{0}(v)$                 eq 1.2-1

where one observes that only the fields $v$  belonging to $C=\left\lbrace v\in {V}_{a}^{1},divv=0\text{dans}\Omega \right\rbrace$  provide finite values. The fields $v$  must therefore satisfy the so-called incompressibility condition $divv=\text{tr}\varepsilon (v)=0$ . This is why one must use the quasi-incompressible elements for a limit-load calculation with the Von Mises criterion [R3.06.08](doc:r3.06.08).

The limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ given by the kinematic approach is:

${\lambda}_{\lim}=\underset{\mathrm{v}\in {V}_{a}^{1}}{\text{Inf}}{S}_{e}\left(\mathrm{v}\right)=\underset{\begin{array}{c}\mathrm{v}\in {V}_{a}^{1}\\ L\left(v\right)>0\end{array}}{\text{Inf}}\frac{{S}_{e}\left(v\right)}{L\left(v\right)}=\underset{l>0}{\text{Sup}}\underset{\mathrm{v}\in {V}_{a}}{\text{Inf}}\left({S}_{e}\left(\mathrm{v}\right)-l\left(L\left(\mathrm{v}\right)-1\right)\right)$

where $l$ is the Lagrange multiplier.

At the optimum one obtains a solution $u$ and the limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ (non-uniqueness of $u$ but uniqueness of ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ ). Thus, every loading ${L}_{0}(v)+\lambda L(v)$  with $0\le \lambda \le {\lambda}_{\lim}$  is supportable. Beyond ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ , the equilibrium problem has no solution.

**Note:**

*There exist situations where, even if* ${L}_{0}(v)$ *is not supportable alone, the combination* ${L}_{0}(v)+\lambda L(v)$ *, for* ${\lambda}_{1}\le \lambda \le {\lambda}_{2}$ *, becomes so over a certain interval, and not only for two collinear loadings.*

**Note:**

*The limit load calculated for a two-dimensional problem, in plane strain, is necessarily greater than that obtained for this problem modelled in plane stress. This result therefore provides an upper bound. If one wishes to treat a problem in plane stress, it is then necessary to carry out the kinematic approach on a three-dimensional modelling.*

### 1.3 Regularisation of the kinematic approach by the Norton-Hoff-Friaâ method

The numerical implementation of the kinematic approach requires the minimisation of the non-differentiable functional ${S}_{e}(v)$ . Numerous regularisation techniques exist [bib4]. The Norton-Hoff-Friâa method is used here [bib2], [bib7]. It rests on precursor work by Casciaro in 1971. It consists in replacing the support function $\pi (\varepsilon )$  by the regularised and differentiable support function ${\pi}^{\mathrm{NH}}(\varepsilon )$ . It is adjustable by a regularisation parameter $m$  ($1\le m\le 2$ ), whose limit value $m\to {1}^{+}$ leads to convergence towards the support function $\pi (\varepsilon )$ :

${\pi}^{\text{NH}}(\varepsilon )=\frac{{k}^{1-m}}{m}{(\pi (\varepsilon ))}^{m}$                             eq 1.3-1

The scalar $k$ in [eq 1.3-1] is homogeneous to a stress. One denotes the space of admissible velocities adapted to the viscous flow problem for the Norton-Hoff law of order $m$ :

${V}_{a}^{\mathrm{m1}}=\left\lbrace v\in {L}^{m}(\Omega ),\text{et}\varepsilon (v)\in {L}^{m}(\Omega ),v=0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{u},L(v)=1\right\rbrace$

One defines on this space the regularised functional ${S}_{e}^{m}(v)$ :

${S}_{e}^{m}(v)={\int}_{\Omega}\frac{{k}^{1-m}}{m}\pi {(\varepsilon (v))}^{m}d\Omega -{L}_{0}(v)$

The minimisation problem $\underset{v\in {V}_{a}^{\mathrm{m1}}}{\text{Inf}}\left[{S}_{e}^{m}(v)\right]$ is well posed thanks to the properties of the spaces ${L}^{m}(O)$  and admits a unique solution ${u}_{m}$ , for which the value attained by the

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_88.svg)

is precisely ${\lambda}_{m}$ . One denotes the space of the incompressible fields of ${V}_{a}^{m1}$ by:

$\tilde{{V}_{a}^{m1}}=\left\lbrace v\in {V}_{a}^{m1}\text{tel}\text{que}divv=0\right\rbrace$

One then shows that this problem can also be written in the form of the search for the saddle-point $({\lambda}_{m},{u}_{m})$  of the following Lagrangian:

$\underset{l\in \text{IR}}{\text{Max}}\left[\underset{\mathrm{v}\in \tilde{{V}_{a}^{m}}}{\text{Inf}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\left\lbrace {\int}_{\Omega}\frac{A\left(m\right)}{m}.{\left(\sqrt{\epsilon (\mathrm{v}).\epsilon (\mathrm{v})}\right)}^{m}d\Omega -{L}_{0}(\mathrm{v})-l\left(L(\mathrm{v})-1\right)\right\rbrace \right]$         eq 1.3-2

with:    $A(m)={k}^{1-m}{(\frac{2}{3})}^{m/2}.{\sigma}_{y}^{m}={\sigma}_{y}^{2-m}.{(3\mu )}^{m-1}.{(\frac{2}{3})}^{m/2}$ .

One notes that $A(m)$  is increasing with $m$ (if $E\ge {\sigma}_{y}$ , which is the case in practice) and homogeneous to a stress, and remains bounded when $m\to {1}^{+}$ . If one chooses $k={\sigma}_{y}$ , then $A(m)={\sigma}_{y}{(\frac{2}{3})}^{m/2}$ and one recovers the incompressible elastic problem when $m=2$ , if one chooses a Young's modulus $E={\sigma}_{y}$ .

One therefore observes that this potential [eq 1.3-2] defines a constitutive law giving the stress tensor $\sigma (u)$ by the Norton-Hoff constitutive relation, see [§2.1].

One thus constructs a decreasing sequence of ${\lambda}_{m}$  and the limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ is the limit of this sequence when $m\to {1}^{+}$ (that is $n\to +\infty$ ):

${\lambda}_{\lim}=\underset{m\to 1}{\lim}(\underset{v\in {V}_{a}^{\mathrm{m1}}}{\text{Inf}}\left[{S}_{e}^{m}(v)\right])=\underset{m\to 1}{\lim}({S}_{e}^{m}({u}_{m}))$                     eq 1.3-3

For the proof one will refer to [bib4] and [bib7].

**Note:**

*If one amplifies the intensity of the loading* $L\to \beta L$ *(whereas one does not consider a permanent loading* ${L}_{0}=0$ *), the solutions depend on the factor* $\beta$ *according to the following relations:*

${u}_{m}(\beta )={\beta}^{-1}{u}_{m}(1);{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}(\beta ))={\beta}^{1-m}{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}(1));{S}_{e}^{m}({u}_{m}(\beta ))={\beta}^{-m}{S}_{e}^{m}({u}_{m}(1))$ *.*

*At convergence for* $m\to {1}^{+}$ *, the conclusion given by the solution* ${u}_{m}(\beta )$ *is indeed the same as that given by* ${u}_{m}(1)$ , *since* ${\lambda}_{\lim}(\beta )={\lambda}_{\lim}(1)/\beta$ *.*

## 2 Numerical aspects of the calculation of the limit load

### 2.1 Norton-Hoff constitutive relation

The stress tensor $\sigma (u)$ satisfies the Norton-Hoff constitutive relation. The stress deviator associated with the strain rate is:

${\sigma}^{D}\left(\mathrm{u}\right)=A\left(m\right).{\left(\sqrt{{\epsilon}^{D}\left(\mathrm{u}\right).{\epsilon}^{D}\left(\mathrm{u}\right)}\right)}^{m-2}.{\epsilon}^{D}\left(\mathrm{u}\right)$     eq 2.1-1

with $\text{tr}\varepsilon (u)=0$ , and: $A(m)={k}^{1-m}{(\frac{2}{3})}^{m/2}{\sigma}_{y}^{m}$ .

This behaviour is integrated in the same way as the incremental elastoplastic Von Mises behaviours [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02). Let us note however that, at an integration point, the calculation of the stress tensor as a function of the strain tensor is explicit, no iterative scheme being used. Moreover, no internal variable is necessary for the integration of this behaviour.

In *Code_Aster* , the calculation of the limit load being independent of the elasticity coefficients *,*  one chooses $k={\sigma}_{y}$ , whence $A(m)={\sigma}_{y}{(\frac{2}{3})}^{m/2}$ . One thus recovers the incompressible elastic problem when $m=2$ , for a Young's modulus $E={\sigma}_{y}$ .

Moreover, the sequence of the scalars $m$ is deduced directly from the list of (fictitious) calculation time instants by:

$m=1+{10}^{1-t}$ ,

so that when the time instant increases, $m$ tends towards 1, and the behaviour approaches a perfect rigid-plastic behaviour, see in one dimension the curves [fig. 2.1-a]. In practice, one chooses the sequence of the values of [tab. 2.1-a].

### 2.2 Piloting

The problem is written in variational form in the following way on the space of incompressible fields.

For a given $m$ , therefore at a given time instant $t$ , knowing the solution at the previous time instant (denoted ${u}^{-},{\lambda}^{-}$ ), find $(\Delta \lambda ,\Delta u)\in \mathrm{IR}\times \tilde{{V}_{a}}$ such that:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{\int}_{\Omega}\sigma ({u}^{-}+\Delta u).\varepsilon (v)d\Omega ={L}_{0}(v)+({\lambda}^{-}+\Delta \lambda )L(v)\forall v\in {\tilde{}V}_{0}\\ L(v)={\int}_{\Omega}f.vd\Omega +{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{f}}F.v\text{dS}=1\end{array}$         eq 2.2-1

- ${L}_{0}$ is the permanent loading and $L$  the loading piloted by the parameter $\lambda$ , cf. [§1.1],
- $\tilde{{V}_{0}}$ is a space of functions discretised on the basis of incompressible finite elements, and is therefore defined by a vector $(U)$  of degrees of freedom.

This problem admits a unique solution for every $1\le m\le 2$ (see [bib4]). For $m=2$ the problem is of incompressible linear elasticity type.

The discretised problem at the time instant $t$ (therefore for a value of $m$ , cf. [tab. 2.1-a]) may be written (omitting the boundary conditions for simplicity):

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{F}_{int}(\Delta U;{U}^{-};..)={F}_{0}^{\text{ext}}+\lambda {F}^{\text{ext}}\\ L({U}^{-}+\Delta U)=1\end{array}$

The search for $\lambda$ ensuring the condition $L(U)=1$ is ensured by a piloting algorithm [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80).

Briefly, the principle is the following: by linearisation of the equations bearing on the internal forces, one obtains, for iteration $n$ of Newton's algorithm, cf. [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01):

$\underset{{K}_{T}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left[\frac{\partial {F}_{int}}{\partial U}(\Delta {U}^{n})\right]}}\left[\delta U\right]=\underset{{R}^{\text{cst}}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left[{F}_{0}^{\text{ext}}-{F}_{int}(\Delta {U}^{n})\right]}}+\lambda \underset{{R}^{\text{pilo}}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left[{F}^{\text{ext}}\right]}}$                     eq 2.2-2

One can now express the corrections of displacements $\delta U$ and of Lagrange multipliers $\delta \lambda$ as a function of $\lambda$ by means of the resolution of this linear system:

$\left[\delta U\right]=\left[\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}\right]+\lambda \left[\delta {U}^{\text{pilo}}\right]\text{où}\left[\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}\right]={K}_{{T}^{-1}}{R}^{\text{cst}}\text{et}\left[\delta {U}^{\text{pilo}}\right]={K}_{{T}^{-1}}{R}^{\text{pilo}}$         eq 2.2-3

One can substitute the displacement correction $\delta U$ as a function of its expression [eq 2.2-2] into the control equation of the piloting of the system $L(U)=1$ ; there results a scalar equation in $\Delta \lambda$ :

$L({U}^{-}+\Delta {U}^{n}+\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}+\lambda \delta {U}^{\text{pilo}})=1$         that is

${\int}_{\Omega}f.({U}^{-}+\Delta {U}^{n}+\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}+\lambda \delta {U}^{\text{pilo}})d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{f}}F.({U}^{-}+\Delta {U}^{n}+\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}+\lambda \delta {U}^{\text{pilo}})\text{dS}=1$         eq 2.2-4

which in discretised form amounts to:

$\begin{array}{}\\ \sum_{e}{F}^{\text{ext}}.({U}^{-}+\Delta {U}^{n}+\delta {U}^{\text{cst}}+\lambda \delta {U}^{\text{pilo}})=1\end{array}$

which leads to:

$\lambda =\frac{1-\sum_{e}{F}^{\text{ext}}.({U}^{-}+\Delta {U}^{n}+\delta {U}^{\text{cst}})}{\sum_{e}{F}^{\text{ext}}.\delta {U}^{\text{pilo}}}$                         eq 2.2-5

### 2.3 Post-processing of the calculation of the limit load

Having obtained the solution $({\lambda}_{m},{u}_{m})$ , for each time instant, therefore each given $m$ , it remains to use the sequence of the ${\lambda}_{m}$  in order to construct the approximation of the limit load. For this one exploits the properties [eq 1.3-2], [eq 1.3-3], the fact that $A(m)$ is increasing and the property resulting from the minimisation [eq 1.3-2] (see [bib7]).

From these last two, with $1\le r\le s$ , one deduces that for ${u}_{r}$ and ${u}_{s}$ respective solutions (also satisfying the incompressibility and normalisation condition) of [eq 1.3-2] for $m=r$ and

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_175.svg)

:

$({\int}_{\Omega}A(r){(\varepsilon ({u}_{r}).\varepsilon ({u}_{r}))}^{r/2}d\Omega )\le ({\int}_{\Omega}A(s){(\varepsilon ({u}_{s}).\varepsilon ({u}_{s}))}^{s/2}d\Omega )$

Associated with the property [eq 1.3-3], one draws for $1\le r\le s$ , denoting ${\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{r}={\int}_{\Omega}A(r)d\Omega$ :

${\int}_{\Omega}A(r)\sqrt{\varepsilon ({u}_{r}).\varepsilon ({u}_{r})}d\Omega \le {\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{r}^{1-\frac{1}{r}}{({\int}_{\Omega}A(r){(\varepsilon ({u}_{r}).\varepsilon ({u}_{r}))}^{r/2}d\Omega )}^{\frac{1}{r}}\le {\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{s}^{1-\frac{1}{s}}{({\int}_{\Omega}A(s){(\varepsilon ({u}_{s}).\varepsilon ({u}_{s}))}^{s/2}d\Omega )}^{\frac{1}{s}}$ eq 2.3-1

Indeed, this property results from the inclusion of the functional spaces ${L}^{r}\supset {L}^{s}$ , for $1\le r\le s$ , that is also, where $h(x)$ plays the role of a variable measure (conditioned by the strength limit ${\sigma}_{y}$ ):

${\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{r}^{-\frac{1}{r}}{({\int}_{\Omega}h(x){\mid f(x)\mid }^{r}d\Omega )}^{\frac{1}{r}}\le {\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{s}^{-\frac{1}{s}}{({\int}_{\Omega}h(x){\mid f(x)\mid }^{s}d\Omega )}^{\frac{1}{s}}$

One denotes the terms ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$ of the sequence below, which one calculates in practice by post-processing by means of ${u}_{m}$  (the external power being unity:

${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}={\parallel \Omega \parallel }_{m}^{1-\frac{1}{m}}{({\int}_{\Omega}A(m){(\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m}))}^{m/2}d\Omega )}^{\frac{1}{m}}\text{}-{L}_{0}({u}_{m})$                     eq 2.3-2

This sequence ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$ is therefore decreasing for $m\to {1}^{+}$ and one shows [bib7] that it converges towards, which ${\lambda}_{\lim}$  allows a good control. As one can bound from below (knowing that

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_190.svg)

) the first term of [eq 2.3-1]:

${\lambda}_{\lim}\le {\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}_{y}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m})}d\Omega \text{}-{L}_{0}({u}_{m})\le {\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$

one therefore calculates for each value of

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_192.svg)

(therefore of the time instant $t$ ) the limit load ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$ from above, also converging towards ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ :

${\lambda}_{\lim}\le {\hat{\lambda}}_{m}={\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}_{y}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m})}d\Omega \text{}-{L}_{0}({u}_{m})$                 eq 2.3-3

One judges the quality of the approximation of the limit load ${\lambda}_{\lim}$ by comparison of the different values of ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$ which converge towards ${\lambda}_{\lim}$  from above (as $m\to {1}^{+}$ ). These terms are calculated by numerical integration at the Gauss points of the finite elements.

Another interpretation of the interest that this sequence brings lies in the fact that it exploits directly the expression of the support function of the strength convex, that is the dissipatable power in the potential collapse modes, applied to the calculated incompressible and normalised solutions ${u}_{m}$ .

In the case where the permanent loading is zero: ${L}_{0}=0$ , one can easily exploit the stress field (almost statically admissible) calculated with the solution ${u}_{m}$  and obtain a value by estimation of the limit load, which would necessarily be a true lower bound if equilibrium were satisfied exactly (see [bib4]).

One therefore calculates the sequence ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ , which by contrast has no monotonicity properties:

${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}={\int}_{\Omega}\frac{A(m)}{m}.{(\sqrt{\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m})})}^{m}d\Omega .{(\underset{x\in \Omega }{\text{Sup}}(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}).{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m})}}{{\sigma}_{y}}))}^{-1}\le {\hat{\lambda}}_{m}$         eq 2.3-4

This maximisation (of the function called the gauge of the strength convex) is calculated only at the Gauss points of the finite elements. Thus the value obtained, for each

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_205.svg)

, lower than ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$  [bib4], can be considered only as an indication.

By contrast, still in the case where the permanent loads are zero, it makes it possible, with the value from above ${\tilde{\lambda}}_{m}$ , to provide a bracketing of the limit load of the discretised problem.

## 3 Functionalities and verification

In order to carry out a calculation in *Code_Aster*  in limit analysis with the Norton-Hoff-Friaâ regularisation method with the Von Mises strength criterion, one must:

- define the 2D (plane or axisymmetric) or 3D model with the quasi-incompressible finite elements, modellings 3D_INCO_UPG, D_PLAN_INCO_UPG, or AXIS_INCO_UPG;
- ensure the incompressibility condition: GONF=0 in AFFE_CHAR_MECA;
- define only the material characteristic ${s}_{y}$ , the limit load being independent of

![Figure](/figures/r7.07.01/Object_209.svg)

and $\nu$ ,

- define the permanent loading and the one which is parameterised by $\lambda$ ;
- define the discretisation in time, (in practice between ${t}_{min}=1$ and ${t}_{max}=2$ to $5$ );
- carry out a non-linear calculation with the constitutive relation NORTON_HOFF with the command STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03), and the piloting ANA_LIM. One may in practice use line search in order to improve the convergence, and the subdivision of the time step,
- post-process the calculation in order to obtain the limit load with the command POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22).

The use of these commands is detailed in the document [U2.05.04](doc:u2.05.04).

As regards the post-processing, the operator POST_ELEM then produces a table which gives for each time instant of the calculation, that is for increasingly weak regularisations, 2 parameters:

- the parameter 'CHAR_LIMI_SUP' contains an upper bound of the limit load, by integration on each finite element and a sum over the set of the elements of the model: ${\hat{\lambda}}_{m}={\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}_{y}\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m})}d\Omega \text{}-{L}_{0}({u}_{m})$
- and, in the absence of constant loading, (CHAR_CSTE ='NON'), the parameter 'CHAR_LIMI_ESTIMEE' contains an estimate of a lower bound ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}$ corresponding to: ${\underline{\lambda}}_{m}={\int}_{\Omega}\frac{A(m)}{m}.{(\sqrt{\varepsilon ({u}_{m}).\varepsilon ({u}_{m})})}^{m}d\Omega .{(\underset{x\in \Omega }{\text{Sup}}(\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m}).{\sigma}^{D}({u}_{m})}}{{\sigma}_{y}}))}^{-1}\le {\hat{\lambda}}_{m}$

If a constant loading is present, (then it is imperative to enter CHAR_CSTE = 'OUI'), the parameter PUIS_CHAR_CSTE represents the power of the constant loading in the solution velocity field of the problem.

Several verification tests are available, in particular the test SSNV124 [V6.04.124](doc:v6.04.124). On this very simple problem, an analytical calculation makes it possible to obtain the exact limit load in the direction of the loading, as well as the estimates produced by the regularisation method. For more details one will refer to [bib4] and [bib5].

Furthermore complementary validations have been carried out within the framework of comparative studies, such as the European benchmark LISA [bib8, bib10]: on calculations of limit loads in 2D, 2D axisymmetric and 3D, the regularised kinematic method presented here makes it possible to gain a factor of 6 to 10 on the calculation time with respect to an incremental elastoplastic calculation, and makes it possible to obtain a bracketing of the limit load, unlike the methods of the other participants.
