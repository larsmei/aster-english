# v3.04.002 SSLV002 – Definition of local markers of massive elements by an orientation field

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the definition of the orientations of the local markers of the massive elements via a given orientation field under keyword MASSIF / CHAM_ORIE of the AFFE_CARA_ELEM operator.

The application is the use of orientation fields generated automatically from the topology of mesh by Local Frames Generator utility based on MEDC oupling and available in Salome (from version 9.11).

Local Frames Generator calculates local $(L,T,N)$ benchmarks from the topology of a slender structure (typically d-eolian blades or underwater thrusters with leading and leaking edges), or obtained from a generator (typically a spring).

The $L$ vector allows you to follow the direction of the thrust or generator. The $T$ vector is the vector tangent to the outer skin of the mesh. The $N$ vector allows you to form the direct orthonormized marker. The post-processing utility then returns the information in the form of a field with 7 components corresponding to the SD_CARA_ELEM.CARMASSI aster data structure. It is such a field that is expected at the keyword CHAM_ORIE input of the AFFE_CARA_ELEM / MASSIF.

In this test, a pipe sector consisting of an orthotropic material (so defined in relation to local markers), clamped at the lower side is used.It is applied a force distributed over its upper surface.In this configuration, the local markers correspond to the cylindrical marker.

The test consists of two modellings:

- In Modelling A, which serves as a reference, cylindrical markers are obtained ‘hand-made' by calculating the angles d-Euler mesh cell by mesh cell, as was done and validated in the hsnv134b test of the validation repository.
- In Modelling B, the local benchmark field generated automatically from the topology of the mesh of the pipe sector is used with the Local Frames Generator tool.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A 20 cm high quarter of the pipe is considered. The internal diameter is 4,118 cm and the external diameter is 4,746 cm (hence a thickness of 0,628 cm).

The geometry can be viewed in the figure , with the mesh set that will be used.

### 1.2 Material properties

The pipe quarter consists of an orthotropic material with the following properties:

- modules d
- Poisson coefficients: ${\nu}_{\text{LT}}=0,1$ , ${\nu}_{\text{LN}}=0,06$ and ${\nu}_{\text{TN}}=0,06$
- Shear modules: ${G}_{\text{LT}}=5\mathit{GPa}$ , ${G}_{\text{LN}}=2\mathit{GPa}$ and ${G}_{\text{TN}}=2\mathit{GPa}$

It is an isotropic transverse material with the axis of the isotropy axis $N$ (the axis of the cylinder thickness).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

> The bottom side of the pipe quarter is clamped.

A distributed force of $\mathit{FX}=0,5\mathit{MPa}$ , $\mathit{FY}=0,5\mathit{MPa}$ and $\mathit{FZ}=500\mathit{MPa}$ components is applied to the upper surface.
