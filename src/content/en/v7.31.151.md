# v7.31.151 WTNV151 – Consideration of an unsaturated water exchange condition

**Summary:**

The tests presented make it possible to verify that a water exchange term at the edge is correctly taken into account in the modellings AXIS_*HH2*, D_PLAN_*HH2* and 3D_*HH2* . Under certain assumptions, the problem is reduced to a purely hydraulic problem in capillary pressure. This problem is written in the linear case (ECHANGE_THM) as an equation of heat and can thus be the analogy of a thermal problem. Thus, the solution of the hydraulic problem is compared to the solution of the thermal problem, for which the exchange term is already well taken into account.
The modellings d, e, f refer to the validation of the nonlinear relative humidity exchange term (ECHANGE_THM_HR).

## 1 Reference problem

The aim here is to model the drying of a bar with water exchange conditions at the end and which can be assimilated to a thermal problem under certain conditions. The modelling will be done in 3D, 2D (Plane Deformation) and axysimetry.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The vapour pressure is negligible. It is initially fixed to the whole domain at ${p}_{\mathit{vp}}=921,6$ .

The initial ${p}_{\mathit{gz}}(x,0)=10000\mathit{Pa}$ gas pressure and capillary pressure are set to ${p}_{c}(x,0)=10000\mathit{Pa}$.

Which corresponds to a saturation of $0,999$.

The initial temperature is 20°C. The mechanical stresses break down into actual, $\overline{\overline{\sigma}}'$ and hydraulic ${\overline{\overline{\sigma}}}_{p}$ stresses:

$$
\overline{\overline{\sigma}}=\overline{\overline{\sigma}}'+{\overline{\overline{\sigma}}}_{p}
$$

Initially the hydraulic and effective stresses are zero.

The other generalised [voir [u2.04.05](doc:u2.04.05)] stresses are initialized to zero.

### 1.5 boundary conditions

It is imposed:

- zero movements in all directions;
- one temperature, $T$ zero;
- a water exchange condition on the right edge ( [BC] in 2D or face [BCFG] in 3D.

For modellings a,b and c, the exchange will be on the pressures and the $q_{w}^{ext}$ water flow will then be written:

$q_{w}^{ext}.n={\lambda}^{H}\nabla ({p}_{c}.n)={h}^{H}({p}_{\mathit{ext}}-{p}_{c})$ with ${\lambda}^{H}=\frac{K\times {\mathit{kr}}_{w}}{{\mu}_{w}}$ (in $\mathit{Pa}.s$), ${h}^{H}=1,0\times {10}^{-14}$ and ${p}_{\mathit{ext}}=1,5\times {10}^{8}\mathit{Pa}$;

For modellings d,c and e exchange will be on the relative densities (or humiditys):

$q_{w}^{ext}.n= \alpha.\rho_{vp}.\left( exp\left[ \frac{Pc.M_{H2O}}{\rho_{l}.R.T}\right] -{HR}^{ext} \right)$

with

$\rho_{vp} = \frac{Pv_{sat}.M_{H2O}}{\rho_{l}.R.T}$
with $M_{H2O}$ the molar mass of water, $\rho_{l}$ its density, R the constrant of the perfect gases and ${HR}^{ext}$ , $Pv_{sat}$ and $\alpha$ respectively the external humidity the saturated vapour pressure and the exchange coefficient indicated by the users. Here ${HR}^{ext}=0.5$ ; $Pv_{sat}=2341 Pa$ and $\alpha=0.001 m/s$

- a hydraulic flow condition on all other edges.

## 2 reference solution (modellings A, B et C)

A number of assumptions are made to make the analogy with the thermal case:

- temperature is constant (no thermal calculation);
- the gas pressure is constant;
- The vapour pressure is negligible;
- Fick's broadcast is neglected;
- The material is unformable (no mechanical calculations);
- The gravity is zero;
- Water is incompressible ${\rho}_{w}=\mathit{cste}$ .

It is recalled that Darcy's law (see [r7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11)) for the gas phase states:

$$
{M}_{\mathit{gz}}=-{\rho}_{\mathit{gz}}{\lambda}_{\mathit{gz}}^{H}\nabla {p}_{\mathit{gz}}
$$

with:

- ${M}_{\mathit{gz}}$ the gas flow;
- ${\rho}_{\mathit{gz}}$ the density of the gas;
- ${\lambda}_{\mathit{gz}}^{H}$ the hydraulic conductivity of the gas;
- ${p}_{\mathit{gz}}$ gas pressure.

Or:

$$
{p}_{\mathit{gz}}=\mathit{cste}\Rightarrow \nabla {p}_{\mathit{gz}}=0
$$

So:

$$
{M}_{\mathit{gz}}={M}_{\mathit{as}}+{M}_{\mathit{vp}}=0
$$

As we neglect Fick's broadcast, then:

$$
{M}_{\mathit{ad}}={M}_{\mathit{vp}}=0
$$

By and:

$$
{M}_{\mathit{as}}=0
$$

Thus, the problem of the liquid water body conservation equation can be simplified:

$$
\dot{{m}_{w}}+\text{Div}({M}_{w})=0
$$

Gold ${m}_{w}={\rho}_{w}\varphi S(1+\mathit{Tr}(\epsilon ))-{\rho}_{w}^{0}{\varphi}^{0}{S}^{0}$ , with:

- ${\rho}_{w}$ the density of water, ${\rho}_{w}^{0}$ the density in the initial state;
- $\varphi =\frac{{V}_{\mathit{vide}}}{V}$ the porosity of the material, ${V}_{\mathit{vide}}$ corresponding to the unfilled pores, $V$ to the porosity of the material in the initial state;
- $S=\frac{{V}_{\mathit{lq}}}{{V}_{\mathit{vide}}}$ water saturation, ${S}^{0}$ initial saturation;
- $\epsilon$ the deformation of the material;

The material is considered to be unformable: $\epsilon =0$ and $\varphi =\mathit{cste}={\varphi}^{0}$ and the water is incompressible, ${\rho}_{w}={\rho}_{w}^{0}$. Then:

$$
{m}_{w}(t)={\rho}_{w}^{0}\varphi (S(t)-{S}^{0})
$$

This implies:

$$
\dot{{m}_{w}}(t)={\rho}_{w}\varphi {\partial}_{t}S({p}_{c}(t))={\rho}_{w}\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})\times \dot{{p}_{c}}(t)
$$

As the effects of gravity are neglected, Darcy's law for the liquid phase writes:

$$
{M}_{\mathit{lq}}={M}_{w}=-{\rho}_{w}{\lambda}_{\mathit{lq}}^{H}(S)\nabla {p}_{w}
$$

${p}_{c}={p}_{\mathit{gz}}-{p}_{\mathit{lq}}={p}_{\mathit{atm}}-{p}_{w}$ Gold . Then $\nabla {p}_{c}=-\nabla {p}_{w}$ and therefore ${M}_{w}={\rho}_{w}{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)\nabla {p}_{c}$ .

By replacing ${M}_{w}$ and $\dot{{m}_{w}}$ with their last expressions in () and assuming ${\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)=\frac{K\times {K}_{\mathit{rl}}(S)}{\mu}$ constant:

$$
{\rho}_{w}\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})\times \dot{{p}_{c}}(t)+{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)\text{Div}({\rho}_{w}.\nabla {p}_{c})=0
$$

Water is assumed to be incompressible, therefore:

$$
{\rho}_{w}\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})\times \dot{{p}_{c}}(t)+{\rho}_{w}{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)\text{Div}(\nabla {p}_{c})=0
$$

${\rho}_{w}\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})\times \dot{{p}_{c}}(t)+{\rho}_{w}{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)\text{Div}(\text{}\nabla {p}_{c})=0$

With the Laplacian operator:

$$
{\rho}_{w}\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})\times \dot{{p}_{c}}(t)+{\rho}_{w}{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)\Delta {p}_{c}=0
$$

Finally:

$$
{\partial}_{t}{p}_{c}+\frac{{\lambda}_{w}^{H}(S)}{\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})}\Delta {p}_{c}=0
$$

To close the system, we add:

- the initial condition: ${p}_{c}(x,0):=10000\mathit{Pa}$;
- mixed condition on [BC] (or [BCFG] according to geometry): ${\lambda}^{H}\nabla ({p}_{c}.n)={h}^{H}({p}_{\mathit{ext}}-{p}_{c})$ .

This formulation is similar to an equation of the heat without source term, of the form:

$$
\rho {C}_{p}{\partial}_{t}T+\text{Div}(q)=0
$$

By taking the Fourier law that links the heat flow $q$ to the temperature gradient, namely:

$$
q=-\lambda \nabla T
$$

Assuming $\lambda$ is a constant, material characteristic:

$$
{\partial}_{t}T-\frac{\lambda}{\rho {C}_{p}}\Delta T=0
$$

Where $\rho$ is the density of concrete, ${C}_{p}$ the specific heat of the material and $\lambda$ the thermal conductivity.

The thermal problem is already treated in THER_NON_LINE. The results of the thermal problem can then be compared with those of the THM problem, taking closure conditions of the same type as in the case THM i.e. by posing:

$\begin{array}{c}T(x,0)=\mathit{cste}\\ \lambda (\text{}\nabla T.n)={h}_{T}({T}_{\mathit{ext}}-T)\mathit{au}\mathit{bord}\end{array}$

By ensuring further that:

- $T(x,0)=\mathit{cste}:={p}_{c}(x,0)=10000$ ;
- $-\rho {C}_{p}≔\varphi {S}^{'}({p}_{c})$
- $\lambda :={\lambda}^{H}$
- ${h}_{T}:={h}^{H}$
- ${T}_{\mathit{ext}}:={p}_{\mathit{ext}}$

The modellings AXIS, PLAN_DIAG, and 3D modellings AXIS_THH2MS, D_PLAN_THH2MS and 3D_THH2MS are then used as the basis for this comparison.

## 9 Summary of the results

In the case of a pressure exchange, the solutions of the modellings AXIS_*HH2* , D_PLAN_*HH2* and 3D_*HH2* are similar to the reference solutions of the modellings AXIS, PLAN_DIAG, and 3D, respectively. The term hydraulic exchange is therefore correctly represented. In the case of a nonlinear exchange of relative humidity (or density), the water flow recovered is checked for the analytical flow.
