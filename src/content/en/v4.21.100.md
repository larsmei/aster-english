# v4.21.100 TTLL100 - Thermal shock on a flat wall with exchange condition

**Summary:**

This transient linear thermal test consists of imposing a cold thermal shock on an infinite plane wall using a limit exchange condition. The shock is modelled by a linear $\Delta T=–100°C$ ramp in ${10}^{-2}s$.

The problem is dealt with in plan.

The reference solution is an analytical.

The test is performed on 2 modellings: (TRIA3, QUAD4) and (TRIA6, QUAD9).

The transient linear thermal algorithm is tested when the mass matrix is diagonalized (modelling PLAN_DIAG with ‘的mass dumping').

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v4.21.100/10000A9A00001BB3000014ACE499FE74620A6962.svg)

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\mathrm{AB}}=\stackrel{ˉ}{\mathrm{BC}}=L=0.1m$

$x(\mathrm{M1})=0.02m$

$x(\mathrm{M2})=0.08m$

### 1.2 Material properties

$\lambda =1W/m°C$

$\rho {C}_{P}=1000J/{m}^{3}°C$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

$\lambda {\frac{\partial T}{\partial n}\mid }_{x=\pm L}=h({T}_{\mathrm{ext}}-T(x,t))$ exchange

with : $h=100.W/{m}^{2}°C$

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{T}_{\mathrm{ext}}{(C)}_{t=0}=100.\\ {T}_{\mathrm{ext}}{(C)}_{t={10}^{-2}}=0.\end{array}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

$T(x,0)=100°C$ for all $x$

Time Discretization (t):

| 10 steps for | $[0.,\mathrm{1.D}–2]$ | either | $\Delta t={10}^{–3}s$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 steps for | $[1D–2,\mathrm{1.D}–1]$ | either | $\Delta t={10}^{–2}s$ |
| 9 steps for | $[\mathrm{1.D}–1,1.]$ | either | $\Delta t={10}^{–1}s$ |
| 5 steps for | $[1.,2.]$ | either | $\Delta t=2.{10}^{–1}s$ |
| 8 steps for | $[2.,10.]$ | either | $\Delta t=1s$ |

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

$\frac{T(x,t)-{T}_{p}}{{T}_{0}-{T}_{p}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{A}_{n}\exp(-{\xi}_{n}^{2}\frac{\lambda}{\rho {C}_{p}{L}^{2}}t)\cos({\xi}_{n}\frac{x}{L})$

| $x=$ | abscissa |
| --- | --- |
| $t=$ | Time |
| ${T}_{0}=$ | Initial temperature |
| ${T}_{p}=$ | Temperature imposed |
| $n=$ | $1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$ |

With ${\xi}_{n}$ positive roots of ${\xi}_{n}\tan{\xi}_{n}=\mathit{hL}/\lambda =10.$

et ${A}_{n}=\frac{4\sin{\xi}_{n}}{2{\xi}_{n}+\sin(2{\xi}_{n})}$

### 2.2 Baseline

Temperatures at $\mathrm{M1}$ ($x=0.02$ ) and $\mathrm{M2}$ ($x=0.08$ ) points,

and at different times ($t=0.1,0.5,2.0$ and $10.0$ ).

The reference values are obtained by calculating the first 30 terms of the series (Mathematica).

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

- INCROPERA F.P., DE WITT D.P., Fundamentals of heat and mass transfer. Third Edition. 1990.

## 5 Summary of the results

PLAN_DIAG Modelling gives fairly satisfactory results. Although the mesh has little d-d-elements in thickness, the difference in temperature remains below 3.2%.

Although the thermal shock is brutal, the diagonalization of the mass matrix allows a temperature solution that does not negulate during the transient.
