# v6.08.101 SSND101 – constitutive law for viscous shock absorbers on discreet elements

**Summary:**

The response of the DIS_VISC nonlinear viscous shock absorber model, of the generalised 的Zener type, cf. [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17), formulated on discrete elements for mesh cells SEG2 or POI1, is tested.

Although it is a viscous law, it is the STAT_NON_LINE operator that is used for validation. The discrete d的elements response is analyzed for a nonlinear viscous constitutive law under a harmonic load. The modellings are:

  1. static non-linear cycle,

  1. static linear cyclic and dissipation calculation,

  1. static non-linear creep,

  1. static nonlinear, Modelling a shock absorber of type MAXWELL.

The discrete modellings and elements tested are in $\mathrm{3D}$ with modellings DIS_T and DIS_TR and the basic features: (K_T_D_L, K_TR_D_L, K_T_D_N, K_TR_D_N, A_T_D_L, M_T_D_L), depending on the type of mesh cell and d的element.

This behavior is also validated for dynamic stresses in test case SDND107 [V5.01.107](doc:v5.01.107) with DYNA_VIBRA and DYNA_NON_LINE operators.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Description of the device

L-slipper is represented by the rheological model below.

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.101/10000000000002C1000001320033E4135DAD2D7F.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a: Rheological model of viscous shock absorber.

The values of the different ${E}_{1}$, ${E}_{2}$, ${E}_{3}$ and the characteristics of the nonlinear viscous part ${C}_{3}$ , ${\alpha}_{3}$ are derived from tests. The equations governing the behaviour are [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17):

${\dot{F}}_{1}\left(\frac{1}{{E}_{1}}+\frac{1}{{E}_{3}}+\frac{{E}_{2}}{\left({E}_{1}.{E}_{3}\right)}\right)=\left({\dot{U}}_{2}-{\dot{U}}_{1}\right)\left(1+\frac{{E}_{2}}{{E}_{3}}\right)-{\langle \langle \frac{{F}_{1}}{{C}_{3}}\left(1+\frac{{E}_{2}}{{E}_{1}}\right)-\frac{{E}_{2}}{{C}_{3}}\left({U}_{2}-{U}_{1}\right)\rangle \rangle }^{1/{\alpha}_{3}}$

with $\begin{array}{cc}{\langle \langle x\rangle \rangle }^{a}={x}^{a}& \mathit{si}x\ge 0\\ {\langle \langle x\rangle \rangle }^{a}=-{∣x∣}^{a}& \mathit{si}x\le 0\end{array}$

The dissipation increment is:

$\Delta D={C}_{3}.{∣\frac{{F}_{1}}{{C}_{3}}\left(1+\frac{{E}_{2}}{{E}_{1}}\right)-\frac{{E}_{2}}{{C}_{3}}\left({U}_{2}-{U}_{1}\right)∣}^{1+1/{\alpha}_{3}}$

### 1.2 modellings

The modellings tested are on elements DIS_T then DIS_TR, mesh cells SEG2 then mesh cells POI1. The stiffness features of the discreet elements, which are used for predicting nonlinear algorithm, are therefore of the type: K_T_D_L, K_TR_D_L, K_T_D_N, K_TR_D_N, depending on the type of d的element.

Note: The units of the parameters must be in accordance with the unit of forces, length unit and time unit of the [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17) problem. For all modellings units are homogeneous to [N], [m], [s].

#### 1.2.1 Modelling A

This Modelling allows testing the static nonlinear cyclic behaviour of the law.

#### 1.2.2 Modelling B

This nonlinear static model allows the dissipation of a stabilized cyclic load to be tested in addition to the constitutive law. Dissipation is compared to a theoretical value obtained in the particular case ${\alpha}_{3}=1.0$. For a cyclic load with ${\alpha}_{3}\ne 1$ the theoretical calculation of dissipation is not accessible.

#### 1.2.3 Modelling C

This nonlinear static Modelling allows testing of constitutive law during a creep test. The displacement is imposed and remains constant. The response of constitutive law and dissipation are compared to the theoretical values obtained in the particular case ${\alpha}_{3}=0.5$ .

#### 1.2.4 Modelling D

This Modelling validates the fact that it is possible to model a Maxwell type shock absorber by affecting the suitable material characteristics. It is possible to make the stiffeners to the infinite by using the UNSUR_K key words when defining the material. In this particular case $\mathit{K1}=120.0$ , $\mathit{K2}=0$ , $\mathit{K3}\to \infty$ or $\mathit{UNSUR}\text{\_}\mathit{K3}=0$ . The modeled shock absorber is shown in the following figure.

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.101/100000000000011F00000067F003BCD69532C94E.png)

**Figure** 1.2.4-a: Maxwell type damper.

When the discreet is a SEG2, one of the nodes is blocked, on the other a displacement condition is imposed. When the discreet is a POI1 the displacement condition is imposed on this node.

${U}_{0}.\sin(2\pi .\mathit{f.t})$ with $f=5\mathit{Hz}$

In Modelling C, displacement is imposed and remains constant: ${U}_{0}=0.1$
