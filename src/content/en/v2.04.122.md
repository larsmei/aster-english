# v2.04.122 SDLV122 - Extrapolation of local measurements to a complete model (3D)

**Summary:**

This is a linear dynamic test $\mathrm{3D}$ .

The purpose is to test the PROJ_MESU_MODAL command in the case of an $\mathrm{3D}$ system. This command allows to project dynamic transient experimental responses into a number of points on a modal basis of numerical modelling.

This test contains 2 modellings:

- the projection (constraints) is made on a modal base concept of type [mode_meca],
- the projection (constraints) is made on a modal base concept of type [modal_base].

For the 2 modellings, the experimental measurements provided are identical and allow to test the search for nodes vis-à-vis and the consideration of a local orientation.

In both cases, the reference solution is obtained by direct calculation with *Code_Aster*; projection is performed in the case of a favourable number of modes equal to the number of measurements. The stress responses obtained after projection are identical to the reference stresses given in data.

For model A, the displacement and deformation responses obtained after projection are in perfect harmony with the reference solutions. The values of the speeds and accelerations derived from the modal contributions identified are similar to those obtained by direct calculation. The low differences observed are due to the approximation errors generated by the determination via a linear schematic of the speeds and accelerations.

## 2 Reference solutions

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

- analytical solution:

A analytical solution of this problem exists. It is described in[1]:

In this case, the modal superposition solution of this problem is written:

$u(x,t)=\frac{{P}_{0}}{\mathit{EA}}x-\frac{8{P}_{0}I}{{\pi}^{2}\mathit{EA}}\underset{s=1}{\overset{\infty}{\Sigma}}\left[\frac{{(-1)}^{s-1}}{{(\mathrm{2s}-1)}^{2}}\sin((\mathrm{2s}-1)\frac{\pi x}{\mathrm{2I}})\cos((\mathrm{2s}-1)\frac{\pi c}{\mathrm{2I}}t)\right]$

with: $c=\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{EA}}{m}}$: wave propagation speed in the bar

- Reference solution retained:

The analytical solution with infinite sum on the modes, it is preferable that the reference solution and the projection solution match the same configuration, with the same number of modes.

In addition, to avoid problems related to the discretization of the digital mesh, the reference solution chosen is the answer provided by the direct calculation made with *Code_Aster* with the DYNA_VIBRA command. Simulates the displacement, speed and constraint at a few structural points. Simulated responses were placed in a universal format file (IDEAS).

### 2.2 Baseline

For Modelling A, the results are compared for displacements, speeds, accelerations, deformations and stresses along the $x$ axis, nodes $\mathrm{N2}$ and $\mathit{N4}$ at 3 different moments. $\mathrm{N4}$ corresponds to a measurement node and $\mathrm{N2}$ n的en is not one. We also check whether the measured field component is obtained exactly after the measurement has expanded on the numerical model. This compares the extended field component according to the observation direction (moving $\mathrm{N3}$ following DX, $\mathit{N5}$ following DX and SIXX following node $\mathrm{N4}$).

For model B, the comparison of the results is based on the nodes $\mathit{N3}$ and $\mathrm{N4}$ stresses at 3 different moments.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

The chosen reference allows to rule out uncertainties related to the discretization of the digital mesh. The number of modes of the projection base is equal to the number of measurements, so the solution of the inversion is accurate (as opposed to a solution approached from a generalised inverse problem).

In the case of projection on a [mode_meca] concept, the modal bases of the reference solution and the projection solution are identical, the responses to displacements, deformations and stresses obtained must therefore be similar to the reference responses. Some approximation errors may appear on the speeds and accelerations that are determined by a linear schedule in time.

In the case of projection on a [modal_base] concept, the modal bases of the reference solution and the projection solution contain the same number of modes but are different. Since the reference calculation is not possible on a [modal_base] concept, the comparison of the results is limited to responses corresponding to the measurements provided.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics and meshes

- This Modelling tests the readout of dataset 58 of a universal format file (IDEAS), containing both displacement, speed and stress fields. The expansion on the numerical model is also performed by using the mixed measurement (movement, speed and stress).
- Digital mesh:

The mesh digital is made with I-DEAS Master Series 5 version. It has 2667 nodes and 3328 mesh cells linear $\mathrm{3D}$ type.

- Experimental mesh:

The mesh of measurement includes only 5 elements point and 5 nodes position as shown in the following figure:

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.122/10000000000003EA00000075119BD22E13249E41.png)

### 3.2 Characteristics of the measures

The experimental measurements provided are:

- ${\sigma}_{xx}$ axial stresses, following $x$ direction to nodes $\mathit{N3}$ , $\mathrm{N4}$ and $\mathrm{N5}$ ,
- axial displacement to node $\mathrm{N3}$ ,
- node $\mathrm{N5}$ axial speed .

Time sampling is constant: the initial time is $0s$, the time step is ${10}^{-5}s$ and the number of minutes is 1001 (i.e. up to a final time of $0.01s$).

The values are derived from the direct calculation made with *Code_Aster* .

### 3.3 Modal base characteristics

Modes are stored in a concept of [mode_meca] type, containing the first three dynamic traction modes. These modes are obtained by blocking the transverse movements (i.e. following $\mathrm{DY}$ and $\mathrm{DZ}$) of the nodes of the neutral fibre and the nodes of the upper line ($x=0.$ to $4.$ $y=0.1$ and $z=0.$). Their eigenfrequencies ($326.5\mathrm{Hz}$ , $980.0\mathrm{Hz}$ and $1634.5\mathrm{Hz}$ ) are close to the own traction frequencies calculated analytically ($324.3\mathrm{Hz}$ , $972.9\mathrm{Hz}$ and $1621.5\mathrm{Hz}$ ).

### 4.2 Characteristics of measures

The experimental measurements provided are:

- At nodes $\mathrm{N3}$ , $\mathrm{N4}$ and $\mathrm{N5}$ :

The data are the axial stresses, applied in the direction $x$ .

Time sampling is constant: the initial time is $0s$, the time step is ${10}^{-5}s$ and the number of minutes is 1001 (i.e. up to a final time of $0.01s$).

The values are derived from the direct calculation made with *Code_Aster* .

### 4.3 Modal base characteristics

Modes are stored in a [base_modal] concept, containing the first two dynamic traction modes and the static mode in node $\mathrm{N3INF}$ for degree of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$. The interface is Craig-Bampton type. The database therefore contains a total of 3 modes.

**Note:**

The number of modes is very small, so the solution depends on the modal basis. However, the modes determined for this Modelling are not the same as those of the reference modal base, and it is not possible to perform direct calculation with Code_Aster on a concept [ *base_modale]. Only responses corresponding to the measurements provided can therefore be validated. No comparison can be made on the other responses.*

## 5 Summary of the results

For both modellings, the stress responses obtained after projection are identical to the reference stresses obtained by direct calculation with *Code_Aster* and provided in data.

For model A, the displacement and deformation responses are in perfect alignment with the reference solutions. The values of the speeds and accelerations obtained after projection are similar to those obtained by direct calculation. The low differences observed are due to the approximation errors generated by the determination of the speeds and accelerations by a linear schematic in time.

Cases where the number of modes is not equal to the number of measurements are not tested (generally inverse problem); in particular, the Tikhonov regularisation method is not tested.
