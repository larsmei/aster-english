# 1 **v3.04.110** SSLV110 - Elliptical fissure in infinite medium

**Summary:**

This is a static test for a three-dimensional problem. This test allows to calculate the local energy return rate on the crack bottom by the theta method (command CALC_G in FEM and CALC_G_XFEM in X-FEM).

The radius of the integration crowns is variable along the crack, and the local energy return rate is calculated using two different methods (LEGENDRE and LINEAIRE/LAGRANGE). The integration crowns are defined in two ways: by the variable radius data (R_INF_FO and R_SUP_FO) and by the number of layers data (NB_COUCHE_INF and NB_COUCHE_SUP).

The value of the test is the validation of the theta method in $\mathrm{3D}$ and the following points:

- comparison of results with an analytical solution,
- stability of the results vis-à-vis the integration crowns,
- comparison between two different methods for the calculation of local $G$,
- 2 equivalent loads (distributed pressure and volume loading).
- Calculation of $\mathrm{K1}$ from $G$ and formula d

This test contains 3 different modellings (A,F,G).

Modelling F tests the calculation of $\mathrm{K1}$ for an unmesh crack (X-FEM method). It also compares errors made on the calculation of $\mathit{K1}$ with the POST_K1_K2_K3 operator or the CALC_G_XFEM operator.

The G Modelling validates the calculation of the equivalent stress intensity factor in the presence of cohesive zones (see [R7.02.18](doc:r7.02.18) documentation), by the CALC_G_XFEM operator.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

It is an elliptical crack plunged into an supposed infinite medium. Only one eighth of a parallelepiped is modelled:

### 1.2 Material properties

$E=210\mathrm{000.MPa}$

$\nu =0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Symmetrical to the 3 main plans:

${U}_{x}=0.$ in the $X=0$ plane.

${U}_{Y}=0.$ in the $Y=0$ plane.

${U}_{Z}=0.$ in $Z=0.$ plane out of crack

$P=1\mathrm{MPa}$ in $Z=1250\mathrm{mm}$ plane ($A$ Modelling)

either:

$\mathrm{FZ}={8.10}^{-4}N/{\mathrm{mm}}^{3}$ on all volume elements (load equivalent to previous) (modellings $F$ and $G$ ).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is an analytical solution from SIH [bib1] and [bib2].

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.110/Object_1.png)

It is noted that $\alpha$ angle here refers to the parametric angle of point $M$ (angle versus $\mathit{Ox}$ axis of the $M$ projected on the $b$ radius circle) and not the polar coordinate of this point.

Here: $a=25\mathit{mm}$ and $b=6\mathit{mm}$ , so $k=0,9707728$

The values of the integral elliptical $E(k)$ are tabulated in [bib3], depending on $\mathit{asin}(k)$ which is $76,11°$ here. You can then find: $E(k)=1,0672$ .

Hence the stress intensity factor in $\mathit{MPa.}\sqrt{}\mathit{mm}$: ${K}_{I}(\alpha )=4.0680{\left[{\sin}^{2}\alpha +\frac{{b}^{2}}{{a}^{2}}{\cos}^{2}\alpha \right]}^{1/4}$

Then, from the formula d       , (planar deformation):

$g(\alpha )=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}{K}_{I}{(\alpha )}^{2}$

## 4 Modelling F

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

In this model, the crack is not meshed. X-FEM is used.

Given the symmetries of the problem, it is possible to represent only one eighth of the structure (as is done in Modelling A). However, with the X-FEM method, it is not possible to represent a crack that is located in a plane of symmetry (on the edge of the modelled domain). Therefore, in this Modelling one quarter of the structure, i.e. a portion of $90°$ of the ellipse, is modelled.

The mesh is composed of mesh cells HEXA8, evenly distributed along the $X$ and $Y$ axes and distributed in geometrically progressive along the $Z$ axis so that in the $Z=0$ plane, the mesh cells are approximately $10\mathit{mm}$ side cubes.

Symmetrical conditions are applied on the $X=0$ and $Y=0$ faces. The rigid mode along the $Z$ axis is blocked by blocking the $Z$ movement of the point located in $(0,0,-1250\mathit{mm})$ .

**Loading:** Unit pressure distributed on both normal sides of the block:

$P=1\mathit{MPa}$ in $Z=\pm 1250\mathit{mm}$ planes.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes : 21000

Number of mesh cells and types: 13000 PENTA6 and 12500 HEXA8 (mesh linear)

Since this initial mesh is far too coarse for accurate calculation of stress intensity factors along the crack bottom, an automatic refinement procedure for mesh cells near the crack bottom is used, as recommended in the [U2.05.02](doc:u2.05.02) documentation.

The target size of the mesh cells desired is $b/9$ . This will induce 5 successive refinements. The mesh cells size of the mesh so refined is then $h=0,39\mathit{mm}$ .

The refined mesh (the one on which the mechanical calculation is performed) has as its characteristics:

- 26484 nodes
- 7720 TETRA4, 10650 PYRAM5 et 20080 HEXA8

This mesh induces 99 points along the crack bottom and takes into account the blocking conditions 118404 equations in the system to be solved

### 4.3 Tested quantities and results

The values tested are the stress intensity factors $\mathrm{K1}$ along the crack bottom calculated either by CALC_G_XFEM or by POST_K1_K2_K3.

For CALC_G_XFEM, the integration crown is $2h-4h$. The default smoothing (Legendre) is used.

For POST_K1_K2_K3, the maximum curvilinear abscisse is $4h$. To reduce the calculation times of POST_K1_K2_K3, only 20 points are post-processed evenly distributed along the crack bottom.

Note that the calculation time for the Legendre smoothing of CALC_G is insensitive to this number.

The values are tested at $A$ ($s=0$) and $B$ ($s=26,7$) points.

## 5 Modelling G

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The geometry and loading are identical to the F Modelling: a quarter of the structure is modelled, a pressure is applied to the upper face. In this Modelling, the initial crack is meshed. Compared to the F Modelling, cohesive zones are introduced into the extension of the crack. This extension is represented by level-set, so that the discontinuity is taken into account by XFEM Modelling, as for F Modelling. The CZM_LIN_MIX cohesive law is introduced in this model XFEM by the DEFI_CONTACT command.

**Loading:** Unit pressure distributed on the side of the block opposite the lip plane:

$P=1\mathrm{MPa}$ in the $Z=1250\mathrm{mm}$ plane.

The cohesive parameters are chosen so that this will open some cohesive elements near the initial crack bottom:

  - To avoid a complete break, but simply a decohesion close to the initial crack tip, we take ${G}_{c}>{G}_{max}$, with ${G}_{max}$ the maximum local $G$ on the long front, while keeping the same order of quantity for both values. In our case, ${G}_{c}=2.5\times {10}^{-4}{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-1}$ against ${G}_{max}=7.2\times {10}^{-5}{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-1}$.
  - To observe a decohesion in the vicinity of the tip, the characteristic size of the ${l}_{c}=\frac{E{G}_{c}}{(1-{\nu}^{2}){\sigma}_{c}^{2}}$ cohesive area is chosen so as to cover some elements while remaining small in front of the $h\le {l}_{c}\le a$ structure size. In this test case, to reduce the calculation time, we took ${l}_{c}=14\mathit{mm}$, which leads to ${\sigma}_{c}=2\mathit{MPa}$, to compare with typical sizes of elements $h=1\mathit{mm}$ on the small side of the ellipse, and $h=2\mathit{mm}$ on the large side.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes : 4522

Number of mesh cells and types : 22300 TETRA4

### 5.3 Tested quantities and results

The values tested are the $\mathrm{K1}$ equivalent stress intensity factors along the crack bottom calculated by CALC_G. To obtain a regular result, only 5 points are post-processed evenly distributed along the crack bottom (NB_POINT_FOND=5). The values at the end points of the front $A$ ($s=0$) and $B$ ($s=26,7$) are tested.

'LAGRANGE' smoothing is used for $\theta$ and 'LAGRANGE_NO_NO' for $G$ .

## 6 Summary of the results

Calculation of $G$ or $K$ local:

- for a mesh crack, the 2 methods (LEGENDRE and LINEAIRE/LAGRANGE) give substantially the same results (less than $\text{5 \%}$ error compared to analytical solution) except for point $B$ (point end of ellipse on the large axis) where the Lagrange method is the most accurate;
- load case: values obtained with volume loading are slightly higher than those obtained with imposed stresses (including for $G$ values). The differences are minimal and due to different numerical integrations on the volume term and the edge term;
- $\text{X-FEM}$ method allows to evaluate stress intensity factors $K$ on an uncracked mesh with an error of less than $\text{10\%}$.
