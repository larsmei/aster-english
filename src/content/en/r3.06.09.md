# r3.06.09 Mechanical joint finite elements and coupled hydromechanical joint finite elements

**Summary:**

This documentation deals with the description of the linear and quadratic mechanical joint finite elements. The modellings in pure mechanics (xxx_JOINT) support both types of mesh, whereas the coupled hydromechanical modellings (xxx_JOINT_HYME) are implemented only for quadratic meshes. One proceeds to the elimination of fictitious pressure degrees of freedom for the quadratic modellings in pure mechanics. These modellings make it possible to simulate the evolution of a crack along a predetermined path. The second type of element also takes into account the interaction of the mechanics with a fluid flow inside the crack.

One presents in turn the following points:

1. geometry of the elements
2. local frame of the joint and passage matrix from the global frame to the local frame
3. displacement jump in the joint
4. gradient of the fluid pressure
5. nodal vector of the internal forces together with the elementary tangent matrix

## 1 Geometry of the elements

### 1.1 Geometry of the linear 2D joint

The 2D joint element is a four-node quadrangle (QUAD4) with two short sides and two long sides [12] and [34] (see figure1) which represent the two segments ${\Gamma}^{+}$ and ${\Gamma}^{-}$ of an interface (or lips of a crack) between two two-dimensional sub-domains. By convention the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ is given by nodes 3 and 4 and the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ by nodes 1 and 2. The normal $n$ is directed from the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ towards the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ .

### 1.2 Geometry of the quadratic 2D joint

These elements are eight-node quadrangles (QUAD8); nodes 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 carry mechanical displacement degrees of freedom. Nodes 6 and 8 carry pressure degrees of freedom in order to represent the fluid flow for the coupled hydromechanical modelling. They are eliminated for the purely mechanical modellings.

By convention the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ is given by nodes 3, 4, 7 and the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ by nodes 1, 2, 5. The normal $n$ is directed from the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ towards the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ .

### 1.3 Geometry of the linear 3D joint

The 3D joint elements make it possible to represent a surface $S$ between two volume sub-domains ${\Omega}^{+}$ and ${\Omega}^{-}$ . They are compatible with the mesh of the sub-domains. If the volume is meshed with HEXA8 elements, the joints to be used are also HEXA8 (eight-node hexahedra). If the volume is meshed with PENTA6 or TETRA4 elements, the joints to be used are PENTA6 (six-node pentahedra).

In order to distinguish the upper surfaces ${S}^{+}$ (associated with ${\Omega}^{+}$ ) and the lower surface ${S}^{-}$ (associated with ${\Omega}^{-}$ ), it is necessary to impose a very specific local numbering of the nodes (see figure3).

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.09/Shape15.gif)

**Figure3** : Diagram of the 3D joint elements HEXA8 and PENTA6 with the correct local numbering.

By convention the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ is given by nodes 1, 2, 3, 4 for the HEXA8 (or 1, 2, 3 for the PENTA6) and the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ by nodes 5, 6, 7, 8 for the HEXA8 (or 4, 5, 6 for the PENTA6). The normal $n$ is directed from the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ towards the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ .

### 1.4 Geometry of the quadratic 3D joint

These joint elements can be used for the coupled HYME modellings or for the modellings in pure mechanics by the elimination of pressure degrees of freedom. In 3D they make it possible to represent a surface $S$ between two volume sub-domains *in pure mechanics* ${\Omega}^{+}$ and ${\Omega}^{-}$ . They are compatible with the mesh of the sub-domains. If the volume is meshed with HEXA20 elements, the joints to be used are also HEXA20 (twenty-node hexahedra). If the volume is meshed with PENTA15 or TETRA4 elements, the joints to be used are PENTA15 (15-node pentahedra).

In order to distinguish the upper surfaces ${S}^{+}$ (associated with ${\Omega}^{+}$ ) and the lower surface ${S}^{-}$ (associated with ${\Omega}^{-}$ ) it is necessary to impose a very specific local numbering of the nodes (see figure4).

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.09/Shape16.gif)

**Figure4** : Diagram of the coupled HYME joint elements in 3D, HEXA20 and PENTA15, with the correct local numbering.

For the HEXA20, the mid-side nodes 13, 14, 15, 16 carry pressure degrees of freedom for the fluid flow, and all the others carry mechanical displacement degrees of freedom.

For the PENTA15, the mid-side nodes 10, 11, 12 carry pressure degrees of freedom for the fluid flow, and all the others carry mechanical displacement degrees of freedom.

By convention the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ is given by nodes 1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 11, 12 for the HEXA20 (or 1, 2, 3, 7, 8, 9 for the PENTA15) and the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ by nodes 5, 6, 7, 8, 17, 18, 19, 20 for the HEXA20 (or 4, 5, 6, 13, 14, 15 for the PENTA15). The normal $n$ is directed from the face ${\Gamma}^{-}$ towards the face ${\Gamma}^{+}$ .

### 1.5 Automatic construction of joint mesh cells

The command MODI_MAILLAGE used with the keyword ORIE_FISSURE makes it possible to impose the correct local numbering of the joint mesh cells in 2D or in 3D (see doc. [U4.23.04](doc:u4.23.04)).

Furthermore, techniques are available for automatically creating joint elements in a mesh which does not contain any (see [U2.05.07](doc:u2.05.07)).

## 2 Local frame and passage matrix

It is necessary to construct a local frame for the element in order to define the displacement jump $\delta$ (input data of the constitutive laws: see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11) and [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)). Furthermore, one defines the passage matrix $R$ from the global frame to the local frame. This part is valid for the joint modellings in pure mechanics and for the coupled hydromechanical modellings.

### 2.1 2D case

Let $(X,Y)$ be the global frame. The direction given by the long sides [12] and [34] of the 2D joint element makes it possible to define a local frame $(n,t)$ for the joint element (see figure1):

> $t=\frac{\overrightarrow{12}}{\parallel \overrightarrow{12}\parallel }$ , $n=t\wedge (X\wedge Y)$

The passage matrix from the global frame to the local frame is expressed:

> $R=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{n}_{x}& {n}_{y}\\ {t}_{x}& {t}_{y}\end{array}\right]$

### 2.2 3D case

One denotes by $(X,Y,Z)$ the global frame. For the construction of the local frame of the joint element, one uses the covariant basis of the corresponding surface element. If one denotes by $s({\xi}^{1,}{\xi}^{2})$ the parametrised position of a point of the surface element:

$s({\xi}^{1,}{\xi}^{2})=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathrm{Nb}}{N}_{n}({\xi}^{1},{\xi}^{2}){s}^{n}$

where ${N}_{n}$ and ${s}^{n}$ denote respectively the shape function and the geometric position of the node $n$ , and $\mathrm{Nb}$ the number of nodes of the surface element. One defines the local covariant basis $({a}_{1,}{a}_{2})$ in the following manner:

${a}_{1}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathrm{Nb}}\frac{\partial {N}_{n}}{\partial {\xi}^{1}}{s}^{n}$ ${a}_{2}=\frac{\partial s}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathrm{Nb}}\frac{\partial {N}_{n}}{\partial {\xi}^{2}}{s}^{n}$

These two vectors are in fact vectors tangent to the element at a given point. The right-handed orthonormal local basis $(n,t,\tau )$ is then constructed in the following manner:

$t=\frac{{a}_{1}}{\parallel {a}_{1}\parallel }$ $n=\frac{t\wedge {a}_{2}}{\parallel {a}_{2}\parallel }$ $\tau =n\wedge t$

The passage matrix from the global frame to the local frame is given by:

$R=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{n}_{x}& {n}_{y}& {n}_{z}\\ {t}_{x}& {t}_{y}& {t}_{z}\\ {\tau}_{x}& {\tau}_{y}& {\tau}_{z}\end{array}\right]$

## 3 Displacement jump

The joints are intended to represent two facing faces; they involve only the interpolation functions and the integration points of the corresponding *surface* elements (in 3D) or *line* elements (in 2D):

In 2D: for the QUAD4 joint (or the HYME QUAD8 joint), the line element is the SEG2

In 3D: for the PENTA6 joint (or the HYME PENTA15 joint) the surface element is the TRIA3

> for the HEXA8 joint (or the HYME HEXA20 joint) the surface element is the QUAD4.

One calls ${N}_{n}$ the shape function of the node $n$ of the surface element 1
. ${U}^{\text{+n}}$ and ${U}^{\text{-n}}$ denote respectively the nodal displacements of the segments ${\Gamma}^{+}$ and ${\Gamma}^{-}$ in 2D or of the faces ${S}^{+}$ and ${S}^{-}$ in 3D.

In the local frame, the displacement jump $\delta$ is discretised from the shape functions ${N}_{n}$ . At the Gauss point $g$ , it is expressed as the difference of the displacements of the faces (or segments) + and − :

${\delta}_{g}=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathrm{Nb}}R({U}^{+n}-{U}^{-n}){N}_{n}^{g}$

where $\mathrm{Nb}$ is the number of nodes of the surface element and where $R$ is the passage matrix in 2D and in 3D, which makes it possible to express the nodal displacements in the local frame. One can summarise the preceding expression in a matrix ${M}_{g}^{U}$ which acts on the vector of the nodal displacements of the element: $U$ , in order to construct the displacement jump in the local frame:

> ${\delta}_{g}={M}_{g}^{U}U$

The matrix ${M}_{g}^{U}$ is of dimension $\mathrm{ndim}\times {\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}$ , with ${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}$ the number of *mechanical* degrees of freedom:

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=8$ for the 2D joint,

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=24$ for the 3D HEXA joint

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=18$ for the 3D PENTA joint

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=12$ for the 2D HYME joint

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=48$ for the 3D HEXA HYME joint

${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{U}=36$ for the 3D PENTA HYME joint

In what follows, one uses the generic term: “surface” for 2D as for 3D.

## 4 Gradient of the fluid pressure

The HYME joint elements have, in addition to the mechanical degrees of freedom $U$ , nodal fluid-pressure degrees of freedom (one per node) denoted $P$ .

For the QUAD8, the following nodes 6, 7, 8 carry these pressure degrees of freedom. The reference quadratic element used for the approximation of the pressure is the SEG3.

For the HEXA20, nodes 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 carry these pressure degrees of freedom. The reference surface element used for the approximation of the pressure is the QUAD8.

For the PENTA15, the following nodes 10, 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 carry these pressure degrees of freedom. The reference surface element used for the approximation of the pressure is the TRIA6.

The fluid flow law (cubic law, see [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)) involves the pressure gradient in the direction of the flow, estimated at the Gauss point *g* in the classical manner:

> $\nabla {p}_{g}=\sum_{n=1}^{\mathrm{Nb}}{P}_{n}\nabla {N}_{n}^{g}$

where $\mathrm{Nb}$ is the number of pressure nodes and ${N}_{n}^{g}$ the value of the shape function of the node $n$ at the Gauss point $g$ . To simplify the writing one writes:

> $\nabla {p}_{g}={M}_{g}^{P}P$

The matrix ${M}_{g}^{U}$ is of dimension $(\mathrm{ndim}-1)\times {\mathrm{Nddl}}_{P}$ : with ${\mathrm{Nddl}}_{P}$ the number of *fluid* degrees of freedom:

${\mathit{Nddl}}_{P}=3$ for the 2D HYME joint

${\mathit{Nddl}}_{P}=8$ for the 3D HEXA HYME joint

${\mathit{Nddl}}_{P}=6$ for the 3D PENTA HYME joint

## 5 Internal forces and tangent matrix

### 5.1 Purely mechanical case

The formulation of the mechanical problem (see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11) and [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)) involves the work of the forces along the discontinuity, which is none other than the surface energy associated with the cracking of the structure:

${W}_{s}(\delta )=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\psi ({\delta}_{g})$

with $\psi$ the surface energy density and ${\omega}_{g}$ the weight of the Gauss point $g$ . This makes it possible to define the vector of the internal forces:

${F}_{int}^{U}=\frac{\partial {W}_{s}(\delta )}{\partial U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\delta}_{g}}\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial U}$

In the preceding expression, the first term is given by the cohesive constitutive law (see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11)). This corresponds to the stress vector $\overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}$ (or cohesive force) at the Gauss point $g$ :

$\frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\delta}_{g}}=\overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}$

The second term comes from the definition of the displacement jump in part 3:

$\frac{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{\partial U}={M}_{g}^{U}$

The nodal vector of the internal forces is therefore expressed in the following manner:

${F}_{int}^{U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{U}}^{t}\overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}$

Within the framework of a Newton algorithm, in order to solve the non-linear equilibrium problem, it is useful to have the elementary tangent matrix, that is to say the derivative of the internal forces with respect to the nodal displacements. In the case of the joint element, it is expressed simply:

${K}^{\mathrm{UU}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{int}^{U}}{\partial U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{U}}^{t}\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{M}_{g}^{U}$

The latter is based on the tangent operator: $\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}$ specific to the cohesive constitutive law adopted (see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11)).

### 5.2 Coupled hydromechanical case

The HYME joints, in addition to the nodal forces related to the mechanics ${F}_{int}^{U}$ onto which one transfers the fluid pressure at the Gauss point onto the normal component $\overrightarrow{{p}_{g}}=({p}_{g},0,0)$ (expressed in the local frame of the crack):

> ${F}_{int}^{U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{U}}^{t}(\overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}-\overrightarrow{{p}_{g}})$

have nodal forces for the fluid flow on the nodes which carry pressure degrees of freedom.

The formulation of the hydraulic problem (see [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)) involves the work of the fluid forces along the flow path (inside the crack):

> ${W}_{F}(\nabla p)=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}H(\nabla p)$

with $H$ the surface energy density and ${\omega}_{g}$ the weight of the Gauss point $g$ . This makes it possible to define the vector of the internal forces:

${F}_{int}^{P}=\frac{\partial {W}_{F}(\nabla p)}{\partial P}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}\frac{\partial H}{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}\frac{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}{\partial P}$

In the preceding expression, the first term is given by the cubic constitutive law of the fluid (see [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)). This corresponds to the hydraulic flux $\overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}$ at the Gauss point $g$ :

$\frac{\partial H}{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}=\overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}$

According to the definition of the pressure gradient in part 4. The second term is given by:

$\frac{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}{\partial P}={M}_{g}^{P}$

The nodal vector of the internal forces is therefore expressed in the following manner:

${F}_{int}^{P}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{P}}^{t}\overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}$

Within the framework of a Newton algorithm, in order to solve the non-linear equilibrium problem, it is useful to have the tangent matrix, that is to say the derivative of the internal forces with respect to the degrees of freedom. The derivative of the internal forces on the displacement degrees of freedom with respect to the nodal displacements gives the term identical to that of the matrix obtained in pure mechanics in part 5.1:

${K}^{\mathrm{UU}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{int}^{U}}{\partial U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{U}}^{t}\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{M}_{g}^{U}$

In the case of the hydraulic coupling, the ${F}_{int}^{U}$ depend explicitly on the pressure (see the expression above), hence:

> ${K}^{\mathrm{UP}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{int}^{U}}{\partial P}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{U}}^{t}{X}_{g}$

with ${X}_{g}=\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{p}_{g}}}{\partial P}=(-{N}_{g}^{i},0,0)$ with $i=1\text{à}\mathrm{Nb}$ , $\mathrm{Nb}$ the number of pressure nodes (or also the number of pressure degrees of freedom per element) and ${N}_{n}^{g}$ the value of the shape function of the node $n$ at the Gauss point $g$ .

The derivative of the internal forces on the pressure degrees of freedom with respect to the nodal displacements gives:

> ${K}^{\mathrm{PU}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{int}^{P}}{\partial U}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{P}}^{t}\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}{M}_{g}^{U}$

Finally, the derivative of the internal forces on the pressure degrees of freedom with respect to the nodal pressures gives:

> ${K}^{\mathrm{PP}}=\frac{\partial {F}_{int}^{P}}{\partial P}=\sum_{g}{\omega}_{g}{{M}_{g}^{P}}^{t}\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}}{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}{M}_{g}^{P}$

The elementary tangent matrix (non-symmetric) is expressed in the following manner:

> $K=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}^{\mathrm{UU}}& {K}^{\mathrm{UP}}\\ {K}^{\mathrm{PU}}& {K}^{\mathrm{PP}}\end{array}\right]$

The latter is based on the components of the tangent operator: $\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{\sigma}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}$ , $\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}}{\partial {\delta}_{g}}$ and $\frac{\partial \overrightarrow{{w}_{g}}}{\partial \nabla {p}_{g}}$ specific to the cohesive constitutive law adopted (see [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25)).
