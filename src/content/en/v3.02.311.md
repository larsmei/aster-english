# v3.02.311 SSLP311 - Central slash in a rectangular, two-material, uniformly tensile plate

**Summary:**

This test is based on the independent validation of version 3 in the mechanical of the breakage.

It is a two-dimensional static test with two-material in the presence of an oblique internal interface crack.

The behaviour of the structure (bi-material) is isotropic linear elastic.

The test case consists of four modellings flat stresses in which the influence of the $\theta$ crack tilt is studied (4 cases).

The stress intensity factor calculation is not available for a crack at the interface of a bi-material, so comparison with the reference solution is only done on the energy release rate, calculated with the CALC_G operator.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.311/100002010000025A0000028F556CC108A0407A87.png)

Four values of the angle $\theta$ are considered: 15°, 30°, 45° and 60°.

Other dimensions are chosen such as $H=\mathrm{2W}=\mathrm{4a}$ .

The value of $a$ is $1.E-3m$.

### 1.2 Material properties

**Material No. 1**

Elastic, linear, isotropic, Young's modulus ${E}_{1}=2E+12\mathit{Pa}$ and Poisson's ratio ${\nu}_{1}=0,3$ .

**Material No. 2**

Elastic, linear, isotropic, Young's modulus ${E}_{2}=2E+11\mathrm{Pa}$ and Poisson's ratio ${\nu}_{2}=0,3$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

- The rigid modes are blocked by the following boundary conditions:

$\mathrm{UX}=\mathrm{UY}=0$ at the lower left corner of the model.

$\mathrm{UY}=0$ on the lower edge.

- Loading: ${\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{0}$ uniform voltage on the top edge.

The value of ${\sigma}_{0}$ is $100\mathrm{MPa}$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Border elements method, with quadratic elements [bib1].

The calculation of ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ is performed by an integral contour (integral M [bib2]) in which the stresses and displacements calculated in the part are involved, as well as the stresses and displacements derived from analytically defined asymptotic solutions, in which ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ are alternatively zero.

For comparison, the calculation of $K$ is also done by the virtual extension method.

The relationship between the overall $G$ energy return rate and ${K}_{j}$ is as follows [bib3]:

$G=\beta ({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$

with:

$\beta =\frac{1}{16C{h}^{2}(\alpha \pi )}(\frac{1+{\kappa}_{1}}{{\mu}_{1}}+\frac{1+{\kappa}_{2}}{{\mu}_{2}})$ et $\begin{array}{c}{\kappa}_{i}=\frac{3-{\nu}_{i}}{1+{\nu}_{i}}\\ {\mu}_{i}=\frac{{E}_{i}}{2(1+{\nu}_{i})}\\ \alpha =\frac{1}{2\pi }\ln\left[(\frac{{\kappa}_{1}}{{\mu}_{1}}+\frac{1}{{\mu}_{2}}){(\frac{{\kappa}_{2}}{{\mu}_{2}}+\frac{1}{{\mu}_{1}})}^{-1}\right]\end{array}$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

It is estimated that the difference between the contour integrals method and the virtual extension method is generally less than 0.05%.

## 7 Summary of the results

The calculation of ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ is not available for a crack located at the interface of a bimeterium, and comparison is therefore done directly on the energy release rate $G$ .

$G$ is not accurate on the first crown in all cases of crack tilt, which confirms that you should avoid taking a zero $\mathrm{Rinf}$ radius. As for the other crowns, the differences are in the order of 0.4%. For the case of tilt $\theta =60°$ the difference exceeds 1%. Overall, the results are satisfactory for $G$.
