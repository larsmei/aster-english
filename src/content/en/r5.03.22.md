# r5.03.22 Constitutive law in large rotations and small strains

**Summary:**

The formulation adopted to treat large rotations and small strains is described here. This formulation is valid for all the constitutive laws defined under COMPORTEMENT of the command STAT_NON_LINE and provided with the three-dimensional (3D), axisymmetric (AXIS), plane-strain (D_PLAN) and plane-stress (C_PLAN) modellings.

This functionality is selected by means of the keyword DEFORMATION ='GROT_GDEP' under COMPORTEMENT.

## 1 Some definitions

A few definitions of tensors related to large strains are recalled here.

The deformation-gradient tensor $F$ is the name given to the tensor that takes one from the initial configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ to the current deformed configuration $\Omega (t)$ .

$\mathrm{F}=\frac{\partial \widehat{\mathrm{x}}}{\partial \mathrm{X}}=\mathrm{Id}+{\nabla}_{\mathrm{X}}\mathrm{u}$ with $\mathrm{x}=\widehat{\mathrm{x}}(\mathrm{X},t)=\mathrm{X}+\mathrm{u}$                 eq 1-1

where $X$ is the position of a point in ${\Omega}_{0}$ , $x$ the position of this same point after deformation in $\Omega (t)$ and $u$ the displacement.

Various strain tensors can be obtained by eliminating the rotation in the local transformation. This can be done in two ways, either by using the polar decomposition theorem, or by directly computing the variations of length and of angle (variation of the scalar product).

In the Lagrangian description (that is to say on the initial configuration) one obtains:

- By polar decomposition:

$\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{R}\mathrm{U}$                                             eq 1-2

where $R$ is the rotation tensor (orthogonal) and $U$ the right pure-strain tensor (symmetric and positive definite).

- By a direct computation of the strains:

$\mathrm{E}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathrm{C}-\mathrm{Id})$ with $\mathrm{C}={\mathrm{F}}^{T}\mathrm{F}$                             eq 1-3

where $E$ is the Green-Lagrange strain tensor and $C$ the right Cauchy-Green tensor.

The tensors $U$ and $C$ are related by the following relation:

$\mathrm{C}={\mathrm{U}}^{2}$                                                 eq 1-4

## 2 Constitutive laws in Green-Lagrange kinematics

The Green-Lagrange tensor $E$ can serve as the “kinematic input” to a constitutive law, which then relates the evolution of $E$ to the PK2 stress (as the second Piola-Kirchhoff stress) $S$ :

$\mathrm{S}=\widehat{\mathrm{S}}(\mathrm{E},\dot{\mathrm{E}})$                                                 eq 2-1

The latter is related to the Cauchy stress by the following relation:

$\sigma =\frac{1}{det(\mathrm{F})}\mathrm{F}\mathrm{S}{\mathrm{F}}^{T}$                                                 eq 2-2

This kinematic choice is activated under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT via the keyword DEFORMATION='GREEN_LAGRANGE', for the constitutive laws that allow it, that is to say those for which this kinematic choice is relevant (e.g.: Signorini behaviour, RELATION='ELAS_HYPER').

## 3 Assumption of small strains and large rotations

When the strains are small, there is no fundamental difficulty in writing the constitutive laws: the various “large strain” models lead to the same “small strain” model, and this both for isotropic and for anisotropic behaviours. Only the geometric difficulty associated with the finite rotation remains.

To write the model in large rotations and small strains, one starts from the polar decomposition of $F$ , that is $\mathrm{F}=\mathrm{R}\mathrm{U}$ . Since the tensor $U$ is a pure strain tensor and moreover a small one, the stress tensor ${\sigma}^{\ast }$ associated with this strain history $U$ can be computed by a small-strain constitutive law. It then suffices to subject this tensor ${\sigma}^{\ast }$ to the rotation $R$ in order to obtain the stress tensor $\sigma$ associated with the strain history $F$ , as follows:

$\sigma =\mathrm{R}{\sigma}^{\ast }{\mathrm{R}}^{T}$                                             eq 2-1

This scheme can be summarised as follows:

$\mathrm{F}\to \mathrm{U}=\mathrm{Id}+\epsilon \stackrel{\text{ldc}\text{HPP}}{\to }{\sigma}^{\ast }\to \sigma =\mathrm{R}{\sigma}^{\ast }{\mathrm{R}}^{T}$                     eq 2-2

The drawback of this calculation chain is that it requires the polar decomposition of $F$ . Two assumptions are made in order to avoid it.

On the one hand, in order to avoid the computation of $U$ , the HPP strain $\varepsilon$ can be approximated by the Green strain $E$ , taking advantage of the fact that the strains are small:

$E=\frac{1}{2}({F}^{T}F-\mathrm{Id})=\frac{1}{2}(U-\mathrm{Id})(U+\mathrm{Id})=\varepsilon +\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}^{2}\approx \varepsilon$             eq 2-3

${\sigma}^{\ast }$ is then deduced from this by the “small strain” constitutive law.

On the other hand, in the same way, in order to avoid the computation of $R$ , the HPP stress tensor ${\sigma}^{\ast }$ can be approximated by the second Piola-Kirchhoff tensor $S$ :

$\mathrm{S}=J{\mathrm{F}}^{-1}\sigma {\mathrm{F}}^{-T}=\text{Det}(\mathrm{U}){\mathrm{U}}^{-1}{\sigma}^{\ast }{\mathrm{U}}^{-1}={\sigma}^{\ast }+{\sigma}^{\ast }\text{O}(\epsilon )\approx {\sigma}^{\ast }$             eq 2-4

$\sigma$ is then deduced by:

$\sigma =\frac{1}{J}FS{F}^{T}$                                     eq 2-5

Finally, in the presence of large rotations and small strains, it is sufficient to write the “small strain” constitutive law with, as input, the history of the Green strains $E$ , and as output, the history of the Piola-Kirchhoff stresses $S$ . This approach is valid both for isotropic and for anisotropic constitutive laws.

As for the appropriate variational formulation, it is that adopted in hyperelasticity (behaviour ELAS, ELAS_VMIS_XXX under COMPORTEMENT with strains of type GROT_GDEP). For further details, refer to the associated reference document [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20). One must however be sure that the problem under study does indeed induce small strains, because otherwise the simplifications [eq 2-3] and [eq 2-4] can no longer be made. Without this assumption, the discrepancy with a plastic behaviour increases rapidly with the intensity of the strains.

**Note:** the GROT_GDEP model is used exclusively for certain structural elements (plates, shells) but also within the framework of a call to the MFront solver in the general case. In the other cases (standard isoparametric elements outside MFront), GDEF_LOG, SIMO_MIEHE or GREEN_LAGRANGE must be used, according to the behaviours, in order to activate the taking into account of large strains.
