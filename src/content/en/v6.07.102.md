# v6.07.102 COMP002 – Visco-elastic-plastic behavior test. Simulation in one point hardware

**Summary:**

This test uses a simulation of a loading path in stresses or deformations in a material point, i.e. on a model such that the stress and deformation states are homogeneous at any time. It thus allows to test a number of models of visco-elastic-plastic behavior, with the aim of checking the robustness of their numerical integration, their insensitivity to a change in units, the correct consideration of the control variables on which the coefficients of the model depend, the invariance with an overall rotation applied to the problem, the accuracy of the tangent matrix.

Modelling A: This Modelling allows to validate the LEMAITRE model in 3D.

Modelling B: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_CIN1_CHAB model in 3D.

Modelling C: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_CIN2_CHAB model in 3D.

Modelling D: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_ENDO_LEMA model in 3D.

Modelling E: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_TAHERI model in 3D.

Modelling F: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_ISOT_LINE model in 3D.

Modelling G: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_ISOT_TRAC model in 3D.

H Modelling: This Modelling allows to validate the VISC_CIN2_MEMO model in 3D.

Modelling I: This Modelling allows to validate the VISCOCHAB model in 3D.

Modelling J: This Modelling allows to validate the MONOCRISTAL model in CPLAN and 3D.

K Modelling: This Modelling allows to validate the VMIS_JOHN_COOK model in 3D.

Modelling L: This Modelling allows to validate the HAYHURST model in 3D.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.3.1 Characteristics of loading routes

Two loading routes have been defined to handle $\mathrm{3D}$ and $\mathrm{2D}$ plan cases. They are common to all constitutive laws. Each of them meets the following criteria:

- a combined plastic deformation, $p$ , from 4 to $\text{5\%}$ on the entire journey,
- an increase of $1\text{\%}$ in the cumulative plastic deformation $p$ during a portion of the journey,
- In the presence of viscosity, a deformational stress rate of 10-3, 10-4 and ${10}^{-5}{\text{s}}^{-1}$ respectively was estimated approximately by considering an equivalent deformation of $5\text{\%}$ over the entire journey: either 50, 500 and 5000 seconds for $\text{v1}$ , $\text{v2}$ and $\text{v3}$ respectively. The tests returned correspond to a speed of ${10}^{-5}{\text{s}}^{-1}$ .

This calibration was conducted on the VMIS_ISOT_LINE law and then postponed on the other laws.

The proposed loading causes a decoupled variation of each component of the tensor of the deformations by successive step. A cyclic discharge path is proposed covering the tensile and compression states and an inversion of the shear signs in order to test a wide range of values.

Schematically, it follows a course on 8 segments $\text{[O-A-B-C-O-C’-B’-A’-O]}$ where the second part of the $\text{[O-C’-B’-A’-O]}$ route is symmetrical with the origin of the first $\text{[O-A-B-C-O]}$.

#### 1.3.2 Application of solicitations

The study of a material point (using SIMU_POINT_MAT [U4.51.12](doc:u4.51.12) macro-command) is re-examined by applying for a homogeneous element by imposing:

- in $\mathrm{3D}$ , the 6 components of the strain tensor:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]$

- in $\mathrm{2D}$ the three components of the tensor

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}\end{array}\right]$

For a more general writing, the strain tensor imposed will be decomposed into a hydrostatic and deviatoric part on shear bases:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}\end{array}\right]=p\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& 0\\ 0& 1\end{array}\right]+d\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& 0\\ 0& -1\end{array}\right]+{\varepsilon}_{xy}\left[\begin{array}{cc}0& 1\\ 1& 0\end{array}\right]$ en $\mathrm{2D}$ ,

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]=p\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]+{d}_{1}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& -1& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]+{d}_{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& -1\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& 0& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& 0\end{array}\right]$ en 3D.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

## 2 reference solution

This test performs, for each model, an intercomparison between the reference solution (obtained with a very fine time step), the solution with medium coarse discretization, the solution with temperature effect (or another control variable), the solution by changing the unit system ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{MPa}$), and the solution obtained after rotation or symmetry.

### 2.1 Definition of robustness test cases

We propose 3 angles of analysis to test the robustness of integration of constitutive laws:

- equivalent problem studies
- verification of the tangent matrix
- study of time step discretization

For each of these, the relative differencess are studied in terms of evolution between several calculations using the same law but with different parameters or calculation options. The exploitation is concerned with the invariants of the stress tensor: tensor trace, Von-Mises stress and internal variables of a scalar nature: usually it is the cumulative plasticity.

The overall convergence criteria are the default values for Code_Aster. (RESI_GLOB_RELA=10-6, ITER_GLOB_MAXI=10) A common Newton schema for the re-update of the tangent matrix was adopted:

- calculation of the prediction tangent matrix at each converged increment (REAC_INC=1)
- calculation of the coherent tangent matrix at each Newton iteration (REAC_ITER=1).

### 2.2 Studies of equivalent problems

For rough discretization of the journeys: 1 time step for each segment of the journey, the solution obtained for each law is compared to 3 strictly equivalent problems for the condition of the material point:

- $\mathrm{Tpa}$ , same route with a unit change, substitutes $\mathrm{Pa}$ for $\mathrm{MPa}$ in material data and possible law parameters,
- Trot, route by imposing the same $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after rotation: $R\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}{R}^{T}$ where $R$ is a rotation matrix. For $\mathrm{2D}$ case, the rotation angle will be $\alpha =0.9$ radian, for $\mathrm{3D}$ configuration, we chose the angles d-Euler with arbitrary values { $\psi =0.9$ radian $\theta =0.7$ radian and $\phi =0.4$ radian },
- Tsym, route by imposing the $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after a symmetry: $x$ and $y$ to $\mathrm{2D}$ rotation, $x$ to $y$ rotation, $y$ to $z$ and $z$ to $x$ to 3D.

For each of these problems, the solution (invariants in stresses, cumulative equivalent plastic deformation) must be identical to the basic solution, obtained with the same discretization in time. The reference value of the difference is therefore 0. This means in practice that the difference found must be of the order of machine precision is about 1.E-15.

### 2.3 Test of the tangent matrix

The tangential matrix is also tested for each behavior, by difference with the matrix obtained by disturbance. Here again, the reference value is 0.

### 2.4 Study of time step discretization

The behavior of the integration of laws in relation to discretization is studied. For the same Modelling, therefore a given behavior, several discretizations are studied in different times, multiplying by 5 the number of steps of the loading path. In the reference [1], the discretization is pushed up to 3125 increments per segment on the same principle. Here, to limit the duration of the tests, we limit ourselves to three successive refinements. This leads to the following discretization:

| Number of intervals per loading segment | 5 | 25 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Total number of steps on the entire journey | 40 | 200 |
| Calculation | T1 | Reference solution TROP |

The reference solution, ${T}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ , is the one obtained for $\text{N}=25$ , which is 200 steps for the entire journey. These different solutions allow to judge the sensitivity to the large time step and the robustness of integration.

To show the convergence speed as a function of time step, the solutions presented in [1] are reported here up to 3125 time step for each of the 8 segments of the loading path.

## 15 Executive summary

For all the visco-elastic-plastic behaviors tested, the results are satisfactory:

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{MPa}$), or following rotation or symmetry of the load
- The results converge correctly with the time step, and the integration schemes are robust, since they allow the use of large time step. However, for these models, we should note that the viscosity of time step is more sensitive than for elasto-plastic models.
- the tangent matrices are correct because they are similar to the tangent matrices calculated by disturbance.
