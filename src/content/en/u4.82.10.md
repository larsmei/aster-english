# u4.82.10 Operator CALC_G_XFEM

## 1 Purpose

This operator computes the following fracture-mechanics quantities, in 2D and in 3D, for unmeshed cracks (X-FEM method):

- the energy release rate by the theta method in the case of a linear or non-linear thermoelastic problem [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) and [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03), in statics or in dynamics [R7.02.02](doc:r7.02.02), and in elastoplasticity [R7.02.07](doc:r7.02.07);
- the stress intensity factors $\mathrm{K1}$, $\mathrm{K2}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ by the singular-displacement method in the case of a linear thermoelastic problem [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05);
- the maximisation of $G$ and of $\mathrm{K1}$ under bound constraints.

Before a first use, it is advisable to refer to the corresponding reference documents and user's guides, in particular the document [U2.05.01](doc:u2.05.01).

This operator generates a concept of type table_sdaster.

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operand RESULTAT

```text
/    RESULTAT    =    resu
```

Name of a result concept of type evol_elas, evol_noli, dyna_trans or mode_meca. This operand makes it possible to recover the displacement field (and the velocity and acceleration fields for a dynamic analysis).

The model and the material field, which are required for the analysis, are also extracted from the result data structure. The analysis options available for each type of modelling are recalled in the table below.

|  | **Computation of** $G$ | **Computation of** $K$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **D_PLAN / C_PLAN** | CALC_G | CALC_K_G |
| **AXIS** | CALC_G | CALC_K_G |
| **3D** | CALC_G | CALC_K_G |

**Table 3.1: Availability, by modelling, of the analysis options.**

**Notes on the material properties:**

The characteristics of the material, recovered in the data structure resu, are the following:

- Young's modulus E,
- Poisson's ratio NU,
- thermal expansion coefficient ALPHA (for a thermomechanical problem),
- yield stress SY (for a non-linear elastic problem),
- slope of the tension curve D_SIGM_EPSI (for a non-linear elastic problem with linear isotropic hardening).

For the computation of the energy release rate, these characteristics may depend on the geometry (option ‘CALC_G’) and on the temperature (option ‘CALC_G’). They must be independent of the temperature for the computation of the stress intensity factors.

The characteristics SY and D_SIGM_EPSI are treated only for a non-linear elastic problem with Von Mises hardening. The computation of the stress intensity factors is treated only in linear elasticity.

> **Note:**

*For the computation of the stress intensity factors (option* *'CALC_K_G'*), the characteristics must be defined on all the materials, including on the edge elements, because of the computation method [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05). To make sure of this, it is advisable to do an *AFFE = _F (TOUT = 'OUI') in the command* *AFFE_MATERIAU* [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03), even if the overloading rule is used afterwards.

The stress intensity factors obtained with the option CALC_K_G are computed by evaluating the bilinear form of G with a purely mechanical singular solution (Westergaard asymptotic solution). If a thermomechanical problem is solved, the singularity due to the thermal field is then not taken into account.

An indicator of the error due to this approximation can be obtained by evaluating the difference between G and G_IRWIN. In practice, the quantity $\frac{∣G-{G}_{\mathit{irwin}}∣}{∣G∣}$ is evaluated at every point of the crack front, and the arithmetic mean of it is then taken. If this mean exceeds 50%, it is considered that one is outside the domain of validity of the approach, and an alarm message is issued.

**Bimaterial problem:**

**1st case**: one has a bimaterial but the crack tip is in a single material, cf. Figure 3.1-a. If one is assured that the crown, defined between the lower radius R_INF and the upper radius R_SUP, is supported by elements of the same material, the analysis is possible whichever option is chosen. Otherwise only the option 'CALC_G' is possible.

![Figure](/figures/u4.82.10/100000000000011D000000A0E85AEC46829111A0.png)

Figure 3.1-a: Bimaterial: 1st case

**2nd case**: one has a bimaterial in which the crack tip is at the interface, cf. Figure 3.1-b. To date, only the option for computing the energy release rate (option 'CALC_G') is available. The computation of stress intensity factors is not possible in this case.

![Figure](/figures/u4.82.10/100000000000011D000000A2A36EFE688D672774.png)

**Figure 3.1-b: Bimaterial: 2nd case**

**Computation of equivalent stress intensity factors of a model with cohesive forces:** For studies of crack propagation in the presence of cohesive forces, one may be led to compute equivalent stress intensity factors, according to a very specific procedure of surface integrals over the cohesive zone, detailed in the reference documentation [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19). A few adjustments of the syntax are then necessary:

- the keywords R_INF/R_SUP must not be supplied, because it is not necessary to define a torus for the field $\theta$;
- the keyword COMPORTEMENT must not be supplied; all the information is extracted from the result;
- only the smoothings LAGRANGE and LAGRANGE_NO_NO are available;
- only the option CALC_K_G is available.

A typical syntax is proposed at the end of this documentation.

### 3.3 Keyword EXCIT and operands CHARGE/FONC_MULT

```text
◊    EXCIT = _F(    ♦    CHARGE    =    charge
◊    FONC_MULT = fmult )
```

The keyword EXCIT makes it possible to recover a list of loadings charge, coming from the commands AFFE_CHAR_MECA or AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01), and the possible associated multiplicative coefficients fmult.

**The keyword** **EXCIT is optional and must not be supplied in the general case.**

If the keyword EXCIT is absent from the command, the loading taken into account is that extracted from resu. If the loading is supplied via EXCIT, then it is this loading that will be used in CALC_G_XFEM. If the loading supplied in EXCIT is different from that present in resu (consistency of the name and of the number of loads, of the load–function pairs), an alarm is issued and the analysis continues with the loadings indicated by the user. Caution: this use is valid only when the result is created via the operator CREA_RESU. Indeed, CREA_RESU makes it possible to define a result with or without application of loads. In the latter case, EXCIT gives the possibility of defining the loading directly in CALC_G_XFEM.

The loadings currently supported by the various modellings and that may have a meaning in fracture mechanics are the following:

- Body force: ROTATION, FORCE_INTERNE, PESANTEUR.
- Surface force on the lips of the crack: FORCE_CONTOUR (2D), FORCE_FACE (3D), PRES_REP.
- Thermal expansion: the temperature is transmitted via AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC
- In the case of a thermomechanical problem, the temperature is transmitted via the material properties (AFFE_MATERIAU / AFFE_VARC / EVOL). The thermal expansion is therefore automatically taken into account in the analysis with CALC_G.

**Note:**

*The loadings not supported by an option are* **ignored**. *To date, the following loadings, which may have a meaning in fracture mechanics, are not treated:*

- *FORCE_NODALE*
- *FORCE_ARETE*
- *DDL_IMPO* *on the lips of the crack*
- *FACE_IMPO*
- *PRE_EPSI*

It is important to note that the only loadings taken into account in a fracture-mechanics analysis with the method $\theta$ are those supported by the elements inside the crown, where the theta vector field is non-zero (between R_INF and R_SUP [R7.02.01 §3.3]). **The only types of load liable to influence the computation of** $G$ **are therefore the body loadings (gravity, rotation), a non-uniform temperature field or forces applied on the lips of the crack.**

**Warning:**

- *If several loadings of the same kind (for example a body force) appear in the analysis, they are combined with one another for the post-processing. It is not, however, possible to date to carry out this combination if loadings of type* *FORMULE* are present*: the analysis then ends in error.*
- *An exclusion rule is also applied when a pre-strain field (via* 'PRE_EPSI' *) and an initial-stress field are present simultaneously.*
- *It is not possible to date to associate a load defined from a* *FORMULE and a multiplicative coefficient (* FONC_MULT *). In this case, the analysis ends in error.*
- *The kinematic loads (* AFFE_CHAR_CINE *and* AFFE_CHAR_CINE_F *) cannot be taken into account in the analysis.*
- *For the option* CALC_K_G *, if a loading is imposed on the lips of the crack (* PRES_REP *or* FORCE_CONTOUR *), then the mesh cells of the lips must* **necessarily** *be oriented correctly (using* ORIE_PEAU_2D *or* ORIE_PEAU_3D *) prior to the computation of K (meshed-crack case only).*
- *If an analysis is carried out in large transformations (keyword* DEFORMATION = 'GROT_GDEP' *or* 'PETIT_REAC'), the loadings supported in the vicinity of the crack front (more precisely in the integration torus) must be dead loads, typically an imposed force and not a pressure [R7.02.03§2.4]; these loads must have been declared as non-follower in STAT_NON_LINE *. Nevertheless, follower loads may be defined far from the crack, because they then do not take part in the computation of G.*
- *It is noted that the computation of* CALC_G_XFEM *with the modelling* *AXIS is not available for the results of thermomechanical analyses in large strain and large rotation.*

### 3.4 Keyword THETA

The theta field is computed in CALC_G_XFEM from the keywords R_INF/R_INF_FO, R_SUP/R_SUP_FO and FISSURE.

The various cases are described in the table below according to the analysis option, the modelling (2D or 3D) and the type of crack (meshed crack or not).

|  | **CALC_G** | **CALC_K_G** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **2D** | ◊ R_INF, R_SUP... ♦ FISSURE | ◊ R_INF, R_SUP... ♦ FISSURE |
| **3D** | ◊ R_INF, R_SUP... ♦ FISSURE | ◊ R_INF, R_SUP... ♦ FISSURE |

Advice on the choice of the crowns (in CALC_G_XFEM):

- *Avoid using a theta field defined with a lower radius* R_INF *equal to zero. The displacement fields are singular at the crack front and introduce inaccurate results in fracture-mechanics post-processing.*
- *It is advisable to use the command* CALC_G_XFEM *successively with at least three theta fields of different crowns in order to make sure of the stability of the results. In the event of a significant variation (greater than 5–10%) one must question whether the whole modelling has been properly taken into account.*
- *For the option* *CALC_K_G in 2D-axisymmetric conditions, the radius of the crowns must be small compared with the radius of the crack front in order to have the best possible accuracy. It is forbidden to have crowns of radius greater than the radius of the crack front.*

#### 3.4.1 Operand FISSURE

```text
♦    FISSURE    =    fiss,
```

This operand makes it possible to supply the information relating to the crack defined by the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08).

This keyword is mandatory.

In the few cases where a propagation study is carried out with cohesive elements, this crack is of type COHESIF (keyword TYPE_DISCONTINUITE in DEFI_FISS_XFEM), and the operator then automatically carries out a computation of equivalent stress intensity factors by a specific method of surface integrals over the cohesive zone, detailed in [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19), §6.3.

#### 3.4.2 Operands R_INF, R_INF_FO, R_SUP, R_SUP_FO

These operands make it possible to compute the theta field when it has not been determined beforehand. They correspond respectively to the lower and upper radii of the crowns (a scalar or, in 3D, a function of the curvilinear abscissa).

The two radii may be introduced either by constant real values, which are arguments of the simple keywords R_INF and R_SUP, or by functions of the curvilinear abscissa on the oriented crack front, which are arguments of the simple keywords R_INF_FO and R_SUP_FO.

Some advice is given below.

In 3D, when the radii are not a function of the curvilinear abscissa, the operands R_INF and R_SUP are optional. If they are not indicated, they are computed automatically from the maximum h of the sizes of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the crack front. These mesh-cell sizes at each node of the front are computed in the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM and are present in the concept or fiss_xfem [D4.10.01](doc:d4.10.01). It has been chosen to set R_SUP = 4h and R_INF = 2h. If the automatically computed value is chosen for R_SUP and R_INF, one should nevertheless make sure that these values (displayed in the .mess file) are consistent with the dimensions of the structure.

In the case of a defect that is initially open and whose front is not plane, it is not at present possible to compute the energy release rate.

#### 3.4.3 Operand NUME_FOND

```text
◊    NUME_FOND        =    n,
```

This keyword is optional.

It may happen, for certain structures, that the crack front is discontinuous. In the case of a crack defined by DEFI_FISS_XFEM the crack front is then cut into several parts.

The operand NUME_FOND makes it possible to indicate on which of these sub-parts of the crack front one wishes to carry out the analysis. By default, the analysis is carried out on the first crack front.

#### 3.4.4 Operand NB_POINT_FOND

```text
◊    NB_POINT_FOND    =    nbnofo,
```

This keyword is optional.

By default, the analysis is carried out on all the points of the crack front, that is all the points of intersection between the crack front and the edges of the mesh, for an unmeshed crack. The points of the crack front may be very irregularly spaced, which may lead to troublesome oscillations in the parameters $G(s)$ or $K(s)$ computed.

The operand NB_POINT_FOND makes it possible to fix a priori the number of post-processing points, in order to improve the regularity of the results. The $\mathit{nbnofo}$ points are evenly distributed along the crack front. Some advice is given in § 3.7.

This keyword can be used only for a LAGRANGE-LAGRANGE smoothing.

### 3.5 Keyword COMPORTEMENT

```text
◊    COMPORTEMENT = _F(
```

This factor keyword makes it possible to redefine the constitutive behaviour of the material. But the use of this keyword must **not be systematic**: indeed, by default, the constitutive law used in CALC_G_XFEM is identical to that used for the mechanical analysis (via MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE). Supplying the keyword comportement creates an alarm, but the analysis continues; it is up to the user to check that the constitutive behaviours during the mechanical solution and during the computation of G are identical.

The computation of the energy release rate $G$ has a meaning only in linear or non-linear **elasticity**.

Finally, the only command variable (see [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03): operator AFFE_MATERIAU, keyword AFFE_VARC) allowed for the computation of the energy release rate is the temperature 'TEMP'.

**Notes:**

- Nothing forbids assigning a different constitutive behaviour when computing the displacements (for example elastoplastic) and then carrying out this post-processing with another relation (for example non-linear elastic). A consistency check is carried out on the constitutive behaviours used for the analysis and for the post-processing, and an alarm message is issued if there is a difference; the user is responsible for the interpretation of the results obtained [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03).
- *For example, if the loading is perfectly radial and monotonic, the analyses in non-linear elasticity and in elastoplasticity lead to the same results.*

For further details, refer to [U2.05.01](doc:u2.05.01).

Linear elastic constitutive relation, that is the relation between the strains and the stresses considered is linear [R7.02.01 §1.1].

```text
/    'ELAS_VMIS_TRAC'
```

Non-linear elastic constitutive relation, of Von Mises type with non-linear isotropic hardening. The necessary material data of the material field are supplied in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU (cf. [R7.02.03 §1.1] and [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20)).

```text
/    'ELAS_VMIS_PUIS'
```

Non-linear elastic constitutive relation, of Von Mises type with non-linear isotropic hardening defined by a power law. The necessary material data of the material field are supplied in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU (cf. [R7.02.03 §1.1] and [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20)).

#### 3.5.2 Operand ETAT_INIT

```text
◊    ETAT_INIT=_F(SIGM = siefelga)
```

In the case of an incremental elastic constitutive relation, it is possible to define a non-zero initial stress state.

The use of this keyword requires beforehand the definition of an initial state in the operator STAT_NON_LINE when solving the analysis to be post-processed by CALC_G_XFEM.

STAT_NON_LINE allows this definition in two ways (see u4.51.03):

- with an initial state of result type, supplied under the keyword EVOL_NOLI of the factor keyword ETAT_INIT
- with an initial state of field type, supplied under the keywords SIGM/DEPL/VARI/STRX/COHE of the factor keyword ETAT_INIT

To date, post-processing with CALC_G_XFEM of analyses using the keywords EVOL_NOLI/DEPL/VARI/STRX/COHE is not possible. Only post-processing with CALC_G_XFEM of analyses using the keyword SIGM is possible.

The initial stress field supplied may be of type SIEF_ELGA only (with the possibility of creating them from CREA_CHAMP in particular).

In every case, this initial stress field must be **self-equilibrated**, in the absence of a crack, with the boundary conditions alone. The user must check that the initial stress field is admissible by applying it in the keyword ETAT_INIT of the operator STAT_NON_LINE, with a linear elastic constitutive behaviour (RELATION = 'ELAS'), with the boundary conditions alone; the mechanical result must be the same stress field without additional strains (see ).

Given the difficulty of validating the formulation that has been implemented, it is not at present admissible to combine a pre-strain (*via* the keyword PRE_EPSI of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA) and an initial stress.

#### 3.5.3 Operand DEFORMATION

This keyword makes it possible to define the assumptions used for the computation of the strains. For further details on the strain formalisms, see the paragraph DEFORMATION of [U4.51,11].

To begin with, the quantities computed by CALC_G_XFEM are defined only in small strains. It is therefore not possible to use for CALC_G_XFEM a formalism other than PETIT, GROT_GDEP or PETIT_REAC. If this is the case, the analysis stops in error.

Next, there must in theory be consistency between the strain formalisms used for the mechanical analysis itself and the post-processing in question here. This would therefore mean that the mechanical analyses themselves must be carried out with the formalisms PETIT, GROT_GDEP or PETIT_REAC only. However, the possibility is left of carrying out the post-processing in small strains (PETIT) from the result of a mechanical analysis carried out with another formalism (for example GDEF_LOG). In this case, an alarm is issued, and it is up to the user to decide whether the result supplied has a meaning or not. In order to carry out a more reliable computation of the energy release rate in large strains, it is advisable to use the crack-opening equivalence method presented briefly in U2.05.01.

```text
◊    DEFORMATION =

/    'PETIT'    : les déformations utilisées dans la relation de comportement sont les relations linéarisées. Cela signifie que l'on reste en Hypothèse de Petites Perturbations: petits déplacements, petites rotations et petites déformations. Dans ce cas, les calculs des grandeurs sont licites et validés. Cette option est la seule possible pour les fissures non maillées.

/    'GROT_GDEP'    : Permet de traiter les grandes rotations et les grands déplacements, mais en restant en petites déformations

/    'PETIT_REAC'    : disponible uniquement en comportement incrémental, c'est une approximation des grandes déformations pour laquelle les incréments de déformations sont calculés dans la géométrie actuelle (réactualisée). Elle n'est valable que pour de petits incréments et pour des rotations faibles [:ref:`U4.51.11 <U4.51.11>`].
```

### 3.6 Operand OPTION

```text
♦    OPTION    =    /    'CALC_G'    ,
/    'CALC_K_G',
```

#### 3.6.1 OPTION = 'CALC_G' [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) and [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03)

It makes it possible to compute the energy release rate $G$ by the theta method in 2D or in local 3D for a linear or non-linear thermoelastic problem.

In 2D, for the AXIS modelling, the result obtained must be divided by the radius at the crack front, cf. § 4.2.

#### 3.6.2 OPTION = 'CALC_K_G' [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05)

This option computes **in 2D and in 3D** the energy release rate $G$ and the stress intensity factors ${K}_{1}$, ${K}_{2}$ and ${K}_{3}$ in plane linear thermoelasticity by the singular-field method (use of the bilinear form of $G$, [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05)).

**Notes:**

- *For this option, only linear elastic analyses without an initial state, or linear elastic analyses with an initial stress, are available.*
- *For this option in 2D, if* INFO *is equal to 2, the computation and the printout (in the file* *MESSAGE) of the propagation angle of the crack are generated. This angle, computed according to 3 criteria (* $\mathrm{K1}$ *or* $G$ *maximum,* $\mathrm{K2}$ minimum) according to the formulae of AMESTOY, BUI and DANG-VAN [R7.02.05 §2.5], is given to within 10 degrees.

### 3.7 Keyword LISSAGE

> The domain of application of this keyword is limited to the local 3D case.

#### 3.7.1 Operand LISSAGE_THETA

```text
◊     LISSAGE_THETA=    /    'LEGENDRE'            [DEFAUT]
/    'LAGRANGE'
```

The trace of the theta field on the crack front may be discretised either according to the basis of the $N$ first Legendre polynomials ('LEGENDRE'), or according to the shape functions associated with the discretisation of the crack front ('LAGRANGE') [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01).

LISSAGE_THETA='LEGENDRE': $\theta (s)$ is discretised on a basis of Legendre polynomials ${\gamma}_{j}(s)$ of degree $j$ ($0<j<{\mathit{Deg}}_{max}$) where ${\mathit{Deg}}_{max}$ is the maximum degree given under the keyword DEGRE (between 0 and 7).

LISSAGE_THETA='LAGRANGE': $\theta (s)$ is discretised on the shape functions of node $k$ of the crack front: ${\phi }_{k}(s)$.

#### 3.7.2 Operand LISSAGE_G

```text
LISSAGE_G =        /    'LEGENDRE'    ,            [DEFAUT]
/    'LAGRANGE'    ,
/    'LAGRANGE_NO_NO'
```

$G(s)$ may be discretised either according to the Legendre polynomials ('LEGENDRE'), or according to the shape functions of the nodes of the crack front ('LAGRANGE'). The method 'LAGRANGE_NO_NO' comes from the LAGRANGE-LAGRANGE method but it is simplified [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01).

If smoothing of theta by Legendre polynomials has been retained at the previous keyword, then the smoothing of $G$ must also be of Legendre type.

The options available in *Aster* are summarised in the following table:

|  |  | **Theta** |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  |  | **LEGENDRE polynomials** | **Shape functions** |
| $G(s)$ | **LEGENDRE polynomials** | LISSAGE_THETA= 'LEGENDRE' LISSAGE_G= 'LEGENDRE' |  |
| **Shape functions** |  | LISSAGE_THETA= 'LAGRANGE' LISSAGE_G= 'LAGRANGE' or 'LAGRANGE_NO_NO' |  |

#### 3.7.3 Operand DEGRE

```text
◊    DEGRE    =    n
```

$n$ is the maximum degree of the Legendre polynomials used for the decomposition of the field $\theta$ on the crack front [§3.12] (when LISSAGE_THETA ='LEGENDRE').

By default $n$ is set to 5. The value of $n$ must lie between 0 and 7.

If the discretisations LISSAGE_THETA ='LAGRANGE' and LISSAGE_G = 'LEGENDRE' are retained, one must have $n<\mathrm{NNO}$, where $\mathrm{NNO}$ is the number of nodes on the crack front [R7.02.01 §2.3].

**Advice on smoothing:**

- *it is difficult to give a preference to one or the other smoothing method. In principle the two give equivalent numerical results. Nevertheless smoothing of type* 'LAGRANGE' *is a little more costly in CPU time than smoothing of type* 'LEGENDRE'*;
- *smoothing of type* *'LEGENDRE' is sensitive to the maximum degree of the polynomials chosen. The maximum degree must be defined as a function of the number of nodes on the crack front* *NNO. If* $n$ *is too large with respect to* $\mathit{NNO}$ the results are poor [U2.05.01 §2.4];
- *oscillations may appear with smoothing of type* *'LAGRANGE', in particular if the mesh comprises quadratic elements or if the crack is not meshed. If the mesh is radiating at the crack front (meshed crack), it is then recommended to define crowns* *R_INF and* *R_SUP coinciding with the boundaries of the elements. A smoothing of type* *'LAGRANGE_NO_NO' makes it possible to limit these oscillations;*
- *for meshed and unmeshed cracks, when smoothing of type* 'LAGRANGE' *is used it is recommended to use the operand* NB_POINT_FOND *in order to guarantee an even distribution of the computation points on the crack front. The choice of a ratio of the order of 5 between the total number of points on the crack front (to be looked for in the information printed in the message file by the command* DEFI_FISS_XFEM *) and the number of computation points seems appropriate in order to limit the oscillations;*
- *the use of at least two types of smoothing with several integration crowns and the comparison of the results is* **indispensable** *in order to check the validity of the model.*

${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}$ is the energy release rate obtained from the stress intensity factors ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ (and ${K}_{3}$) with the following formulae:

> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$ in plane stress
> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$ in plane strain and axisymmetric conditions
> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})+\frac{{K}_{\mathrm{III}}^{2}}{2\mu }$ in 3D

with $E$ Young's modulus and $\nu$ Poisson's ratio and $\mu =\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$. The comparison between $G$ and ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}$ makes it possible to make sure of the consistency of the results: too large a discrepancy must lead one to check the parameters of the analysis (refinement of the mesh, choice of the crowns for theta, smoothing in 3D...).

The command IMPR_TABLE [U4.91.03](doc:u4.91.03) makes it possible to print the results in the desired format.

## 4 Normalisation of the energy release rate G

### 4.1 2D plane stress and plane strain

In dimension 2 (plane stress and plane strain), the crack front is reduced to a point and the value $G(\theta )$ coming from the command CALC_G_XFEM is independent of the choice of the field $\theta$:

> $G=G(\theta )\forall \theta \in \Theta$

### 4.2 Axisymmetry

In axisymmetric conditions the value $G(\theta )$ obtained with *Aster* for the option CALC_G must be normalised:

> $G=\frac{1}{R}G(\theta )$

where $R$ is the distance from the crack front to the axis of symmetry [R7.02.01 §2.4.4].

For the option CALC_K_G, the values of $G$ and of $K$ supplied in the result table are directly the local values; they must therefore not be normalised.

### 4.3 Symmetry of the model

If only half of the solid is modelled with respect to the crack:

- either specify the keyword SYME='OUI' in the commands concerned;
- or do not forget to multiply by 2 the values of the energy release rate $G$ or $G(s)$, and by 4 those of ${G}_{\mathrm{Irwin}}$*.* In addition the values of the stress intensity factors corresponding to the symmetry mode must also be multiplied by 2.
