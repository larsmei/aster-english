# r4.20.01 Indicators of unloading and of loss of proportionality of the loading in elastoplasticity

**Summary**

A set of scalar parameters, called indicators, is presented, making it possible to assess an unloading or a loss of proportionality of a loading in the course of its history. Two types of indicator are proposed: indicators in the form of scalar fields, making it possible to detect the zones of the structure undergoing unloadings or non-radial loadings, and global indicators integrated over a zone of the structure chosen by the user. The latter are intended more specifically for assessing the validity of the energy release rate in non-linear fracture mechanics.

The indicators described in this document are available:

- for the local indicators, under the command CALC_CHAMP, options DERA_ELGA and DERA_ELNO;
- for the global indicators, under the command POST_ELEM, options INDIC_SEUIL and INDIC_ENER.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Definition of a proportional loading

Considering a structure subjected to a thermo-mechanical loading over the time interval $[0,t]$, this loading is said to be proportional (or, equivalently, radial) at the material point $p$ if the stress field represented at that point by the tensor $\sigma$ is proportional to a tensor independent of the time instant under consideration, the coefficient of proportionality being a monotonic function of time. Formally, this is expressed by:

$\forall \tau \left[0,t\right],\sigma (P,\tau )=\alpha (\tau ){\sigma}_{0}(P),\alpha (\tau )>0$ a monotonic function on $[0,t]$.

This definition implies, in particular, that the principal directions of the stresses remain constant, at the point under consideration, throughout the loading path (these directions may of course vary from one point to another).

### 1.2 Importance of proportional loading and usefulness of indicators

For plastic materials, the mechanical fields depend on the whole history elapsed along the loading path. The flow rules therefore have an incremental character, and their integration depends on each loading case. A notable exception is precisely proportional loading, for which the flow rule can be integrated once and for all. For example, the Prandtl–Reuss plasticity law based on the von Mises criterion can be replaced by a non-linear elastic law (called the Hencky–Mises law). Strictly proportional loading cases are fairly rare. Indeed, a large number of conditions must be satisfied in order to realise such a case [bib1], and these are seldom verified for industrial structures. It may even be said that, for structures presenting geometric defects such as cracks, these conditions are never strictly verified.

When the loadings are multiaxial, cyclic, or transient thermo-mechanical, certain portions of the loading path may be strongly non-proportional. It is then useful to locate these portions and to assess the extent of the loss of proportionality, in order for example to adjust the time discretisation of the elastoplastic problem for the portion under consideration, or to gauge the validity of certain post-processings (in fracture mechanics, for example).

### 1.3 Different types of indicator of loss of proportionality

It seems difficult to define a single, simple quantity (grandeur) which could detect both spatial zones of loss of proportionality and portions of the loading path (temporal zones) at a material point. This is why scalar quantities are proposed, each with its own specificity: two, defined by fields measuring at each point the unloading and the deviation of the stresses between two time steps (local indicators), and two others of a more global nature, characterising, in a given zone of the structure, a non-proportional loading history.

**Note:**

*These indicators are closely linked to the time discretisation of the problem. In particular, if this discretisation is too coarse, the unloading or the loss of radiality occurring during the time increment may very well not be detected.*

### 1.4 Indicators of elastoplastic unloading

Another application of the unloading indicators consists in alerting the user if, in the event of significant unloading, the choice of a kinematic hardening would provide a solution very different from the isotropic hardening that was used (cf. CR-AMA-11.035 [3]).

This is of practical interest: in several studies, the behaviour very often chosen “by default” (because often only a tensile curve is available) is VMIS_ISOT_TRAC. Now, if local unloadings are possible, this may typically lead to overestimating the stresses (at imposed strain) or underestimating the strains (at imposed stress). It therefore seems relevant to warn the user if, when laws of von Mises with isotropic hardening have been used, there is a risk of obtaining incorrect results once the unloading becomes too large (and therefore no longer remains within the initial elastic domain). This may indicate that constitutive laws with kinematic hardening should be used (VMIS_CINE_LINE, VMIS_ECMI*, VMIS_CIN1_CHAB, etc.).

## 2 Local indicators

The aim of these indicators is to identify the zones of the structure where, at a particular time instant, either an unloading or a loss of radiality of the stress field occurs. They are produced in the post-processing of a static or dynamic analysis, 2D or 3D, using an elastic or non-elastic constitutive law. They take the form of fields of scalars, which may be examined by plotting their isovalues with a graphical post-processor.

### 2.1 Unloading indicators

#### 2.1.1 Local indicator of total unloading

This indicator measures, at the point $M$ and between the time instants $t$ and $t+\Delta t$, the relative variation of the norm of the stresses in the von Mises sense. It is written formally: ${I}_{1}=\frac{\parallel \sigma (M,t+\Delta t)\parallel -\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel }{\parallel \sigma (M,t+\Delta t)\parallel }$. This quantity is negative in the event of local unloading at the point M. The norm $\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel$ may be written in four different ways, according to the choice of the modeller:

1. $\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\sigma}^{D}.{\sigma}^{D}}$ , where ${\sigma}^{D}$ is the deviatoric part of the stress tensor (this norm is useful in plasticity with isotropic hardening).
2. $\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\sigma .\sigma }$ , where the whole of the stress tensor is considered, in order to detect for example decreases in the hydrostatic pressure.
3. $\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}({\sigma}^{D}-X).({\sigma}^{D}-X)}$ , with $X$ the back-stress tensor in the case of an elastoplastic law with kinematic hardening.
4. $\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}(\sigma -X).(\sigma -X)}$

#### 2.1.2 Local indicator of elastoplastic unloading

This indicator makes it possible to determine whether the unloading remains elastic or whether there would be a risk of plastification if a purely kinematic hardening were used. It is an “extreme” indicator, given that, for metals, isotropic and kinematic hardenings are both present.

The option DERA_ELGA therefore computes (in addition to the components DCHA_V and DCHA_T) the components:

I_decha=IND_DCHA :

- IND_DCHA=0 initial value with no stress.
- IND_DCHA=1 if elastic loading
- IND_DCHA=2 if plastic loading
- IND_DCHA=-1 if admissible elastic unloading (whatever the type of hardening)
- IND_DCHA=-2 if improper unloading (plastification would have occurred with a kinematic hardening).

VAL_DCHA: indicates the proportion by which the criterion is left (see below).

The procedure is as follows: for the laws VMIS_ISOT* only, at each integration point, at each time instant $t$, from the stress tensor $\sigma (t)$, the accumulated equivalent plastic strain $p(t)$, and the isotropic hardening curve $R(p(t))$,

- Initialisation: IND_DCHA=0, VAL_DCHA=0.
- as long as $p(t)=0$, IND_DCHA=1 (elasticity),
- if IND_DCHA=-2 (improper-unloading criterion reached), nothing further is done
- if $\Delta p(t)>0$:
- if one is loading, that is if the angle between the stress-increment tensor and the total-stress tensor is “small”: $\frac{\tilde{\sigma}(t-\Delta t)\cdot \Delta \tilde{\sigma}}{∣∣\tilde{\sigma}(t-\Delta t)∣∣∣∣\Delta \tilde{\sigma}∣∣}>0$ IND_DCHA=2; (points between A and B on the figure).
- if one is unloading, IND_DCHA=-2 (rare case: this means that, within one time step, the yield surface is crossed in a direction remote from that of the previous step)
- if $\Delta p(t)=0$:
- if IND_DCHA>-1, the tensor $X$ is computed (centre of the elastic domain if one were in pure kinematic hardening, that is if the initial yield surface, represented by a circle in the deviatoric plane, had translated up to the current point) by:

$X=\sigma (t)\frac{R(p)-R(0)}{R(p)}$

- if IND_DCHA=-1, the tensor $X$ already computed is used.
- the “kinematic” criterion ${(\sigma (t)-X)}_{\mathrm{eq}}\le R(0)$ is computed
- If this criterion is satisfied, then the unloading would also be elastic with a kinematic hardening, and it is therefore “admissible”: IND_DCHA=-1 (any point between B and E on figure 2). In order to apply the criterion at the next time instant, $X$ is stored (6 components)
- otherwise, IND_DCHA=-2 and this value is retained (because the remainder of the analysis would be modified if the hardening were kinematic). (points between E and F, or between C and C' on the following figure). $\text{VAL\_DCHA}=\frac{∥\sigma -X∥}{{R}_{0}}$

### 2.2 Indicators of loss of radiality

#### 2.2.1 Local indicator of radiality of the loading

This indicator measures, at the point $M$ and between the time instants $t$ and $t+\Delta t$, the change in the direction of the stresses. It is written:

${I}_{2}=1-\frac{\mid \sigma (M,t).\Delta \sigma \mid }{\parallel \sigma (M,t)\parallel \parallel \Delta \sigma \parallel }$ , where the scalar product “.” is associated with one of the four preceding norms. This quantity is zero when radiality is preserved during the time increment. This criterion may also be interpreted as the quantity $1-\cos(\theta )$, where $\theta$ is the angle between $\sigma$ and $\Delta \sigma$. This real-valued indicator ranges between 0 for radial loadings and 1. It is useful in particular for determining the validity of an elastoplastic solution in fracture mechanics.

#### 2.2.2 Indicator of the error due to the time discretisation

It provides a measure of the error $\eta$ due to the discretisation in time, directly related to the rotation of the normal to the yield surface. The angle between ${n}^{-}$, the normal to the plasticity criterion at the beginning of the time step (time instant t-), and ${n}^{+}$, the normal to the plasticity criterion computed at the end of the time step, is computed as follows: ${I}_{\eta}=\frac{1}{2}∣∣\Delta n∣∣=\frac{1}{2}∣∣{n}^{+}-{n}^{-}∣∣=∣\sin(\frac{\alpha}{2})∣$. This indicator is directly linked to the norm of the variation of the normal to the plasticity convex (this can easily be generalised to any elastoplastic law with normal flow), and it may also be interpreted as the sine of half the angle between the two normals. This provides a measure of the error (see [4]). This criterion is operational for the elastoplastic von Mises behaviours with isotropic, linear kinematic and mixed hardening. It may be used to drive the automatic subdivision of the time step.

## 3 Global indicators

These indicators are intended to detect whether, in the course of the history of the structure and up to the current time instant $t$, and for a zone of the structure chosen by the modeller, there has been a loss of proportionality of the loading (these indicators therefore leave a trace of the history, unlike the local indicators, which are instantaneous). They can be used only in the framework of an elastoplastic behaviour with isotropic hardening (in 2D or 3D).

### 3.1 Indicator on the plasticity parameters

This quantity makes it possible, in the case of von Mises plasticity with isotropic hardening, on the one hand to know (on average over a zone ${\Omega}_{S}$ of the domain $\Omega$) whether the stresses and the plastic strains have the same directions and whether the plastic threshold is reached at the current time instant, and on the other hand whether, in the course of the history, the plastic strain has changed direction. This quantity is written:

${I}_{3}=\frac{1}{{\Omega}_{S}}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{S}}(1-\frac{\mid \sigma .{\varepsilon}^{p}\mid }{({\sigma}_{Y}+R(p))p})d\Omega$ ,

where ${\sigma}_{Y}$ is the initial plastic threshold, $R$ the extension of the yield surface associated with hardening, and $p$ the accumulated plastic strain. The scalar product is associated with the norm in the von Mises sense. This indicator is normalised and has a value lying between zero and one. It is zero if the loading has retained its proportional character at every point of ${\Omega}_{S}$ throughout the elapsed history.

**Note 1:**

The indicator is not affected if, in the course of the history, there have been unloadings followed by elastic reloadings without a change in the direction of the stresses when one returns to the threshold (cf. [Figure 3.1-a]).

**Note 2:**

*Three ingredients linked to plasticity enter the formulation of this indicator:*

- *the discrepancy between the direction of the current stresses and that of the current plastic strains (* $\sigma .\varepsilon p\ne \parallel \sigma \parallel \parallel {\varepsilon}^{p}\parallel$ *),*
- *the position of the stresses relative to the current threshold (* $\parallel \sigma \parallel \le ({\sigma}_{Y}+R(p))$ *),*
- *the discrepancy between the norm of the current plastic strain and the accumulated plastic strain (* $\parallel {\varepsilon}^{p}\parallel \ne p$ *).*

A loss of proportionality may have occurred in the course of the history without the indicator detecting it through the first two ingredients (that is, at the end of the loading one may have coincidence of the directions of the stresses and of the plastic strains, and be on the plastic threshold). On the other hand, one will have $\parallel {\varepsilon}^{p}\parallel \ne p$, and the indicator will necessarily be greater than zero; the user will therefore be warned of the loss of proportionality.

**Note 3:**

*If the indicator necessarily detects a loss of proportionality in a zone, in practice that zone must contain sufficiently many material points with non-proportional loading. Indeed, if a very large zone is chosen with few points concerned, the normalisation of the indicator, carried out by dividing by the volume of the zone, implies a certain “crushing” towards zero of the value of the quantity. Typically, for a structure containing a defect which is a source of non-proportionality, it is advantageous to choose an integration zone* ${\Omega}_{S}$ *surrounding the defect with a small neighbourhood, in order to obtain a significant value of the indicator.*

### 3.2 Energy indicator

This indicator has the same function as the previous one, but is based on the energy density. It is written:

${I}_{4}=\frac{1}{{\Omega}_{S}}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{S}}(1-\frac{\psi}{\omega})d\Omega$ ,

where $\omega$ is the strain-energy density defined by: $\omega (t)={\int}_{0}^{t}\sigma .\dot{\varepsilon}d\tau$, and $\psi$ is the elastic energy density associated with the tensile curve if the material were considered as non-linear elastic. More precisely, this quantity is written:

$\begin{array}{}\psi (\varepsilon (t))=\frac{1}{2}{\text{Ktr}}^{2}(\varepsilon )+\frac{2\mu }{3}{\parallel \varepsilon \parallel }^{2},\text{si}\parallel \sigma \parallel <({\sigma}_{Y}+R(p)),\\ \psi (\varepsilon (t))=\frac{1}{2}{\text{Ktr}}^{2}(\varepsilon )+\frac{{R}^{2}(P)}{6\mu }+{\int}_{0}^{P}R(q)\text{dq},\text{si}\parallel \sigma \parallel =({\sigma}_{Y}+R(p)),\end{array}$

with $K$ the bulk modulus, $\mu$ the Lamé shear coefficient, $R$ the threshold of the tensile curve associated with the norm of the plastic strain $P=\parallel {\varepsilon}^{p}\parallel$ (this may differ from the true plastic threshold, because $P\ne p$ if the loading is non-proportional). This indicator is likewise normalised between 0 and 1. It is zero for a loading which has always retained its proportional character $(\psi \ne \omega )$.

## 4 Functionalities and verification

The indicators presented here may be used in the post-processing of a mechanical analysis, and are available for the finite elements of continua in 2D (plane-strain, plane-stress or axisymmetric mode, triangular or quadrilateral mesh cells) or 3D (hexahedral, tetrahedral, pentahedral or pyramid mesh cells). Line elements, beams, plates and shells are excluded from this application.

### 4.1 Local indicators

These indicators are accessible after a static or dynamic analysis, whatever the constitutive law of the material. The operator CALC_CHAMP provides the options 'DERA_ELGA' and 'DERA_ELNO' for the unloading indicator ${I}_{1}$ and the loss-of-radiality indicator ${I}_{2}$, evaluated at the nodes or at the Gauss points of the element [2]. In the general case, the first two norms described in paragraph 2.1 are computed, the last two being used only if an elastoplastic analysis with kinematic hardening has previously been carried out.

The components of the field thus computed are:

- DCHA_T: the unloading indicator ${I}_{1}$ computed from the stress tensor;
- DCHA_V: the unloading indicator ${I}_{1}$ computed from the stress deviator;
- IND_DCHA: the unloading indicator ${I}_{\mathrm{decha}}$
- IND_DCHA=0 initial value with no stress
- IND_DCHA=1 if elastic loading
- IND_DCHA=2 if plastic loading
- IND_DCHA=-1 if admissible elastic unloading (whatever the type of hardening)
- IND_DCHA=-2 if improper unloading (plastification would have occurred with a kinematic hardening).
- VAL_DCHA: the indicator of the proportion by which the criterion is left
- RADI_V: the loss-of-radiality indicator ${I}_{2}$ computed from the stress deviator.
- ERR_RADI: the error indicator ${I}_{\eta}$ due to the time discretisation

### 4.2 Global indicators

These indicators are accessible only after an elastoplastic analysis with isotropic hardening. The operator POST_ELEM provides the options ‘INDIC_SEUIL’ and ‘INDIC_ENER’, corresponding respectively to the global indicators ${I}_{3}$ and ${I}_{4}$. These are evaluated on a mesh-cell group previously defined by the user (for example by the command DEFI_GROUP).
