# r7.04.01 Estimation of the life in high-cycle fatigue and in low-cycle fatigue

**Summary:**

Most industrial structures are subjected to loadings which vary with time and which, repeated a large number of times, can lead to their failure by fatigue. This note presents the principal functionalities of the commands POST_FATIGUE [U4.83.01](doc:u4.83.01) and/or CALC_FATIGUE [U4.83.02](doc:u4.83.02) and/or CALC_CHAMP [U4.81.04](doc:u4.81.04), which make it possible to estimate the endurance limit and the damage accumulation of a component.

The various methods available are:

- linear accumulation: methods based on uniaxial tests (Wöhler, Manson-Coffin

and Taheri methods).

These methods have in common the determination of a damage value from the evolution over time of a **scalar** component characterising, for the damage computation, the stress or strain amplitude of the structure.

To this end, one must extract, by a cycle-counting method, the elementary loading cycles undergone by the structure, determine the elementary damage associated with each cycle and determine the total damage by a linear accumulation rule;

- non-linear accumulation: Lemaître method and Lemaître-Sermage method

These methods make it possible to compute the damage $D$ at each time instant $t$, from the data of the stress tensor $\sigma (t)$ and of the cumulative plastic strain $p(t)$;

- endurance limit: Crossland and Dang Van Papadopoulos criteria

These criteria apply to uniaxial or multiaxial loadings in **periodic** stresses. They supply a criterion value indicating whether or not there is fatigue. The equivalent stresses defined for these criteria can also be used to compute the damage accumulation.

## 1 Introduction

Industrial experience shows that the failures of machine parts or of structures in normal operation are most often due to fatigue. Its masked progressive character very often leads to a sudden failure.

By fatigue one means the modification of the properties of materials consequent upon the application of cycles of forces, cycles whose repetition can lead to the failure of the parts made with these materials [bib1].

Various methods are available for the evaluation of the damage. The second part of this document is devoted to the presentation of the oldest ones, which are methods based on uniaxial tests: the Wöhler method, the Manson-Coffin method and, more recently, methods proposed by S. Taheri (EDF-R&D/AMA).

These methods have in common the determination of a damage value from the evolution over time of a scalar component characterising, for the damage computation, the stress or strain state of the structure.

The evaluation of the damage is based on the use of fatigue curves of the material (Wöhler or Manson-Coffin), associating a stress variation of given amplitude with an admissible number of cycles.

In order to use these curves from a real uniaxial loading, it is necessary to process the stress or strain history by identifying elementary cycles (cf. [§2.2]).

The difficulty of defining a cycle for a complex signal explains the profusion of counting methods which have appeared in the literature [bib2].

Two methods among the most commonly used have been introduced into *Code_Aster*:

- cascade range counting, or the RAINFLOW method,
- the RCC_M rule.

One adds to them a third method which we shall call the “natural” counting method and which respects the order of application of the loading cycles.

For each elementary cycle, one evaluates an elementary damage with the aid of methods based on the Wöhler curves, the Manson-Coffin curves, or both simultaneously.

For the Wöhler method (cf. [§2.3]) the user can correct the stress to be entered into the Wöhler curve by:

- a stress concentration factor ${K}_{T}$, in order to take into account the geometry of the part,
- an elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$,
- a Goodman or Gerber correction in the Haigh diagram in order to take into account the mean stress of the cycle.

On the other hand, one proposes to define the Wöhler curve in three different forms, a form discretised point by point and two analytical forms.

The Manson-Coffin method (cf. [§2.4]) applies to strain loadings. The Manson-Coffin curve is defined in a single form, a form discretised point by point.

The Taheri methods (cf. [§2.5]) likewise apply to strain loadings and require the data of the Manson-Coffin curve and possibly of the Wöhler curve. Their particularity is to take into account the order of application of the elementary loading cycles to the structure, unlike the other two methods.

**Note:**

*Three methods of extraction of the elementary cycles are available: the Rainflow method, the RCC_M rule and “natural” counting.*

*The first two methods do not take into account the order of application of the cycles, which is of no importance for the damage computation by the Wöhler or Manson-Coffin methods.*

For the damage computation by the Taheri methods, one must use the method of extraction of the cycles by “natural” counting [§2.2.3], which respects the order of application of the cycles.

For all of these methods the computation of the total damage undergone by the structure is determined by an accumulation method, the Miner rule.

The third part of this document presents the Lemaître and Lemaître-Sermage methods, which are “analytical” methods making it possible to compute the damage $D$ (at each time instant $t$) from the data of the stress tensor $\sigma (t)$ and of the cumulative plastic strain $p(t)$. These two methods apply to arbitrary stress loadings (uniaxial or multiaxial).

A linear accumulation rule can be used in order to determine the total damage undergone by the structure.

Finally, the Crossland and Dang Van Papadopoulos criteria are presented in the fourth and last part of this document. They apply to arbitrary loadings (uniaxial or multiaxial) in stresses and periodic. They supply a criterion value indicating whether or not there is fatigue.

From the value of the criterion, one can specify a scalar component characterising the state of the structure for the damage computation and determine a damage value by using the Wöhler curve of the material.

## 2 Wöhler, Manson-Coffin and Taheri methods

### 2.1 Extraction of the peaks

The user supplies to *Code_Aster* a function which defines the (scalar) history of the loading at a given point. For this, the keyword HISTOIRE is available.

On this loading history, which may be complex, a first operation of extraction of the peaks is carried out. This operation consists in reducing the loading history to the fundamental peaks alone.

**Note:**

*In fatigue, one calls loading at a given point the value of the response of the structure at that point.*

*In the use of the Wöhler curves, it is a stress at that point.*

*In the use of the Manson-Coffin curves, it is a strain at that point.*

*The loading history is therefore the evolution over time of a stress, or of a strain.*

If the function remains increasing or decreasing over more than two consecutive points, one removes the intermediate points in order to keep only the two extreme points.

One also removes from the loading history the points for which the variation of the value of the stress or of the strain is less than a certain level chosen by the user. This amounts to applying a filter to the loading history. The value of the filter level is introduced by the user under the keyword DELTA_OSCI.

### 2.2 Cycle-counting methods

During their life, industrial structures are generally subjected to complex loadings whose loading levels are variable.

The cycle-counting methods make it possible to extract from the loading history elementary cycles as a function of various criteria.

*Code_Aster* proposes three distinct methods, of which two non-statistical methods are among the most commonly used.

#### 2.2.1 RAINFLOW method

The cascade range-counting method, more often called the RAINFLOW method, defines cycles which physically correspond to hysteresis loops in the stress-strain plane. In the literature, several variants of this method are listed.

The algorithm implemented in *Code_Aster* is essentially that proposed by the AFNOR recommendation A 03-406 of November 1993 [bib3] (with particularities which are specified during the presentation of the detail of the algorithm) and breaks down into three stages:

- A **first stage** which consists in rearranging the loading history $\sigma (t)$ or $\varepsilon (t)$ so that the loading begins with the maximum value, in absolute value, of the loading.

**Note:**

In the AFNOR recommendation A 03-406, no mention is made of a rearrangement of the loading history. This rearrangement is nevertheless carried out in the software POSTDAM [bib2] and taken up in Code_Aster.

- The **second stage** consists in extracting the elementary cycles from the loading history thus rearranged.

The method consists in relying on four successive points of the loading history $(\text{ch}(i),i=1,\text{Nbpoint})$.

One writes:

$\begin{array}{}X=\mid \text{ch}(i+1)-\text{ch}(i)\mid \text{et}Y=\mid \text{ch}(i+2)-\text{ch}(i+1)\mid \\ \text{et}Z=\mid \text{ch}(i+3)-\text{ch}(i+2)\mid .\end{array}$

As long as $Y$ is strictly greater than $X$ or than $Z$, one runs through the loading history by moving one point towards the right (which amounts to incrementing the value of $i$).

As soon as $Y$ is less than or equal to $X$ and less than or equal to $Z$, one considers that one has encountered an elementary cycle which is defined by the two points $(i+1)$ and $(i+2)$. The amplitude of the cycle is given by $\Delta \text{ch}=\mid \text{ch}(i+1)-\text{ch}(i+2)\mid$.

When the cycle is extracted one removes the two points from the loading history and one continues the algorithm.

- The **third stage** consists in treating the residue, that is to say the loading history remaining after the cycle-extraction stage.

To this end, one adds the same residue after itself, subject possibly to certain precautions at the junction according to the values of the extrema considered as well as the value of the first and of the last slope of the residue.

After the last point of the residue comes the first point of the cycle. As a result, the points considered may no longer appear as extrema. In the case where this occurs, they should be eliminated. One encounters eight different cases. In order to treat them explicitly, let us call ${R}_{1}$ and ${R}_{2}$ the first two points of the residue and ${R}_{n-1}$ and ${R}_{n}$ its last two points.

**Notes:**

*The AFNOR recommendation A 03-406 also mentions a possible pre-processing of the signal, which would consist in a filtering of the signal (removal of the parasites) and in a quantisation of the loading history.*

The filtering of the signal is possible, at the user's request (see [§2.1]. Extraction of the peaks).

*The quantisation of the signal can be useful for the speed of exploitation of the results of the fatigue analysis. In practice, the quantisation of the signal consists in cutting the maximum range of the signal into classes of intervals of constant width called steps, and bringing back to a representative value of a given class (its mean value in general) all the values situated in this class. This possibility of pre-processing of the signal, for its part, is not available in Code_Aster.*

*In the special case where the loading history is constant (for example, an applied mean loading), Code_Aster will count the whole loading history as a cycle of zero amplitude.*

#### 2.2.2 RCC_M method

This method consists in forming the elementary loading cycles by beginning with those which cause the greatest variations.

Thus, for a loading history comprising $N$ points, one determines $N/2$ elementary cycles if $N$ is even and $N/2+1$ if $N$ is odd.

The algorithm breaks down into two stages. The first stage consists in ordering the loading history from the smallest to the greatest value of the stress, or of the strain.

The second stage consists, for its part, in forming the elementary cycles with the greatest variation of the value of the stress, or of the strain.

On the rearranged loading history $\text{ch}(t)$, the elementary cycles are defined by:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}\text{VALMAX}={\text{ch}}_{N+1-i}& & \text{pour}i=1,\text{}N/2\\ \text{VALMIN}\text{}=\text{}{\text{ch}}_{i}& & \end{array}$

If $N$ is odd one determines an additional cycle defined by:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}\text{VALMAX}={\text{ch}}_{N/2+1}& & \text{si}{\text{ch}}_{N/2+1}>{\text{ch}}_{m}\\ \text{VALMIN}=–{\text{ch}}_{N/2+1}+2\ast {\text{ch}}_{m}& & \end{array}$

and

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}\text{VALMAX}={\text{ch}}_{N/2+1}& & \text{sinon}\\ \text{VALMIN}=–{\text{ch}}_{N/2+1}+2\ast {\text{ch}}_{m}& & \end{array}$

where ${\text{ch}}_{m}=$ mean stress or mean strain of the loading = $\frac{1}{N}\sum_{1}^{N}{\text{ch}}_{i}$.

#### 2.2.3 “Natural” method

This method consists in generating the cycles in the order of their appearance in the loading history.

Thus, for a loading history of $N+1$ points, one determines $N/2$ elementary cycles if $N$ is even and $N/2+1$ elementary cycles if $N$ is odd.

The method consists in relying on three successive points of the loading history.

One writes $X=\mid \text{ch}(i+1)-\text{ch}(i)\mid$ and $Y=\mid \text{ch}(i+2)-\text{ch}(i+1)\mid$.

If $X\ge Y$ one considers that one has encountered an elementary cycle which is defined by the two points $(i)$ and $(i+1)$.

The amplitude of the cycle is given by $\Delta \text{ch}=\mid \text{ch}(i+1)-\text{ch}(i)\mid$.

If $X<Y$ one considers that one has encountered an elementary cycle which is defined by the two points $(i+1)$ and $(i+2)$.

The amplitude of the cycle is given by $\Delta \text{ch}=\mid \text{ch}(i+2)-\text{ch}(i+1)\mid$.

When the cycle is extracted one removes the two points $(i)$ and $(i+1)$ from the loading history and one continues the algorithm.

If the number of points $(N+1)$ of the loading history is odd, the algorithm described previously makes it possible to treat all the points.

If the number of points $(N+1)$ of the loading history is even, it remains to treat the two remaining points.

One considers that these two points form a cycle defined by the two points $N$ and $(N+1)$. The amplitude of the cycle is given by $\Delta \text{ch}=\mid \text{ch}(N+1)-\text{ch}(N)\mid$.

### 2.3 Damage computation: Wöhler method

The number of cycles to failure is determined by interpolation of the Wöhler curve of the material for a given alternating-stress level (to each elementary cycle there corresponds a stress-amplitude level $\Delta \sigma =\mid {\sigma}_{\max}-{\sigma}_{\min}\mid$ and an alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}=1/2\Delta \sigma$).

The damage of an elementary cycle is equal to the inverse of the number of cycles to failure $D=1/N$.

In the case of a homogeneous uniaxial test with a pure (or symmetrical) alternating stress, the number of cycles to failure is determined from an endurance diagram, also called the Wöhler curve or the $S-N$ curve.

In the case of geometrical defects or of elementary cycles with non-zero mean stress, corrections of the Wöhler curve are necessary before the determination of the number of cycles to failure and therefore of the elementary damage.

#### 2.3.1 Endurance diagram

The endurance diagram, also called the Wöhler curve or the $S-N$ curve (stress–number of cycles to failure curve) is obtained experimentally by subjecting specimens to cycles of periodic forces (generally sinusoidal) of normal amplitude $\sigma$ and of constant frequencies, and by noting the number of cycles $N$ at the end of which failure occurs.

The Wöhler curve is therefore defined for a given material and takes the form:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.01/100008CC000030FD00001DC45CE63C1E8815FFD7.svg)

where ${S}_{\text{alt}}$ = the alternating stress of the cycle = $\frac{1}{2}\mid {\sigma}_{\max}-{\sigma}_{\min}\mid$

One distinguishes three zones on this curve:

- a low-cycle fatigue zone, under high stress, where failure occurs after a very small number of reversals,
- a fatigue or limited-endurance zone, where failure is reached after a number of cycles which increases when the stress decreases,
- an unlimited-endurance zone or safety zone, under low stress, for which failure does not occur before a given number of cycles greater than the life envisaged for the part.

There exist numerous expressions of the endurance diagram:

- The oldest is that of Wöhler:

$\ln(N)=a-{\mathrm{bS}}_{\text{alt}}$                             eq 2.3.1-1

where $N$ is the number of cycles to failure,

${S}_{\text{alt}}$ the alternating stress applied,

$a$ and $b$ two characteristics of the material.

This analytical expression does not account well for a horizontal or asymptotic branch of the complete S-N curve, but it gives an often very good representation of the middle part of the curve.

- As early as 1910, Basquin proposes the formula:

$\ln(N)=a-b\ln({S}_{\text{alt}})$                             eq 2.3.1-2

in order to take into account the curvature of the Wöhler curve which joins the descending branch to the horizontal branch.

$D$ **=** damage of an elementary cycle = $1/N=A{S}_{\text{alt}}^{\beta}$ where $A={e}^{-a}$ and $\beta =b$.

- Another analytical form of the Wöhler curve is proposed in POSTDAM in order to take into account the curve outside the singular zone:

${S}_{\text{alt}}=1/2({E}_{C}/E)\Delta \sigma$                         eq 2.3.1-3

$\begin{array}{cc}\mathrm{où}& {E}_{C}=\text{Module d'Young associé à la courbe de fatigue du matériau,}\\ & E=\text{Module d'Young utilisé pour déterminer les contraintes}.\\ & X={\text{LOG}}_{10}({S}_{\text{alt}})\\ & N={10}^{\mathrm{a0}+\mathrm{a1X}+a2{X}^{2}+{\mathrm{a3X}}^{3}}\\ & D=\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}1/N& \text{si}{S}_{\text{alt}}\ge {S}_{l}& \text{où}{S}_{l}\text{est la limite d'endurance du matériau}\\ 0.& \text{sinon}& \end{array}\end{array}$

**Note:**

*If one takes* $\mathrm{a2}=\mathrm{a3}=0\text{et}{E}_{C}/E=1$ *one recovers the Basquin formula.*

The user can introduce the Wöhler curve in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) in three distinct forms:

- **a form discretised point by point** (keyword WOHLER under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU).

The Wöhler curve is in this case a function which gives the number of cycles to failure $N$ as a function of the alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}$ and for which the user chooses the interpolation mode:

- 'LOG' ----> logarithmic interpolation on the number of cycles to failure and on the alternating stress (Basquin formula piecewise),
- 'LIN' ----> linear interpolation on the number of cycles to failure and on the alternating stress (this interpolation is not recommended because the Wöhler curve is absolutely not linear in this frame).
- 'LIN', 'LOG' logarithmic interpolation on the number of cycles to failure and linear interpolation on the alternating stress, which leads to the expression given by Wöhler.

The user must also choose the type of extension of the function to the right and to the left (if it is necessary to interpolate the function at a point not authorised by the definition of the function the program stops with a fatal error).

- **an analytical Basquin form** (keywords A_BASQUIN and BETA_BASQUIN under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU)

$D=A{S}_{\text{alt}}^{\mathrm{\beta  }}$ It is the constants $A$ and $\beta$ used in this formula which are to be introduced by the user (in accordance with the POSTDAM code).

- an analytical form outside the singular zone

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{\text{alt}}=\text{contrainte alternée}=1/2({E}_{C}/E)\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }\\ X={\text{LOG}}_{10}({S}_{\text{alt}})\\ N={10}^{\mathrm{a0}+\mathrm{a1}X+\mathrm{a2}{X}^{2}+\mathrm{a3}{X}^{3}}\\ D=\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}1/N& \text{si}{S}_{\text{alt}}\ge {S}_{l}& \text{où}{S}_{l}\text{est la limite d'endurance du matériau}\\ 0.& \text{sinon}& \end{array}\end{array}$

The user must introduce:

| ${E}_{C}$ = | Young's modulus associated with the fatigue curve of the material (keyword E_REFE under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU) |
| --- | --- |
| $E$ = | Young's modulus used to determine the stresses (keyword E under the factor keyword ELAS in DEFI_MATERIAU), |

the material constants $\mathrm{a0},\mathrm{a1},\mathrm{a2}\mathrm{et}\mathrm{a3}$ (keywords A0, A1, A2 and A3 under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU)

and ${S}_{l}$ the endurance limit of the material (keyword SL under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU).

*This expression of the damage is available in the same form in the software POSTDAM.*

#### 2.3.2 Influence of the geometrical parameters on the endurance

##### 2.3.2.1 Stress concentration coefficient

Depending on the geometry of the part, it may be necessary to weight the value of the applied stress by the stress concentration coefficient ${K}_{T}.\text{}{K}_{T}$. ${K}_{T}$ is a coefficient which is a function of the geometry of the part, of the geometry of the defect and of the type of loading.

This coefficient is given by the user under the keyword ${K}_{T}$ of the factor keyword COEF_MULT.

It is used in order to apply to the loading history a homothety of ratio ${K}_{T}$, which amounts to multiplying all the values of the loading history by the coefficient ${K}_{T}$.

(The damage computation will be carried out on a loading history $\sigma (t)={K}_{T}\times \sigma (t)$).

##### 2.3.2.2 Elastoplastic concentration coefficient

It may also be necessary to weight the value of the applied stress by the elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$.

The elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$ (referred to in articles B3234.3 and B3234.5 of the RCC_M [bib4]) is defined as being the ratio between the real strain amplitude and the fictitious strain amplitude determined by the elastic analysis.

An acceptable value of the coefficient ${K}_{e}$ can be determined by [bib4]:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccccc}{K}_{e}=1& \text{si}& & \mathrm{\Delta \sigma }<& {\mathrm{3S}}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1+(1-n)(\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }/{\mathrm{3S}}_{m}-1)/(n(m-1))& \text{si}& {\mathrm{3S}}_{m}& <\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }<& {\mathrm{3mS}}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1/n& \text{si}& {\mathrm{3mS}}_{m}& <\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }& \end{array}$

where ${S}_{m}$ is the maximum admissible stress,

and $n$ and $m$ two constants depending on the material.

The elastoplastic factor ${K}_{e}$ is a homothety ratio of the loading. This factor depending on the amplitude of the loading. It is applied, cycle by cycle, to the values of the maximum and minimum stress of each cycle.

The data ${S}_{m}$, $n$ and $m$ are introduced under the keywords SM_KE_RCCM, N_KE_RCCM and M_KE_RCCM under the factor keyword FATIGUE in DEFI_MATERIAU.

The user requests that the elastoplastic concentration factor be taken into account by indicating CORR_KE: 'RCCM' in POST_FATIGUE [U4.83.01](doc:u4.83.01).

#### 2.3.3 Influence of the mean stress

If the part is not subjected to pure or symmetrical alternating stresses, that is to say if the mean stress of the cycle is not zero, the resistance of the material to dynamic loadings (its endurance limit) decreases.

One therefore weights the Wöhler curve in order to compute the effective number of cycles to failure with the aid of various diagrams.

The Haigh diagram makes it possible to determine the evolution of the endurance limit as a function of the mean stress ${\sigma}_{m}$ and of the alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}$.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.01/100004A600001E6300000E5E50450299226B2025.svg)

From a cycle $({S}_{\text{alt}},{\sigma}_{m})$ identified in the signal one computes the value of the corrected alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}^{'}$.

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{Si l'on utilise la droite de Goodman}& {S}_{\text{alt}}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{\text{alt}}}{1-\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}}}\\ \text{Si l'on utilise la parabole de Gerber}& \begin{array}{}\\ {S}_{\text{alt}}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{\text{alt}}}{1-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}})}^{2}}\end{array}\end{array}$

If one uses the Goodman straight line: ${S}_{\text{alt}}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{\text{alt}}}{1-\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}}}$

If one uses the Gerber parabola: ${S}_{\text{alt}}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{\text{alt}}}{1-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}})}^{2}}$

One notes that the latter does not distinguish the mean stress in tension and in compression.

where ${S}_{u}$ is the ultimate strength of the material.

The influence of the mean stress is taken into account only at the user's request (keyword CORR_HAIG).

**Note**:

If the Wöhler curve is defined by the analytical form outside the singular zone [eq 2.3.1-3], ranges of stress variation lying below the endurance limit may find themselves above it. In order to avoid this, one corrects the endurance limit ${S}_{l}$ by taking a corrected endurance limit [bib5]:

${S}_{l}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{l}}{1-\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}}}$ for the Goodman straight line

${S}_{l}^{'}=\frac{{S}_{l}}{1-{(\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}})}^{2}}$ for the Gerber parabola

### 2.4 Damage computation: Manson-Coffin method

The domain of application of the Manson-Coffin method [bib1] is plastic low-cycle fatigue, which as its name indicates presents two fundamental characteristics:

- it is plastic, that is to say that a significant plastic strain occurs at each cycle,
- it is low-cycle, that is to say that the materials have a finite endurance under this type of loading.

In order to describe the behaviour of materials in plastic low-cycle fatigue, one uses tests at imposed alternating strain.

In the case of a homogeneous uniaxial test with an alternating strain, the number of cycles to failure is determined from a resistance diagram, which relates the strain variation to the number of cycles leading to failure.

In the resistance diagram, one separates the total, elastic and plastic strains. These diagrams are also known under the name of Coffin-Manson, who proposed them in 1950.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.01/10000C8200002F21000020A9DC53F8EF5B187EF1.svg)

The relations $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{e}}{2}-\ln(N)$ and $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{p}}{2}-\ln(N)$ are straight lines. The relation $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{t}}{2}-\ln(N)$, for its part, presents a curvature towards the positive strains.

It has been shown that a power relation linked the plastic strain $({\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{p})$ and the elastic strain $({\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{e})$ to the number of cycles to failure, which leads to the following relations:

$\begin{array}{}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{p}=A{N}^{-a}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{e}=B{N}^{-b}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{t}=A{N}^{-a}+B{N}^{-b}\end{array}$

where $a$ and $b$ are two characteristics of the material (in general $a$ is close to $0,5$ and $b$ close to $0,12$); $A$ and $B$, two constants of the material.

The user can introduce the Manson-Coffin curve in a single mathematical form: a form discretised point by point. It is a function which gives the number of cycles to failure $N$ as a function of the strain amplitude $(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{})$.

As for the Wöhler curve, the user can choose the interpolation mode on the number of cycles to failure and on the strain amplitude.

The type of extension of the function to the right and to the left is also at the user's choice.

The damage of an elementary cycle is equal to the inverse of the number of cycles to failure $D=1/N$.

### 2.5 Damage computation: Taheri method

The damage-computation methods proposed by Taheri [bib12] are two in number: they will be named respectively Taheri-Manson and Taheri-mixed. These methods apply to loadings characterised by a scalar component of strain type.

These methods have the particularity of taking into account the order of application of the elementary loading cycles to the structure. For this reason, one must be careful in the choice of the cycle-counting method. It is strongly recommended to use the counting method called the “natural” method [§2.2.3].

#### 2.5.1 Taheri-Manson method

Let there be n elementary cycles of half-amplitude $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{1}}{2},\cdots \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{n}}{2}$.

The value of the elementary damage of the first cycle is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

The computation of the elementary damage of the following cycles is carried out by the algorithm:

- if $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2}\ge \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i}}{2}$

the value of the elementary damage of the cycle $(i+1)$ is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

- if $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2}<\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i}}{2}$

one determines:

$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{i+1}}{2}={F}_{\text{NAPPE}}(\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2},\underset{j<i}{\text{Max}}(\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{j}}{2}))$

then

$\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}^{\ast }}{2}={F}_{\text{FONC}}(\frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{i+1}}{2})$.

${F}_{\text{NAPPE}}$ is the cyclic hardening curve with cyclic pre-hardening of the material.

${F}_{\text{FONC}}$ is the cyclic hardening curve of the material.

The value of the damage of the cycle $(i+1)$ is determined by interpolation of $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}^{\ast }}{2}$ on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

**Note**:

*If all the cycles applied are arranged by increasing value of the strain amplitude, this method is identical to the Manson-Coffin method.*

#### 2.5.2 Taheri-Mixed method

Let there be n elementary cycles, of half-amplitude $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{1}}{2},\cdots \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{n}}{2}$.

The value of the elementary damage of the first cycle is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

The computation of the elementary damage of the following cycles is carried out by the algorithm:

- if $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2}\ge \frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i}}{2}$

the value of the elementary damage of the cycle $(i+1)$ is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

- if $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2}<\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i}}{2}$

one determines:

$\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{i+1}}{2}={F}_{\text{NAPPE}}(\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{i+1}}{2},\underset{j<i}{\text{Max}}(\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{j}}{2}))$

where ${F}_{\text{NAPPE}}$ is the cyclic hardening curve with cyclic pre-hardening of the material.

The value of the damage of the cycle $(i+1)$ is obtained by interpolation of $\frac{{\mathrm{\Delta \sigma }}_{i+1}}{2}$ on the Wöhler curve of the material.

**Note**:

*If all the cycles applied to the structure are arranged by increasing value of the strain amplitude, this method is identical to the Manson-Coffin method.*

The damage of an elementary cycle is equal to the inverse of the number of cycles to failure $D=1/N$.

### 2.6 Computation of the total damage

The simplest and best-known approach for determining the total damage of a part subjected to ${n}_{i}$ cycles of alternating stress ${S}_{\text{alt}}$ or of alternating strain ${E}_{\text{alt}}$ is the linear damage rule proposed by Miner:

$\text{Di}=\frac{{n}_{i}}{{N}_{i}}$

In operation, the structures are subjected to various loadings of different amplitudes. The fatigue undergone is due to the accumulation of the elementary damages and the total damage is computed with the aid of the Miner accumulation rule [bib6]:

${D}_{\text{total}=}\sum_{i}^{}\frac{{n}_{i}}{{N}_{i}}$

In the case of Wöhler and Manson-Coffin, this law assumes that the damage increases linearly with the number of cycles imposed and that it is independent of the loading level and of the order of application of the loading levels (whereas experimentally, one shows that the order of application of the loading is an important factor for the life of the material).

The computation of the total damage is requested by the user with the keyword CUMUL.

The methods proposed by Taheri take into account the order of application of the loading, in the computation of the elementary damages associated with each cycle.

### 2.7 Conclusion

For the methods based on uniaxial tests, the computation of the total damage undergone by a part subjected to a loading history breaks down into several stages:

- extraction of the peaks of the loading history, in order to arrive at a simpler history,
- extraction of the elementary cycles of the loading history by a cycle-counting method,
- computation of the elementary damage associated with each elementary cycle coming from the real history of the loading,
- possibly (and for the Wöhler method), correction of the loading by a stress concentration coefficient ${K}_{T}$,
- possibly (and for the Wöhler method), correction of the loading by an elastoplastic concentration coefficient ${K}_{e}$,
- possibly (and for the Wöhler method), Haigh correction in order to take into account the non-zero value of the mean stress,
- computation of the total damage, by a linear accumulation rule.
## 3 Computation of the generalised Lemaître damage

This damage law concerns the study of the initiation of a macroscopic crack, with the aid of a post-processor of damage mechanics based on a unified formulation of the damage-evolution laws. The latter uses, on the one hand, damage-evolution laws specific to the various mechanisms considered, and, on the other hand, a more general model based on a micro-mechanical analysis of the initiation phenomenon.

This law offers a single formalism which assumes that the various damage mechanisms are all governed by the plastic strains, the elastic strain energy and by an instability process.

### 3.1 The generalised Lemaître law

The generalised Lemaître law consists in an enrichment of the damage-computation method of Lemaitre [bib7] by the introduction of a power law (Lemaître-Sermage model). It is written [bib14]:

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\dot{D}\text{}=\text{}{\left[\frac{Y}{S}\right]}^{s}\dot{p}& \text{si}p>{p}_{D}\\ D\text{}=\text{}0& \text{sinon}\end{array}$                 eq 3.1-1

with:

$Y=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}{2E{(1-D)}^{2}}{R}_{\nu}\text{et}{R}_{\nu}=\frac{2}{3}(1+\nu )+3(1-2\nu ){(\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}})}^{2}$.

$Y$ is the release rate of elastic strain-energy density.

${R}_{\mathrm{\nu  }}$ is the triaxiality function.

$\frac{{\sigma}_{H}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}$ is the triaxiality ratio.

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}{\sigma}_{ij}^{D}{\sigma}_{ij}^{D}}$ is the von Mises equivalent stress.

${\sigma}_{ij}^{D}={\sigma}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{kk}{S}_{ij}$ is the stress deviator.

${p}_{D}$ is the damage threshold.

$S$ and $s$ are material characteristics.

$p(t)$ is the cumulative plastic strain.

This law therefore makes it possible to compute the damage $D(t)$ from the data of the stress tensor $\sigma (t)$ and of the cumulative plastic strain $p(t)$.

Equation [eq 3.1-1] can be written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{(1-D)}^{\mathrm{2s}}\mathrm{dD}\text{}=\text{}{\left[\frac{C}{S}\right]}^{s}\mathrm{dp}& \text{si}p>{p}_{D}\\ D\text{}=\text{}0& \text{sinon}\end{array}$             eq 3.1-2

> with:

> $C=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}{2E}{R}_{\nu}$

The integration of the equations [eq 3.1-2] between ${t}_{i}$ and ${t}_{i+1}$ leads to:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\underset{D({t}_{i})}{\overset{D({t}_{i+1})}{\int}}{(1-D)}^{\mathrm{2s}}\mathrm{dD}\text{}=\text{}\underset{p({t}_{i})}{\overset{p({t}_{i+1})}{\int}}{\left[\frac{C}{S}\right]}^{s}\mathrm{dp}& \text{si}p>{p}_{D}\\ D\text{}=\text{}0& \text{sinon}\end{array}$ eq 3.1-3

One notes that there exists a primitive for the term on the left of equation [eq 3.1-3] but not for that on the right. A numerical integration scheme is therefore used in order to compute the integral. Equation [eq 3.1-3] can be written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{{[1-D({t}_{i})]}^{\mathrm{2s}+1}-{[1-D({t}_{i+1})]}^{\mathrm{2s}+1}}{\mathrm{2s}+1}\text{}=\text{}\frac{1}{2}({\left[\frac{C({t}_{i})}{S({t}_{i})}\right]}^{s}+{\left[\frac{C({t}_{i+1})}{S({t}_{i+1})}\right]}^{s})[p({t}_{i+1})-p({t}_{i})]& \text{si}p>{p}_{D}\\ D({t}_{i+1})\text{}=\text{}0& \text{sinon}\end{array}$ eq 3.1-4

One assumes that $D({t}_{o})=0$. The value of the damage $D({t}_{i}),i=0,n$ for each time instant ${t}_{i}$ can be determined from equation [eq 3.1-4]. In *Code_Aster*, this quantity is named DOM_LEM.

The final damage at failure ${D}_{r}=D({t}_{r})$ is therefore associated with the time to failure ${t}_{r}$.

**Notes**:

- *One considers that the material characteristics* $E$ *(Young's modulus),* $\mathrm{\nu  }$ *(Poisson's ratio) and* $S$ *(material parameter) depend on the temperature* $T$*.*
- *The value of Young's modulus and the value of Poisson's ratio are defined in* *DEFI_MATERIAU* [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) under the factor keyword *ELAS_FO.*
- *The values of* $S$*,* ${p}_{D}$ *and of* $s$ *are defined in* *DEFI_MATERIAU under the factor keyword* *DOMMA_LEMAITRE and the operands* *S,* *ESPS_SEUIL and* *EXP_S. The parameters* $S$ *and* ${p}_{D}$ *may depend on the temperature* *TEMP.*
- *The Lemaître law is obtained by assigning the value* $s=1$

### 3.2 Identification of the parameters of the generalised Lemaître law

One notes that equation [eq 3.1-4] is valid for fatigue and for creep. For fatigue, $p$ is the cumulative plastic strain. For creep, $p$ is the instantaneous plastic strain if one neglects the elastic strain.

One notes that the material parameters $S$ and $s$ depend strongly not only on the temperature $T$ but also on the stress $\sigma$ (via the expression of $C$). The process of determination of $S$ and $s$ from the fatigue tests has been presented in [bib14].

This part aims to present a simple method for identifying the material parameters $S$ and $s$ from uniaxial creep tests.

In a first stage, one fixes the temperature $T$. One will carry out two creep tests at two stress levels ${\sigma}_{1}$ and ${\sigma}_{2}$ sufficiently close for the parameters $S$ and $s$ to be able to be considered as constant between these two stress levels. One denotes ${p}_{\mathrm{r1}}$ and ${p}_{\mathrm{r1}}$ the plastic strains at failure, measured from the creep tests associated with ${\sigma}_{1}$ and ${\sigma}_{2}$, respectively.

Since the stress $\sigma$ is kept constant during the creep test and $D({t}_{o})=0$, ${D}_{r}=D({t}_{r})=1$, equation [eq 3.1-4] can be written for the stress levels ${\sigma}_{1}$ and ${\sigma}_{2}$ as:

$\frac{{S}^{s}}{\mathrm{2s}+1}={[C({\sigma}_{1})]}^{s}.{p}_{\mathrm{r1}}$ eq 3.2-1

$\frac{{S}^{s}}{\mathrm{2s}+1}={[C({\sigma}_{2})]}^{s}.{p}_{\mathrm{r2}}$ eq 3.2-2

The parameters $S$ and $s$ are the solution of the system of equations [eq 3.2-1] and [eq 3.2-2]. From these equations, the parameter $s$ is determined as:

$s=\frac{\log\frac{{p}_{\mathrm{r2}}}{{p}_{\mathrm{r1}}}}{\log\frac{C({\sigma}_{1})}{C({\sigma}_{2})}}$ eq 3.2-3

Then, $S$ can be determined either from equation [eq 3.2-1], or from equation [eq 3.2-2].

For ${\sigma}_{1}<{\sigma}_{2}$, from equation [eq 3.1-7], the value of $s$ is positive (physically acceptable) if and only if ${p}_{\mathrm{r1}}>{p}_{\mathrm{r2}}$. In the contrary case, there exists no positive solution for the system of equations [eq 3.2-1] and [eq 3.2-2], i.e., the Lemaitre damage model is not applicable in this case.

One notes that this simple method makes it possible to identify the parameters $S$ and $s$ at different temperatures and at different levels of applied stress from the experimental/numerical creep curves.

## 4 Crossland and Dang Van Papadopoulos criteria

The criteria [bib9] and [bib13] make it possible, for metallic structures subjected to imposed stresses following a large number of cycles, to distinguish the damaging loadings from the others.

One can classify the criteria into two categories according to the nature of their approach:

- macroscopic approach: Crossland criterion,
- microscopic approach: Dang Van Papadopoulos criterion.

The Crossland and Dang Van Papadopoulos criteria apply to periodic uniaxial or multiaxial loadings.

The aim of these criteria is not to determine a damage value, but a criterion value ${R}_{\text{crit}}$ such that:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{R}_{\text{crit}}\le 0\text{pas}\text{de}\text{dommage}\\ {R}_{\text{crit}}>0\text{dommage}\text{possible}(\text{fatigue}).\end{array}$

### 4.1 Crossland criterion

The Crossland criterion has an empirical character and is written solely from macroscopic variables.

In fact, from test campaigns, one has been able to note that the shear-stress amplitude as well as the hydrostatic pressure played a fundamental role in the fatigue mechanisms of structures.

That is why Crossland postulated the criterion:

${R}_{\text{crit}}={\tau}_{a}+a{P}_{\max}-b$

where

${\tau}_{a}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{0\le {t}_{0}\le T}{\text{Max}}\underset{0\le {t}_{1}\le T}{\text{Max}}\parallel {\sigma}_{({t}_{1})}^{D}-{\sigma}_{({t}_{0})}^{D}\parallel =$ shear-stress amplitude

with ${\sigma}^{D}$ the deviator of the stress tensor.

${P}_{\max}=\underset{0\le t\le T}{\text{Max}}(\frac{1}{3}\text{trace}\sigma )=$ maximum hydrostatic pressure.

$a=({\tau}_{0}-\frac{{d}_{0}}{\sqrt{3}})/(\frac{{d}_{0}}{\sqrt{3}})\text{et}b={\tau}_{0}$

with:

${\tau}_{0}=$ endurance limit in pure alternating shear,

${d}_{0}=$ endurance limit in pure alternating tension-compression.

### 4.2 Dang Van Papadopoulos criterion

It has appeared that the initiation of fatigue cracks is a microscopic phenomenon occurring at a scale of the order of the grain. That is why fatigue criteria, based on local microscopic variables, have been postulated.

The criterion implemented [bib8], [bib9] and [bib10] in *Code_Aster* is the Dang Van Papadopoulos criterion, which is written in the form:

${R}_{\mathrm{crit}}={k}^{\ast }+a{P}_{\max}-b$

where:

| ${k}^{\ast }=\frac{R}{\sqrt{2}}$ | if | $R=\underset{0\le t\le T}{\text{Max}}\sqrt{({\sigma}^{D}(t)-{C}^{\ast }):({\sigma}^{D}(t)-{C}^{\ast })}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  |  |  |
| ${k}^{\ast }=R$ | if | $R=\underset{0\le t\le T}{\text{Max}}\sqrt{{J}_{2}(t)}=\underset{0\le t\le T}{\text{Max}}\sqrt{\frac{1}{2}({\sigma}^{D}(t)-{C}^{\ast }):({\sigma}^{D}(t)-{C}^{\ast })}$ |

with:

1. $R$, the radius of the smallest sphere circumscribed about the loading path in the space of the stress deviators;
2. ${J}_{2}(t)$, the second invariant of the stress deviators;
3. ${C}^{\ast }=\underset{C}{\text{Min}}\underset{t}{\text{Max}}\sqrt{({\sigma}^{D}(t)-C):({\sigma}^{D}(t)-C)}$, the centre of the hypersphere.

**Note:**

*It is the definition of* $R$ *which uses* ${J}_{2}(t)$ *which is programmed.*

${P}_{\max}=$ maximum hydrostatic pressure $=\underset{0\le t\le T}{\text{Max}}(\frac{1}{3}\text{trace}\sigma )$

$a=({\tau}_{0}-\frac{{d}_{0}}{\sqrt{3}})/(\frac{{d}_{0}}{3})$ and $b={\tau}_{0}$

with:

${\tau}_{0}=$ endurance limit in pure alternating shear,

${d}_{0}=$ endurance limit in pure alternating tension-compression.

The basic idea of Papadopoulos is to write that the grain obeys a plasticity criterion of von Mises type instead of the plasticity criterion of Tresca type used by Dang Van.

Papadopoulos carried out a campaign of comparisons between the results supplied by his criterion and experimental results, which shows that the predictions of the Papadopoulos criterion are excellent for affine loadings; they are a little less accurate for non-affine paths.

In his thesis [bib10] Papadopoulos shows that the Crossland criterion and the Dang Van Papadopoulos criterion give the same results for radial loadings.

The algorithm employed for the computation of the radius of the smallest sphere circumscribed about the loading path in the space of the stress deviators is that proposed in [bib11]. It is a recurrent algorithm which rests on the second invariant of the stress deviators.

Let us denote by ${S}_{i}$ the value of the stress deviator at the time instant ${t}_{i}$, ${O}_{n}$ the centre of the hypersphere at iteration $n,{R}_{n}$ the radius of the hypersphere at iteration $n$ and $x$ the “isotropic hardening parameter” of the algorithm.

- Initialisation phase of the algorithm:

$\begin{array}{}{O}_{1}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}{S}_{i}\\ {R}_{1}=0.\end{array}$

- Iteration from stage $n$ to stage $n+1$:

one assumes ${O}_{n}$ and ${R}_{n}$ known. One then computes:

$\begin{array}{}D=\parallel {S}_{i+1}-{O}_{n}\parallel \\ P=D-{R}_{n}\end{array}$

- If $P>0$

$\begin{array}{}{R}_{n+1}={R}_{n}+x.P\\ {O}_{n+1}={S}_{i+1}+{R}_{n+1}\frac{{O}_{n}-{S}_{i}}{\parallel {O}_{n}-{S}_{i+1}\parallel }\end{array}$

- If $P<0$

$\begin{array}{}{R}_{n+1}={R}_{n}\\ {O}_{n+1}={O}_{n}\end{array}$

The algorithm ends when all the points ${S}_{i}$ are in the hypersphere of centre ${O}_{n}$ and of radius ${R}_{n}$.

### 4.3 Computation of a damage value

These two criteria, applicable to periodic multiaxial loadings, make it possible to say whether or not there is damage:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{R}_{\text{crit}}\le 0\text{pas}\text{de}\text{dommage}\\ {R}_{\text{crit}}>0\text{dommage}\text{possible}(\text{fatigue}).\end{array}$

These criteria do not supply a damage value. It may nevertheless be of interest to compute a damage value by using the Wöhler curves of the material. To this end, one must define an equivalent stress ${\sigma}^{\ast }$, a value to be interpolated on the Wöhler curve.

The Wöhler curves can be constructed from shear tests, in which case the endurance limit is ${\tau}_{0}$, but are more generally constructed from tension-compression tests for which the endurance limit is ${d}_{0}({d}_{0}<{\tau}_{0})$.

In order for there to be consistency between the criterion and the Wöhler curve it is necessary that:

$\begin{array}{}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}^{\ast }\le {\tau}_{0}\text{pas}\text{de}\text{dommage}\\ {\sigma}^{\ast }>{\tau}_{0}\text{dommage}\end{array}\right\rbrace \text{pour une courbe de Wöhler définie en cisaillement,}\\ \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}^{\ast }\le {d}_{0}\text{pas}\text{de}\text{dommage}\\ {\sigma}^{\ast }>{d}_{0}\text{dommage}\end{array}\right\rbrace \text{pour une courbe de Wöhler définie en traction-compression}.\end{array}$

It therefore seems to us possible to take:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}^{\ast }={R}_{\text{crit}}+{\tau}_{0}\text{pour une courbe de Wöhler en cisaillement}(\text{ce qui est assez rare}),\\ {\sigma}^{\ast }=({R}_{\text{crit}}+{\tau}_{0})\ast ({d}_{0}/{\tau}_{0})\text{pour une courbe de Wöhler en traction-compression}.\end{array}$

In a general way, the user can take ${\sigma}^{\ast }=({R}_{\text{crit}}+{\tau}_{0})\ast \text{corr}$ where $\text{corr}$ is a correction coefficient introduced by the user.

By default, this coefficient $\mathrm{corr}$ is taken equal to $({d}_{0}/{\tau}_{0})$ (case of the Wöhler curve introduced in tension-compression).

**Note**:

*In the literature, one does not find a presentation of an approach for using a criterion in order to compute a damage value. One knows nevertheless that certain industrial users employ such an approach, but without knowing the form adopted.*

*The approach implemented in Code_Aster is proposed by the AMA department.*

## 5 Conclusion

In this note are set out the various methods of damage computation available either in the operator POST_FATIGUE or in the operator CALC_FATIGUE, or in both commands simultaneously.

One can classify these methods into two large classes:

- estimation of the damage at large numbers of cycles,
- estimation of the damage in plastic low-cycle fatigue.

In the first class of problems, one finds the Wöhler method, based on uniaxial tests, and which applies to stress loadings. One also finds in this class the Crossland criterion, which is an empirical criterion relying on macroscopic quantities, and the Dang Van Papadopoulos criterion, which is based on microscopic phenomena.

The two criteria are addressed to stress loadings which may be uniaxial or multiaxial but periodic.

In the second class of problems, one finds the Manson-Coffin method and the Taheri methods, which apply to strain loadings.

All of the methods based on uniaxial tests (Wöhler method, Manson-Coffin method and Taheri methods) are available in both operators POST_FATIGUE and CALC_FATIGUE.

The criteria, for their part, are available only in POST_FATIGUE.
