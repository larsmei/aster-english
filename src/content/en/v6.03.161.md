# v6.03.161 SSNP161 – Kupfer Biaxial Tests

**Summary:**

Kupfer [1] was interested in characterizing the performance of concrete under biaxial loadings. Two of these tests are modelled in this test case to compare experimental data with results obtained with the Mazars model. Bi-compression and shear behaviour are studied in this test case.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and boundary conditions

In these tests, a concrete plate ($200\times 200\times 50\mathit{mm}$ ) is subjected to a load where the ${\sigma}_{2}/{\sigma}_{1}$ main stress ratio is fixed (${\sigma}_{3}=0$ ). These tests are modelled in two dimensions under the condition of flat stresses (${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ ) using a 4 nodes quadrangle element (QUAD4).

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.161/1000000000000357000001CA4236EF3D652EB857.jpg)

Figure 1.1-a: Modélisation 2D et conditions aux limites des essais biaxiaux

Only a quarter of the concrete plate is modelled. Thus, symmetry conditions are imposed:

- The following $y$ moves are blocked on the lower edge.
- The following $x$ moves are blocked on the left edge.

Two pressures are imposed on the free edges: ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ . The ratio between the loads, and implicitly on the main stresses, is noted $\omega$:

${\sigma}_{2}=\omega {\sigma}_{1}$                                                     (Eq.1)

These pressures follow a linear evolution law.

| Instant | 0 | 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ${P}_{1}$ ($\mathit{MPa}$ ) | 0 | 30 |
| ${P}_{2}$ ($\mathit{MPa}$ ) | 0 | $30\omega$ |

**Table** 1.1-1 **: Evolution of pressures**

### 1.2 Material properties

For the MAZARS model, the following parameters were used:

Elastic behaviour:

$E=34000\text{MPa},\nu =0.19$

Harmful behaviour:

${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{d0}}=1.1.{10}^{-3};\text{Ac}=1.25;\text{At}=1.0;\text{Bc}=1965;\text{Bt}=9000;k=0.7$

These material parameters induce ${f}_{c}$ compression limit of the order of $33\mathit{MPa}$.

### 1.3 Initial conditions

None

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Comparison of tests and simulations

We focused on two $\omega ={\sigma}_{2}/{\sigma}_{1}$ ratio values corresponding to a bi-compression test ($\omega =0.52$ ) and a traction-compression test ($\omega =-0.052$ ). The following figure presents the different results obtained:

Figure 2.1-b:Comparaison des courbes expérimentale et numériques lors de l’essai biaxial $\omega =0.52$

The simulations are driven by force. Therefore, it is not possible to model the softening phase. For the $\omega =0.52$ ratio, the non-recognition of the Poisson's ratio bi-compression evolution model does not allow for the recovery of the long-term deformations. However, the numerical results are close to the experimental points at the beginning of the simulation. *Note*: Until the stabilized version 11.2 of *Code_Aster* , the Mazars model followed the equations defined in the 1984 Mazars thesis [2]. Recently, a reformulation was proposed to fill some gaps in the 1984 model, namely the description of the behaviour of the concrete in bi-compression and pure shear. This 2012 version was implemented from the STA 11.3. It is possible to compare the responses of the different versions of the Mazars model in order to highlight the improvements in the 2012 version. It appears that the 1984 Mazars model underestimates the resistance in bi-compression. Then, for $\omega =-0.052$ , the differences between the results from the two models are weak. Note that the improvements in the 2012 model relate in particular to the behaviour in bi-compression and pure shear. Therefore, this result is logical. The curve obtained from the 2012 Mazars model remains even closer to the experimental points.

## 7 Summary of the results

The version of the 2012 MAZARS model allows the experimental break stress to be recovered with an error of less than 5%.

*Note:* the gain from the 1984 model is mainly visible in bi-compression i.e. for the $\omega =0.52$ ratio. However, the deformation at break is far from the test because this model does not take into account the evolution of the Poisson's ratio in bi-compression.
