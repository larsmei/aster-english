# u2.06.31 User's guide for the modelling of gyroscopy

**Summary:**

Modal analyses, or linear and non-linear dynamic analyses, of shaft lines require the notion of gyroscopic damping to be included. The analysis of a rotational-speed transient (run-up or slowdown) requires, for its part, the notion of gyroscopic stiffness to be included.

Dedicated rotating-machinery codes use a preferential axis for the modelling of rotors ($Y$  for ROTORINSA, $Z$ for CADYRO, etc.). In *Code_Aster* , it is possible, provided a few precautions are taken in the data setup, to model a rotor with any axis of rotation. Once the axis of rotation has been chosen, the positive sense along this axis is defined as the usual **anticlockwise**  sense of rotation.

This user's guide is an aid to the use of the gyroscopic damping and stiffness functionalities. It should enable the user to carry through a consistent and correct rotating-machinery analysis, whatever the axis of rotation of the shaft line.

## 1 Gyroscopic damping and stiffness

### 1.1 Characteristics of gyroscopy

The modellings currently available in Code_Aster for rotating machinery involve structural elements of beam type (for the rotors) and discrete elements (for the discs, the bearings, the supports, etc.).

The computations of the kinetic energy of the Timoshenko beam element (considering the bending strains) or of the discrete element bring out the gyroscopic-effect terms.

For a rotating system, the classical equation of structural dynamics depends on the rotational speeds as follows:

> $M\ddot{\delta}+{C}_{\mathit{gyro}}(\Omega )\dot{\delta}+(K+{K}_{\mathit{gyro}}(\dot{\Omega}))\delta =0$

Where $M$ and $K$ are the classical stiffness and mass matrices of the rotating mechanical system. ${C}_{\mathit{gyro}}(\Omega )$ is a non-symmetric matrix, a function of the rotational speed $\Omega$ , including the gyroscopic-damping characteristics. This gyroscopic-damping matrix is antisymmetric, and its contribution is proportional to the angular velocity.

${K}_{\mathit{gyro}}(\dot{\Omega})$ is a non-symmetric matrix, a function of the rotational acceleration $(\dot{\Omega})$ , including the gyroscopic-stiffness characteristics. This gyroscopic-stiffness matrix is full. Its contribution is proportional to the angular acceleration and is therefore zero in the case of a constant rotational speed. The gyroscopic-stiffness matrix is meaningful only for a speed transient.

Gyroscopic damping and stiffness are currently available in Code_Aster for straight beams (Timoshenko elements POU_D_T and Euler elements POU_D_E) of constant or variable cross-section and of circular shape, as well as for discrete elements. The assumptions retained for the beams are:

- Timoshenko assumption: transverse shear and all the inertia terms are taken into account. This assumption is to be used for low slenderness ratios (POU_D_T elements).
- Euler assumption: transverse shear is neglected. This assumption holds for high slenderness ratios (POU_D_E elements).

The spin speed (along the axis of the beam) may be constant or variable (case of a rotational-speed transient: run-up or slowdown).

The discs are cylinders of revolution whose axis coincides with the axis of the beam. These discs are assumed to be non-deformable and are modelled by discrete elements. The material is homogeneous and isotropic.

Finally, and for further details, the reader may refer to the reference documentation of gyroscopic damping and stiffness in Code_Aster [R5.05.07](doc:r5.05.07).

### 1.2 Computation of the gyroscopic matrices

It is possible to build the gyroscopic damping and stiffness matrices from each element of the model, as for the stiffness and the mass. For structural finite elements of beam type or for discrete elements, it is possible to compute the real elementary matrices corresponding to the analysis options 'MECA_GYRO' and 'RIGI_GYRO'. These operations are called as follows:

AMGYELEM [ **matr_elem_DEPL_R** ] = CALC_MATR_ELEM

**(** / ♦ OPTION: **'MECA_GYRO'**

♦ MODELE: mo [modele]

♦ CHAM_MATER: chmat [cham_mater]

◊ CARA_ELEM: cara [cara_elem]

**)**

RIGYELEM [ **matr_elem_DEPL_R** ] = CALC_MATR_ELEM

**(** / ♦ OPTION: **'RIGI_GYRO'**

♦ MODELE: mo [modele]

♦ CHAM_MATER: chmat [cham_mater]

◊ CARA_ELEM: cara [cara_elem]

**)**

The assembly of the elementary gyroscopic matrices is obtained with the usual operator ASSE_MATRICE [U4.61.22](doc:u4.61.22).

AMGYASS=ASSE_MATRICE(MATR_ELEM=AMGYELEM,

NUME_DDL=NUMEDDL,)

RIGYASS=ASSE_MATRICE(MATR_ELEM=RIGYELEM,

NUME_DDL=NUMEDDL,)

It will be noted that one must use the same numberings and the same storage mode as for the stiffness and mass matrices (operator NUME_DDL [U4.61.11](doc:u4.61.11)).

One can also carry out this operation at the same time as the construction of the other matrices (mass, stiffness, damping, ...) by means of the macro-command ASSEMBLAGE

ASSEMBLAGE( MODELE=MODELE,

CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

CARA_ELEM=CARELEM,

CHARGE=BLOQUAGE,

NUME_DDL=CO('NUMEDDL'),

MATR_ASSE=(_F(MATRICE=CO('RIGIDITE'),

OPTION='RIGI_MECA',),

_F(MATRICE=CO('MASSE'),

OPTION='MASS_MECA',),

_F(MATRICE=CO('AMOR'),

OPTION='AMOR_MECA',),

_F(MATRICE=CO('AMGY'),

OPTION='MECA_GYRO',),

_F(MATRICE=CO('RIGY'),

OPTION='RIGI_GYRO',),),)

### 1.3 Use of the gyroscopic matrices

The computed gyroscopic damping and stiffness matrices ${C}_{\mathit{gyro}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{gyro}}$ are unitary and must therefore be multiplied by the angular velocity and the angular acceleration respectively in order to obtain the gyroscopic effect of a given rotational speed.

The gyroscopic-damping matrix is generally combined, after assembly, with the classical damping matrix by the operator **COMB_MATR_ASSE**  as follows:

OM=124.14*pi/30. # Rotational speed in revolutions/min converted to rad/s

GYOM=COMB_MATR_ASSE(COMB_R=(_F(MATR_ASSE=AMGY, COEF_R=OM,),

_F(MATR_ASSE=AMOR, COEF_R=1.,),))

where AMGY and AMOR are the assembled gyroscopic and viscous damping matrices.

The gyroscopic matrices can be used for the direct linear dynamic analysis with the linear dynamic response operators:

- linear transient on a physical basis **DYNA_VIBRA** [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) (cf. modelling A in [V2.02.126](doc:v2.02.126))
- linear transient on a modal basis **DYNA_VIBRA** [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) (cf. modelling B in [V2.02.126](doc:v2.02.126))
- harmonic **DYNA_VIBRA** [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) (cf. modelling C in [V2,02,126])
- **DYNA_VIBRA**  [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) (cf. modelling C in [V2,02,126])

In the case of a rotational-speed transient treated on a modal basis, the two assembled gyroscopic matrices are combined with the classical damping and stiffness matrices in a manner completely transparent to the user. To this end, it is sufficient to supply in **DYNA_VIBRA** the parameters defining this speed transient (imposed angular-velocity and angular-acceleration laws and assembled gyroscopic matrices, cf. [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) and [U2.06.32](doc:u2.06.32)).

These matrices are indispensable for the complex modal analysis of a rotating system with the eigenvalue-search operator:

- **CALC_MODES** [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02)

For systems with a reduced number of degrees of freedom, a direct modal analysis can be carried out with the operator cited above and with one or other of the solution algorithms (here the 'SORENSEN' algorithm, for example):

MODEG=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=RIGIDITE,

MATR_MASS=MASSE,

MATR_AMOR=GYOM,

OPTION='CENTRE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(FREQ=1.,

NMAX_FREQ=16),

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F(METHODE='SORENSEN'),

VERI_MODE=_F(SEUIL=1.E-3),

)

For a robust quadratic modal analysis (analysis of damped modes), one can use a two-step strategy.

The QZ method is a reliable method for a quadratic modal analysis. However, it is costly. It is therefore to be restricted to systems with a small number of degrees of freedom (a few thousand degrees of freedom) or to problems reduced by modal truncation. One must proceed with a two-stage analysis:

- 1a - compute a reduced undamped modal basis:

MODES=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=RIGIDITE,

MATR_MASS=MASSE,

OPTION='BANDE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(FREQ=(3.,3000.))

);

- 1b - project the mass, stiffness and total damping matrices GYOM onto this basis

(modal truncation):

PROJ_BASE(BASE=MODES,

PROFIL='PLEIN',

MATR_ASSE_GENE=(_F(MATRICE=CO('MAGE'),

MATR_ASSE=MASSE,),

_F(MATRICE=CO('RIGE'),

MATR_ASSE=RIGIDITE,),

_F(MATRICE=CO('OMGE'),

MATR_ASSE=GYOM,),),

);

- 2 - carry out a modal analysis on the generalised matrices obtained by projection:

MOD2ETAG=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=RIGE,

MATR_MASS=MAGE,

MATR_AMOR=OMGE,

OPTION='TOUT',

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F(METHODE='QZ'),

);

One is therefore led to project the antisymmetric and non-symmetric matrices ${C}_{\mathit{gyro}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{gyro}}$ into the subspace defined by a set of real eigenmodes. This operation is possible with the operator **PROJ_MATR_BASE** [U4.63.12](doc:u4.63.12). Note that, in the general case, the projected matrices are not symmetric. They nevertheless remain usable for the computation of the dynamic response to a force or to an imposed motion in the modal space, with the linear dynamic response operator:

- linear transient on a modal basis **DYNA_VIBRA** [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03) (cf. modelling B in [V2,02,126])

## 2 Example of a shaft-line analysis with the axis along X

In this section, we give a concrete example of a shaft line modelled in two different ways (axis of rotation $X$ on the one hand and an arbitrary axis on the other). We rely here on the example, documented in [V6.02.126](doc:v6.02.126), of a simple rotor model. It is guided by 2 bearings (respectively the first and the last node of the rotor), and supports 3 discs (cf. figure below).

![Figure](/figures/u2.06.31/Object_8.svg)

Figure 2-a: Rotor model with 3 discs and 2 bearings taken from [bib1]

We consider two modellings for this test case. In the first, the axis of rotation is along $X$ ; in the second, it is along an arbitrary axis defined by its nautical angles $(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma )$ . The steps of reading the mesh and of assigning models and material being identical for the two modellings (apart from the meshing of the shafts along the desired axis of rotation), we focus on the step which distinguishes them, namely the assignment of the elementary characteristics by the command **AFFE_CARA_ELEM** .

The data setup of the discrete elements is done so as to supply the stiffness and damping characteristics (for the bearings) and the mass and inertias (for the discs) directly in the global coordinate system (keyword REPERE, by default REPERE = 'GLOBAL').

CARELEM=AFFE_CARA_ELEM(MODELE=MODELE,

POUTRE= _F(GROUP_MA='ROTOR',

SECTION='CERCLE',

CARA='R',

VALE=.05,),

DISCRET=(_F(CARA='K_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_A',

REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(0.,5.E7,7.E7,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='K_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_E',

REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(0.,5.E7,7.E7,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='A_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_A',

REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(0.,5000.,7000.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='A_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_E',

REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(0.,5000.,7000.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ1',

> REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(14.58,0.1232,0.0646,0.0646,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ2',

> REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(45.94,0.9763,0.4977,0.4977,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ3',

> REPERE='GLOBAL',

VALE=(55.13,1.1716,0.6023,0.6023,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

),);

## 3 Example of a shaft-line analysis with an arbitrary axis

Below is an example of the data setup of the same shaft line oriented along an arbitrary axis of rotation defined by its nautical angles 1

$(\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma )$ . The mesh of the rotor is built along this axis and the assignment of the elementary characteristics of the corresponding beam-type elements is done directly in the global coordinate system. As, by default, the values of the matrices of the discrete elements are expressed in the global coordinate system, one specifies REPERE = 'LOCAL' in the assignment and one defines the local coordinate system with respect to the global coordinate system by the keyword ORIENTATION [U4,42,01].

CARELEM=AFFE_CARA_ELEM(MODELE=MODELE,

POUTRE= _F(GROUP_MA='ROTOR',

SECTION='CERCLE',

CARA='R',

VALE=.05,),

DISCRET=(_F(CARA='K_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_A',

REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(0.,5.E7,7.E7,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='K_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_E',

REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(0.,5.E7,7.E7,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='A_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_A',

REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(0.,5000.,7000.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='A_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='PALIER_E',

REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(0.,5000.,7000.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ1',

> REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(14.58,0.1232,0.0646,0.0646,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ2',

> REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(45.94,0.9763,0.4977,0.4977,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

_F(CARA='M_TR_D_N',

GROUP_MA='DISQ3',

> REPERE='LOCAL',

VALE=(55.13,1.1716,0.6023,0.6023,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,),),

),

ORIENTATION=_F(GROUP_MA=('DISQ1','DISQ2','DISQ3','PALIER_A','PALIER_E'),

CARA='ANGL_NAUT',

VALE=(α,β,γ),),);

These are the angles which make it possible to pass from the global coordinate system in which the node coordinates are defined to the local coordinate system. For example: the nautical angles which allow the passage from the axis of rotation $X$ to the axis of rotation $Z$ are $(\alpha =0,\beta =-\pi /2,\gamma =0)$; the nautical angles which allow the passage from the axis of rotation $X$ to the axis of rotation $Y$ are $(\alpha =\pi /2,\beta =0,\gamma =0)$.
