# v2.04.130 SDLV130 - Cracked 3D plate subjected to loading in Mode I. Validation of modal calculation with X-FEM

**Summary:**

This test case validates the modal calculation for a $\mathrm{3D}$ plate with a crack introduced by the X-FEM method. Among other things, the newly developed RIGI_MECA, MASS_MECA and RIGI_GEOM options are valid for a X-FEM model. The plate has a horizontal and flat crack, boundary conditions are applied in 3 points to prevent rigid body modes and pressure loading is applied to both the lower and upper sides to open the crack in $I$ mode.

The reference solution, which is the subject of Modelling A, is calculated by *Code_Aster* using the similar model with the gridded crack in a classic way. The first 8 eigenmodes are calculated and their eigenfrequencies are compared.

The operation of post-processing commands for viewing a mode_meca result calculated with a X-FEM model is also tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a $\mathrm{3D}$ plate of $\mathrm{LX}=1m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=10m$ and $\mathrm{LZ}=30m$ dimensions , with a flat, horizontal $a=5m$ length, horizontal crack at mid-height (see [Figure 1.1-a]).

To obtain the reference solution, the problem is treated by the conventional method of the finished elements and the crack is meshed. However, for the validation of the modal calculation with the X-FEM method, the crack is not meshed, and the geometry is actually a healthy plate without cracks. The crack will then be introduced by level set functions directly into the command file using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator.

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.130/1000000000000214000002E4A7AB44D486F0EE8F.png)

Figure 1.1‑1.1-a: Géométrie de la plaque fissurée avec illustration du chargement et des conditions aux limites

The $\text{A (1,0,15)}$ , $\text{B ( 0,0,15 )}$ and $\text{C ( 1,3,15 )}$ points are defined to block rigid modes.

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus : $E=205000\text{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.0$

Density: $\rho =7800{\text{kg/m}}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Boundary conditions are imposed to avoid rigid body modes as follows (see Figure 1.1-a):

- clamped support of node corresponding to point $A$;
- blocking the following $Z$ displacement for point $B$;
- Blocking the following $Y$ and $Z$ moves for the point $C$ .

A uniform pressure $P=\text{10 MPa}$ is applied to the lower and upper faces to open the crack in $I$ mode.

## 2 reference solution

The reference solution is calculated by Modelling A (see next chapter) with the conventional method of elements finished by considering the mesh crack.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

3D modelling with the mesh crack is considered and the conventional elements method is used to perform the calculation. This model will serve as a reference and will allow comparison with the X-FEM method.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular mesh composed of $5\times 30\times 50$ HEXA8, respectively, along the $x$ *,* $y$ *,* $z$ axes (see [Figure 3.2-a]). The two superimposed surfaces are the crack lips.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

In this model, always 3D, the case X-FEM is considered. The crack is no longer meshed, it is introduced into the healthy mesh by the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular mesh composed of $5\times 30\times 50$ HEXA8, respectively, along the $x,y,z$ axes (see [Figure 4.2-a]). It can be seen that the crack-affected mesh cells are partitioned into tetrahedrons by X-FEM operators for numerical integration purposes such as mass and stiffness.

### 4.4 Remarks

As can be seen in the comparison of the results for this model, there are fairly small differences between the eigenfrequencies calculated with the X-FEM model and those calculated with the classical model. These differences are normal, since the mass, elastic rigidity and geometric contribution of stiffness are calculated differently for the elements X-FEM. These are partitioned in tetrahedrons on which we consider different integration patterns compared to the conventional elements. For the modal deformities, we find (see Figure 4.5-a) a very good agreement between the results from the classical calculation and those from the X-FEM calculation.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case allowed the validation of the modal calculation of an $\mathrm{3D}$ structure with cracked X-FEM. The comparison of the results considered here, the eigenfrequencies of the first 8 modes, shows a good agreement between the "classical" calculation where the crack is meshed and the calculation with X-FEM.
