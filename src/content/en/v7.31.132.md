# v7.31.132 WTNV132 - Construction of a floor column with Hujeux law

**Summary**

The following layer placement of a one-dimensional column of soil is numerically modelled, taking into account both hydromechanical coupling and soil plasticisation due to its nonlinear behaviour (by the law of Hujeux [r7.01.23](doc:r7.01.23)). The calculated solutions are compared with results from elements GEFDYN finite code of the École Centrale Paris.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The model consists of $N+1$ elements in total: it is to place $N$ layers of soil ($N=10$ in test case) on an infinitely rigid porous elastic substrate represented by a ${0}^{\mathrm{ème}}$ layer. Each layer consists of element of mesh (quadratic), each of which is a height of $\mathrm{2m}$. The sol column once constructed measures $\mathrm{20m}$ in total. As a matter of principle, the problem is two-dimensional (plane deformations occur in a vertical plane): in fact, one takes place in the hypothesis of a horizontal translational soil invariance, which requires that mechanical deformations and hydraulic fluxes are zero in the horizontal direction (edometric model).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In the model under consideration, the boundary conditions apply to $n+1$ layers present in the $n$ step of calculation, which are the same as for an oedometer (the ground column is a sample of an infinite space by horizontal translation):

The conditions of horizontal invariance:

- ${u}_{x}=\mathrm{0 }$ on the side mesh cells;

A condition for blocking the 0th layer (hardened):

- ${u}_{y}=\mathrm{0 }$ on the bottom mesh cell;

A hydraulic pressure condition at the free surface of the column:

- ${\mathrm{PRE}}_{1}=0$ on the mesh cell on top of the $n+{1}^{\mathrm{ème}}$ layer (last set);

An initial *effective* *isotropic* and *non-zero* stress state in each layer laid, because of the aversion of Hujeux's law for states of stress close to zero:

- ${\sigma}_{xx}'={\sigma}_{yy}'={\sigma}_{zz}'={\sigma}_{0}'=-{20.10}^{+3}\mathrm{Pa}$ in the $n+{1}^{\mathrm{ème}}$ layer (last set);

The loading conditions:

- The whole column is subjected to gravity ($g=9,81m/{s}^{2}$ acceleration and directed according to $-{\overrightarrow{e}}_{y}$);

The construction of the column is done by respecting a time period of $\Delta t={10}^{+6}$ seconds between the beginning of the $n$ step and the $n+1$ step. During this time period, the fluid is diffused and the column is consolidated under the effect of its own weight (sizing). It is important to ensure that this time period is sufficient, by linking it to the permeability value of the porous material 1. In particular, the product of $\Delta t$ with this permeability gives a distance of diffusion of the fluid which must be sufficient (here of the order of $10m$) in relation to the size of the column ($20m$ height).

## 2 Reference solutions

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The **multi-models** method is used to perform the calculation. At each step $n+1$ of the installation, a model containing strictly the $n+1$ layers placed is combined. The states of stress, displacement and internal variables at the end of the previous step are transferred to the next step by field transfer operations. The initial field of displacement of the laid floor layer must vary linearly from bottom to top, because it varies between the value of the settlement of the lower layers and the geometrical rib to be respected, combined with a zero initial displacement.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The solutions are post-treated in terms of settlement. However, the use of the multi-model method does not give direct access to the settlement of the column: the vertical displacement of the last layer laid is the sum of the actual settlement that it has undergone and the settlement that it has already achieved when it was not there: it is the latter component that must be removed.

Consider, for example, layer 4 (see figure below). This layer is placed at the moment $n=4$ . The settlement n-y makes sense only for $n\ge 4$ . $\delta {u}_{4}^{n}$ the settlement increment between $n$ and $n+1$ moments above layer 4. The settlement of layer 4 at the moment $n$ : $\Delta {u}_{4}^{n}$ , is defined as the accumulation of the settlement increments experienced by the layer during the overall process of construction of the soil column, i.e. by laying the successive layers above it ($n\ge 5$ ).

Thus: $\Delta {u}^{n\ge 4}=\sum_{i=4}^{n}\delta {u}_{4}^{i}$ with $\delta {u}_{4}^{n}={u}_{4}^{n}-{u}_{4}^{n-1}$

Thus, by decomposition of the iterative process, the settlement is easily rewritten as:

$\Delta {u}_{4}^{n\ge 4}={u}_{4}^{n}-{u}_{4}^{0}$ .

i.e. the movement above the 4th layer at the moment $n$ (under the influence of the layers above it), *minus its displacement when placed* ($n=4$).

The validation is performed by comparison with GEFDYN solutions provided by the École Centrale Paris.

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.132/Forme2.gif)

At the moment $n\ge 4$:

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta {u}_{4}^{n\ge 4}=\sum_{i=4}^{n}\delta {u}_{4}^{i}\\ =\sum_{i=4}^{n}{u}_{4}^{i}-{u}_{4}^{i-1}\\ ={u}_{4}^{n}-{u}_{4}^{4}={u}_{4}^{n}-{u}_{4,0}\end{array}$

For Modelling C, the PDIL_ELGA elemental option calculation is also performed to validate the numerical developments for modellings HM. The values obtained are tested in non-regression.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The results obtained during the modelling with the Finements GEFD yn software of the Ecole Centrale Paris are precise according to the convergence criteria used in this software. The definition of convergence criteria is specified in the software user manual []. The value of the criteria for displacement and hydraulic pressure is equal to ${10}^{-3}$ and the value of the criteria for mechanical (force) and hydraulic (flux) imbalances is equal to ${10}^{-2}$ .

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The **B** model is *bidimensional* and *quasi-static* . *modelization with integration* *under integrated* (MODELISATION = ‘D_PLAN_HM_SI_DIL) is used. *second gradient expansion model is used.* The constituent parameters of the second gradient model are determined so as not to alter the basic solution of the macroscopic problem. In particular, the small penalization coefficient is chosen: PENA_LAGR = 1,

The second gradient model allows the physical instability phenomena associated with the loss of elliptivity of the stress-deformation tensor to be treated, and which result in a localization of the deformations. Here, the aim is not really to regularize the problem (since there is no material instability), but to implement the second gradient model in a relatively representative test case,

This Modelling is also used to test the possibility of dynamic seismically-suspension analysis with the DYNA_NON_LINE operator. To do this, the blocking condition at the bottom of the static step column is replaced by the assignment of absorbent border elements. Then the same seismic acceleration-sustaining stress is used as in WDNP101 tests.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

For D_PLAN_HM_SI_DIL Modelling, each layer of soil is represented by a element QUAD8, i.e. 11 elements in total.

### 4.3 Tested quantities and results

Unchanged from **A** model for the static part. non-regression also test the maximum acceleration response over a short time interval.

### 4.4 Remarks

These results validate the capacity of the second expansion gradient modelling to be used for calculations with a load of gravity, then with seismic loading.
