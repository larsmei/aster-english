# u4.63.03 Macro-command DEFI_PRES_EC8

**Summary:**

This macro-command builds a formula making it possible to apply pressure fields to the walls of large tanks within the framework of a seismic verification by an equivalent mechanical analysis according to Eurocode 8.
In order to apply this pressure field, this formula must be supplied to AFFE_CHAR_MECA/FORCE_COQUE_FO.

## 1 Purpose and description

The objective of this macro-command is to build a formula making it possible to apply pressure fields to the walls of large tanks within the framework of a seismic verification by an equivalent mechanical analysis according to Eurocode 8.

### 1.1 Total pressure

Equation :eq:`eq-ptot` gives the expression of this total pressure:

$$
P_{totale} = P_{hydro} + \text{sign}(\cos(\theta)) \sqrt{\bigl(\left(P_{ir}+P_{if}\right)^2+P_{c1}^2\bigr)} \pm 0.4 \left(P_{vr} + P_{vf} \right) + P_{slib}
$$

where $\theta$ is the azimuth of the point of application of the pressure.

According to the Newmark combination chosen, $\pm$ becomes $+$, which is the combination "PC+", or $-$, which is the combination "PC-" of the keyword NEWMARK.

### 1.2 Hydrostatic pressure $P_{hydro}$

The hydrostatic pressure is given by:

$$
P_{hydro} = \rho_l g \left( H-z\right)
$$

where $\rho_l$ is the mass density of the water, $g$ is the acceleration of gravity, $H$ the height of water in the tank and $z$ the distance from the point to the bottom of the tank.

### 1.3 Rigid impulsive pressure $P_{ir}$

The rigid impulsive pressure is given by:

$$
P_{ir} = c_0(z)\rho_l H ZPA_h \cos(\theta)
$$

where $ZPA_h$ is the zero-period acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions and

$$
c_0(z) = \frac{8}{\pi^2} \sum^{\infty}_{n=0} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2} \varepsilon_n \cos \Bigl( (2n+1) \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{z}{H} \Bigr)
$$

with

$$
\varepsilon_n = \frac{I_1\biggl[(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{R}{H}  \biggr]} { I'_1\biggl[(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{R}{H} \biggr] }
$$

$I_1$ denotes the modified Bessel function of the first order and $I'_1$ its first derivative. $R$ is the radius of the tank.

When building the formula one limits oneself to the first 20 terms in the expression of $c_0$.

### 1.4 Flexible impulsive pressure $P_{if}$

The flexible impulsive pressure is given by:

$$
P_{if} = \rho_l H \cos(\theta) \Psi(z) \sum^{\infty}_{n=0} d_n \cos\biggl((2n+1) \frac{\pi}{2} \frac{z}{H}  \biggr) \left(Sa(f_{hfh})-ZPA_h\right)
$$

where $Sa(f_{hfh})$ is the acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions at the impulsive frequency of mode n (spectrum at 4%) and

$$
\Psi(z) = \frac   {\int_{0}^{H} \sin\biggl(\frac{z\pi}{2H}\biggr) \Biggl[\frac{\rho_s t(z)}{\rho_l H} + \sum^{\infty}_{n=0} b'_n \cos\biggl((2n+1) \frac{z\pi}{2H}  \biggr)    \Biggr]dz      }    {\int_{0}^{H}  \sin\biggl(\frac{z\pi}{2H}\biggr) \Biggl[\frac{\rho_s t(z)}{\rho_l H} \sin\biggl(\frac{z\pi}{2H}\biggr) + \sum^{\infty}_{n=0} d_n \cos\biggl((2n+1) \frac{z\pi}{2H}  \biggr)    \Biggr]dz      }
$$

with $\rho_s$ the mass density of the steel, $t$ the thickness of the shell wall at the point of application and

$$
b'_n = \frac{8}{\pi^2} \frac{(-1)^n}{(2n+1)^2} \varepsilon_n
$$

$$
d_n = \frac{4}{\pi} \frac{ \frac{1}{H} \int_{0}^{H} \sin\biggl(\frac{z\pi}{2H}\biggr) \cos\biggl((2n+1) \frac{z\pi}{2H}  \biggr) dz       }    {2n+1} \varepsilon_n
$$

For the infinite sum of :eq:`eq-pif`, one limits oneself to the first term (hence the first impulsive mode), which is predominant.

### 1.5 Convective pressure $P_{c1}$

The convective pressure is given by:

$$
P_{c1} = 0.837 \rho_l R \cos(\theta) \frac{\cosh \biggl(\frac{1.841z}{R} \biggr) }{\cosh \biggl(\frac{1.841H}{R} \biggr) }  Sa(f_{c1})
$$

where $Sa(f_{c1})$ is the acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions at the convective frequency of mode 1 (take the spectrum at 0.5%).

### 1.6 Rigid vertical pressure $P_{vr}$

The rigid vertical pressure is given by:

$$
P_{vr} = \rho_l ZPA_v (H-z)
$$

where $ZPA_v$ denotes the zero-period acceleration of the input spectrum in the vertical direction.

### 1.7 Flexible vertical pressure $P_{vf}$

The rigid vertical pressure is given by:

$$
P_{vf} = 0.815 f \rho_l H   \cos\biggl(\frac{z\pi}{2H}\biggr)  \Bigl( Sa(f_{vf}) -  ZPA_v \Bigr) ZPA_v
$$

with:

$$
f = 1.078 + 0.274 \ln \biggl(\frac{H}{R}\biggr) \text{ si } 0.8< \frac{H}{R}
$$

$$
f = 1 \text{ sinon}
$$

$Sa(f_{vf})$ denotes the acceleration read on the input spectrum in the vertical direction at the vertical impulsive frequency (spectrum at 4%).

### 1.8 Free-surface pressure $P_{slib}$

This pressure is supplied directly by the user and is added to the total pressure. It represents the pressure above the water height. For a point of the shell wall situated above the water height, the total pressure is equal to the free-surface pressure.

### 1.9 Computation of the pressures on the bottom of the tank

Let $r$ be the radius, in cylindrical coordinates, of the point of application of the pressure.
The hydrostatic and vertical pressures have no dependence on $r$.

#### 1.9.1 Adaptation of the rigid impulsive pressure

In order to take into account the radius $r$ smaller than the radius $R$ of the tank on the bottom, one modifies the expression of $\varepsilon_n$ (present in equation :eq:`eq-c0`) into $\varepsilon_n(r)$. :eq:`eq-epsn` then becomes:

$$
\varepsilon_n({\color{red}{r}}) = \frac{I_1\biggl[(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{\color{red}{r}}{H}  \biggr]} { I'_1\biggl[(2n+1)\frac{\pi}{2} \frac{R}{H} \biggr] }
$$

#### 1.9.2 Adaptation of the flexible impulsive pressure

Taking the radius of the point of application into account is done by postulating a linear dependence. One therefore adds the factor $r/R$ to equation :eq:`eq-pif`, which gives:

$$
P^{r}_{if}(r) = \frac{r}{R} P_{if}
$$

#### 1.9.3 Adaptation of the convective pressure

Taking the radius of the point of application into account is done by adding a factor using the first Bessel function $J_1$ . Equation :eq:`eq-pc1` becomes in this case:

$$
P_{c1} = 0.837 \rho_l R \cos(\theta) \frac{\cosh \biggl(\frac{1.841z}{R} \biggr) }{\cosh \biggl(\frac{1.841H}{R} \biggr) }  Sa(f_{c1}) {\color{red}\frac{J_1 \bigl(1.841\frac{ r}{R} \bigr) }{J_1(1.841)}}
$$

#### 1.9.4 Non-flat bottoms

The use of the formula created on a tank with a non-flat bottom is not forbidden, but no specific account has been taken of it and no validation of this type of use has been carried out.

### 1.10 Use of the formula produced

The formula created is intended to be supplied to AFFE_CHAR_MECA/FORCE_COQUE_FO. One must then supply a CARA_ELEM for the thickness and a CHAM_MATER for the mass density of the material constituting the tank.
This formula can also be evaluated directly for the purposes of tests or checks. One must then call the formula with 5 parameters: first the 3 coordinates of the point of application, then the thickness of the tank at that point and finally the mass density of the material at that point.

The formula will produce an error message if:

- the coordinate $z$ of the point to be evaluated is less than Z_FOND (see Operands)
- the radius $r$ of the point to be evaluated exceeds RAYON by more than 1%.

In the other cases, a result will be obtained; the user must therefore make sure that the mesh does indeed correspond to the geometry of the tank defined.

In practice, one uses in every case the formulae adapted for the bottom, because one does not know a priori whether the point to be evaluated belongs to the shell walls or to the bottom of the tank. But this amounts to the same thing when $r$ is equal to or very close to $R$.

## 2 Syntax

```text
formule = DEFI_PRES_EC8(
    ◆ Z_FOND = float,
    ◆ RAYON = float,
    ◆ HAUT_EAU = float,
    ◆ RHO_EAU = float,
    ◆ ACCE_SP_H = float,
    ◆ ACCE_FLEX_H_N = float,
    ◆ ACCE_FLEX_V = float,
    ◆ ACCE_SP_V = float,
    ◆ ACCE_CONV_H = float,
    ◇ GRAVITE = float (défaut: 0.0),
    ◇ PRES_SURF_LIBR = float (défaut: 0.0),
    ◇ NEWMARK = / "PC+" (par défaut),
                / "PC-",
    ◇ EVAL = _F(
         ◆ RHO = float,
         ◆ / LIST_H = float,
           / LIST_R_FOND = float,
         ◆ LIST_EPAIS = float,
         ◇ THETA = float (défaut: 0.0),
         ◆ TABLE = co,
      ),
)

◆ : obligatoire
◇ : optionnel
⟐ : présent par défaut
& : ensemble
/ : un seul parmi
| : plusieurs choix possibles
```

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operands Z_FOND, RAYON, HAUT_EAU and RHO_EAU

These four operands make it possible to define the parameters of the tank. The tank necessarily has the Z axis as axis of symmetry.

```text
◆ Z_FOND
```

Z coordinate of the bottom of the tank.

```text
◆ RAYON
```

Radius of the tank. $R$ in the formulae of Purpose and description

```text
◆ HAUT_EAU
```

Height of water inside the tank. $H$ in the formulae. It is indeed a height and not a coordinate. The Z coordinate of the points at the water surface is Z_FOND + HAUT_EAU.

```text
◆ RHO_EAU
```

Mass density of the water or of the liquid inside the tank. $\rho_l$ in the formulae.

### 3.2 Operands ACCE_SP_H, ACCE_FLEX_H_N, ACCE_FLEX_V, ACCE_SP_V and ACCE_CONV_H

These operands make it possible to define the various accelerations.

```text
◆ ACCE_SP_H
```

Zero-period acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions. $ZPA_h$ in the formulae.

```text
◆ ACCE_FLEX_H_N
```

Acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions at the impulsive frequency of mode n (spectrum at 4%). $Sa(f_{hfh})$ in the formulae.

```text
◆ ACCE_FLEX_V
```

Acceleration read on the input spectrum in the vertical direction at the vertical impulsive frequency (spectrum at 4%). $Sa(f_{vf})$ in the formulae.

```text
◆ ACCE_SP_V
```

Zero-period acceleration read on the input spectrum in the vertical direction. $ZPA_v$ denotes the

```text
◆ ACCE_CONV_H
```

Acceleration read on the input spectrum in one of the horizontal directions at the convective frequency of mode 1 (spectrum at 0.5%). $Sa(f_{c1})$ in the formulae.

### 3.3 Operands GRAVITE and PRES_SURF_LIBR

```text
◇ GRAVITE
```

Acceleration of gravity. Zero by default. Makes it possible to take the hydrostatic pressure into account.

```text
◇ PRES_SURF_LIBR
```

Value of the pressure on the free surface. Zero by default. It is added to the total pressure. The only pressure taken into account above the water height.

### 3.4 Operand NEWMARK

```text
◇ NEWMARK = / "PC+" (par défaut),
            / "PC-",
```

Makes it possible to choose the Newmark combination. In equation :eq:`eq-ptot`, $\pm$ is $+$ in the case "PC+" and $-$ in the case "PC-".

### 3.5 Factor keyword EVAL

Once the formula for the total pressure has been created, there is no longer access to the various pressures making up the total pressure. The keyword EVAL makes it possible to evaluate and to store the various pressures as well as the total pressure in a table for a certain number of points of the tank.
The evaluation is always carried out on the points of one and the same line, either a vertical line of the shell wall, or a line of the bottom ($theta$ constant).

```text
◇ EVAL = _F(
     ◆ RHO = float,
     ◆ / LIST_H = float,
       / LIST_R_FOND = float,
     ◆ LIST_EPAIS = float,
     ◇ THETA = float (défaut: 0.0),
     ◆ TABLE = co,
  ),
```

```text
◆ RHO
```

Mass density of the tank at the points to be evaluated. One does not allow it here to vary according to the points.

```text
◆ / LIST_H
```

List of heights on the vertical line of the shell wall to be evaluated. The bottom of the tank is at height 0.

```text
◆ / LIST_R_FOND
```

List of radii on the horizontal line going from the centre towards a low point of the shell wall.

```text
◆ LIST_EPAIS
```

List of the thicknesses corresponding to the points supplied by LIST_H or LIST_R_FOND

```text
◇ THETA
```

Azimuth of the line to which the evaluated points belong. Zero by default; the points then have for coordinates $x$ and $y$ respectively $R$ and 0, and in the other cases $\cos(theta)$ and $\sin(theta)$.

```text
◆ TABLE
```

Name of the table in which the various pressures will be stored for each evaluated point.
