# v2.02.302 SDLL302 – A beam subject to the "El Centro" accelerogram at each end, one step away from the other

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the multi-support in linear transient dynamics on the simple case of a beam subject to acceleration on each of its ends where it is clamped. Two modellings in elements finished Timoshenko beam are proposed, in linear transient dynamics solved on modal basis and on elements finite (direct) basis:

- Model A: the same accelerogram is applied on both ends; mono- and multi-support solutions are compared;
- Model B: the accelerogram is dephased between the two ends.

The B Modelling also helps to validate MOMENT_EQUIVALENT's POST_RCCM/B3600 option.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A beam of length $L=7,62m$ , rectangular cross section ($\mathit{Hy}=0,0508m$ and $\mathit{Hz}=0,0254m$ ) is considered and is oriented on the $\mathit{Ox}$ axis .

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is elastic isotropic with properties of:

- Young's modulus: $E=206,8{10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$
- Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0,3$
- density: $\rho =7780,0\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The beam is clamped at each end.

The load is a seismic excitation perpendicular to the direction of the beam, expressed as an accelerogram. It is taken from a recording of the earthquake called El Centro (). The direction of the earthquake is the $\mathit{Oy}$ axis. The sampling is $0,01s$ .

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.302/100000000000034A00000253F4218E05A6F5E129.png)

Illustration 1: "El Centro" acceleration from 2 s.

For model A, a single-supplied excitation is considered: the same accelerogram is imposed on both ends of the beam.

For model B, a multi-sold excitation is considered: the accelerogram imposed on both ends is the same as in single-solds, but in this case at the opposite end it is applied with a delay of $0,25s$ from the original.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The beam is considered to be at rest before the earthquake arrives.

## 2 reference solution

This test case is a result of the independent validation campaign of *Code_Aster* to earthquake calculations. This document refers to calculations obtained with the same type and number of elements finished beam and compared:

- The software *Castem2000* in a single support situation. In [1], it is indicated that the differences between *Castem2000* and *Code_Aster* obtained on the RMS absolute transverse displacement values in the middle of the beam are less than 0.41%.
- *Abacus* software in single support and multi-support situations [1].
- the software *Hercules* in a single-support situation [2] .

However, in the absence of more information on multi-support results, the test case results are considered to be non-regression and inter-comparison results between different operators (linear transient calculation on modal basis, direct linear transient calculation) and different time integration methods (NEWMARK, DEVOGELAERE).

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

POU_D_T modelling is used.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh contains 20 elements of type SEG2.

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

The first six eigenfrequencies of the clamped beam are tested:

The transverse displacement of the centre of the beam in the direction of the earthquake is tested at different times.

### 3.4 Remarks

The evolution of the movement of the beam centre can be compared between the calculation on a physical basis and the calculation on a modal basis ().

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.302/100000000000034A0000025382315F9614ACF7DB.png)

Illustration 2: SDLL302 a: transient comparison on physical basis vs modal base, transverse displacement in the middle of the beam.

It is noted that the absolute displacement calculated as a transient on a physical basis or as a transient on a modal basis is identical. However, it appears very irregular, especially in comparison with the relative displacement of the same point. It is an artifact due to the method used to integrate the accelerogram and determine the drive displacement.

In the test case the SIMPSON method was used, while the TRAPEZE method (the default value of the INTEGRE keyword of the CALC_FONCTION [U4.32.04](doc:u4.32.04) command) is recommended. It is checked that with this latter method of signal integration a much smoother evolution of absolute displacement is obtained.

### 4.4 Remarks

The following figure shows the evolution of the movement of the centre of the beam by the calculation on a physical basis and by the calculation on a modal basis. It should be noted that these two movements are not comparable in the sense that the calculation on a physical basis gives a relative shift which is difficult to interpret in the multi-support case: the concept of drive displacement is no longer trivial for the calculation on a multi-support basis. Therefore, the two results cannot be compared or drawn from the conclusion.

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.302/100000000000034A00000253C1F9C5FE79DBC3DB.png)

Illustration 3: SDLL302 b: cross displacement in the middle of the beam in multi-support calculation.

In contrast to Modelling A, the modally calculated displacement appears to be smooth (according to the "physical sense" of the engineer). This is simply due to the fact that in Modelling B the default option (the TRAPEZE method) was left to integrate the accelerogram, but it is recommended because it does not create fake oscillations such as those produced by the SIMPSON method).

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case is a non-regression result verification of the multi-support transient calculation. In the absence of external reference and real inter-comparison between the different methods, however, it cannot be considered as a validation of the multi-support functionality.
