# v8.22.302 AHLV302 – Vibro-absorbent input anechoic wave guide

**Summary:**

The objective of this test case is to calculate the pressure fields in an anechoic output waveguide whose acoustic excitation is provided by an boundary condition of speed imposed on an absorbent panel defined in the input section.

This test uses the elements vibro-acoustic finished:

- Modelling A: 3D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P_PHI'
- Model B: 2D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P_PHI'
- Modelling C: AXIS_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P_PHI'
- Modelling D: 3D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P'
- Modelling E: 2D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P'
- Modelling F: AXIS_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_P'
- Modelling G: 3D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_PSI'
- Modelling H: 2D_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_PSI'
- Modelling I: AXIS_FLUIDE, FORMULATION ='U_PSI'

An exact analytical solution exists. This test case allows you to validate the hardware properties definition features for a non-amortized fluid, boundary conditions definition in speed and impedance, as well as the tools for creating and assembling mass matrices, stiffness and acoustic damping. On the other hand, it allows you to validate the acoustic pressure calculation.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The $p$ pressure field checks the Helmholtz equation on the $x$ axis:

$\frac{{d}^{2}p}{{\mathit{dx}}^{2}}+{k}^{2}p=0$ , $x\in [0,L]$

In $x=0$, you can find boundary conditions speed and ${Z}_{\mathit{n0}}$ impedance imposed:

$p(x=0)={Z}_{\mathit{n0}}\left({V}_{n}(x=0)-{V}_{n}^{s}\right)$

what gives in terms of imposed administration:

${V}_{n}(x=0)={V}_{n}^{s}+{A}_{\mathit{n0}}p(x=0)$

In $x=L$, the ${Z}_{\mathit{nL}}$ impedance (or its reverse, ${A}_{\mathit{nL}}$ admittance) is imposed:

$\frac{-\frac{\mathit{dp}}{\mathit{dx}}}{-i\rho \omega }={A}_{n}p$

The pressure in the waveguide is as follows:

$p(x)={C}_{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathit{ikx}}+{C}_{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathit{ikx}}$

Thus, the pressure derivative can be expressed:

$\frac{\mathit{dp}(x)}{\mathit{dx}}=-{\mathit{ikC}}_{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathit{ikx}}+{\mathit{ikC}}_{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathit{ikx}}$

Boundary conditions $x=0$ allows you to write:

$-{\mathit{ikC}}_{1}+{\mathit{ikC}}_{2}=-i\rho \omega {V}_{n}^{s}-i\rho \omega {A}_{\mathit{n0}}({C}_{1}+{C}_{2})$

The boundary condition $x=L$ allows you to write:

${\mathit{ikC}}_{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathit{ikL}}-{\mathit{ikC}}_{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathit{ikL}}=-i\rho \omega {A}_{\mathit{nL}}({C}_{1}{\mathrm{e}}^{-\mathit{ikL}}+{C}_{2}{\mathrm{e}}^{\mathit{ikL}})$

These last two relationships allow to write a system of equations whose resolution allows to determine the constants ${C}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}$ and thus the pressure and particle speed in any point of the waveguide.

For simplicity, the analytical expression of the ${C}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}$ coefficients is not provided here.

### 2.2 Baseline

The pressure is calculated at $A$ , $B$ , $C$ and $D$ points.
