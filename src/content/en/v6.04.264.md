# v6.04.264 SSNV264 – Non-linear isotropic hardening viscoplasticity VISC_ISOT_NL under triaxial loading and shearing

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the constitutive law VISC_ISOT_NL integration algorithm. The problem studied is a hollow cylinder with a triaxial + shear stress state with constant components (space) in the cylindrical marker. This is the viscoplastic version of the [V6.04.263](doc:v6.04.263) test, which was limited to plasticity independent of the loading speed.

The problem is implemented:

- AXIS_SI in Modelling A
- in AXIS_GRAD_VARI in Modelling B
- in 3D in Modelling C
- in 3D_GRAD_VARI in Modelling D

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This is a section of a cylinder in the case of modellings with axial symmetry or a quarter of a cylinder (symmetries) in the case of modellings 3D. The cylinder has $H=15\text{mm}$ height, ${R}_{i}=50\text{mm}$ internal radius and ${R}_{e}=100\text{mm}$ external radius.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

This is a load in force imposed. They are carried out at the edges and in volume according to the formulas of the analytical solution, in space and time. This load is supplemented by a minimum boundary condition to block the movement of vertical rigid bodies (a vertical point of the circumference at the intersection of the lower face and the inner wall) and the symmetry conditions in the modellings 3D.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Elastic solution of the problem with imposed initial forces, as described in the analytical solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The methodology described in the [V6.04.263](doc:v6.04.263) specification, in which an axial symmetry stress state is imposed and the ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}={\sigma}_{\theta \theta }$ , ${\sigma}_{zz}$ , and ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}$ components are not dependent on the $(r,z)$ position, is used. The equilibrium equations in the volume are verified by imposing volume forces in the cylinder and superfacial forces on the cylinder edges:

  - in volume: $f=-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}/r{e}_{z}$
  - $z=H$ top edge: $T={\sigma}_{zz}{e}_{z}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{r}$
  - $z=0$ lower edge: $T=-{\sigma}_{zz}{e}_{z}-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{r}$
  - $r={R}_{i}$ inner wall: $T=-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}{e}_{r}-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{z}$
  - $r={R}_{e}$ exterior wall: $T={\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}{e}_{r}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{z}$

The deformation field components in the cylindrical marker are also constant (space) and are limited to ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}={\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }$ , ${\epsilon}_{zz}$ and ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}$ . This deformation field is geometrically compatible with the following (axial symmetry) displacement field:

${u}_{r}={\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}r;{u}_{z}={\epsilon}_{zz}z+2{\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}(r-{R}_{i})$

where the integration constant has been fixed so that ${u}_{z}({R}_{i},0)=0$ , boundary condition which blocks the rigid body movement.

The path of the transient in the space of monotonous estradial stresses:

$\sigma (x,t)=q(t){\Sigma}^{0}(x)$

${\Sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{0}={\Sigma}_{\theta \theta }^{0}=\frac{1}{6};{\Sigma}_{zz}^{0}=\frac{2}{3};{\Sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}$

And in this case, ${\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}=q$. The equation of evolution of plastic deformation easily integrates since the direction is fixed, noting $\kappa$ the numbing variable:

${\epsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\kappa \mathit{dev}({\Sigma}^{0})$

Finally, the elastic deformation is deduced directly from the stress and allows to trace the $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{p}$ deformation and displacements:

${\epsilon}^{e}=q\left[\frac{1-\nu }{E}{\Sigma}^{0}-\frac{\nu}{E}\mathit{tr}{\Sigma}^{0}\mathit{Id}\right]$

The evolution of $\kappa$ is therefore still to be determined according to the loading level $q(t)$. $R(\kappa )$ is noted the working function:

$R(\kappa )={R}_{0}+{R}_{H}\kappa +{R}_{1}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{1}\kappa })+{R}_{2}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{2}\kappa })+{R}_{K}{(\kappa +{p}_{0})}^{{\gamma}_{M}}$

The Norton law provides the following expression that links the load to the nutrification variable:

$\dot{\kappa}={\left(\frac{⟨q-R(\kappa )⟩}{K}\right)}^{n}$

Rather than explicitly solving this differential equation, we can start by inverting:

$q=R(\kappa )+V(\dot{\kappa});V(\dot{\kappa})=K{\dot{\kappa}}^{\frac{1}{n}}$

By choosing a linear $\kappa (t)={\dot{ϵ}}_{0}t$ evolution in time, the load to be applied by the previous relationship is inferred. In practice, one considers a transient of 1s, with ${\dot{ϵ}}_{0}=0.05{s}^{-1}$ .

The initial state of $t=0$ remains to be considered. The nutring variable is zero as well as plastic deformation, taking into account previous relationships. On the other hand, the applied stress is not zero since $q(0)=V({\dot{ϵ}}_{0})={q}_{0}$ . The initial mechanical state is therefore characterized by non-zero deformation, solution of the problem **elastic** under load ${q}_{0}$ .

In the case of a plastic gradient formulation, the results remain unchanged, as the $\kappa$ nutrification variable is homogeneous, so that its gradient is zero: non-local effects do not appear in this problem (to the errors of spatial discretization near).

### 2.2 Baseline

It will be ensured that by targeting a 5% cumulative plastic deformation achieved in a second at constant rate, the model is well restored to the expected stress levels (${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}$), plastic displacements and deformations (${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}^{p}$).
