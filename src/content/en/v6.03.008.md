# v6.03.008 SSNP008 - Stability of a two-tiered slope with waterline and matrix suction

**Summary:**

This test case is one of the tutorials of the Geostudio SLOPE/W software oriented to beginner engineers who want to analyze slope stability using the equilibrium limit method. It highlights the main stability analysis elements such as soil stratification, material definition, research area and hydraulic pressure field[1].

In this test case the SLOPE/W results are reproduced with the `CALC_STAB_PENTE` macro-command using all 4 LEM methods available in the macro, which allows you to validate the following features:

- Definition of hydrostatic pressure.
- Definition of the pressure field by the water line.
- Consider the matrix suction by constant suction angle and limit its maximum value.
- Initialization of EFWA by Bishop result in Morgenstern-Price and Spencer methods.

**By comparing the result with the reference value, the FS result of CALC_STAB_PENTE shows an error of less than 1.0% for all the FS resolution methods.**

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The Fellenius method is used to resolve the safety factor and locate the critical surface. According to the preliminary study, the abscisses of the ends of the fracture surface are fixed at 7.6m and 30.8m to reduce the calculation time. In the overall search, the search space is discretized into 15 potential fracture surfaces. The slippery mass is divided into 15 slices of soil. 4 refinements of the mesh are performed at the end of the critical surface location to obtain the value of the independent FS of the mesh.

### 3.2 Tested quantities and results

The safety factor is tested at the last refinement of the mesh and the geometric parameters of the critical surface. non-regression tests are also added to the intermediate results, including the FS before the refinement of the mesh and the number of surfaces tested.
The results are shown in Table 3.

**Table 3**: Reference values and intermediate results of modelling A

| **NUME_RAFF** | **Identification** | **Aster result** | **Reference value** | **Error/Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | FS | 1.2519802618407652 | 1.256 | 0.32% |
| 0 | CENTRE_X | 23.994507712938862 | 23.615194 | 1.6% |
| 0 | CENTRE_Y | 20.12325789401816 | 19.344014 | 4.0% |
| 0 | RAYON | 17.50069056883949 | 16.883 | 3.7% |
| 0 | NB_SURF_TEST | 21 | non-regression | 0,0001% |
| 0 | FS | 1.202741792451407 | non-regression | 0,0001% |

The error in the FS is 0.32%, which is probably caused by the difference in Fellenius formulation with SLOPE/W.

### 3.3 Summary of the results

The result of the FS from the CALC_STAB_PENTE macro-command gives the difference 0.32%
in relation to reference solution. This proves the relevance of the Fellenius method
with hydraulic pressure, distributed pressure and matrix suction.

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The same configurations as Modelling A are used except that Morgenstern-Price is used to resolve the safety factor.
The critical surface being circular, the same critical surface as the model B (simplified Bishop method) is used to disable the EFWA by putting `N = 1` and `ITER_MAXI = 0` in order to speed up the calculation and avoid uncertainty in the result due to the stochastic optimization algorithm.
