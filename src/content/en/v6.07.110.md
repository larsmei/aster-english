# v6.07.110 COMP010 – Thermomechanical validation of elastoviscoplastic laws

**Summary**

This test validates the temperature variation in the elastoviscoplastic constitutive laws. These tests allow to check the following two points:

- Thermal expansion is well calculated (with consideration of the variation of thermal expansion with temperature)
- The variation of material coefficients with temperature is correct, especially in incremental behavior resolution,

The validated laws of behaviour are as follows:

- $A$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the GRAN_IRRA_LOG model
- $B$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the LEMAITRE model
- $C$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the LEMA_SEUIL model
- $E$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the LEMAITRE_IRRA model
- $F$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the VISC_IRRA_LOG model
- $G$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the VISC_TAHERI model
- $H$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the ROUSS_VISC model
- $I$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the model
- $J$ Modelling: this Modelling allows to validate the META_LEMA_ANI model

## 1 Methodology

It is a double simulation, the first in thermomechanical, the second in pure mechanics. The first will be validated in comparison with the second, assuming of course that the behaviour tested provides a correct solution in pure mechanics.

The first simulation (thermo-mechanical solution that l-on is seeking to validate) consists of applying a temperature variation on a material point, with a deformation imposed zero on the $x$ axis

$$
{\epsilon}_{xx}^{1}=0
$$

The temperature imposed is increasing linearly as a function of time. The transient is made up of NCAL not.According to the modellings:

- Modellings $A$ , $C$ , $E$ , $F$ : temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=0°C$ to ${T}_{max}=500°C$ and reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=0°C$ .
- Modellings $B$ , $G$ : temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=20°C$ to ${T}_{max}=500°C$ and the reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=20°C$ .
- $H$ Modelling: temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=20°C$ to ${T}_{max}=800°C$ and reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=20°C$.
- $I$ Modelling: temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=20°C$ to ${T}_{max}=200°C$ and reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=20°C$.
- $J$ Modelling: temperature varies from ${T}_{0}=700°C$ to ${T}_{max}=1000°C$ and reference temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=700°C$.

The second simulation (which must be equivalent to the first) consists of applying to the same material point a deformation imposed following $x$ such as:

$$
{\epsilon}_{xx}^{2}=-{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=-\alpha (T)(T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})
$$

In pure mechanical design on NCAL moments of thermomechanical calculations. Each $i$ calculation, the load is made of thermal deformation.

$$
{\epsilon}_{xx}^{2}=-{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}=-\alpha ({T}_{i})({T}_{i}-{T}_{\mathit{ref}})
$$

The initial load consists of the deformations, stresses and internal variables of the previous mechanical calculation, the stresses being corrected for the variation of the Young's modulus

Indeed, for any behavior (assuming the additive decomposition of deformations):

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}=E(T)({\varepsilon}_{xx}-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}-{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p})
$$

In the first case:

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}^{1}=E(T)(0-{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}-{\epsilon}_{xx}^{p})
$$

In the second case:

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}^{2}=E(T)(\epsilon -{\epsilon}_{xx}^{p})
$$

Therefore, at every moment, you can apply ${\varepsilon}_{xx}=-{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=-\alpha (T)(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})$ for mechanical calculation.

In addition, in order to obtain the same results in both cases, it is necessary, at each time step of the second simulation, to perform the pure mechanical calculation with coefficients whose values are interpolated according to the temperature at the current moment. This interpolation is performed in the command file of the test, in a loop in outside time at SIMU_POINT_MAT / STAT_NON_LINE.

## 2 Interpretation of results

The purpose is to verify with TEST_TABLE that the result obtained at each moment of the thermomechanical transient of the first simulation is identical to the result obtained with the second simulation.

The validation is done by comparing the fields calculated at each step of the transient on the one hand with the result of a mechanical calculation on the other hand. The reference value being the component of the field extracted at a given moment $i$ from the first thermomechanical simulation performed on NCAL moments. The calculated value is that obtained at the end of the mechanical calculation $i+1$ of the loop on the NCAL.
