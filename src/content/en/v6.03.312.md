# v6.03.312 SSNP312 - DMT94.132 Interface-parallel crack in bimetallic CT test piece

**Summary:**

This test is based on the independent validation of version 3 in the mechanical of the breakage.

This is a two-dimensional static test (plane deformations) for calculating a crack parallel to the interface between two materials for a nontrivial geometry in a bounded domain.

The structure has an elastoplastic behaviour of Von Isotropic hardening bees. The calculations are conducted in nonlinear elasticity.

The objective of this test case is to study the sensitivity of $G$ to crown choice.

It consists of two modellings $\mathrm{2D}$ planees in which the influence of an imposed incremental displacement is studied. The first model uses linear elements, the other quadratic elements.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

Given the dissymmetry of the materials, the entire test piece is modelled.

**Blocks:**

| $\mathrm{UX}=\mathrm{UY}=0$ | point $B$ (centre of lower pin) |
| --- | --- |
| $\mathrm{UX}=0$ | point $A$ (centre of the top pin) |

**Imposed displacement charge:**

$$
0\le \mathrm{UY}\le 1\mathrm{mm}
$$

The load is therefore monotonous increasing.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution used is a semiempirical formula resulting from the work of the ASTM [bib1].

### 2.2 Baseline

The formula of the ASTM for Rice $J$ integral is as follows:

${J}_{\mathit{ASTM}}=(2+0,522\mathrm{\ast }{b}_{0}/w)\mathrm{\ast }A/{b}_{0}$ ,

where ${b}_{0}=w-{a}_{0}$ is the initial length of the ligament and where $A$ is the area under the load-moving curve to point $A$ , i.e. the applied load work.

The integral ${J}_{\mathit{ASTM}}$ is compared in the figure below to the energy release rate $G$ resulting from a elements calculation finished with CASTEM2000 and theta method [bib2]. The force-moving response curve and the crack lip opening calculated by elements are also shown.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

It should be noted that the reference solution is not an exact solution and does not generally apply to bi-materials. However, it is usable for this study because the crack is not located at the interface of the two materials.

The difference maximum between the CASTEM2000 results and the ASTM formula is about $\text{9 \%}$ for the first crown (closest to the crack) and maximum loading. This difference decreases when you take crowns further away from the crack bottom.

Castem 2000 was performed on the same mesh model as the A model of this test case.

## 5 Summary of the results

It is recalled that reference solution is not an exact solution and does not generally apply to bi-materials, but calculations are made in non-linear elasticity on a crack; in all fairness, elastoplasticity calculations should be made on a cut.

Model A (degree 1) gives results consistent with those of the reference.

Modelling B (degree 2) shows an difference of about $\text{8\%}$ on the $G$ value.

It can be noted that the crowns far from the crack provide more accurate and stable results than those close to the crack bottom.
