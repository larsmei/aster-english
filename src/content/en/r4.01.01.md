# r4.01.01 Pre- and post-processing for thin shells in composite materials

**Summary:**

The results of the plate-element theory set out in the documentation [r3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) are extended to the case of multilayer orthotropic materials. The documentation proposed brings together the thermal and thermo-elasto-mechanical aspects. The use of these materials is theoretically valid only in the case of a geometric symmetry with respect to the middle sheet of the plate. It is therefore necessary that the membrane-bending coupling be zero.

## 1 Introduction

The modelling of the thermo-mechanical behaviour by a shell theory of structures made up of laminated composite materials presents, with respect to the homogeneous isotropic case, a certain number of particular features:

- the coefficients entering the linear constitutive relations which link the mechanical and thermal quantities (grandeurs) defined on the middle surface of the shell must be calculated from the spatial distribution through the thickness of the various materials,
- the constituent materials of the shell are in general orthotropic:
- one must define, at each point of the middle surface of the shell, a material direction fixing the frame in which the constitutive relations are described,
- the form of the anisotropy produced on the global behaviour of the shell may be arbitrary,
- finally, couplings between quantities characterising phenomena which are symmetric and antisymmetric with respect to the middle surface may appear (bending-membrane coupling, coupling between the mean temperature and the mean gradient through the thickness). In thermomechanics the results presented are however theoretically valid only when the membrane-bending coupling is zero,
- the analysis of the fracture or of the damage of these structures requires one to return to a level of description finer than that supplied by the shell models: the criteria are formulated, layer by layer through the thickness, as a function of the "three-dimensional" stresses.

The pre-processing allows the user to "build" the quantities entering the shell theories from a simple spatial description of the distribution of the various materials (position, thickness, orientation).

The post-processing comes into play once the structural analysis is completed, in order to supply, layer by layer, an evaluation of a few fracture or damage criteria.

The choice made here is to specify the pre- and post-processings so that they are independent, within the framework of the shell models adopted, of the type of element chosen by the user in order to carry out the structural analysis. Indeed, the numerical difficulties of the analysis of shells and of the representation of their geometry lead one to propose, depending on the situations, several types of shell or plate finite elements.

The note is divided into three parts. The first briefly recalls the assumptions of the shell theory used for the thermo-mechanical analyses and the expressions of the homogenised coefficients to be introduced. The second specifies the choices adopted for the description of the orientation of the materials with respect to the elements, as well as a few notations. The last part details the application of these choices to the case of shells made up of homogeneous plies.

In order to allow the use of the analysis options available in *Code_Aster* , it is therefore necessary to define pre- and post-processing commands for laminated composite materials which are compatible with the existing commands.

## 2 Homogenised characteristics of a thin shell in thermoelasticity and in thermal analysis

### 2.1 Notation - Assumptions

The shell is made up of different layers of orthotropic materials arranged parallel to the middle surface $\Sigma$ (see r4.01.01-fig-coque).

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.01/1000020000000800000002F293CE4575D168D4A9.png)

*Shell*

Denoting $({x}_{\gamma})$ the coordinates $({x}_{1},{x}_{2})$ on $\Sigma$ and ${x}_{3}$ the coordinate normal to the surface ${x}_{3}\in \left]-h,h\right[$  one may define the various characteristics of the materials entering the thermal analysis and the thermoelasticity. One will furthermore assume that **one of the axes of orthotropy coincides with the normal** $n$ **at the point** $({x}_{\gamma})$ **to the shell** $\Sigma$ .

- Conductivity: ${k}_{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)$ and ${k}_{33}\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)$
- Volumetric heat capacity: $\mathrm{\rho c}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$
- Expansion coefficients: ${d}_{\text{αβ}}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$
- Elastic stiffness (plane stress): ${\Lambda}_{\text{αβλ}\mu }({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$
- Shear stiffness: ${\Lambda}_{\mathrm{\alpha 3\lambda 3}}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$
- Density: $\rho ({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})$

The Greek indices run over $\left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace$ . The system $\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)$ does not necessarily correspond to the axes of orthotropy of the materials in the tangent plane.

### 2.2 Thermal analysis

One places oneself within the framework of the thermal shell model described in [r3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01) and []. A "shell" temperature field is represented by the three fields $({T}^{m},{T}^{s},{T}^{i})$ defined on $\Sigma$ in the following way through the thickness:

$$
T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})=\sum_{j=1}^{3}{T}^{j}({x}_{\gamma}){P}_{j}({x}_{3})={T}^{m}({x}_{\gamma}){P}_{1}({x}_{3})+{T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma}){P}_{2}({x}_{3})+{T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma}){P}_{3}({x}_{3})
$$

Where the ${P}_{j}$ are the Lagrange polynomials:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}I=\left]-h,h\right[:& {P}_{1}({x}_{3})=1-{({x}_{3}/h)}^{2}\\ & {P}_{2}({x}_{3})=\frac{{x}_{3}}{\mathrm{2h}}(1+{x}_{3}/h)\\ & {P}_{3}({x}_{3})=-\frac{{x}_{3}}{\mathrm{2h}}(1-{x}_{3}/h)\end{array}
$$

The interpretation of the fields ${T}^{j}$ is then the following. First ${T}^{m}\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)$ , which represents the temperature on the middle surface of the shell):

$$
{T}^{m}({x}_{\gamma})=T({x}_{\gamma},0)
$$

Then the temperature on the upper surface of the shell:

$$
{T}^{s}({x}_{\gamma})=T({x}_{\gamma},+h)
$$

And the temperature on the lower surface of the shell:

$$
{T}^{i}({x}_{\gamma})=T({x}_{\gamma},-h)
$$

Thanks to the representation (), one calculates the bilinear form ${K}_{S}^{T}$ of $({T}^{m},{T}^{s},{T}^{i})\equiv T$ from the form of the 3D problem (the indices $ij$ take the values $m,s,i$ ):

$$
{K}_{\Sigma}^{T}\left(T,\tau \right)={\int}_{\Sigma}\left({A}_{\text{αβ}}^{ji}.{T}_{,\alpha }^{i}.{\tau}_{,\beta }^{j}+{B}^{ji}.{T}^{i}.{\tau}^{j}\right)d\Sigma
$$

With the usual convention of summation on repeated indices). Where $\tau$ is a virtual temperature field and where:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{A}_{\alpha \beta }^{ij}={A}_{\alpha \beta }^{ji}={A}_{\beta \alpha }^{ij},{B}^{ij}={B}^{ji}\\ {A}_{\alpha \beta }^{ij}({x}_{\gamma})={\int}_{I}{k}_{\alpha \beta }({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}){P}_{i}({x}_{3}){P}_{j}({x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {B}^{ij}({x}_{\gamma})={\int}_{I}{k}_{33}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})\frac{\partial {P}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{3}}({x}_{3})\frac{\partial {P}_{j}}{\partial {x}_{3}}({x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}
$$

The bilinear form linked to the volumetric heat capacity in the evolution problem is written:

$$
\begin{array}{c}M(T,\tau )={\int}_{S}{C}^{ij}.{T}^{i}.{t}^{j}\\ {C}^{ij}({x}_{g})={\int}_{I}\mathrm{\rho c}({x}_{g},{x}_{3}){P}_{i}({x}_{3}){P}_{i}({x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}
$$

### 2.3 Thermo-mechanics

One places oneself within the framework of the Love-Kirchhoff shell modelling (thin shell) or of the Reissner-Mindlin modelling (thick shell). In both cases, the sections are assumed to remain plane. The strains of the plane tangent to $\Sigma$ are therefore expressed, through the thickness, by means of the strain tensors ${E}_{\alpha \beta }({x}_{\gamma})$ , of curvature change ${K}_{\alpha \beta }({x}_{\gamma})$ and of distortion ${\gamma}_{\alpha}({x}_{\gamma})$ of the surface []:

$$
{e}_{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)={E}_{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)+{x}_{3}{K}_{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)
$$

The material undergoing a local strain of thermal origin given by (${T}^{\text{réf}}$  is the reference temperature):

$$
{e}_{\alpha \beta }^{\text{th}}({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})=(T({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})-{T}^{\text{réf}}){d}_{\alpha \beta }({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3})
$$

The local stress field is given by the thermoelastic law in plane stresses:

$$
{s}_{\alpha \beta }={L}_{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }\left({e}_{\lambda \mu }-{e}_{\lambda \mu }^{\text{th}}\right)
$$

That is, with the previous model for $T$ :

$$
\begin{array}{c}\text{}{s}_{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)={L}_{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)\left[{E}_{\lambda \mu }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)+{x}_{3}{K}_{\lambda \mu }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)-{e}_{\lambda \mu }^{\text{th}}\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)\right]\end{array}
$$

With:

$$
{e}_{\lambda \mu }^{\text{th}}\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)=\left(\sum_{j=1}^{3}{T}^{j}\left({x}_{\gamma}\right).{P}_{j}\left({x}_{3}\right)-{T}^{\text{réf}}\right){d}_{\lambda \mu }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)
$$

The generalised forces (bending ${M}^{\alpha \beta }$ and membrane ${N}^{\alpha \beta }$ ) are linked to $\sigma$ by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{M}^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)={\int}_{I}{s}^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right){x}_{3}{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {N}^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)={\int}_{I}{s}^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right){\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}
$$

So that the constitutive law of the shell is written at the point ${x}_{\gamma}$ :

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{M}^{\alpha \beta }=& {P}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{K}_{\lambda \mu }+{Q}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{E}_{\lambda \mu }+{M}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }\\ {N}^{\alpha \beta }=& {R}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{E}_{\lambda \mu }+{Q}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{K}_{\lambda \mu }+{N}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }\end{array}
$$

Where:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{P}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }=& +{\int}_{I}{L}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }({x}_{3}){x}_{3}^{2}{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {Q}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }=& +{\int}_{I}{L}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }({x}_{3}){x}_{3}{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {R}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }=& +{\int}_{I}{L}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }({x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {N}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }=& -{\int}_{I}{L}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{e}_{\lambda \mu }^{\text{th}}{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {M}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }=& -{\int}_{I}{L}^{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{e}_{\lambda \mu }^{\text{th}}{x}_{3}{\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}
$$

When the temperature is calculated by the thermal model, one may express the thermal forces directly as a function of the three components $({T}^{m},{T}^{s},{T}^{i})$ :

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{}{M}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }=-\left[{\int}_{I}{L}_{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{d}_{\lambda \mu }({x}_{3}){P}_{j}({x}_{3}){x}_{3}{\text{dx}}_{3}\right]({T}^{j}-{T}^{\text{réf}})={\text{DM}}_{j}^{\alpha \beta }({T}^{j}-{T}^{\text{réf}})\\ {N}_{\text{th}}^{\alpha \beta }=-\left[{\int}_{I}{L}_{\alpha \beta \lambda \mu }{d}_{\lambda \mu }({x}_{3}){P}_{j}({x}_{3}){\text{dx}}_{3}\right]({T}^{j}-{T}^{\text{réf}})={\text{DN}}_{j}^{\alpha \beta }({T}^{j}-{T}^{\text{réf}})\end{array}
$$

The quantities $\text{DN}$ and $\text{DM}$ depend only on the constituent materials of the shell and on their distribution.

**Note :**

*When the arrangement of the materials is symmetric with respect to $Sigma$ , certain integrals, being a sum of odd terms, vanish: ${Q}^{alpha beta lambda mu }=0$ , ${text{DM}}_{1}^{alpha beta }={text{DM}}_{3}^{alpha beta }=0$ and ${text{DN}}_{2}^{alpha beta }=0$ .*

The shear forces and the transverse shear stresses are obtained by writing the local equilibrium equations without body force:

$$
{\sigma}_{,j}^{ij}=0
$$

Which makes it possible to write the shear force:

$$
{V}^{\alpha}\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)={M}_{,\beta }^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma}\right)
$$

And the shear stress:

$$
{\sigma}^{\alpha 3}\left({x}_{\gamma},{x}_{3}\right)=-{\int}_{-h}^{{x}_{3}}{\sigma}_{,\beta }^{\alpha \beta }\left({x}_{\gamma},z\right)\mathit{dz}
$$

Using the fact that ${\sigma}^{\alpha 3}\left({x}_{\gamma ,+h}\right)={\sigma}^{\alpha 3}\left({x}_{\gamma ,-h}\right)=0$ . The role of the pre-processing is to calculate the various quantities $A$ , $B$ , $C$ , $P$ , $Q$ , $R$ , $\mathit{DM}$ and $\mathit{DN}$ , from the description of the material (number, orientation and thickness of the various layers, local characteristics $\rho \text{c}$ , $k$ , $\rho$ , $L$ and $d$ ).

## 3 Frames in the plane tangent to the shell. Matrix notation

### 3.1 Frames

One considers the global frame of the structure $(X,Y,Z)$ . In the case of laminated composites the orientation of the single layer is defined with respect to a reference direction ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ in the tangent plane ($T$ ).

This vector ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ is determined by the projection of a vector ${X}_{1}$ , given by the user under the keyword ANGL_REP of AFFE_CARA_ELEM, onto the tangent plane ($T$ ) at an arbitrary point of the shell.

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.01/100002000000030A00000183EC01C7805F6A8F3E.png)

Figure 3.1-1: Definition of the local frame

The vector ${X}_{1}$ is defined by the user by two oriented angles:

${\alpha}_{1}$ : between $\mathrm{OX}$ and ${X}_{1\text{proj}(X,Y)}$

${\alpha}_{2}$ : between ${X}_{1\text{proj}(X,Y)}$ and ${X}_{1}$

${\alpha}_{1}$ : takes one from the direction $\mathrm{0X}$ to the projection, in the plane $\mathrm{XOY}$ , of the vector ${X}_{1}$ .

${\alpha}_{2}$ : takes one from this projection to ${X}_{1}$ itself: see figure .

In the cases where, in a given zone of the shell, $T$ is orthogonal to ${X}_{1}$ , the user will have to define another vector (in practice for certain mesh cells).

For a finite element of plane-facet type, contained in the tangent plane $T$ , one defines the orthonormal frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ local to the element by means of the numbering of the vertices. For example for the triangle:

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.01/10000200000000E10000005BF32EB56D5A5BA58A.png)

**Figure 3.1-b : Local frame of the element** $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$

The oriented angle ${j}_{0}=({V}_{1},{e}_{\text{réf}})$ makes it possible to pass from the frame local to the element to the reference frame.

### 3.2 Matrix notation

In thermal analysis as in thermo-mechanics, the programming of the elements requires one to express the elasticity and conduction operators in the local frame of the finite element $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ . One is accustomed to simplify the representation of the tensor quantities as follows.

#### 3.2.1 Thermal analysis

One represents the tensor quantities in the frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ :

$$
{A}_{\alpha \beta }^{ij}\left((i,j)\in {(m,s,i)}^{2}\text{et}(\alpha ,\beta )\in {(1,2)}^{2}\right)
$$

In a vector form with six vectors, taking the symmetries into account (see r4.01.01-caracteristique-thermique):

$$
{A}^{ij}=(\begin{array}{c}{A}_{11}^{ij}\\ {A}_{22}^{ij}\\ {A}_{12}^{ij}\end{array})={\int}_{-h}^{h}{P}_{i}({x}_{3}).{P}_{j}({x}_{3}).(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{11}\\ {k}_{22}\\ {k}_{12}\end{array}){\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

Where $k=\left(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{11}\\ {k}_{22}\\ {k}_{12}\end{array}\right)$ denotes the thermal conductivity vector built with the aid of the tensor $(\begin{array}{cc}{k}_{\alpha \beta }& \begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cc}0& 0\end{array}& {k}_{33}\end{array})$ (see r4.01.01-notations-hypotheses), and of ${P}_{i}({x}_{3})$ , the Lagrange polynomials through the thickness. One does the same for ${B}^{ij}$ and ${C}^{ij}$ .

Placing oneself in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ , one uses the transformation matrix ${P}_{k}^{(m)}$ of the conductivity tensor $k=(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{11}\\ {k}_{22}\\ {k}_{12}\end{array})$ from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ towards the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ []:

$$
{P}_{k}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& 2\text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& -2\text{CS}\\ -\text{CS}& \text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

It follows that the transformation matrix ${({P}_{k}^{m})}^{-1}$ of the conductivity tensor from the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ towards $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$  is given by:

$$
{P}_{{k}^{{(m)}^{-1}}}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& -2\text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& 2\text{CS}\\ \text{CS}& -\text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

#### 3.2.2 Thermomechanics

One likewise represents in a vector form in the frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ :

- on the one hand, the normal stresses ${\sigma}_{11},{\sigma}_{22}$ , the shear ${\sigma}_{12}$ in the plane and the transverse shear ${\sigma}_{13}$ and ${\sigma}_{23}$ :

$$
\sigma =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{11}\\ {\sigma}_{22}\\ {\sigma}_{12}\end{array}\right)
$$

- on the other hand, the corresponding strains:

$$
\epsilon =\left(\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{11}\\ {\epsilon}_{22}\\ {\gamma}_{12}\end{array}\right)
$$

which are decomposed with the generalised membrane strains $E$ and bending strains $K$ :

$$
\epsilon \left({x}_{3}\right)=\epsilon \left(u\right)\left({x}_{3}\right)-{\epsilon}^{\text{th}}\left({x}_{3}\right)
$$

And for the thermal part:

$$
{\epsilon}^{\text{th}}\left({x}_{3}\right)=d\left({x}_{3}\right)\left({T}_{\left({x}_{3}\right)}-{T}^{\text{réf}}\right)
$$

Pour une ordonnée

${x}_{3}\in ]-h,h[$ , et:


$$
E=\left(\begin{array}{c}{E}_{11}\\ {E}_{22}\\ {E}_{11}\end{array}\right)
$$

Where $d$ is the vector associated with the thermal expansion coefficients. The stress vector $\sigma$ is obtained by means of the stiffness matrix (3 x 3):

$$
\sigma =R.(\varepsilon (u)-{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})
$$

With $R$ , the inverse of the compliance matrix (see r4.01.01-thermomecanique). Placing oneself in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ , one uses the transformation matrix ${P}^{(m)}$ of the strain tensor $\varepsilon =(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{11}\\ {\varepsilon}_{22}\\ {\gamma}_{12}\end{array})$ from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$  towards the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ []:

$$
{P}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& \text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& -\text{CS}\\ -2\text{CS}& 2\text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

Placing oneself in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ , one uses the transformation matrix ${P}_{2}^{(m)}$ of the strain tensor $\frac{1}{2}\gamma =(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{13}\\ {\varepsilon}_{23}\end{array})$ from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$  towards the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ :

$$
{P}_{2}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}C& S\\ -S& C\end{array}\right]
$$

Likewise, placing oneself in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ , the transformation matrix ${P}_{s}^{(m)}$ of the stress tensor $\sigma =(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{11}\\ {\sigma}_{22}\\ {\sigma}_{12}\end{array})$ from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ towards the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ is:

$$
{P}_{s}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& 2\text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& -2\text{CS}\\ -\text{CS}& \text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

It follows that the expression of the transformation matrix from the frame associated with ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ to the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ for the stresses above is such that:

$$
{P}_{\sigma}^{{(m)}^{-1}}={P}_{\sigma}^{(m)}(-{\varphi}_{0}){=}^{t}{P}^{(m)}
$$

This property will be particularly useful in the remainder of the presentation.

## 4 Shells made up of homogeneous layers

### 4.1 Description of the layers

One considers the shell made up of a stack of ${N}_{\text{couch}}$ layers (parallel to the tangent plane) through the thickness $]-h,h[$ , each constituted of one of the ${M}_{\text{mater}}$ homogeneous orthotropic materials (laminated shell see r4.01.01-fig-coque-stratifiee).

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.01/10000200000001040000009591580DAD68BCE5C7.png)

*Laminated shell*

A layer $n$ is defined by:

- its thickness ${e}_{n}$ with the ordinates of the lower and upper interfaces:

$$
{x}_{3}^{n-1}=-h+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}{e}_{j}
$$

- the constituent material $m$ , and its physical characteristics,
- the angle ${\phi }_{n}$ of the first direction of orthotropy (denoted $L$ ) in the tangent plane ($T$ ) with respect to the reference direction ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ (see figure).

**Note :**

*In the case of a layer made up of fibres in a resin matrix, the first direction of orthotropy corresponds to the direction of the fibres.*

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.01/1000020000000743000008001E0FBD1542900386.png)

Figure 4.1-1: Definition of the frame on an orthotropic layer

### 4.2 Thermal analysis

The expression of the vectors ${A}^{ij}((i,j)\in {\left\lbrace m,s,i\right\rbrace }^{2},i\le j)$ defined in (see r4.01.01-thermique) is obtained from the conductivities ${k}_{m}$ of the material $m$ constituting the layers $n$ . In the cases of orthotropy $(L,T)$ of the material $m$ , the conductivity coefficients are:

$$
{k}_{(L,T)}=(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{L}\\ {k}_{T}\\ 0\end{array})
$$

In the case of a transversely isotropic material, the coefficient ${k}_{33}$ is equal to ${k}_{T}$ . In order to have the expression of ${A}^{ij}$ in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ one must apply the following rotation, from the orthotropy frame to the element frame, as set out in r4.01.01-reperes-plan-tangent:

$$
{k}^{(m)}=(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{11}\\ {k}_{22}\\ {k}_{12}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}\\ \text{CS}& -\text{CS}\end{array}){(\begin{array}{c}{k}_{L}\\ {k}_{T}\end{array})}_{(L,T)}
$$

The vectors ${A}^{ij}$ may then be expressed by integration through the thickness of the contributions of the layers:

$$
{A}^{ij}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{P}_{i}({x}_{3})\cdot {P}_{j}({x}_{3})\cdot {k}_{(m)}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

The terms ${B}^{ij}((i,j)\in {\left\lbrace 2,3\right\rbrace }^{2},i\le j)$ are:

$$
{B}^{ij}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}\frac{\partial {P}_{i}({x}_{3})}{\partial {x}_{3}}\cdot \frac{\partial {P}_{j}({x}_{3})}{\partial {x}_{3}}\cdot {k}_{33(m)}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

Likewise for ${C}^{ij}$ :

$$
{C}^{ij}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{P}_{i}({x}_{3})\cdot {P}_{j}({x}_{3})\cdot {\mathrm{\rho C}}_{(m)}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

### 4.3 Thermomechanics

#### 4.3.1 Constitutive relation

In the case of laminated shells, one shows that the relation between the strains $\varepsilon$ and the stress $\sigma$ in the layer $n$ depends on the constants of the orthotropic material $m$ . For the elastic coefficients, one has ${E}_{\text{LL}}^{(m)}$ , ${E}_{\text{TT}}^{(m)}$ , ${\nu}_{\text{LT}}^{(m)}$ , ${G}_{\text{LT}}^{(m)}$ , ${G}_{\text{LZ}}^{(m)}$ and ${G}_{\text{TZ}}^{(m)}$ and the expansion coefficients ${d}_{}^{(m)}$ and ${d}_{\text{TT}}^{(m)}$ . In the axes of orthotropy $(L,T)$ of the material $m$ , the compliance matrix $S$ is expressed by:

$$
{S}_{(m)}{\mid}_{(L,T)}={\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{1}{{E}_{\text{LL}}}& -\frac{{\nu}_{\text{LT}}}{{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}& 0\\ -\frac{{\nu}_{\text{TL}}}{{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}& \frac{1}{{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1}{{G}_{\text{LT}}}\end{array}\right]}_{(m)}
$$

The stiffness ${\Lambda}_{(m)}={S}_{(m)}^{-1}$ being:

$$
{\Lambda}_{(m)}{\mid}_{(L,T)}={\left[\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{E}_{\text{LL}}}{1-{\nu}_{\mathrm{TL}}\cdot {\nu}_{\text{LT}}}& \frac{{\nu}_{\text{TL}}\cdot {E}_{\text{LL}}}{1-{\nu}_{\mathrm{TL}}\cdot {\nu}_{\text{LT}}}& 0\\ \frac{{\nu}_{\text{LT}}\cdot {E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}{1-{\nu}_{\mathrm{TL}}\cdot {\nu}_{\text{LT}}}& \frac{{E}_{\mathrm{TT}}}{1-{\nu}_{\mathrm{TL}}\cdot {\nu}_{\text{LT}}}& 0\\ 0& 0& {G}_{\text{LT}}\end{array}\right]}_{(m)}
$$

The transverse shear stiffness is for its part expressed in the following way:

$$
{\Lambda}_{\tau (m)}{\mid}_{(L,T)}\text{}=\text{}{\left[\begin{array}{cc}{G}_{\mathrm{LZ}}& 0\\ 0& {G}_{\mathrm{TZ}}\end{array}\right]}_{(m)}
$$

Placing oneself in the element frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ , one uses the transformation matrix ${P}^{(m)}$ of the strain tensor defined in (r4.01.01-reperes-plan-tangent) from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$  towards the orthotropy frame:

$$
{P}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& 2\text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& -2\text{CS}\\ -\text{CS}& \text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right]
$$

Likewise the expansion vector is expressed in the frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ :

$$
{d}^{(m)}=(\begin{array}{c}{d}_{11}\\ {d}_{22}\\ {d}_{12}\end{array})={P}^{{m}^{-1}}{(\begin{array}{c}{d}_{\text{LL}}\\ {d}_{\text{TT}}\\ 0\end{array})}_{(L,T)}=(\begin{array}{cc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}\\ 2\text{CS}& -2\text{CS}\end{array}){(\begin{array}{c}{d}_{LL}\\ {d}_{\mathrm{TT}}\end{array})}_{(L,T)}

$$

On a donc dans la couche

$n$ (matériau : $m$ ), en ${x}_{3}$ :


$$

With:

\epsilon \left(u\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{E}_{11}\\ {E}_{22}\\ {E}_{12}\end{array}\right)+{x}_{3}\left(\begin{array}{c}{K}_{11}\\ {K}_{22}\\ {K}_{12}\end{array}\right)

#### 4.3.2 Transverse shear

The transverse shear stiffness of each layer is written in the frame $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ in the same way as the expansion:

$$
{\Lambda}_{t(m)}{\mid}_{({V}_{1},{V}_{2})}={}^{t}\text{}{P}_{2}^{(m)}.{\Lambda}_{t(m)}.{P}_{2}^{(m)}
$$

With ${P}_{2}^{(m)}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}C& S\\ -S& C\end{array}\right]$ the vector transformation matrix from $({V}_{1},{V}_{2})$ towards the orthotropy frame. The global transverse shear stiffness of the shell $\left[{R}_{c}\right]$ being calculated so as to be equal to that given by the law of three-dimensional elasticity [], the matrix $\left[{R}_{c}\right]$ is defined so that the surface density of transverse shear energy ${U}_{2}$ obtained for a three-dimensional distribution of the stresses ${\sigma}_{13}$ and ${\sigma}_{23}$ is identical to that associated with the Reissner-Mindlin plate model, denoted ${U}_{2}$ . Finally:

$$
{U}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{-h}^{h}\langle \tau \rangle {\left[{\Lambda}_{\tau \left(m\right)}\right]}^{-1}\left\lbrace \tau \right\rbrace {d}_{3}
$$

And:

$$
{U}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}V{\left[{R}_{c}\right]}^{-1}V=\frac{1}{2}\left({\int}_{-h}^{h}\left\lbrace \tau \right\rbrace {d}_{3}\right){\left[{H}_{c}\right]}^{-1}\frac{1}{2}\left({\int}_{-h}^{h}\left\lbrace \tau \right\rbrace {d}_{3}\right)
$$

With $\langle \tau \rangle =\langle {\sigma}_{13}{\sigma}_{23}\rangle$ . With the equilibrium equations:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{13}=-{\int}_{-h}^{{x}_{3}}({\sigma}_{11,1}+{\sigma}_{12,2}){d}_{3}\\ {\sigma}_{23}=-{\int}_{-h}^{{x}_{3}}({\sigma}_{12,1}+{s}_{22,2}){d}_{3}\end{array}
$$

And the conditions:

$$
0={\sigma}_{13}={\sigma}_{23}
$$

The plane stresses ${\sigma}_{11}$ , ${\sigma}_{22}$ and ${\sigma}_{12}$ are expressed as a function of the resultant forces, making the assumption of pure bending and of the absence of membrane/bending coupling. It follows that:

$$
\sigma ({x}_{3})={x}_{3}.{\Lambda}_{(m)}({x}_{3}){P}^{-1}.M
$$

Where $P$ is the bending stiffness matrix of the whole of the multilayer, defined by the first equation of (). These calculations, as well as the following ones, are to be carried out in a single frame. In *Code_Aster*  one chooses the frame intrinsic to the element. One must therefore transform the matrix A into this frame. One then has:

$$
\left\lbrace \tau ({x}_{3})\right\rbrace ={D}_{1}({x}_{3})V+{D}_{2}({x}_{3})\left\lbrace \lambda \right\rbrace
$$

With:

$$
V=\langle {M}_{11,1}+{M}_{12,2};{M}_{12,1}+{M}_{22,2}\rangle
 Et:
$$

$$
\langle \lambda \rangle =\langle {M}_{11,1}-{M}_{12,2};{M}_{12,1}-{M}_{22,2};{M}_{22,1};{M}_{11,2}\rangle
$$

And:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{D}_{1}={\int}_{-{x}_{3}}^{h}-\frac{z}{2}\left[\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}+{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}+{A}_{32}\\ {A}_{31}+{A}_{23}& {A}_{22}+{A}_{33}\end{array}\right]\text{dz}\\ {D}_{2}={\int}_{-{x}_{3}}^{h}-\frac{z}{2}\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{A}_{11}-{A}_{33}& {A}_{13}-{A}_{32}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{12}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{31}\\ {A}_{31}-{A}_{23}& {A}_{33}-{A}_{22}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{32}& {\mathrm{2A}}_{21}\end{array}\right]\text{dz}\end{array}
$$

${U}_{1}$ is therefore written:

$$
{U}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\langle V\mid \lambda \rangle \left[\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{11}& {C}_{12}\\ {C}_{{12}^{T}}& {C}_{22}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \frac{V}{\lambda}\right\rbrace
$$

With:

$$
\begin{array}{}\underset{2\times 2}{{C}_{11}}={\int}_{-h}^{h}{{D}_{{1}^{T}}{\Lambda}_{\tau (m)}}^{-1}{D}_{1}{d}_{3}\\ \underset{2\times 4}{{C}_{12}}={\int}_{-h}^{h}{{D}_{{1}^{T}}{\Lambda}_{\tau (m)}}^{-1}{D}_{2}{d}_{3}\\ \underset{4\times 4}{{C}_{22}}={\int}_{-h}^{h}{{D}_{{2}^{T}}{\Lambda}_{\tau (m)}}^{-1}{D}_{2}{d}_{3}\end{array}
$$

Hence the final displacements:

$$
{U}_{1}={U}_{2}\iff \langle V\mid \lambda \rangle \left[\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{11}-{{H}_{c}}^{-1}& {C}_{12}\\ {C}_{{12}^{T}}& {C}_{22}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \frac{V}{\lambda}\right\rbrace =0\forall V,\left\lbrace \lambda \right\rbrace
$$

One therefore proposes the solution ${H}_{c}={C}_{{11}^{-1}}$ . The transverse-shear correction coefficients correspond to the ratio of the terms of ${H}_{c}$ to the integral through the thickness of the laminate of the terms of ${\Lambda}_{\tau (m)}$ .

#### 4.3.3 Generalised forces

The generalised forces, put in a vector form, are obtained by integration through the thickness of the shell, summing the contributions of the layers (of thickness ${e}_{n}={x}_{3}^{n}-{x}_{3}^{n-1}$ ):

$$
\begin{array}{c}M=(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{11}\\ {M}_{22}\\ {M}_{12}\end{array})={\int}_{l}\sigma \cdot {x}_{3}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{\sigma}_{(n)}\cdot {x}_{3}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}\\ \begin{array}{}\\ N=(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{11}\\ {N}_{22}\\ {N}_{12}\end{array})={\int}_{l}\sigma \cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{\sigma}_{(n)}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}\end{array}
$$

If one expresses them as previously (with $m$ the material of the layer $n$ ):

$$
{\sigma}_{(n)}={\Lambda}_{(m)}\cdot (E+{x}_{3}\cdot K-{d}_{(m)}(T({x}_{3})-{T}^{{}_{\text{réf}}}))
$$

One may write the generalised forces in the form:

$$
\begin{array}{}M-{M}^{\text{th}}=P\cdot K+Q\cdot E\\ N-{N}^{\text{th}}=Q\cdot K+R\cdot E\end{array}
$$

With $P,Q,R$ 3 x 3 matrices expressed by:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}P=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{x}_{3}^{2}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}& =\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}\cdot \frac{1}{3}\cdot ({({x}_{3}^{n})}^{3}-{({x}_{3}^{n-1})}^{3})\\ Q=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{x}_{3}\cdot {\text{dx}}_{3}& =\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}\cdot \frac{1}{2}\cdot ({({x}_{3}^{n})}^{2}\cdot {({x}_{3}^{n-1})}^{2})\\ R=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}\cdot ({x}_{3}^{n}-{x}_{3}^{n-1})& =\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}\cdot {e}_{n}\end{array}
$$

The shear force $V$ is obtained by differentiation of the moment (see r4.01.01-cisaillement-transverse). The generalised forces of thermal origin are calculated directly:

$$
\begin{array}{}{M}^{\text{th}}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}.{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}{x}_{3}.(T({x}_{3})-{T}^{\text{réf}}).{d}_{(m)}.{\text{dx}}_{3}\\ {N}^{\text{th}}=\sum_{n=1}^{{N}_{\text{couch}}}{\Lambda}_{(m)}.{\int}_{{x}_{3}^{n-1}}^{{x}_{3}^{n}}(T({x}_{3})-{T}^{\text{réf}}).{d}_{(m)}.{\text{dx}}_{3}\end{array}
$$

#### 4.3.4 Localisation of the stresses (post-processing)

Conversely, following a finite-element analysis and the obtaining of the strains $E$ and of the curvature changes $K$ , one may then calculate the stress field ${\sigma}_{(n)}(n=1,{N}_{\text{couch}})$ in each layer of the element.

It is necessary to calculate, in each layer $(n)$ , the matrix ${\Lambda}_{(m)}$ and the terms $(T({x}_{3})-{T}^{\text{réf}}).{d}_{(m)}$ (see r4.01.01-notation-matricielle) ($m={\text{mat}}_{n}$  represents the material characteristics of the layer $n$ ).

The stresses ${\sigma}_{\alpha \beta }$ at an ordinate ${x}_{3}\in ]{x}_{3}^{n-1},{x}_{3}^{n}[$ in the layer ($n$ ) are then:

$$
{\sigma}_{(n)}({x}_{3})={\Lambda}_{(m)}.\left[E+{x}_{3}.K-{d}_{(m)}(T({x}_{3})-{T}_{\text{réf}})\right]

#### 4.3.5 Calculation of the fracture criteria in the layers (post-processing)

The limit values of the fracture stresses depend on the material of the layer, on the direction and on the sense of the loading (for a group of elements corresponding to the same material field):

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{ccc}\begin{array}{c}{\text{mat}}_{n}\end{array}& \begin{array}{c}X\text{: limite en traction dans le sens L}\\ {X}^{'}\text{: limite en compression dans le sens L}\\ Y\text{: limite en traction dans le sens T}\\ {Y}^{'}\text{: limite en compression dans le sens T}\\ S\text{: limite en cisaillement dans le sens LT}\end{array}& \begin{array}{c}(\text{1ère direction orthotropie : sens des fibres})\\ (\text{1ère direction orthotropie : sens des fibres})\\ (\text{2ème direction orthogonale à la 1 ère})\\ (\text{2ème direction orthogonale à la 1 ère})\\ \end{array}\end{array}\\ \end{array}$

It is necessary to calculate the stresses in the frame of the layer (defined by the axes of orthotropy) from the stresses in the frame of the element. The angle between ${V}_{1}$ and ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ is ${\varphi}_{0}$ , and that between ${e}_{\text{réf}}$ and the orthotropy frame is ${\varphi}_{n}$ :

$$
{\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{L}\\ {\sigma}_{T}\\ {\sigma}_{LT}\end{array}\right)}_{n}=\left(\begin{array}{ccc}{C}^{2}& {S}^{2}& +2\text{CS}\\ {S}^{2}& {C}^{2}& -2\text{CS}\\ -\text{CS}& +\text{CS}& {C}^{2}-{S}^{2}\end{array}\right){\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{11}\\ {\sigma}_{22}\\ {\sigma}_{12}\end{array}\right)}_{n}
$$

For the maximum-stress criterion, the following five criteria are calculated per layer:

$$
\textrm{Pour}
n=1,N-\text{couch}
$$

The Tsai-Hill criterion is written in each layer in the following manner:

$$
{C}_{\text{TH}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{L(n)}^{2}}{{X}_{({\text{mat}}_{n})}^{2}}-\frac{{\sigma}_{L(n).}{\sigma}_{T(n)}}{{X}_{({\text{mat}}_{n})}^{2}}+\frac{{\sigma}_{T(n)}^{2}}{{Y}_{({\text{mat}}_{n})}^{2}}+\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LT}(n)}^{2}}{{S}_{({\text{mat}}_{n})}^{2}}
$$

The material has failed when ${C}_{\text{TH}}\ge 1$ . The values $X$ and $Y$ are replaced by ${X}^{'}$ and ${Y}^{'}$ when the corresponding stresses $({\sigma}_{L(n)},{\sigma}_{T(n)})$ are negative.
