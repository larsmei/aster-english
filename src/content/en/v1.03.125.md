# v1.03.125 UMAT002 – Code_Aster-Umat Multiaxially Loading Interface Test

**Summary:**

A comparison of *Code_Aster-* Umat and *Code-Aster* with ELAS behaviour is performed on a linear elastic problem. This test uses a simulation of a deformation loading path into a material point, i.e. on a model such that the stress and deformation states are homogeneous at any time. The load path is multiaxial in order to verify the robustness and reliability of the digital integration, its insensitivity to a change in units, the invariance to an overall rotation applied to the problem, the accuracy of the tangent matrix.

Modelling A: This Modelling allows to validate the UMAT model in $\mathrm{3D}$.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This is a material point, representative of a homogeneous state of stress and deformation.

### 1.2 Material properties

#### 1.2.1 Umat data

The coefficients of Umat behavior are (see [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)):

$\mathrm{C1}=\lambda =\frac{E\nu }{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}$

$\mathrm{C2}=\mu =\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$

$\mathrm{C3}=\tilde{\lambda}=\frac{\lambda}{20}$

$\mathrm{C4}=\tilde{\mu}=\frac{\mu}{20}$

$\mathrm{C5}=\tilde{\nu}=0$

DEFI_MATERIAU / UMAT=_F(LISTE_COEF=(C1, C2, C3, C4, C5))

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The load is identical to that of COMP001, cf. [V6.07.101](doc:v6.07.101) tests.

#### 1.3.1 Characteristics of loading routes

The proposed loading causes a decoupled variation of each component of the tensor of the deformations by successive step. A cyclic discharge path is proposed covering the tensile and compression states and an inversion of the shear signs in order to test a wide range of values.

Schematically, it follows a course on 8 segments $[O-A-B-C-O-C’-B’-A’-O]$ where the second part of the $[O-C’-B’-A’-O]$ route is symmetrical with the origin of the first $[O-A-B-C-O]$.

#### 1.3.2 Application of solicitations

The study of a material point (using SIMU_POINT_MAT [U4.51.12](doc:u4.51.12) macro-command) is re-examined by applying for a homogeneous element by imposing in $\mathrm{3D}$ the 6 components of the deformation tensor:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]$

For a more general writing, the strain tensor imposed will be decomposed into a hydrostatic and deviatoric part on shear bases:

$\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array}\right]=p\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]+{d}_{1}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& -1& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]+{d}_{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& -1\end{array}\right]+\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{xz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& 0& {\varepsilon}_{yz}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xz}& {\varepsilon}_{yz}& 0\end{array}\right]$ en 3D.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

No stress and deformation.

## 2 reference solution

This test performs, for each model, an intercomparison between the reference solution (obtained with a very fine time step), the solution with medium coarse discretization, the solution with temperature effect (or another control variable), the solution by changing the unit system ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{MPa}$), and the solution obtained after rotation or symmetry.

### 2.1 Definition of robustness test cases

We propose 3 angles of analysis to test the robustness of integration of constitutive laws:

- study of equivalent problems
- verification of the tangent matrix
- study of time step discretization

For each of these, the relative differencess are studied in terms of evolution between several calculations using the same law but with different parameters or calculation options. The exploitation is concerned with the invariants of the stress tensor: tensor trace, Von-Mises stress and internal variables of a scalar nature: usually it is the cumulative plasticity.

### 2.2 Study of equivalent problems

For rough discretization of the journeys: 1 time step for each segment of the journey, the solution obtained for each law is compared to 3 strictly equivalent problems for the condition of the material point:

- $\mathrm{Tpa}$ , same route with a unit change, substitutes $\mathrm{Pa}$ for $\mathrm{MPa}$ in material data and possible law parameters,
- $\mathrm{Trot}$ , route by imposing the same $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after rotation: ${}^{t}R\cdot \stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}\cdot R$ where $R$ is a rotation matrix defined in part of the following arbitrary angles d
- $\mathrm{Tsym}$ , route by imposing the $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varepsilon}$ tensor after a symmetry: $x$ to $y$ , $y$ to $z$ and $z$ to $x$ to $\mathrm{3D}$ .

For each of these problems, the solution (invariants in stresses, cumulative equivalent plastic deformation) must be identical to the basic solution, obtained with the same discretization in time. The reference value of the difference is therefore 0. This means in practice that the difference found must be of the order of machine precision is about 1.E-15.

### 2.3 Test of the tangent matrix

The tangential matrix is also tested for each behavior, by difference with the matrix obtained by disturbance. Here again, the reference value is 0.

### 2.4 Study of time step discretization

The behaviour of law integration in relation to discretization is studied. For the same model, therefore a given behavior, several discretizations are studied in different times, multiplying by 5 the number of steps of the loading path.

## 4 Summary of the results

The results are satisfactory and validate the interface between *Code_Aster* and UMAT in small deformations.

- the results are valid when changing the physical unit of the problem ($\mathrm{Pa}$ to $\mathrm{Mpa}$), or following rotation or symmetry of the load
- The results converge correctly with the time step, and the integration schemes are robust, since they allow the use of large time step. However, for these models, we should note that the viscosity of time step is more sensitive than for elasto-plastic models.
- the tangent matrices are correct because they are similar to the tangent matrices calculated by disturbance.
