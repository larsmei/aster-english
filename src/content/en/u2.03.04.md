# u2.03.04 User's guide for thermo-metallurgical-mechanical analyses on steels

**Summary**

The objective of this note is to give the information necessary for a user to be able to carry out easily a thermo-metallurgical-mechanical analysis in *Code_Aster*. This type of analysis concerns steels which undergo structural transformations in the course of a heating or of a cooling.

## 1 The main lines of the thermo-metallurgical-mechanical analysis

In *Code_Aster*, the thermal, metallurgical and mechanical analyses are uncoupled. The successive stages of a complete analysis are as follows:

1. A thermal analysis is carried out, which makes it possible to obtain the temperature field at each node.

2. As a post-processing of the thermal analysis, the metallurgical analysis is carried out, which makes it possible to obtain the proportion of the different metallurgical phases at each node and possibly the associated hardness. In Code_Aster, two different types of material can be treated, which undergo metallurgical transformations: steels or ZIRCALOY. Only materials of steel type are of interest here. For a steel, five different metallurgical phases can be taken into account: ferrite, pearlite, bainite and martensite, called the $\alpha$ phase, and austenite, called the gamma phase.

3. From the temperature field and the metallurgical phases, the mechanical analysis is carried out by choosing a constitutive model which takes into account the various possible effects of the metallurgical transformations. The fields of stresses, of strains and of internal variables are thus obtained at each Gauss point.

## 2 What to do in order to carry out a thermo-metallurgical-mechanical analysis?

### 2.1 Stage 1: which documents to read – summary

#### 2.1.1 For the thermal part

The document [R5.02.02](doc:r5.02.02) contains the information necessary for the understanding of a non-linear thermal analysis.

In *Code_Aster*, for a non-linear analysis, heat diffusion is treated with an enthalpy formulation. One can supply either the conductivity and the enthalpy as a function of the temperature, or the conductivity and the specific heat $\rho {C}_{p}$ as a function of the temperature.

#### 2.1.2 For the metallurgical constitutive-model part

The document [R4.04.01](doc:r4.04.01) of *Code_Aster* describes the various metallurgical models.

**Brief summary:**

When a material is heated, the $\alpha$ phases transform into the $\gamma$ phase. When the material is cooled, the austenite transforms, depending on the cooling rate, into ferrite and/or pearlite and/or bainite and/or martensite. One must therefore define, for the heating, the kinetics of the transformation $\alpha \to \gamma$ and, for the cooling, the nature and the kinetics of the possible transformations $\gamma \to \alpha$.

**Transformation kinetics on heating:**

The evolution law of the austenite is given by the equation:

$\dot{{Z}_{\gamma}}=\frac{{Z}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{Z}_{\gamma}}{\tau}$

with ${Z}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}0& \text{si}& T\le \mathrm{Ac1}\\ \frac{T-\mathrm{Ac1}}{\mathrm{Ac3}-\mathrm{Ac1}}& \text{si}& \mathrm{Ac1}\le T\le \mathrm{Ac3}\\ 1& \text{si}& T\ge \mathrm{Ac3}\end{array}$

and $\tau =\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{\tau}_{1}& \text{si}& T\le \mathrm{Ac1}\\ {\tau}_{1}+\frac{T-\mathrm{Ac1}}{\mathrm{Ac3}-\mathrm{Ac1}}({\tau}_{3}-{\tau}_{1})& \text{si}& \mathrm{Ac1}\le T\le \mathrm{Ac3}\\ {\tau}_{3}& \text{si}& T\ge \mathrm{Ac3}\end{array}$

where ${Z}_{\gamma}$ is the proportion of phase $\gamma$, $\mathrm{Ac1}$ the quasi-static temperature of the start of transformation of the phases, $\mathrm{Ac3}$ $\alpha$ the quasi-static temperature of the end of transformation of the phases $\alpha$ and ${\tau}_{1}$, ${\tau}_{3}$ two coefficients of the material. ${Z}_{\mathrm{eq}}$ corresponds to the evolution of the transformed austenite fraction during quasi-static evolutions. The temperatures of the start $\mathrm{Ac1}$ and of the end $\mathrm{Ac3}$ of the austenitic transformation and the parameters ${\tau}_{1}$ and ${\tau}_{3}$ can be identified from experimental data supplying, for different heating rates, the proportion of austenite formed as a function of the temperature. Details on the method of identification of the coefficients will be found in [bib1].

**Example:**

*For a 16MND5 steel, the coefficients are* $\mathit{Ac1}=716°C$ *,* $\mathit{Ac3}=802°C$ *,* ${\tau}_{1}=12\text{s}$ *and* ${\tau}_{3}=0.5\text{s}$ *.*

**Transformation kinetics on cooling:**

For the ferritic, pearlitic and bainitic transformations, the kinetics is given by the following relation:

$\dot{Z}=f(T,\dot{T},Z,{M}_{s},{d}^{c})\frac{{(T-{M}_{s})}^{+}}{(T-{M}_{s})}$ with $Z=\lbrace {Z}_{F},{Z}_{P},{Z}_{B}\rbrace$

where ${M}_{s}$ represents the temperature of the start of the martensitic transformation, ${d}^{c}$ the austenitic grain size and ${(X)}^{+}$ the positive part of $X$. For the evolution functions $f$, no particular forms are imposed and the identification of $f$ reduces to the definition of diagrams of TRC type (transformation on Continuous Cooling). This diagram makes it possible to define the evolutions of ferrite, pearlite and bainite associated with a thermal history on cooling and with given austenitisation conditions (for a given grain size ${d}^{c}$).

For the martensitic transformation, the Koistinen-Marburger kinetics given by the equation is used:

${Z}_{M}=(1-{Z}_{F}-{Z}_{P}-{Z}_{B})\left[1-\exp(\alpha ({M}_{s}-{T}^{+}))\right]$

${M}_{s}=\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{\mathrm{s0}}& \text{si}& {Z}_{F}+{Z}_{P}+{Z}_{B}\le \mathrm{seuil}\\ {M}_{\mathrm{s0}}+\mathrm{Akm}({Z}_{F}+{Z}_{P}+{Z}_{B})+\mathrm{Bkm}& \text{si}& {Z}_{F}+{Z}_{P}+{Z}_{B}>\mathrm{seuil}\end{array}$

where ${M}_{\mathrm{s0}}$ represents the temperature of the start of the martensitic transformation when the latter is total, and $\alpha$, $\mathrm{Akm}$, $\mathrm{Bkm}$ and $\mathrm{seuil}$ are material parameters.

In the simplest case, the temperature ${M}_{s}$ can be taken constant and therefore equal to ${M}_{\mathrm{s0}}$. For a 16MND6 steel, ${M}_{\mathrm{s0}}$ is equal to $365°C$.

**Note:**

*The TRC diagrams relate to given austenitisation conditions, to which a value of the grain size* $d$ *corresponds. This grain size results from the thermal history undergone on heating and no longer evolves on cooling. In Code_Aster, it is possible to compute, from the thermal history on heating, the evolution of the grain size and to take its effect on the metallurgical behaviour on cooling into account (see the reference test cases hsnv126a.comm, hsnv126b.comm and mtlp102a.comm for the use).*

**Note:**

*It is possible in Code_Aster to compute the hardness* $\mathrm{Hv}$ *of the multiphase mixture given by the relation* $\mathrm{Hv}=\sum_{k=1}^{5}{Z}_{k}{\mathrm{Hv}}_{k}$ *where* ${\mathrm{Hv}}_{k}$ *is the hardness associated with the phase* $k$ *and supplied under the operator* *DEFI_MATERIAU under the keyword* *’DURT_META’. The hardness of the multiphase mixture is obtained by the operator* *CALC_META with the option ’DURT_ELNO’ (hardness at the nodes per element).*

#### 2.1.3 For the mechanical constitutive-model part with effects of the metallurgical transformations

The reference document of *Code_Aster* is the note [R4.04.02](doc:r4.04.02).

Several constitutive models are available in the code. They make it possible to model the following different phenomena: plastic behaviour or viscous behaviour, linear or non-linear isotropic hardening or linear kinematic hardening, transformation plasticity, restoration of hardening of metallurgical origin, restoration of hardening of viscous origin. An analysis can be carried out in small strains but also in large strains (beware: the large strains for a model with kinematic hardening are not activated). For an understanding of the large-strain aspect, read the reference documents [R5.03.21](doc:r5.03.21) (large strains without metallurgical effect) and [R4.04.03](doc:r4.04.03) (large strains with metallurgical effects).

**Brief summary:**

The effects of the structural transformations on the mechanical behaviour are of 4 types:

- the mechanical characteristics of the material which undergoes transformations are modified. In particular, the plastic characteristics (yield stresses in particular) and the thermal expansion coefficient are strongly affected. For the yield stress of the multiphase point, a non-linear law of mixtures is used, given by:

${\sigma}_{y}=\left[1-g(\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i})\right]{\sigma}_{y\gamma }+g(\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}){\sigma}_{y\alpha }$ , ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{ya}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}{\sigma}_{y\alpha i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}}$

where ${Z}_{i}$ is the proportion of each $\alpha$ phase and $g$ a function of the ${Z}_{i}$.

- the volume expansion or contraction which accompanies the structural transformations results in a spherical "transformation" strain which is superimposed on the thermal strain. In general, this effect is grouped with that due to the modification of the thermal expansion coefficient. The thermal strain is given by:

${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}={Z}_{\gamma}\left[{\alpha}_{\gamma}(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})-(1-{Z}_{\gamma}^{r})\Delta {\varepsilon}_{f\gamma }^{{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right]+\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}\left[{\alpha}_{f}(T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}})+{Z}_{\gamma}^{r}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{f\gamma }^{{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}}\right]$

where ${\alpha}_{\gamma}$ and ${\alpha}_{f}$ are the expansion coefficients of the austenitic and ferritic phases, respectively. $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{f\gamma }^{{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}}$ represents the difference in compactness between the two phases at the reference temperature. One has ${Z}_{\gamma}^{r}=1$ when the reference phase is the austenitic phase and ${Z}_{\gamma}^{r}=0$ when the reference phase is the ferritic phase.

- a transformation taking place under stresses can give rise to an irreversible strain, even for stress levels very much lower than the yield stress of the material. This phenomenon is called transformation plasticity. In small strains, this additional term appears in the expression of the total strain. The evolution law of the strain which accompanies this phenomenon is written:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{\mathit{pt}}=\frac{3}{2}\tilde{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{K}_{i}F{'}_{i}({Z}_{\gamma})\cdot \langle \dot{{Z}_{i}}\rangle$

where $\tilde{\sigma}$ is the deviator of the stress tensor, $\langle X\rangle$ the positive part of $X$, ${K}_{i}$ and ${F}_{i}$, coefficients of the 4 ferritic phases. It is considered that this phenomenon does not exist during austenitic transformations.

- finally, during the transformation one can have a phenomenon of restoration of hardening: the hardening of the parent phase is not (or not totally) transmitted to the newly created phases. The newly created phases can either be born with a virgin hardening state, or inherit only part of the hardening of the parent phase, or inherit the whole of the hardening of the parent phase.

In the case of isotropic hardening, the plastic strain $p$ is no longer characteristic of the hardening state and other variables must be defined for each phase, denoted ${r}_{k}$. The isotropic hardening is then written:

$R=(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)){R}_{\gamma}+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}\cdot {R}_{i}$ , $Z=\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}$

where ${R}_{k}$ is the hardening variable of the phase $k$, which can be linear or non-linear with respect to ${r}_{k}$, and $\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)$ a function depending on $Z$ such that $\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)\in [0,1]$.

The evolution laws of the variables ${r}_{i}$ are given by:

$\begin{array}{ccc}{\dot{r}}_{\gamma}=\dot{p}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}\langle -\dot{{Z}_{i}}\rangle ({\theta}_{i\gamma }{r}_{i}-{r}_{\gamma})}{{Z}_{\gamma}}-\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{(C{r}_{\mathrm{moy}})}^{m}}}& \text{si}& {Z}_{\gamma}>0\\ \dot{{r}_{i}}=\dot{p}+\frac{\langle {Z}_{i}\rangle ({\theta}_{\gamma i}{r}_{\gamma}-{r}_{i})}{{Z}_{\gamma}}-\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{(C{r}_{\mathrm{moy}})}^{m}}}& \text{si}& {Z}_{i}>0\end{array}$

${r}_{\mathrm{moy}}=\sum_{k=1}^{5}{Z}_{k}{r}_{k}$

$C=\sum_{k=1}^{5}{Z}_{k}{C}_{k}$

$m=\sum_{k=1}^{5}{Z}_{k}{m}_{k}$

${C}_{k}$ and ${m}_{k}$ are the viscous-restoration coefficients associated with the phase $k$, ${\theta}_{i\gamma }$ and ${\theta}_{\gamma i}$ characterise the proportion of hardening transmitted during the transformation $\alpha \to \gamma$ and during the transformation $\gamma \to \alpha$, respectively. The memory is non-existent if $\theta =0$, complete if $\theta =1$.

In an equivalent manner, one writes in the case of kinematic hardening:

$X=(1-\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z))+\frac{\stackrel{ˉ}{f}(Z)}{Z}\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}\cdot {X}_{i}$ , $Z=\sum_{i=1}^{4}{Z}_{i}$

where ${X}_{k}$ is the kinematic hardening variable of the phase $k$, which is linear with respect to the variable ${\alpha}_{k}$:

${X}_{k}=\frac{2}{3}{H}_{k}{\alpha}_{k}$

The evolution laws of the kinematic variables ${\alpha}_{k}$ are given by:

$\begin{array}{ccc}{\dot{\alpha}}_{\gamma}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}+\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{4}\langle \dot{{Z}_{i}}\rangle ({\theta}_{i\gamma }{\alpha}_{i}-{\alpha}_{\gamma})}{{Z}_{\gamma}}+\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{3}{2}{(C{\alpha}_{\mathrm{eq}})}^{m}\frac{\alpha}{{\alpha}_{\mathrm{eq}}}}}& \text{si}& {Z}_{\gamma}>0\\ {\dot{\alpha}}_{i}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}+\frac{\langle \dot{{Z}_{i}}\rangle ({\theta}_{\gamma i}{\alpha}_{\gamma}-{\alpha}_{i})}{{Z}_{i}}+\underset{\text{uniquement en viscosité}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{3}{2}{(C{\alpha}_{\mathrm{eq}})}^{m}\frac{\alpha}{{\alpha}_{\mathrm{eq}}}}}& \text{si}& {Z}_{i}>0\end{array}$

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}{{(\sigma -X)}_{\mathrm{eq}}}$

where ${H}_{k}$ are the hardening slopes associated with each phase $k$.

For a plasticity model, the plastic multiplier is obtained by writing the consistency condition $\dot{f}=0$ and one has:

$\dot{p}\ge 0$ , $f\le 0$ and $\dot{p}f=0$

In the viscous case, $\dot{p}$ is written:

$\dot{p}={(\frac{\langle f\rangle }{\eta})}^{n}$

where $f$ is the plasticity threshold given by:

$f={\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}-R-{\sigma}_{y}$ in the case of isotropic hardening

$f={(\sigma -X)}_{\mathrm{eq}}-{\sigma}_{y}$ in the case of kinematic hardening
