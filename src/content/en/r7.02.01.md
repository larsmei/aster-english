# r7.02.01 Energy release rate in linear thermoelasticity

**Summary:**

The calculation of the energy release rate $G$ by the $\theta$ method in 2D or in 3D is presented for a linear thermoelastic problem. It is explained how the field $\theta$ is introduced and how the energy release rate is calculated with the operator [CALC_G](doc:u4.82.03).

Mechanical-reliability studies for the evaluation of the probability of fracture initiation additionally require its derivative with respect to a domain variation driven by another field. The implementation of this option in the code is detailed.

The calculation of $G$ is available both for a meshed crack (classical finite elements) and for a non-meshed crack (enriched finite elements: X-FEM method). The calculation of its derivative, for its part, is available only in 2D, for a meshed crack.

## 1 Calculation of the energy release rate by the \theta method in linear thermoelasticity

### 1.1 Constitutive relation

A cracked elastic solid occupying the domain $\Omega$ of the space ${R}^{2}$ or ${R}^{3}$ is considered. Let:

- $u$ be the displacement field,
- $T$ the temperature field,
- $f$ the field of body forces applied on $\Omega$ ,
- $g$ the field of surface forces applied on a part $S$ of $\partial \Omega$ ,
- $U$ the field of displacements imposed on a part ${S}_{d}$ of $\partial \Omega$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.01/100009580000117700000CB65A5C46D55689BF30.svg)

*Cracked elastic solid*

For simplicity, the setting adopted is **linear elasticity and small strains**, but this approach extends to non-linear elasticity [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03), to large displacements and large rotations, to dynamics...

One denotes by:

- $\varepsilon$ the strain tensor,
- ${\varepsilon}^{0}$ the pre-strain tensor,
- ${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}$ the tensor of strains of thermal origin,
- $\sigma$ the stress tensor,
- ${\sigma}^{0}$ the tensor of initial stresses,
- $\Psi (\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T)$ the free-energy density,
- $\Lambda$ the elasticity tensor.

$\varepsilon$ is related to the displacement field $u$ by:

The **free-energy density** $\Psi (\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T)$ is identified by a tensile and expansion test in small strains. $\Psi (\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T)$ is a convex and differentiable function.

The constitutive law of an elastic material is written in the form:
$$
\sigma = \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial \varepsilon }(\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T)
= \Lambda (\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}-{\varepsilon}^{0})+{\sigma}^{0}

\text{avec} \quad {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}_{ij} = \alpha (T-{T}_{\mathrm{ref}}){\delta}_{ij}
$$
In the case where the initial strains ${\varepsilon}^{0}$ and the initial stresses are zero, the free-energy density is written:

The **constitutive relation** is written:

$\lambda$ and $\mu$ are the Lamé coefficients.

$\alpha$ is the thermal expansion coefficient.

${T}_{\mathrm{réf}}$ is the reference temperature.

$K$ , the bulk modulus, is related to the Lamé coefficients by: $3K=3\lambda +2\mu$ .

The constitutive relation in terms of Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ is:
$$
{\sigma}_{ij} = \frac{E}{1+\nu } \left( {\varepsilon}_{ij} + \frac{\nu}{1-2\nu } (\mathrm{tr} \varepsilon ) {\delta}_{ij} \right)
- \frac{\alpha E}{1-2\nu } (T-{T}_{\mathrm{réf}}) {\delta}_{ij}
$$

$$
\text{avec :} \quad
\lambda = \frac{\nu E}{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}, \quad
\mu = \frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}, \quad
3K = \frac{E}{1-2\nu }
$$
### 1.2 Potential energy and equilibrium relations

The spaces of kinematically admissible fields $V$ and ${V}_{0}$ are defined.
$$
V = \lbrace v \text{ admissibles, } v=U \text{ sur } {S}_{d} \rbrace, \quad
V_{0} = \lbrace v \text{ admissibles, } v=0 \text{ sur } {S}_{d} \rbrace
$$
With the assumptions of [§1.1] (and for ${\sigma}^{0}={\varepsilon}^{0}=0$),
the **equilibrium relations in weak form** are:
$$
\begin{cases}
    u \in V, \\
    \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} {v}_{i,j} \, d\Omega =
    \int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} {v}_{i} \, d\Omega +
    \int_{S} {g}_{i} {v}_{i} \, d\Gamma, \quad \forall v \in V_0.
\end{cases}
$$
They are obtained by minimising the global potential energy of the system:

Indeed, if this functional is minimal for the displacement field $u$ , then:
$$
\delta W = \int_{\Omega} \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{ij}} \delta {\varepsilon}_{ij} \, d\Omega
- \int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} {g}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Gamma
$$

$$
= \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} \frac{1}{2} (\delta {v}_{i,j} + \delta {v}_{j,i}) \, d\Omega
- \int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} {g}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Gamma
$$

$$
= \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} \delta {v}_{i,j} \, d\Omega
- \int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} {g}_{i} \delta {v}_{i} \, d\Gamma = 0
$$
The equilibrium equations and the constitutive relation are thus recovered, having set: ${\sigma}_{ij}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{ij}}$ .

### 1.3 Lagrangian expression of the energy release rate

By definition [bib10], the local energy release rate $G$ is defined by the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy with respect to the domain $\Omega$ :

This energy release rate can be calculated by the $\theta$ method, which is a **Lagrangian method of derivation of the potential energy** [bib4] [bib2]. Transformations ${F}^{\eta}:M\to M+\eta \theta (M)$ of the reference domain $\Omega$ into a domain ${\Omega}^{\eta}$ are considered, modelling crack propagations, which associate a spatial point $M$ with a material point $P$ . These transformations must modify only the position of the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ . The fields $\theta$ must therefore be tangent to $\partial \Omega$ , that is, denoting by $n$ the normal to $\partial \Omega$ :
$$
\theta \in \Theta = \lbrace \mu \text{ tels que } \mu \cdot n = 0 \text{ sur } \partial \Omega \rbrace
$$
**Note**

This family of transformation functions must be sufficiently regular. In particular, it must be at least twice piecewise differentiable in $P$ and in $\eta$ (so that the second partial derivatives commute) and realise a diffeomorphism for each value of the parameter $\eta$ (this ensures the reversibility of the process).

Let $m$ be the unit normal to $\Gamma_0$, lying in the plane tangent to $\partial \Omega$
(that is, tangent to the crack plane) and oriented towards the interior of $\Omega$.

*Crack front in 3D*

According to proposition 7 of [bib4], the local energy release rate $G$ is the solution of the variational equation:

where $G(\theta )$ is defined by the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy $W(u(\eta ))$ at equilibrium with respect to the initial evolution of the crack front $\eta$ :

The quantity $\theta .m$ represents the normal velocity of the crack front. Furthermore, $G(\theta )$ has the same value whether one is dealing with a straight propagation [Figure1.3-b] (a) or a curved propagation [Figure1.3‑b] (b), in so far as the latter has the same tangent at the start (thereafter nothing can be said). On the other hand, nothing can be said about the case of propagation in a direction making an angle [bib5] [Figure 1.3-b] (c).

*Different propagation geometries*

In what follows, when no confusion is possible, . will denote the Lagrangian derivative in a virtual crack propagation of velocity $\theta$ . Let $\phi (\eta ,M)$ be an arbitrary spatial (or Eulerian) field defined on ${R}^{+}\times \Omega$ ; we shall denote its material (or Lagrangian) representation $\stackrel{ˉ}{\varphi}(\eta ,P)=\varphi (\eta ,{F}^{\eta}(P))$  and its material (or Lagrangian) derivative with respect to $\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta }$ this virtual propagation $\dot{\varphi}={(\frac{\partial \stackrel{ˉ}{\varphi}}{\partial \eta })}_{\eta =0}$ .

- Adopting two different viewpoints (Eulerian and Lagrangian) structurally introduces notions of crossed differentiability. Thus, this material derivative of a spatial field, called the Lagrangian derivative, consists in differentiating $\phi (\eta ,M)$ while keeping the material point $P={({F}^{\eta})}^{-1}(M)$ fixed. The field is transposed into the Lagrangian representation, then differentiated with respect to $\eta$ before being converted back into the Eulerian representation.
- It is recalled that this Lagrangian derivative is related to the Eulerian derivative $\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta }$ by the relation $\dot{\varphi}=\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta }+\nabla \varphi .\theta$

**Notes [bib6]**

**Note [bib4]**

The Eulerian derivative $\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta }$ depends only on $\Omega$ restricted to $\delta \Omega$, that is, on the trace of $\Omega$ on the crack front.

With these notations, the energy release rate in this propagation $\theta$ is written
(using the **Reynolds transport theorem**):
$$
-G(\theta ) = \int_{\Omega} \dot{\psi} - \dot{f}_{i} u_{i}
+ (\psi - f_{i} u_{i}) {\theta}_{k,k} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} \dot{g}_{i} u_{i}
+ g_{i} u_{i} \left( {\theta}_{k,k} - \frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {n}_{k}} n_{k} \right) d\Gamma
$$
Now,
$$
\dot{\Psi}(\varepsilon ,{\varepsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T) =
\frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{ij}} \dot{{\varepsilon}_{ij}}
+ \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{ij}^{0}} {\dot{{\varepsilon}_{ij}}}^{0}
+ \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{0}} {\dot{{\sigma}_{ij}}}^{0}
+ \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial T} \dot{T}
$$
$T, f, g, {\sigma}^{0}, {\varepsilon}^{0}$ being assumed independent of $\eta$,
that is, being the restriction to $\Omega$ (or $\delta \Omega$) of fields defined on $\mathbb{R}^{3}$,
one has the following relations:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \dot{T} = {T}_{,k} {\theta}_{k}, \\
    \dot{f}_{i} = {f}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k}, \\
    \dot{g}_{i} = {g}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k}, \\
    {\dot{{\varepsilon}_{ij}}}^{0} = {{\varepsilon}_{ij,k}}^{0} {\theta}_{k}, \\
    {\dot{{\sigma}_{ij}}}^{0} = {{\sigma}_{ij,k}}^{0} {\theta}_{k}.
\end{cases}
$$
Indeed, if one considers loadings and materials $\varphi$ which are the **restriction to the geometry**
$\Omega$ (or a part of its boundary) of fields defined on the whole of $\mathbb{R}^{3}$:
$$
\exists \Phi \quad \text{tel que} \quad \varphi = {\Phi}_{\mid \Omega} = 0.
$$
Derivation with respect to the parameter $\eta$ commuting with this restriction, one has the result:
$$
\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta } = \left( \frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial \eta } \right)_{\mid \Omega} = {0}_{\mid \Omega} = 0.
$$
- This assumption holds only for sufficiently regular fields (for example belonging to Sobolev spaces of $\Omega$). Their definition must not be affected by the variation of the boundary.
- In the case of the derivation of the energy release rate with respect to a domain variation, the Eulerian derivative of the temperature field can no longer be neglected.

**Notes**

Furthermore, it has also been assumed that the **Eulerian derivatives of the material characteristics**
$\varphi$ are zero, which is true only on the discretised problem with the current functionalities
of the operator [DEFI_MATERIAU](doc:u4.43.01). Their gradient on each element is also zero by construction
(they are discretised as ${P}_{0}$, that is, constant per finite element);
it follows that the Lagrangian derivative is zero:
$$
\dot{\varphi} = \underbrace{\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial \eta }}_{=0}
+ \underbrace{\nabla \varphi \cdot \theta}_{=0}, \quad \text{pour} \quad \varphi \in \lbrace E, \nu , \alpha , {T}_{\mathrm{ref}}\rbrace.
$$
Warning!
With material characteristics that vary within finite elements of the calculation crown $\theta$, this simplification is no longer valid.
$$
\begin{aligned}
    \text{Comme} \quad \Psi (\epsilon ,{\epsilon}^{0},{\sigma}^{0},T) &= \frac{1}{2}
    (\epsilon - {\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}} - {\epsilon}^{0} + {\Lambda}^{-1} {\sigma}^{0})
    \Lambda (\epsilon - {\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}} - {\epsilon}^{0} + {\Lambda}^{-1} {\sigma}^{0}), \\
    \text{on a} \quad \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\epsilon}_{ij}} &= {\Lambda}_{ijkl}
    \left({\epsilon}_{kl} - {\epsilon}_{kl}^{\mathit{th}} - {\epsilon}_{kl}^{0}\right) + {\sigma}_{ij}^{0} = {\sigma}_{ij}, \\
    \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\epsilon}_{ij}^{0}} &= -{\Lambda}_{ijkl}
    \left({\epsilon}_{kl} - {\epsilon}_{kl}^{\mathit{th}} - {\epsilon}_{kl}^{0} + {\Lambda}_{\mathit{klpq}}^{-1} {\sigma}_{\mathit{pq}}^{0}\right) = -{\sigma}_{ij}, \\
    \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}^{0}} &= \left({\epsilon}_{ij} - {\epsilon}_{ij}^{\mathit{th}}
    - {\epsilon}_{ij}^{0} + {\Lambda}_{ijkl}^{-1} {\sigma}_{kl}^{0}\right) = {\Lambda}_{ijkl}^{-1} {\sigma}_{kl}.
\end{aligned}
$$
Hence:
$$
\dot{\Psi} = {\sigma}_{ij} \dot{{\epsilon}_{ij}} - {\sigma}_{ij} {\epsilon}_{ij,k}^{0} {\theta}_{k}
+ {\Lambda}_{\mathit{ijpq}}^{-1} {\sigma}_{\mathit{pq}} {\sigma}_{ij,k}^{0} {\theta}_{k}
+ \frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T} {T}_{,k} {\theta}_{k}.
$$
Furthermore, according to proposition 2 of [bib4]:
$$
(\dot{\varphi}_{i,j}) = {\dot{\varphi}}_{i,j} - {\varphi}_{i,p} {\theta}_{p,j}.
$$

$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{ij} = \frac{1}{2} \left( {\dot{u}}_{i,j} + {\dot{u}}_{j,i} \right)
- \frac{1}{2} \left( {u}_{i,p} {\theta}_{p,j} + {u}_{j,p} {\theta}_{p,i} \right).
$$
One can eliminate $\dot{u}$ from the expression of $G(\theta )$ by noting that $\dot{u}$ is kinematically admissible and by using the **equilibrium equation**:
$$
\int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} {\dot{u}}_{i,j} d\Omega =
\int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} {\dot{u}}_{i} d\Omega +
\int_{S} {g}_{i} {\dot{u}}_{i} d\Gamma +
\int_{{S}_{d}} {\sigma}_{ij} {n}_{j} {U}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k} d\Gamma.
$$
Hence:
$$
G(\theta) = \int_{\Omega} \dot{\psi} - f_{i} \dot{u}_{i} - \dot{f}_{i} u_{i}
+ (\psi - f_{i} u_{i}) \theta_{k,k} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} (g_{i} \dot{u}_{i} + \dot{g}_{i} u_{i}) \, d\Gamma
- \int_{S} g_{i} u_{i} \left( \theta_{k,k} - \frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {\eta}_{k}} \eta_{k} \right) d\Gamma
$$

$$
= \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} \dot{u}_{i,j} - f_{i} \dot{u}_{i} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} g_{i} \dot{u}_{i} \, d\Gamma
- \int_{\Omega} \dot{f}_{i} u_{i} \, d\Omega
- \int_{\Omega} \frac{1}{2} {\sigma}_{ij} (u_{i,j} \theta_{p,j} + u_{j,p} \theta_{p,i}) \, d\Omega
$$

$$
+ \int_{\Omega} \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial T} \dot{T} + (\psi - f_{i} u_{i}) \theta_{k,k} \, d\Omega
- \int_{S} \dot{g}_{i} u_{i} + g_{i} u_{i} \left( \theta_{k,k} - \frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {\eta}_{k}} \eta_{k} \right) d\Gamma
$$

$$
+ \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} {\varepsilon}_{ij,k}^{0} \theta_{k}
- {\Lambda}_{\mathrm{ijpq}}^{-1} {\sigma}_{\mathrm{pq}} {\sigma}_{ij,k}^{0} \theta_{k} \, d\Omega.
$$
Finally:
$$
G(\theta ) = \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} {u}_{i,p} {\theta}_{p,j} - \psi {\theta}_{k,k}
- \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial T} {T}_{,k} {\theta}_{k} d\Omega

+ \int_{\Omega} {\sigma}_{ij} {\varepsilon}_{ij,k}^{0} {\theta}_{k}
- {\Lambda}_{\mathrm{ijpq}}^{-1} {\sigma}_{\mathrm{pq}} {\sigma}_{ij,k}^{0} {\theta}_{k} d\Omega

+ \int_{\Omega} {f}_{i} {u}_{i} {\theta}_{k,k} + {f}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k} {u}_{i} d\Omega

+ \int_{S} {g}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k} {u}_{i} + {g}_{i} {u}_{i}
\left( {\theta}_{k,k} - \frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {\eta}_{k}} {\eta}_{k} \right) d\Gamma

- \int_{{S}_{d}} {\sigma}_{ij} {\eta}_{j} {U}_{i,k} {\theta}_{k} d\Gamma.
$$
- In Euler–Lagrange strains, the first integrand becomes ${h}_{i,p}{\sigma}_{ij}{u}_{i,p}{\theta}_{p,j}$ with ${h}_{i,j}={\delta}_{i,j}+{u}_{i,j}$.
- In axisymmetry, one has the formal analogy* $(x,y)\iff (r,z)$ and all the components of the gradients involving the circumferential component are zero, except  ${\varphi}_{\theta ,\theta }=\frac{{\varphi}_{r}}{r}$. Moreover, the surface element is multiplied by r in order to take into account the calculation of the integral for one unit of radian.
- The possibility of taking imposed displacement fields into account has not been developed. Moreover, these are not constrained by the crack propagation, since they appear via the equilibrium condition.
- In the surface term, there are derivatives normal to the surface which have no meaning for the skin elements. One therefore resorts to differential geometry and to the contravariant derivatives in order to better handle this integrand on the calculation surface.

## 2 Discretisation of the energy release rate

### 2.1 $\theta$ method in dimension 2

It is recalled that the energy release rate $G$ is the solution of the variational equation:
$$
\int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} G(s) \theta (s) \cdot m(s) \, ds = G(\theta), \quad \forall \theta \in \Theta
$$
where:

- $m$ is the unit normal to the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$, lying in the plane tangent to
$\partial \Omega$ and oriented towards the interior of $\Omega$,
- $\Theta = \lbrace \mu \text{ tels que } \mu \cdot n = 0 \text{ sur } \partial \Omega \rbrace$.

In dimension 2, the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ reduces to a point $M_{0}$, and
one can choose a unit field $\theta$ in the neighbourhood of this point, so that: $G(M_{0}) = G(\theta)$

![Fond de fissure en 2D](/figures/r7.02.01/100004B400000EC800000EC8E1B316750A6AAC09.svg)

***Figure 2.1-a: Crack front in 2D***

### 2.2 $\theta$ method in dimension 3

The dependence of $G(\theta )$ on the field $\theta$ on the crack front is more complex.
The scalar field $G(s)$ can be discretised on a basis which we shall denote
${({p}_{j}(s))}_{1\le j\le N}$.

![Discrétisation du fond de fissure en 3D](/figures/r7.02.01/1000055C00000A2100000B795564713E0B73BAC3.svg)

***Figure 2.2-a: Discretisation of the crack front in 3D (curvilinear abscissa)***

Let ${G}_{j}$ be the components of $G(s)$ in this basis:
$$
G(s) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} G_{j} p_{j}(s).
$$
A basis of test functions for the fields $\theta$, of length $P$, is also given:
$$
\tilde{\Theta}=\lbrace {\theta}^{i} \in \Theta, \quad i=1, \dots, P \rbrace.
$$
$\tilde{\Theta}$ is a subset of finite length of the set $\Theta$.

$G(s)$ being the solution of the variational equation:
$$
\int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} G(s) \theta (s) \cdot m(s) \, ds = G(\theta), \quad \forall \theta \in \Theta,
$$
the coefficients ${G}_{j}$ then satisfy in particular:
$$
\int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} \left( \sum_{j=1}^{N} G_{j} p_{j}(s) \right) \bar{\theta}^{i} \cdot m(s) \, ds = G(\theta^{i}),
\quad \forall i \in [1,P],
$$
that is:
$$
\sum_{j=1}^{N} \left( \int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} p_{j}(s) \bar{\theta}^{i} \cdot m(s) \, ds \right) G_{j} = G(\theta^{i}),
\quad \forall i \in [1,P].
$$
The coefficients ${G}_{j}$ can therefore be determined by solving the linear system with $P$ equations and $N$ unknowns:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \sum_{j=1}^{N} a_{ij} G_{j} = b_{i}, \quad i = 1, P, \\
    a_{ij} = \int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} p_{j}(s) \bar{\theta}^{i}(s) \cdot m(s) \, ds, \\
    b_{i} = G(\theta^{i}).
\end{cases}
$$
This system has a solution if one chooses $P$ independent fields $\theta^{i}$ such that $P \geq N$.
It may contain more equations than unknowns, in which case it is solved in the **least-squares sense**.

**Note:**

*In reality, a basis of test functions is given for the trace of the fields* $\theta$ *on the crack front* ${\Gamma}_{0}$,
*denoted* $\bar{\theta}$ *and defined by* $\bar{\theta}(s) = {\theta}_{\mid {\Gamma}_{0}}(s)$.
*The datum of the value of* $\bar{\theta}^{i}$ *on the crack front* ${\Gamma}_{0}$ *then suffices to identify the field* $\theta^{i}$ *over the whole domain*.

### 2.3 Choice of the discretisation of G in dimension 3

#### 2.3.1 Description of the approximation spaces

**Note:**

- **In dimension 2**, there is no problem because, by choosing a unit field $\theta$
in the neighbourhood of the crack front, one obtains the relation $G = G(\theta)$.
The energy release rate is therefore independent of the field $\theta$.

In dimension 3, the dependence of $G(\theta )$ on the field $\theta$ on the crack front is more complex.
One calculates the local energy release rate $G(s)$, solution of the variational equation:
$$
\int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} G(s) \theta (s) \cdot m(s) \, ds = G(\theta ), \quad \forall \theta \in \Theta.
$$
Two families of bases of test functions for $\bar{\theta}$ and of decomposition for $G(s)$ have been chosen:

- **The Legendre polynomials** ${\gamma}_{j}(s)$, of degree $j$ with $0 \leq j \leq \mathrm{Deg}_{\max}$.
- **The shape functions** associated with node $k$ of ${\Gamma}_{0}$, denoted ${\phi }_{k}(s)$,
with $1\leq k\leq \mathrm{NNO}$ (number of nodes of ${\Gamma}_{0}$),
of degree one for the linear elements and two for the quadratic elements.

Recall that the **Legendre polynomials** constitute a non-normalised orthogonal family. They are obtained by the recurrence relation:
$$
(n+1) P_{n+1}(t) - (2n+1) t P_{n}(t) + n P_{n-1}(t) = 0.
$$
In particular:
$$
P_{0}(t) = 1, \\
P_{1}(t) = t, \\
P_{2}(t) = \frac{3t^2 - 1}{2}, \\
P_{3}(t) = \frac{5t^3 - 3t}{2}.
$$
They are normalised in the form:
$$
{\gamma}_{j}(s) = \sqrt{\frac{2j+1}{l}} P_{j} \left(\frac{2s}{l} - 1\right),
$$
where:

- $s$ is the curvilinear abscissa of ${\Gamma}_{0}$,
- $l$ is the length of the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$.

![Polynômes de Legendre](/figures/r7.02.01/100010E00000292200000AA5887E98D141BC3DC8.svg)

***Figure 2.3-b: Legendre polynomials***

The maximum degree is limited to $\mathrm{Deg}_{\max} = 7$.

The shape functions $\varphi_{k}(s)$ are associated with the discretisation of ${\Gamma}_{0}$.

![Fonctions de forme du fond de fissure (éléments linéaires)](/figures/r7.02.01/10000D7E0000277B000007573790E29CFDC98B43.svg)

***Figure 2.3-c: Shape functions of the crack front (linear elements)***

Recall that one is led to discretise $G(s)$ along the crack front:
$$
G(s) = \sum_{j=1}^{N} G_{j} p_{j}(s).
$$
and to introduce a basis of test functions ${(\bar{\theta}^{i}(s))}_{1\leq i\leq N}$
for the trace of the field $\theta$ on the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$.

There are therefore several possible choices of discretisation, summarised in the table below:

|  | **LEGENDRE polynomials** | **Shape functions** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $G(s)$ | $G(s)=\sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NDEG}}{G}_{j}{\gamma}_{j}(s)$ | $\sum_{j=1}^{\mathrm{NNO}}{G}_{j}{\varphi}_{j}(s)$ |
| $\bar{\theta}(s)$ | ${({\gamma}_{i}(s))}_{1\leq i\leq \mathrm{NDEG}}$ | ${({\varphi}_{i}(s))}_{1\leq i\leq \mathrm{NNO}}$ |

#### 2.3.2 First case: LEGENDRE discretisation

$G(s)$ is decomposed on the Legendre polynomials:
$$
G(s) = \sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NDEG}} G_{j} \gamma_{j}(s).
$$
The basis of the test functions for $\theta (s)$ is defined from the Legendre polynomials:
$$
\tilde{\Theta} = \lbrace {\theta}^{i} \in \Theta \mid \bar{\theta}^{i}(s) \cdot m(s) = {\varphi}_{i}(s),
\quad i = 1, \dots, \mathrm{NDEG} \rbrace.
$$
The $\mathrm{NDEG}$ components $G_{j}$ are determined by solving the linear system
with $\mathrm{NDEG}$ equations:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NDEG}} a_{ij} G_{j} = b_{i}, \quad i = 1, \dots, \mathrm{NDEG}, \\
    \text{avec} \quad
    \begin{cases}
        a_{ij} = \int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} \gamma_{i}(s) \gamma_{j}(s) \, ds, \\
        b_{i} = G(\theta^{i}).
    \end{cases}
\end{cases}
$$
The Legendre polynomials forming an **orthonormal basis** on ${\Gamma}_{0}$, one has $a_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$
and the linear system therefore reduces to:
$$
G_{j} = G(\theta^{j}),
$$
and therefore:
$$
G(s) = \sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NDEG}} G(\theta^{j}) \gamma_{j}(s).
$$
#### 2.3.3 Second case: LINEAIRE discretisation

$G(s)$ is defined by the shape functions of the $\mathrm{NNO}$ nodes of the crack front:
$$
G(s) = \sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NNO}} G_{j} \varphi_{j}(s).
$$
The basis of the test functions for $\theta (s)$ is defined from the shape functions of the $\mathrm{NNO}$
nodes of the crack front:
$$
\tilde{\Theta} = \lbrace {\theta}^{i} \in \Theta \mid \bar{\theta}^{i}(s) \cdot m(s) = {\varphi}_{i}(s),
\quad i = 1, \dots, \mathrm{NNO} \rbrace,
$$
where ${\varphi}_{i}(s)$ is the shape function of node $i$ of the crack front.

System to be solved:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \sum_{j=0}^{\mathrm{NNO}} a_{ij} G_{j} = G(\theta^{i}), \quad (i=1,\dots,\mathrm{NNO}), \\
    \text{avec} \quad a_{ij} = \int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} \varphi_{i}(s) \varphi_{j}(s) \, ds.
\end{cases}
$$
**Linear elements:**
If one has linear elements, the shape functions are defined as follows:
$$
\varphi_{1}(x) = \frac{1}{2} (1 - x), \quad \varphi_{2}(x) = \frac{1}{2} (1 + x).
$$
The coefficients of the matrix $A_{ij}$ are given by:
$$
a_{i(i-j)} = a_{i(i+j)} = 0, \quad \text{si } j \geq 2.
$$

$$
a_{i(i-1)} = \int_{{\Gamma}_{0}} \varphi_{i}(s) \varphi_{i-1}(s) \, ds
= \int_{s_{i-1}}^{s_{i}} \varphi_{i}(s) \varphi_{i-1}(s) \, ds.
$$

$$
a_{i(i-1)} = \frac{s_{i} - s_{i-1}}{2} \int_{-1}^{+1} \varphi_{i}(x) \varphi_{i-1}(x) \, dx
= \frac{s_{i} - s_{i-1}}{2} \int_{-1}^{+1} \frac{1}{4} (1 - x^2) \, dx
= \frac{1}{6} (s_{i} - s_{i-1}).
$$

$$

+ \frac{s_{i+1} - s_{i}}{2} \int_{-1}^{+1} \varphi_{1}^{2}(x) \, dx.

$$

$$

$$

\end{bmatrix}

$$

#### 2.3.4 Correction of the values at the ends of the crack front for the LINEAIRE discretisation

When the **crack front emerges**, a particular additional treatment is carried out.

It has been observed that, for the **linear discretisation**, the numerical values of $G({\theta}^{i})$
for $i=1$ and $i=n$ are particularly “wrong”. This is probably due to the order of the singularity,
which is no longer $-1/2$ in the vicinity of the free surfaces.

Indeed, the order of the singularity at the edges depends on $\nu$ (and on the angle between the crack front and the free surface,
as well as on the angle between the normal to the crack surface and the normal to the free surface).
Even for a crack orthogonal to the free surface, the singularity is not of order $-1/2$ if $\nu \neq 0$.

**Correction at the edge points**
In order to improve the results at the edge points, the values of $G({\theta}^{i})$ are modified for $i=1$
and $i=n$ in the case of the **linear discretisation**. The coefficients $K_{j}({\theta}^{i})$ are also modified,
for $j=I, II, III$.

This treatment is applied even in the presence of symmetry conditions on the edges of the front, although in this case
the order of the singularity is indeed $-1/2$ and no treatment is theoretically necessary.

**Case 1: Linear discretisation and linear mesh**
One makes the assumption that:
$$
G(s_1) = G(s_2) = G(s_3) = G_a, \quad G(s_n) = G(s_{n-1}) = G(s_{n-2}) = G_b.
$$
Then, by multiplying the matrix $A$ by the vector $G(s)$, one obtains:
$$
G({\theta}^{1}) = \frac{1}{2} (s_2 - s_1) G_a
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{2}) = \frac{1}{2} (s_3 - s_1) G_a
$$

$$
\vdots
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n-1}) = \frac{1}{2} (s_n - s_{n-2}) G_b
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n}) = \frac{1}{2} (s_n - s_{n-1}) G_b
$$
Which leads to the relations:
$$
G({\theta}^{1}) = G({\theta}^{2}) \frac{(s_2 - s_1)}{(s_3 - s_1)}
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n}) = G({\theta}^{n-1}) \frac{(s_n - s_{n-1})}{(s_n - s_{n-2})}
$$
**Case 2: Linear discretisation and quadratic mesh**
One makes the assumption that:
$$
G(s_1) = G(s_2) = G(s_3) = G_a, \quad G(s_n) = G(s_{n-1}) = G(s_{n-2}) = G_b.
$$
Then, by multiplying the matrix $A$ by the vector $G(s)$, one obtains:
$$
G({\theta}^{1}) = \frac{1}{6} (s_3 - s_1) G_a
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{2}) = \frac{4}{6} (s_3 - s_1) G_a
$$

$$
\vdots
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n-1}) = \frac{4}{6} (s_n - s_{n-2}) G_b
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n}) = \frac{4}{6} (s_n - s_{n-2}) G_b
$$
Which leads to the relations:
$$
G({\theta}^{1}) = \frac{1}{4} G({\theta}^{2})
$$

$$
G({\theta}^{n}) = \frac{1}{4} G({\theta}^{n-1})
$$
### 2.4 Implementation of G in linear thermoelasticity

#### 2.4.1 Types of elements and of loadings

It is possible to calculate, in linear thermoelasticity, the energy release rate:

- In **2D**: $G(\theta )$
- In **3D** (local value): $G(s)$

These calculations are carried out by means of the command **CALC_G** [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03).

The virtual crack-propagation field $\theta$ is calculated automatically in the command CALC_G,
from the information supplied by the user.

These calculations are valid for the following **modellings**:

- **D_PLAN** *(meshed or non-meshed crack)*
- **C_PLAN** *(meshed or non-meshed crack)*
- **AXIS** *(meshed or non-meshed crack)*
- **3D** *(meshed or non-meshed crack)*

The calculations are valid for the following **thermomechanical loadings**:

- $f$: field of **body forces** applied on $\Omega$
(mechanical loads of type **PESANTEUR, ROTATION, FORCE_INTERNE**).
- $g$: field of **surface forces** applied on a part $S$ of $\delta \Omega$
(including on the crack lips: **PRES_REP, FORCE_FACE**).
- $T$: **temperature field** (transmitted via the material field: **AFFE_MATERIAU / AFFE_VARC**).
- ${\varepsilon}^{0}$: **pre-strain** field (**PRE_EPSI**).

These loadings may depend on time and on space.
The term due to the displacements imposed on ${S}_{d}$ is not taken into account.
Dirichlet conditions must therefore not be imposed on the crack lips.

The material properties $(E, \nu, \alpha )$ may:

- Depend on the temperature $T$.
- Vary in space, while remaining constant per element.

An initial-stress field ${\sigma}^{0}$ may also be taken into account.

#### 2.4.2 Necessary environment

**Meshed crack:**

For the calculation of the energy release rate (option G), the crack must first be characterised by means of the concept of type fond_fiss (in 3D: ordered list of the nodes of the front, mesh cells of the upper lip and of the lower lip; in 2D: crack-front node and normal to the crack), with the command DEFI_FOND_FISS [U4.82.01](doc:u4.82.01).

When the crack lies along an axis of symmetry, one may represent only half of the model, and specify the symmetry of the loading by the keyword SYME in DEFI_FOND_FISS. By default, it is assumed that there is no symmetry. If the model exhibits a symmetry, CALC_G detects it automatically via the concept fond_fiss.

**Non-meshed crack (X-FEM method):**

In 2D as in 3D, the crack must be defined, for the mechanical analysis and for the post-processing, by means of the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM. The keyword FISSURE must be supplied in CALC_G_XFEM.

If the crack is not meshed, it is not possible to take into account any symmetries of the model with respect to the crack lips.

The calculation of $G$ is carried out in post-processing, solely from the displacement field which is the solution of the analysis on the model considered. In particular, the free-energy density and the stresses are calculated from the displacement field and from the material characteristics.

#### 2.4.3 Calculations of the various terms of the energy release rate

The complete expression for $G(\theta )$ is given in [§1.3]. Each term will be detailed. The field $\theta$ is zero outside a disc of radius ${R}_{\sup}(s)$ defined in chapter 3 [Figure 3.3-a]. Note that, since all the terms involve $\theta$ or its gradient, the elementary terms are zero outside this disc of radius ${R}_{\sup}(s)$ . In the command CALC_G, it is therefore not necessary to specify the loadings which do not apply in this zone.

##### 2.4.3.1 Classical elementary term

The classical elementary term (TCLA) is defined by:
$$
\text{TCLA} = {\sigma}_{ij} {u}_{i,p} {\theta}_{p,j} - \psi (\varepsilon (u),T) {\theta}_{k,k}
$$
The elastic energy density $\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T)$ is expressed in linear thermoelasticity as follows:

**1. In 3D and in AXIS**
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{1}{2} \lambda ({\varepsilon}_{ii})^2 + \mu {\varepsilon}_{ij} {\varepsilon}_{ij} - {\psi}_{\mathrm{th}}
$$
**2. In plane strain**
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{(1-\nu)E}{2(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)} ({\varepsilon}_{xx}^{2} + {\varepsilon}_{yy}^{2})
+ \frac{\nu E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)} {\varepsilon}_{xx} {\varepsilon}_{yy}
+ \frac{E}{(1+\nu)} {\varepsilon}_{xy}^{2} - {\psi}_{\mathrm{th}}
$$
**3. In plane stress**
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{E}{2(1-\nu^2)} ({\varepsilon}_{xx}^{2} + {\varepsilon}_{yy}^{2})
+ \frac{\nu E}{(1-\nu^2)} {\varepsilon}_{xx} {\varepsilon}_{yy}
+ \frac{E}{(1+\nu)} {\varepsilon}_{xy}^{2} - {\psi}_{\mathrm{th}}
$$
where the thermal component is given by:
$$
{\Psi}_{\mathrm{th}} = 3K\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}) {\varepsilon}_{ii}
- \frac{9}{2} K {\alpha}^{2} (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}})^2
$$
The parameters are defined as follows:
$$
3K = \frac{E}{1-2\nu}, \quad \lambda = \frac{\nu E}{(1+\nu)(1-2\nu)}, \quad 2\mu = \frac{E}{1+\nu}
$$
- $E$: **Young's modulus**
- $\nu$: **Poisson's ratio**
- $\lambda, \mu$: **Lamé coefficients**
- $\alpha$: **Thermal expansion**

The **elastic energy density** $\psi (\varepsilon (u),T)$ can be written in a general form:
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{1}{2} K (\varepsilon_{kk} - 3\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}))^2
+ \frac{2\mu}{3} \varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}^2
$$
with:
$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}^2 = \frac{3}{2} \varepsilon_{ij}^{D} \varepsilon_{ij}^{D}, \quad
\varepsilon_{ij}^{D} = \varepsilon_{ij} - \frac{1}{3} \varepsilon_{kk} \delta_{ij}
$$
or alternatively:
$$
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{eq}}^2 = \frac{1}{2} (3\varepsilon_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij} - \varepsilon_{kk}^2)
$$
Thus, the elastic energy density can also be written in the following form:
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{1}{2} K \varepsilon_{kk}^2
- 3K\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}) \varepsilon_{kk}
+ \frac{9}{2} K \alpha^2 (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}})^2
+ \mu \varepsilon_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij}
- \frac{\mu}{3} \varepsilon_{kk}^2
$$
or alternatively:
$$
\Psi (\varepsilon (u),T) = \frac{\lambda}{2} \varepsilon_{kk}^2 + \mu \varepsilon_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij}
- 3K\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}) \varepsilon_{kk}
+ \frac{9}{2} K \alpha^2 (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}})^2
$$
##### 2.4.3.2 Body-force term

The body-force term (TFOR) is defined by:
$$
\mathrm{TFOR} = f_{i} u_{i} \theta_{k,k} + f_{i,k} \theta_{k} u_{i}
$$
##### 2.4.3.3 Surface-force term

The surface-force term (TSUR) is written:
$$
\mathrm{TSUR} = g_{i,k} \theta_{k} u_{i} + g_{i} u_{i}
\left(\theta_{k,k} - \frac{\partial \theta}{\partial n_{k}} n_{k} \right)
$$
**Note:**
*In this surface term, derivatives normal to the surface appear.
They have no meaning for the skin elements used.
Differential geometry and the contravariant derivatives are therefore used
in order to better handle this integrand on the calculation surface.*

##### 2.4.3.4 Thermal term

The thermal term (THER) is written:
$$
\mathrm{THER} = -\frac{\partial \psi}{\partial T} T_{,k} \theta_{k}
$$
with:
$$
\frac{\partial \Psi}{\partial T} (\varepsilon (u),T) =
\left[ \frac{1}{2} \frac{\mathrm{dK}(T)}{\mathrm{dT}}
(\varepsilon_{kk} - 3\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}))
- 3K \left( \alpha + \frac{d\alpha (T)}{\mathrm{dT}}
(T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}) \right) \right]
(\varepsilon_{kk} - 3\alpha (T - T_{\mathrm{réf}}))
$$
##### 2.4.3.5 Pre-strain and initial-stress term

The pre-strain and initial-stress term (TINI) is written:
$$
\mathrm{TINI} = \left[ \sigma_{ij} \varepsilon_{ij,k}^{0}
- \Lambda_{\mathrm{ijpq}}^{-1} \sigma_{\mathrm{pq}} \sigma_{ij,k}^{0} \right] \theta_{k}
$$
One may note that **if** $\sigma = 0$, **then** $\mathrm{TINI} = 0$.

**Note 1:**
*As for the rest of this documentation, the presence of initial stresses
is valid only in the case of isotropic linear elasticity.
In order to be activated, one must be in an **incremental constitutive relation** (COMPORTEMENT)
with the relation ELAS. It **is not possible** to supply an initial-stress field
in any other case.*

**Note 2:**
*In all cases, the initial-stress field must be **self-equilibrated** in the absence of a crack, with the boundary conditions alone.
The user can (and should) check that the initial-stress field is admissible by applying it in ETAT_INIT
of the operator STAT_NON_LINE, with an incremental elastic behaviour (COMPORTEMENT, RELATION = 'ELAS').
The mechanical result must then give the **same stress field without additional strains**.*

**Note 3:**
*The initial-stress field must have been used beforehand in the thermomechanical analysis.
The procedure for calculating the energy release rate in the presence of initial stresses is detailed below.
One may also refer to the dedicated test case.*

## 3 Introduction of the field \theta

### 3.1 Conditions to be satisfied

The field $\theta$ is a vector field, defined on the cracked solid, which represents the transformation of the domain during a crack propagation in the sense of [§1]. The transformation must modify only the position of the crack front and not the boundary of the domain $\delta \Omega$ , that is: $\theta .n=0$  on ($n$  normal to $\delta \Omega$ ). Moreover, the field $\theta$ **must be regular**  on $\Omega$ [bib4].

Because of the singularity of the displacement field, it is advantageous from the numerical point of view to use fields $\theta$ which are constant in a neighbourhood of ${\Gamma}_{0}$ , thus cancelling in this neighbourhood the singular terms $\psi {\theta}_{k,k}-{\sigma}_{ij}{u}_{i,p}{\theta}_{p,k}$ in $G(\theta )$ .

### 3.2 Choice of the field $\theta$ in dimension 3

#### 3.2.1 Construction method

The field $\theta$ must be constructed so as to satisfy the following conditions:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \theta_n = \theta \cdot n = 0, & \text{sur le bord du domaine } \delta \Omega \text{ (où } n \text{ est la normale à } \delta \Omega \text{)} \\
    \bar{\theta} = \bar{\theta}_0, & \text{donné sur le fond de fissure } \Gamma_0
\end{cases}
$$
where $\bar{\theta}$ represents the trace of $\theta$ on $\Gamma_0$.

Two toric surfaces $T$ and $S$ *(deformed cylinders)* surrounding the crack front $\Gamma_0$ are defined.
In order to satisfy the first condition above, it is crucial that the crown $S$ be strictly included in $\Omega$.

![Construction du champ theta en 3D](/figures/r7.02.01/Object_537.png)

***Figure 3.2.1-a: Construction of the field** $\theta$ **in 3D (overall view)***

**One denotes:**

$R_{\inf}(s)$: **variable radius** of $T$ and
$R_{\sup}(s)$: **variable radius** of $S$

![Construction du champ theta en 3D (plan de coupe)](/figures/r7.02.01/10000724000013390000123147E883C7029B2BF3.svg)

***Figure 3.2.1-b: Construction of the field** $\theta$ **in 3D (cross-sectional plane)***

At every point of $\Gamma_0$, located by its curvilinear abscissa $s$,
a normal plane $P$ is defined, in which the field $\theta$ is introduced according to the following rules:

- Inside the torus $T$:
$$
\theta_n(r(s)) = \bar{\theta}_0(s), \quad \text{pour } 0 \leq r(s) \leq R_{\inf}(s)
$$
- Outside the cylinder $S$:
$$
\theta_n(r(s)) = 0, \quad \text{pour } r(s) \geq R_{\sup}(s)
$$
- In the intermediate crown $S \setminus T$:
$$
\theta_n \text{ varie linéairement en fonction de } r(s)
$$
- Continuity condition:
$$
\theta_n \text{ est continu dans } S(R_{\sup}(s))
$$
This approach is **geometric**:
it relies on the definition of **two radii** $R_{\inf}(s)$ and $R_{\sup}(s)$,
and on the use of **distance calculations** between a current point and the crack front
in order to determine the value of $\theta$ at that point.

#### 3.2.2 Calculation algorithms

The modulus $|\theta|$ of the field $\theta$ is given over the whole crack front $\Gamma_0$.
It may be defined by the user or calculated automatically via the $\theta$ method.

The problem consists in determining the field $\theta$ (norm and direction) at every point of the domain $\Omega$.

One denotes:

- $|\theta|_i$: modulus of the field $\theta$ at node $i$ of $\Gamma_0$
- $R_{\inf}^i$: inner radius for node $i$
- $R_{\sup}^i$: outer radius for node $i$

The procedure for calculating the field $\theta$ at every point of $\Omega$ is as follows:

**Calculation of the field** $\theta$ **at each point of** $\Gamma_0$

The modulus $|\theta|_i$ being given, the direction of $\theta$ must be determined. The geometric conditions to be satisfied are: $\theta$ must lie locally in the plane tangent to the crack lips, $\theta$ must be normal to the edge to which it belongs.

In the general case (non-plane crack front), $\theta$ being calculated at the nodes,
its direction is averaged over the two edges of $\Gamma_0$ which have this node in common.

![Construction du champ theta en 3D (normales)](/figures/r7.02.01/10000FD40000145C00000BC8DDB7F8F76EC771ED.svg)

*Figure 3.2.2-a: Construction of the field $\theta$ in 3D (normals)*

Let $F_1$ and $F_2$ be two faces belonging to the crack lips and containing the successive edges
$T_1$ and $T_2$ of the crack front $\Gamma_0$.

One begins by calculating:

- The normal $n_1$ to the edge $T_1$ in the plane of the face $F_1$
- The normal $n_2$ to the edge $T_2$ in the plane of the face $F_2$

The normals being of unit length, one deduces:
$$
n = \frac{n_1 + n_2}{2}
$$
The field $\theta$ at a point $M \in \Gamma_0$ is then given by:
$$
\theta (M) = |\theta (M)| \cdot n
$$
The faces $F_i$ are assumed to be plane:

- If $F_i$ is a triangle, the plane of the face $F_i$ is defined directly.
- If $F_i$ is a quadrilateral, it is divided into two triangles $F_{i1}$ and $F_{i2}$.
The equations of the two planes containing these faces must then be calculated and two calculations of normals per edge $T_i$ must be carried out.

This calculation requires knowledge of the faces belonging to the crack lips and containing an edge of $\Gamma_0$.
The user must supply all the surface elements belonging to the crack lips.
These faces appear in one or more mesh-cell groups and are described in the connectivities of the surface elements.
The algorithm sorts these faces in order to retain only those having two vertices on $\Gamma_0$.

The steps of the algorithm are:

1. For each node of $\Gamma_0$, extraction of the mesh cells belonging to the crack lips.
2. Filtering of the mesh cells in order to keep only those having two nodes on $\Gamma_0$.
3. Retrieval of the type of the face (triangle or quadrilateral) and calculation of the equation of the tangent plane or planes.
4. For each edge of $\Gamma_0$ with vertices $N_i$ and $N_{i+1}$, calculation of the normals $n_{i,1}$, $n_{i+1,1}$, $n_{i,2}$ and $n_{i+1,2}$.

The field $\theta$ is then calculated according to the following algorithm:
$$
\forall N_i \in \Gamma_0 :
\begin{cases}
    n_i = \frac{1}{2} (n_{i,1} + n_{i,2}) \\
    \theta (N_i) = |\theta|_i \cdot n_i
\end{cases}
$$
End of the loop over the vertices $N_i$ of $\Gamma_0$.

![Notations des normales au fond de fissure](/figures/r7.02.01/100007740000140D000009ECABD6B6B7689DC7E8.svg)

*Figure 3.2.2-b: Notation of the normals to the crack front*

**Calculation of the projection of each point of** $\Omega$ **onto** $\Gamma_0$

For each node $M$:

- Retrieval of the coordinates of $M$
- Loop over the nodes $N_k$ of $\Gamma_0$, with $k=1,...,\mathrm{NNO}-1$:

  - Retrieval of the coordinates of $N_k$ and $N_{k+1}$
  - Calculation of:
$$
s_k = \frac{N_k N_{k+1} \cdot N_k M}{\| N_k N_{k+1} \|}
$$
  - Adjustment of $s_k$:
$$
\begin{cases}
    s_k < 0 \Rightarrow s_k = 0 \\
    s_k > 1 \Rightarrow s_k = 1
\end{cases}
$$
  - Calculation of the coordinates of the projection:
$$
\bar{M}_k = N_k + s_k (N_k N_{k+1})
$$
  - Calculation of the distance:
$$
d_k = d(M, \bar{M}_k)
$$
- End of the loop
- Determination of $i$ such that:
$$
d_i = \min_k (d_k)
$$
- Knowing $i$, one retrieves $N_i$, $N_{i+1}$, $s_i$ and the projection $\bar{M}$ of $M$ onto $\Gamma_0$, such that:
$$
N_i \bar{M} = s_i N_i N_{i+1}
$$
![Calcul de la projection](/figures/r7.02.01/Object_517.svg)

**Calculation of the field** $\theta$ **at each point of** $\Omega$

Loop over the nodes $M$:

1. Calculation of the projection $\bar{M}$ of $M$ onto $\Gamma_0$ (cf. above; this gives $N_i, N_{i+1}, s_i$).
2. Calculation of the distance:
$$
d = d(M, \bar{M})
$$
3. Calculation of $\theta(\bar{M})$ by linear interpolation:
$$
\theta (\bar{M}) = (1 - s_i) \theta (N_i) + s_i \theta (N_{i+1})
$$
4. Calculation of $R_{\inf}(s_i)$ and $R_{\sup}(s_i)$ by linear interpolation:
$$
R_{\inf}(s_i) = (1 - s_i) R_{\inf}^i + s_i R_{\inf}^{i+1}

R_{\sup}(s_i) = (1 - s_i) R_{\sup}^i + s_i R_{\sup}^{i+1}
$$
5. Assignment of $\theta(M)$ according to the value of $d$:
$$
\begin{cases}
    d > R_{\sup}(s_i) \Rightarrow \theta(M) = 0 \\
    d < R_{\inf}(s_i) \Rightarrow \theta(M) = \theta(\bar{M}) \\
    R_{\inf}(s_i) \leq d \leq R_{\sup}(s_i) \Rightarrow
    \alpha = \frac{d - R_{\inf}(s_i)}{R_{\sup}(s_i) - R_{\inf}(s_i)}, \quad
    \theta(M) = (1 - \alpha) \theta(\bar{M})
\end{cases}
$$
- End of the loop

*Figure 3.2.2-d: Calculation of the field $\theta$ in 3D*

### 3.3 Choice of the field $\theta$ in dimension 2

This is a particular case of dimension 3.
The crack front $\Gamma_0$ reduces to a point.
The user chooses:

- the radii $R_{\inf}$ and $R_{\sup}$
- the modulus $|\theta_0|$ at the crack front

The field $\theta$ is constructed so as to satisfy the following conditions:
$$
\begin{cases}
    \theta (r) = 0, \quad \text{si } r \geq R_{\sup} \\
    \theta (r) = |\theta_0| n, \quad \text{si } r \leq R_{\inf} \\
    \theta (r) = \frac{R_{\sup} - r}{R_{\sup} - R_{\inf}} |\theta_0| n, \quad \text{si } R_{\inf} \leq r \leq R_{\sup}
\end{cases}
$$
In order to satisfy the condition $\theta \cdot n = 0$ on $\delta \Omega$,
one must ensure that the crown of radius $R_{\sup}$ is strictly included in $\Omega$.

### 3.4 Other method: field $\theta$ defined as a function of the number of layers

Alternatively, the use of the operands NB_COUCHE_INF and NB_COUCHE_SUP makes it possible to define the integration crowns of the field $\theta$ not in terms of radius, but in terms of the number of layers of elements. The notion of a layer is here defined by the connectivity of the mesh: the first layer of elements consists of all the elements connected to the crack front ${\Gamma}_{0}$ , the second layer of elements consists of all the elements connected to the first layer, and so on.

![Description de l'image](/figures/r7.02.01/10000201000001E70000014F0848ADD14884E51D.png)

*Figure 3.4: Element layers 1 to 3 around the crack front (2D)*

The field $\theta$ being a field at the nodes, a notion of node layer is derived from the notion of element layer:

- The nodes of the crack front are assigned the layer number 0
- The vertex nodes of the elements of the first layer of elements (excluding the nodes of the front) are assigned the layer number 1.
- The vertex nodes of the elements of the second layer of elements (excluding the nodes of the first layer) are assigned the layer number 2.
- And so on, until the layer number NB_COUCHE_SUP is reached. All the nodes outside the crown thus defined are assigned the layer number NB_COUCHE_SUP.
- Any mid-side nodes of edges are assigned a layer number equal to the average of the layer numbers of the vertex nodes of the edge.

Once each node of the mesh has a layer number, the field $\theta$ is calculated by means of the methods presented in section 3.2 (3D) and 3.3 (2D). R_SUP and R_INF are then simply replaced by NB_COUCHE_SUP and NB_COUCHE_INF, and the distance from the node to the crack front is replaced by the layer number of the node.

### 3.5 Other method

The user can specify the field $\theta$ directly by using the command
**CREA_CHAMP** [U4.72.04](doc:u4.72.04).

This command makes it possible to assign the field $\theta$:

- **Node by node**
- **By node group**

This offers maximum flexibility for defining a custom field according to the specific needs of the model.
