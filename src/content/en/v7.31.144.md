# v7.31.144 WTNV144 - Consolidation of a saturated and fractured poro-elastic soil column: use of the XFEM method

**Summary:**

This is a validation test to ensure the correct functioning of the extended elements finite method in the case of the fractured saturated HM coupling model.

The purpose of this validation test is to test the correct consideration of the discontinuity of the pressure field in the massif on both sides of the fracture and the hydromechanical behaviour of the elements HM-XFEM.

The aim is to impose two different loads on both sides of the fracture at the top of a soil column and to observe the evolution of pore pressure in each part. This test is similar to the case of validation wtnl100, with the only difference that we introduce into the Modelling a fracture (interface type) treated by the XFEM approach. The results are then compared to the analytical solution of the validation case wtnl100.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of problem 2D (modelization A and B)

This column has an interface type discontinuity (unmesh interface which is introduced into the model via the "level-sets* via the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator). The bar is completely crossed by discontinuity (at the level of the approximation of the fields of displacement and pore pressure of the massif, only enrichment **Heaviside** is taken into account).

The temperature within the column is uniform regardless of the $t$ moment. The column is also fully saturated by a fluid (for example water) and the effects of gravity forces are not taken into account.

In order to have a one-dimensional solution (according to the $y$ direction of the space marker) the Poisson's ratio is taken null.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions, initial conditions and loads

#### 1.4.1 Boundary conditions case 2D

The movements are blocked on the [AD] and [BC] sides in the horizontal direction, and on the lower [AB] face in both the vertical and horizontal direction. Horizontal movements are blocked on the interface.

The pore pressure at the top of the column is zero whatever the moment, i.e. $p\left(H,t\right)=0.0$ .

#### 1.4.2 Boundary conditions case 3D

The movements are blocked on the face [ABFE]. The movements on (0x) are blocked on the faces [EADH], [FBCG] as well as on the interface. The movements on (Oy) are blocked on the faces [ABCD] and [EFGH].

The pore pressure at the top of the column is zero whatever the moment, i.e. $p\left(H,t\right)=0.0$ .

#### 1.4.3 Loading and initial conditions

In order to start from a different load on either side of the fracture, an initial discontinuity of the pressure field on the column roof is created in the column. Thus, for the left part of the column, the imposed load is ${F}_{G}=-1.0\mathit{Pa}$ and for the right part of the column, the imposed load is ${F}_{D}=-1.54\mathit{Pa}$.

The initial pressure conditions for hydraulic balance are therefore written:

- ${p}^{G}(y,0)=-\frac{{F}_{G}}{b}=1.0\mathit{Pa}$
- ${p}^{D}(y,0)=-\frac{{F}_{D}}{b}=1.54\mathit{Pa}$

#### 1.4.4 Note on modelling used

Given the discontinuity of the pressure field at the top of column [V7.30.100](doc:v7.30.100), it is noted that:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}p(y,0)=-\frac{{F}_{0}}{b}\mathit{si}y<H\\ p(y,0)=0\mathit{si}y=H\end{array}$

This particularity of the solution gives instability in the numerical resolution (appearance of oscillations) of the coupled problem at the top of the column. This is related to the name respect of the LBB [V7.30.100](doc:v7.30.100) condition.

In the classic case, D_PLAN_HMD Modelling is used to achieve this. However, in HM-XFEM only the extension of the D_PLAN_HM Modelling was carried out. The results (for the HM-XFEM case) obtained at the top of the column are therefore to be taken with caution. Indeed, the results obtained with the HM-XFEM model are less accurate at the top of the column, especially for small times. But these results are similar to those obtained with the D_PLAN_HM Modelling in the classic case.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is an analytical solution. This test to validate the discontinuity of the mass pressure, we will focus only on the theoretical resolution of the mass conservation equation:

$\frac{{b}^{2}}{{E}_{0}}\frac{\partial {p}_{\mathit{lq}}(y,t)}{\partial t}-\left(\frac{{K}^{int}.{k}_{\text{lq}}^{\text{rel}}}{{\mu}_{\text{lq}}^{\rbrace }\frac{{\partial}^{2}{p}_{\text{lq}}(y,t)}{\partial {y}^{2}}\right)=0$

Since the differential equation above is homogeneous, with constant coefficients, the resolution method by separable variables is used (see annex 1 for the resolution of this equation).

In view of the initial conditions and the limits referred to in paragraph 1.4., the expression of the pore pressure for the left column is expressed by:

${P}^{G}(y,t)=\frac{-{\mathrm{4F}}_{G}}{\pi b}\sum_{m=1}^{+\infty }\frac{{\left(-1\right)}^{m-1}}{\mathrm{2m}-1}{e}^{-\lambda E{\pi}^{2}{(\mathrm{2m}-1)}^{2}\frac{t}{{\mathrm{4b}}^{2}{H}^{2}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi y(\mathrm{2m}-1)}{\mathrm{2H}}\right)$

and the expression of the pore pressure for the right column is expressed by:

${P}^{D}(y,t)=\frac{-{\mathrm{4F}}_{D}}{\pi b}\sum_{m=1}^{+\infty }\frac{{\left(-1\right)}^{m-1}}{\mathrm{2m}-1}{e}^{-\lambda E{\pi}^{2}{(\mathrm{2m}-1)}^{2}\frac{t}{{\mathrm{4b}}^{2}{H}^{2}}}\cos\left(\frac{\pi y(\mathrm{2m}-1)}{\mathrm{2H}}\right)$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

PRE1 pore pressure and SIYY stress are tested at different heights in the column and at different times.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

None of the solutions are analytical.
