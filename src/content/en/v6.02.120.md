# v6.02.120 SSNL120 - Cyclical Response of 1D Concrete Behaviour Laws

**Summary:**

In this example, the concrete behaviour model of Mazars [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08) in their 1D version is tested using element multifibre beam [R3.08.08](doc:r3.08.08) axially-suspension.

- For Modelling A, the load consists of traction with loading and unloading followed by compression also with loading and unloading. It allows to test tensile and compression resistance, to highlight crack closing phenomena and to test anelastic deformations.
- For model B, the material parameters all depend on temperature. The load is composed of traction with loading and unloading followed by compression also with loading and unloading. The temperature changes during the loading of traction and compression.

## 1 General characteristics

### 1.1 Geometry

Console beam of unit length, square section of unit side.

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.120/10000000000001CB000000A5F5115CC3E2BD5D9D.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus concrete: $E=3,7272{10}^{10}\mathit{Pa}$

Tensile strength of concrete: ${F}_{\mathit{tj}}=4.{10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}$

Concrete compression strength: ${F}_{\mathit{cj}}=40.{10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}$

### 1.3 boundary conditions

clamped support en $A$ : $\mathit{dx}=\mathit{dy}=\mathit{dz}=0$ et $\mathit{drx}=\mathit{dry}=\mathit{drz}=0$ .

### 1.4 Loading

The axial deformation is increased and decreased by imposing a $B$ displacement in the $x$ direction. Two functions are defined, the first involves the material in traction and then in compression, the second involves the material in cyclic loading.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

Longitudinal mesh of beam: 2 nodes and 1 element (POU_D_EM).

The concrete part of the cross-section of the beam is meshed with 4 fibres.

**Note:**

*The problem is* *1D* *, a single fiber may seem sufficient, but this would lead to zero terms in the rigidity matrix (the own inertia of the fibres being not taken into account) and to an error in the resolution of the equations system.*

Concrete is modelled with the Mazars damage model in version 1D [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08). The material parameters used are as follows:

Elasticity part:

$E=3.72720E+10\mathit{Pa}$ , $\mathit{NU}=2.0E-01$

Non-linear part:

$\mathit{AC}=1.71202987E+00$ , $\mathit{BC}=2.01163780E+03$ , $\mathit{BT}=1.21892353E+04$ ,

$\mathit{BETA}=1.10E+00$ , $\mathit{AT}=7.00E-01$ , $\mathit{EPSD0}=8.20396008E-05$ ,

$\mathit{SIGM}\text{\_}\mathit{ELS}=35.0E+06\mathit{Pa}$ , $\mathit{EPSI}\text{\_}\mathit{ELU}=3.5E-03$

This corresponds to a concrete with:

- a peak compression stress of $\mathrm{40.963MPa}$ , corresponding to a peak deformation of $1.75754E-03$ .
- a traction peak stress of $\mathrm{3.05778MPa}$

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The model is identical to Modelling A: same geometry, same boundary condition. The load requires the material in traction and then in compression (loading No. 1 of Modelling A).

Concrete is modelled with the Mazars damage model in version 1D [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08). All the material parameters depend on temperature.

The DEFI_MATER_GC command allows you to determine the parameters required for the constitutive law. The only data required for the command is the constraint: ${F}_{\mathit{cj}}$ .

| **Temperature** | 20°C | 50°C | 80°C | 110°C |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ${F}_{\mathit{cj}}$ | 40MPa | 35MPa | 35MPa | 30MPa |

*Notes:*

*The values of the concrete material characteristics are not physical* s *.* C *e test case is a test case non-regression.*

The temperature-dependent evolutions of the different parameters are built from the DEFI_FONCTION command.
