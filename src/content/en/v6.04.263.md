# v6.04.263 SSNV263 – Non-linear isotropic plasticity VMIS_ISOT_NL under triaxial loading and shearing

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the constitutive law VMIS_ISOT_NL integration algorithm. The problem studied is a hollow cylinder subjected to a triaxial stress state + shearing whose components are constant (in space) in the cylindrical marker.

The problem is implemented:

- AXIS_SI in Modelling A
- in AXIS_GRAD_VARI in Modelling B
- in 3D in Modelling C
- in 3D_GRAD_VARI in Modelling D

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This is a section of a cylinder in the case of modellings with axial symmetry or a quarter of a cylinder (symmetries) in the case of modellings 3D. The cylinder has $H=15\text{mm}$ height, ${R}_{i}=50\text{mm}$ internal radius and ${R}_{e}=100\text{mm}$ external radius.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is subject to the constitutive law elastoplastic isotropic hardening VMIS_ISOT_NL with the following characteristics:

| $E=195000\text{MPa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\nu =0.3$ | Poisson's ratio |
| ${R}_{0}=490\text{MPa}$ | Scoring (constant term) |
| ${R}_{H}=3000\text{MPa}$ | Scoring (linear term) |
| ${R}_{1}=60\text{MPa}$ | Scoring (exponential term 1) |
| ${\gamma}_{1}=8500$ | Scoring (exponential term 1) |
| ${R}_{2}=250\text{MPa}$ | Scoring (exponential term 2) |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

This is a load in force imposed. They are carried out at the edges and in volume according to the formulas of the analytical solution. This load is supplemented by a minimum boundary condition to block the movement of vertical rigid bodies (a vertical point is blocked from the circumference at the intersection of the lower face and the inner wall), as well as the symmetry conditions in the modellings 3D.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Natural initial conditions (zero internal variables, zero deformations).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

An axial symmetry stress state is being applied, with ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}$ , ${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }$ , ${\sigma}_{zz}$ and ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}$ components not dependent on $(r,z)$ . The ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}={\sigma}_{\theta \theta }$ equilibrium equations are also limited to the ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}={\sigma}_{\theta \theta }$ position . The equilibrium equations in the volume are verified for as long as a vertical volume strength is imposed:

${f}_{z}=-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}}{r}$

The equilibrium equations at the edges of the cylinder require to impose $T$ overfacial forces equal to $\sigma \cdot n$ where $n$ designates the normal exit to the surface. They write:

  - $z=H$ top edge: $T={\sigma}_{zz}{e}_{z}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{r}$
  - $z=0$ lower edge: $T=-{\sigma}_{zz}{e}_{z}-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{r}$
  - $r={R}_{i}$ inner wall: $T=-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}{e}_{r}-{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{z}$
  - $r={R}_{e}$ exterior wall: $T={\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}{e}_{r}+{\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}{e}_{z}$

The stress field is then at balance with the load imposed.

Considering the shape of the behavior equations, including their isotropy, the deformation field components in the cylindrical marker are also constant (in space) and are limited to ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}={\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }$ , ${\epsilon}_{zz}$ and ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}$ . This deformation field is geometrically compatible with the following displacement field (axial symmetry):

${u}_{r}={\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}r;{u}_{z}={\epsilon}_{zz}z+2{\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}(r-{R}_{i})$

where the integration constant has been fixed so that ${u}_{z}({R}_{i},0)=0$ , boundary condition which blocks the rigid body movement.

The path of the transient is now to be fixed in the stress space. To simplify the integration of the behavior, a monotonous radial path is chosen. The stress is thus imposed in the form $\sigma =q{\Sigma}^{0}$ , where $q\ge 0$ is the intensity of the stresses. The following components are chosen for ${\Sigma}^{0}$ in the cylindrical marker:

${\Sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{0}={\Sigma}_{\theta \theta }^{0}=\frac{1}{6};{\Sigma}_{zz}^{0}=\frac{2}{3};{\Sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}^{0}=\frac{1}{2}$

It is a tensor with an equivalent stress of vonMises equal to 1, so that ${\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}=q$ . One notes ${N}^{0}$ its deviator. The equation of evolution of plastic deformation easily integrates since the direction is fixed and equal to ${N}^{0}$ :

${\epsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\kappa {N}^{0}$

The $\kappa$ hardening variable depends (plasticly loaded) only on the equivalent stress $q$ , via the consistency equation:

$q=R(\kappa )={R}_{0}+{R}_{H}\kappa +{R}_{1}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{1}\kappa })+{R}_{2}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{2}\kappa })+{R}_{K}{(\kappa +{p}_{0})}^{{\gamma}_{M}}$

It is obviously easier to set a target value for $\kappa$ (5%, for example) and deduce the required $q$ load (830.364 MPa).

Finally, the elastic deformation is directly deduced from the stress:

${\epsilon}^{e}=q\left[\frac{1-\nu }{E}{\Sigma}^{0}-\frac{\nu}{E}\mathit{tr}{\Sigma}^{0}\mathit{Id}\right]$

It allows to trace $\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{p}$ deformation and displacement.

In the case of a plastic gradient formulation, the results remain unchanged, as the $\kappa$ nutrification variable is homogeneous, so that its gradient is zero: non-local effects do not appear in this problem (to the errors of spatial discretization near).

Finally, the history of the point movement of the structure can also be applied to the stress response. This approach is used to test the case with the Lüders plate in the nutwork (a stress approach is not possible because of the perfect plastic character of the plate). The stresses are unchanged beyond the plate and blocked at the equivalent stress of the plate for deformations below the plateau limit.

### 2.2 Baseline

It will be ensured that by targeting a cumulative plastic deformation of 5%, the model is well restored to the expected stress levels (${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}$), displacements and plastic deformations (${\epsilon}_{\mathit{rz}}^{p}$).
