# u2.02.01 User's guide for the plate, shell, grid and membrane elements

> **Summary.**

This document is a user's guide for the plate, shell, grid and membrane modellings.

Shell and plate elements are used in the numerical modelling of thin structures with a middle surface that is plane (plate modelling) or curved (shell modelling). Grid modellings are used for the numerical modelling of reinforcement and prestressing cables in reinforced-concrete structures. Membrane modelling makes it possible to model thin structures whose bending stiffness is negligible.

**Mechanics**:

The DKT models are the most complete, because, for thin or slightly thick structures ($h/L<1/20$ ), they are able to represent a wide range of linear or non-linear mechanical phenomena while remaining efficient. For large-transformation problems with a follower pressure and with no contact taken into account, it is better to prefer the COQUE_3D models. If, in addition to the shell aspect and the non-linear phenomena (contact, follower pressure), one is not interested in the bending part (an inflatable balloon, for example), it is better to use the MEMBRANE models in large transformations. Where the structure is thick and the transverse shear and the pinching need to be treated properly, it is better to prefer the DST models in linear mechanics and to prefer the 3D model in non-linear analysis. Finally, for a geometry of revolution and with a loading that remains of revolution, it is better to prefer COQUE_AXIS, in linear analysis as in non-linear analysis.

**Thermal**:

There are three shell models in code_aster capable of treating thermal analysis: COQUE (surface), COQUE_PLAN and COQUE_AXIS (1D).

## 1 Introduction

Shell and plate elements are used in particular to model thin structures in which the ratios between the dimensions (thickness/characteristic length) are much smaller than $1/10$ (thin shells) or of the order of $1/10$ (thick shells). For very thin shells, it is recommended not to exceed a ratio of $1/500$, in order to avoid numerical locking problems which underestimate the displacements and therefore give incorrect results.

These modellings can be used in linear and non-linear mechanics, under the assumptions of small strains and small displacements, or under the assumptions of large displacements and large rotations, depending on the modellings. A thin-shell modelling is also available in linear transient thermal analysis.

Two categories of thin-structure elements are described in this document:

- The **plate** elements, which are plane. The curvature of the structure to be represented is not taken into account, and it is therefore necessary to use a large number of elements in order to approximate the geometry of the structure correctly (facet aspect).
- The **shell** elements, which are curved.

The triangular and quadrangular plane plate elements are described in the reference documentation [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03)); they comprise the following modellings:

- DKT: TRIA3 mesh cell, DKT element; QUAD4 mesh cell, DKQ elements (geometrically linear);
- DKTG: TRIA3 mesh cell, DKT element; QUAD4 mesh cell, DKQ elements (geometrically linear or non-linear);
- DST: TRIA3 mesh cell, DST element; QUAD4 mesh cell, DSQ element (geometrically linear);
- Q4G: QUAD4 mesh cell, Q4G element (geometrically linear).
- Q4GG: TRIA3 mesh cell, T3G element; QUAD4 mesh cell, Q4G elements (geometrically linear)

The curved shell elements derived from 3D models with a shell kinematics are grouped under the following modellings:

- COQUE_3D: TRIA7 and QUAD9 mesh cells, 3D structure of arbitrary geometry ([R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04) in geometrically linear analysis, [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05) in geometrically non-linear analysis and [R3.03.07](doc:r3.03.07) with follower pressures);
- COQUE_AXIS: SEG3 mesh cell, shells with symmetry of revolution about the $\mathrm{0Y}$ axis ([R3.07.02](doc:r3.07.02) in geometrically linear analysis);

The classical membrane elements are grouped under the MEMBRANE modelling and may have as geometric support the TRIA3, QUAD4, TRIA6, QUAD8, TRIA7 and QUAD9 mesh cells. They are membrane elements with a membrane stiffness only (no rotational degree of freedom). There is no offset. The membrane models can also be used in large transformations.

There are specific plate elements to represent the reinforcement layers:

- GRILLE_EXCENTRE: TRIA3 or QUAD4 mesh cells (geometrically linear). This modelling corresponds to orthotropic DKT plate elements with 3 or 4 nodes, offset relative to the concrete mid-surface (a single reinforcement direction). The concrete may be modelled by 3-node DKT or DST plate elements. The reinforced-concrete structure is then represented by the superposition of the GRILLE_EXCENTRE modellings and of that used for the concrete (DKT or DST).
- GRILLE_MEMBRANE: TRIA3, QUAD4, TRIA6 or QUAD8 mesh cells (document [R3.08.07](doc:r3.08.07)): these are reinforcement elements (a single reinforcement direction) working in membrane only (no rotational degree of freedom). There is no offset.

## 2 Mechanics

### 2.1 Modelling capabilities

#### 2.1.1 Recall of the formulations

##### 2.1.1.1 Geometry of the plate and shell elements

For the plate and shell elements one defines a reference surface, or middle surface, plane (the $xy$ plane, for example) or curved ($x$  and $y$ define a set of curvilinear coordinates) and a thickness $h(x,y)$ . This thickness must be small compared with the other dimensions of the structure to be modelled. The figure below illustrates these different configurations.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.01/1000000000000398000002CE701615A87E47C684.png)

Figure 2.1.1.1-a: Assumption in the theory of plates and shells

A local frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$, different from the global frame $\mathit{OXYZ}$, is attached to the middle surface $\omega$. The position of the points of the plate or of the shell is given by the curvilinear coordinates $({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2})$ of the middle surface and the elevation ${\xi}_{3}$ relative to this surface. For plates, the curvilinear coordinate system is a local Cartesian coordinate system.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.01/1000000000000286000001221105B84ECF23D991.png)

Figure 2.1.1.1-b: Definition of a middle surface

To represent shells with symmetry of revolution about an axis (COQUE_AXIS), knowledge of the section of revolution, or of the trace of the middle surface, is sufficient, as [Figure 2.1.2.1-a] shows. These shells are supported on a line mesh and, at a point $m$ of the middle surface, a local frame $(n,t,{e}_{z})$ is defined by:

$t=\frac{{\mathrm{Om}}_{,s}}{∥{\mathrm{Om}}_{,s}∥}$ , $n\mathrm{\wedge }t={e}_{z}$

When one wishes to model a solid of arbitrary shape (non-plane), shell elements may be used to account for the curvature, or else plate elements. In the latter case, the geometry is approximated by a network of facets.

#### 2.1.2 Formulation of the plate, shell and volumetric shell elements

##### 2.1.2.1 Geometrically linear formulation

In this formulation, the displacements are assumed to be small, so the initial geometry and the deformed geometry may be superimposed. These elements are based on shell theory, according to which:

- the straight sections, which are the sections perpendicular to the reference surface, remain straight; the material points located on a normal to the undeformed middle surface remain on a straight line in the deformed configuration. It follows from this approach that **the displacement fields vary linearly through the thickness of the plate or of the shell**. If $u$, $v$ and $w$ denote the displacements of a point $q(x,y,z)$ along $x$, $y$ and $z$, one thus has:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u(x,y,z)\\ v(x,y,z)\\ w(x,y,z)\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u(x,y)\\ v(x,y)\\ w(x,y)\end{array}\right\rbrace +z\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\beta}_{x}(x,y)\\ {\beta}_{y}(x,y)\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The associated strain tensor is then written: $\varepsilon (x,y,z)=e(x,y)+\gamma (x,y)+\mathit{z.}\chi (x,y)$ .

The first term $e$ comprises the membrane strains (for a plate element these are the strains in the plane of the element), the second $\gamma$ the transverse shear strains, and the third $z.\chi$ the bending strains, associated with the curvature tensor $\chi$. For thick plates or shells, the transverse shears $\gamma$ are taken into account according to the formulation proposed by Reissner, Hencky, Bollé and Mindlin. This formulation encompasses the approach without transverse shear, in which the tensor $\gamma$ is zero, developed by Kirchhoff for thin plates or shells, according to which the material points located on a normal $n$ to the undeformed middle surface remain on the normal to the deformed surface.

- **The transverse stress** ${\sigma}_{zz}$ **is zero**, because it is considered negligible compared with the other components of the stress tensor (plane-stress assumption).
- One does not describe the variation of the thickness, nor that of the transverse strain ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$, which can be computed using the preceding plane-stress assumption.
- Taking the transverse shear into account depends on correction factors determined a priori by energy equivalences with 3D models, so that the transverse shear stiffness of the plate model is as close as possible to that defined by the theory of three-dimensional elasticity. For homogeneous plates, the transverse shear correction factor based on this method is $k=5/6$ .
- The shear stresses of the plate elements (DKT/Q, DST/Q, Q/T3G) take account of the possible offset of the elements (free-edge assumption in particular, and a quadratic distribution through the thickness). However, for two identical plates from the material point of view, bonded one on the other with an offset of their half-thicknesses, the assumption used to compute the shear stresses does not make it possible to compare the shear solution with a model made up of a single plate. Indeed, in the case of the single plate, one obtains a parabola through the thickness that vanishes at the ends; in the case of the two plates, one will have two parabolas, vanishing at the ends of each plate. To compare two bonded plates with a single plate, recourse to DEFI_COMPOSITE will be necessary.

**Note:**

*The determination of the correction factors is based, for Mindlin, on equivalences of the natural frequency associated with the transverse-shear vibration mode. One then obtains* $k={\pi}^{2}/12$ *, a value very close to* $5/6$ *.*

These elements involve locally:

- Five kinematic variables for arbitrary plate and shell elements; the membrane displacements $u$ and $v$ in the reference plane $z=0$, the transverse displacement $w$ and the rotations ${\beta}_{x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}$ of the normal to the middle surface in the planes $yz$ and $xz$ respectively.
- Three kinematic variables for the line elements; the displacements $u$ and $v$ in the reference plane $z=0$ and the rotation ${\beta}_{n}$ of the normal to the middle surface in the plane $xy$ .

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.01/10000000000004900000024516D39EBCF2B862A9.png)

Figure 2.1.2.1-a: Kinematic variables for the various plate and shell elements

- Three membrane stress resultants denoted $\mathit{Nxx},\mathit{Nyy},\mathit{Nxy}$ and three bending moments denoted $\mathit{Mxx},\mathit{Myy},\mathit{Mxy}$, whatever the plate or shell element; two shear forces denoted $\mathit{Vx}$ and $\mathit{Vy}$ in the case of arbitrary plate and shell elements.

![Figure](/figures/u2.02.01/100000000000045000000108EB8EC81BD9B92B26.png)

Figure 2.1.2.1-b: Stress resultants for a plate or shell element

##### 2.1.2.2 Geometrically non-linear formulation, Euler buckling

In the **geometrically non-linear** formulation, one is in the presence of large displacements and large rotations; the initial geometry and the deformed geometry cannot be superimposed.

The formulation, described in the reference documentation [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05), is based on a 3D continuum approach, degenerated by the introduction of a Hencky‑Mindlin‑Naghdi shell kinematics in plane stress into the weak formulation of equilibrium. The strain measure retained is the Green-Lagrange measure, energetically conjugate to the second Piola-Kirchhoff stresses. The equilibrium formulation is therefore a total Lagrangian formulation. The transverse shear is treated in the same way as in the linear case [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04).

The element retained in non-linear analysis is a volumetric shell element (COQUE_3D) with a curved middle surface, as presented in the previous paragraph, whose support mesh cells are QUAD9 and TRIA7.

It is possible to apply follower pressures on these elements; their formulation is described in the reference document [R3.03.07](doc:r3.03.07). This loading has the particular feature of following the geometry of the structure during its deformation (for example, the hydrostatic pressure always remains perpendicular to the deformed geometry).

Linear buckling, also called **Euler buckling**, described in the reference documentations [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05) and [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03), appears as a particular case of the geometrically non-linear problem. It is based on a linear dependence of the displacement, strain and stress fields with respect to the load level.

The elements retained in linear buckling are:

- the plate element (DKT) with a plane middle surface, as presented in the previous paragraph, whose support mesh cells are QUAD4 and TRIA3.
- the volumetric shell element (COQUE_3D) with a curved middle surface, as presented in the previous paragraph, whose support mesh cells are QUAD9 and TRIA7.

#### 2.1.3 Comparison between the elements

##### 2.1.3.1 The differences between the plate and shell elements

Shell elements are curved elements, whereas plate elements are plane. The variation of the metric of the geometry (that is, its radius of curvature) as a function of its thickness is taken into account for the shell elements, but not for the plate elements. This variation of the metric implies a coupling between the membrane and bending effects for non-plane structures, which cannot be observed with plane plate elements for a homogeneous material (see [bib1]).

The choice of the shape functions for the discretisation of these elements is different, because the curved shell elements have a larger number of degrees of freedom. Thus, the plate elements are linear elements in membrane, whereas the shell elements are quadratic.

##### 2.1.3.2 The differences between the plate elements

One distinguishes the elements with transverse shear (DST, DSQ and Q4G) from the elements without transverse shear (DKT and DKQ). The DST and DKT elements have triangular support mesh cells with 3 nodes ($3\times 5=15$  degrees of freedom) and the DKQ, DSQ and Q4G elements have quadrangular support mesh cells with 4 nodes ($4\times 5=20$  degrees of freedom).

**Important note**:

*For the 4-node plate elements (DSQ,* *DKQ and* *Q4G), the 4 nodes must be coplanar in order for plate theory to be valid. This check is* *carried out systematically by Code_Aster, and the user is warned if one of the elements of the mesh does not satisfy this condition.*

In the case of the elements with transverse shear, in order to avoid locking of the elements in transverse shear (overestimation of the stiffness for very small thicknesses), one method consists in building substitute shear fields that are constant on the edges of the element, whose value is the integral of the shear on the edge in question. In *Code_Aster*, the plate and shell elements with transverse shear use this method so as not to lock in transverse shear. This shear locking comes from the fact that the elastic shear energy is a term proportional to $h$ ($h$  being the thickness of the plate or of the shell), much larger than the elastic bending energy term, which is proportional to ${h}^{3}$ . When the thickness becomes small compared with the characteristic length (the ratio $h/L$ is less than $1/20$ ), for certain shape functions, the minimisation of the predominant term in $h$ leads to a poor representation of the pure bending modes, for which the deflection is no longer computed correctly (see [bib1] page 295 with $h/L=0.01$ ).

The Q4G element is a four-node quadrilateral element without transverse shear locking, with bilinear shape functions in $x$ and $y$ to represent $w$, ${\beta}_{x}$ and ${\beta}_{y}$ .

The main difference between the Q4G modelling and DST comes from the fact that, for the latter, shape functions including a quadratic term are used to discretise the rotations ${\beta}_{s}$ where $s=(x,y)$ .

The consequence for the Q4G element is a piecewise constant approximation of the curvatures, which implies meshing sufficiently finely in the directions loaded in bending.

**Note on the** **DST** **element**:

*Problems have been identified on the triangular* *DST* *element in the case of bending loading. The results on an equivalent mesh are markedly poorer than those of the* *DSQ* *and depend strongly on the nature of the mesh (see test case ssls141, modellings E and F, V3.03.141). Users are advised:*

- *to prefer the* *DSQ element to the* *DST element*
- *on the* *DST* *elements, to prefer free meshes to mapped meshes (better results)*
- *to avoid meshes that are unsymmetric with respect to the loading (if symmetries exist)*

**Note on the** **PLAQ_MITC** **element**:
The PLAQ_MITC element was implemented from a Fenics library. It is a plate element with no membrane effect taken into account, based on a Reissner-Mindlin theory.

##### 2.1.3.3 The differences between the shell elements

One distinguishes the axisymmetric shell elements COQUE_AXIS from the COQUE_3D elements.

The former are used to model structures of revolution of axis $\mathit{Oy}$, and the latter in all the other cases. For these shell elements, the support mesh cells are line mesh cells with 3 nodes. The number of degrees of freedom of these elements is 9.

The arbitrary shell elements COQUE_3D have triangular support mesh cells with 7 nodes or quadrangular support mesh cells with 9 nodes:

- In the case of triangular mesh cells, the number of degrees of freedom for the translations is 6 (the unknowns are the displacements at the vertex nodes and at the midpoints of the sides of the triangle) and that of the rotations is 7 (the unknowns are the 3 rotations at the preceding points and at the centre of the triangle). The total number of degrees of freedom of the element is therefore $\mathit{Nddle}=3\times 6+3\times 7=39$ .
- In the case of 9-node quadrangular mesh cells, the number of degrees of freedom for the translations is 8 (the unknowns are the displacements at the vertex nodes and at the midpoints of the sides of the quadrangle) and that of the rotations is 9 (the unknowns are the 3 rotations at the preceding points and at the centre of the quadrangle). The total number of degrees of freedom of the element is therefore $\mathit{Nddle}=3\times 8+3\times 9=51$ . These elements therefore have roughly twice as many degrees of freedom as the corresponding plate elements of the DKT family. Their cost in time, for an equal number, in an analysis will therefore be higher.

The COQUE_3D elements automatically take into account the metric correction between the middle surface and the upper and lower surfaces. For the line elements, this correction must be activated by the user (see paragraph  13 ). The metric correction contributes a term in $h/L$ to the stress and a term in ${(h/L)}^{2}$ to the displacement (see [V7.90.03](doc:v7.90.03)). For plates this correction is irrelevant.

For the shell elements, the shear correction coefficient $k$ in isotropic constitutive behaviour can be modified by the user. This shear correction coefficient is given in AFFE_CARA_ELEM under the keyword A_CIS. By default, if the user specifies nothing in AFFE_CARA_ELEM, this amounts to using the REISSNER theory with shear; the shear coefficient is then set to $k=5/6$ . If the shear coefficient $k$ is equal to 1, one is within the HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI theory, and if it becomes very large ($k>{10}^{6.}h/L$ ) one approaches the LOVE-KIRCHHOFF theory.

In practice it is recommended not to change this coefficient. Indeed, these elements provide a physically correct solution, whether the shell is thick or thin, with the coefficient $k=5/6$ .

All the shell elements present in *Code_Aster*  are protected against shear locking, like the plate elements (see § 2.1.3.2 ). Selective integration is used for this purpose [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03).

However, this property is lost if a displacement field is projected onto a shell element from an analysis on a plate element (with the PROJ_CHAMP command). One should not be surprised by the shear stresses subsequently obtained by a linear analysis from the displacements (for small thicknesses).

The characteristics that can be assigned on the plate or shell elements are:

- The thickness EPAIS, constant on each mesh cell, since the mesh represents only the mid-surface.
- The transverse shear correction coefficient A_CIS for isotropic curved shells.
- Taking into account the metric correction MODI_METRIQUE between the middle surface and the upper and lower surfaces (effective only for COQUE_AXIS).
- A reference direction making it possible to define a local frame in the tangent plane at every point of a shell. The local frame is built either using the two "nautical" angles $\alpha$ and $\beta$ (supplied in degrees), which define a vector $v$ whose projection onto the plane tangent to the shell fixes the direction ${x}_{l}$ . or, if the VECTEUR keyword is present, by the 3 components of the vector $v$ One may define a single vector $V$ for the whole structure, or one per zone (keywords GROUP_MA / MAILLE). The construction of the local frame is defined in AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01). This local frame will be called the **user frame** hereafter.

The definition of this reference axis is also useful for defining the orientation of the fibres of a multilayer or orthotropic shell (see the DEFI_COMPOSITE operator [U4.42.03](doc:u4.42.03)).

- The number of layers COQUE_NCOU used for the integration through the thickness of the shell, in the STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE operators (DKT, COQUE_3D, COQUE_AXIS modellings).
- A feature of DEFI_GROUP makes it possible to create automatically a mesh-cell group whose normal lies within a given solid angle, of axis the reference direction.

This command can be used in pre-processing to assign non-isotropic material data, or in post-processing after a shell analysis.

- The offset (constant for all the nodes of the mesh cell) EXCENTREMENT of each of them relative to the support mesh cell. This distance is measured along the normal to the support mesh cell. In the offset case, the rotary inertias are necessarily taken into account and INER_ROTA is set to OUI.
- COEF_RIGI_DRZ defines a fictitious stiffness coefficient (necessarily small) on the degree of freedom of rotation about the normal to the shell. ##### 2.2.4.5 Shell connections with other mechanical elements

These connections must satisfy the requirements established in [bib4], which are found in particular in the 3D-beam connection in [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03).

The connections available with the plate and shell elements are the following:

- **Beam–shell** connection: this consists in establishing the link between an end node of a beam element and a group of edge mesh cells of shell elements. Beam and plate theories only know of cuts normal to the fibre or to the middle surface. The connections can take place only along these fibres or middle surfaces. The beam–shell connection is **feasible for beams whose neutral fibre is orthogonal to the normals to the facets of the plates or of the shells.**  Extending it to other configurations (a beam arriving perpendicular to the plane of a plate, for example) requires a feasibility study, because the plate or shell elements have no stiffness associated with a rotation in the plane perpendicular to the normal to the middle surface. The connection is usable by using the keyword LIAISON_ELEM: (OPTION: ‘COQ_POU’) of AFFE_CHAR_MECA.
- **Shell–pipe** connection: this consists in establishing the link between an end node of a pipe element and a group of edge mesh cells of shell elements. The formulation of the shell–pipe connection is presented in the reference document [R3.08.06](doc:r3.08.06). Pipe and plate theories only know of cuts normal to the fibre or to the middle surface. The connections can take place only along these fibres or middle surfaces. The shell–pipe connection is feasible for pipes whose neutral fibre is orthogonal to the normals to the facets of the plates or of the shells. The connection is usable by using the keyword LIAISON_ELEM: (OPTION: ‘COQ_TUYAU’) of AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

- **Shell – solid 3D** connection: the **shell–3D** solid connection is under study, but it will be limited at first to the cases where the normal to the solid is orthogonal to the normal to one of the facets of the plate or shell element (see [bib4]).
- **Connection between shell elements**: to connect two shell elements to one another, one uses the LIAISON_COQUE keyword of AFFE_CHAR_MECA(_F) (documentation [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01)). The 3D constitutive behaviours can also be used by means of the De Borst method [R5.03.09](doc:r5.03.09).

When plate-type or shell elements become coplanar, the stiffness problem about the normal should be kept under control. Indeed, by construction, these elements have no stiffness in this direction: DRZ is a **fictitious degree of freedom**  which avoids the singularity of the DKT matrices in the global frame.

The COEF_RIGI_DRZ coefficient in the AFFE_CARA_ELEM operator makes it possible to modify this value. The default value is sufficient in most cases, but, in certain situations such as:

- thin plate elements used with a large thickness (DKT, for example);
- a non-linear analysis (STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE);
- DKT-type elements on warped quadrangles in general;
- COQUE_3D with large-transformation kinematics;
- a plate subjected to a rotation about its normal.

Advice for the DKT on COEF_RIGI_DRZ: a poor value penalises the convergence of the analyses. The result, if it has converged, remains **correct**, but the rate of convergence is reduced. For example, a linear elastic analysis requires more than one Newton iteration to converge. The value of COEF_RIGI_DRZ must then be changed and reduced. Nevertheless, care is needed in the choice of this coefficient, because a value that is too small can make the matrix singular. It is also possible to enrich the DKT models by taking the stiffness along DRZ into account. To do this, it is enough to take COEF_RIGI_DRZ negative. In this latter case, one notes that one does not quite have a DKT model as it is formulated in the literature, but a DKT model enriched by a kinematics in DRZ: DRZ becomes a **physical** degree of freedom.

Advice for COQUE_3D: conversely, for COQUE_3D, it is better to favour an ever larger COEF_RIGI_DRZ in order to avoid singular matrices.

The RESULTAT concept of STAT_NON_LINE/DYNA_NON_LINE contains displacement, stress and internal-variable fields at the integration points, always computed at the Gauss points:

- DEPL: displacement fields.
- SIEF_ELGA: stress tensor per element at the integration points (COQUE_3D and DKT) in the user frame. For each layer, one stores through the thickness, and for each thickness at the surface integration points. Thus, if one wants information on a stress for layer NC, at level NCN (NCN = -1 if lower, NCN = 0 if middle, NCN = +1 if upper) for the surface integration point NG, one must look at the value given by the point defined in the POINT option such that: NP = 3*(NC-1)*NPG+(NCN+1)*NPG+NG where NPG is the total number of surface integration points of the COQUE_3D element (7 for the triangle and 9 for the quadrangle) and of the DKT element. For the GRILLE_EXCENTRE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE modellings, one simply stores one value per integration point: the SIXX component in the direction of the reinforcement. For the DKTG and Q4GG modellings, SIEF_ELGA contains the 6 generalised forces (membrane forces, bending moments, shear forces) per Gauss point. In the general case, SIEF_ELGA is a Cauchy stress field, but for COQUE_3D it is a stress field of the second Piola-Kirchoff type.
- VARI_ELGA: field of internal variables (DKT and COQUE_3D) per element at the surface integration points. For each surface integration point, the information on the layers is stored starting with the first, level ‘INF’. The number of variables represented is therefore 2*NCOU*NBVARI, where NBVARI represents the number of internal variables.

It can be enriched with the following fields, computed in post-processing by the CALC_CHAMP operator:

- EFGE_ELNO: activates the computation of the tensor of generalised forces per element at the nodes (membrane forces, bending moments, shear forces), in the user frame (defined in paragraph [§2.2.2]).
- VARI_ELNO: activates the computation of the internal-variable field per element at the nodes through the thickness (by layer SUP/MOY/INF through the thickness, unless otherwise stated).

##### 2.3.2.2 Detail on the integration points

The numbering of the surface Gauss points for the COQUE_3D elements is given in R3.07.04 §4.7.1. Take care with the order of the Gauss points for the 9-point formula, which is not the same as that adopted for the isoparametric elements.

For the DKT, COQUE_3D and COQUE_AXIS modellings, in the case of non-linear analyses, the integration method for the plate and shell elements is an integration method by **layers**, the number of which is defined by the user. For each layer, except for the GRILLE modelling, a three-point Simpson method is used, at the middle of the layer and on the upper and lower skins of the layer. For $N$ layers, the number of integration points through the thickness is $\mathrm{2N}+1$ .

To treat the material non-linearities, it is recommended to use 3 to 5 layers through the thickness, for a number of integration points equal to 7, 9 and 11 respectively. For the tangent stiffness, one computes for each layer, in plane stress, the contribution to the membrane, bending and membrane–bending coupling stiffness matrices. These contributions are added and assembled to obtain the total tangent stiffness matrix. For each layer, one computes the stress state and the set of internal variables, at the middle of the layer and on the upper and lower skins of the layer. This information is available in VARI_ELGA and SIEF_ELGA. In post-processing one can access the transverse shear stress and strain values obtained from the derivative of the moments. To do this, even in non-linear analysis, an elastic relation is assumed for the transverse constitutive behaviour.

For the GRILLE_EXCENTRE and GRILLE_MEMBRANE modellings of reinforced-concrete structures, there is only one integration point per layer.

##### 2.3.2.3 Geometric non-linear behaviour

The geometrically non-linear analyses (large displacements and large rotations), available with the COQUE_3D modelling, are carried out using the STAT_NON_LINE operator, using, under the COMPORTEMENT keyword, DEFORMATION = 'GROT_GDEP'.

The geometrically non-linear analyses (large displacements and large rotations), available with the MEMBRANE modelling, are carried out using the STAT_NON_LINE operator, using, under the COMPORTEMENT keyword, DEFORMATION = 'GROT_GDEP'.

The geometrically non-linear analyses (large displacements and small strains), available with the exce modelling, are carried out using the STAT_NON_LINE operator, using, under the COMPORTEMENT keyword, DEFORMATION = 'GROT_GDEP'.

It is possible to apply follower pressures on the COQUE_3D elements. This loading has the particular feature of following the geometry of the structure during its deformation (for example: the hydrostatic pressure always remains perpendicular to the deformed geometry). To take this type of loading into account, the following information must be specified in the STAT_NON_LINE operator:

STAT_NON_LINE (

EXCIT =_F( CHARGE = pres

TYPE_CHARGE = 'SUIV' )

)

The geometric non-linear behaviour of structures may present instabilities (buckling, snap‑through/snap-back...). The determination and the crossing of these limit points cannot be obtained by imposing the loading; however, the load-control options ‘DDL_IMPO’ or ‘LONG_ARC’ of the STAT_NON_LINE operator make it possible to pass these critical points.

The use of the MEMBRANE element in geometrically non-linear analysis can be delicate because of the strong non-linearities and of its lack of bending stiffness. The following points should be borne in mind:

- Use of the pretension: the use of an initial pretension via N_INIT in the AFFE_CARA_ELEM operator [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01) is a particular feature of structural elements with no resistance to bending; it is found in code_aster for the cable elements. Indeed, a membrane subjected to a force perpendicular to its initial surface, as is the case with a pressure, undergoes a rigid-body motion, which causes the analysis to stop. One way of getting round this problem is to create an initial stiffness, called "geometric", in order to allow the convergence of the first increment. It is then normal for the convergence time of this first time step to be rather long, so one should not hesitate to allow a high number of Newton iterations (>100) and to adjust the value of the initial tension N_INIT. The pretension is a generalised force and is written in the form ${N}_{\mathit{init}}={h\sigma }_{0}$ with $h$ the thickness and ${\sigma}_{0}$ the prestress; it is recommended to start by taking a small prestress value (for example $1.E-3$ ) and then to increase this value by multiplying it by 10 until convergence is obtained. It is moreover preferable to take $h$ and as independent parameters and then to enter ${h\sigma }_{0}$ as the argument under the N_INIT keyword. The value ${\sigma}_{0}$ is sometimes difficult to find, but it is useful only for the first time step, so it can be tested quickly.
- Initial force and stiffness: the very strong non-linearity of the element implies that convergence may be poor when the applied force is too small relative to the stiffness of the material. This ratio is difficult to quantify, but the user should not hesitate to set a high force at the first time step, even if this means activating time-step splitting via the DEFI_LIST_INST command. This remark is particularly true when a pressure is applied.
- Line search (and contact): one way of improving convergence noticeably is to activate the line search (keyword RECH_LINEAIRE in the STAT_NON_LINE command [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)). This is sometimes indispensable for the convergence of the analyses, in particular during the first time steps. The line search is unfortunately not compatible with contact problems in code_aster; this may lead one to have to split the problems into two parts: a first part without contact, with the line search, and a second part with the contact only.
- Load control: if, after varying the value of the initial tension and the value of the initial force, one cannot make the analysis converge at the first increment, it is also possible to use load control (keyword PILOTAGE in STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)). Load control is also incompatible with contact.

##### 2.3.2.4 Linear buckling

Linear buckling analyses are similar to the search for natural frequencies and vibration modes. The problem to be solved is expressed in the form:

Find $(\lambda ,X)\in (\mathrm{ℝ},{\mathrm{ℝ}}^{N})$ such that $\mathit{AX}=\lambda \mathit{BX}$

| where | $A$ | is the stiffness matrix |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | $B$ | is the geometric stiffness matrix (computed with the RIGI_GEOM option of CALC_MATR_ELEM), available for the DKT, DKTG and COQUE_3D modellings |
|  | $\lambda$ | is the critical load |
|  | $X$ | is the buckling mode associated with the critical load |

The CALC_MODES operator [U4.52.02] is used to determine the critical load and the associated buckling mode.

The IMPR_RESU operator makes it possible to write the mesh and/or the results of an analysis to the listing in ‘RESULTAT’ format, or to a file in a format that can be viewed by post-processing tools external to Aster: RESULTAT and ASTER format (documentation [U4.91.01](doc:u4.91.01)), CASTEM format (documentation [U7.05.11](doc:u7.05.11)), IDEAS format (documentation [U7.05.01](doc:u7.05.01)), MED format (documentation [U7.05.21](doc:u7.05.21)) or GMSH format (documentation [U7.05.32](doc:u7.05.32)).

## 3 Thermal analysis

For the solution of chained thermo-mechanical problems, thermal shell finite elements [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01) must be used for the thermal analysis. These elements are plate elements, or line elements in the case of structures of revolution or of structures invariant along an axis. The curvature of the structure is not taken into account in itself. The metric of the tangent plane of each element is computed assuming that all the vertices are coplanar. These elements assume an a priori parabolic distribution of temperature through the thickness, which results from an asymptotic expansion in linear thermal analysis for a small shell thickness, when the temperature variations are not too large. It should be noted that a model based on a richer expansion of the temperature field through the thickness sees its terms of order higher than two converge towards zero when the shell is thin. One therefore cannot treat thermal-shock problems with a strong variation of the temperature profile through the thickness with these shells. The instructions for use of these elements are presented in [U1.22.01](doc:u1.22.01).

## 4 Thermo-mechanical chaining

For the solution of chained thermo-mechanical problems, thermal shell finite elements [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01) must be used for the thermal analysis; their temperature field is recovered as input data of *Code_Aster* for the mechanical analysis. There must therefore be compatibility between the thermal field given by the thermal shells and that recovered by the mechanical shells. The latter is defined by the knowledge of the 3 fields TEMP_INF, TEMP_MIL and TEMP_SUP given on the lower skin, the middle and the upper skin of the shell. **Notes:**

- *The nodes of the thermal shell elements and of the mechanical plate or shell elements must correspond. The meshes for the thermal analysis and for the mechanical analysis will therefore have the same number and the same type of mesh cells.*
- *The surface thermal shell elements are treated as plane elements by projection of the initial geometry onto the plane defined by the first 3 vertices. For the chaining of analyses with curved mechanical elements, it is therefore necessary that the geometry of the plate not be too far from that of the shell. When the structure is curved, the thermal analysis must therefore be meshed sufficiently finely in order to have correct results in anticipation of the mechanical part. Only the line thermal elements are perfectly associated with the corresponding line elements in mechanics, because they take into account the curvature of the meshed structure.*
- *Chaining with multilayer materials is not available at present.*
- *Thermo-mechanical chaining is also possible if one knows, analytically or from experimental measurements, the variation of the temperature field through the thickness of the structure or of certain parts of the structure. In this case one works with a temperature map defined a priori; the temperature field is no longer given by the three values* *TEMP_INF,* *TEMP_MIL and* *TEMP_SUP of the thermal analysis obtained by* *EVOL_THER. The* *DEFI_NAPPE operator makes it possible to create such temperature profiles from the data supplied by the user. These profiles are assigned by the* *CREA_CHAMP and* *CREA_RESU commands (see test case* *hsns100b). It will be noted that it is not necessary, for the mechanical analysis, that the number of integration points through the thickness be equal to the number of discretisation points of the temperature field through the thickness. The temperature field is automatically interpolated at the integration points through the thickness of the plate or shell elements.*
- The thermal evolution that can be associated with the material field by *AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC*  must be ready to be used by the finite elements of the mechanical model. A problem arises for shell-type or pipe elements, which use a temperature varying through the thickness on the different layers. For these elements, it is necessary to prepare the computation of the temperature on the layers upstream of the *AFFE_MATERIAU* command. To do this, the user must use the *CREA_RESU* command with the *PREP_VARC* operation (PREparation of the Command VaRiables") which makes it possible to compute the temperature in the layers of a shell, starting either from a temperature defined by a map of functions or from a temperature computed by *Code_Aster* with a shell model *(TEMP_MIL/TEMP_INF/TEMP_SUP)* .

A few recommendations concerning the field of use of these elements:

- *Thin structures* **:** for these structures, whose h/L ratio is less than 1/20, the transverse shear effects can be neglected and Kirchhoff theory applies. It is recommended to use, for this type of structure, DKT-DKQ plate elements or curved shell elements (COQUE_3D, _AXIS). **It is recommended to use preferably the** **DKT and DKQ** **elements**, which give very good results on the displacements and more approximate results on the stresses (to be recommended for vibration analyses). Even if a large number of these elements must be used, the execution times remain reasonable compared with those of the curved elements. It is recommended not to exceed a ratio h/L=1/500 in order to avoid problems linked to numerical locking.
- *Thick structures:*  for these structures, one will use DST, DSQ and Q4G plate elements, which take transverse shear into account with a shear correction factor $k=5/6$ (Reissner theory), or **preferably curved shell elements**. The shear correction factor makes it possible to pass from a Hencky-Mindlin-Naghdi theory for $k=1$ , to a Reissner theory for $k=5/6$ . The shear coefficient is adjustable only for the curved shell elements, but it is recommended not to modify its default value.

**When the Q4G modelling has been preferred, a small mesh-sensitivity study must be carried out**. The tests show, indeed, that this modelling requires a mesh that is sufficiently fine in the directions loaded in bending in order to obtain small errors.

The DKT, DKQ, DST, DSQ and Q4G elements are plane elements; they do not take the curvature of the structures into account, so it is necessary to refine the mesh where the curvature is significant if one wants to avoid spurious bending.

The variation of the metric of the geometry (that is, its radius of curvature) as a function of its thickness is taken into account:

- automatically for the COQUE_3D modelling.
- defined by the user for the COQUE_AXIS modelling.

The optimal mechanical element in statics, according to all the test cases of paragraph [§2.5], is the 9-node shell element **MEC3QU9H**, which makes it possible to obtain good displacements and good stresses thanks to its P2 interpolation in membrane. It is a versatile element which can be used both to represent very thin structures ($h/L\le 1/100$ ) and thicker ones. As, moreover, **the 7-node shell element** **MEC3TR7H is less efficient**, the user is **advised to mesh the shell structure with as many quadrangles as possible**.

- *Material non-linearity:*  the non-linear constitutive behaviours (plasticity, etc.) in plane stress are available for the curved shell elements (COQUE_3D, COQUE_AXIS) and the DKT-DKQ plate elements only. The plastic constitutive behaviour does not include the transverse shear terms, which are treated elastically, because the transverse shear is decoupled from the plastic constitutive behaviour. For a good representation of the progression of plasticity through the thickness, it is recommended to use, for the numerical integration, 3 to 5 layers through the thickness, for a number of Gauss points equal to 3, 5 and 11 respectively.
- *Geometric non-linearity* **:**  the geometric non-linearities (large displacements, large rotations) in plane stress are available for the curved shell elements COQUE_3D only.
- *Euler buckling* **:**  this type of analysis is available with the curved shell elements COQUE_3D and the plate elements DKT and DKTG.

Elements corresponding to the mechanical elements exist in thermal analysis; the thermo-mechanical couplings are therefore available both for the plate elements and for the shell elements. At present these couplings are not possible for multilayer materials.
