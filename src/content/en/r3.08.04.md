# r3.08.04 Beam element with 7 degrees of freedom for taking warping into account

**Summary:**

This document presents the element POU_D_TG, which is a straight-beam finite element with taking into account of the warping of the cross-sections. It makes it possible to analyse beams with thin open cross-sections, with restrained or free torsion.

As regards bending, the normal and shear forces, this element is based on the element POU_D_T, which is a straight-beam element with transverse shear (Timoshenko model).

For the element POU_D_TG, the cross-section is assumed constant (of arbitrary shape) and the material is homogeneous and isotropic, with linear elastic behaviour.

The element POU_D_TGM is to be used for non-linear behaviours.

This reference documentation relies on the general reference documentation of beams, in linear elasticity [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). It describes the specific features of the straight-beam element with warping POU_D_TG.

## 1 Field of use

The development of the Timoshenko beam elements with warping (modelling POU_D_TG) in *Code_Aster* was carried out initially with the aim of computing the behaviour of pylons. It was mainly a matter of analysing structures formed of beams with a thin open profile (angles), for which the warping is significant. The non-linear behaviours are to be used with the element POU_D_TGM (multifibre beam). These non-linear behaviours concern only tension and bending. The shear due to the shear force, as well as the warping and the bimoment (force linked to the warping), remain linked by an elastic behaviour, for want of being able to express a non-linear behaviour on these quantities. The description of torsion with warping is valid for the use of the elements POU_D_TG and POU_D_TGM with the linear operators (MECA_STATIQUE, DYNA_VIBRA, ...) or the non-linear ones (STAT_NON_LINE, DYNA_NON_LINE, ...).

## 3 Kinematics specific to torsion with warping

The kinematics used to represent the displacement of the beam cross-sections is identical to that of the straight Timoshenko beams [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) as regards tension–compression and bending–shear. One details here only the torsion.

Two possibilities are to be considered for the modelling of the torsional behaviour of non-circular cross-sections [bib], which always produces a warping of the cross-section.

- The torsion is free (Saint-Venant torsion): the warping of the cross-sections is non-zero (it may even be significant for a thin open cross-section), but it is independent of the position on the axis $x$ of the beam (constant as a function of $x$ ) and there is no axial stress due to the torsion.
- The torsion is restrained (Vlassov): the warping is non-zero, and moreover non-uniform axial stresses (whose resultant force is called the bimoment) exist in the beam.

The element POU_D_TG makes it possible to treat these two configurations: the torsion may be free or restrained. The user will have access to the warping in both cases; on the other hand, the bimoment will be non-zero only in the case of restrained torsion. Note that at the location of the connection of the beams, the transmission of the warping depends on the type of connection. In general, the torsion in an assembly of beams is restrained. The warping may then be blocked at the connection points.

Note:

*With elements without modelling of the warping (POU_D_T,* *POU_D_E), one can treat the case of free torsion (the displacements other than the warping will be correct), but not the case of restrained torsion.*

One can decouple the effects of torsion and of bending in a local frame (translated from the principal frame of inertia) having as its origin the centre of torsion. The centre of torsion is the point which remains fixed when the cross-section is subjected to the torsional moment alone. It is also called the shear centre because a force applied at this point does not produce a rotation about $x$ .

The displacements in the plane of the cross-section will therefore be expressed in this frame. The axial displacements remain expressed in the principal frame of inertia linked to the centre of gravity $G$ , in order to keep a decoupling of the bending displacements and of the tension–compression.

The displacement of an arbitrary point of the cross-section is then written in general form (free or restrained torsion):

$\begin{array}{}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u(x,y,z)\\ v(x,y,z)\\ w(x,y,z)\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{G}(x)\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}z{\theta}_{y}(x)\\ 0\\ w(x)\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-y{\theta}_{z}(x)\\ v(x)\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,x}(x)\\ -(z-{z}_{c}){\theta}_{x}(x)\\ (y-{y}_{c}){\theta}_{x}(x)\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$

Displacement = membrane + bending/ $y$  + bending/ $z$  + torsion with warping

The components are expressed in the principal frame of inertia (centred at $G$ ): $x$ is directed along the axis of the beam, $y$ and $z$ are the two other principal axes of inertia.

The term $\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,x}(x)$ represents the axial displacement due to the warping of the cross-section. $\omega (y,z)$ is the warping function (expressed in ${m}^{2}$ , but which has no obvious physical interpretation).

The strains at an arbitrary point of the cross-section are then:

$\begin{array}{}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{xx}(x,y,z)\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{xy}(x,y,z)\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{xz}(x,y,z)\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{{G}_{,x}}(x)\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}z{\theta}_{y,x}(x)\\ 0\\ {\gamma}_{xz}(x)\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-y{\theta}_{z,x}(x)\\ {\gamma}_{xy}(x)\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,xx}(x)\\ ({\omega}_{,y}-(z-{z}_{c})){\theta}_{x,x}(x)\\ ({\omega}_{,z}+(y-{y}_{c})){\theta}_{x,x}(x)\end{array}\right\rbrace \\ {\gamma}_{xy}(x)={v}_{,x}-{\theta}_{z}\\ {\gamma}_{xz}(x)={w}_{,x}+{\theta}_{y}\end{array}$

$\text{Déformation}=\text{membrane}+\text{flexion}/y+\text{flexion}/z+\text{torsion avec gauchissement}$

The term $\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,xx}(x)$ is zero in the case of free torsion: one has indeed ${\theta}_{x,xx}(x)=0$ , since the warping is independent of $x$ . It is not negligible in the case of restrained torsion.

The isotropic elastic constitutive law is written (making the assumption of plane stresses in the directions $y$ and $z$ ):

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}(x,y,z)\\ {\sigma}_{xy}(x,y,z)\\ {\sigma}_{xz}(x,y,z)\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}E.{\varepsilon}_{xx}(x,y,z)\\ G.2{\varepsilon}_{xy}(x,y,z)\\ G.2{\varepsilon}_{xz}(x,y,z)\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The generalised forces in the cross-section are expressed as a function of the stresses for a homogeneous cross-section by [bib]:

| $N(x)=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xx}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Normal force |
| --- | --- |
| ${V}_{y}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xy}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Shear force along $y$ |
| ${V}_{z}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xz}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Shear force along $z$ |
| ${M}_{y}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}z.{\sigma}_{xx}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Bending moment about $y$ |
| ${M}_{z}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}-y.{\sigma}_{xx}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Bending moment about $z$ |
| ${M}_{x}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}((y-{y}_{c}).{\sigma}_{xz}(x,y,z)-(z-{z}_{c}).{\sigma}_{xy}(x,y,z))\text{ds}$ | Torsional moment |
| ${M}_{\omega}(x)=\underset{S}{\int}\omega .{\sigma}_{xx}(x,y,z)\text{ds}$ | Bimoment (associated with the warping) |

${M}_{\omega}(x)$ represents the generalised force associated with the warping. It is expressed in ${\mathrm{N.m}}^{2}$ . One may give an illustration of it as in [bib] for a beam with an $\text{I}$ cross-section (the bimoment acts here along $z$ only):

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.04/10000000000001220000010FF87E64B9DC720055.png)

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.04/100000000000011D0000011C2280BB9892C6F02F.png)

For an isotropic and homogeneous elastic behaviour in the cross-section, the generalised forces are therefore expressed directly as a function of the displacements by the following relations:

$\begin{array}{}N(x)=E.S.{u}_{,x}\\ {V}_{y}(x)={\text{Gk}}_{y}S({v}_{,x}-{\theta}_{z})\\ {V}_{z}(x)={\text{Gk}}_{z}S({w}_{,x}+{\theta}_{y})\\ {M}_{y}(x)=E.{I}_{y}{\theta}_{\text{y,}x}\\ {M}_{z}(x)=E.{I}_{z}{\theta}_{z,x}\\ {M}_{x}(x)=G.J.{\theta}_{\text{x,x}}\\ {M}_{\omega}(x)=E.{I}_{\omega}.{\theta}_{\text{x,xx}}\end{array}$

where ${k}_{y},{k}_{z}$ are the shear coefficients. The warping does not intervene at the level of the shear forces, because these are expressed in the frame linked to the shear centre. Indeed, the warping function $\omega$ is such that:

$\begin{array}{}\underset{S}{\int}\omega (y,z)\text{ds}=0\\ \underset{S}{\int}y.\omega (y,z)\text{ds}=0\\ \underset{S}{\int}z.\omega (y,z)\text{ds}=0\end{array}$

And the warping constant is expressed as a function of $\omega$ by: $\underset{S}{\int}{\omega}^{2}(y,z)\text{ds}={I}_{\omega}$

## 4 Straight beam element with warping: stiffness and mass matrices

The elementary stiffness and mass matrices for the element POU_D_TG are identical to those of the straight Timoshenko beam element (POU_D_T) as regards the tension–compression and bending–shear terms [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). The approach is identical; one simply recalls the result.

This implies that, in the case of free torsion, one retains the properties of exactness of the solution at the nodes for the degrees of freedom of bending and of tension–compression.

On the other hand, we shall see that, as regards restrained torsion, one carries out an approximation which does not make it possible to recover this property in the general case.

The stiffness matrices are always computed with the option 'RIGI_MECA', and the mass matrices with the option 'MASS_MECA'. But the option 'MASS_MECA_DIAG' (diagonalised mass matrix) has not been implemented for this element (this option is mainly useful for problems of rapid dynamics, which is not the preferential field of use of this element).

The degrees of freedom of the element are those of the Timoshenko beams, plus one degree of freedom per node making it possible to compute the terms relative to the warping:

At each of the two nodes of the element, the degrees of freedom are:

| $u,v,w$ | translations along the axes $x,y,z$ | $\text{DX}\text{DY}\text{DZ}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ${\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},{\theta}_{z}$ | rotations about the axes $x,y,z$ | $\mathrm{DRX}\mathrm{DRY}\mathrm{DRZ}$ |
| ${\theta}_{\text{x,x}}$ | derivative of the torsional rotation along $x$ | $\text{GRX}$ |

The local coordinates are expressed in the principal frame of inertia. The element POU_D_TG therefore comprises 14 degrees of freedom. The reference element is defined by: $0<x<L$

### 4.1 Tension–compression the degrees of freedom are $u`ou :math:$mathrm{DX}`

The stiffness matrix of the element is: $K=\frac{\text{ES}}{L}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]$

The (consistent) mass matrix is written: $M=\frac{\rho \text{SL}}{6}(\begin{array}{cc}2& 1\\ 1& 2\end{array})$

### 4.2 Bending in the plane Gxz, degrees of freedom: w, qy or Dz, Dry

The stiffness matrix is written for the bending motion in the principal plane of inertia $(\mathrm{Gxz})$ :

$K=\frac{12{\text{EI}}_{y}}{{L}^{3}(1+{\varphi}_{y})}(\begin{array}{cccc}1& -\frac{L}{2}& -1& -\frac{L}{2}\\ & \frac{(4+{\varphi}_{y}){L}^{2}}{12}& \frac{L}{2}& \frac{(2-{\varphi}_{y}){\text{L}}^{2}}{12}\\ \text{Sym}& & 1& \frac{L}{2}\\ & & & \frac{(4+{\varphi}_{y}){\text{L}}^{2}}{12}\end{array})$

The transverse shear is taken into account by the term: ${\varphi}_{y}=\frac{12{\text{EI}}_{y}}{{k}_{z}{\text{SGL}}^{2}}$

For the mass matrix, $\text{w}(\text{x,}t)$ and ${\theta}_{y}(\text{x,}t)$ are discretised on the basis of the test functions introduced for the computation of the stiffness matrix, namely:

$\begin{array}{}w(x,t)={\xi}_{1}(x){w}_{1}(t)+{\xi}_{2}(x){\theta}_{{y}_{1}}(t)+{\xi}_{3}(x){w}_{2}(t)+{\xi}_{4}(x){\theta}_{{y}_{2}}(t)\\ {\theta}_{y}(x,t)={\xi}_{5}(x){w}_{1}(t)+{\xi}_{6}(x){\theta}_{{y}_{1}}(t)+{\xi}_{7}(x){w}_{2}(t)+{\xi}_{8}(x){\theta}_{{y}_{2}}(t)\end{array}$

The interpolation functions used for the translations (${\xi}_{1}$ to ${\xi}_{4}$ ) are Hermite polynomials of degree 3; those which are used for the rotations (${\xi}_{5}$ to ${\xi}_{8}$ ) are of degree 2: for $0<x<L$ , they are defined by [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01):

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{cc}{\xi}_{1}(x)=\frac{1}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\left[2{(\frac{x}{L})}^{3}-3{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}-{\varphi}_{y}\frac{x}{L}+(1+{\phi }_{y})\right]& {\xi}_{5}(x)=\frac{6}{L(1+{\varphi}_{y})}\frac{x}{L}\left[1-\frac{x}{L}\right]\\ {\xi}_{2}(x)=\frac{L}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\left[-{(\frac{x}{L})}^{3}+\frac{4+{\varphi}_{y}}{2}{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}-\frac{2+{\varphi}_{y}}{2}(\frac{x}{L})\right]& {\xi}_{6}(x)=\frac{1}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\left[3{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}-(4+{\varphi}_{y})(\frac{x}{L})+(1+{\varphi}_{y})\right]\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{cc}{\xi}_{3}(x)=\frac{1}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\text{}\left[-2{(\frac{x}{L})}^{3}+3{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}+{\varphi}_{y}(\frac{x}{L})\right]& {\xi}_{7}(x)=\frac{-6}{L(1+{\varphi}_{y})}\frac{x}{L}\left[1-\frac{x}{L}\right]\\ {\xi}_{4}(x)=\frac{L}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\left[-{(\frac{x}{L})}^{3}+\frac{2-{\varphi}_{y}}{2}{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}+\frac{{\varphi}_{y}}{2}(\frac{x}{L})\right]& {\xi}_{8}(x)=\frac{1}{1+{\varphi}_{y}}\text{}\left[3{(\frac{x}{L})}^{2}+(-2+{\varphi}_{y})(\frac{x}{L})\right]\end{array}\end{array}$     [1]

The expression of the mass matrix is:

$\begin{array}{}M=\frac{\rho S}{{(1+{\phi }_{y})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccccc}\frac{13L}{35}+\frac{\mathrm{7L}{\phi }_{y}}{10}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{3}& & -\frac{{\text{11L}}^{2}}{210}-\frac{{\text{11L}}^{2}{\phi }_{y}}{120}-\frac{{L}^{2}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{24}& & \frac{\mathrm{9L}}{70}+\frac{\mathrm{3L}{\phi }_{y}}{10}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{6}& \frac{13{L}^{2}}{420}+\frac{{\mathrm{3L}}^{2}{\phi }_{y}}{40}+\frac{{L}^{2}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{24}\\ & & \frac{{L}^{3}}{105}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}}{60}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{120}& & -\frac{13{L}^{2}}{420}-\frac{{\text{3L}}^{2}{\phi }_{y}}{40}-\frac{{L}^{2}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{24}& -\frac{{L}^{3}}{140}-\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}}{60}-\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{120}\\ & & & & \frac{13L}{35}+\frac{\mathrm{7L}{\phi }_{y}}{10}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{3}& -\frac{11{L}^{2}}{210}+\frac{11{L}^{2}{\phi }_{y}}{120}+\frac{{L}^{2}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{24}\\ \text{sym}& & & & & \frac{{L}^{3}}{105}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}}{60}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{120}\end{array})\\ +\frac{\rho {I}_{y}}{{(1+{\phi }_{y})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& -\frac{1}{10}+\frac{{\phi }_{y}}{2}& -\frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& -\frac{1}{10}+\frac{{\phi }_{y}}{2}\\ & \frac{\mathrm{2L}}{15}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}}{6}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{3}& \frac{1}{10}-\frac{{\phi }_{y}}{2}& -\frac{L}{30}-\frac{L{\phi }_{y}}{6}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{6}\\ & & \frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& \frac{1}{10}-\frac{{\phi }_{y}}{2}\\ \text{sym}& & & \frac{\mathrm{2L}}{15}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}}{6}+\frac{L{\phi }_{y}^{2}}{3}\end{array})\end{array}$

### 4.3 Bending in the plane Gxy, degrees of freedom: v, qz or Dy, Drz

Likewise, for the bending motion about the axis $(\mathrm{Gz})$ , in the principal plane of inertia $(\mathrm{Gxy})$ , the stiffness matrix is written:

$K=\frac{12{\text{EI}}_{z}}{{L}^{3}(1+{\varphi}_{z})}(\begin{array}{cccc}1& \frac{L}{2}& -1& \frac{L}{2}\\ & \frac{(4+{\varphi}_{z}){\text{L}}^{2}}{12}& -\frac{L}{2}& \frac{(2-{\varphi}_{z}){\text{L}}^{2}}{12}\\ & & 1& -\frac{L}{2}\\ \text{sym}& & & \frac{(4+{\varphi}_{z}){\text{L}}^{2}}{12}\end{array})$

The transverse shear is taken into account by the term:

${\varphi}_{z}=\frac{12{\text{EI}}_{z}}{{k}_{y}{\text{SGL}}^{2}}$

In order to compute the mass matrix, $v(\text{x,}t)$ and ${\theta}_{z}(\text{x,}t)$ are discretised by:

$\begin{array}{}v(\text{x,}t)={\xi}_{1}(x){\text{v}}_{1}(t)-{\xi}_{2}(x){\theta}_{{z}_{1}}(t)+{\xi}_{3}(x){\text{v}}_{2}(t)-{\xi}_{4}(x){\theta}_{{z}_{2}}(t)\\ {\theta}_{z}(\text{x,}t)=-{\xi}_{5}(x){\text{v}}_{1}(t)+{\xi}_{6}(x){\theta}_{{z}_{1}}(t)-{\xi}_{7}(x){\text{v}}_{2}(t)+{\xi}_{8}(x){\theta}_{{z}_{2}}(t)\end{array}$

We then obtain the following mass matrix:

$\begin{array}{}M=\frac{\rho S}{{(1+{\varphi}_{z})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{13L}{35}+\frac{\mathrm{7L}{\varphi}_{z}}{10}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{3}& \frac{11{L}^{2}}{210}+\frac{11{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}}{120}+\frac{{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{24}& \frac{\mathrm{9L}}{70}+\frac{\mathrm{3L}{\varphi}_{z}}{10}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{6}& -\frac{13{L}^{2}}{420}-\frac{{\mathrm{3L}}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}}{40}-\frac{{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{24}\\ & \frac{{L}^{3}}{105}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}}{60}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{120}& \frac{13{L}^{2}}{420}+\frac{{\mathrm{3L}}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}}{40}+\frac{{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{24}& -\frac{{L}^{3}}{140}-\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}}{60}-\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{120}\\ & & \frac{13L}{35}+\frac{\mathrm{7L}{\varphi}_{z}}{10}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{3}& \frac{11{L}^{2}}{210}-\frac{11{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}}{120}-\frac{{L}^{2}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{24}\\ \text{sym}& & & \frac{{L}^{3}}{105}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}}{60}+\frac{{L}^{3}{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{120}\end{array})\\ +\frac{\rho {I}_{z}}{{(1+{\varphi}_{z})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& \frac{1}{10}-\frac{{\varphi}_{z}}{2}& -\frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& \frac{1}{10}-\frac{{\varphi}_{z}}{2}\\ & \frac{\mathrm{2L}}{15}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}}{6}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{3}& -\frac{1}{10}+\frac{{\varphi}_{z}}{2}& -\frac{L}{30}-\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}}{6}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{6}\\ & & \frac{6}{\mathrm{5L}}& -\frac{1}{10}+\frac{{\varphi}_{z}}{2}\\ \text{sym}& & & \frac{\mathrm{2L}}{15}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}}{6}+\frac{L{\varphi}_{z}^{2}}{3}\end{array})\end{array}$

### 4.4 Torsion and warping, degrees of freedom: qx, qx,x or Drx, Grx

As regards torsion, the formulation is obviously different from that of the beams without warping of the reference [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). The virtual work of the internal forces is written for torsion [bib]:

${W}_{int}={\int}_{o}^{L}{\theta}_{\text{x,x}}^{\text{*}}.G.J.{\theta}_{\text{x,x}}+{\theta}_{\text{x,xx}}^{\text{*}}.E.{I}_{\omega}.{\theta}_{\text{x,xx}}\text{dx}$

The interpolation functions of the torsional rotation must be of class $\mathrm{C2}$ , since they must make it possible to interpolate the second derivative of the rotation.

By using the equilibrium equations, one shows in [bib] that the analytical solution involves hyperbolic interpolation functions in $x$ . This then makes it possible to obtain exact results at the nodes. This is not the choice made for *Code_Aster*: one has chosen, for the sake of simplicity for the numerical integration as well as in order to avoid the numerical problems of evaluation of the hyperbolic functions, polynomials of degree 3 of Hermite type, of the same kind as those used for bending [eq]. One writes them here on the reference element [-1, 1] according to [bib] (instead of $0<x<L$ previously):

$\begin{array}{}\xi =\frac{\mathrm{2x}}{L}-1\\ -1\le \xi \le 1\end{array}$ , $\begin{array}{}{N}_{1}(\xi )=\frac{1}{4}{(1-\xi )}^{2}(2+\xi )\\ {N}_{2}(\xi )=\frac{L}{8}(1-\xi )(1-{\xi}^{2})\\ {N}_{3}(\xi )=\frac{1}{4}{(1+\xi )}^{2}(2-\xi )\\ {N}_{4}(\xi )=\frac{L}{8}(1+\xi )(-1+{\xi}^{2})\end{array}$

The interpolation for the torsional rotation and its derivative is:

${\theta}_{x}(\xi )={N}_{1}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x}}^{1}+{N}_{2}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x,x}}^{1}+{N}_{3}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x}}^{2}+{N}_{4}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x,x}}^{2}$

${\theta}_{x,x}(\xi )={N}_{1,x}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x}}^{1}+{N}_{2,x}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x,x}}^{1}+{N}_{3,x}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x}}^{2}+{N}_{4,x}(\xi ){\theta}_{{}_{x,x}}^{2}$

The reference [bib] notes that this approximation corresponds to a limiting case of the hyperbolic interpolation, obtained for $\sqrt{\frac{\text{GJ}}{{\text{EI}}_{\omega}}}\to 0$ . However, this parameter not being dimensionless, it is difficult to define a priori the values for which the approximation is acceptable. The numerical tests carried out show that one converges rapidly towards the solution when the size of the elements decreases.

The stiffness matrix corresponding to this approximation is then written:

$K={K}_{T}+{K}_{\omega}=\frac{\text{GJ}}{30L}(\begin{array}{cccc}36& \mathrm{3L}& -36& \mathrm{6L}\\ & {\mathrm{4L}}^{2}& -\mathrm{3L}& -{L}^{2}\\ & & 36& -\mathrm{3L}\\ \text{sym}& & & {\mathrm{4L}}^{2}\end{array})+\frac{{\text{EI}}_{\omega}}{{L}^{3}}(\begin{array}{cccc}12& \mathrm{6L}& -12& \mathrm{6L}\\ & {\mathrm{4L}}^{2}& -\mathrm{6L}& {\mathrm{2L}}^{2}\\ & & 12& -\mathrm{6L}\\ \text{sym}& & & {\mathrm{4L}}^{2}\end{array})$

The mass matrix may be obtained in several ways [bib]:

- the most complete method would consist in computing the inertia terms with the interpolation functions above, taking into account the additional term:
- ${W}_{\text{iner}}=-{\int}_{o}^{L}{\theta}_{{}_{\text{x,x}}}^{\text{*}}.\rho .{I}_{\omega}.{\ddot{\theta}}_{\text{x,x}}\text{dx}$
- in *Code_Aster*, a simpler method has been chosen: the mass matrix is identical to that of the element POU_D_T. One retains the terms already defined for tension–compression and bending–shear and one uses a linear approximation for the torsion. The coefficients of the mass matrix associated with the warping are zero with this approach.

### 4.5 Offset of the torsional axis with respect to the neutral axis

At the centre of torsion $C$ , the effects of bending and of torsion are decoupled; one can therefore use the results established in the previous chapter.

The coordinates of the point $C$ are to be supplied to AFFE_CARA_ELEM: one gives the components of the vector $\mathrm{GC}$ ($G$ being the centre of gravity of the cross-section) in the principal frame of inertia:

$\text{GC}=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ {e}_{y}\\ {e}_{z}\end{array})$

One can determine them numerically from the plane mesh of the cross-section with the aid of the operator MACR_CARA_POUTRE [R3.08.03](doc:r3.08.03).

Once the point $C$ has been determined, one recovers as in [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) the components of the displacement at the centre of gravity $G$ by considering the rigid-body relation:

$u(G)=u(C)+\text{GC}\wedge \Omega$ with $\Omega =(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})$ the rotation vector, $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{G}{\text{= u}}_{C}\\ {v}_{G}{\text{= v}}_{C}{\text{+e}}_{z}{\theta}_{x}\\ {w}_{G}{\text{= w}}_{C}-{e}_{y}{\theta}_{x}\text{}.\end{array}$

The change of variables is written in the same way as for POU_D_T, with 2 additional degrees of freedom:

## 5 Geometric stiffness - Prestressed structure

This matrix is computed by the option rigi_geom. It is used to treat buckling problems or vibrations of prestressed structures. In the case of a prestressed structure, therefore subjected to initial forces (known and independent of time), one cannot neglect in the equilibrium equation the terms introduced by the change of geometry from the stress-free state to the prestressed state. This change of geometry modifies the equilibrium equation only by the addition of a term which is a function of the displacements and of the prestress, whose associated matrix is called the geometric stiffness matrix and which is expressed by:

${W}_{G}=\underset{{V}_{o}}{\int}\frac{\partial {u}_{k}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial {x}_{i}}{\sigma}_{ij}^{o}\frac{\partial {v}_{k}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial {x}_{j}}\text{dV}$

where ${\sigma}_{ij}^{o}$ denotes the prestress tensor. This term appears naturally if one introduces the GREEN-LAGRANGE strain tensor into the virtual work of the strain:

$\begin{array}{}{E}_{xx}={\varepsilon}_{xx}+{\eta}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{2}\left[{(\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x})}^{2}+{(\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x})}^{2}+{(\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x})}^{2}\right]\\ 2{E}_{xy}=2{\varepsilon}_{xy}+2{\eta}_{xy}=\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}+\left[\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial y}+\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial y}\right]\\ 2{E}_{xz}=2{\varepsilon}_{xz}+2{\eta}_{xz}=\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}+\left[\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial z}\right]\end{array}$

In the expression of these strains, the quadratic terms ${(\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x})}^{2},\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial y}\text{et}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial x}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}}{\partial z}$ are neglected here, following the assumption commonly made by most authors [bib]. For a beam model, the initial-stress tensor reduces, in the local axes of the beam, to the components ${\sigma}_{xx},{\sigma}_{xy}$ and ${\sigma}_{xz}$ . One uses the kinematics introduced in [§2]:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}(x,y,z)={u}_{G}(x)+z{\theta}_{y}(x)-y{\theta}_{z}(x)+\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,x}(x)\\ {u}_{y}^{\mathrm{3D}}(x,y,z)={v}_{C}(x)-(z-{z}_{c}){\theta}_{x}(x)\\ {u}_{z}^{\mathrm{3D}}(x,y,z)={w}_{C}(x)+(y-{y}_{c}){\theta}_{x}(x)\end{array}$

and the expression of the generalised forces as a function of the stresses:

${N}^{0}=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xx}^{o}\text{ds}{V}_{{}_{y}}^{0}=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xy}^{o}\text{ds}{V}_{{}_{z}}^{0}=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xz}^{o}\text{ds}{M}_{{}_{y}}^{0}=\underset{S}{\int}z{\sigma}_{{}_{xx}}^{0}\text{ds}{M}_{{}_{z}}^{0}=\underset{S}{\int}-y{\sigma}_{{}_{xx}}^{0}\text{ds}$

One assumes, moreover, that ${N}^{0}$ , ${V}_{{}_{y}}^{0}$ , ${V}_{{}_{z}}^{0}$ are constant in the discretised element (which is inaccurate, for example, for a vertical beam subjected to its self-weight). The moments are assumed to vary linearly:

${M}_{y}^{0}=\left({M}_{\mathit{y2}}^{0}-{M}_{\mathit{y1}}^{0}\right)\frac{x}{L}+{M}_{\mathit{y1}}^{0}$ $\frac{\partial {M}_{y}^{0}}{\partial x}-{V}_{z}^{0}=0$

${M}_{z}^{0}=\left({M}_{\mathit{z2}}^{0}-{M}_{\mathit{z1}}^{0}\right)\frac{x}{L}+{M}_{\mathit{z1}}^{0}$ $\frac{\partial {M}_{z}^{0}}{\partial x}+{V}_{y}^{0}=0$

These assumptions make it possible to express ${W}_{G}$ for a straight beam with warping in the following way:

$\begin{array}{c}{W}_{G}={\int}_{0}^{L}{N}^{0}\left({v}_{,x}\delta {v}_{,x}+{w}_{,x}\delta {w}_{,x}\right)+{N}^{0}\left(\frac{{I}_{y}+{I}_{z}}{A}+{y}_{c}^{2}+{z}_{c}^{2}\right){\theta}_{x,x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}\\ +{z}_{c}{N}^{0}\left({v}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}+{\theta}_{x,x}\delta {v}_{,x}\right)-{y}_{c}{N}^{0}\left({w}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}+{\theta}_{x,x}\delta {w}_{,x}\right)\\ -{M}_{y}^{0}\left({w}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{,x}+{\theta}_{,x}\delta {w}_{,x}\right)-{M}_{z}^{0}\left({v}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{,x}+{\theta}_{,x}\delta {v}_{,x}\right)\\ -\left({y}_{c}{V}_{y}^{0}-{z}_{c}{V}_{z}^{0}\right)\left({\theta}_{x,x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{\theta}_{x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}\right)+{V}_{y}^{0}\left({w}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{\theta}_{x}\delta {w}_{,x}\right)\\ -{V}_{z}^{0}\left({v}_{,x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{\theta}_{x}\delta {v}_{,x}\right)+\left(\left({\mathrm{2y}}_{c}-\frac{{I}_{{\mathit{yr}}^{2}}}{{I}_{z}}\right){M}_{z}^{0}+\left(-{\mathrm{2z}}_{c}+\frac{{I}_{{\mathit{zr}}^{2}}}{{I}_{y}}\right){M}_{y}^{0}\right){\theta}_{x,x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}\\ +\left(-\frac{{I}_{\mathit{yr2}}}{{I}_{z}}\frac{{\text{dM}}_{z}^{0}}{\text{dx}}+\frac{{I}_{\mathit{zr2}}}{{I}_{y}}\frac{{\text{dM}}_{y}^{0}}{\text{dx}}\right)\left({\theta}_{x,x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{\theta}_{x}\delta {\theta}_{x,x}\right)\end{array}$

with the terms:

$\begin{array}{c}{I}_{{\mathit{yr}}^{2}}=\underset{S}{\int}y\left({y}^{2}+{z}^{2}\right)\text{ds}\\ {I}_{{\mathit{zr}}^{2}}=\underset{S}{\int}z\left({y}^{2}+{z}^{2}\right)\text{ds}\end{array}$

which represent the non-symmetry of the cross-section. In the case where the cross-section possesses two axes of symmetry (therefore $C$ coincides with $G$ ), these terms are zero.

Caution: these terms (which are named IYR2 and IZR2 in the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM) are not currently computed by MACR_CARA_POUTRE. The user must therefore supply them from tabulated values for each type of cross-section (angle, rectangle, ...).

Moreover, in order to be able to treat the problems of lateral-torsional buckling of thin beams, loaded essentially by bending moments and normal forces, one must add the assumption of moderate rotations in torsion [bib], [bib].

This is expressed by the following modification of the displacement field (only for the computation of the geometric stiffness):

${u}_{x}^{\mathrm{3D}}(x,y,z)={u}_{G}(x)+z({\theta}_{y}(x)+{\theta}_{x}(x){\theta}_{z}(x))-y({\theta}_{z}(x)-{\theta}_{x}(x){\theta}_{y}(x))+\omega (y,z){\theta}_{x,x}(x)$

The origin of this expression cannot be detailed here. It is the subject of the thesis of DE VILLE DE GOYET [bib] on the buckling of beams with thin open cross-sections. The assumption of moderate torsional rotations (and not infinitesimal ones) makes it possible to model correctly the lateral-torsional buckling of a thin-section beam in torsion (torsion–bending coupling).

The assumption of moderate rotations leads one to add to ${W}_{{}_{G}}^{0}$ the term ${W}_{G}^{1}$  :

${W}_{G}^{1}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{o}^{L}-{M}_{z}^{o}\delta ({\theta}_{x}{\theta}_{y,x}+{\theta}_{y}{\theta}_{x,x})+{M}_{y}^{o}\delta ({\theta}_{x}{\theta}_{z,x}+{\theta}_{z}{\theta}_{x,x})+{V}_{y}^{o}\delta ({\theta}_{x}{\theta}_{y})+{V}_{z}^{o}\delta ({\theta}_{x}{\theta}_{z})$

Finally, one obtains the geometric stiffness matrix by discretising ${W}_{{}_{G}}={W}_{{}_{G}}^{0}+{W}_{{}_{G}}^{1}$ with the aid of the same interpolation functions as the stiffness matrix of [§4.4]. After having computed these matrices, one must carry out a change of frame as in [§4.5]. One then obtains a geometric stiffness matrix of the form:

${K}_{G}=(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{1}& {A}_{2}\\ {A}_{2}& {A}_{3}\end{array})$

## 6 Loadings

The various types of loading available for the element POU_D_TG are:

| **Types or options** |  |
| --- | --- |
| CHAR_MECA_FR1D1D | distributed loading by real values |
| CHAR_MECA_FF1D1D | distributed loading by function |
| CHAR_MECA_PESA_R | loading due to gravity |
| CHAR_MECA_TEMP_R | 'thermal' loading |
| CHAR_MECA_EPSI_R | loading by imposition of a strain (of thermal-stratification type) |

The loadings are computed in the same way as for the elements without warping [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01). There is therefore nothing particular to the element POU_D_TG. The other types of loading described in [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) are not available for this element.

As regards the warping, it is possible to give boundary conditions involving the degree of freedom $\mathit{GRX}$ (which makes it possible to model the restrained torsion: $\mathrm{GRX}=0$ ), but on the other hand nothing is provided for assigning a loading of bimoment type, whose physical interpretation is difficult to establish.

Concerning the connection between elements, the transmission of the warping is an open question, as the reference [bib] points out: the continuity of the variable $\mathit{GRX}$ from one element to the next (on which the warping depends directly) depends in fact on the technology of the connection between the various beams (weld in the axis, in which case the warping can be transmitted in full, connection by a gusset plate, ...).

For an assembled structure such as a lattice, it seems more reasonable to assume that the torsion is restrained, therefore that the warping is zero at the ends. In order to assess the influence of this assumption, one may refer to the test SSLL102 (beam with an angle cross-section), whose modellings C and D use the element POU_D_TG, with free torsion for modelling C and restrained torsion for modelling D [V3.01.102B].

One observes that, for the bending loading, the difference in the displacement is small (2.5%), but for a torsional loading one obtains for this cross-section a non-zero lateral displacement (lateral-torsional buckling) whose value differs appreciably according to the assumption made:

${u}_{z}=2.2{10}^{-5}$ for free torsion and ${u}_{z}=2.62{10}^{-5}$ for restrained torsion.

Likewise, the rotation varies strongly:

${\theta}_{x}=3,79{10}^{-4}$ for free torsion and ${\theta}_{x}=6,39{10}^{-4}$ for restrained torsion (GRX is zero at the ends).

### 6.1 Distributed loadings, options: CHAR_MECA_FR1D1D and CHAR_MECA_FF1D1D

The loadings are given under the keyword FORCE_POUTRE, either by real values in AFFE_CHAR_MECA (option CHAR_MECA_FR1D1D), or by functions in AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F (option CHAR_MECA_FF1D1D). The loading is given only by distributed forces, not by distributed moments.

The right-hand side associated with the distributed tension–compression loading is:

$\begin{array}{}\left[\begin{array}{c}{f}_{1}\\ {f}_{2}\end{array}\right]\end{array}$ with ${f}_{1}={\int}_{0}^{1}{f}_{\text{ext}}(x)(1-\frac{x}{L})\text{dx}$

${f}_{2}={\int}_{0}^{1}{f}_{\text{ext}}(x)\frac{x}{L}\text{dx}$

For a constant or linearly varying loading, one obtains:

$\begin{array}{}{F}_{{x}_{1}}=L(\frac{{n}_{1}}{3}+\frac{{n}_{2}}{6}),\\ {F}_{{x}_{2}}=L(\frac{{n}_{1}}{6}+\frac{{n}_{2}}{3}).\end{array}$

${n}_{1}$ and ${n}_{2}$ are the components of the axial loading at points 1 and 2 coming from the user's data, placed back into the local frame.

If ${t}_{{y}_{1}},{t}_{\mathrm{y2}},{t}_{\mathrm{z1}}$ and ${t}_{\mathrm{z2}}$ are those of the shear force, one has:

$\begin{array}{cccc}{F}_{{y}_{1}}=L(\frac{7{t}_{{y}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{3{t}_{{y}_{2}}}{20})& & {M}_{{z}_{1}}={L}^{2}(\frac{{t}_{{y}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{{t}_{{y}_{2}}}{30})& \\ {F}_{{y}_{2}}=L(\frac{3{t}_{{y}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{7{t}_{{y}_{2}}}{20})& & {M}_{{z}_{2}}=-{L}^{2}(\frac{{t}_{{y}_{1}}}{30}+\frac{{t}_{{y}_{2}}}{20}),& \\ {F}_{{z}_{1}}=L(\frac{7{t}_{{z}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{3{t}_{{z}_{2}}}{20}),& & {M}_{{y}_{1}}=-{L}^{2}(\frac{{t}_{{z}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{{t}_{{z}_{2}}}{30}),& \\ {F}_{{z}_{2}}=L(\frac{3{t}_{{z}_{1}}}{20}+\frac{7{t}_{{z}_{2}}}{20}),& & {M}_{{y}_{2}}={L}^{2}(\frac{{t}_{{z}_{1}}}{30}+\frac{{t}_{{z}_{2}}}{20}).& \end{array}$

### 6.2 Gravity loading, option: CHAR_MECA_PESA_R

The gravity force is given by the magnitude of the acceleration $g$ and a unit vector $n$ indicating the direction of the loading.

**Notes** **(simplifying assumption):**

The shape functions used for this computation are those of the Euler–Bernoulli model.

The approach is similar to that used for the distributed forces, provided that one first transforms the loading vector due to gravity into the local frame of the element. One obtains, in the local frame of the beam:

$\begin{array}{}{F}_{{x}_{i}}={\int}_{o}^{L}{\xi}_{i}\rho Sg\cdot x\text{dx}\\ ({\xi}_{1}=1-\frac{x}{L},{\xi}_{2}=\frac{x}{L})\end{array}$ whence: $\begin{array}{}{F}_{{x}_{1}}=\rho g\cdot xL(\frac{S}{3}+\frac{S}{6})\text{}\text{au point 1,}\\ {F}_{{x}_{2}}=\rho g\cdot xL(\frac{S}{6}+\frac{S}{3})\text{}\text{au point 2}\end{array}$

**Bending in the plane** $(\mathrm{Gxz})$ **:**

$\begin{array}{}{F}_{{z}_{1}}=\rho g\cdot zL(\frac{7S}{20}+\frac{3S}{20})\\ {M}_{{y}_{1}}=-\rho g\cdot z\text{}{L}^{2}(\frac{S}{20}+\frac{S}{30})\\ {F}_{{z}_{2}}=\rho g\cdot zL(\frac{3S}{20}+\frac{7S}{20})\\ {M}_{{y}_{2}}=\rho g\cdot z{L}^{2}(\frac{S}{30}+\frac{S}{20})\end{array}$

**Bending in the plane** $(\mathrm{Gxy})$ **:**

$\begin{array}{}{F}_{{y}_{1}}=\rho g\cdot yL(\frac{7S}{20}+\frac{3S}{20})\\ {M}_{{z}_{1}}=\rho g\cdot y\text{}{L}^{2}(\frac{S}{20}+\frac{S}{30})\\ {F}_{{y}_{2}}=\rho g\cdot yL(\frac{3S}{20}+\frac{7S}{20})\\ {M}_{{z}_{2}}=-\rho g\cdot y{L}^{2}(\frac{S}{30}+\frac{S}{20})\end{array}$

### 6.3 Thermal loading, option: CHAR_MECA_TEMP_R

In order to obtain this loading, one must compute the axial displacements induced by the temperature difference $T-{T}_{\text{référence}}$ :

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}=-L\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{référence}})\\ {u}_{2}=L\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{référence}})\end{array}$

($\alpha$  : thermal expansion coefficient)

Then, one simply computes the forces induced by $F=\text{K u}$ .

Since $K$ is the local stiffness matrix of the element, one must then carry out a change of frame in order to obtain the values of the components of the loading in the global frame.

### 6.4 Loading by imposed strain, option: CHAR_MECA_EPSI_R

One computes, as for the elements POU_D_T, the loading from a state of strain (this option was developed in order to take into account the thermal stratification in the piping). The model takes into account only work in tension–compression and in pure bending (no shear force, no torsional moment).

The strain is given by the user with the aid of the keyword PRE_EPSI in AFFE_CHAR_MECA. By prescribing $\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\frac{\partial {\theta}_{y}}{\partial x}$ and $\frac{\partial {\theta}_{z}}{\partial x}$ on the beam, one obtains the elementary right-hand side associated with this loading:

at node 1:

$\begin{array}{cccc}& {F}_{{x}_{1}}& =& E{S}_{1}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\\ & {M}_{{y}_{1}}& =& E{I}_{{y}_{1}}\frac{\partial {\theta}_{y}}{\partial x},\\ & {M}_{{z}_{1}}& =& E{I}_{{z}_{1}}\frac{\partial {\theta}_{z}}{\partial x},\end{array}$

at node 2:

$\begin{array}{cccc}& {F}_{{x}_{2}}& =& E{S}_{2}\frac{\partial u}{\partial x},\\ & {M}_{{y}_{2}}& =& E{I}_{{y}_{2}}\frac{\partial {\theta}_{y}}{\partial x},\\ & {M}_{{z}_{2}}& =& E{I}_{{z}_{2}}\frac{\partial {\theta}_{z}}{\partial x}\end{array}$

## 7 Wrench of the forces - Nodal forces and reactions

### 7.1 Options disponibles

The various post-processing options available for the element POU_D_TG are:

| **Types or options** |  |
| --- | --- |
| EFGE_ELNO | wrench of the forces at the 2 nodes of each element |
| SIEF_ELGA | force field necessary for the computation of the nodal forces (option 'FORC_NODA') and of the reactions (option 'REAC_NODA'). |
| FORC_NODA | nodal forces expressed in the global frame |
| REAC_NODA | nodal reactions |

### 7.2 The wrench of the forces

#### 7.2.1 Generalised forces, option: EFGE_ELNO

One seeks to compute, at the two nodes of each "beam" element constituting the mesh of the structure under study, the forces exerted on the "beam" element by the rest of the structure. The values are given in the local basis of each element. By integrating the equilibrium equations, one obtains the forces in the local frame of the element:

${R}_{\text{LOC}}={K}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}{u}_{\text{LOC}}+{M}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}{\ddot{u}}_{\text{LOC}}-{f}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}$

where: ${R}_{\text{LOC}}=(-{N}^{1},-{V}_{Y}^{1},-{V}_{Z}^{1},-{M}_{T}^{1},-{M}_{Y}^{1},-{M}_{Z}^{1},-{M}_{\omega}^{1},{N}^{2},{V}_{Y}^{2},{V}_{Z}^{2},{M}_{T}^{2},{M}_{Y}^{2},{M}_{Z}^{2},{M}_{\omega}^{2})$

| ${K}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}$ | elementary stiffness matrix of the beam element, |
| --- | --- |
| ${M}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}$ | elementary mass matrix of the beam element, |
| ${f}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}$ | vector of the 'distributed' forces on the beam element, |
| ${u}_{\text{LOC}}$ | 'degree of freedom' vector restricted to the beam element, |
| ${\ddot{u}}_{\text{LOC}}$ | 'acceleration' vector restricted to the beam element. |

One then changes the signs of the forces at node 1.

Indeed, taking for example the case of tension–compression, one shows [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) that the forces in the element (option EFGE_ELNO) are obtained by:

$\left[\begin{array}{c}-N(o)\\ N(L)\end{array}\right]=\left[K\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}u(o)\\ u(L)\end{array}\right]-\left[\begin{array}{c}{f}_{1}\\ {f}_{2}\end{array}\right]$

#### 7.2.2 Generalised forces, option: SIEF_ELGA

The option "SIEF_ELGA" is implemented for reasons of compatibility with other options. It serves only for the computation of the nodal forces. It produces a force field by elements. It is computed by:

${R}_{\text{LOC}}={K}_{\text{LOC}}^{e}{u}_{\text{LOC}}$

### 7.3 Computation of the nodal forces and of the reactions

#### 7.3.1 Nodal forces, option: FORC_NODA

This option computes a vector of nodal forces over the whole structure, expressed in the global frame. It produces a field at the nodes in the command CALC_CHAMP by assembly of the elementary terms.

For this computation, one uses the principle of virtual work and one writes [R5.03.01]:

$F={Q}^{T}\sigma$

where ${Q}^{T}$ symbolically represents the matrix associated with the divergence operator. For an element, one writes the work of the virtual strain field:

$({Q}^{T}\sigma ){u}^{\text{*}}={\int}_{\Omega}\sigma (u)\varepsilon ({u}^{\text{*}})\forall {u}^{\text{*}}$ **kinematically admissible**

For the beam elements, one simply computes the nodal forces by assembly of the elementary nodal forces computed by the option SIEF_ELGA, which are expressed by:

$\left[{F}_{\text{LOC}}\right]=\left[{K}_{\text{LOC}}\right]\left[{U}_{\text{LOC}}\right]$

#### 7.3.2 Nodal reactions, option: REAC_NODA

This option, called by CALC_CHAMP, makes it possible to obtain the reactions $R$ at the supports, expressed in the global frame, from the nodal forces $F$ by:

$R=F-{F}^{\text{char}}+{F}^{\text{iner}}$

${F}^{\text{char}}\text{et}{F}^{\text{iner}}$ being the nodal forces associated respectively with the given loadings (concentrated and distributed) and with the inertia forces.
