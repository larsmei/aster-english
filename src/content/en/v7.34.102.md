# v7.34.102 WDNP102 – Reflection and absorption of a compression wave along a poroelastic column

**Summary:**

The efficiency of the elements paraaxials in hydromechanical modelling is tested. The case concerns the propagation of a compression wave in a poroelastic column. The wave is introduced and absorbed by the element paraaxial located at the base of the column after having reflected at its top. Two test cases are produced, one testing the 2D and the other testing the 3D.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The mesh of the poroelastic column is given opposite. The propagation axis is directed according to $(\mathit{OY})$. The total height of the column is $25m$ , for $0.4m$ width. The first 5 meters from the bottom are discretized by elements of $1m$ thickness while the following $20m$ are discretized by elements of $0.2m$ thickness. The entire column contains 105 elements.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and initials

A compression wave in the column is to be propagated from bottom to top with a blockage of the upper part so that there is reflection of the wave at this point and inverse propagation from top to bottom. An absorbent border (element paraaxial) is placed at the base of the column in order to completely amortize the reflected wave.

The column is subject to gravity, so geostatic conditions in both actual stresses and hydraulic pressure pre-exist initially in the column. The initial column balance is not obvious and requires the following procedure:

- The base of the column, flanked by the element paraaxial (red), is blocked with very stiff springs. A prior static calculation ( **Figure 1** ) allows to recover a field of stresses and hydraulic pressure in balance with the boundary conditions and loading;
- Nodal forces (resulting from both mechanical and hydraulic forces, in green) are calculated in springs ( **Figure 2** );
- These nodal forces are reinjected at the base where the springs are simultaneously deactivated by very low stiffness ( **Figure 3** ). The boundary conditions thus defined allow to produce a perfectly balanced initial hydromechanical state;
- Dynamic calculation can occur by injecting the wave from the element paraaxial ( **Figure 4** );

To account for horizontal translation invariance (problem 1D), lateral displacements are consolidated by an restraint type LIAISON_DDL.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics A

Two-dimensional HM Modelling case in flat deformations.

The time discretization is from ${10}^{-3}s$ to $0,2s$ .

### 2.2 Results

The following figures present the speed and hydraulic pressure profiles along the column as a function of time. A reflection of the wave at the top of the column is observed, followed by an inverse propagation of the reflected wave. This reflected wave is then absorbed at the base by the absorbent border.

This test case differs from test case SDLV121 in the following points:

- it demonstrates that the elements paraaxials are also functional with HM modelling;
- it is a closer reference to the real problems encountered, where it is necessary to start from an initially balanced state taking into account a field of geostatic forces;
