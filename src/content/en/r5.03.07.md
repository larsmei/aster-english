# r5.03.07 Rousselier model for ductile fracture

**Summary**

The Rousselier model describes the damage due to the plastic growth of cavities in a metal. It makes it possible to model cracking and ductile fracture. The constitutive relation is elastoplastic or viscoplastic with isotropic hardening. It allows plastic volume changes and is written in small strains. The writing in large strains with a modified Simo and Miehe formulation, in the elastoplastic case only, is described in [R5.03.06](doc:r5.03.06).

This model is available in the command STAT_NON_LINE through the keyword RELATION='ROUSS_PR' or 'ROUSS_VISC' under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT and with the keyword DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC'.

This model is implemented for the three-dimensional (3D), axisymmetric (AXIS), plane-stress and plane-strain modellings (C_PLAN, D_PLAN).

The writing and the numerical treatment of this model are presented.

## 1 Introduction

The mechanisms at the origin of the ductile fracture of metals are associated with the development of cavities within the material. One generally distinguishes three phases:

- nucleation: this is the initiation or nucleation of the cavities, at sites which correspond preferentially to the second-phase particles present in the material,
- growth: this is the phase which corresponds to the development proper of the cavities, driven essentially by the plastic flow of the metallic matrix which surrounds these cavities,
- coalescence: this is the phase which corresponds to the localisation of the strain between the cavities in order to create macroscopic cracks.

The Rousselier model [bib1], [bib2], [bib3] presented here is based on microstructural hypotheses which introduce a microstructure consisting of cavities and of a matrix whose elastic strains are negligible compared with the plastic strains. In this case, and in the absence of nucleation of new cavities, the porosity $f$ , defined as the ratio between the cavity volume ${V}^{c}$ and the total volume $V$ of the representative elementary volume, is directly related to the macroscopic plastic strain by:

$\frac{{\rho}_{0}}{\rho}=\frac{1-{f}_{0}}{1-f}$ avec $f=\frac{{V}^{c}}{V}\mathrm{\iff }\dot{f}=(1-f)\mathrm{tr}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$         eq 1-1

where ${f}_{0}$ denotes the initial porosity, ${\rho}_{o}$ and $\rho$ are respectively the density in the initial and current configurations (one takes in what follows ${\rho}_{o}=1$ ) and ${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ the plastic strain rate of the total volume $V$ .

The construction of the model rests on a thermodynamic and phenomenological analysis which leads to writing the *plastic* potential $F$ in the following form:

$F(\tau ,p,f)={\tau}_{\text{eq}}+{\sigma}_{1}{D}_{1}f\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})-R(p)$                 eq 1-2

where $\tau =\sigma /\rho$ is the Kirchhoff stress, $\sigma$ is the Cauchy stress, $R$ the isotropic hardening as a function of the cumulative plastic strain $p$ , ${\sigma}_{1}$ and ${D}_{1}$ material parameters. The presence in the plastic potential of the hydrostatic stress ${\tau}_{m}$ authorises the plastic volume changes.

In the case of nucleation of new cavities, one considers that the volume fraction created is proportional to the cumulative plastic strain. It is therefore sufficient to replace $f$ by $f+{A}_{n}p$ in the equations of the model. ${A}_{n}$ is a material parameter. Equation [eq 1-1] is not modified.

In the viscoplastic case, one writes the *viscoplastic* potential ${F}^{\text{vp}}$ as a function of the plastic potential $F$ :

${F}^{\text{vp}}=\Lambda (F,p,f)$                                         eq 1-3

One will consider only the particular case such that:

$\dot{p}=\frac{\partial \Lambda }{\partial F}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}{\left[\text{sh}(\frac{F}{{\sigma}_{0}})\right]}^{m}$                                 eq 1-4

which reduces to a power function (Norton-type law) when the two material parameters ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}$ and ${\sigma}_{0}$ are very large.

In what follows, one presents the constitutive relations of the Rousselier model and its numerical integration.

| ${A}_{m}$ | hydrostatic part of the tensor $\mathrm{A}$ defined by ${A}_{m}=\frac{\text{tr}\mathrm{A}}{3}$ |
| ${A}_{\text{eq}}$ | von Mises equivalent value defined by ${A}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}:\tilde{A}}$ |
| $:$ | doubly contracted product: $\mathrm{A}:\mathrm{B}=\sum_{i,j}{A}_{ij}{B}_{ij}=\text{tr}({\text{AB}}^{\mathrm{T}})$ |
| $\otimes$ | tensor product: $(\mathrm{A}\otimes \mathrm{B}{)}_{ijkl}={A}_{ij}{B}_{kl}$ |
| $\lambda ,\mu ,E,\nu ,K$ | coefficients of isotropic elasticity |

Furthermore, within the framework of a time discretisation, all the quantities $Q$ evaluated at the previous time instant are indexed by ${-}^{}$ , the quantities evaluated at the time instant $t={t}^{-}+\Deltat$ are not indexed and the increments are denoted by $\Delta$ . One thus has:

$Q={Q}^{-}+\Deltaq$

The numerical solution is carried out by a $\theta$ -method, with $0\le \theta \le 1$ . For all the quantities, one defines:

${Q}^{\theta}={Q}^{-}+\theta \Deltaq$

## 3 Rousselier model

We now describe the derivation of the equations of the Rousselier model presented in the introduction.

### 3.1 Derivation of the equations of the model

One assumes that the *specific* free energy decomposes into three parts: a hyperelastic part which depends only on the elastic strain, a part linked to the hardening mechanism and a part linked to the damage:

$\Phi ({\varepsilon}^{e},p,f)={\Phi}^{e}({\varepsilon}^{e})+{\Phi}^{p}(p)+{\Phi}^{f}(f)$                             eq 3.1-1

The Clausius–Duhem inequality is written (one does not consider the thermal part):

$\tau :\dot{\varepsilon}-\dot{\Phi}\ge 0$                                         eq 3.1-2

an expression in which $\dot{\varepsilon}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{e}+{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ represents the strain rate.

The dissipation is also written:

$(\tau -\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\varepsilon}^{e}}):{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{e}+\tau :{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial p}\dot{p}-\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial f}\dot{f}\ge 0$             eq 3.1-3

The second principle of thermodynamics then requires the following expression for the elastic stress–strain relation:

$\tau =\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\varepsilon}^{e}}$                                             eq 3.1-4

One defines the thermodynamic forces associated with the elastic strain, with the cumulative plastic strain and with the porosity in accordance with the framework of generalised standard materials:

$\tau ({\varepsilon}^{e})=\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial {\varepsilon}^{e}}$                                             eq 3.1-5

$A(p)=\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial p}$                                             eq 3.1-6

$B(f)=\frac{\partial \Phi }{\partial f}$                                             eq 3.1-7

There then remains for the dissipation:

$\tau :{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-A\dot{p}-B\dot{f}\ge 0$                                 eq 3.1-8

The principle of maximum dissipation applied from the *viscoplastic* potential ${F}^{\text{vp}}(\tau \text{,A,B})$ makes it possible to deduce from it the evolution laws of the plastic strain, of the cumulative plastic strain and of the porosity, namely:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{\partial {F}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial \tau }$                                         eq 3.1-9

$\dot{p}=-\frac{\partial {F}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial A}$                                         eq 3.1-10

$\dot{f}=-\frac{\partial {F}^{\text{vp}}}{\partial B}$                                         eq 3.1-11

One assumes that ${F}^{\text{vp}}(\tau ,A,B)$ is a function of the *plastic* potential $F(\tau ,A,B)$ and that the latter decomposes into two terms depending respectively on the second invariant of $\tau$ coupled to $A$ and on the first invariant of $\tau$ coupled to $B$ :

${F}^{\text{vp}}=\Lambda (F)=\Lambda ({F}_{\text{vM}}({\tau}_{\text{eq}},A)+{F}_{m}({\tau}_{m},B))$                     eq 3.1-12

By hypothesis, the first term decomposes additively like the von Mises potential:

${F}_{\text{vM}}({\tau}_{\text{eq}},A)={\tau}_{\text{eq}}-A(p)-{R}_{0}={\tau}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)$                 eq 3.1-13

In order not to obtain a trivial result, the decomposition of the second term must be multiplicative:

${F}_{m}({\tau}_{m},B)=g({\tau}_{m})h(B)$                                     eq 3.1-14

Taking account of equation [eq 1-1], the evolution laws for $\text{tr}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ and $\dot{f}$ lead to the equality:

$\frac{g'({\tau}_{m})}{g({\tau}_{m})}=(\frac{-1}{1-f})\frac{{h}^{'}(B(f))}{h(B(f))}$                                 eq 3.1-15

The two sides of this equation are functions of the two independent variables ${\tau}_{m}$ and $f$ , so they are equal to a constant of dimension the inverse of a stress; this is the material parameter $1/{\sigma}_{1}$ . The dimensionless parameter ${D}_{1}$ appears in the integration of $g'/g$ :

$g({\tau}_{m})={D}_{1}{\sigma}_{1}\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})$                                     eq 3.1-16

The function $B(f)$ and the inverse function $f={h}_{1}(B)$ are unknown. The simplest and most natural choice is to take ${h}_{1}\equiv h$ , which gives:

$h(B)\equiv {h}_{1}(B)=f$                                         eq 3.1-17

${h}^{'}(B)=\frac{\mathrm{df}}{\text{dB}}=-\frac{1}{{\sigma}_{1}}f(1-f)$                                 eq 3.1-18

The plastic potential is finally written:

$F={\tau}_{\text{eq}}+{\sigma}_{1}{D}_{1}f\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})-R(p)$                         eq 3.1-19

The evolution law for $\dot{p}$ gives:

$\dot{p}=\frac{d\Lambda (F)}{\text{dF}}=V(F)$                                         eq 3.1-20

The function $V(F)$ defines the viscosity of the material. One will consider only the particular case such that:

$V(F)={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}{\left[\text{sh}(\frac{F}{{\sigma}_{0}})\right]}^{m}$                                     eq 3.1-21

which reduces to a power function (Norton-type law) when the two material parameters ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}$ and ${\sigma}_{0}$ are very large. Inversely one has:

$F-S(\dot{p})=0$                                             eq 3.1-22

$S(\dot{p})={\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\dot{p}}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]$                                     eq 3.1-23

In the case of time-independent plasticity, the previous equation becomes $F=0$ (plasticity criterion or threshold) and $\dot{p}$ is given by the consistency equation $\dot{F}=0$ if $F=0$ and $\dot{p}=0$ if $F<0$ .

The equations of the model are now completely defined, in the case without nucleation of new cavities. In the case of nucleation of new cavities, one considers that the volume fraction created is proportional to the cumulative plastic strain. It is therefore sufficient to replace $f$ by $f+{A}_{n}p$ in the equations of the model. ${A}_{n}$ is a material parameter. Equation [eq 1-1] is not modified.

### 3.2 Equations of the model

One summarises the equations of the model deduced from the thermodynamic and phenomenological analysis which precedes:

${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}={\tau}_{\text{eq}}+{\sigma}_{1}{D}_{1}(f+{A}_{n}p)\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})-R(p)-{\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\dot{p}}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]=0$ **eq 3.2-1**

$\tau =\frac{\sigma}{\rho}=\left[\lambda (\mathrm{Id}\otimes \mathrm{Id})+2\mu \mathrm{II}\right]:{\varepsilon}^{e}$                                 eq 3.2-2

$\rho =\frac{1-f-{A}_{n}p}{1-{f}_{0}}$                                             eq 3.2-3

${\tilde{\dot{\varepsilon}}}^{p}=\dot{p}\frac{3\tilde{\sigma}}{2{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}=\dot{p}\frac{3\tilde{\tau}}{2{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$                                         eq 3.2-4

$\mathrm{tr}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{p}{D}_{1}(f+{A}_{n}p)\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})$                                 eq 3.2-5

$\dot{f}={A}_{1}(1-f)\mathrm{tr}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$                                         eq 3.2-6

with ${A}_{1}=1$ , this parameter being introduced solely for numerical reasons.

## 4 Numerical formulation

### 4.1 Keywords, material data and internal variables

With a view to the foreseeable applications, the model has been implemented under two distinct keywords: ‘ROUSS_PR‘ for the plastic model with cavity nucleation or ‘ROUSS_VISC‘ for the viscoplastic model without nucleation. This makes it possible to avoid unnecessary numerical computations. The corresponding simplified equations are obtained from the general equations by setting respectively ${\sigma}_{0}=0$ or ${A}_{n}=0$ .

The whole set of parameters of the model is supplied under the factor keywords ‘ROUSSELIER‘ or ‘ROUSSELIER_FO‘ and ‘TRACTION‘ (in order to define the tension curve) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU ([U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)). The parameters of the viscoplastic model (${\sigma}_{0}$ , ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}$ and $m$ ) are supplied by the keyword ‘VISC_SINH‘.

The internal variables produced in *Code_Aster*  are:

- V1, the cumulative plastic strain $p$ ,
- V2, the porosity $f$ ,
- V3 to V8, the elastic strain tensor ${\varepsilon}^{e}$ ,
- V9, the plasticity indicator (0 if the last computed increment is elastic, 1 if regular plastic solution, 2 if singular plastic solution).

We now present the numerical integration of the constitutive law and give the expression for the tangent matrix (options FULL_MECA and RIGI_MECA_TANG).

### 4.2 Expression for the discretised model

The numerical solution is carried out by a $\theta$-method, with $0\le \theta \le 1$ , and in an incremental manner. For all the quantities $Q$ , one defines:

$Q={Q}^{-}+\Deltaq$

${Q}^{\theta}={Q}^{-}+\theta \Deltaq$

The incremental writing requires taking account of the possible variation of the material properties (owing, for example, to a change of temperature during the time step).

The discretised system of equations is:

${\tilde{\tau}}^{\theta}=2\mu \theta \Delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}^{e}}+\frac{2\mu \theta +(1-\theta )2{\mu}^{-}}{2{\mu}^{-}}\tilde{{\tau}^{-}}=2\mu \theta (\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{p})+\frac{2\mu \theta +(1-\theta )2{\mu}^{-}}{2{\mu}^{-}}\tilde{{\tau}^{-}}$         eq 4.2-1

${\tau}_{m}^{\theta}=K\theta \text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{e}+\frac{\mathrm{3K}\ast \theta +(1-\theta ){\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{\tau}_{m}^{-}=K\theta (\text{tr}\Delta \varepsilon -\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})\frac{\mathrm{3K}\ast \theta +(1-\theta ){\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{\tau}_{m}^{-}$     eq 4.2-2

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\Delta p\frac{3{\tilde{\tau}}^{\theta}}{2{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\theta}}$                                         eq 4.2-3

$\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta {\text{pD}}_{1}({f}^{\theta}+{A}_{n}{p}^{\theta})\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}^{\theta}}{{\sigma}_{1}})$                     eq 4.2-4

$\Deltaf ={A}_{1}(1-{f}^{\theta})\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$                                     eq 4.2-5

${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}={\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\theta}+{\sigma}_{1}{D}_{1}({f}^{\theta}+{A}_{n}{p}^{\theta})\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}^{\theta}}{{\sigma}_{1}})-R({p}^{\theta})-{\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\Delta p}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]=0$ **eq 4.2-6**

This system reduces to the solution of a single scalar equation for the unknown $\Deltaf$ , knowing $\Delta \varepsilon$ , $\Deltat$ and the quantities ${Q}^{-}$ . One notes that $\rho$ does not intervene in the algorithm, whereas it will intervene in the computation of the consistent tangent matrix. One computes successively:

${\tau}_{m}^{\theta}=\frac{\mathrm{3K}\ast \theta +(1-\theta ){\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{{\mathrm{3K}}^{-}}{\tau}_{{m}^{-}}+K\theta (\text{tr}\Delta \varepsilon -\frac{\Delta f}{{A}_{1}(1-{f}^{\theta})})$             eq 4.2-7

$\Deltap$ is the positive root of the second-degree equation:

${A}_{n}\theta {(\Delta p)}^{2}+({f}^{\theta}+{A}_{n}{p}^{\text{\_}})\Delta p-\frac{\Delta f}{{A}_{1}(1-{f}^{\theta})}\frac{1}{{D}_{1}\exp({\tau}_{m}^{\theta}/{\sigma}_{1})}=0$             eq 4.2-8

${\tilde{\tau}}^{\theta}=(1-\frac{3\mu \theta \Delta p}{{\left[\frac{2\mu \theta +(1-\theta )2{\mu}^{-}}{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\tau}}^{-}+2\mu \theta \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}\right]}_{\text{eq}}})(\frac{2\mu \theta +(1-\theta )2{\mu}^{-}}{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\tau}}^{-}+2\mu \theta \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon})$             eq 4.2-9

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\theta}={\left[\frac{2\mu \theta +(1-\theta )2{\mu}^{-}}{2{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\tau}}^{-}+2\mu \theta \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}\right]}_{\text{eq}}-3\mu \theta \Delta p$                 eq 4.2-10

The scalar equation for $\Deltaf$ is equation [eq 4.2-6] ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}=0$ .

**Note 1**:

*As* $\Deltaf$ *is very small in the greater part of the structure, it would be preferable to use* $\Deltap$ *as the principal unknown. But in this case it is not possible to reduce to a scalar equation, which makes the use of a Newton-type method more difficult. This is also one of the reasons why equations* [eq 1-1] *,* [eq 3.2-6] and [eq 4.2-5] *have not been modified by the introduction of cavity nucleation.*

**Note 2**:

*Equation* [eq 3.2-6] *can be integrated exactly:*

$\text{tr}{\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{1}{{A}_{1}}\ln(\frac{1-{f}_{0}}{1-f})$

*whence:*

$\text{tr}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{1}{{A}_{1}\theta }\ln(\frac{1-{f}^{-}}{1-{f}^{\theta}})$

*As the numerical parameter* ${A}_{1}$ *may be modified discontinuously, the derived form* [eq 4.2-5] *has been retained, including in the computation of the consistent tangent matrix. If the use of the parameter* ${A}_{1}$ *were to be abandoned in a later version, one would have to consider the use of the integrated form.*

**Note 3**:

*The integrated form* ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}=0$ *is used, including in plasticity instead of the consistency relation* $\dot{F}=0$ *which gives* $\dot{p}$ *. The consistent tangent matrix is computed with this integrated form.*

### 4.3 Solution of the non-linear scalar equation

The solution of the equation ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}(\Deltaf )=0$ is carried out by a Newton algorithm with controlled bounds in the routine LCROUS. ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}(\Deltaf )$ and its derivative with respect to $\Deltaf$ are computed in the routine RSLPHI called by LCROUS. The initial values of the bounds are:

- lower bound: $\Delta {f}_{1}=0$ since ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}(0)<0$ (one has checked beforehand that the elastic branch (negative threshold) is not the solution),
- upper bound: $\Delta {f}_{2}$ such that ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}(0)>0$ sought by bisection between 0 and $1-{f}^{-}$ (first value for this search: $\frac{1-{f}^{-}}{2}$ ).

The Newton algorithm starts with the value $\Deltaf =0$ . Whatever the value found for $\Deltaf$ one therefore notes for what follows that the function ${\Phi}_{\text{vp}}^{\theta}(\Deltaf )$ and its derivative with respect to $\Deltaf$ are computed at least for $\Deltaf =0$ and $\frac{1-{f}^{-}}{2}$ .

The developments carried out in order to improve the convergence and the robustness of the algorithm are described in [bib5].

### 4.4 Expression for the tangent matrix of the behaviour

One gives here the expression for the tangent matrix (option FULL_MECA during the Newton iterations, option RIGI_MECA_TANG for the first iteration).

For the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, the tangent operator is the same as that which relates ${\varepsilon}^{e}$ to $\sigma$ in [eq 3.2-2].

For the option FULL_MECA, the tangent matrix is obtained by linearising the system of equations which governs the constitutive law: [eq 4.2-1] to [eq 4.2-6]. It is therefore a *consistent* tangent matrix.

In order to simplify the expressions, one denotes in this paragraph [§4.5]: $Q$ for ${Q}^{\theta}$ , the quantities all being expressed at the time instant ${t}^{\theta}={t}^{-}+\theta \Deltat$ . The consistent tangent matrix is:

$\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=\rho \left[{a}_{3}\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{Id}\otimes (\frac{{a}_{1}-{a}_{3}}{3}\mathrm{Id}+{a}_{2}\tilde{\tau})+\tilde{\tau}\otimes ({a}_{4}\tilde{\tau}+\frac{{a}_{5}}{3}\mathrm{Id})+\tau \otimes ({y}_{4}(\frac{{a}_{1}}{\mathrm{3K}}-1)\mathrm{Id}+\frac{{y}_{5}}{\text{K}}\tilde{\tau})\right]$     eq 4.4-1

This operator is computed in the routine RSLJPL. The coefficients are computed as follows:

${a}_{1}=\mathrm{3K}+{y}_{1}K{\tau}_{\text{eq}}({z}_{7}+{z}_{2}\theta \Deltap )$                                 eq 4.4-2

${a}_{2}=\mu ({y}_{1}+{y}_{3}){\sigma}_{1}$                                             eq 4.4-3

${a}_{3}=\frac{2\mu {\tau}_{\text{eq}}}{{z}_{5}}$                                                     eq 4.4-4

${a}_{4}=3\mu {y}_{2}{x}_{2}$                                                 eq 4.4-5

${a}_{5}=3\mu {y}_{1}{\sigma}_{1}$                                                 eq 4.4-6

${a}_{6}=3\muk \theta \Deltap -{a}_{2}{\tau}_{\text{eq}}{\sigma}_{1}$                                         eq 4.4-7

${y}_{1}=-\frac{3{\text{Kz}}_{6}{z}_{1}(f+{A}_{n}p)}{{x}_{1}{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$                                     eq 4.4-8

${y}_{2}=-\frac{\mathrm{3\mu }}{{x}_{1}{z}_{5}{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}$                                                 eq 4.4-9

${y}_{3}=-\frac{3{\text{Kz}}_{6}{z}_{1}{A}_{n}\theta \Deltap }{{x}_{1}{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$                                         eq 4.4-10

${y}_{4}=\frac{{A}_{1}{z}_{8}}{{z}_{1}}+\frac{{z}_{9}{\sigma}_{1}}{{z}_{7}+{z}_{2}\theta \Deltap }$                                         eq 4.4-11

${y}_{5}=\frac{{A}_{1}{a}_{2}{z}_{8}}{{z}_{1}}-\frac{{z}_{9}{a}_{6}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}({z}_{7}+{z}_{2}\theta \Deltap )}$                                 eq 4.4-12

${z}_{1}=1+{A}_{1}\theta \Delta {\text{pD}}_{1}(f+{A}_{n}p)\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})$                             eq 4.4-13

${z}_{2}=\mathrm{3\mu }+{R}_{\text{vp}}$                                                 eq 4.4-14

${z}_{3}=K(f+{A}_{n}p){z}_{1}-{A}_{1}{\sigma}_{1}(1-f)$                             eq 4.4-15

${z}_{4}={R}_{\text{vp}}\theta \Deltap -{\tau}_{\text{eq}}$                                             eq 4.4-16

${z}_{5}={\tau}_{\text{eq}}+3\mu \theta \Deltap$                                             eq 4.4-17

${z}_{6}={D}_{1}\exp(\frac{{\tau}_{m}}{{\sigma}_{1}})$                                             eq 4.4-18

${z}_{7}={z}_{6}{\sigma}_{1}(f+{A}_{n}p)$                                         eq 4.4-19

${z}_{8}=\frac{1-f}{1-f-{A}_{n}p}$                                                 eq 4.4-20

${z}_{9}=\frac{{A}_{n}}{1-f-{A}_{n}p}$                                                 eq 4.4-21

${x}_{1}={z}_{3}{z}_{6}({z}_{7}+{z}_{2}\theta \Deltap )+{z}_{1}{z}_{2}{\sigma}_{1}-{x}_{3}$                                 eq 4.4-22

${x}_{2}=-{z}_{3}{z}_{6}\theta \Deltap ({z}_{4}+{z}_{7})-{z}_{1}{z}_{4}{\sigma}_{1}+{x}_{3}\theta \Deltap$                         eq 4.4-23

${x}_{3}={A}_{n}{z}_{1}{z}_{6}{\sigma}_{1}^{2}$                                                     eq 4.4-24

${R}_{\text{vp}}=\frac{\text{dR}(p)}{\text{dp}}+\frac{1}{\theta \Deltat }\frac{\text{dS}(\Deltap /\Deltat )}{d\dot{p}}$                                     eq 4.4-25

For the plastic model with cavity nucleation ‘ROUSSELIER_PR‘ and for the viscoplastic model without nucleation ‘ROUSSELIER_VISC‘, the corresponding simplified equations are obtained from the equations above by setting respectively ${R}_{\text{vp}}=\text{dR}(p)/\text{dp}$ and ${A}_{n}=0$ .
