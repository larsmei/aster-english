# v7.12.303 HSLA303 - Pressure Cylinder and Thermal Dilation

**Summary:**

The calculation is done in axisymmetric. The purpose of the test is to validate pre-deformations (keyword PRE_EPSI).

The cylinder is subjected to homogeneous thermal expansion ($\Delta T$ constant).

The procedure followed is as follows:

- either ${\varepsilon}_{1}$ the deformation field resulting from a first calculation, the cylinder being subjected to a homogeneous thermal expansion $\Delta T$ (${U}_{1}$ the resulting displacement field),
- in a second calculation, the cylinder is subjected to internal pressure, with the deformation field ${\varepsilon}_{1}$ (i.e. ${U}_{2}$ the resulting displacement field) as a pre-deformation,
- The results are then compared with the $U$ field , obtained with pressurized cylinder but without pre-deformations. We must have the relation: ${U}_{2}=U+{U}_{1}$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

- The deformation due to pressure alone is given by:

${\varepsilon}_{zz}=\frac{(1-2\nu )(2{R}_{e}-h)}{\mathrm{4Eh}}p=3.714\times {10}^{-3}$ , ${R}_{e}=$ external radius

- The axial displacement due to pressure is given by:

${U}_{z}=Z{\varepsilon}_{zz}$

- The deformations due to thermal loading are:

${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}={\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }={\varepsilon}_{zz}=\alpha \Delta T=1.2\times {10}^{-3}$

- The radial displacement due to thermal loading is:

${U}_{r}=r{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}=1.2\times {10}^{-3}r$

### 2.2 Baseline

- Radial and axial deformation and displacement at $A,B,C,D$ points due to thermal loading.
- Deformation and axial displacement at $A,B,C,D$ points due to pressure.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

### 3.4 Remarks

- The purpose of the test is not to obtain a high precision in the results, but simply to verify the relationship: ${U}_{2}=U+{U}_{1}$; therefore, the calculation was only made with a coarse mesh.
- The relationship sought is well verified at the free end of the cylinder.
- The deformation field resulting from thermal expansion is also verified to be equal to $1.2\times {10}^{-3}$.

## 4 Summary of the results

The PRE_EPSI option (pre-deformations in constant) provides quite satisfactory results.
