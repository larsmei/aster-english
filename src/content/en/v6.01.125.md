# v6.01.125 SSNA125 - Near-incompressible hollow sphere with internal and external pressure

**Summary:**

A sphere modelled in axisymmetry is considered to be subjected to internal and external pressures. The calculation is linearly elastic. The simulation is compared to an analytical solution.

This test validates elements AXIS in almost uncompressible.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.01.125/100002010000024500000280E2B57DFCABC6078A.png)

The sphere has an inside ${R}_{i}=1m$ radius and an external ${R}_{e}=10m$ radius.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material features are $E=10^5\mathit{Pa}$ and $\nu =0.4999$. The constitutive law is linear elastic.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

The following conditions are imposed:

- An imposed pressure of $300\mathit{Pa}$ on the internal surface of the ${R}_{i}$ $\sigma .{e}_{r}(r={R}_{i})={P}_{i}{e}_{r}$ sphere;
- An imposed pressure of $150\mathit{Pa}$ on the external ${R}_{e}$ CD-ray surface of the $\sigma .{e}_{r}(r={R}_{e})=-{P}_{e}{e}_{r}$ sphere;
- Symmetrical conditions: $\mathit{DY}=0$ on BD, $\mathit{DX}=0$ on AC.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The spherical coordinate field is written as:

$U(r,\theta ,\varphi )={u}_{r}(r,\theta ,\varphi ){e}_{r}+{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta ,\varphi ){u}_{\theta}+{u}_{\varphi}(r,\theta ,\varphi ){e}_{\varphi}$

In our case, a pressure is applied only according to the direction ${e}_{r}$. So, we can write the displacement field in the form:

$U(r)={u}_{r}(r){e}_{r}$

The equation of Lamé Navier for this problem is written:

$\rho f=(\lambda +2\mu )\nabla \wedge (\nabla \wedge u)+\rho b$

On a: $f=0$ et $b=0$ .

The displacement field solution of this equation is of the form:

$U(r)=({C}_{1}r+{C}_{2}/{r}^{2}){e}_{r}$

Boundary conditions allows to obtain the expression of constants:

${C}_{2}=\frac{(-{P}_{i}+{P}_{e}){R}_{e}^{3}{R}_{i}^{3}}{4\mu ({R}_{i}^{3}-{R}_{e}^{3})}$

${C}_{1}=\frac{1}{3\lambda +2\mu }(-{P}_{i}+\frac{4\mu {C}_{2}}{{R}_{i}^{3}})$

The strain tensor is written,

${ϵ}_{\mathit{rr}}={C}_{1}-\frac{{\mathrm{2C}}_{2}}{{r}^{3}}$

${ϵ}_{\theta \theta }={C}_{1}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{r}^{3}}$

${ϵ}_{\varphi \varphi }={C}_{1}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{r}^{3}}$

${ϵ}_{\theta \varphi }={ϵ}_{\theta r}={ϵ}_{r\varphi }=0$

and the stress tensor is written:

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}={\mathrm{3C}}_{1}\lambda +2\mu ({C}_{1}-\frac{{\mathrm{2C}}_{2}}{{r}^{3}})$

${\sigma}_{\theta \theta }={\mathrm{3C}}_{1}\lambda +2\mu ({C}_{1}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{r}^{3}})$

${\sigma}_{\varphi \varphi }={\mathrm{3C}}_{1}\lambda +2\mu ({C}_{1}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{r}^{3}})$

${\sigma}_{\theta \varphi }={\sigma}_{\theta r}={\sigma}_{r\varphi }=0$

The boundary conditions that allows to obtain the ${C}_{1},{C}_{2}$ coefficients are then:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}({R}_{i})=-{P}_{i}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}({R}_{e})=-{P}_{e}\end{array}$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The stress trace at Gauss points is compared with the analytical solution $trace(\sigma)=-449.54954955 \mathrm{Pa}$.
All numeric values are given in SI unit.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

> There is no uncertainty about the solution, as it is analytical.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

A.F. Bower, *Applied Mechanics of Solids* , Taylor and Francis, 2010.
