# r7.01.03 Constitutive law BETON_DOUBLE_DP with a double Drucker-Prager criterion for the cracking and the compression of concrete

**Summary:**

The model presented in this document, constitutive behaviour BETON_DOUBLE_DP, is a non-linear constitutive law for concrete. It is based on the theory of plasticity; it is valid for three-dimensional stress states. The modelling assumptions adopted are the following:

- a reversibility domain of the stresses bounded by two criteria of Drucker-Prager type,
- a hardening of each of the criteria,
- in compression, a positive hardening up to a peak, then a negative hardening,
- in tension, a negative hardening exclusively,
- a dependence of the shape of the post-peak curves in both cases (tension/compression) on the size of the finite element (the shape of this curve is linked to the negative hardening and to the fracture energy),
- normal plastic flow rules (associated plasticity) and an isotropic hardening formulation,
- the taking into account of the dependence of the elasticity thresholds on temperature,
- the taking into account of the dependence of Young's modulus on temperature.

## 2 Introduction

### 2.1 Main characteristics of the model

The model presented in this document is a non-linear constitutive law for concrete. It is based on the theory of plasticity; it is valid for three-dimensional stress states. The modelling assumptions adopted take up in part the models developed by G. Heinfling [bib2] and J. F. Georgin [bib1] and are the following:

- there exists a reversibility domain of the stresses bounded by two criteria of Drucker-Prager type,
- each of the criteria hardens; the failure domain corresponds to the maximum of the reversibility domain,
- in compression, the hardening is positive up to a peak, then it becomes negative,
- in tension, the hardening is negative exclusively,
- the post-peak curves in both cases (tension/compression) vary with the size of the finite element (the shape of this curve is linked to the negative hardening and to the fracture energy),
- the plastic flow is governed by a normality rule (associated plasticity); the formulation of the hardenings is of isotropic type,
- the elasticity modulus and the reversibility thresholds vary with temperature.

**Note**:

*The terminology of tension criterion and compression criterion is open to discussion. We shall use it out of habit, being* *well aware that a tensile stress state can lead to the activation of the so-called compression criterion.*

### 2.2 Why two Drucker-Prager criteria

The authors of the theses cited in references [bib1] and [bib2] use a Drucker-Prager criterion in compression and a Rankine criterion in tension. They justify these choices by physical considerations, showing that the reversibility domain thus obtained is close to experimental reality. On the other hand they limit their modellings to two-dimensional stress states. We have preferred to replace the tension criterion by a surface also of Drucker-Prager type. By this choice, one is freed from certain difficulties, particularly in the three-dimensional formulations. The 3D surface defining the stress states admissible with respect to tension is no longer a pyramid (Rankine 3D) but a conical surface whose apex lies on the hydrostatic axis. The trace of the “so-called tension” criterion on the deviatoric plane is no longer a triangle, but a circle. The formulation obtained is simpler. The difference between the two criteria is slight for stress states close to plane-stress states. On the other hand, for strongly confined stress states, the two approaches (Rankine and Drucker-Prager) differ, which is a limitation of the proposed model.

### 3.2 Mathematical expression of the reversibility domain

It is defined by the inequality:

$f(\sigma ,A)\le 0$ **eq 3.2‑** 3.2-1

in which $A$ represents the thermodynamic forces associated with the internal variables (we denote by $\alpha$ the set of the internal variables).

For the concrete model that we present here, equation [ eq 3.2- 3.2-1 ] takes the particular form

${f}_{\text{comp}}(\sigma ,{A}_{c})=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{oct}}+a.{\sigma}_{\text{oct}}}{b}-\phi {f}_{c}^{'}+{A}_{c}\le 0$ **eq 3.2‑** 3.2-2

${f}_{\text{trac}}(\sigma ,{A}_{t})=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{oct}}+c.{\sigma}_{\text{oct}}}{d}-{f}_{t}^{'}+{A}_{t}\le 0$ **eq 3.2‑** 3.2-3

${f}_{{}_{\text{comp}}}^{H}(\sigma ,{A}_{c})=\frac{a.{\sigma}_{\text{oct}}}{b}-\phi {f}_{c}^{'}+{A}_{c}\le 0$ **eq 3.2‑** 3.2-4

${f}_{{}_{\text{trac}}}^{H}(\sigma ,{A}_{t})=\frac{c.{\sigma}_{\text{oct}}}{d}-{f}_{t}^{'}+{A}_{t}\le 0$ **eq 3.2‑** 3.2-5

Equations [ eq 3.2- 3.2-2 ] and [ eq 3.2- 3.2-3 ] correspond respectively to the “compression” and “tension” thresholds. Equations [ eq 3.2- 3.2-4 ] and [ eq 3.2- 3.2-5 ] limit the reversibility threshold in the domain of isotropic tension; they amount to excluding the abscissa axis in [Figure 3.1-b] beyond the points ${P}_{t}$ or ${P}_{c}$ . It is clear that only one of these last two conditions is sufficient. For the unhardened material, the choice of the coefficients is such that ${\mathrm{OP}}_{t}<{\mathrm{OP}}_{c}$ and condition [ eq 3.2- 3.2-5 ] implies [ eq 3.2- 3.2-4 ].

We shall see later that the hardening can reverse the order of the points ${P}_{t}$ and ${P}_{c}$ , making condition [ eq 3.2- 3.2-4 ] more constraining than [ eq 3.2- 3.2-5 ].

### 3.3 Failure criterion. Choice of the coefficients a, b, c and d

When the stress state reaches the boundary of the reversibility domain, plastic strains develop and the thresholds move: they harden. The compression threshold “enlarges” at first, then decreases, whereas the tension threshold can only decrease. The failure threshold corresponds to the maximum domain that can be reached; it is represented in [Figure 3.3-a] in a plane-stress diagram:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_50.svg)

The hardening of the thresholds is expressed mathematically by the evolution of the quantities ${A}_{c}$ and ${A}_{t}$  , the failure thresholds corresponding to the maxima of the functions ${f}_{c}=\phi {f}_{c}'-{A}_{c}$ and ${f}_{t}={f}_{t}'-{A}_{t}$ . In the models retained, these functions are such that: $\mathrm{Max}{f}_{c}={f}_{c}'$ and $\mathrm{Max}{f}_{t}={f}_{t}^{'}$ ;

### 3.5 Hardening

For our model, there are two internal variables; they are denoted ${\kappa}_{c}^{p}$ for the internal variable “said to be of compression” and ${\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ for that “said to be of tension”. #### 3.5.2 Hardening curves and post-peak moduli

##### 3.5.2.1 Smeared-cracking model

The introduction of a post-peak softening behaviour into the stress–strain relations poses a major problem. Under static loading, beyond a certain stress level, corresponding to the onset of the softening behaviour, the equations governing the equilibrium of the structure lose their elliptic nature. These equations of the mechanical problem then form an ill-posed system of partial differential equations whose number of solutions is multiple. This problem results in a non-objectivity with respect to the mesh. There follows a pathological sensitivity of the numerical solution to the fineness and to the orientation of the mesh.

In order to solve this problem, or at least to limit its consequences on the reliability of the predicted solution, it is necessary to use so-called regularisation techniques. The object of these techniques is to enrich the mechanical description of the medium, so as to be able to describe non-homogeneous strain states, and to preserve the mathematical nature of the problem. One carries out this regularisation by introducing, into the constitutive law, a characteristic length or internal length, related to the width of the localisation zone. Several techniques are possible for improving the mechanical description of the softening medium. They constitute localisation limiters. The implementation of these techniques generally requires delicate numerical developments. An intermediate approach between the use of the classical models and the implementation of these localisation limiters consists in making the post-peak slope of the stress–strain relation depend on the size of the element, so as to dissipate a constant energy at failure. This approach constitutes a step towards a non-local description of the continuous medium.

Let us first consider a real crack of surface $S$ whose measure is A [Figure 3.5.2.1-a]. $S$ is a surface of discontinuity of the displacement field $u$ . One assumes that, to create this discontinuity, one must expend an energy $W$ whose expression is: $\text{W=}{\int}_{S}{G}_{f}(x)\text{dS}$ , ${G}_{f}$ being a property of the material.

Let us now consider that one wishes to represent the same phenomenon, by representing not a displacement discontinuity but a plastic strain uniformly distributed in a volume $V$ .

The energy dissipated will be: $\text{W=}{\int}_{v}\text{dV}{\int}_{0}^{{t}_{r}}{\sigma}^{ij}\frac{d{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{p}}{\mathrm{dt}}\mathrm{dt}$ , where one has denoted

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_153.svg)

the “time to failure”.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/100032D6000069BB0000300F91B95DBCD515C472.svg)

**Figure** **3.5.2.1-a**

Making the following series of assumptions:

- the crack is plane,
- ${G}_{f}$ is constant along the crack and therefore $\text{W=}A.{G}_{f}$ ,
- $V$ is a cylinder of base $S$ and of height ${L}_{\text{ver}}$ ,
- ${g}_{f=}{\int}_{0}^{{t}_{t}}{\sigma}^{ij}\frac{{\mathrm{d\varepsilon }}_{ij}^{p}}{\text{dt}}\text{dt}$ is constant in $V$ .

One finally arrives at the relation:

$W={\text{Vg}}_{f}=V{\int}_{0}^{{t}_{t}}{\sigma}^{ij}\frac{{\mathrm{d\varepsilon }}_{ij}^{p}}{\text{dt}}\text{dt}=A.{G}_{f}$ **eq 3.5.2.1-1**

Or again:

${g}^{f}={\int}_{0}^{{t}_{r}}{\sigma}^{ij}\frac{d{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{p}}{\mathrm{dt}}\mathrm{dt}=\frac{{G}_{f}}{{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}}$ **eq 3.5.2.1-2**

One easily sees that: ${g}^{f}={\int}_{0}^{{\kappa}_{u}}f(\kappa )\mathrm{d\kappa }$ , a writing in which the quantities $({g}^{f},f,{\kappa}_{u},\kappa )$ represent respectively $({g}_{t}^{f},{f}_{t},{\kappa}_{t}^{u},{\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ in tension and $({g}_{c}^{f},{f}_{c},{\kappa}_{c}^{u},{\kappa}_{c}^{p})$ in compression. The datum of ${g}^{f}$ therefore determines ${k}_{u}$ , both in tension and in compression:

${g}_{c}^{f}={\int}_{0}^{{k}_{c}^{u}}f({\kappa}_{c}^{p}){\mathrm{d\kappa }}_{c}^{p}=\frac{{G}_{c}^{f}}{{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}}$

${g}_{t}^{f}={\int}_{0}^{{k}_{t}^{u}}f({\kappa}_{t}^{p}){\mathrm{d\kappa }}_{t}^{p}=\frac{{G}_{t}^{f}}{{L}_{\mathrm{ver}}}$

The quantity ${g}^{f}$ is therefore linked to the slope of the post-peak curve in a stress–hardening-variable diagram, which is linked to the post-peak slope in a stress–strain diagram.

Suppose for example that the stress–strain relation is linear in the post-peak regime. Let us call ${E}_{T}<0$ the post-peak slope in the diagram $(\sigma ,\varepsilon )$ and $h<0$ the corresponding slope in the diagram $(f,\kappa )$ [Figure 3.5.2.1-b]. One has the relations $h=\frac{{\text{EE}}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}\iff {E}_{T}=\frac{\text{hE}}{E+h}$ which show that one must have:

$-h<E$ , failing which the diagram $(\sigma ,\varepsilon )$ presents a snap-back.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/100019A6000069BB00002B3414D1DFA66CC7357A.svg)

**Figure** **3.5.2.1-b**

The condition $-h<E$ is called the applicability condition; it will be expressed by an inequality on ${g}^{f}$ and therefore on ${L}_{\text{ver}}$ .

Within the framework of a solution by the finite-element method, the representative elementary volume of the cracked medium can be assimilated to an element of the mesh. The characteristic length (denoted hereafter ${l}_{c}$ ) introduced in the equivalent-fracture-energy method corresponds to the length $\mathit{Lver}$ . During an analysis corresponding to an arbitrary structure, the determination of this characteristic length is delicate. It depends on the position of the crack plane, on the dimensions and on the type of the elements ...

A simple estimate for the two-dimensional cases can be expressed in the form:

${l}_{c}=r\sqrt{{A}_{e}}$ where ${A}_{e}$ is the area of the element considered, and $r$ , a correction factor, equal to 1 for the quadratic elements, and $\sqrt{2}$ for the linear elements.

One can extend this formulation to the 3D case: ${l}_{c}=r\sqrt[3]{{V}_{e}}$ where ${V}_{e}$ denotes the volume of the element.

Concerning the evolution of the hardening with temperature, we consider, as in [bib2], that the fracture energies and the failure strengths depend not on the current temperature

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_185.svg)

of the material point considered at time $t$ , but on the maximum temperature reached at that point from the beginning of the loading up to time $t$ . When we need to show the dependence of the quantities on temperature, we shall denote:

- $\theta$ the maximum of the temperature from the beginning of the loading,
- ${f}_{c}^{'}(\theta )$ the compressive strength,
- ${f}_{t}^{'}(\theta )$ denotes the tensile strength,
- ${f}_{c}(\theta ,{\kappa}_{c}^{p})$ the hardening curve in compression,
- ${f}_{t}(\theta ,{\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ the hardening curve in tension.

##### 3.5.2.2 Behaviour of concrete in tension and linear post-peak curve

In this modelling, the concrete is assumed elastic up to its tensile strength ${f}_{t}^{'}$ . The curve ${f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ in tension is represented in [Figure 3.5.1-c] and is entirely defined by the tensile strength of the material, the fracture energy ${G}_{t}^{f}$ , and the characteristic length ${l}_{c}$ .

The mathematical expression of this curve is:

${f}_{t}(\theta ,{\kappa}_{t}^{p})={f}_{t}^{'}(\theta )(1-\frac{{\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{{\kappa}_{t}^{u}(\theta )})$ **eq 3.5.2.2-1**

The equivalence of the dissipated energy makes it possible to write:

## 4 Plastic flow

In this paragraph, we give the expression of the plastic strain rates, distinguishing the so-called general case where the stress state lies on a “regular” zone of the boundary of the reversibility domain and the case where it lies at the apex of one of the cones.

### 4.1 General form of the normality rule

In the space $(\sigma ,A)$ , the inequalities [ eq 3.2- 3.2-2 ], [ eq 3.2- 3.2-3 ], [ eq 3.2- 3.2-4 ], [ eq 3.2- 3.2-5 ], define a convex domain that we shall denote ${C}_{(\sigma ,A)}$ . We shall denote by ${\Psi}_{c}$ the indicator function of this convex set:

${\psi}_{c}(\sigma ,A)=\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}0\text{si}(\sigma ,A)\in {C}_{(\sigma ,A)}\\ \infty \text{sinon}\end{array}$                             eq 4.1‑ 4.1-1

When the boundary of the reversibility domain is reached, irreversible plastic strains develop, according to the classical theory of plasticity.

For a standard material, [bib4] the flow law satisfies the principle of maximum plastic work, which is expressed by the equation:

$({\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p},\dot{\alpha})\in \partial {\Psi}_{c}$ **eq 4.1‑** 4.1-2

where $\partial {\Psi}_{c}$ denotes the subdifferential of the function ${\Psi}_{c}$ . We recall [bib3] that the subdifferential of a convex function at a point $x$ is the set of the vectors $z$ such that: $f({x}^{\text{*}})\ge f(x)+\langle z,{x}^{\text{*}}-x\rangle \forall {x}^{\text{*}}$

One then easily sees that [ eq 4.1- 4.1-2 ] implies:

${\Psi}_{c}({\sigma}^{\text{*}},{A}^{\text{*}})\ge {\Psi}_{c}(\sigma ,A)+{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}({\sigma}^{\text{*}}-\sigma )+\dot{\alpha}({A}^{\text{*}}-A)\forall {\sigma}^{\text{*}}\mathrm{et}{A}^{\text{*}}$ **eq 4.1‑** 4.1-3

Taking into account the definition of the characteristic function, one easily sees that [ :ref:`          eq 4.1- 4.1-3  <Ref523040324>` ] is equivalent to:

> ${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}\sigma +\dot{\alpha}A\ge {\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}{\sigma}^{\text{*}}+\dot{\alpha}{A}^{\text{*}}\forall {\sigma}^{\text{*}}\mathrm{et}{A}^{\text{*}}\in {C}_{(\sigma ,A)}$ **eq 4.1‑** 4.1-4

In other words the plastic flow is such that the pair $(\sigma ,A)$ realises the maximum of the plastic dissipation among the admissible thermodynamic forces.

### 4.2 Expression of the plastic flow in the regular part

When the function $f$ is differentiable at the point considered $(\sigma ,A)$ the normality rule is written simply

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }$ **eq 4.2‑** 4.2-1

$\dot{\alpha}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial f}{\partial A}$ **eq 4.2‑** 4.2-2

$\dot{\lambda}$ and $f$ satisfying the Kuhn–Tucker conditions:

$\begin{array}{c}\dot{\lambda}\ge 0\\ f\le 0\\ \dot{\lambda}.f=0\end{array}\rbrace$ **eq 4.2‑** 4.2-3

The hardening variable is linked to the plastic multiplier by the hardening law. Using the plastic work, one can write: $\dot{\kappa}f=\sigma {\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}$ .

If $f$ is a function homogeneous of order 1 with respect to the tensorial variable $\sigma$ , one has $\sigma \frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }=f$ , which leads to the equality: $\dot{\lambda}=\dot{\kappa}$ and therefore finally to the equations:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{c}^{p}=\dot{{\kappa}_{c}^{p}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathit{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }$ **eq 4.2‑** 4.2-4

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p}=\dot{{\kappa}_{t}^{p}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }$ **eq 4.2‑** 4.2-5

### 4.3 Expression of the plastic flow at the apex of a cone

We give two presentations of the same result. The first presentation uses the theory of generalised standard materials and the subdifferentials; the second starts from an equality posed a priori on the plastic work.

#### 4.3.1 Demonstration by the general theory of standard materials

The domain ${C}_{(\sigma ,A)}$  consists of two cones. The function ${\Psi}_{c}$ is not differentiable either at the intersection of these two cones, or at the apex of each of these cones. When the point $(\sigma ,\mathrm{A})$ belongs to the intersection of the two cones, the previous equations remain valid, with the precision that the compressive and tensile plastic strains develop at the same time. This so-called “multi-criterion” case is moreover treated in [bib4]. We shall content ourselves here with treating the case where $(\sigma ,A)$ is at the apex of a cone, and we shall choose the most frequent case of the apex of the tension cone, knowing that the case of the apex of the compression cone is treated in exactly the same way.

The criteria are rewritten using the variables ${\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}$ and ${\sigma}_{H}$ , which are more practical in the analytical developments.

${f}_{\text{trac}}(\sigma ,{A}_{t})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3d}}{\sigma}^{\text{eq}}+\frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}-{f}_{t}^{'}+{A}_{t}\le 0$                     4.3.1-1

${f}_{{}_{\text{trac}}}^{H}(\sigma ,{A}_{t})=\frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}-{f}_{t}^{'}+{A}_{t}\le 0$ **4.3.1-2**

We therefore consider a case where:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}^{\text{eq}}=0\\ \frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}-{f}_{t}^{'}+{A}_{t}=0\end{array}\rbrace$ **4.3.1-3**

Starting from [ eq 4.1- 4.1-4 ], we shall compute the plastic dissipation as being the maximum of ${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}{\sigma}^{\text{*}}+\dot{\alpha}{A}^{\text{*}}$ for all the pairs ${\sigma}^{\text{*}},{A}^{\text{*}}\in {C}_{(\sigma ,A)}$

#### 4.3.2 Demonstration by the plastic work

The starting point is to consider that, relative to the developments carried out at regular points, it is essentially the relations [eq 4.2-1] and [eq 4.2-2], called normality rules, that can no longer be written. Now the relation [eq 4.2-1] implies the equality ${\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\sigma \dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}$ , which can, for its part, be maintained.

We shall therefore start from the equation:

${\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\sigma \dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}$ **eq 4.3.2-1**

${\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\dot{{\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{p}}s+3{\sigma}_{H}\dot{{\varepsilon}_{H}}$ **eq 4.3.2-2**

At the apex of the tension cone, one has the relations [eq 4.3.1-3], which, substituted into [eq 4.3.2-2], give, also using [ eq 3.5- 3.5-2 ]:

${\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})=3\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})\dot{{\varepsilon}_{H}}$ **eq 4.3.2-3**

And one therefore recovers the relation [eq 4.3.1-12]: . $3\dot{{\varepsilon}_{H}}=\frac{c}{d}{\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}$

## 5 Numerical integration of the constitutive law

### 5.1 The global problem and the local problem: reminders

For a given structure (geometry and material), and for a given loading, the displacement, stress and internal-variable fields are found by solving a set of non-linear partial differential equations formed from the equilibrium equations and the constitutive laws. Document [bib5] presents the algorithm of which we give a summary here:

${\text{u}}_{i-1}$ known; computation of the prediction $\Delta {\text{u}}_{i}^{0}$

*Newton equilibrium iterations n*

${\text{u}}_{i}^{n}$ known; $\Delta {\text{u}}_{i}^{n}={\text{u}}_{i}^{n}-{\text{u}}_{i-1}$

*Loop over elements el*

*Loop over Gauss points g*

With the loading condition of imposed strain:

The result of this integration will supply:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{{g}_{i}}^{{\mathrm{el}}^{n}}=\sigma ({t}_{i})\\ {\varepsilon}_{{g}_{i}}^{{p}^{{\mathrm{el}}^{n}}}={\varepsilon}^{p}({t}_{i})\\ {\alpha}_{{g}_{i}}^{{\mathrm{el}}^{n}}=\alpha ({t}_{i})\end{array}$

The object of this chapter is to present the numerical integration of these equations. It is a system of non-linear differential equations that we solve by an implicit Euler method. From now on, the quantities at the beginning of the time step (known) will be denoted with a superscript , whereas the unknowns at the end of the time step (all unknown except $\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{{g}_{i}}^{{\mathrm{el}}^{n}}$ ) will be denoted without a subscript. For an arbitrary quantity $a$ one denotes $\Deltaa =a-{a}^{-}$ .

One always begins by computing an elastic solution ${\sigma}^{e}$ , assuming that there is no evolution of the plastic strains and of the internal variables. If at least one of the criteria is violated by this elastic solution, plastic flows must be computed. One must then distinguish the regular cases, for which the solution $\sigma$ lies on the regular part of one of the cones or at their intersection, from the so-called singular cases where the solution $\sigma$ is at the apex of one of the two cones. The logic making it possible to examine and to choose these different cases, and the algorithm that follows from it, are relatively complex. We therefore present first the treatment of each of the cases and explain their sequencing afterwards, in chapter [§ 5.7 ].

### 5.2 Numerical treatment of the regular case.

One presents in detail only the case where plastic strains develop both in tension and in compression and where therefore the solution $\sigma$ belongs to the intersection of the two cones. Let us note however that, even if ${\sigma}^{e}$ violates both criteria, the final solution $\sigma$ may very well finally belong to only one of the hardened cones. One is therefore led to seek solutions of which one postulates that they belong to one of the two cones or to both. The case where it belongs to only one of the two cones is easily deduced from the more general case presented here. The equations that we have to solve are finally:

${s}^{e}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{s}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-1

${\sigma}_{{H}^{e}}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{\sigma}_{{H}^{-}}+{\mathrm{3K\Delta \varepsilon }}_{H}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-2

$s={s}^{e}-2\mu (\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{c}^{p}+\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p})$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-3

${\sigma}_{H}={\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\mathrm{3K}({\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{{H}_{c}}^{p}+{\mathrm{\Delta \varepsilon }}_{{H}_{t}}^{p})$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-4

${f}_{\mathrm{comp}}(\sigma ,{\kappa}_{c}^{p})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3b}}{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}+\frac{a}{b}{\sigma}_{H}-{f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{p})=0$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-5

> $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{c}^{p}=\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-6

${f}_{\mathrm{trac}}(\sigma ,{\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3d}}{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}+\frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}-{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})=0$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-7

$\Delta {\varepsilon}_{t}^{p}=\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-8

Taking the isotropic and deviatoric parts of the plastic strains, equations [eq 5.2-6] and [ eq 5.2- 5.2-8 ] give:

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{c}^{p}=\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}b}\frac{s}{{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-9

$\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{H}_{c}}^{p}=\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\frac{a}{\mathrm{3b}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-10

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p}=\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}d}\frac{s}{{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-11

$\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{H}_{t}}^{p}=\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}\frac{c}{\mathrm{3d}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-12

Substituting [eq 5.2-9] and [eq 5.2-11] into [ eq 5.2- 5.2-3 ], one finds:

$s={s}^{e}-2\mu (\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}b}+\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}d})\frac{s}{{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-13

which shows that $\text{s}$ is parallel to ${\text{s}}^{e}$ , whence one deduces:

$\frac{s}{{\sigma}^{\text{eq}}}=\frac{{\text{s}}^{e}}{{\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}}$ **eq 5.2‑** 5.2-14

Substituting [eq 5.2-14] into [eq 5.2-9] and [eq 5.2-11] one finds:

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{c}^{p}=\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}b}\frac{{s}^{e}}{{\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}}$                                 eq 5.2‑5.2-15

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p}=\frac{\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{\sqrt{2}d}\frac{{s}^{e}}{{\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}}$                                 eq 5.2‑5.2-16

One then substitutes [ eq 5.2- 5.2-15 ] and [ eq 5.2- 5.2-16 ] into [ eq 5.2- 5.2-3 ] and [ eq 5.2- 5.2-4 ], and one expresses the criteria [eq5.2-5] and [ eq 5.2- 5.2-7 ] with these new results. This leads to two equations having as unknowns $\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}$ and $\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ :

$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3b}}{\sigma}^{{e}^{\text{eq}}}+\frac{a}{b}{\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{c}^{p}(\frac{\mathrm{2\mu }}{{\mathrm{3b}}^{2}}+\frac{{\text{Ka}}^{2}}{{b}^{2}})-{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{t}^{p}(\frac{\mathrm{2\mu }}{3\text{bd}}+\frac{\text{Kac}}{\text{bd}})-{f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{{p}^{-}}+{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{c}^{p})=0$         eq 5.2‑5.2-17

$\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3d}}{\sigma}^{{e}^{\text{eq}}}+\frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{c}^{p}(\frac{\mathrm{2\mu }}{3\text{bd}}+\frac{\text{Kac}}{\text{bd}})-{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{t}^{p}(\frac{\mathrm{2\mu }}{{\mathrm{3d}}^{2}}+\frac{{\text{Kc}}^{2}}{{d}^{2}})-{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{-}}+{\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{t}^{p})=0$         eq 5.2‑5.2-18

It is this system of two equations in two unknowns that must finally be solved. In the case where the functions ${f}_{c}$ and ${f}_{t}$ are linear, that is to say in the case where one is in a linear post-peak regime in compression as in tension, it is a linear system which will therefore be solved in one iteration. In the case either of the pre-peak regime in compression (which is always non-linear), or of modellings with non-linear post-peak regimes, the system [ eq 5.2- 5.2-17 ] and [ eq 5.2- 5.2-18 ] is solved by a Newton method:

One denotes by ${f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}(\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p},\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ the compression criterion considered as a function of the sole variables $\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}$ and $\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ , and likewise for tension:

${f}_{\text{comp}}^{\text{*}}(\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p},\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3b}}{\sigma}^{{e}^{\text{eq}}}+\frac{a}{b}{\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}(\frac{2\mu }{{\mathrm{3b}}^{2}}+\frac{{\text{Ka}}^{2}}{{b}^{2}})-\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}(\frac{2\mu }{3\text{bd}}+\frac{\text{Kac}}{\text{bd}})-{f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{{p}^{-}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p})$

${f}_{\text{trac}}^{\text{*}}(\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p},\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{3d}{\sigma}^{{e}^{\text{eq}}}+\frac{c}{d}{\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}(\frac{2\mu }{3bd}+\frac{\text{K}ac}{bd})-\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}(\frac{2\mu }{3{d}^{2}}+\frac{K{c}^{2}}{{\text{d}}^{2}})-{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{-}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$

The i-th Newton iteration for the system [ eq 5.2- 5.2-17 ] – [ eq 5.2- 5.2-18 ] is:

${\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\\ \Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}\end{array}\right\rbrace }^{i+1}={\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\\ \Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}\end{array}\right\rbrace }^{i}-{J}_{i}^{-1}{\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}(\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p},\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})\\ {f}_{\mathrm{tracp}}^{\text{*}}(\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p},\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})\end{array}\right\rbrace }^{i}$

The Jacobian ${J}_{i}$ is:

${J}_{i}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}}{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}& \frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}}{\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}\\ \frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}^{\text{*}}}{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}& \frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}^{\text{*}}}{\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}\end{array}\right]$

With:

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}}{\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}=-(\frac{2\mu }{{\mathrm{3b}}^{2}}+\frac{K{a}^{2}}{{b}^{2}})-\frac{\partial {f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{{p}^{-}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p})}{\partial \Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}$

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}^{\text{*}}}{\partial \Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}=-(\frac{2\mu }{\mathrm{3bd}}+\frac{K\mathrm{ac}}{\mathrm{bd}})$

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}^{\text{*}}}{\partial \Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}=-(\frac{2\mu }{\mathrm{3bd}}+\frac{K\mathrm{ac}}{\mathrm{bd}})$

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\text{trac}}^{\text{*}}}{\partial \Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}=-(\frac{2\mu }{{\mathrm{3d}}^{2}}+\frac{{\text{Kc}}^{2}}{{d}^{2}})-\frac{\partial {f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{-}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})}{\partial \Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}$

The initial Jacobian of the system is deduced from the values of the derivatives at $\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}=0$ and ${\mathrm{\Delta \kappa }}_{t}^{p}=0$ , which amounts to solving the non-linear system starting from the zero solution. The non-linearities are introduced by the softening curves. In the post-peak part, when they are linear, convergence occurs in one iteration. When they are non-linear, convergence requires only a few iterations. Starting from the zero solution therefore poses no convergence problem. This amounts to starting from the linearisation of the criteria in the neighbourhood of the elastic prediction.

### 5.4 Treatment of the non-regular cases

In this paragraph, we describe the discrete treatment of the equations corresponding to the projection onto the apex of the tension cone, [eq 4.4-10] to [eq 4.4-13], knowing that the projection onto the apex of the compression cone is carried out in the same way. Equations [eq 4.4-10] and [eq 4.4-12] define the plastic flow in this case, whereas equation [eq 4.4-13] is a condition of acceptability of the projection onto the apex of the cone.

#### 5.4.1 Computation of the stresses and of the plastic strains

The discrete forms of [eq 4.4-10] to [eq 4.4-13] are:

$s=0$ **eq** **5.4.1-1**

${\sigma}_{H}=\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$                                 eq 5.4.1-2

$3\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{t}_{H}}^{p}=\frac{c}{d}\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ **eq 5.4.1-3**

The relation [ eq 5.2- 5.2-4 ] established in the regular case is always valid; one uses it jointly with [eq5.4.1-3] in [eq 5.4.1-1] and one obtains:

> ${\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\frac{c}{d}K\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}=\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ **eq 5.4.1-4**

The relation [eq 5.4.1-4] is a non-linear equation with respect to the variable $\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ that one solves by a Newton algorithm, which makes it possible to compute $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{{t}_{H}}^{p}$ by [eq 5.4.1-3] and ${\sigma}_{H}$ by [eq 5.4.1-2]. $s={s}^{e}-2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p}=0$ **eq 5.4.1-5**

This last equation makes it possible to compute $\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{t}^{p}$ and the plastic strains are completely known.

##### 5.4.2.1 A priori and a posteriori acceptability

For the tension criterion and for the post-peak part of the compression criterion, ${\sigma}_{H}=\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$ is a decreasing function of the hardening variable $\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ . One deduces from this that ${\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}^{-}\le {\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}$ and therefore that:

${\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}\frac{\mathrm{cK}}{\sqrt{2}\mu }\le {\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}^{-}\Rightarrow {\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}\frac{\mathrm{cK}}{\sqrt{2}\mu }\le {\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}$

The condition ${\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}\frac{\mathrm{cK}}{\sqrt{2}\mu }\le {\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}^{-}$ is called the a priori acceptability condition because it can be computed as soon as the elastic prediction is available. The condition ${\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}\frac{\mathrm{cK}}{\sqrt{2}\mu }\le {\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}$ is called the a posteriori acceptability condition.

These conditions have a simple graphical interpretation. One can easily see that, in the case of a regular solution, one has:

$\frac{{\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}}-{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}{{\sigma}_{H}^{e}-{\sigma}_{H}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}\mu }{\mathrm{cK}}$

This shows that the stress solution is obtained by projecting the point $({\sigma}_{H}^{e},{\sigma}^{{e}^{\mathrm{eq}}})$ parallel to a direction $(\mathrm{cK},\sqrt{2}\mu )$ in a diagram $({\sigma}_{H},{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}})$ , as indicated in [Figure 5.4.2-a]. The zones of acceptability of the projection onto the apex are cones whose apex is that of the reversibility cone and which are bounded on the one hand by the axis ${\sigma}_{H}>{\mathrm{OP}}_{t}$ and by a half-line issuing from the same point and of direction $(\mathrm{cK},\sqrt{2}\mu )$ .

In the case where the projection onto the apex of the cone is acceptable a posteriori, it may be that a regular solution also exists, as one can see in [Figure 5.4.2-b].

#### 5.4.4 Interchange of the apices of the tension and compression cones

A priori, the apex of the compression cone corresponds to a hydrostatic tensile pressure much greater than that of the apex of the tension cone. But, as one can see in [Figure 5.4.4-a], one can find a loading history that never activates the tension criterion, that activates and strongly hardens the compression criterion until it is made strictly included in the reversibility domain of the tension criterion.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_480.svg)

**Figure** **5.4.4-a**

When the two criteria have thus been inverted, only the compression criterion should a priori intervene any longer. It may then be that the solution is a projection onto the apex of the compression cone, which is treated exactly like the projection onto the apex of the tension cone.

#### 5.4.5 Projection onto the apex of both cones

In the case where the two cones have been interchanged and in the case where the elastic prediction violates both criteria, it may be that both the solution of projection onto the apex of the compression cone and that onto the apex of the tension cone are finally acceptable. In these situations, no criterion makes it possible to select one solution rather than the other and one will therefore seek a simultaneous projection onto the apex of both cones, which must therefore share the same apex, as indicated in [Figure5.4.5-a].

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_481.svg)

**Figure** **5.4.5-a**

The solution with projection onto both apices is obtained by solving the system:

${\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\frac{a}{b}K\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}-\frac{c}{d}K\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}=\frac{b}{a}{f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p})$ **eq 5.4.5-1**

${\sigma}_{H}^{e}-\frac{a}{b}K\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p}-\frac{c}{d}K\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p}=\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})$             eq 5.4.5-2

The stress state is given by:

$s=0$

> ${\sigma}_{H}=\frac{d}{c}{f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{t}^{p})=\frac{b}{a}{f}_{c}({\kappa}_{c}^{{p}^{\text{\_}}}+\Delta {\kappa}_{c}^{p})$ **eq 5.4.5-3**

### 5.5 Determination of the tangent operator

During the iterations of the Newton–Raphson algorithm, it is necessary to compute the tangent stiffness matrix. The construction of the latter plays an important role in the stability, the rapidity and the precision of the solution method. In order to retain these properties, the tangent stiffness matrix must be constructed from an operator linking the stress increment to the strain increment in a precise way at the end of the process of return onto the yield surfaces. Hooke's matrix, as well as the thermal strains, intervene as constants during the determination of the consistent tangent operator, constructed at the end of the iteration in the increment concerned.

The computation of the consistent tangent constitutive operator takes the plastic strains into account. For reasons of simplicity, we have chosen to compute the tangent constitutive operator in rate form.

#### 5.5.1 Rate tangent operator with a single active criterion

In the case of a single active criterion, for example the compression criterion, the computation of the rate tangent constitutive operator is the following:

One therefore uses the rate equations, in elastoplastic loading:

$\dot{\sigma}-H(\dot{\varepsilon}-{\dot{\kappa}}_{c}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })=0$ **eq 5.5.1-1**

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }\dot{\sigma}+\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{\dot{\kappa}}_{c}^{p}=0$ **eq 5.5.1-2**

The rate tangent operator is defined by:

$\dot{\sigma}=D\dot{\varepsilon}$ **eq 5.5.1-3**

Identifying [eq 5.5.1-3] with [eq 5.5.1-1] and [eq 5.5.1-2], one finds classically:

$D=H-\frac{1}{\delta}H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }H$ **eq 5.5.1-4**

with:

> $\delta =\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}$ **eq 5.5.1-5**

#### 5.5.2 Rate tangent operator with two active criteria

In the case where both criteria are activated, the compression criterion and the tension criterion, the computation of the rate tangent constitutive operator is the following:

One starts from:

$\dot{\sigma}-H(\dot{\varepsilon}-{\dot{\kappa}}_{c}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-{\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })=0$ **eq 5.5.2-1**

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }\dot{\sigma}+\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{\dot{\kappa}}_{c}^{p}=0$ **eq 5.5.2-2**

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }\dot{\sigma}+\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{\dot{\kappa}}_{t}^{p}=0$ **eq 5.5.2-3**

One arrives at:

$D=H-H\left[\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }({\delta}_{\mathrm{cc}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }+{\delta}_{\mathrm{ct}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma })+\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }({\delta}_{\mathrm{tc}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }+{\delta}_{\mathrm{tt}}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma })\right]$ eq 5.5.2-4

with:

${\delta}_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}})}{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}})(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}})-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })}$ **eq 5.5.2-5**

${\delta}_{\mathrm{ct}}=\frac{-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })}{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}})(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}})-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })}$ **eq 5.5.2-6**

${\delta}_{\mathrm{ct}}=\frac{-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })}{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}})(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}})-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })}$ e **q 5.5.2-7**

${\delta}_{\mathrm{cc}}=\frac{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}})}{(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}})(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }-\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}})-(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma })(\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}{}^{T}}{\partial \sigma }H\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma })}$ **eq 5.5.2-8**

The expression seems costly to express in terms of matrix products and of computation. But, when the operations are carried out in the appropriate order, it suffices to compute few terms. Moreover, it is the same terms that intervene several times. One must compute the derivatives of the criteria with respect to the stress, and with respect to the plastic multipliers, then the sums and the products with the real values, to finish with the constitution of the matrices and their sums.

Finally, the resulting matrix has the advantage of being symmetric, which suits the standard solution with *Code_Aster* .

#### 5.5.3 Successive derivatives of the criteria in tension and in compression

##### 5.5.3.1 Successive derivatives of the criteria with respect to the stress

The derivatives of the isotropic and deviatoric components of the stress with respect to the stress tensor are expressed in the following way:

Defining the vector ${\pi}_{0}=\left[\begin{array}{c}1\\ 1\\ 1\\ 0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right]$

the derivatives of the criteria with respect to the stress tensor are expressed in the following way:

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}b{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}+\frac{a}{\mathrm{3b}}{\pi}_{0}$

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{s}{\sqrt{2}d{\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}}+\frac{c}{\mathrm{3d}}{\pi}_{0}$

##### 5.5.3.2 Successive derivatives of the criteria with respect to the plastic multipliers

Derivative of the compression criterion in the case of a linear post-peak curve:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}=\frac{-4}{3}.{f}_{c}^{'}.(\frac{1}{{\kappa}_{e}}-\frac{{\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{{{\kappa}_{e}}^{2}})\mathrm{si}{\kappa}_{c}^{p}\le {\kappa}_{e}\\ \frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}={f}_{c}^{'}.(\frac{1}{({\kappa}_{t}^{u}-{\kappa}_{e})})\mathrm{si}{\kappa}_{e}\le {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\le {\kappa}_{t}^{u}\end{array}$

Derivative of the compression criterion in the case of a non-linear post-peak curve:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}=\frac{-4}{3}.{f}_{c}^{'}.(\frac{1}{{\kappa}_{e}}-\frac{{\kappa}_{c}^{p}}{{{\kappa}_{e}}^{2}})\mathrm{si}{\kappa}_{c}^{p}\le {\kappa}_{e}\\ \frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{comp}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{c}^{p}}=2.{f}_{c}^{'}.(\frac{({\kappa}_{c}^{p}-{\kappa}_{e})}{{({\kappa}_{c}^{u}-{\kappa}_{e})}^{2}})\mathrm{si}{\kappa}_{e}\le {\kappa}_{c}^{p}\le {\kappa}_{c}^{u}\end{array}$

Derivative of the tension criterion in the case of a linear post-peak curve:

$\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}=\frac{{f}_{t}^{'}}{{\kappa}_{t}^{u}}\mathrm{si}{\kappa}_{t}^{p}\le {\kappa}_{t}^{u}$

Derivative of the tension criterion in the case of an exponential post-peak curve: $\frac{\partial {f}_{\mathrm{trac}}}{\partial {\kappa}_{t}^{p}}={f}_{t}^{'}(\frac{a}{{\kappa}_{t}^{u}}){e}^{-\frac{a.{\kappa}_{t}^{p}}{{\kappa}_{t}^{u}}}$

### 5.7 General solution flowchart

The flowchart comprises the different stages of the solution, with the treatment of the projections onto the apices of the compression and tension cones in the following way:

**at the beginning of the algorithm,**

*one carries out a projection onto the apex of the tension cone:*

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a priori in tension** ,
- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a priori in compression** and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis,

*one carries out a simultaneous projection onto the apices of the tension and compression cones:*

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a priori in compression** and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis, and the projection onto the apex of the tension cone has not given a valid solution,

*one carries out a projection onto the apex of the compression cone:*

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a priori in compression** and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis, and the projection onto the apex of the tension cone has not given a valid solution, and the simultaneous projection onto the apices of both cones has not given a valid solution,

**in the middle of the algorithm,**

one carries out one, two or three standard solutions with projection onto the compression criterion or onto the tension criterion or onto both criteria at once,

and **at the end of the algorithm** ,

when the standard solutions with the activation of one criterion (tension or compression) or of both criteria at once have not given a solution,

one carries out a projection onto the apex of the tension cone:

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a posteriori in tension** ,
- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a posteriori in compression** (and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis,

one carries out a simultaneous projection onto the apices of the tension and compression cones:

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a posteriori in compression** and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis, and the projection onto the apex of the tension cone has not given a valid solution,

one carries out a projection onto the apex of the compression cone:

- when the elastic prediction satisfies the condition of projection **a posteriori in compression** and the apices of the tension and compression cones have been interchanged on the hydrostatic axis, and the projection onto the apex of the tension cone has not given a valid solution, and the simultaneous projection onto the apices of both cones has not given a valid solution.

At the end of each solution that has converged, one carries out the following checks of conformity of the solution:

- validity of the solution with respect to the second criterion, when the solution has been carried out with a single criterion. In all cases, it suffices to check that the two criteria computed with the final stress are negative or zero,
- conformity of the solution: one computes during the solution the final equivalent stress. It sometimes happens that the solution lies beyond the apex of the cone that hardens, which leads to a “negative” equivalent stress. Numerically, this is expressed by a final criterion that is strictly positive. To check the conformity of the solution, it suffices to check that the two criteria computed with the final stress are negative or zero,
- validity of the projections onto the apices of the cones. One must check that after the solution, when one knows the hardening of the criterion, the slope of the straight line linking the elastic prediction to the projection is lower than the slope of the projection direction (condition of a posteriori projection onto the apex of the cones). In the contrary case, this means that there exists a solution with a standard resolution.

**Note:**

In the case of the projections onto the apices of the cones, one systematically begins with the projection onto the apex of the tension cone. If this solution is valid, one keeps it. In the contrary case, if the tension criterion is activated, one carries out a solution with projection onto the apices of both cones. If the new solution is valid, one keeps that one. Otherwise, one carries out a solution with projection onto the apex of the compression cone alone.

**Note:**

*In the case where the conditions of projection onto the apices of the cones are activated, one of the three solutions must be valid, but for particularly large elastic jumps, it may be that the solution does not succeed. The solution is then to carry out a subdivision of the time step.*

**Note:**

*The projection onto the apices of both cones simultaneously assumes that the tension criterion is activated. It may very well be that, in the case of a permutation of the apices, only the compression criterion is found to be activated. One must then carry out a projection onto the apex of the compression cone alone.*

snap-back with the initial values of the coefficients c and d

We show in this appendix the snap-back problem encountered in the simulation of a uniaxial tension test followed by uniaxial compression, in the case where the choice of the coefficients $c$ and $d$ of the tension criterion corresponds to a situation where the tension criterion intersects the axes in a plane-stress diagram, that is to say the choice of the coefficients [eq 3.3-1] and [eq 3.3-2] leading to a reversibility domain represented in [Figure3.3-b]:

The assumptions are the following:

- one takes into account only the tension criterion,
- the hardening curve is of the type: ${f}_{t}({\kappa}_{t}^{p})={f}_{t}^{'}+h{\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ ,
- one simply denotes: $\lambda ={\kappa}_{t}^{p}$ so that the hardening curve is written:     ${f}_{t}={f}_{t}^{'}+\mathrm{h\lambda }$ ,
- the Poisson's ratio is zero,
- the hardening is negative ,
- the applicability condition is satisfied: $-E<h<0$ .

One considers a uniaxial test driven in strain along x, as indicated in Figure 5-

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_515.svg)

Figure 5‑a

In the other directions $y$ and $z$ , the conditions imposed are conditions of zero stresses: ${\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{zz}=0$ .

One begins by imposing a tensile strain ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{1}$ such that there is plastification in tension, but without the tensile limit falling back to 0. This is the point ${P}_{1}$ in the stress–strain diagram. One denotes by ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{0}$ the strain for which the tensile failure limit appears for the first time. Beyond the point ${P}_{1}$ , one imposes a decrease of the strain, which leads to an elastic unloading of the material, down to the value ${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{2}<0$ of the strain for which one again has a plastification, but this time under compressive stress. The object of this appendix is essentially to study the behaviour of the model not retained (the one corresponding to the formulae [eq 3.3-1] and [eq 3.3-2]) beyond the point ${P}_{2}$ .

A1.1    Computation of the stresses and of the strains during the loading

Taking into account the assumptions recalled above, the stress invariants are:

${I}_{1}={\sigma}_{xx}$ **eq** **A1.1-1**

$\sqrt{{J}_{2}}=\frac{\mid {\sigma}_{xx}\mid }{\sqrt{3}}$ **eq A1.1-2**

${\sigma}^{\mathrm{eq}}=\mid {\sigma}_{xx}\mid$ **eq A1.1-3**

The plastic flow is computed by:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{xx}^{p}=\frac{c+\sqrt{2}}{3d}\dot{\lambda}$ **eq A1.1-4**

A1.1.1    Paths 0P0 and P0P1

Taking into account the relations [eq A1.1-1] to [eq A1.1-4], and taking into account the fact that along this path the stresses ${\sigma}_{xx}$ are positive, one easily finds

${\sigma}_{{xx}_{1}}=E(\frac{h{\varepsilon}_{{xx}_{1}}+\frac{c+\sqrt{2}}{\mathrm{3d}}{f}_{t}^{'}}{h+\frac{{(c+\sqrt{2})}^{2}E}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}})$ **eq A1.1.1-1**

A1.1.2    Path P1P2

By definition, $\mathit{P1P2}$ is an elastic unloading path, the point $\mathit{P2}$ being such that the criterion is reached there again

${\sigma}_{{xx}_{2}}=\frac{\mathrm{3d}}{c-\sqrt{2}}{\sigma}_{{xx}_{1}}$ **eq A1.1.2-1**

${\varepsilon}_{{xx}_{2}}=(\frac{\mathrm{3d}}{c-\sqrt{2}})\frac{{\sigma}_{{xx}_{1}}}{E}+{\varepsilon}_{{xx}_{1}}$                             eq A1.1.2-2

A1.1.3    Beyond the point P2

One is now interested in the slope of the curve at the point $\mathit{P2}$ in the frame $({\sigma}_{xx},{\varepsilon}_{xx})$ . More precisely one is interested in the slope of this curve for a dissipative solution.

Writing that the material remains plastic beyond the point $\mathit{P2}$ , that is to say that the stress state remains on the criterion — which hardens —, one finds:

$\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}=\frac{\mathrm{3d}}{c-\sqrt{2}}h\dot{\lambda}$ **eq A1.1.3-1**

Moreover, computing the increment of plastic flow, and substituting it into the computation of the stress increment, one finds:

$\dot{{\varepsilon}_{xx}}=\frac{\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}}{E}+\frac{\dot{\lambda}}{\mathrm{3d}}(c-\sqrt{2})$ **eq A1.1.3-2**

One can then eliminate $\dot{\lambda}$ between [eq A1.1.3-1] and [eq A1.1.3-2] and one obtains:

> $\frac{\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}}{\dot{{\varepsilon}_{xx}}}=\frac{hE}{h+E\frac{{(c-\sqrt{2})}^{2}}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}}={E}_{{T}_{2}}$ **eq A1.1.3-3**

This formula gives the slope of the response in the plane $({\sigma}_{xx},{\varepsilon}_{xx})$

The numerator is always negative since $h$ is negative .

The sign of ${\frac{\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}}{\dot{{\varepsilon}_{xx}}}\mid }_{2}={E}_{{T}_{2}}$ depends on the sign of the denominator; thus two cases arise:

If $h<-E\frac{{(c-\sqrt{2})}^{2}}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}$ then ${\frac{\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}}{\dot{{\varepsilon}_{xx}}}\mid }_{2}={E}_{{T}_{2}}$ is positive and one has a snap-back configuration.

If $h>-E\frac{{(c-\sqrt{2})}^{2}}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}$ then ${\frac{\dot{{\sigma}_{xx}}}{\dot{{\varepsilon}_{xx}}}\mid }_{2}={E}_{{T}_{2}}$ is negative and there is no snap-back.

A new condition appears in order to avoid snap-backs, a condition that we compare with the one already mentioned, but which in fact concerned the possible snap-backs at the point $\mathit{P1}$ .

Slope at the point $\mathit{P1}$ in the frame $({\varepsilon}_{xx},{\sigma}_{xx})$ :    ${E}_{{T}_{1}}=\frac{hE}{h+E}$ ,

Slope at the point $\mathit{P2}$ in the frame $({\varepsilon}_{xx},{\sigma}_{xx})$ :    ${E}_{{T}_{2}}=\frac{hE}{h+E\frac{{(c-\sqrt{2})}^{2}}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}}$ .

Now $\frac{{(c-\sqrt{2})}^{2}}{{\mathrm{9d}}^{2}}=3{\alpha}^{2}=3{(\frac{{f}_{t}^{'}}{{f}_{c}^{'}})}^{2}$ .

One can for example express ${E}_{{T}_{2}}$ as a function of ${E}_{{T}_{1}}$ by eliminating $h$ :

${E}_{{T}_{2}}=\frac{{E}_{{T}_{1}}E}{3{\alpha}^{2}E+{E}_{{T}_{1}}(1-3{\alpha}^{2})}$ .

For example,

${E}_{{T}_{2}}=\infty$ for ${E}_{{T}_{1}}=-\frac{{\mathrm{3\alpha }}^{2}E}{(1-{\mathrm{3\alpha }}^{2})}$ .

By way of example, for $E=32000\mathit{Mpa}$ , ${f}_{t}^{'}=3\text{Mpa}$ and ${f}_{c}^{'}=38,3\text{Mpa}$ one finds ${E}_{{T}_{1}}\approx -601$ . Thus ${E}_{{T}_{1}}$ is very small relative to $E$ , as illustrated in [Figure A1.1.3-a].

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.03/Object_557.svg)

**Figure** **A1.1.3-a**

Thus, a condition implying that there is no snap-back at the point $\mathit{P2}$ would be too restrictive and would lead to choosing a material that is practically not brittle in tension.

It is for this reason that we have preferred to modify the expression of the coefficients $c$ and $d$ as indicated in paragraph [§ 3.3 ].

That said, and even if the solution adopted, consisting in modifying the coefficients $c$ and $d$ , seems reasonable, the example treated in this appendix shows that a very simple problem can finally be a structural problem: there are in this example equilibrium conditions, namely the conditions: ${\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{zz}=0$ .
