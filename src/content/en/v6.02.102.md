# v6.02.102 SSNL102 - Nonlinear behaviour of a corneal assembly

**Summary:**

This test considers a discrete element at 2 nodes subjected to a two-dimensional tensile and moment load requesting degrees of freedom in translation and rotation.

The analysis is static with an incremental nonlinear behavioral relationship expressed by an internal adimensional variable combining two-dimensional generalized forces and displacements.

The behavioral relationship consists of 2 mechanisms associated with 2 curves connecting each other by concavity.

The test is useful in fully simulating possible loading and unloading routes, including the transition between mechanisms.

The results correspond to the numerical solution of the unknown problem at 1 (the variable of the current mechanism) obtained by the reversal of the curve of the behavioral relationship in each of the 2 mechanisms in relation to an imposed force.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A discreet element of zero size at 2 nodes.

Local benchmark = global benchmark.

A K_TR_D_L rigidity matrix by default (associated with an element DIS_TR_L)

$1.6N/\mathrm{mm}$ in translation, $1.9N/\mathrm{mm}$ in rotation.

The stiffness characteristics of $x$ local direction and $y$ rotation are modified by a ASSE_CORN behavioral relationship introduced by a characteristic material.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

A loading path (crossing and discharge) is reproduced on a element in each of the 2 assembly mechanisms of the bidirectional behavioral relationship (stress according to the $x$ direction and moment around the $y$ axis ). This one expresses the reduced displacements relative to the reduced effort. The mechanisms and the constitutive law assembly are described in figures [Figure 1.2-a] and [Figure 1.2-b].

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

The numerical solution of the inversion of a nonlinear relationship. There is one unknown at a time: the internal variable of the mechanism. The other values are deduced from it. The calculation is direct for the first mechanism, incremental for the second (discussion in synthesis in [§5]).

## 4 Summary of the results

The test is worth representing the completeness of possible load journeys with multiple factors of slope failure: load-discharge, mechanism transition.

However, the dimension of the problem allows you to have only one unknown (the current internal variable), solution of the reversal of the constitutive law curve: direct solution for the first mechanism and incremental for the second.

The reduction of the problem allows (if one converges) to trust *Aster* as "rule to calculate" and to consider the result as a "exact" numerical solution.
