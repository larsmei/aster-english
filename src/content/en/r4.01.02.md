# r4.01.02 Anisotropic elasticity

**Summary**

This document deals with anisotropic thermoelasticity, used for the modellings of 3D and 2D continuous media (C_PLAN, D_PLAN, AXIS), or the layers of composite shells.

The thermoelastic medium may be anisotropic in the 3 directions (one speaks of orthotropic elasticity), or isotropic in two directions (one speaks of transversely isotropic elasticity), or it may present a cubic symmetry.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The objective of this document is to give the expression for the compliance and Hooke matrices for orthotropic, transversely isotropic and cubic-symmetry thermoelastic materials in the 3D, 2D plane-stress, 2D plane-strain and axisymmetric cases.

Strictly speaking, for linear thermoelastic materials, the stresses are linear functions of the strains and of the temperature differential. One writes:

${\sigma}_{ij}={H}_{ijkl}\left({\epsilon}_{kl}-{\alpha}_{kl}\left(T-{T}_{\mathit{réf}}\right)\right)$

We speak of Hooke “matrices” because, for the sake of simplification, we have not adopted the notation of a fourth-order tensor for $\mathrm{H}$ , in favour of the notation of W. Voigt, in which the second-order tensors are represented by vectors with 6 components and the fourth-order tensors by $6\times 6$ matrices.

The symmetric nature of $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ and the adoption for these second-order tensors of a vector form make it possible to write:

$\left\lbrace \sigma \right\rbrace =\left[\mathrm{H}\right]\left(\left\lbrace \epsilon \right\rbrace -\left\lbrace \alpha \right\rbrace \left(T-{T}_{\mathit{réf}}\right)\right)$

where $\left\lbrace \sigma \right\rbrace$ and $\left\lbrace \epsilon \right\rbrace$ are the vector representation of the second-order tensors $\sigma$ and $\epsilon$ , and where $\left[\mathrm{H}\right]$ , the Hooke matrix, is a $6\times 6$ matrix, necessarily symmetric, that is a priori 21 independent coefficients, in the three-dimensional case:

$\left[\mathrm{H}\right]=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{cccccc}{H}_{11}& {H}_{12}& {H}_{13}& {H}_{14}& {H}_{15}& {H}_{16}\\ & {H}_{22}& {H}_{23}& {H}_{24}& {H}_{25}& {H}_{26}\\ & & {H}_{33}& {H}_{34}& {H}_{35}& {H}_{36}\\ \text{SYM}& & & {H}_{44}& {H}_{45}& {H}_{46}\\ & & & & {H}_{55}& {H}_{56}\\ & & & & & {H}_{66}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The vector $\left\lbrace \alpha \right\rbrace$ denotes the vector representation of the second-order thermal-expansion tensor, necessarily symmetric, that is a priori 6 independent coefficients, in the three-dimensional case.

## 2 Typology of the Hooke matrices

### 2.1 Orthotropy

This is a situation in which the elastic material exhibits two symmetries with respect to two perpendicular planes (*orthorhombic* symmetry). The elasticity tensor has a priori 9 independent coefficients, a consequence of the relations obtained with these two symmetries among the 21 coefficients.

The thermal-expansion tensor has a priori 3 independent coefficients, a consequence of the relations obtained with these two symmetries.

In the orthotropy axes:

$\left[\mathrm{H}\right]=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{cccccc}{H}_{11}& {H}_{12}& {H}_{13}& 0& 0& 0\\ & {H}_{22}& {H}_{23}& 0& 0& 0\\ & & {H}_{33}& 0& 0& 0\\ \text{SYM}& & & {H}_{44}& 0& 0\\ & & & & {H}_{55}& 0\\ & & & & & {H}_{66}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ $\left\lbrace \alpha \right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\alpha}_{11}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ {\alpha}_{22}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ {\alpha}_{33}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ 0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace$

### 2.2 Transverse isotropy

Transverse (or rotational) isotropy is a restriction of orthotropy in which one has isotropy in one of the two orthogonal planes of elastic symmetry, following an invariance by rotation of $2\pi /3$ about the axis orthogonal to the plane of transverse isotropy, for example ${x}_{3}=0$ . The elasticity tensor has a priori 5 independent coefficients.

The matrix $\left[\mathrm{H}\right]$ will have the same form as for orthotropy but with four additional relations between the components. Thus one will have, for transverse isotropy in the plane ${x}_{3}=0$ :

${H}_{11}={H}_{22}$ ;    ${H}_{13}={H}_{23}$ ;    ${H}_{44}={H}_{55}$     and    $2{H}_{44}={H}_{11}-{H}_{12}$             [eq2.2-1]

The thermal-expansion tensor has a priori 2 independent coefficients:

${\alpha}_{11}={\alpha}_{22}$                                         eq2.2-2]

### 2.3 Isotropy

The material is isotropic if $\left[\mathrm{H}\right]$ remains invariant under any change of frame. The elasticity tensor has a priori 2 independent coefficients. There is only one thermal-expansion coefficient

## 3 Hooke matrix and compliance matrix

### 3.1 Notation

Instead of using the indices 1, 2 and 3 to identify the axes of the Cartesian frame, one will use the corresponding indices $L$ , $T$ and $N$ :

  - $L$ for longitudinal
  - $T$ for transverse
  - $N$ for normal

![Figure](/figures/r4.01.02/Object_8.svg)

The coefficients which appear are the following:

| **Keyword** | **Notation** | **meaning** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E_L | ${E}_{L}$ | Longitudinal Young's modulus |
| E_T | ${E}_{T}$ | Transverse Young's modulus |
| E_N | ${E}_{N}$ | Normal Young's modulus |
| G_LT | ${G}_{\text{LT}}$ | Shear modulus in the plane $(L,T)$ |
| G_TN | ${G}_{\text{TN}}$ | Shear modulus in the plane $(T,N)$ |
| G_LN | ${G}_{\text{LN}}$ | Shear modulus in the plane $(L,N)$ |
| NU_LT | ${\nu}_{\text{LT}}$ | Poisson's ratio in the plane $(L,T)$ |
| NU_TN | ${\nu}_{\text{TN}}$ | Poisson's ratio in the plane $(T,N)$ |
| NU_LN | ${\nu}_{\text{LN}}$ | Poisson's ratio in the plane $(L,N)$ |
| ALPHA_L | ${\alpha}_{L}$ | Mean longitudinal thermal-expansion coefficient |
| ALPHA_T | ${\alpha}_{T}$ | Mean transverse thermal-expansion coefficient |
| ALPHA_N | ${\alpha}_{N}$ | Mean normal thermal-expansion coefficient |

**Very important note:**

${\nu}_{\text{LT}}$ *is different from* ${\nu}_{\mathit{TL}}$ *:*

*If one applies a tension along the axis* $L$ *:*

${\epsilon}_{\text{LL}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}$ *(Hooke's law along one direction).*

This tension is accompanied, proportionally, by a contraction $-{\nu}_{\text{LT}}.\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}$ along the axis $T$ , and by a contraction $-{\nu}_{\text{LN}}.\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}$ along the axis $N$ .

The first index indicates the axis on which the effect of the loading is exerted and the second index indicates the direction of the loading.

One then applies a tension along the axis $T$ , then a tension along $N$ , and one obtains:

$\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{\text{LL}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}-{\nu}_{\text{TL}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{TT}}}{{E}_{T}}-{\nu}_{\text{NL}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{NN}}}{{E}_{N}}\\ {\epsilon}_{\text{TT}}=-{\nu}_{\text{LT}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}+\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{TT}}}{{E}_{T}}-{\nu}_{\text{NT}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{NN}}}{{E}_{N}}\\ {\epsilon}_{\text{NN}}=-{\nu}_{\text{LN}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}}{{E}_{L}}-{\nu}_{\text{TN}}\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{TT}}}{{E}_{T}}+\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{NN}}}{{E}_{N}}\end{array}\rbrace$                         [eq3.1-1]

The compliance matrix ${\left[\mathrm{H}\right]}^{-1}$ being symmetric, one deduces from it:

$\frac{{\nu}_{\text{LT}}}{{E}_{L}}=\frac{{\nu}_{\text{TL}}}{{E}_{T}}$ ; $\frac{{\nu}_{\text{LN}}}{{E}_{L}}=\frac{{\nu}_{\text{NL}}}{{E}_{N}}$ ; $\frac{{\nu}_{\text{TN}}}{{E}_{T}}=\frac{{\nu}_{\text{NT}}}{{E}_{N}}$

#### 3.2.2 Transverse isotropy

Transverse isotropy is defined here in the plane $(L,T)$ , and the orthotropy direction is therefore $N$ . One may draw the reader's attention to the fact that this convention differs from a usual convention which designates by “longitudinal direction” the orthotropy direction of transversely isotropic materials.

#### 3.2.3 Elasticity with cubic symmetry

Elasticity with cubic symmetry occurs when, in addition to the three Cartesian planes of symmetry, the six planes rotated by 45° are likewise planes of symmetry. One then has 3 independent elastic coefficients. This corresponds to an elasticity matrix of the form:

$\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{H}_{1111}& {H}_{1122}& {H}_{1122}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ {H}_{1122}& {H}_{1111}& {H}_{1122}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ {H}_{1122}& {H}_{1122}& {H}_{1111}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& {H}_{1212}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& {H}_{1212}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\\ \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}& {H}_{1212}\end{array}\right]$

Given the cubic symmetry, it remains to determine 3 coefficients:

${E}_{L}={E}_{N}={E}_{T}=E,\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}{G}_{\text{LT}}={G}_{\text{LN}}={G}_{\text{TN}}=G,\phantom{\rule{6em}{0ex}}{\nu}_{\text{LN}}={\nu}_{\text{LT}}={\nu}_{\text{LN}}=\nu$

One notes that the expansion coefficients satisfy: ${\alpha}_{T}={\alpha}_{L}={\alpha}_{N}$ .

In order to reproduce elasticity with cubic symmetry with ELAS_ORTH, it is sufficient to compute the orthotropy coefficients such that the elasticity matrix obtained is of the form above:

$\begin{array}{c}{H}_{1111}=\frac{E(1-\nu )}{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}\\ {H}_{1122}=\frac{\nu E}{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}\\ {H}_{1212}={G}_{\text{LT}}={G}_{\text{LN}}={G}_{\text{TN}}\end{array}$

hence, so long as $(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )\ne 0$ (that is to say $\nu$ different from $0.5$ ).

$\frac{{H}_{1122}}{{H}_{1111}}=\frac{\nu}{1-\nu }$ which supplies $\nu =\frac{1}{1+\frac{{H}_{1111}}{{H}_{1122}}}$ then $E={H}_{1111}\frac{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}{(1-\nu )}$

$\left[\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{LL}}\\ {\sigma}_{\text{TT}}\\ 0\\ {\sigma}_{\text{LT}}\end{array}\right]=\frac{1}{1-{\nu}_{\text{LT}}.{\nu}_{\text{TL}}}\left[\begin{array}{cccc}{E}_{L}& {\nu}_{\text{TL}}{E}_{T}& 0& 0\\ {\nu}_{\text{LT}}{E}_{L}& {E}_{T}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& {G}_{\text{LT}}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{\text{LL}}\\ {\epsilon}_{\text{TT}}\\ {\epsilon}_{\text{NN}}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{\text{LT}}\end{array}\right]$

$\left[\mathrm{H}\right]$ – Orthotropy in plane stress
