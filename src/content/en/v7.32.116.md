# v7.32.116 WTNP116 - Consolidation problem for the permanent HM model

**A SUPPRIMER**

**Summary:**

A problem of consolidation into 2D dimension is studied here, with an infinite length of land depending on one of its dimensions. This test is used to validate several developments:

1. Hydro-Mechanic modelling in saturated porous medium and in permanent state (D_PLAN_HM_P Modelling). Compared to the existing D_PLAN_HM Modelling, this law retains the writing of the mechanical equilibrium of the porous medium but the temporal derivation of the mass input is eliminated in the writing of the conservation of the water mass.
2. the functionality of the residue error indicators developed specifically for permanent HM modelling via the option ‘ERME_ELEM' of the CALC_ERREUR command.
3. The resolution by chaining of HM equations and in particular the direction of passage of the control variables from hydraulics to mechanics

This is a proposal of 3 modellings, which differs only in the orientation of geometry compared to the horizontal axis. Horizontal geometry (modellings A and C) and rotational geometry with an angle of $45°$ (modelization B) are considered.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Given the symmetry of the boundary conditions, the solution is independent of $y$. For the mechanical part, the mechanical balance of the skeleton is written in projection on the $(\mathrm{0x})$ axis:

$({\lambda}_{1}^{M}+2{\lambda}_{2}^{M})\frac{{\partial}^{2}{u}_{x}}{\partial {x}^{2}}-b\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}-\mathrm{rg}=0$

where ${\lambda}_{1}^{M}$ and ${\lambda}_{2}^{M}$ designate the Lamate coefficients of the material. For the hydraulic part, the retention of the water mass is written

$\frac{\partial {M}_{x}}{\partial x}=0$

with

$\frac{{M}_{x}}{\rho}={\lambda}^{H}(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x}-\rho g)$

where ${\lambda}^{H}$ is the hydraulic conductivity. The pressure is then given by the formula

$p={P}_{0}+\rho g(L-x)$

Horizontal ${u}_{x}$ displacements are given by

${u}_{x}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{r-b\rho }{{\lambda}_{1}^{M}+2{\lambda}_{2}^{M}}\mathrm{gx}(x-2L)+\frac{b{P}_{0}}{{\lambda}_{1}^{M}+2{\lambda}_{2}^{M}}x$

### 2.2 Baseline

The formulas giving the coefficients of Lamé as a function of Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio are recalled

${\lambda}_{1}^{M}=\frac{E\nu }{(1+\nu )(1-2\nu )}$ et ${\lambda}_{2}^{M}=\frac{E\nu }{2(1+\nu )}$

The reference result is the value of displacement and pressure at point $P$ .
