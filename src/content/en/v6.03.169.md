# v6.03.169 SSNP169 – Full disk through an ununiform X-FEM interface

**Summary:**

This test validates the X-FEM contact treatment as part of the small disturbance hypothesis.

The first step is to consider a full disc cut off by a circular interface. Then, a squareplein is considered also cut off by a circular interface. In both cases, the interface is the contact surface. The rigidity of the solid, represented by the Young's modulus, plays an important role in the assessment of the value of deformations and fluctuations in the contact pressure.

A analytical solution has been developed for this problem to validate the calculated numerical results. Contact pressure and displacement values are tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure consists of a cylindrical crown crossed by a circular X-FEM interface. The ${R}_{2}$ radius defines the radius of the circular interface (Figure 1.1-1) where the contact will be defined.

The characteristic dimensions of the structure are:

${R}_{1}=1,0m;{R}_{2}=0,6m$

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.169/1000000000000437000003884E578C9FFBED25E9.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Structure Geometry**

### 1.2 Material properties

The Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio of the outer crown material are given by ${E}_{1}$ and ${\nu}_{1}$ (respectively ${E}_{2}$ and ${\nu}_{2}$ for the inner crown).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In the following, we deal with the case of a full disk under unsettled external pressure. However, the boundary conditions (as well as the analytical solution) are generalizable in the case of the square surface crossed by a circular interface. The case of the square surface allows us to process an mesh that would not have the same symmetries as the interface: in the case of the disk, we use a regular mesh radiating, while in the case of the square, one uses a regular mesh along the edges of the square.

The following Modelling of the application of an ununiform pressure on the $r={R}_{1}$ edge of the outer crown: $p={\alpha}_{0}+{\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ , $\theta$ being the polar angle describing the position of the point $\theta =\arctan(\frac{Y}{X})$ . For analytical calculation, the problem for a $p={\alpha}_{0}$ pressure is solved in the first part, and then the problem for a pressure of the $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ form is solved . For the complete solution, the superposition principle is applied.

Dirichlet boundary conditions are imposed. The displacements are imposed on the outer surface in $r={R}_{1}$. A ${R}_{3}=\mathrm{0.2m}$ radius will also be defined, where the displacements are imposed, equivalent to those produced by a full disc (Figure 1.3-1).

For the square surface, displacements will be imposed on the outer surfaces, as well as on the ${R}_{3}=\mathrm{0.2m}$ radius disc.

Two calculations are performed for each model: a first interface contact calculation and a second interface contact pressure calculation.

#### 1.3.1 Boundary conditions in the case of uniform pressure

The edges of the solids are subjected to displacements ($r={R}_{1}$ and $r={R}_{3}$) equivalent to applying a pressure $p={\alpha}_{0}$ to the edge of the outer crown ($r={R}_{1}$).

$\begin{array}{c}{\xi}_{X}(r)=f(r)\cos(\arctan(\frac{Y}{X}))\\ {\xi}_{Y}(r)=f(r)\sin(\arctan(\frac{Y}{X}))\end{array}$

$f(r)$ function of radial displacement is given according to material properties and $p$ pressure. In the case of flat deformations (MODELISATION = 'D_PLAN') one has:

$\begin{array}{c}f({R}_{1})={A}_{1}{R}_{1}+\frac{{B}_{1}}{{R}_{1}}\\ f({R}_{3})={A}_{2}{R}_{3}\end{array}$                                         éq 1.1

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{(1+{\nu}_{1})(1-2{\nu}_{1})}{{E}_{1}}\frac{-p{R}_{1}^{2}+\lambda {R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}};{B}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}(-p+\lambda )\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{-(1+{\nu}_{2})(1-2{\nu}_{2})}{{E}_{2}}\lambda \end{array}$ éq 1.2

where $\lambda$ is the contact pressure whose analytical expression is:

$\lambda =2p\frac{(1-{\nu}_{1}^{2})}{{E}_{1}}\frac{\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}}{\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}+{R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{1})}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}+\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}(1-2{\nu}_{2})}$                         éq. 1.3

#### 1.3.2 Boundary conditions in the case of variable pressure

The edges of the crowns are subjected to displacements ($r={R}_{1}$ and $r={R}_{3}$ ) equivalent to applying a pressure $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ to the edge of the outer crown ($r={R}_{1}$ ).

$\begin{array}{c}{\xi}_{X}(r,\theta )={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\cos(\theta )-{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\sin(\theta )\\ {\xi}_{Y}(r,\theta )={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\sin(\theta )+{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\cos(\theta )\end{array}$

${u}_{r}(r,\theta )$ function of radial displacement, and ${u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )$ tangential displacement function are given according to material properties, $p$ pressure, geometric characteristics, and square of the ratios between ${f}_{1}={(\frac{{R}_{2}}{{R}_{1}})}^{2};{f}_{2}={(\frac{{R}_{3}}{{R}_{2}})}^{2}$ rays. In the case of flat deformations (MODELISATION = 'D_PLAN') one has:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}({R}_{1,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}{R}_{1}+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{R}_{1}^{3}}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})-{\nu}_{1}(4{B}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}({R}_{1,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{1}{R}_{1}+{\mathrm{6B}}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{R}_{1}^{3}}-2\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})-{\nu}_{1}({\mathrm{4B}}_{1}{R}_{1}^{3}-4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{R}_{1}})]\sin(2\theta )\\ {u}_{r}({R}_{3,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{2}{R}_{3})-{\nu}_{2}(4{B}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}({R}_{3,}\theta )=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{2}{R}_{3}+{\mathrm{6B}}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3})-{\nu}_{2}({\mathrm{4B}}_{2}{R}_{3}^{3})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$ éq 1.4

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+{f}_{1}^{2}+2{f}_{1})}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{B}_{1}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{2}^{2}}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(3{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1})-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+3{f}_{1}^{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{1}={R}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}+3)-\lambda ({\mathrm{3f}}_{1}+1)}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{D}_{1}=-{R}_{2}^{2}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+2)-\lambda (2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2};{B}_{2}=\frac{-\lambda }{6{R}_{2}^{2}};\end{array}$             éq 1.5

where $\lambda$ is the contact pressure whose analytical expression is:

$\lambda =\frac{{\mathit{coef}}_{1}}{{\mathit{coef}}_{2}+{\mathit{coef}}_{3}}{\alpha}_{1}$ éq 1.6

such that:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{coef}}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)+{\nu}_{1}(12{f}_{1}^{2}+8{f}_{1}+12)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-3{f}_{1}^{3}-15{f}_{1}^{2}-9{f}_{1}-5)+{\nu}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{3}+18{f}_{1}^{2}+6{f}_{1}+6)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{3}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{6{E}_{2}}(-3+2{\nu}_{2})\end{array}$ éq 1.7

## 2 reference solution

Here we develop an analytical solution for the problem presented above. This solution will be developed in the context of the hypothesis of small deformations considering that the materials of the crowns are isotropic, governed by a linear elastic law without temperature variation.

The solution on the move of the problem has the following generic form:

$u={u}_{r}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{r}}+{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{\theta}}+{u}_{z}(r,\theta ,z).\underline{{e}_{z}}$

The problem will be solved in the context of the flat deformation hypothesis. Our loading in the form $p={\alpha}_{0}+{\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ will decouple the problem resolution to a part where the pressure is uniform $p={\alpha}_{0}$, and a part where the pressure is variable $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$.

### 2.1 Uniform pressure

Using the symmetries of the problem and the hypothesis of invariance according to z of the plane deformations, the solution of the problem takes the following form:

${u}_{r}={u}_{r}(r)$

${u}_{\theta}=0$                                             éq 2.1

${u}_{z}=0$

Using the equation of Lamé-Navier:

$\begin{array}{c}(\lambda +\mu )\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\mu \Delta \underline{u}+\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                             éq 2.2

where $\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}$ are the zero density forces here, and the Laplacian formula:

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta \underline{u}=\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\underline{\mathit{rot}}\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})\end{array}$                                 éq 2.3

Eq 2.2 can be written as:

$\begin{array}{c}(\lambda +2\mu )\underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .(\underline{u}))+\mu \underline{\mathit{rot}}\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})+\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                     éq 2.4

or still using $\underline{\mathit{rot}}(\underline{u})=\vec{0}\text{et}\underline{\mathit{fd}}=\vec{0}\text{et}\underline{u}={u}_{r}(r).\underline{{e}_{r}}$:

$\begin{array}{c}\nabla .(\underline{u})=\frac{d}{\mathit{dr}}{u}_{r}(r)+\frac{1}{r}{u}_{r}(r)\\ \underline{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla .\underline{u})=\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}(r{u}_{r}(r))].{\underline{e}}_{r}\\ \text{soit encore}(\lambda +2\mu )\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}[\frac{1}{r}\frac{d}{{d}_{r}}(r{u}_{r}(r))]=0\end{array}$                         éq 2.5

By integrating the equation, solid 1 is obtained by the following form of the displacement field:

${u}_{r}={C}_{1}r+\frac{{D}_{1}}{r}\text{}{u}_{\theta}=0\text{}{u}_{z}=0$                                     éq 2.6

and for solid 2 the following form of the moving field:

${u}_{r}={C}_{2}r\text{}{u}_{\theta}=0\text{}{u}_{z}=0$                                     éq 2.7

To determine the ${C}_{1},{D}_{1},{C}_{2}$ constants, we still have to impose the pressure and displacement limit conditions. To do this, we must first calculate the deformations and then the stresses associated with the displacement field.

Deformations are the symmetrical part of the displacement gradient.

$\begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\mathit{rr}}={C}_{1}-\frac{{D}_{1}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {ϵ}_{\theta \theta }={C}_{1}+\frac{{D}_{1}}{\mathit{r²}}\\ {ϵ}_{zz}={ϵ}_{r\theta }={ϵ}_{\mathit{rz}}={ϵ}_{\theta z}=0\end{array}$                                     éq 2.8

And for the solid 2:

$\begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\mathit{rr}}={C}_{1}\\ {ϵ}_{\theta \theta }={C}_{1}\\ {ϵ}_{zz}={ϵ}_{r\theta }={ϵ}_{\mathit{rz}}={ϵ}_{\theta z}=0\end{array}$                                     éq 2.9

Applying the Hooke law:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}=\lambda \mathit{tr}(\underline{\underline{ϵ}})\underline{\underline{1}}+2\mu \underline{\underline{ϵ}}$                             éq 2.10

the following general form is obtained for the constraints for solid 1:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{{E}_{1}}{1+{\nu}_{1}}(\frac{{C}_{1}}{1-2{\nu}_{1}}-\frac{{D}_{1}}{\mathit{r²}})\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{E}_{1}}{1+{\nu}_{1}}(\frac{{C}_{1}}{1-2{\nu}_{1}}+\frac{{D}_{1}}{\mathit{r²}})\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=\frac{2{\nu}_{1}{E}_{1}{C}_{1}}{(1+{\nu}_{1})(1-2{\nu}_{1})}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}={\sigma}_{\theta z}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                                 éq 2.11

and the following general form for the constraints for solid 2:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{{E}_{2}}{1+{\nu}_{2}}(\frac{{C}_{2}}{1-2{\nu}_{2}})\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{E}_{2}}{1+{\nu}_{2}}(\frac{{C}_{2}}{1-2{\nu}_{2}})\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=\frac{2{\nu}_{2}{E}_{2}{C}_{2}}{(1+{\nu}_{2})(1-2{\nu}_{2})}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{\mathit{rz}}={\sigma}_{\theta z}=\underline{0}\end{array}$                                 éq 2.12

We're laying:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{{E}_{1}}{(1+{\nu}_{1})(1-2{\nu}_{1})}{C}_{1}\text{}{B}_{1}=\frac{{E}_{1}}{1+{\nu}_{1}}{D}_{1}\text{}{A}_{2}=\frac{{E}_{2}}{(1+{\nu}_{2})(1-2{\nu}_{2})}{C}_{2}\end{array}$                      éq 2.13

All we have to do is calculate the ${A}_{1,}{B}_{1,}{A}_{2}$ values. ${\lambda}_{n}$ is the contact pressure between the two crowns, such as:

$\begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{1rr}}({R}_{2}).(-{\underline{e}}_{r})={\lambda}_{n}{\underline{e}}_{r}\\ {\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{2rr}}({R}_{2}).{\underline{e}}_{r}=-{\lambda}_{n}{\underline{e}}_{r}\end{array}$                                     éq 2.14

with boundary conditions:

$\begin{array}{c}{\underline{\underline{\sigma}}}_{\mathrm{1rr}}({R}_{1}).{\underline{e}}_{r}=-\mathit{p.}{\underline{e}}_{r}\end{array}$                                     éq 2.15

The condition of continuity on the interface movement between the two crowns in contact gives further:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r;1}(\mathit{R2})={u}_{r;2}(\mathit{R2})\end{array}$                                     éq 2.16

So we have 4 equations for the 4 unknown $\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1},{B}_{1},{A}_{2,}{\lambda}_{n}\end{array}$ .

The system of the first 3 equations allows us to obtain:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{-p{R}_{1}^{2}+{\lambda}_{n}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}};{B}_{1}=(-p+{\lambda}_{n})\frac{{R}_{1}^{2}{R}_{2}^{2}}{{R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2}}\\ {A}_{2}=-{\lambda}_{n}\end{array}$                             éq 2.17

and the continuity equation on the displacement finally allows to have the contact pressure:

${\lambda}_{n}=\frac{2p{R}_{1}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{1})}{{R}_{1}^{2}+{R}_{2}^{2}(1-2{\nu}_{1})+\frac{{E}_{1}}{{E}_{2}}\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{1+{\nu}_{1}}(1-2{\nu}_{2})({R}_{1}^{2}-{R}_{2}^{2})}$                 éq 2.18

### 2.2 Variable pressure

Using the z-invariance hypothesis of flat deformations, the solution of the problem takes the following form:

> $\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}={u}_{r}(r,\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}={u}_{\theta}(r,\theta )\\ {u}_{z}=0\end{array}$ éq 2.19

Throughout the rest, the parameters specific to each solid will be noted by an i index, with i=1.2.

In the absence of volume forces, a form of the Airy function proposed by Michell [1], in polar coordinates, will be used:

$\begin{array}{c}\chi (r,\theta )={A}_{01}{r}^{2}+{A}_{02}{r}^{2}\log(r)+{A}_{03}\log(r)+{A}_{04}\theta \\ +({A}_{11}{r}^{3}+{A}_{12}\mathit{rlog}(r)+{A}_{13}{r}^{-1})\cos(\theta )+{A}_{14}r\theta \sin(\theta )\\ +({B}_{11}{r}^{3}+{B}_{12}r\log(r)+{B}_{13}{r}^{-1})\sin(\theta )+{B}_{14}r\theta \cos(\theta )\\ +\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}({A}_{\mathit{n1}}{r}^{n+2}+{A}_{\mathit{n2}}{r}^{-n+2}+{A}_{\mathit{n3}}{r}^{n}+{A}_{\mathit{n4}}{r}^{-n})\cos(n\theta )\\ +\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}({B}_{\mathit{n1}}{r}^{n+2}+{B}_{\mathit{n2}}{r}^{-n+2}+{B}_{\mathit{n3}}{r}^{n}+{B}_{\mathit{n4}}{r}^{-n})\sin(n\theta )\end{array}$ éq 2.20

The terms of the non-zero Cauchy stress tensor are expressed in terms of the function of Airy as follows:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial r}+\frac{1}{{r}^{2}}\frac{{\partial}^{2}\chi }{\partial {\theta}^{2}}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{\partial}^{2}\chi }{\partial {r}^{2}}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }=\frac{-\partial }{\partial r}(\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial \theta })\\ {\sigma}_{zz}=\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })\end{array}$     éq 2.21

Our $p={\alpha}_{1}.\cos(2\theta )$ pressure varies in $\cos(2\theta )$, we will only take the portion that varies in $\cos(2\theta )$ in the Airy function. The Airy function will then be written:

$\chi (r,\theta )=(A{r}^{2}+B{r}^{4}+\frac{C}{{r}^{2}}+D)\cos(2\theta )$ éq 2.22

From there, non-zero stresses can be expressed in the polar marker for solid 1:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{1}=(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}-6\frac{{C}_{1}}{{r}^{4}}-4\frac{{D}_{1}}{{r}^{2}})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }^{1}=({\mathrm{2A}}_{1}+{\mathrm{12B}}_{1}{r}^{2}+6\frac{{C}_{1}}{{r}^{4}})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{1}=2({A}_{1}+{\mathrm{3B}}_{1}{r}^{2}-3\frac{{C}_{1}}{{r}^{4}}-\frac{{D}_{1}}{{r}^{2}})\sin(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{zz}^{1}=\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })\end{array}$         éq 2.23

And for the solid 2:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}=(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{2})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }^{2}=({\mathrm{2A}}_{2}+{\mathrm{12B}}_{2}{r}^{2})\cos(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{2}=2({A}_{2}+{\mathrm{3B}}_{2}{r}^{2})\sin(2\theta )\\ {\sigma}_{zz}^{2}=\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })\end{array}$ éq 2.24

and the terms of the deformation tensor using the Hooke law:

$\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}=\frac{1}{E}((1+\nu )\underline{\underline{\sigma}}-\nu \mathit{tr}(\underline{\underline{\sigma}})\underline{\underline{I}})$ éq 2.25

Deformity expression will be used to express displacements in the polar marker.

On a:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}={\epsilon}_{\mathit{rr}}\\ \frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }=r{\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }-{u}_{r}\\ \frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \theta }-\frac{{u}_{\theta}}{r})={\epsilon}_{r\theta }\end{array}$                             éq 2.26

By integrating these relationships and using the symmetries of the problem, one can express the movements for solid 1:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}^{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{1}r+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{r}^{3}}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{r})-{\nu}_{1}({\mathrm{4B}}_{1}{r}^{3}+4\frac{{D}_{1}}{r})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}^{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{{E}_{1}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{1}r+{\mathrm{6B}}_{1}{r}^{3}+2\frac{{C}_{1}}{{r}^{3}}-2\frac{{D}_{1}}{r})-{\nu}_{1}({\mathrm{4B}}_{1}{r}^{3}-4\frac{{D}_{1}}{r})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$     éq 2.27

And for the solid 2:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{r}^{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[(-{\mathrm{2A}}_{2}r)-{\nu}_{2}({\mathrm{4B}}_{2}{r}^{3})]\cos(2\theta )\\ {u}_{\theta}^{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{{E}_{2}}[({\mathrm{2A}}_{2}r+{\mathrm{6B}}_{2}{r}^{3})-{\nu}_{1}({\mathrm{4B}}_{1}{r}^{3})]\sin(2\theta )\end{array}$     éq 2.28

Now that we have expressed all our fields according to ${A}_{1,}{B}_{1,}{C}_{1,}{D}_{1,}{A}_{2,}{B}_{2}$ constants, we have to calculate them according to geometrical characteristics and loading.

The contact pressure between the two solids shall be noted.

The boundary conditions are:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{1}({R}_{1})=-{\alpha}_{1}\cos(2\theta ):\text{pression externe appliquée}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{1}({R}_{1})=0:\text{pression tangentielle nulle sur le bord extérieur du solide 1}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{2}({R}_{2})=-\lambda :\text{pression de contact appliquée par le solide 1 sur le solide 2}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathit{rr}}^{1}({R}_{2})=-\lambda :\text{pression de contact appliquée par le solide 2 sur le solide 1}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{1}({R}_{2})=0:\text{pas de frottement entre les deux solides}\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }^{2}({R}_{2})=0:\text{pas de frottement entre les deux solides}\end{array}$

So we have 6 equations for the 6 unknowns: ${A}_{1,}{B}_{1,}{C}_{1,}{D}_{1,}{A}_{2,}{B}_{2,}{C}_{2,}{D}_{2}$ .

By posing: ${f}_{1}={(\frac{{R}_{2}}{{R}_{1}})}^{2}$

The system of 6 equations allows us to have:

$\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1}=\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+{f}_{1}^{2}+2{f}_{1})}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{B}_{1}=\frac{-1}{{R}_{2}^{2}}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}(3{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1})-\lambda ({f}_{1}^{3}+3{f}_{1}^{2})}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};\\ {C}_{1}={R}_{2}^{4}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}+3)-\lambda ({\mathrm{3f}}_{1}+1)}{6{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}};{D}_{1}=-{R}_{2}^{2}\frac{{\alpha}_{1}({f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+2)-\lambda (2{f}_{1}^{2}+{f}_{1}+1)}{2{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}\\ {A}_{2}=\frac{\lambda}{2};{B}_{2}=\frac{-\lambda }{{\mathrm{6R}}_{2}^{2}}\end{array}$ éq 2.29

The contact pressure can be expressed analytically

Using the continuity of radial displacement at the contact interface:

${u}_{r}^{1}({R}_{2})={u}_{r}^{2}({R}_{2})$ éq 2.30

The contact pressure can be expressed analytically:

$\lambda =\frac{{\mathit{coef}}_{1}}{{\mathit{coef}}_{2}+{\mathit{coef}}_{3}}{\alpha}_{1}$ éq 2.31

such that:

$\begin{array}{c}{\mathit{coef}}_{1}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-12{f}_{1}^{2}-8{f}_{1}-12)+{\nu}_{1}(12{f}_{1}^{2}+8{f}_{1}+12)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{2}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{1}}{6{E}_{1}{(1-{f}_{1})}^{3}}[(-3{f}_{1}^{3}-15{f}_{1}^{2}-9{f}_{1}-5)+{\nu}_{1}(2{f}_{1}^{3}+18{f}_{1}^{2}+6{f}_{1}+6)]\\ {\mathit{coef}}_{3}=\frac{1+{\nu}_{2}}{6{E}_{2}}(-3+2{\nu}_{2})\end{array}$ éq 2.32

## 9 Conclusion

This test case allows the continuous contact formulation combined with X-FEM to be validated in the presence of curved contact surfaces with linear and quadratic grids, in 2D. It was possible to establish convergence orders for the continuous contact method combined with X-FEM and to verify a convergence of energy of 1 and displacement of 2 for linear grids, 2 for energy and 3 for quadratic grids, for sufficiently rich integration schemes, when the contact interface is mesh compliant and the mesh is radiating or non-compliant.
