# v5.01.121 SDND121 – Spring mass-responding system with forced excitation shock

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a transient analysis by modal recombination of a discrete nonlinear system to an degree of freedom. Nonlinearity consists of a contact with shock on a rigid plane. The mass is subjected to forced excitation. The problem is stiff, i.e. the stiffness is very different between the free flight phases and the contact phases. This problem allows to test the energy balance for the different patterns of temporal integration, as well as kinematics.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a rigid slider modeled by a single node. The slider is equipped with a spring reminder. It is subjected to an ${F}_{\mathit{ext}}(t)=a\sin(2\pi ft)$ harmonic external force. It is carried a normal shock condition against a rigid plane, treated by penalization.

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Geometry**

### 1.2 Material properties

Properties of the structure :

$m=156\mathrm{kg}$

${k}_{\mathit{rappel}}=2\cdot {10}^{6}N/m$

Impact stop properties:

${k}_{\mathrm{choc}}={10}^{10}N/m$

${c}_{\mathrm{choc}}=0\mathrm{Ns}/m$

${\mathrm{jeu}}_{\mathrm{choc}}=1\mathrm{mm}$

### 1.3 Initial conditions, at limits and loading

The slider leaves with zero initial conditions: ${x}_{0}={x}_{t=0}=0$ and $\dot{x}{}_{0}=\dot{x}{}_{t=0}=0$ .

He's moving in one direction.

The external force is sinusoidal: ${F}_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)=\mathrm{Fa}\cdot \sin(2\pi ft)$ , with $\mathrm{Fa}=3\cdot {10}^{3}N$ and $f=5\mathrm{Hz}$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

In a free flight phase, the equation of motion is written $m\ddot{x}+{k}_{\mathrm{rappel}}x={F}_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)$. Given the sinusoidal form of ${F}_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)$, this equation allows an analytical solution. It is possible to calculate numerically, with as great precision as you want, the ${t}_{in}$ moment of input into the contact, checking $x({t}_{in})={\mathrm{jeu}}_{\mathrm{choc}}$.

The $m\ddot{x}+{c}_{\mathrm{choc}}\dot{x}+({k}_{\mathrm{rappel}}+{k}_{\mathrm{choc}})x={F}_{\mathrm{ext}}(t)$ is the same as the analytical solution. With the boundary conditions from the contact moment, the ${t}_{\text{out}}$ is numerically calculated for the contact output moment.

By doing so it is possible to obtain the complete solution of the problem.

Note: The analytical formulae are not given here. Files containing formulae and allowing the calculation of the complete solution are attached with the command file.

### 2.2 Baseline

The energy balance, the match between contact forces and kinematics, and kinematics are tested.

For the energy balance, ${E}_{i}^{\mathrm{cin}}=\frac{1}{2}m\dot{x}{}_{i}^{2}$ kinetic energies, potential ${E}_{i}^{\mathit{pot}}=\frac{1}{2}{k}_{\mathit{rappel}}{x}_{i}^{2}$ , of ${E}_{i}^{\mathit{choc}}=\frac{1}{2}{k}_{\mathit{choc}}{p}_{i}^{2}$ shock ($p$ is penetration; this expression is valid only if there is no shock damping), injected by the external force ${E}_{i}^{\mathrm{inj}}=\sum_{j=1}^{i}{f}_{j}^{\mathrm{ext}}\dot{x}{}_{j+\delta \frac{1}{2}}\Delta t$ (with $\delta =1$ for Euler's schema, $\delta =0$ for the centered differences schema). The total energy ${E}_{i}^{\mathit{tot}}={E}_{i}^{\mathit{cin}}+{E}_{i}^{\mathit{pot}}+{E}_{i}^{\mathit{choc}}$ is obtained. Finally, the overall error on the energy balance is calculated by ${\mathit{erreur}}_{\mathit{globale}}^{\mathit{énergie}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i}{({E}_{i}^{\mathit{tot}}-{E}_{i}^{\mathit{inj}})}^{2}}{\sum_{i}{({E}_{i}^{\mathit{inj}})}^{2}}}$ , which is ideally 0.

For the matching of contact forces and kinematics, the quantity ${\mathit{erreur}}_{\mathit{globale}}^{\mathit{force}}=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i}{({F}_{i}^{\mathit{choc}}-{k}_{\mathit{choc}}{p}_{i})}^{2}}{\sum_{i}{({k}_{\mathit{choc}}{p}_{i})}^{2}}}$ is calculated, which is ideally 0.

For kinematics, the calculated moments of input and output are compared to the analytical moments.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

The solution is analytical in pieces. The moments of input and output of contact are determined numerically to the nearest ${10}^{-9}s$.

## 3 Modelling

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The slider is modeled by an mesh cell to an node (type POI1), located at rest in $O=(0,0,0)$. The obstacle is of type 'PLAN_Z'. To avoid any shock with the symmetrical plane, the center of both planes is sufficiently shifted: ORIG_OBST=(-0.5.0.0.,0.,). As the real game between the slider and the right plane must be $1\mathit{mm}$ , an artificial game JEU=(0.5+Jeu_choc) is used.

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.121/100000000000031A000001540392815A8755E460.tif)

**Figure** 3.1-a **: Modeled Geometry**

The time integration is performed over a duration $T=4s$ either with the Euler schema or with the pattern of the centred differences ('ADAPT_ORDRE2' that is forced to a constant time step thanks to the parameters COEF_MULT_PAS=1.0, COEF_DIVI_PAS=1.0). The problem is stiff (the ratio of the stiffeners between the free flight and contact phases is about 5000), so we are forced to use a very low time step of $\Delta t=4\cdot {10}^{-6}s$.

A calculation is also made with 'ADAPT_ORDRE2' actually variable steps, whose purpose is not precision, but simply checking the match between contact forces and kinematics.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

> The mesh consists of a single node and a single mesh cell of the POI1 type.

## 4 Summary of the results

The results are very similar to analytical solution, both for the Euler diagram and for the pattern of the centred differences. The stiffness change moments obtained by these two diagrams are identical.

Energy balance sheets have an error of less than $0,1J$ in both cases. This value is to be relativized with the very low time step which tends to reduce differences, but such a low time step is necessary to deal with this stiff problem.

Finally, there is a perfect fit (to the numerical accuracy close to) between contact forces and kinematics, which means that the processing of contact by penalisation is done correctly.
