# v6.03.143 SSNP143 – Validation of clavage/sawing for dam seal law

**Summary:**

Two industrial procedures on dams are tested: clavage and sawing.

The clavage is a pressure injection of concrete between the platings of a dam. It is an intermediate step in the construction of a vault dam, it is used to strengthen its seal after the construction phase of vertical plating. The sawing is a similar procedure during which the dam is sawn to release the stresses. This can occur before or after the watering. This test allows to validate both procedures by clavage/sawing between the two regular platings, which are either clamped on the ground and subjected to gravity force (modellings A, B, E, F, G, H), or compressed on the sides (modellings C and D). The procedure is defined via a PRES_CLAVAGE and SCIAGE key word in DEFI_MATERIAU for the material JOINT_MECA_RUPT/FROT. The corresponding laws bear the same names. The behaviour of the two-dimensional and three-dimensional joints, corresponding to modellings: PLAN_JOINT and 3D_JOINT.

Modelling A: Industrial Clavage 2D; JOINT_MECA_RUPT law

Modelling B: Industrial 3D clavage; JOINT_MECA_RUPT law

Modelling C: 2D theoretical sawing; JOINT_MECA_RUPT/FROT laws

Modelling D: 3D theoretical sawing; JOINT_MECA_RUPT/FROT laws

Modelling E: 2D sawing; JOINT_MECA_RUPT law

Modelling F: 3D industrial sawing; JOINT_MECA_RUPT law

Modelling G: 2D sawing; JOINT_MECA_FROT law

Modelling H: 3D industrial sawing; JOINT_MECA_FROT law

For clavage, the thickness profile of the joints after clavage is compared with the results provided by another calculation code (SOURCE_EXTERNE *GEFDYN)* . For sawing a comparison is made with a theoretical estimate (modelization C and D). NON_REGRESSION tests are also carried out of the values of the thickness of the joint, together with corresponding forces.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and loading

Two dam pads are considered, represented by regular cubes of length $L=\mathrm{5m}$ , height $H=\mathrm{10m}$ and depth $P=\mathrm{1m}$ . The distance between the pads is assumed not to be zero to simplify the generation of the mesh of the joints ($\epsilon =\mathrm{1mm}$ ). The clamped support varies according to the modellings.

#### 1.1.1 modellings A,B,E,F,G,H

The lower parts of the plots are clamped. The plots are subject to their gravity weights. The loading takes place in two stages:

- The plots are balanced under gravity loading. The joint opens in the upper part because of the compression of the lower parts of the plots. One of the two industrial procedures is subsequently applied:

    - The clavage procedure is activated: "concrete is injected" between the PRES_CLAVAGE=$5{10}^{4}\mathit{Pa}$ pads. The profile of the joint thickness is then changed to obtain the clavage pressure on the crack lips. This profile is then stored by one of the internal variables to "memorize" the amount of concrete injected.
    - The sawing procedure is activated via SCIAGE key word. The thickness of the ${\delta}_{\mathit{scie}}$ saw is selected so that the top part of the plot is only partially sawn.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Clash procedure

We take as reference the solution given by the calculation code *GEFDYN* . This procedure has been validated on many industrial works. The opening of the seal after clavage is tested.

### 2.2 Sawing procedure

As with sawing we do not have a solution from an external code. This is then theoretically validated in modellings C and D, which accept a $\mathrm{1D}$ solution.

First, a homogeneous compression ${\delta}_{\mathit{impo}}$ is applied to the left side of the plot. The load is almost $\mathrm{1D}$ and thus the stress equivalence is present in the joint and in the plot, allowing us to estimate the value of the stress applied $\sigma$:

| ${\delta}_{\mathit{plot}}+{\delta}_{\mathit{joint}}={\delta}_{\mathit{impo}}$ | $\sigma 2L/E+\sigma /\text{pena\_contact}/{K}_{N}={\delta}_{\mathit{impo}}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

This gives:

$\sigma ={\delta}_{\mathit{impo}}/(\frac{1}{2LE}+\frac{1}{\text{pena\_contact}{K}_{N}})$

Once the constraint is known, the constitutive law can be applied to find the joint opening: ${\delta}_{\mathit{joint}}=\sigma /\text{pena\_contact}/{K}_{N}$ .

In case of sawing the value of ${\delta}_{\mathit{impo}}$ is reduced by the thickness of the saw, which sets the sawing.

These values of $\sigma \text{et}{\delta}_{\mathit{joint}}$ are subsequently tested in modellings C and D.

## 3 Modelling A: 2D JOINT_MECA_RUPT clavage

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The simulation is performed with PLAN_JOINT Modelling. elements is of the TRIA3 type for the plots and QUAD4 for the elements joint. The corresponding constitutive law is JOINT_MECA_RUPT, the associated material is the same name. The elements surfactic are elastic.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

A linear mesh of two plots and crack is made.

Elements density (plates): 548 TRIA3

Elements joint: 20 QUAD4

The first test is performed on the profile values of the joint thickness after clavage by comparing it with the results of GEFDYN. ${\delta}_{n}$ ($\mathit{V7}$ ) is noted the normal joint opening after clavage:

| --- | --- | --- |
| ${\delta}_{n}$ height: $2m$ | 6.38e-7 | 6 % |
| ${\delta}_{n}$ height: $5m$ | 2.14e-6 | 7% |
| ${\delta}_{n}$ height: $8m$ | 3.88e-6 | 7% |

#### 3.3.2 Non-regression Tests

Normal ${\delta}_{n}$ ($\mathit{V7}$) joint opening values, as well as normal ${\sigma}_{n}$ stresses, are tested at different heights ($2m,4.5m,8.5m$ ).

## 11 Summary of the results

The clavage procedure implemented in *Code_Aster* via JOINT_MECA_RUPT law and the PRES_CLAVAGE keyword coincides with the results of the *GEFDYN* code.

The sawing procedure implemented in *Code_Aster* for JOINT_MECA_RUPT and JOINT_MECA_FROT laws via the SCIAGE keyword is validated on a $\mathrm{1D}$ theoretical test, it gives good results on tests close to the actual situation on dams.
