# r4.04.04 Metallurgical constitutive models of Zircaloy

**Summary:**

This document presents the metallurgical constitutive models describing the structural transformations, on heating and on cooling, undergone by Zircaloy (fuel-rod cladding) between about $700°C$ and $1000°C$ .

## 1 Introduction

The fuel claddings of pressurised-water nuclear reactors are made of zirconium alloy. These alloys undergo metallurgical transformations between $700°C$ and $1000°C$ , where they pass from a phase of hexagonal close-packed structure to a phase of cubic structure. In certain analysis cases, such as the scenarios of a core accident by loss of primary coolant (LOCA, APRP), the cladding reaches temperatures of the order of $1000°C$ and then undergoes metallurgical transformations. In order to analyse the mechanical behaviour of the cladding in these situations, it is necessary to take into account the influence of the metallurgy on the mechanics (modification of the mechanical characteristics).

The present document concerns the modelling of the structural transformations of Zircaloy on heating and on cooling, and this at a scale which, while remaining “reasonable” for the metallurgist, is easily usable by the mechanical engineer.

The metallurgy analyses in *code_aster*  are carried out either during the integration of the mechanical constitutive law, or with the dedicated operator CALC_META, in “post-processing” of a thermal evolution analysis. In this second case, the choice of the model is made with the keyword RELATION= ‘ZIRC’ under COMPORTEMENT. This type of modelling can be carried out within *Code_Aster*  for all the elements (PLAN, AXIS, 3D) of the PHENOMENE "THERMIQUE". For the definition of the metallurgical constitutive behaviour of Zircaloy, the entry of the factor keyword META_ZIRC under the command DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) is necessary. Finally, the definition of the initial metallurgical state can be carried out by means of the command CREA_CHAMP, under the factor keyword ETAT_INIT of the operator CALC_META.

The models implemented in *Code_Aster*  in order to model the metallurgical transformations of Zircaloy are models developed by the CEA. These models have been identified on the basis of dilatometry and calorimetry tests for the cladding alloys (standard and new).

For the mechanical constitutive behaviour with the effect of the structural transformations, one has the choice between several models:

- either the same mechanical constitutive relations developed initially for the vessel steel 16MND5. These are all the relations declared in the operator STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword COMPORTEMENT of the type META_XX_XX. These are elastoplastic or elastoviscoplastic models, isotropic hardening (linear or non-linear) or kinematic, with or without taking into account the phenomena of transformation plasticity and of recovery of hardening of metallurgical origin (cf. R4.04.02 and R4.04.03).
- or the law specific to Zircaloy. This is a threshold-free anisotropic elastoviscous model (Hill matrix). The keyword under COMPORTEMENT is META_LEMA_ANI (cf. R4.04.05); in this case, the metallurgical phase is an internal variable of the constitutive law and is determined during the integration of the latter.

## 2 Presentation of the model

### 2.1 Proportion at equilibrium

Zircaloy has a hexagonal close-packed structure called phase $\alpha$, stable up to temperatures of the order of $700°C$. Beyond about $700°C$ an allotropic transformation towards a cubic phase $\beta$ begins, and it is complete in the neighbourhood of $975°C$ .

The proportion of the phase ${Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}$ at equilibrium is given by the following equation, of Johnson–Mehl–Avrami type:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}=0& \text{pour}& T\le {T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}\\ {Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}=1-\exp\left(-{(K(T-{T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}))}^{n}\right)& \text{pour}& {T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}\le T\le {T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}\\ {Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}=1& \text{pour}& T>{T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}\end{array}
$$

where ${T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ is the temperature of the start of the transformation $\alpha \iff \beta$ at equilibrium, ${T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ the temperature of the end of the transformation at equilibrium, $T$ the temperature and $K$ and $n$ two material parameters.

Equivalently, by inverting the equation , one obtains the equivalent temperature ${T}^{\mathit{eq}}$ as a function of the proportion ${Z}_{\beta}$ of phase $\beta$ :

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{T}^{\mathit{eq}}={T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}+\frac{1}{K}{\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{1-{Z}_{\beta}}\right)\right)}^{1/n}& \text{pour}0\le {Z}_{\beta}\le 0,99\\ {T}^{\mathit{eq}}={T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}& \text{pour}0,99\le {Z}_{\beta}\le 1\end{array}
$$

The temperature of the end of the transformation at equilibrium ${T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ is chosen such that it corresponds to a proportion $0,99$ of transformed phase $\beta$ , that is:

$$
{T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}={T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}+\frac{1}{K}{\left(\log\left(\frac{1}{1-0,99}\right)\right)}^{1/n}
$$

### 2.2 Evolution equation on heating

The transformation on heating is the transformation $\alpha \Rightarrow \beta$ .

The temperature of the start of the phase transformation on heating ${T}_{c}$ depends on the temperature rate on heating and is given by the equation:

$$
{T}_{c}={T}_{c}^{1}{({V}_{\mathit{ch}})}^{{T}_{c}^{2}}
$$

With ${V}_{\mathit{ch}}$ the heating rate in $°C/s$ and ${T}_{c}\ge {T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ .

The evolution model of the phase $\beta$ on heating is given by the following differential equation (Holt model):

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

${T}^{\mathit{eq}}({Z}_{\beta})$ is the equilibrium temperature corresponding to the instantaneous proportion ${Z}_{\beta}$ of phase $\beta$ and given by the equation . ${T}_{c}^{1}$ , ${T}_{c}^{2}$ , ${A}_{c}$ , $\frac{E}{R}$ and $M$ are material parameters.

### 2.3 Evolution equation on cooling

The transformation on cooling is the transformation $\beta \Rightarrow \alpha$ .

The temperature of the start of the phase transformation on cooling ${T}_{r}$ depends on the temperature rate on cooling and is given by the equation:

$$
{T}_{r}={T}_{r}^{1}+{T}_{r}^{2}\ln({V}_{\mathit{ref}})
$$

With ${V}_{\mathit{ref}}$ the cooling rate in $°C/s$ and ${T}_{r}\le {T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ .

The evolution model of the phase $\beta$ on cooling is given by the following differential equation:

| :math:frac{{mathit{dZ}}_{beta}}{mathit{dt}}=- | T-{T}^{mathit{eq}} | expleft({A}_{r}+{B}_{r} | T-{T}^{mathit{eq}} | right){Z}_{beta}(1-{Z}_{beta})` for ${T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}\le T\le {T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

${T}_{r}^{1}$ , ${T}_{r}^{2}$ , ${A}_{r}$ and ${B}_{r}$ are material parameters.

### 2.4 Conditions of use of the metallurgical model for arbitrary temperature transients

#### 2.4.1 A few rules

- During the analyses, if the proportion of phase $\beta$ is strictly greater than $0,99$ , one rounds to one. For a heating rate lower than $0.1°C/s$ , one uses ${T}_{c}={T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ .
- If $0\le {Z}_{\beta}\le 0,99$ , one must apply the following rule:

  - If $T>{T}^{\mathit{eq}}\iff {Z}_{\beta}<{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}$ , one applies the heating model (even if the temperature rate is negative)
  - If $T<{T}^{\mathit{eq}}\iff {Z}_{\beta}>{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}$ , one applies the cooling model (even if the temperature rate is positive)

#### 2.4.2 Algorithm

One considers an arbitrary temperature transient $T(t)$ .

| *Note: in order to compute the temperatures of the start of transformation on heating* ${T}_{c}$ *and on cooling* ${T}_{r}$ *, it is necessary to compute the heating and cooling rates, respectively. In order to compute them, one uses the sliding-secant method (and not the instantaneous rate), whence steps 1 and 2 below.* |
| --- |

| *Note: once the threshold temperatures* ${T}_{c}$ *or* ${T}_{r}$ *have been exceeded and for as long as the transformation is not complete (on heating or on cooling), one integrates the evolution equations even if the temperature passes back through the threshold.* |
| --- |

- *Step 1* : Search for the time instant ${t}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ (or ${t}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ ) corresponding to the temperature of the start ${T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ (or of the end ${T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ , respectively) of the transformation at equilibrium.
- Case where ${Z}_{\beta}=0$ initially: search for ${t}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$
- Case where ${Z}_{\beta}=1$ initially: search for ${t}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$
- *Step 2* : Search for the time instant ${t}_{c}$ (or ${t}_{r}$ ) corresponding to the temperature of the start of transformation ${T}_{c}$ (or ${T}_{r}$ , respectively) using the sliding-secant method:
- Case where ${Z}_{\beta}=0$ initially: search for the time instant at which the temperature $T(t)$ exceeds ${T}_{c}$ .
- If $T(t)>{T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ , one increments the time, one computes ${T}_{c}$ and one tests the following condition:

$$
\text{(C1)}:T(t)>{T}_{c}={T}_{c}^{1}{\left(\frac{T(t)-{T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}}{t-{t}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}}\right)}^{{T}_{c}^{2}}
$$

If the condition $(C1)$ is true one passes to step (3).

- If $T(t)\le {T}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ without ${T}_{c}$ having been reached, one must then update ${t}_{d}^{\mathit{eq}}$ by starting step 1 again from the current time instant.
- Case where ${Z}_{\beta}=1$ initially: search for the time instant at which the temperature $T(t)$ passes back through ${T}_{r}$ .
- If $T(t)<{T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ , one increments the time, one computes ${T}_{r}$ and one tests the following condition:

| :math:text{(C2)}:T(t)<{T}_{r}={T}_{r}^{1}+{T}_{r}^{2}lnleft(frac{ | T(t)-{T}_{f}^{mathit{eq}} | }{t-{t}_{f}^{mathit{eq}}}right)` |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

If the condition $(C2)$ is true one passes to step (3).

- If $T(t)\ge {T}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ without ${T}_{r}$ having been reached, one must then update ${t}_{f}^{\mathit{eq}}$ by starting step 1 again from the current time instant.
- *Step 3* : Once ${T}_{c}$ (or ${T}_{r}$ has been reached, one computes step by step the evolution of the fraction of phase $\beta$ appearing, by means of the Holt equation (on heating) or of the cooling equation according to the sign of $(Z-{Z}_{\mathit{eq}})$ and this for as long as the fraction of phase $\beta$ remains lower than $0,99$ and greater than zero, and even if one passes through a temperature peak.
- *Step 4* : If in the course of step 3 the fraction of phase $\beta$ becomes equal to 1 (or 0), one starts step 1 again from the current time instant.

## 3 Numerical formulation

Knowing at the previous time instant the temperature and the proportion of phase $\beta$ and at the current time instant the temperature, one seeks to determine the proportion of phase $\beta$ at the current time instant ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}$ .

At a given time instant, one seeks the solution ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}$ such that $G({Z}_{\beta}^{t})=0$ , an equation which is solved by a Newton method with controlled bounds:

$$
{Z}_{\beta}^{i+1}={Z}_{\beta}^{i}-\frac{G({Z}_{\beta}^{i})}{G'({Z}_{\beta}^{i})}
$$

The stopping criterion is given by the following condition:

$$
\text{Si}G({Z}_{\beta}^{i+1})\le {10}^{-6}\text{alors}{Z}_{\beta}^{i+1}={Z}_{\beta}^{t}
$$

### 3.1 Determination of the direction of the evolution

In order to know which model is to be integrated at a given time instant $t$ , it suffices to make the following observations:

- If ${Z}_{\beta}^{t-1}<{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ and if one integrates the cooling model, one will necessarily have ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}<{Z}_{\beta}^{t-1}<{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ . Now, this is contrary to the condition of application of the cooling model, which assumes that ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}>{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ . One must therefore choose the heating model.
- If ${Z}_{\beta}^{t-1}>{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ and if one integrates the heating model, one will necessarily have ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}>{Z}_{\beta}^{t-1}>{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ . Now, this is contrary to the condition of application of the heating model, which assumes that ${Z}_{\beta}^{t}<{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ . One must therefore choose the cooling model.

### 3.2 Integration of the equations

#### 3.2.1 Model for META_LEMA_ANI

In the case of a use of the mechanical constitutive law META_LEMA_ANI, the metallurgical phase is an internal variable of the constitutive law. It is therefore determined during the integration of the latter. The solution of these equations is then ensured by the code generator MFront.

### 3.3 Models for the other cases

In every other case, the integration is carried out in the following manner:

- **Heating model**: the solution is such that ${Z}_{\beta}^{t-1}\le {Z}_{\beta}^{t}<{Z}_{\beta}^{\mathit{eq}}({T}^{t})$ . The function ${G}_{c}({Z}_{\beta})$ and its derivative are given by:

| :math:G{'}_{c}({Z}_{beta})=1+MDelta t{A}_{c}expleft(-frac{E}{RT}right){ | T-{T}_{mathit{eq}} | }^{M-1}T{'}_{mathit{eq}}` |  |

### 4.2 Use of the model

In the case of a use of the mechanical constitutive law META_LEMA_ANI, the metallurgical phase is an internal variable of the constitutive law. It is therefore determined during the integration of the latter.

For every other constitutive law, the instructions below are to be respected.

In order to activate this model, it suffices to enter, in the command CALC_META, under the keyword COMPORTEMENT, the relation ‘ZIRC’ (COMPORTEMENT=_F(RELATION=’ZIRC’)).

The material parameters are entered under the factor keyword META_ZIRC of DEFI_MATERIAU.

Finally, the definition of the initial metallurgical state can be carried out by means of the command CREA_CHAMP, under the factor keyword ETAT_INIT of the operator CALC_META. It is obligatory to enter the internal variables V1, V2 and V4.
