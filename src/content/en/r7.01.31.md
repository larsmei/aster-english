# r7.01.31 Constitutive law for reinforced-concrete plates GLRC_DAMAGE

**Summary:**

This documentation presents the theoretical formulation and the numerical integration of the constitutive law GLRC_DAMAGE [bib1]. It is written in a global manner in terms of resultant forces and moments for finite-element modellings of plates. This law incorporates the elastic and damageable behaviour in bending coming from the concrete and the elastoplastic behaviour coming essentially from the steel reinforcement, on the basis of the material characteristics of the two materials and of the composition of the cross-section of the reinforced-concrete plate. The result is a cyclic damageable elastoplastic behaviour, suited to dynamic studies of reinforced-concrete structures. The present GLRC_DAMAGE model does not take membrane damage into account and is therefore not very accurate when the loads on the plate are dominated by membrane effects. On the other hand, the failure of a plate depends above all on the behaviour of the steel, modelled by the elastoplastic part of the model. One therefore considers that failure should be represented correctly even in membrane. A model similar to this one, GLRC_DM, is capable of representing membrane/bending damage better, but does not take into account the yielding phase of the steels and therefore cannot be used to simulate failure.

**Note:**  The orthotropic elasticity induced by the orthogonal network of reinforcement is taken into account only within the framework of a linear analysis. For the non-linear analysis, **this model must not be used** .

This constitutive law is used mainly for the chaining of analyses between code_aster and Europlexus. **Warning! It must be used only in the direction from code_aster towards Europlexus, and not in the other direction.**

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Global constitutive models

The global models represent the evolutions of the material within the structure under study – beam and plate – on the basis of a relation between the generalised strain quantities (extension, curvature, distortion) and the generalised forces (membrane forces, bending, shear forces). These models are « fitted » beforehand with the aid of a frozen local analysis (for example with the aid of the results of a limit analysis of the cross-sections), as a function of the characteristics of the concrete and steel materials constituting the plate and of the distribution of these within the cross-section, cf. []. The schematic principle is as follows:

$$
d(\epsilon ,\kappa ,\gamma )\underset{\begin{array}{c}\mathit{loi}\mathit{globale}\mathit{de}\mathit{comportement}\\ \uparrow \\ \mathit{analyse}\mathit{locale}\end{array}}{\to }d(N,M,T)
$$

This local analysis must take the various couplings into account: for example, the evolution in bending depends on the value of the applied normal force. The non-linear equilibrium of the structure is treated at the global level on the generalised forces, via the plate kinematics considered.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.31/Object_2.svg)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Reinforced-concrete slab.**

Since the local analysis is implemented only in pre-processing (within the framework of a monotonic-load analysis), one has no immediate means of returning, in the course of the analysis, to the local analysis of the stresses from the generalised internal forces. Indeed, the dissipative character of the irreversible constitutive laws requires one to store, in the course of the cycles, the evolution of the internal variables at every time instant if one wishes to compute the stresses at a particular point. One could envisage launching in parallel the three-dimensional constitutive law according to [eq 1.1-1] and integrating through the thickness in order to return to a global behaviour, but the cost and the complexity of such an approach seem an obstacle. It should be noted that this could be a means of estimating the error committed by a global constitutive law. On the other hand, this approach has not yet been adopted in *Code_Aster* .

This type of model can usefully be validated by comparison with a direct analysis carried out with a local model.

### 1.2 Objectives of the law GLRC_DAMAGE

The initial formulation of the global reinforced-concrete plate model GLRC_DAMAGE, established by Koechlin in 2002, is found in [bib1], [bib2] and [bib3].

This model was first developed for dynamic applications with collapse under impact of reinforced-concrete structures. The elastoplastic response of the model is essential for this type of application. Indeed, the dissipation of energy by yielding of the steels is significant. Taking into account the damage by cracking of the concrete makes it possible to make the first phases of the non-linear behaviour more accurate. Within the framework of seismic applications, one may expect a reversed situation: the damage and the response after cracking are essential, whereas it is rare to go as far as mobilising the generalised yielding of the steels. It nevertheless seems advantageous to have one and the same model in order to treat these two families of applications.

The formulation of the model is established within the framework of the thermodynamics of irreversible processes. It combines plasticity with hardening, brought in particular by the steels, and the damage brought by the concrete cracking during the bending of the plate. The plastic behaviour is constructed on the basis of the limit analysis in bending of a reinforced-concrete plate. It is described with the aid of the framework of generalised standard materials. On the basis of experimental results, cf. [bib1], a linear kinematic hardening has been chosen in order to treat the cyclic behaviour. The damage is introduced in order to represent the loss of elastic stiffness which takes place by cracking of the concrete before the yielding of the steels. The damage threshold is assumed constant. This behaviour is assumed independent of the loading rates (the dissipations are instantaneous).

## 2 Formulation of the model

One presents below the formulation of the model GLRC_DAMAGE, under the formalism of the thermodynamics of irreversible processes.

One must note that the use of this model is associated with that of a plate element. If one chooses the family of finite elements **DKT**  (supported modelling: DKTG), one adopts the **Love-Kirchhoff** theory, that is to say that one considers no transverse distortion through the thickness of the plate. The model GLRC_DAMAGE could be usable with the thick-plate finite elements Q4G, but this extension has not yet been carried out.

The mesh cell of the finite element is assumed to be placed on the mid-surface of the slab (at $z=0$ ).

With a view to being able to use the constitutive model GLRC_DAMAGE in two different types of analysis, one has chosen, according to the case:

1. **for a linear elastic analysis of a reinforced-concrete plate:**  to take into account the orthotropy induced by the orthogonal network of steel reinforcement, as well as the bending–membrane coupling in the case of unequal reinforcement layers, via a homogenised steel–concrete elastic behaviour;
2. **for a non-linear damageable elastoplastic analysis of a reinforced-concrete plate:**  to neglect the orthotropy and the bending–membrane coupling in the elastic phase. This assumption makes it possible to simplify the model, by supposing that, in the presence of strongly non-linear phenomena, the orthotropic elasticity becomes negligible, especially when modelling failure. Moreover, in practice one expects the walls, shear walls and the other structural elements to be reinforced more or less equally between the two principal orthogonal directions. This has the effect of reducing the effect of the elastic orthotropy. On the other hand, one very often chooses asymmetric reinforcement in order to optimise it as a function of the direction of the loading due to self-weight. This tends to induce a membrane–bending coupling in elasticity in addition to that in plasticity. However, even if the model neglects the asymmetry in elasticity, its influence in elastoplasticity, the predominant behaviour at failure, can be controlled through threshold functions, which themselves may be asymmetric.

The model is defined by the description of the state variables, which represent the mechanical system at each material point of the middle surface of the plate, the surface density of free energy which encompasses the form of the constitutive relations and the type of hardening, the expression of the plasticity and damage criteria, and the irreversible evolution laws, deduced from Drücker's principle of maximum work.

### 2.1 State variables

The global state variables are the following. First of all the global strain variables:

1. a membrane strain tensor: $\epsilon$ defined in the tangent plane of the plate.
2. a curvature tensor: $\kappa$ defined in the tangent plane of the plate.

Then the internal variables:

1. two damage variables associated with the upper part, ${d}_{1}$ and the lower part, ${d}_{2}$ of the plate. They are each capped at a value, ${d}_{1}^{max}$ and ${d}_{2}^{max}$ .
2. two plastic curvature tensors associated with the yielding of the steel layers, upper and lower ${\kappa}_{1}^{p},{\kappa}_{2}^{p}$ .
3. two plastic membrane strain tensors associated with the yielding of the steel layers, upper and lower, ${\epsilon}_{1}^{p},{\epsilon}_{2}^{p}$ .
4. a second-order tensor of kinematic-hardening internal variables $\alpha$ .

### 2.2 Free energy: linear elastic case

The surface density of free energy is an additive expression of the elastic membrane and bending contributions:

$$
{\Phi}_{e}^{S}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon :{H}_{m}:\epsilon +\frac{1}{2}\kappa :{H}_{f}:\kappa +\epsilon :{H}_{\mathit{mf}}:\kappa
$$

The tensors ${H}_{m}$ , ${H}_{f}$ , ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}$ (bending–membrane coupling in the case of unequal reinforcement layers through the thickness) are described in [§3.1]. In the present state of the model, one assumes that:

${H}_{\mathit{mf}}=0$

therefore that the plate is symmetric and that there is no elastic membrane–bending coupling. In the model, the membrane–bending coupling can appear only as a result of an evolution towards elastoplasticity (cf. §2.5).

### 2.3 Free energy: damageable elastoplastic case

In this case, one neglects the elastic orthotropy induced by the reinforcement in the two directions of the plane, as well as the elastic bending–membrane coupling (for unsymmetric reinforcement layers). One therefore treats the steel reinforcement as an isotropic elastic membrane, cf. [].

The surface density of free energy is an additive expression of the elastoplastic membrane contributions, the damageable elastoplastic bending contributions, and the kinematic hardening:

$$
{\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}^{S}(\epsilon ,{\epsilon}^{p},\kappa ,{\kappa}^{p},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2,}\alpha )={\Phi}_{e,m}^{S}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})+{\Phi}_{\mathit{ed},f}^{S}(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})+{\Phi}_{p}^{S}(\alpha )+H({d}_{j}-{d}_{j}^{max})
$$

with the hardening energy:

$$
{\Phi}_{p}^{S}(\alpha )=\frac{1}{2}\alpha :C:\alpha
$$

where $C$ is a Prager kinematic-hardening tensor. In practice the tensor $C$ is diagonal, with a coefficient ${C}_{m}$ in membrane and another ${C}_{f}$ in bending; one therefore has:

$C=(\begin{array}{cccccc}{C}_{m}& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& {C}_{m}& 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& {C}_{m}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& {C}_{f}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& {C}_{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& {C}_{f}\end{array})$

In [eq 2.3.1], $H$ denotes an indicator function of the domain of admissibility of the thermodynamic potential. Concretely, it serves to limit the evolution of the damage above ${d}_{j}^{max}$ . The ${d}_{j}^{max}$ are identified by [eq 3.2.11].

The membrane and bending energy densities are given by:

1. In membrane:

$$
{\Phi}_{e,m}^{S}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})=\frac{1}{2}(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p}):{H}_{m}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})
$$

1. In bending:

$$
{\Phi}_{\mathit{ed},f}^{S}(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathit{tr}{(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})}^{2}{\xi}_{f}(\mathit{tr}(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p}),{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})}}_{i}^{2}{\xi}_{f}({\tilde{(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})}}_{i},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})
$$

where one introduces the Lamé parameters in bending ${\lambda}_{f}$ and ${\mu}_{f}$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{\lambda}_{f}=\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\lambda \\ {\mu}_{f}=\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\mu \end{array}$

$h$ being the thickness of the plate and $\lambda$ , $\mu$ the Lamé coefficients of the homogenised material.

${\tilde{(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})}}_{i}$ denotes the $i$ -th eigenvalue of $\kappa -{\kappa}^{p}$ . One will subsequently denote by ${\kappa}^{e}$ the elastic curvature-variation tensor defined according to the assumption of partition of the bending strains by: ${\kappa}^{e}=\kappa -{\kappa}^{p}$ .

And finally, one defines the characteristic function of the bending damage ${\xi}_{f}$ :

$$
{\xi}_{f}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=\frac{1+\gamma {d}_{1}}{1+{d}_{1}}H(x)+\frac{1+\gamma {d}_{2}}{1+{d}_{2}}H(-x)
$$

In this expression $H$ is the Heaviside function and $\gamma$ a damage parameter lying between 0 and 1. This function ${\xi}_{f}$ characterises the reduction of the stiffnesses by damage. It is decreasing for ${d}_{1}$ , ${d}_{2}$ positive. It is convex (thanks to the choice of $\gamma$ , identified by the procedure described in [§3.2.2]) in order to ensure the stability of the « material » of the reinforced-concrete slab.

### 2.4 Damageable elastoplastic constitutive law

The damageable elastoplastic constitutive law (state law) supplies the dual variables: the membrane forces, the bending moments, which are second-order tensors defined on the tangent plane of the plate, the damage forces and the irreversible hardening tensors. They are written:

1. Membrane force:

$$
N=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}^{S}}{\partial \epsilon }={H}_{m}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})
$$

1. Bending moment:

$$
M=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}^{S}}{\partial \kappa }={H}_{f}^{d}(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2}):(\kappa -{\kappa}^{p})
$$

The membrane elasticity tensor ${H}_{m}$ is given in [§3.1], whereas ${H}_{f}^{d}$ is the damageable elasticity tensor which depends on the damage variables ${d}_{1}$ , ${d}_{2}$ and also on the signs of certain components of ${\kappa}^{e}=\kappa -{\kappa}^{p}$ (of the trace and of the eigenvalues, in particular). One recalls that in [eq. 2.4.1] and [eq. 2.4.2] the elastic membrane–bending coupling is neglected (see [§2.2]). Moreover, because of the presence of the eigenvalues of the elastic curvatures in the expression of the free energy (see [eq. 2.3.4]), one computes the generalised stresses by using equations [eq. 2.4.1], [eq. 2.4.2] in the principal frame. The details of the transformation between the frames are available in [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32). One also specifies that, according to the assumption of isotropic elasticity, the membrane–bending coupling is due solely to the elastoplastic process through ${\epsilon}^{p}$ and ${\kappa}^{p}$ (cf. §2.5).

**Note:**

*One observes that the principal frame of the moments is the same as that of the elastic curvatures. Likewise, the principal frame of the membrane forces is the same as that of the* *elastic strains. In the absence of damage,* ${\xi}_{f}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=1$ *: one indeed recovers an isotropic elastic plate behaviour.*

1. Damage forces, for $j=1,2$ :

$$
{Y}_{j}=-\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}}{\partial {d}_{j}}=\frac{1-\gamma }{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathit{tr}{({\kappa}^{e})}^{2}H({(-1)}^{j}\mathit{tr}({\kappa}^{e}))+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i}{({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{e})}^{2}H({(-1)}^{j}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{e}))
$$

One observes that the ${Y}_{j}$ defined by [eq. 2.4.3] are positive (it is a restitution of surface energy, whose SI unit is J/m²) if $\gamma \in [0,1]$ .

1. Irreversible plasticity forces and moments:

$$
{N}^{p}=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}^{S}}{\partial {\epsilon}^{p}}={H}_{m}:(\epsilon -{\epsilon}^{p})=N
$$

1. Kinematic-hardening back-stress tensors:

$$
{X}^{m}=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathit{epd}}^{S}}{\partial {\alpha}_{m}}=-{C}_{m}:{\alpha}_{m}
$$

### 2.5 Criteria – threshold surfaces

#### 2.5.1 Plasticity criterion

The **plasticity criterion**  of Johansen with kinematic hardening is duplicated for the plasticity of the upper part (index 1) and of the lower part (index 2) of the plate. This criterion couples the membrane plasticity with that in bending. If $x$ and $y$ denote the directions of the orthogonal reinforcement of the concrete plate, for $j=1,2$ , the criterion is written:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{f}_{j}^{p}(N-{X}^{m},M-{X}^{f})& =& -({M}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{f}-{M}_{\mathit{jx}}^{p}({N}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{m}))\\ & \times & ({M}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{f}-{M}_{\mathit{jy}}^{p}({N}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{m}))+{({M}_{xy}-{X}_{xy}^{f})}^{2}\le 0\end{array}
$$

The ${f}_{j}^{p}$ define a convex domain (cf. [bib4]) of reversibility, parametrised by 4 functions: ${M}_{\mathit{jx}}^{p}({N}_{xx})$ and ${M}_{\mathit{jy}}^{p}({N}_{yy})$ . These functions are constructed with the aid of the limit analysis of reinforced-concrete beam cross-sections representative of the cross-section of the plate under study, taken in the direction of the reinforcing bars, cf. [bib1, bib2]. One observes that only the differences between the loading state and the hardening back-stress tensors intervene in the expression of the criterion. This is characteristic of models with kinematic hardening.

The dissipation potential associated with this criterion is given by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\Psi}_{\mathit{tot}}=N:\dot{\epsilon}+M:\dot{\kappa}-{\dot{\Phi}}_{\mathit{epd}}={\Psi}_{p}+{\Psi}_{d}\\ {\Psi}_{p}=N:{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}+M:{\dot{\kappa}}^{p}-{\dot{\alpha}}_{m}:{C}_{m}:{\alpha}_{m}-{\dot{\alpha}}_{f}:{C}_{f}:{\alpha}_{f}\\ {\Psi}_{d}={Y}_{1}{\dot{d}}_{1}+{Y}_{2}{\dot{d}}_{2}\end{array}
$$

#### 2.5.2 Damage criterion

The brittle **damage criterion**  without hardening is defined by a scalar. This criterion is duplicated in order to distinguish positive bendings from negative bendings. It is written:

$$
{f}_{j}^{d}({Y}_{j})={Y}_{j}(\kappa ,{d}_{j})-{k}_{j}\le 0
$$

This criterion represents a convex domain (cf. [bib2]) of reversibility parametrised by the thresholds ${k}_{1}$ and ${k}_{2}$ which define the appearance of the first bending cracks in the reinforced-concrete plate. Their SI unit is $J/{m}^{2}$ . They correspond to a limitation of the surface density of elastic energy. This criterion is associated with the positive dissipation potential:

$$
{\Psi}_{d}({d}_{j},{\dot{d}}_{j})={k}_{j}{\dot{d}}_{j}\ge 0
$$

This damage criterion is basic, but it is its combination with the effect of the damage on the elastic stiffness, cf. the function ${\xi}_{f}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})$ [eq. 2.3.5], which acts on the response of the model.

### 2.6 Plastic flow laws

The plastic flow law is written (according to the normality rule with respect to the criterion [eq. 2.5.1]):

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}={\dot{\epsilon}}_{1}^{p}+{\dot{\epsilon}}_{2}^{p}={\lambda}_{1}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{1}^{p}}{\partial N}+{\lambda}_{2}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{2}^{p}}{\partial N}
$$

$$
{\dot{\kappa}}^{p}={\dot{\kappa}}_{1}^{p}+{\dot{\kappa}}_{2}^{p}={\lambda}_{1}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{1}^{p}}{\partial M}+{\lambda}_{2}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{2}^{p}}{\partial M}
$$

$$
{\dot{\alpha}}^{m}={\dot{\alpha}}_{1}^{m}+{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}^{m}={\lambda}_{1}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{1}^{p}}{\partial {X}^{m}}+{\lambda}_{2}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{2}^{p}}{\partial {X}^{m}}=-(-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{1}^{p}-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{2}^{p})={\dot{\epsilon}}^{p}
$$

$$
{\dot{\alpha}}^{f}={\dot{\alpha}}_{1}^{f}+{\dot{\alpha}}_{2}^{f}={\lambda}_{1}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{1}^{p}}{\partial {X}^{f}}+{\lambda}_{2}^{p}\frac{\partial {f}_{2}^{p}}{\partial {X}^{f}}=-(-{\dot{\kappa}}_{1}^{p}-{\dot{\kappa}}_{2}^{p})={\dot{\kappa}}^{p}
$$

where the ${\lambda}_{j}^{p}$ are the plastic multipliers, positive or zero, for the positive bendings and the negative bendings. They are shared by the membrane flow and by that in bending. One deduces from [eq. 2.6.3] and [eq. 2.6.4], in the manner usual for linear kinematic hardening, that the hardening internal variables in membrane and in bending are equal respectively to the plastic strains and to the plastic curvatures. From this there follow the following relations on the back-stress tensors in membrane and bending:

$$
{X}^{m}=-{C}_{m}:{\epsilon}^{p}
$$

$$
{X}^{f}=-{C}_{f}:{\kappa}^{p}
$$

It must be noted that this choice of a Prager tensor ${C}_{m}$ identical both in tension and in compression is open to criticism. Indeed, in plastic compression, the concrete and the steel intervene, whereas in tension only the steel contributes (the concrete having failed).

The plasticity criteria [eq. 2.4.1] may be reached at the same moment (for particular regimes of biaxial bending), so the flow may take place from the two criteria reached at once. The consistency condition gives two additional relations:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}{\lambda}_{j}^{p}{\dot{f}}_{j}^{p}(N,M)=0& \text{si}& {\lambda}_{j}^{p}>0& \text{alors}& {\dot{f}}_{j}^{p}(N,M)=0\end{array}
$$

### 2.7 Evolution law of the damage variables

The evolution law of the bending damage is written, for the positive bendings and the negative bendings (according to the normality rule with respect to the criterion [eq. 2.5.3]):

$$
{\dot{d}}_{j}={\lambda}_{j}^{d}\frac{\partial {f}_{j}^{d}}{\partial {Y}_{j}}
$$

The ${\lambda}_{j}^{d}$ are the damage multipliers, positive or zero. One recalls that the ${Y}_{j}$ , defined by [eq. 2.4.3], are positive by construction (energy restitution).

The consistency condition gives two additional relations:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}{\lambda}_{j}^{d}{\dot{f}}_{j}^{d}({Y}_{j})=0& \text{si}& {\lambda}_{j}^{d}>0& \text{alors}& {\dot{f}}_{j}^{d}({Y}_{j})=0\mathrm{\iff }{\dot{Y}}_{j}=0\end{array}
$$

The two damage variables may evolve simultaneously.

## 3 Parameters of the law

With global models such as GLRC_DAMAGE one seeks to have a simpler representation of the non-linear phenomena, by using numerical methods that are more efficient and more robust. Consequently, it is difficult to attribute a physical meaning to all the parameters of the model, because most of them encompass several phenomena. Thus, it is strongly recommended that the parameters of the model be validated by a comparative study between the GLRC_DAMAGE approach and a finer approach, such as modellings by multifibre beams, multilayer shells or 3D, on a part sufficiently representative of the structure to be analysed. Failing which, the error of an analysis using the model GLRC_DAMAGE cannot be estimated or controlled.

In any case, the parameters of the law are determined in a simplified manner with the aid of the analysis of the monotonic behaviour of a reinforced-concrete cross-section, except for the linear elastic behaviour, where it is also possible to resort to a homogenised plate approach, cf. for example [bib4]. One assumes that the set of parameters describing the elastic behaviour is identifiable independently of the plasticity and damage parameters. Moreover, the homogenisation methods allow us to determine the global elastic behaviour with very good accuracy. One considers in [§3.1] two approaches for homogenising the elastic behaviour: in one of them one makes the assumption of an equivalent isotropic medium and in the other the orthotropy is taken into account. At present, only the isotropic approach is available in *Code_Aster* . Moreover, it is only the isotropic approximation which can be used in combination with plasticity and damage. In principle, an extension to orthotropic behaviours in linear and in non-linear analysis could be envisaged within an equivalent theoretical framework. The limitation to the isotropic cases was chosen on the one hand because the phenomenon of orthotropy was judged negligible at failure, the simulation of which is the principal aim of the model, and on the other hand in order to lighten the formulation of the model and the identification of the parameters.

The parameters of the non-linear behaviours are more delicate to determine than the elasticity parameters. For the damage this is simpler, because their number is smaller. On the other hand, for the plasticity, the user must supply a function, determining the limit yield moment as a function of the membrane force. Moreover, the kinematic hardening is given by four Drucker tensors, each having three parameters. In this version their number is artificially reduced by assuming that these tensors are the same for the two plasticity thresholds. If the behaviours in elasticity and with damage can be identified without having a reference modelling (or a test), for the plastic behaviour this is strongly discouraged.

One denotes by $h$ the height of the cross-section (thickness of the plate). One denotes by ${\Omega}_{x}^{\sup}={A}_{x}^{\sup}/{d}_{x}^{\sup}$ and ${\Omega}_{y}^{\sup}={A}_{y}^{\sup}/{d}_{y}^{\sup}$ the reinforcement densities in the two directions, cf. [], []. ${A}_{x}^{\sup}$ ( *resp* . ${A}_{y}^{\sup}$ ) is the cross-sectional area of a steel bar in the direction $x$ ( *resp* . $y$ ) of the upper layer. One does likewise for the lower layer: ${\Omega}_{x}^{\inf}={A}_{x}^{\inf}/{d}_{x}^{\inf}$ , ${\Omega}_{y}^{\inf}={A}_{y}^{\inf}/{d}_{y}^{\inf}$ . In general, all the quantities having *sup*  as a superscript correspond to the upper part of the plate, whereas those with *inf*  correspond to its lower part.

### 3.1 Identification of the linear elastic behaviour parameters

The linear elastic behaviour is *a priori*  orthotropic and incorporates a membrane–bending coupling. In order to carry out an elastic analysis prior to a non-linear analysis, one wishes to represent as well as possible this type of behaviour of the reinforced-concrete structure.

One proposes to identify the linear elastic behaviour coefficients in two ways:

- by **the orthotropic approach**  in which one constructs the membrane–bending elastic matrix from the elastic characteristics of the concrete (${E}_{b}$ , ${\nu}_{b}$ ), of the steel (${E}_{a}$ ) and from the geometric characteristics of the reinforced-concrete cross-section, cf. ().
- by **the isotropic approach**  in which one determines the elastic parameters of the equivalent homogenised medium.

The global elastic law of the reinforced-concrete slab with membrane and bending coupling is written with the tensors ${H}_{m}$ , ${H}_{f}$ , ${H}_{\mathit{mf}}$ and is given in the local orthogonal frame linked to the reinforcement by:

$$
(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {\mathrm{2N}}_{xy}\\ {M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {\mathrm{2M}}_{xy}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}(\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{m}& {H}_{1122}^{m}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{m}& {H}_{2222}^{m}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{m}\end{array})& (\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{2222}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{\mathit{mf}}\end{array})\\ (\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{2222}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{\mathit{mf}}\end{array})& (\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{f}& {H}_{1122}^{f}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{f}& {H}_{2222}^{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{f}\end{array})\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{xy}\\ {\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\end{array})
$$

The local orthogonal frame linked to the reinforcement is defined with AFFE_CARA_ELEM (factor keyword COQUE, keyword ANGL_REP).

In this expression, ${H}_{ijkl}^{m}$ are the membrane stiffnesses, the ${H}_{ijkl}^{f}$ are the bending stiffnesses and the ${H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{mf}}$ are the membrane–bending coupling stiffnesses. The orthotropy imposes, in this frame, that the terms ${H}_{ij12}$ be zero. In the case of two symmetric reinforcement grids, one has membrane–bending decoupling: ${H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{mf}}=0$ .

One verifies that necessarily: ${H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{1111}^{f}-{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}^{2}>0$ , and likewise in the other direction. In the same way one must have: ${H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{2222}^{m}-{({H}_{1212}^{m})}^{2}>0$ , ${H}_{1111}^{f}{H}_{2222}^{f}-{({H}_{1212}^{f})}^{2}>0$ , still because of the positive-definite character of the elasticity tensor.

- **Orthotropic approach (** unavailable in Code_Aster at present)

One constructs the coefficients directly by the following approximate relations:

| $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1111}^{m}=\frac{{E}_{b}h}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}+{E}_{a}{\langle \Omega \rangle }_{x}\\ {H}_{2222}^{m}=\frac{{E}_{b}h}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}+{E}_{a}{\langle \Omega \rangle }_{y}\end{array}$ , $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1122}^{m}=\frac{{\nu}_{b}{E}_{b}h}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}\\ {H}_{1212}^{m}=\frac{{E}_{b}h}{1+{\nu}_{b}}\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1111}^{f}=\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}+\frac{{E}_{a}{h}^{2}}{4}{\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{x}\\ {H}_{2222}^{f}=\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}+\frac{{E}_{a}{h}^{2}}{4}{\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{y}\end{array}$ , $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1122}^{f}=\frac{{\nu}_{b}{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}\\ {H}_{1212}^{f}=\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1+{\nu}_{b})}\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}=\frac{{E}_{a}h}{2}{\langle \chi \Omega \rangle }_{x}\\ {H}_{2222}^{\mathit{mf}}=\frac{{E}_{a}h}{2}{\langle \chi \Omega \rangle }_{y}\end{array}$ , $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}=0\\ {H}_{1212}^{\mathit{mf}}=0\end{array}$ where one has set, in order to simplify, the expressions: ${\langle \Omega \rangle }_{x}={\Omega}_{x}^{\sup}+{\Omega}_{x}^{\inf}$ , ${\langle \Omega \rangle }_{y}={\Omega}_{y}^{\sup}+{\Omega}_{y}^{\inf}$ ${\langle \chi \Omega \rangle }_{y}={\chi }_{y}^{\sup}{\Omega}_{y}^{\sup}+{\chi }_{y}^{\inf}{\Omega}_{y}^{\inf}$ , ${\langle \chi \Omega \rangle }_{y}={\chi }_{y}^{\sup}{\Omega}_{y}^{\sup}+{\chi }_{y}^{\inf}{\Omega}_{y}^{\inf}$ ${\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{x}={\chi }_{x}^{{\sup}^{2}}{\Omega}_{x}^{\sup}+{\chi }_{x}^{{\inf}^{2}}{\Omega}_{x}^{\inf}$ , ${\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{x}={\chi }_{x}^{{\sup}^{2}}{\Omega}_{x}^{\sup}+{\chi }_{x}^{{\inf}^{2}}{\Omega}_{x}^{\inf}$ | (3.1.2) |
| --- | --- |

One thus assumes that the steels do not contribute any stiffness in membrane distortion of the plate, nor in torsion.

**Note:**

The orthotropic approach is not available in the current version. It is envisaged to introduce it in the forthcoming developments of the model.

- **Isotropic approach**

One constructs the global elastic matrix of eq. 3.1.1 by assuming:

$$
(\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{m}& {H}_{1122}^{m}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{m}& {H}_{2222}^{m}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{m}\end{array})=\frac{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}h}{1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{ccc}1& {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}& 0\\ {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}\end{array})
$$

for the membrane part and

$$
(\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{f}& {H}_{1122}^{f}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{f}& {H}_{2222}^{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{f}\end{array})=\frac{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})}(\begin{array}{ccc}1& {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}& 0\\ {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}\end{array})
$$

for the bending part. One also neglects the membrane–bending coupling:

$$
(\begin{array}{ccc}{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ {H}_{1122}^{\mathit{mf}}& {H}_{2222}^{\mathit{mf}}& 0\\ 0& 0& {H}_{1212}^{\mathit{mf}}\end{array})=0
$$

By comparison with [eq. 3.1.3] and [eq. 3.1.4], one opts for the following relations, favouring the mean in-plane behaviour and averaging over the directions *x*  and *y* , which give the four necessary elastic coefficients, starting from the notations [eq 3.1.2]:

$$
{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}={\nu}_{b}\frac{2{E}_{b}h}{2{E}_{b}h+{E}_{a}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})({\langle \Omega \rangle }_{x}+{\langle \Omega \rangle }_{y})}
$$

$$
{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}={\nu}_{b}\frac{2{E}_{b}h}{2{E}_{b}h+3{E}_{a}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})({\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{x}+{\langle {\chi }^{2}\Omega \rangle }_{y})}
$$

$$
{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}={E}_{b}\frac{{\nu}_{b}(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m})}^{2})}{{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}
$$

Of the two elastic approaches only the second ( *ii)*  is available at present.

The elastic coefficients of the concrete (${E}_{b}$ , ${\nu}_{b}$ ) as well as those of the steels ${E}_{a}$ are supplied under the keyword ELAS of DEFI_MATERIAU. The characteristics of the layout of the steels in the concrete plate ( ${\Omega}_{x}^{\inf}$ , ${\Omega}_{x}^{\sup}$ , ${\Omega}_{y}^{\inf}$ , ${\Omega}_{y}^{\sup}$ , ${\chi }_{x}^{\inf}$ , ${\chi }_{x}^{\sup}$ , ${\chi }_{y}^{\inf}$ , ${\chi }_{y}^{\sup}$ ) are supplied under the keyword NAPPE of DEFI_GLRC.

The height $h$ of the cross-section is also supplied by the operator DEFI_GLRC, keyword BETON, operand EPAIS. The directions of the local orthotropy frame are defined by the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM, keyword COQUE with the operand ANGL_REP. The linear elastic behaviour is usable in non-linear analysis under the keyword COMPORTEMENT with the operand RELATION = ‘GLRC_DAMAGE’.

### 3.2 Identification of the damageable elastoplastic behaviour parameters

The damage model, [§2.3], is formulated under the assumption of isotropy (see [§3.1, ii]), which is a reasonable approximation in most cases. Moreover, one admits that the bending–membrane coupling (terms ${H}_{ijkl}^{\mathit{mf}}$ ) in the elastic phase of the behaviour is negligible.

According to [§3.1, ii] one identifies the bending–membrane elasticity tensor (cf. the tensors ${H}_{m}$ and ${H}_{f}$ defined by [eq 2.3.3] and [eq. 2.3.4]):

$$
(\begin{array}{c}{N}_{xx}\\ {N}_{yy}\\ {\mathrm{2N}}_{xy}\\ {M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {\mathrm{2M}}_{xy}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{cc}\frac{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}h}{1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m})}^{2}}(\begin{array}{ccc}1& {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}& 0\\ {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{m}\end{array})& (0)\\ (0)& \frac{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})}(\begin{array}{ccc}1& {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}& 0\\ {\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}\end{array})\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{xx}\\ {\epsilon}_{yy}\\ 2{\epsilon}_{xy}\\ {\kappa}_{xx}\\ {\kappa}_{yy}\\ 2{\kappa}_{xy}\end{array})
$$

#### 3.2.1 Identification of the damage thresholds

One must identify the damage thresholds defined by [eq. 2.4.3] from the cracking limits in pure uniaxial tension and in pure uniaxial bending (in the positive direction ${M}_{1}^{d}$ and the negative direction ${M}_{2}^{d}$ ) of the reinforced-concrete slab, themselves defined from the tensile strength threshold of the concrete ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}\ge 0$ (cf. [bib2]). One uses for this purpose the analytical solution of the case of a reinforced-concrete beam reinforced in the same way as the slab. One retains the approximation consisting in considering reinforcement layers averaged along the directions $x$ and $y$ . One will therefore admit that one has ${H}_{1111}^{m}\mathrm{\equiv }{H}_{2222}^{m}$ , ${H}_{1111}^{f}\mathrm{\equiv }{H}_{2222}^{f}$ and ${H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}\mathrm{\equiv }{H}_{2222}^{\mathit{mf}}$ . Not taking this approximation into account leads to heavy and scarcely necessary computations.

In positive uniaxial pure bending ${M}_{xx}$ , with ${N}_{xx}=0$ , ${\epsilon}_{\alpha y}=0$ , ${\kappa}_{xy}=0$ and , ${\kappa}_{yy}=-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{\kappa}_{xx}$ , $\alpha =\lbrace  x,y\rbrace  ` , l’endommagement du béton est atteint d’abord en peau inférieure de la dalle en béton armé : :math:`{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}=\frac{{E}_{b}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}({\epsilon}_{xx}+{\kappa}_{xx}h/2)$ . One therefore has the following relations, cf. [eq. 3.1.2], [eq. 3.1.3] and [eq. 3.1.4]:

${\epsilon}_{xx}=-\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}}{\kappa}_{xx}$

then

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{xx}& =& ({H}_{1111}^{f}-{H}_{1122}^{f}{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}-\frac{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}{{H}_{1111}^{m}}){\kappa}_{xx}\\ & =& ({H}_{1111}^{f}(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})-\frac{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}{{H}_{1111}^{m}}){\kappa}_{xx}\end{array}
$$

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}=\frac{{E}_{b}{\kappa}_{xx}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}(\frac{h}{2}-\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})=\frac{{E}_{b}{M}_{xx}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}\frac{{H}_{1111}^{m}}{(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2}){H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{1111}^{f}-{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}^{2}}(\frac{h}{2}-\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})
$$

whence:

$$
{M}_{1}^{d}=(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}}{{E}_{b}}\frac{(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2}){H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{1111}^{f}-{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}^{2}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}}{(\frac{h}{2}-\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})}^{-1}
$$

In the case where one neglects the bending–membrane coupling, ${H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}=0$ , one simply has:

$$
{M}_{1}^{d}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}{h}^{2}}{6}\frac{{\nu}_{b}}{{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})
$$

Likewise, in negative uniaxial pure bending ${M}_{xx}$ , with ${N}_{xx}=0$ , ${\epsilon}_{\alpha y}=0$ , ${\kappa}_{xy}=0$ and , ${\kappa}_{yy}=-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{\kappa}_{xx}$ , $\alpha =\lbrace  x,y\rbrace  ` , l’endommagement du béton est atteint d’abord en peau supérieure de la dalle en béton armé : :math:`{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}=\frac{{E}_{b}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}({\epsilon}_{xx}-{\kappa}_{xx}h/2)$ . One therefore has the following relations:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}=-\frac{{E}_{b}{\kappa}_{xx}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}(\frac{h}{2}-\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})=-\frac{{E}_{b}{M}_{xx}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}\frac{{H}_{1111}^{m}}{(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2}){H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{1111}^{f}-{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}^{2}}(\frac{h}{2}+\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})
$$

whence:

$$
{M}_{2}^{d}=-(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}}{{E}_{b}}\frac{(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2}){H}_{1111}^{m}{H}_{1111}^{f}-{({H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}})}^{2}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}}{(\frac{h}{2}+\frac{{H}_{1111}^{\mathit{mf}}}{{H}_{1111}^{m}})}^{-1}
$$

In the case where one neglects the bending–membrane coupling, one simply has:

$$
{M}_{1}^{d}=-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ft}}{h}^{2}}{6}\frac{{\nu}_{b}}{{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})
$$

**Note:**

One verifies that: ${M}_{1}^{d}\ge 0$ and ${M}_{2}^{d}\le 0$ .

It only remains to relate these cracking moments to the thresholds ${k}_{1}$ , ${k}_{2}$ defined in [eq. 2.5.3]. Since the loading is applied starting from a virgin state, ${d}_{1}={d}_{2}=0$ . Whence the damage forces (energy restitution), cf. [eq. 2.4.3]:

${Y}_{j}=\frac{1-\gamma }{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathit{tr}{({\kappa}^{e})}^{2}H({(-1)}^{j}\mathit{tr}({\kappa}^{e}))+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i}{({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{e})}^{2}H({(-1)}^{j}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{e}))$

where one applies ${d}_{j}=0$ , ${\kappa}^{p}=0$ , ${\kappa}^{e}=\kappa$ , ${\kappa}_{xy}=0$ and ${\kappa}_{yy}=-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{\kappa}_{xx}$ in order to obtain:

${Y}_{j}=\frac{1}{2}(1-\gamma )({\lambda}_{f}{(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2}+{\mu}_{f}){\kappa}_{xx}^{2}$

By using [eq. 3.1.1] of the document [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32):

${\lambda}_{f}=\frac{{h}^{3}{\nu}_{f}{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{12(1-{\nu}_{f}^{2})}$ , ${\mu}_{f}=\frac{{h}^{3}{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{24(1+{\nu}_{f})}$

one obtains:

$\begin{array}{ccc}{Y}_{j}& =& \frac{{h}^{3}}{24}(1-\gamma ){E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}\frac{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{\kappa}_{xx}^{2}\\ & =& \frac{{h}^{3}}{24}(1-\gamma ){E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}\frac{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{(\frac{{M}_{xx}}{{H}_{1111}^{f}(1-{({\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}^{2})})}^{2}\\ & =& \frac{{h}^{3}}{24}(1-\gamma ){E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}\frac{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{(\frac{12}{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{M}_{xx})}^{2}\\ & =& \frac{6}{{h}^{3}}\frac{(1-\gamma )}{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}\frac{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{M}_{xx}^{2}\end{array}$

then:

$$
{k}_{1}=\frac{6}{{h}^{3}}\frac{(1-\gamma )}{{E}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}\frac{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}(1-{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f})}{1+{\nu}_{\mathit{éq}}^{f}}{({M}_{1}^{d})}^{2}
$$

These thresholds have as their SI unit the joule.

The damageable linear elastic behaviour is usable in non-linear analysis under the keyword COMPORTEMENT with the operand RELATION = ‘GLRC_DAMAGE’. The parameters of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU MF1 and MF2 correspond to ${M}_{1}^{d}$ and ${M}_{2}^{d}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Identification of the bending-damage slope

Following the relations developed in [§3.2] of [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32), the damaging slope is proportional to the parameter $\gamma$ :

$$
{p}_{f}=\gamma {p}_{\mathit{élas}}
$$

The parameter $\gamma$ corresponds to the parameter GAMMA supplied in the operator DEFI_GLRC.

#### 3.2.3 Identification of the maximum level of bending damage

In [eq. 2.3.1], provision has been made to limit the level of bending damage of the reinforced-concrete plate, with the aid of the values of ${d}_{j}^{max}$ . One associates these values with the moment–curvature slopes, for the two loading directions $j=1,2$ , and with respect to the elastic slope, see also []:

$\frac{{p}_{2,j}}{{p}_{\mathit{élas}}}=\frac{1+\gamma {d}_{j}^{max}}{1+{d}_{j}^{max}}$

whence, for $j=1,2$ :

$$
{d}_{j}^{max}=\frac{{p}_{\mathit{élas}}-{p}_{2,j}}{{p}_{2,j}-{p}_{f}}
$$

In the operator DEFI_GLRC one supplies QP1 and QP2 for ${p}_{2,1}$ and ${p}_{2,2}$ .

#### 3.2.4 Identification of the plastic behaviour parameters

For the behaviours in elasticity and with damage, it is possible to obtain the values of the parameters analytically from the material and geometric properties of the reinforced concrete. In order to characterise the parameters of the plastic behaviour, it is imperative to refer to a finer modelling (multifibre beam, multilayer shell or 3D). Software of the MOCO type (see [bib10]) is recommended for the automatic identification of the GLRC models. In the long term, it is planned that such a tool be integrated into *Code_Aster* . In the current version, the identification of the elastoplastic part is left entirely to the care of the users.

For the identification, it is more reasonable to identify the parameters of the non-linear behaviour from tests, numerical or experimental, with a monotonic loading. For example, one may use a test with homogeneous curvatures and bending moments. Such a test is pseudo-one-dimensional and can be represented entirely with a single graph, on which one can identify the damage and plasticity thresholds as well as the slopes corresponding to the various loading phases, see . In order to measure the effect of the membrane force the monotonic bending test must be combined with a membrane loading.

On the , one distinguishes five phases:

1. elastic phase characterised by the slope ${p}_{\mathit{élas}}$
2. phase corresponding to the damage of the concrete (slope ${p}_{f}$ ),
3. recovery of the stiffness due to the steels after the maximum damage has been reached (slope ${p}_{2}$ )
4. yielding of the steels (slope ${p}_{p}$ ).
5. elastic unloading: the value of the unloading slopes lies in the interval $[{p}_{\mathit{élas}},{p}_{f}]$ for phases i) to iii) and is equal to ${p}_{2}$ for phase iv).

## 4 Numerical integration of the constitutive law

The law GLRC_DAMAGE was initially designed for fast-dynamics analyses resorting to explicit time-integration schemes. The version of the model is almost identical to the initial version and is therefore not optimised for static analyses or for implicit dynamics. Consequently, the model is liable to be insufficiently robust and insufficiently efficient for an analysis with large time steps. It is planned that the numerical integration of the model be improved.

### 4.1 Evaluation of the damage

The damage model used in GLRC_DAMAGE has been extended to the membrane–bending coupling and placed in the model GLRC_DM (see [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32)). One therefore refers the reader to [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32) for the details concerning the numerical integration of the damage part. The computation in GLRC_DAMAGE is simpler, because one neglects the influence of the membrane energy on the evolution of the damage.

### 4.2 Evaluation of the plastic flow

The integration of the elastoplastic part is the most delicate part of the model. For the moment, one does not have a method with a satisfactory robustness for large increments of (pseudo)-time. One summarises below the particular features of the model presented in [§2], which one can hardly solve by the classical approaches, based on Newton's method.

- **Double cones:**  The elastic domain of each of the threshold functions defined in [§2.5.1] is not convex and can be represented by a double cone in the space of the generalised stresses (see ). Obviously, it is only the cone close to the origin which represents the true elastic domain. Thus, the resolution of the plastic admissibility must be carried out by adding two inequalities:

$$
{g}_{1}^{p}(N-{X}^{m},M-{X}^{f})=({M}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{f})-{M}_{\mathrm{1x}}^{p}({N}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{m})+({M}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{f})-{M}_{\mathrm{1y}}^{p}({N}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{m})\le 0
$$

$$
{g}_{2}^{p}(N-{X}^{m},M-{X}^{f})=({M}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{f})-{M}_{\mathrm{2x}}^{p}({N}_{xx}-{X}_{xx}^{m})+({M}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{f})-{M}_{\mathrm{2y}}^{p}({N}_{yy}-{X}_{yy}^{m})\le 0
$$

With the inequalities  and , one eliminates the non-physical solutions of the plastic-admissibility equations, ${f}_{j}^{p}(N-{X}^{m},M-{X}^{f})\le 0$ , ${\lambda}_{j}^{p}\ge 0$ . The functions ${g}_{j}^{p}$ define the two planes, ${g}_{j}^{p}(N-{X}^{m},M-{X}^{f})=0$ , separating the non-physical cones from the cones determining the elastic domain. On the other hand, the introduction of the inequalities into the system prevents us from using algorithms of Newton type without important modifications.

- **Apexes of the cones**  : the other drawback, also linked to the shape of the elastic domain in the space of the generalised stresses, comes from the two apexes of the cones of the elastic domain (see ). This property can also make the convergence of the iterative algorithm difficult.

Because of the two drawbacks mentioned above one cannot apply the radial-return algorithm, the one most often used for the solution of plasticity problems. In its place one has implemented a « cutting plane » algorithm, combined with bisection. The details of the algorithm are available in [bib1].

### 4.3 Evaluation of the tangent operator

At present, the tangent operator of the model GLRC_DAMAGE is not consistent and does not guarantee the quadratic convergence of the global Newton process. Its implementation is based on an older version of the model.
