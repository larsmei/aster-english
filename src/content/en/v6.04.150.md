# v6.04.150 SSNV150 - Triaxial Traction with constitutive law BETON_DOUBLE_DP

**Summary**

This validation case is intended to verify the 3D BETON_DOUBLE_DP behavior model formulated in the thermoplasticity framework, for the description of the nonlinear behaviour of concrete, in traction, and in compression, with consideration for irreversible variations in the thermal and mechanical characteristics of concrete, particularly sensitive at high temperature.

The description of cracking is treated in the plasticity framework, using energy equivalence, by identifying the density of cracking energy in $I$ mode, with the plastic work of an equivalent homogeneous medium, where plastic deformation is evenly distributed, in an "elementary" zone. This approach preserves the continuity of model formulation, over the whole of its behavior, and helps to avoid the possible numerical difficulties when changing the material's condition.

The pathological sensitivity of the numerical solution to spatial discretization (mesh), generated by the introduction of a softening behaviour of concrete in traction and compression, is partially resolved by introducing a cracking or breaking energy, depending on a characteristic length ${l}_{c}$, related to the size of the elements. The resolution of the model's constituent equations is performed by an implicit schema.

This is a cube with 8 nodes subjected to a triaxial traction, while on the required displacement. This load leads to the particular case of a hydrostatic stress state, solved by projection at the top of the tensile cone, when placed in an equivalent stress/hydrostatic stress diagram. It is a test case with analytical solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is calculated analytically, knowing that in traction only the tensile criterion is activated, and that in the case of hydrostatic loading, the top of the tensile cone is projected. Therefore, a linear system must be solved from an equation to an unknown one, which allows the cumulative plastic deformation to be obtained in tensile. This one then allows the calculation of deformations and stresses.

### 2.2 Calculation of the reference reference solution

For more details on notations and equationization, see [r7.01.03](doc:r7.01.03). Only the main equations are recalled here.

The displacement is "$a$", the displacement imposed according to the directions $x$ , $y$ and $z$ . The deformation tensor is of the form $(a,a,a,0.,0.,0.)$ by taking the usual notations of *code_aster* (three main components, three shear components).

The stress tensor is $(\sigma ,\sigma ,\sigma ,0.,0.,0.)$ in model A.

**General model equations:**

The model's constituent equations are written by distinguishing the isotropic part of the deviatoric part of the stress and deformation tensors.

$$
\sigma_H = \frac{1}{3} \, \mathrm{tr}(\sigma)
\qquad
s = \sigma-\frac{1}{3} \, \mathrm{tr}(\sigma)\, I
\qquad   \varepsilon_H = \frac{1}{3} \, \mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon)
\qquad   \tilde{\varepsilon} = \varepsilon-\frac{1}{3} \, \mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon)\, I
$$

$$
\sigma = s + \sigma_H I
\qquad
\text{et}
\qquad
\varepsilon = \tilde{\varepsilon} + \varepsilon_H I
$$

The equivalent constraint is then written:

In the case of an incremental formulation, and a variable constitutive law, noting with an exponent *"e"* the elastic components of the stress and deformation, we obtain:

$$
s^{e} = \frac{\mu^{+}}{\mu^{-}}\, s^{-} + 2 \mu^{+} \, \Delta\tilde{\varepsilon}
\qquad
\text{et}
\qquad
\sigma_H^{e} = \frac{K^{+}}{K^{-}}\, \sigma_H^{-} + 3 K^{+} \, \Delta \varepsilon_H
$$

The $(f_{comp})$ compression and $(f_{trac})$ traction criteria are expressed as follows:

$f_{comp} = \frac{\tau_{oct} + a.\sigma_{oct}}{b} - f_c(\lambda_c) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3b}\sigma^{eq} + \frac{a}{b}\sigma_H - f_c(\lambda_c)$

$f_{trac} = \frac{\tau_{oct} + c.\sigma_{oct}}{d} - f_t(\lambda_t) = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{3d}\sigma^{eq} + \frac{c}{d}\sigma_H - f_t(\lambda_t)$

$\lambda_c$: plastic compression multiplier

$\lambda_T$: plastic pull multiplier

and $a, b, c, d$ the coefficients of the model

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case has very satisfactory results compared to analytical solution, less than 7.10-5% with a low number of iterations (1 or 2 iterations). The solution is obtained from a linear equation in the case of a linear tensile nutring curve, but the resolution uses a Newton algorithm in a more general setting.

The stress criterion which occurs during loading can be hardened, resulting in a reduction in stress (component $xx$ , $yy$ and $zz$ ), which is also equal to the hydrostatic stress.
