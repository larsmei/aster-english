# v6.02.135 SSNL135 – Determination of MEKELEC console ruin loads

**Summary**

In this test, we are looking to validate in the nonlinear geometrical field and multifibre Timoshenko beam material l-element, available in *Code_Astervia* POU_D_TGM Modelling. To illustrate the possibilities offered by this element, we propose to numerically determine the ruin loads of the MEKELEC pylon console and compare them with test results.

The MEKELEC console is subjected to different load cases and for each case the predicted ruin load and the predicted ruin mode are compared to those observed in the tests. The results show a very good prediction of the ruin modes with, however, a disparity in the loads that is explained more by the simplified Modelling adopted for the console than by the gaps in the finite element used.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Structure geometry

The MEKELEC console is studied. It is a P4T pylons console, whose dimensions have been reduced to facilitate the instrumentation of the tests while maintaining a structure whose ruin modes can be varied and cover those encountered in a real lattice tower.

The structure consists of 10 horns assembled on their wings with bolts, or via pods. Reference can be made to [3] for detailed plans of the console.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method used for the calculation of the reference solution

The reference solution was obtained by instrumentation in tests on a MEKELEC console reproduction[1]. The ruin load is that for which a very large displacement of the point force application occurs without it being possible to achieve a steady state (non-almost static evolution of the structure).

In any case, the structure was completely discharged at 40% and 80% of the design load to prevent the games in the assemblies from disrupting the ruin load.

### 2.2 Baseline

The ruin loads determined during the tests are presented for each load case:

#### 3.4.3 Remarks

The numerical response of the structure is obtained by moving the loaded node, which allows to observe the softening due to the ruin and thus to be able to test the maximum value of the pilot state (parameter ETA_PILOTAGE).

The ruin load for this case of load is correctly estimated, however it is noted that the experimental response and the numerical response differ widely, in particular because of an elastic slope largely overestimated in the *Aster* calculation. The rigidity of the structure is indeed not well transcribed in the model: the pods that allow to ensure the connections at the head and at the foot of the console are supposed to have a rigid behavior whereas they are plates.

However, it can be observed that the overall appearance of experimental and numerical responses is the same with a good description of the slope change (plasification at the console head) and the limit load (horizontal tangent at the force-moving curve).

#### 4.4.3 Remarks

The numerical response of the structure is obtained by steering the entire structure in d-arc length, which allows us to observe the softening due to the ruin and thus to be able to test the maximum value of the pilotage state (parameter ETA_PILOTAGE).

Indeed, the tests showed the importance of geometric nonlinearities (elasto-plastic blazing of the upper panel corners), there is no redistribution of effort following a plasticization, the ruin is brutal. L-dweaking is concomitant with the end of the elastic slope.

## 6 Summary of the results

POU_D_TGM multifibre beam L-element for use in geometric nonlinear and material allows in this test case to simulate the behavior of a pylonic console up to and beyond the first softening if the moving structure is piloted (in the case of a plasticization ruin) or by arc length (in the case of a blazing ruin).

The calculation is fast and does not require any adjustment to carry it out (if this is the setting up of the pilotage). The results, if they differ from the experience, do not call into question the performance of the element. Indeed, the observed disparities are to be considered in the choice of model (of assemblies in particular).
