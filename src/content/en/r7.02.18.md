# r7.02.18 Hydromechanical element coupled with XFEM

**Summary:**

In this document we present the formulation of a new type of finite element. It is a hydromechanical element crossed by a discontinuity (an interface or a crack) whose design rests on the use of the extended finite-element method in the formulation of the discretised equations of the poro-mechanical model for a porous medium saturated with fluid.

## 1 Introduction

The formulation of the coupled HM-XFEM model is based on the equations of the joint model [R7.02.15](doc:r7.02.15) as well as on those of the THHM constitutive model [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11) in the saturated case. Classically, the joint model is used to model in 2D the behaviour of a hydraulic joint or of a discontinuity in the presence of a fluid flow within it, thus generating a fluid pressure. The joint model makes it possible to take into account:

- the preferential flow of the fluid in the discontinuity, conditioned by the opening of the latter,
- the exchange of fluid between the porous medium and the discontinuity crossing it,
- the propagation of the discontinuity,
- the deformation of the porous medium induced by the fluid pressure.

Within the framework of the classical finite-element method, the use of such a model presents a major drawback. Indeed, it is necessary to represent the discontinuity explicitly in the mesh and to make the lips of the latter coincide with the edges of the elements constituting the mesh. This implies that, in the course of its evolution, one must have recourse to elaborate projection algorithms for the updating of its geometry. This step can prove very costly in computation time for complex geometries.

In order to free oneself from this constraint linked to the mesh, we envisage the introduction of a new hydromechanical (HM) element coupled with the extended finite-element method (XFEM) [1,2,3]. This method, based on the partition-of-unity principle [4], ensures greater flexibility for modellings involving more or less complex geometries. Indeed, with this method, the discontinuity is no longer represented physically in the mesh but symbolically, by enriching the approximated fields with supplementary degrees of freedom. For more details on the extended finite-element method in *Code_Aster* , the user may refer to the documentation [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) (mechanical case only).

In the literature certain authors have already envisaged the coupling of the XFEM method with the coupled poro-mechanical HM model. This is the case of [5] in the case of the dynamic analysis of porous media in unsaturated conditions. The extension to the THM case for a saturated medium crossed by an impermeable interface is envisaged in [6]. Other authors such as [7] have proposed a model able to take into account the singularity of the pressure field at the tip of the discontinuity, by adapting the expression of the singular functions for the discontinuity tip. However, the models developed by these authors take into account neither the exchange phenomena which may exist between the surrounding medium (called the rock mass in the remainder of this document) and the discontinuity, nor the propagation of the latter within the porous medium. This point will be taken into account in the model developed in the present documentation by the introduction of the regularised cohesive laws into the weak formulation of the mechanical equilibrium equation (see §  3.1.2 ).

From a practical point of view, the principal difficulty in the formulation of the HM-XFEM element is the construction of the various approximation spaces of the mechanical and hydraulic quantities. Respect of the LBB stability condition is essential in order to obtain a unique and convergent solution [8,9]. The violation of this condition (that is, not choosing the right degree of interpolation of the various approximated fields, see §  5.2.1 ) leads to oscillations of the solution. The coexistence of the HM-XFEM elements and of the classical HM elements (those to which the documentations [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11) and [R7.01.10](doc:r7.01.10) refer) is also a delicate point, in particular as regards the distribution of the degrees of freedom at the various nodes (vertices or mid-sides) of each type of element (see §  5.2.1 ).

In the remainder of this documentation we shall recall the framework of study of the problem, then, in a second stage, the fundamental equations of the poro-mechanical model involved in the formulation of the HM-XFEM model. Finally we shall proceed to the discretisation of the variational forms of the equilibrium equations, both in time (by means of a $\theta$ -scheme) and in space (by means of the XFEM method).

The presentation of the HM-XFEM model and its validation have been the subject of a scientific publication [15].

## 2 Presentation of the problem

### 2.1 Definition of the domain of study

Let $\Omega \subset {\mathrm{ℝ}}^{d}$ with $d\in \lbrace 2,3\rbrace$ be a domain crossed entirely by a crack or a permeable interface. Let $n$ be the outward normal to the boundary $\partial \Omega$ of the domain, and ${n}_{c}$ that of the interface ${\Gamma}_{c}$ .

It is possible to decompose:

- the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ into $\partial \Omega ={\Gamma}_{u}\cup {\Gamma}_{t}\cup {\Gamma}_{p}\cup {\Gamma}_{F}$ where the boundary conditions (of Dirichlet and Neumann type) are imposed for the hydraulics (on ${\Gamma}_{p}$ and ${\Gamma}_{F}$ ) and for the mechanics (on ${\Gamma}_{u}$ and ${\Gamma}_{t}$ ),
- the interface into ${\Gamma}_{c}={\Gamma}_{f}\cup {\Gamma}_{1}\cup {\Gamma}_{2}$ where ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$ represent the lips of the discontinuity. One imposes flux conditions on ${\Gamma}_{1}$ , ${\Gamma}_{2}$ and ${\Gamma}_{f}$ and cohesive surface forces on ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$ .

On the other hand there exists a longitudinal flow of fluid at the level of the interface. ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ are the fluxes due to the exchanges between the rock mass and the interface, and are expressed in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-2}}.{s}^{\text{-1}}$ . These fluxes come from the interface and are directed respectively towards the upper and lower parts of the rock mass at the level of the lips ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${\Gamma}_{2}$ of the interface. They are directed from the discontinuity towards the rock mass.

The pressure field at the level of the rock mass is denoted $p$ and that at the level of the interface is denoted ${p}_{f}$ (field induced by the fluid circulating at the level of the interface). The displacement field is denoted $u$ and the displacement jump at the level of the interface is denoted $⟦u⟧$ .

Let ${P}_{1}$ be a point of ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ a point of ${\Gamma}_{2}$ and ${n}_{c}={n}_{c}^{1}$ the outward normal to ${\Gamma}_{1}$ and ${n}_{c}^{2}$ the outward normal to ${\Gamma}_{2}$ . The normal displacement jump (taken negative or zero in opening and positive in interpenetration of the lips) is therefore defined in the following way:

$⟦u⟧.{n}_{c}=(u({P}_{1})-u({P}_{2})).{n}_{c}⩽0$

On the , one indicates the conventions adopted for taking the displacement jump into account at the level of the interface.

Figure 2.2-2: Definition of the displacement jump at the level of the discontinuity

For the rock mass, as for the interface, the effective-stress hypothesis is taken into consideration. Thus:

- the total stress in the rock mass is denoted $\sigma$ ,
- the total stress (linked to the cohesive forces) at the level of the interface is denoted ${t}_{c}$ .

The small-perturbation hypothesis is adopted. On the other hand one considers that the mechanical and hydraulic quantities are isotropic.

## 3 Constitutive equations of the HM-XFEM model

### 3.1 Equations for the mechanics

#### 3.1.1 Equilibrium equation (case of the rock mass)

In the case of the rock mass the equation of conservation of momentum (taking the volume forces into account) can be put in the form:

$\text{Div}(\sigma )+r{F}^{m}=0$

with:

- $\sigma ={\sigma}^{'}-\mathit{bp}1$ (under the effective-stress hypothesis) where $b$ is the Biot coefficient,
- $r$ the homogenised density such that in the saturated case $r={r}_{0}+{m}_{w}$ where ${r}_{0}$ is the homogenised density in the reference configuration and ${m}_{w}$ the mass supplies of water,
- $r{F}^{m}$ represent the volume forces acting on $\Omega$ (in practice these are the gravity forces).

#### 3.1.2 Cohesive-zone model (case of the interface)

In order to take into account the propagation of the discontinuity, the irreversibility of the fracturing and the non-interpenetration of the lips of the discontinuity, one chooses to model the behaviour of the interface or of the crack by means of a cohesive law.

For more information concerning the establishment of these laws, the user may refer to the documentation [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11). As far as we are concerned, at the level of the discontinuity, it is possible to distinguish 3 zones:

- a completely open zone, at the level of which the total stress on the lips of the discontinuity is equal to ${p}_{f}{n}_{c}$ on ${\Gamma}_{2}$ and equal to $-{p}_{f}{n}_{c}$ on ${\Gamma}_{1}$ . In this zone the value of the stress is due principally to the fluid circulating there,
- a cohesive zone (or Fracture Processing Zone (FPZ)) whose opening depends on the value of the total stress, which is then equal to ${t}_{c}={t}_{c}^{'}-{p}_{f}n$ , $n$ being the outward normal to the surface concerned ${\Gamma}_{1}$ or ${\Gamma}_{2}$ . Beyond a certain opening ${w}_{c}$ , the stress corresponds to that of the preceding zone,
- and a sound adherent zone at the level of which the lips of the discontinuity are in contact and do not interpenetrate.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.18/10000000000002290000013040597239E9D4C164.jpg)

Figure 3.1.2-1: Distribution of the stresses at the level of the discontinuity
On the one locates the various stress zones associated with the cohesive-zone model. The crack front is then naturally localised at the boundary between the cohesive zone and the adherent zone.

The constitutive law used for the cohesive laws takes the form of a softening relation between the cohesive stress ${t}_{c}'$ and the displacement jump $⟦u⟧$ at the level of the lips of the discontinuity.

Thus one sets:

${t}_{c}'\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\frac{\partial \psi }{\partial ⟦u⟧}$

with $\psi$ the surface energy, whose expression depends on the cohesive law used.

The cohesive law adopted for the hydromechanical XFEM elements is the law CZM_LIN_MIX detailed in [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19). It is a non-regularised cohesive law (therefore with an infinite initial slope). ${\sigma}_{c}$ is the critical stress from which the damage begins in the cohesive zone. ${G}_{c}=\frac{{\sigma}_{c}{w}_{c}}{2}$ corresponds to the quantity of energy to be supplied in order to break completely a unit of surface.

Figure 3.1.2-2: Mixed cohesive law.

The writing of the boundary conditions for the mechanics on the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ and at the level of the interface is written:

| Boundary conditions for the mechanics |
| --- |
| $u=0$ on ${\Gamma}_{u}$ (imposed displacements)$\sigma \cdot n=t$ on ${\Gamma}_{t}$ (imposed surface forces)$\sigma \cdot {n}_{c}^{1}={t}_{c}^{1}$ on ${\Gamma}_{1}$ (imposed cohesive surface forces)$\sigma \cdot {n}_{c}^{2}={t}_{c}^{2}$ on ${\Gamma}_{2}$ (imposed cohesive surface forces) |

### 3.2 Equations for the hydraulics

#### 3.2.1 Mass conservation equation (case of the rock mass)

In the case of the rock mass the mass conservation equation is put in the form:

$\frac{\partial {m}_{w}}{\partial t}\text{}+\text{}\text{Div}(M)=0$

with:

- ${m}_{w}$ the mass supplies (per unit volume) expressed in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-3}}$ ,
- $M$ the mass fluxes expressed in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-2}}.{s}^{\text{-1}}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Mass conservation equation (case of the interface)

In the case of the interface the mass conservation equation is put in the form:

$\frac{\partial w}{\partial t}\text{}+\text{}\text{Div}(W)=0$

with:

- $w$ the mass supplies (per unit surface) in the crack expressed in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-2}}$ ,
- $W$ the mass fluxes in the crack expressed in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-1}}.{s}^{\text{-1}}$ ,

#### 3.2.3 Boundary conditions for the hydraulics

The writing of the boundary conditions for the hydraulics on the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ and at the level of the interface makes it possible to obtain:

| Boundary conditions for the hydraulics |
| --- |
| $p=0$ on ${\Gamma}_{p}$ (imposed pressure)$M\cdot n={M}_{\text{ext}}$ on ${\Gamma}_{F}$ (for the rock mass)$W\cdot {n}_{c}={W}_{\text{ext}}$ on ${\Gamma}_{f}$ (for the discontinuity)$M\cdot {n}_{c}^{1}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{1}$ on ${\Gamma}_{1}$ (equality of the rock-mass/discontinuity fluxes)$M\cdot {n}_{c}^{2}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{2}$ on ${\Gamma}_{2}$ (equality of the rock-mass/discontinuity fluxes) |

An additional boundary condition to be taken into account is the continuity of the pressure ${p}_{f}$ at the level of each of the lips of the interface. This condition is necessary because of the very small thickness of the interface. It is expressed by the following linear relation:

- ${p}^{\inf}={p}_{f}$ on ${\Gamma}_{1}$
- ${p}^{\sup}={p}_{f}$ on ${\Gamma}_{2}$

with:

- ${p}^{\sup}$ the fluid pressure above the interface (field enriched by XFEM),
- ${p}^{\inf}$ the fluid pressure below the interface (field enriched by XFEM).

### 3.3 Equations of the poro-mechanical model

In this part one merely recalls the useful equations of the poro-mechanical model developed in the documentation [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11).

#### 3.3.1 Expression of the mass supplies

- Case of the rock mass

The mass supplies can be put in the form (with ${S}_{\mathit{lq}}=1$ ):

${m}_{w}=\rho \varphi (1+{\epsilon}_{v})$

with:

- $\rho ,\varphi$ respectively the density of the water (in ${\mathit{kg.m}}^{\text{-3}}$ ), the Eulerian porosity variable,
- ${\epsilon}_{v}=\mathit{Tr}(\epsilon )=\mathit{Tr}({\nabla}_{s}u)$ the volumetric strain (where $\mathit{Tr}$ is the linear trace map).
- Case of the interface

The mass supplies can be put in the form:

$w=\rho ⟦u⟧.{n}_{c}$

with:

- $\rho$ respectively the density of the water,
- $⟦u⟧.{n}_{c}$ the normal opening of the discontinuity (in $m$ ).

#### 3.3.2 Expression of the mass fluxes

- Case of the rock mass

The mass flux in the rock mass follows Darcy’s law. Thus one sets:

$M=\lambda \rho (-\nabla p+\rho {F}^{m})$

with $\lambda =\frac{{K}^{int}}{{\mu}^{}}$ the mobility of the liquid where ${K}^{int}$ is the intrinsic permeability of the rock mass (in ${m}^{2}$ ) and $\mu$ the dynamic viscosity of the fluid (in $\mathit{Pa.s}$ ).

**Note:**

*The expression of the mobility in reality involves the relative permeability of the fluid* ${k}_{w}^{\text{rel}}({S}_{\text{w}})$ *(which is a function of the water saturation and is given by the Mualem/Van Genuchten law* [1 *0]), that is* $\lambda =\frac{{K}^{int}{k}_{w}^{\text{rel}}}{{\mu}^{}}$ *.* *Given that the medium is saturated (* ${S}_{\text{w}}=1$ *), the relative permeability to water is therefore equal to* $1$ *.*

- Case of the interface

The mass flux in the discontinuity can be written as a function of the cubic law [11] (one neglects the effects of gravity). Thus one sets:

$W=-\frac{\rho {(⟦u⟧.{n}_{c})}^{3}}{12\mu }\nabla {p}_{f}$

with $\mu$ the dynamic viscosity of the fluid (in $\mathit{Pa.s}$ ).

#### 3.3.3 Evolution of the porosity variable

The evolution of the (Eulerian) porosity variable characterising the rock mass is given in the saturated isothermal case by:

$d\varphi =(b-\varphi )\left(d{\epsilon}_{v}\text{}+\text{}\frac{\mathit{dp}}{{K}_{s}}\right)$

with ${K}_{s}$ the bulk modulus of the solid matrix (in $\mathit{Pa}$ ) and $b$ the Biot coefficient.

#### 3.3.4 Evolution of the density of the fluid

- Case of the rock mass

The evolution of the density of the fluid in the rock mass is given in the saturated isothermal case by:

$\frac{d\rho }{{\rho}^{}}\text{}=\text{}\frac{\text{dp}}{{K}_{w}^{}}$

with ${K}_{w}$ the compressibility coefficient of the liquid (in $\mathit{Pa}$ ).

- Case of the interface

The evolution of the density of the fluid at the level of the interface is given in the saturated isothermal case by:

$\frac{d\rho }{{\rho}^{}}\text{}=\text{}\frac{{\text{dp}}_{f}}{{K}_{w}^{}}$

#### 3.3.5 Derivative of the mass supplies

## 4 Variational formulation

Before giving the expression of the variational formulations of the equilibrium equations presented above, we shall give the definition of the approximation spaces of the displacement fields, of the pressures (of the rock mass and of the interface), of the fluxes ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ and of the Lagrange multipliers $\lambda ,\mu ` et du saut de déplacement :math:`w$ useful for the cohesive-zone model:

- the space of the kinematically admissible displacement fields on the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ is such that:

${U}_{0}=\lbrace {u}^{\text{*}}\in {H}_{1}(\Omega )/{u}^{\text{*}}\text{}\text{discontinu à travers}{\Gamma}_{c},{u}^{\text{*}}=0\text{}\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{u}\rbrace$

- the space of the admissible pressure fields on the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ is such that:

${P}_{0}=\lbrace {p}^{\text{*}}\in {H}_{1}(\Omega )/{p}^{\text{*}}\text{}\text{discontinue à travers}{\Gamma}_{c},{p}^{\text{*}}=0\text{}\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{p}\rbrace$

- the space of the unknowns ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ is such that:

${Q}_{1}=\lbrace {q}_{1}^{\text{*}}\in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{c})/{q}_{1}^{\text{*}}\in {\Gamma}_{1}\rbrace$ and ${Q}_{2}=\lbrace {q}_{2}^{\text{*}}\in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{c})/{q}_{2}^{\text{*}}\in {\Gamma}_{2}\rbrace$

- the space of the pressure unknowns ${p}_{f}$ is such that:

${F}_{0}=\lbrace {p}_{f}^{\text{*}}\in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{c})/{p}_{f}^{\text{*}}\text{continue sur}{\Gamma}_{c}\rbrace$

- the space of the unknowns of the Lagrange multipliers $\lambda ,\mu ` et du saut de déplacement :math:`w$ is such that:

${L}_{0}=\lbrace {\lambda}^{\text{*}}\in {H}^{-1/2}({\Gamma}_{c})/{\lambda}^{\text{*}}\text{continue sur}{\Gamma}_{c}\rbrace$

### 4.1 Weak formulation of the mechanical problem

As explained in [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19), within the framework of the “mortar”-type formulation for the cohesive-zone model, the displacement jump $w$ is introduced as a new unknown of the problem, which will not be discretised as $⟦u⟧$ but will be a projection of it onto a reduced space ${M}_{h}$ (see §  5.2.2 ). The total energy of the problem is then written:

$E(u,\lambda ,w)=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\Omega}{\varepsilon}(u):C:{\varepsilon}(u)d\Omega -{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}t\cdot ud{\Gamma}_{t}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\Pi \left(w,\lambda \right)d{\Gamma}_{c}$

$\Pi (w,\lambda )$ is the surface energy density and $t$ the surface forces imposed on ${\Gamma}_{t}$ . The Lagrange multiplier $\lambda ` sera discrétisé sur le même espace que :math:`w$ (see [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19)).

The solution of the continuous problem consists in a minimisation under equality constraints $\left(u,w,\lambda \right)=\underset{{w}^{\text{*}}=⟦{u}^{\text{*}}⟧}{\mathit{argmin}}E\left({u}^{\text{*}},{\lambda}^{\text{*}},{w}^{\text{*}}\right)$ . We can write the associated Lagrangian as:

$L(u,w,\lambda ,\mu )=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\Omega}{\varepsilon}(u):C:{\varepsilon}(u)d\Omega -{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}t\cdot ud{\Gamma}_{t}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\Pi (w,\lambda )d{\Gamma}_{c}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\mu \cdot \left(⟦u⟧-w\right)d{\Gamma}_{c}$

The Lagrange multiplier $\mu ` sera également discrétisé sur l'espace réduit :math:`{M}_{h}$ . The writing of the optimality conditions of this Lagrangian leads to the following variational formulation:

| Equilibrium equation | $\forall {u}^{\text{*}}\in {U}_{0,}{\int}_{\Omega}\sigma (u):{\varepsilon}({u}^{\text{*}})d\Omega -{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}t\cdot {u}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{t}+{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\mu \cdot ⟦{u}^{\text{*}}⟧d{\Gamma}_{c}=0$ |
| --- | --- |
| Projection of the displacement jump | $\forall {\mu}^{\text{*}}\in {L}_{0},{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left(⟦u⟧-w\right)\cdot {\mu}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}=0$ |
| Expression of the cohesive force | $\forall {w}^{\text{*}}\in {L}_{0,}-{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left[\mu -{t}_{c}(\lambda +rw)\right]\cdot {w}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}=0$ |
| Interface law | $\forall {\lambda}^{\text{*}}\in {L}_{0,}-{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\frac{\left[\lambda -{t}_{c}(\lambda +rw)\right]}{r}\cdot {\lambda}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}=0$ |

$r$ is the augmentation parameter (see [R7.02.19](doc:r7.02.19)). Let us recall that:

- $\sigma ={\sigma}^{'}-\mathit{bp}1$
- ${t}_{c}={t}_{c}^{'}-{p}_{f}n$

## 5 Discretisation of the problem

### 5.1 Time discretisation

The mass-conservation equations in the case of the rock mass and of the interface involve explicitly in their formulations the time variable $t$ . In order to discretise these equations, we use a $\theta$ -scheme.

In terms of notation a variable indexed by a $+$ is a variable taken at the end of the time step and a variable indexed by a $-$ is a variable taken at the beginning of the time step which is *a priori* known. One sets $\Delta t={t}^{+}-{t}^{-}$ .

#### 5.1.1 Discretisation of the mechanical equation

The time discretisation of the equations for the mechanics does not involve a $\theta$ -scheme. They are expressed at the instant $+\text{}$ (that is to say after the prediction phase) and are written:

#### 5.2.1 Representation of the HM-XFEM element and associated degrees of freedom

On each of the figures presented previously, the lists at each node of the element contain the degrees of freedom (dofs) associated with each category of nodes (vertex nodes or mid-side nodes).

The degrees of freedom:

- $a$ and $b$ are associated respectively with the classical part and the enriched part of the approximation of the displacement field ${u}^{h}$ ,
- $c$ and $d$ are associated respectively with the classical part and the enriched part of the approximation of the pressure field in the rock mass ${p}^{h}$ ,
- ${p}_{f}$ are associated with the approximation of the pressure field ${p}_{f}^{h}$ at the level of the interface,
- ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ are associated with the approximation of the hydraulic Lagrange-multiplier fields ${q}_{1}^{h}$ and ${q}_{2}^{h}$ .
- $\lambda ` , :math:$mu ` and $w$ are respectively associated with the approximation of the Lagrange multipliers ${\lambda}^{h}$ and ${\mu}^{h}$ and of the displacement jump ${w}^{h}$ .

The hydromechanical elements present in *Code_Aster* use a “mixed interpolation”, in order to reduce the oscillations of the numerical solution [14]. The displacement field is thus interpolated quadratically whereas the pore-pressure field is interpolated linearly. The degrees of freedom $c$ and $d$ are therefore carried only by the vertex nodes whereas the degrees of freedom $a$ and $b$ are carried both by the mid-side nodes and the vertex nodes. As regards the fields associated with the discontinuity: ${p}_{f}^{h}$ , ${q}_{1}^{h}$ , ${q}_{2}^{h}$ , ${\lambda}^{h}$ , ${\mu}^{h}$ and ${w}^{h}$ , the degrees of freedom are carried only by the vertex nodes. In addition, their approximation space is reduced (see §  5.2.2 ) in order to respect the LBB stability condition [8,9]. This condition indeed imposes a hierarchy between the approximation spaces, failing which one observes the appearance of oscillations and the non-uniqueness of the solution of the coupled problem.

Furthermore it is important to note that the HM-XFEM elements which share vertex nodes with the classical HM elements must undergo an additional treatment. One must indeed set to zero the enriched degrees of freedom (for the pressure fields in the rock mass and the displacement fields) but also set to zero the degrees of freedom ${p}_{f}$ , ${q}_{1}$ , ${q}_{2}$ , $\lambda ` , :math:$mu ` and $w$ at the level of the vertex nodes in common between these two types of element. The elimination procedure used is that described in § 4.4 of the documentation [R5.03.54](doc:r5.03.54).

#### 5.2.2 Reduced space for the discretisation of the fields associated with the discontinuity

For a detailed description of the discretisation of the contact unknowns (and a fortiori of all the unknowns associated with the discontinuity), the reader may refer to the documentation [R5.03.54](doc:r5.03.54), §5. In short, the initial components of the multiplier are defined on the vertex nodes $K$ of the intersected parent elements (see fig.). The implementation of such contact elements is detailed in [R5.03.54](doc:r5.03.54), §4. One then imposes equality relations between certain of these initial components in order to arrive at a smaller number ${N}_{\lambda}$ of effectively independent degrees of freedom. These relations are carried by certain intersected edges $V$ , called vital edges: a truly independent degree of freedom $I$ is shared by a group of nodes of $K$ (see fig.), which produces an extended contact shape function ${\psi}_{I}:=\sum_{i\in I}{N}_{i}$ (see fig.). The algorithm for selecting such vital edges, and therefore for constructing the reduced space, is detailed in the documentation [R5.03.54](doc:r5.03.54), §6. The multiplier field is then obtained by interpolation on the parent elements and the discrete multiplier is the trace of this field on the interface:

${M}_{h}:=\left\lbrace \sum_{I}{\mu}_{I}{\psi}_{I}{\text{|}}_{\Gamma},{\mu}_{I}\in {\mathrm{ℝ}}^{d}\right\rbrace$

Figure 5.2.2-1: Mesh non-conforming to the crack and reduced space for the interface

In our case, the fields associated with the discontinuity are not only the Lagrange multipliers $\lambda ,\mu ` et le saut de déplacement :math:`w$ useful for the cohesive law CZM_LIN_MIX but also the hydraulic fields associated with the discontinuity: the fluid pressure in the discontinuity ${p}_{f}$ , and the fluxes ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ . #### 5.2.3 Approximations of the fields with XFEM

The approximation of the fields present can therefore be put in the form (the test fields used in the weak forms of the equilibrium equations are approximated in the same manner):

- for the displacement field:

${u}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nn}}{a}_{i}{\mathrm{\phi }}_{i}(x)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathit{nne}}{b}_{j}{\mathrm{\phi }}_{j}(x)\stackrel{~}{H}(\mathit{lsn}(x))$

- for the pressure field in the rock mass:

${p}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nns}}{c}_{i}{\psi}_{i}(x)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\sum_{j=1}^{\mathit{nnse}}{d}_{j}{\psi}_{j}(x)\stackrel{~}{H}(\mathit{lsn}(x))$

- for the pressure field in the interface:

${p}_{f}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{({p}_{f})}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

- for the hydraulic Lagrange-multiplier fields:

${q}_{1}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{({q}_{1})}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

${q}_{2}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{({q}_{2})}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

- for the Lagrange-multiplier fields and the displacement jump useful for the cohesive-zone model:

${\lambda}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{\lambda}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

${\mu}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{\mu}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

${w}^{h}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{\mathit{nnc}}{w}_{i}\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$

with:

- $\mathit{lsn}$ the normal *level set*  whose iso-zero represents the interface. For more details on the use of the *level set*  with XFEM in *Code_Aster* , the reader may refer to the documentation [R7.02.13](doc:r7.02.13),
- $\stackrel{~}{H}$ the function discontinuous across the discontinuity located by $\mathit{lsn}(x)=0$ (see [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12))
- ${\phi }_{i}(x)$ the shape functions of the quadratic element used for the interpolation of the displacement field,
- ${\psi}_{i}(x)$ the shape functions of the linear parent element used for the interpolation of the pressure field in the rock mass.
- $\stackrel{~}{{\psi}_{i}}(x)$ the linear shape functions used for the interpolation of the fields associated with the discontinuity. They differ from the shape functions of the linear parent element ${\psi}_{i}(x)$ in the case where vertex nodes of the linear parent element belong to no edge intersected by the discontinuity (see §  5.4 and [R5.03.54](doc:r5.03.54) for more details). This situation appears only for the non-simplicial elements.
- $\mathit{nn}$ the set of the nodes of the mesh and $\mathit{nne}$ the set of the enriched nodes of the mesh.
- $\mathit{nn}$ the set of the vertex nodes of the mesh and $\mathit{nne}$ the set of the enriched vertex nodes of the mesh.
- $\mathit{nnc}$ the vertex nodes belonging to an edge intersected by the discontinuity ${\Gamma}_{c}$ .

**Note:**

As we can observe in the definition of the approximations of the displacement and rock-mass pressure fields, the enrichment due to the presence of a crack tip is not taken into account. In the formulation of the mechanical problem, we rely on a regularised cohesive-zone model. The advantage of such a model (historically introduced by Barenblatt [12] in order to improve Griffith's theory concerning fractured media [13]) is to overcome the fact that the stresses are infinite at the crack tip. Consequently, the enrichment by singular functions of the approximation of the displacement field is inappropriate here, but entirely conceivable as regards the enrichment of the approximation of the pressure field in the rock mass as suggested by [7].

## 6 Solution of the coupled problem

### 6.1 Linearisation of the coupled problem

#### 6.1.1 Linearisation by the Newton–Raphson method

The coupled problem being non-linear (the non-linearity of the problem is due to the mass terms of the variational formulations of the mass-conservation equations for the rock mass and for the interface and to the cohesive terms for the mechanics) we proceed to its linearisation by means of the Newton–Raphson method.

Let $F$ be the non-linear system associated with the variational formulations of the mass-conservation equations (for the rock mass and for the interface), of the mechanical equilibrium equation and of the continuity condition of the pressure ${p}_{f}$ at the level of the interface. Let ${x}^{k}$ be the vector of the nodal unknowns at Newton iteration $k$ such that:

${x}^{k}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\lbrace {u}^{k}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}{p}^{k}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}{p}_{f}^{k}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}{q}_{1}^{k}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}{q}_{2}^{k}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}\lambda \phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}\mu \phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}w\rbrace }^{T}$

At iteration $k+1$ (the vector of the nodal unknowns ${x}^{k+1}$ is not known) one sets:

$F({x}^{k+1})=0$

In order to be able to determine ${x}^{k+1}$ , we have recourse to a Taylor expansion of $F$ (which is a vector function assumed continuous and differentiable) in the neighbourhood of ${x}^{k}$ (then known at iteration $k+1$ ). Thus the linear system at iteration $k+1$ is written:

$-F({x}^{k})=\frac{\partial F({x}^{k})}{\partial {x}^{k}}\cdot \delta {x}^{k}$

with $\delta {x}^{k}={x}^{k+1}-{x}^{k}$ the increment of the values of the nodal unknowns between two successive iterations (which is an unknown at iteration $k+1$ ), $\frac{\partial F({x}^{k})}{\partial {x}^{k}}$ the tangent matrix and $F({x}^{k})$ the right-hand side. These last two terms are known at iteration $k+1$ and are functions of ${x}^{k}$ .

#### 6.1.2 Integral writing of the linearised problem

In what follows one considers that the unknowns are denoted with a $\delta$ and the test fields with a $\text{*}$ as a superscript.

The linear system at Newton iteration $k+1$ is written (for a time step):

- mechanical equilibrium equation

$\forall {u}^{\text{*}}\in {U}_{0}$ :

$\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}^{\text{' +}}(\delta u):\epsilon ({u}^{\text{*}})d\Omega \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}-\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{\Omega}b\delta p(1:\epsilon ({u}^{\text{*}}))d\Omega \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}-\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{\Omega}{\rho}^{+}b{F}^{m+}\mathit{Tr}(\nabla (\delta u)){u}^{\text{*}}d\Omega \hfill \\ \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}-\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{\Omega}\left(\frac{{\rho}^{+}(b-{\varphi}^{+})}{{K}_{s}^{}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\rho}^{+}{\varphi}^{+}}{{K}_{w}^{}}\right)\delta p{F}^{m+}{u}^{\text{*}}d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\delta \mu \cdot ⟦{u}^{\text{*}}⟧d{\Gamma}_{c}\hfill \\ \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}-{\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}^{\text{' +}}(u):\epsilon ({u}^{\text{*}})d\Omega \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{\Omega}b{p}^{+}(1:\epsilon ({u}^{\text{*}}))d\Omega \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{\Omega}{r}^{+}{F}^{m+}{u}^{\text{*}}d\Omega \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}+\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{t}}{t}^{+}{u}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{t}\hfill \\ \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}-\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\mu \cdot ⟦{u}^{\text{*}}⟧d{\Gamma}_{c}\hfill \end{array}$

- projection of the displacement jump

$\forall {\mu}^{\text{*}}\in {L}_{0}$ :

${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left(⟦\delta u⟧-\delta w\right)\cdot {\mu}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}=-{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left(⟦u⟧-w\right)\cdot {\mu}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}$

- cohesive stresses

$\forall {w}^{\text{*}}\in {L}_{0}$ :

$\begin{array}{c}-{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left[\delta \mu -\frac{\partial {t'}_{c}}{\partial (\lambda +rw)}\cdot \left(\delta \lambda +r\delta w\right)\right]\cdot {w}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}-{\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\delta {p}_{f}{n}_{c}\cdot {w}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}\hfill \\ ={\int}_{{\Gamma}_{c}}\left[\mu -{t'}_{c}(\lambda +rw)+{p}_{f}{n}_{c}\right]\cdot {w}^{\text{*}}d{\Gamma}_{c}\hfill \end{array}$
