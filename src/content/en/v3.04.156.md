# v3.04.156 FORMA07 - Practical work of the Advanced Use Training: Circular crack in infinite medium

**Summary:**

This $\mathrm{3D}$ test is in quasi-static form, and is part of the post-processings validation in linear elastic fracture mechanics. The structure with a circular crack is set to tensile.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A cube subjected to a tensile force with a horizontal circular crack of $a=2m$ radius is considered. A cube on the side of $10a$ will be considered.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.156/1000000000000257000001F26ACBD3D1950A819C.png)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: geometry**

### 1.2 Material properties

A homogeneous isotropic material with linear elasticity is considered to have the following characteristics:

- Young's modulus $E=\mathrm{200 000}\mathit{Mpa}$ ,
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0,3$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The structure is in traction ($\sigma =1\mathit{MPa}$ ).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method used for reference solution

For a circular $a$ radius crack in an infinite medium, subject to uniform $\sigma$ traction as normal in the lip plane, the local $G(s)$ energy return rate is independent of the curvilinear abscisse along the $s$ crack front and is expressed as follows[1]:

$G(s)=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{\pi E}4{\sigma}^{2}a$

The stress factor ${K}_{I}(s)$ is given by the formula of Irwin:

$G(s)=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}{K}_{I}^{2}$ , i.e. ${K}_{I}(s)=\frac{2\sigma \sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}}$

### 2.2 Baseline

With the numerical values of the statement, we find: ${K}_{I}=1,5957\mathit{MPa}.\sqrt{m}$ and $G=11,59{\mathit{J.m}}^{-2}$ .

### 2.3 Bibliographical references

  1. Tada, P. Paris, G. Irwin, The stress analysis of cracks manual, 3rd edition, 2000

## 4 Modelling B: X-FEM 3D

The problem considered is the same as that studied in Part II. However, unlike the previous part where the crack was meshed, this part considers a healthy mesh. The crack is therefore not meshed. For this, the X-FEM method is used.
