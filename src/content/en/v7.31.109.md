# v7.31.109 WTNV109 - Water and mechanical loading of saturated porous medium

**Summary:**

A three-dimensional problem of thermo-hydro-mechanical coupling of a saturated porous medium is considered.

The test consists of studying the effect of mechanics and hydraulics on the thermal. The element is stretched by imposing a shift in the direction $z$ , hydraulic pressure is imposed on the whole domain and the effect of these loads on the temperature of the model is studied. The aim is to look at the very low coupling of poromechanical to the thermal. The first time step is limited.

The models studied are $\mathrm{2D}$ planes (DPQ8 and DPTR6) and $\mathrm{3D}$ density (HEXA20) with linear behavior for hydraulics and thermals.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Introduction

The test case study examines the thermo-hydro-mechanical behavior of a saturated porous medium consisting of a single fluid: water in its liquid phase. It is used in THM Modelling in *Code_Aster*. The constitutive law of the associated fluid is LIQU_SATU type.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is one-dimensional because it depends only on the vertical coordinate. Therefore, we will reason on a linear axis according to z.

If gravity is neglected and there is no external heat source, the thermal equation is given by the following expression:

$$
{h}_{l}^{m}\frac{d{m}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}+\frac{\delta Q'}{\mathrm{dt}}D{M}_{l}+\mathrm{divq}=0
$$

Where ${h}_{l}^{m}$ is the water mass enthalpy, ${m}_{l}$ its mass, ${M}_{l}$ its flux, q the heat flux and $Q'$ the unconvected heat.

The mass conservation equation is as follows:

$$
\frac{d{m}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}+D{M}_{l}=0
$$

In the absence of water flow, the equation is simplified to become:

$$
\frac{\delta Q'}{\mathrm{dt}}+\mathrm{divq}=0
$$

In this equation, the quantity $Q'$ represents the heat received by the system in a transformation for which there are no heat inputs by fluid input. Low pressure variation and parameters are chosen so that the temperature varies little and therefore:

$\frac{dQ'}{T}=\frac{dQ'}{{T}_{0}}$

$\mathrm{dQ}'$ therefore has this expression:

$\mathrm{dQ}'=3{\alpha}_{0}{K}_{0}{T}_{0}d{\varepsilon}_{v}+{C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\mathrm{dT}-3{\alpha}_{l}^{m}{T}_{0}{\mathrm{dp}}_{l}$

with:

- ${\alpha}_{0}$ the homogenized thermal expansion coefficient equivalent to that of solid ${\alpha}_{s}$ .
- ${K}_{0}$ the drained coefficient of elasticity.
- ${C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}$ heat has constant deformation which has as expression ${C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}={C}_{0}^{\sigma}-9{T}_{0}{K}_{0}{\alpha}_{0}^{2}$ and ${C}_{0}^{\sigma}$ heat at constant stress.
- ${\alpha}_{l}^{m}$ The relative thermal expansion coefficient of the liquid relative to the skeleton, it is expressed as: ${\alpha}_{l}^{m}=(b-\phi ){\alpha}_{s}+\phi {\alpha}_{l}$ with b the coefficient of Biot, $\phi$ the porosity and ${\alpha}_{l}$ the coefficient of expansion of the liquid.
- $d{\varepsilon}_{v}$ The variation of density deformation.
- ${\mathrm{dp}}_{l}$ The fluid pressure variation.

In addition, the heat flow has the following expression: $q=-{\lambda}_{T}\partial \frac{T}{\partial z}$ where ${\lambda}_{T}$ is the coefficient of thermal conductivity.

By replacing $\mathrm{dQ}'$ and q with their value in the equation and limiting itself to time step $\Delta t$ , we obtain:

$3{\alpha}_{0}{K}_{0}{T}_{0}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{v}-3{\alpha}_{l}^{m}{T}_{0}\Delta {p}_{l}+{C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}\Delta T=-\Delta tdq={\lambda}_{l}\Delta t\frac{{\partial}^{2}T}{\partial {z}^{2}}$

Considering temperatures and initially zero movements, we can write:

$\Delta T=T(t)-T(0)=T$ et $\Delta \varepsilon =\varepsilon (t)-\varepsilon (0)=\varepsilon$ et $\Delta {p}_{l}={p}_{l}(t)-{p}_{l}(0)={p}_{l}$

So at the first time step, we'll have:

$T-a\frac{{\partial}^{2}T}{\partial {z}^{2}}=b$

with $a=\frac{{\lambda}_{l}\Delta T}{{C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}}$ and $b=-\frac{3{\alpha}_{0}{K}_{0}{T}_{0}\varepsilon -3{\alpha}_{l}^{m}{T}_{0}{p}_{l}}{{C}_{0}^{\varepsilon}}$

The variational formulation of this expression (in the one-dimensional case) is then as follows:

$$
{\int}_{\Omega}T.\stackrel{ˆ}{T}\mathrm{dz}+a{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\frac{\partial \stackrel{ˆ}{T}}{\partial z}\mathrm{dz}-a{\int}_{\partial \Omega }\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}\stackrel{ˆ}{T}\mathrm{dz}={\int}_{\Omega}b.\stackrel{ˆ}{T}\mathrm{dz}
$$

To establish the analytical solution one considers a single element of degree 1 since in Modelling THM the thermo-hydraulic part is treated with linear elements.

Or a linear element:

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.109/Object_23.svg)

Boundary conditions: $\frac{\partial T}{\partial z}=0$ at both ends (z=0.5 and z = -0.5)

The temperature on the basis of the shape functions is written as follows:

$T(z,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{i=2}{T}^{i}(t){\lambda}_{i}(z)$

with

${\lambda}_{1}(z)=0,5(1+\mathrm{2z})$

et

${\lambda}_{2}(z)=0,5(1-\mathrm{2z})$

The following matrices are then introduced:

$\left[A\right]=\left[{A}_{ij}\right];{A}_{ij}={\int}_{-0,5}^{0,5}{\lambda}_{i}{\lambda}_{j}\mathrm{dz}$

$\left[B\right]=\left[{B}_{ij}\right];{B}_{ij}={\int}_{-0,5}^{0,5}\frac{\partial {\lambda}_{i}}{\partial z}\frac{\partial {\lambda}_{j}}{\partial z}\mathrm{dz}$

which leads to:

$\left[A\right]=\frac{1}{6}\left[\begin{array}{cc}2& 1\\ 1& 2\end{array}\right]$ et $\left[B\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]$

The following are usually noted:

$\lbrace T\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}^{1}\\ {T}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

et $\lbrace \frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {T}^{1}}{\partial t}\\ \frac{\partial {T}^{2}}{\partial t}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The equation then becomes

$[A]\lbrace T\rbrace +a[B]\lbrace T\rbrace -a[A]\lbrace \frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\rbrace =[A]\lbrace b\rbrace$

with the boundary conditions imposed (zero downward displacement and zero temperature fluxes at both ends), the following shall be applied:

$\lbrace b\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\\ b\end{array}\right\rbrace \mathrm{et}\lbrace \frac{\partial T}{\partial t}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace$

What ultimately gives us

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}^{1}\\ {T}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace +a{[A]}^{-1}[B]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}^{1}\\ {T}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\\ b\end{array}\right\rbrace$

Finally, we get:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}1+\mathrm{6a}& -\mathrm{6a}\\ -\mathrm{6a}& 1+\mathrm{6a}\end{array}\right]\lbrace T\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}^{1}\\ {T}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

For a short time of 100s, we'll have $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}^{1}\\ {T}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{2.10}^{-14}\\ 1,045{.10}^{-7}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

We'll consider ${T}^{1}$ null.
