# r7.01.32 Constitutive law for reinforced-concrete plates GLRC_DM

**Summary:**

This documentation presents the theoretical formulation and the numerical integration of the constitutive law GLRC_DM, which can be used with the modelling DKTG. It belongs to the so-called “global” models used for thin structures (beams, plates and shells). The non-linear phenomena, such as plasticity or damage, are directly related to the generalised strains (extension, curvature, distortion) and the generalised stresses (membrane forces, bending forces and shear forces). Thus, this constitutive law applies with a plate or shell finite element. This makes it possible to save, compared with a multi-layer approach, CPU time as well as memory. The advantage over multi-layer shells is even greater when one of the constituents of the plate behaves in a quasi-brittle manner (concrete, for example), since the global model makes it possible to avoid localisation problems.

The constitutive law GLRC_DM models damage under membrane loading and under bending loading of reinforced-concrete plates, with the aid of “homogenised” parameters. This constitutive model therefore represents an evolution with respect to the model GLRC_DAMAGE, which treats damage only under bending loading. The structure of the damage model of GLRC_DM resembles that of GLRC_DAMAGE, and both are inspired by ENDO_ISOT_BETON. Unlike GLRC_DAMAGE, GLRC_DM does not make it possible to model a possible yielding, which makes it less suitable for simulating extreme loadings. Moreover, the constitutive behaviour GLRC_DM is isotropic before damage: the orthotropy brought by the steel reinforcement layers is neglected.

One may consider using the constitutive behaviour GLRC_DM “alone” on a plate modelling in order to represent reinforced concrete, or else only in order to represent the concrete alone, associating it then with modellings of steel reinforcement grids, which makes it possible to represent the orthotropy and possibly the non-symmetry of the reinforcement layers. This latter choice also simplifies the parameterisation of this constitutive behaviour.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Global models

A plate constitutive model said to be global, or a structural-element model in general, means that the constitutive law is written directly in terms of a relation between the generalised stresses and the generalised strains. The global approach to modelling the behaviour of structures applies in particular to composite structures, for example reinforced concrete (see figure r7.01.32-fig-1), and represents an alternative to the so-called local or semi-global approaches, which are finer but more costly modellings (see  and ). In the local approach one uses a fine modelling for each of the phases (steel, concrete) and their interactions (bond), and in the semi-global approach one exploits the slenderness of the structure to simplify the description of the kinematics, which leads to the PMF (multi-fibre beam) or multi-layer shell models.

The interest of the global model lies in the fact that the corresponding finite element requires only one integration point through the thickness, and above all in the obtaining of a homogenised behaviour. This advantage is even more important in the analysis of reinforced concrete, since one bypasses the localisation problems encountered when modelling concrete without reinforcement. Obviously, a global model represents the local phenomena in a coarse manner and requires more validation before its application to industrial examples.

### 1.2 Objectives of the law GLRC_DM

The model GLRC_DM is capable of representing the damage of a reinforced-concrete plate, when the latter remains limited, that is, without reaching failure. It was inspired by the model GLRC_DAMAGE and is complementary to it. If GLRC_DAMAGE can also represent damage, this is possible only for bending loading, with no impact on the membrane behaviour. On this point GLRC_DM is more faithful to the physics, but unlike GLRC_DAMAGE it does not make it possible to take plasticity into account. Being simpler, GLRC_DM is also more efficient in terms of computation cost and numerical robustness.

One aims at an identical behaviour in the directions $\mathrm{Ox}$ and $\mathrm{Oy}$ ; moreover the upper and lower reinforcement layers are assumed to be identical.

## 2 Formulation of the constitutive model

The use of the theory of thin plates and shells makes it possible to describe efficiently the mechanical behaviour of reinforced-concrete structures, which are generally slender.

In a first step of the construction of the model, the existence of a homogenised medium with the same mechanical behaviour as the reinforced-concrete structure of interest is assumed. For simplicity, it is assumed that this medium is isotropic and that the structural element studied is symmetric with respect to its middle surface. These assumptions are not indispensable for the formulation, but were made in order to simplify the approach. Moreover, their impact on the behaviour is estimated to be smaller than that of cracking, which is at the centre of interest of the model.

It must be noted that the use of this model is associated with that of a plate element. It can be used only in the framework of **DKT** finite elements (supported modelling: DKTG), corresponding to the theory of **Love-Kirchhoff**, in which any transverse distortion through the thickness of the plate is neglected.

### 2.1 Free energy

For a homogeneous isotropic continuous medium with linear elastic behaviour, the volumetric density of the free energy can be written as:

${\Phi}_{e}(\varepsilon )=\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\varepsilon )}^{2}+\mu \sum_{i=1}^{3}{{\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{i}}^{2}$

where $\lambda ,\mu$ are the Lamé coefficients, $\varepsilon$ the strain tensor and ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{i}$ its eigenvalues. As for the model ENDO_ISOT_BETON, damage is introduced by a function $\xi (.,{d}_{i})$ , ${d}_{i}$ being a damage variable. Thus, for a damageable medium, the free energy is written:

$$
{\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}(\varepsilon ,{d}_{j})=\frac{\lambda}{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\varepsilon )}^{2}\xi (\mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon ),{d}_{j})+\mu \sum_{i=1}^{3}{{\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{i}}^{2}\xi ({\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})
$$

with the function $(x,d)\in {ℝ}^{2}\to \xi (x,d)\le 1$ satisfying a priori $\frac{\partial \xi }{\partial d}\le 0$ in order to represent the loss of stiffness linked to damage, and $\frac{\partial \xi }{\partial x}=0 \quad \mathrm{pour} \quad x\in \left]-\infty ,0\right[\cup \left]0,+\infty \right[$ , the jump at $0$ making it possible to represent the discontinuity of behaviour between tension and compression.

Equation  is valid for a continuous medium and it will be applied to a Love-Kirchhoff plate $\omega \times \left]\frac{-h}{2};\frac{h}{2}\right[$ , of thickness $h$ (one writes $z={x}_{3}$ ), where kinematic assumptions of Hencky-Mindlin are made (see ):

$\left(\begin{array}{}{U}_{1}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\\ {U}_{2}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\\ {U}_{z}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\end{array} \right)=\underset{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{cinématique}\mathrm{de}\mathrm{plaque}\\ {u}^{s}\in {V}_{s}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left(\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2})\\ {u}_{2}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2})\\ {u}_{z}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2})\end{array} \right)+z\left(\begin{array}{}{\theta}_{2}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2})\\ -{\theta}_{1}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2})\\ 0\end{array} \right)}}+\underset{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{déplacement}\mathrm{complémentaire}\\ {u}^{c}\in {V}_{c}\end{array}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\left(\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}^{c}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\\ {u}_{2}^{c}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\\ {u}_{z}^{c}({x}_{1,}{x}_{2,}z)\end{array} \right)}}$

where $U={({U}_{1}{U}_{2}{U}_{z})}^{T}$ is the displacement field in 3D, $u={({u}_{1}{u}_{2}{u}_{z})}^{T}$ the displacement of the middle surface and ${\theta}_{x}$ , ${\theta}_{y}$ its rotations. Thus, the strain tensor, defined as:

${\varepsilon}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial {U}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}+\frac{\partial {U}_{j}}{\partial {x}_{i}}\right),i,j=\mathrm{1..3}$

is also written as:

$$
\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{11}=\underset{{\varepsilon}_{11}^{s}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\epsilon}_{11}+z{\kappa}_{11}}}+{u}_{1,1}^{c}\\ {\varepsilon}_{22}=\underset{{\varepsilon}_{22}^{s}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\epsilon}_{22}+z{\kappa}_{22}}}+{u}_{2,2}^{c}\\ {\varepsilon}_{12}=\underset{{\varepsilon}_{12}^{s}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\epsilon}_{12}+z{\kappa}_{12}}}+\frac{1}{2}({u}_{2,1}^{c}+{u}_{1,2}^{c})\\ {\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{1z}}={\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{1z}}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}{u}_{3,1}^{c}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{2z}}={\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{2z}}^{c}=\frac{1}{2}{u}_{3,2}^{c}\\ {\varepsilon}_{zz}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{c}={u}_{3,z}^{c}\end{array}
$$

where $\epsilon$ is the membrane-extension tensor, defined in the plane:

${\epsilon}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial {u}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}+\frac{\partial {u}_{j}}{\partial {x}_{i}}\right),i,j=\mathrm{1..2}$

and $\kappa$ the curvature-change tensor, defined in the plane:

${\kappa}_{11}=\frac{\partial {\theta}_{2}}{\partial {x}_{1}}$ , ${\kappa}_{22}=\frac{-\partial {\theta}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{2}}$ , ${\kappa}_{12}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial {\theta}_{2}}{\partial {x}_{2}}-\frac{\partial {\theta}_{1}}{\partial {x}_{1}}\right)$

relations to which is added the plane-stress assumption ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ , ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{1z}}=0$ , ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{2z}}=0$ , which will determine the complementary displacement field ${u}^{c}\in {V}_{c}$ . In the theory used here, only two rotation components ${\theta}_{x}$ and ${\theta}_{y}$ are introduced, which implies that the curvature-change tensor is 2D and has only 3 independent components.

Introducing these kinematic assumptions, cf. , into the expression of the free energy, , one can determine the eigenvalues of the strain ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{i}=\tilde{{(\epsilon +z\kappa )}_{i}}$ . These eigenvalues being, in general, non-polynomial functions of the coordinate $z$ , the integral $\int{\phi }_{\mathrm{ed}}\mathrm{dz}$ is not computable analytically. This formulation would therefore not be suitable for application to structural elements.

Instead of the formulation , a formulation of the free energy defined directly in generalised strains, $\epsilon$ and $\kappa$ , will rather be used:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}(\epsilon ,\kappa ,{\varepsilon}_{zz},{d}_{j})& =& \frac{\lambda}{2}{(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}^{2}.{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})+\mu (\sum_{i=1}^{2}{{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}^{2}.{\xi}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})+{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{2})\\ & +& \frac{\lambda}{2}{z}^{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\kappa )}^{2}.{\xi}_{f}(\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ),{d}_{j})+\mu {z}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}}^{2}.{\xi}_{f}({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i},{d}_{j})+z(.)\end{array}
$$

where $z(.)$ contains all the terms coupling $\epsilon$ and $\kappa$ , which disappear after integration over $z$ , if one makes the assumption that the plate/beam is symmetric with respect to the middle surface. One thus obtains the surface density of the free energy:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}(\epsilon ,\kappa ,{\varepsilon}_{zz},{d}_{j})& =& {\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}{\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}(\epsilon ,\kappa ,{\varepsilon}_{zz},{d}_{j})\mathrm{dz}\\ & =& \frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{2}{(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}^{2}.{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})+{\mu}_{m}(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}^{2}.{\xi}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})+{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{2})\\ & +& \frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\kappa )}^{2}.{\xi}_{f}(\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ),{d}_{j})+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}}^{2}.{\xi}_{f}({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i},{d}_{j})\end{array}
$$

where $h$ is the thickness of the plate and ${\lambda}_{m},{\lambda}_{f},{\mu}_{m},{\mu}_{f}$ are defined in section r7.01.32-parametres-elastique-endommageable . The surface density of the free energy is strictly convex for ${d}_{j}=0$ (the elastic coefficients having been correctly chosen).

In  and  the assumption has also been made that the damage is not affected by the extension in $z$ , which is expressed by the absence of ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ from the arguments of the damage indicator ${\xi}_{m}$ . This is justified by the objective of this model, which is to represent cracking perpendicular to the middle surface, which is triggered either under membrane loading or in bending, but never in extension by ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ . Moreover, from the point of view of the numerical solution addressed further below, this assumption facilitates the local computation of the damage variables ${d}_{j}$ and the satisfaction of the plane-stress assumption ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ .

As for the damage variable, it is described by two components, one of the two representing globally the damage on the side of the upper face of the plate and the other representing the damage on the side of the lower face of the plate:

$d(z)=\left\{ \begin{array}{ccc}{d}_{1}& \text{si}& z\ge 0\\ {d}_{2}& \text{si}& z<0\end{array}\right.$

It remains to define the characteristic functions of the damage ${\xi}_{m}(.,{d}_{j})$ and ${\xi}_{f}(.,{d}_{j})$ , so that the formulation, , is complete:

${\xi}_{m}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}((\frac{1+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{d}_{1}}{1+{d}_{1}}+\frac{1+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{d}_{2}}{1+{d}_{2}})H(x)+(\frac{{\alpha}_{c}+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}{d}_{1}}{{\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{1}}+\frac{{\alpha}_{c}+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}{d}_{2}}{{\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{2}})H(-x))\in [0,1]$

et

${\xi}_{f}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=\frac{\alpha +{\gamma}_{f}{d}_{1}}{\alpha +{d}_{1}}H(x)+\frac{\alpha +{\gamma}_{f}{d}_{2}}{\alpha +{d}_{2}}H(-x)\in [0,1]$

where $H(.)$ is the Heaviside function.

The functions $(x,d)\in {ℝ}^{2}\to \xi (x,d)\le 1$ , which are expressed in the same manner as that chosen in the law ENDO_ISOT_BETON, cf. [r7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04), by operating a change of variable on ${d}_{i}$ , offer the advantage of giving a constant slope in the damage phases. They are decreasing $\frac{\partial \xi }{\partial d}\le 0$ and convex in order to represent the loss of stiffness linked to damage, and $\frac{\partial \xi }{\partial x}=0\mathrm{pour}x\in \left]-\infty ,0\right[\cup \left]0,+\infty \right[$ , the jump at $0$ making it possible to represent the change of behaviour between tension and compression, however without introducing a discontinuity. The damage variables ${d}_{i}$ grow up to $+\infty$ .

The characteristic functions of the damage ${\xi}_{m}(.,{d}_{j})$ and ${\xi}_{f}(.,{d}_{j})$ vary from $1$ to respectively ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}$ or ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}$ , and ${\gamma}_{f}$ , for ${d}_{j}\to +\infty$ . This model therefore describes a partial but not total damage of the material.

The damage parameters ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}$ for tension in membrane, ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}$ for compression in membrane and ${\gamma}_{f}$ for bending, may have values in $[0,1]$ , so that the model is not softening, which would lead to difficulties of dependence on the spatial discretisation and of convergence. One will choose ${\gamma}_{.}\approx 0$ when the corresponding phenomenon will have more impact on the damage and ${\gamma}_{.}\approx 1$ when the latter will be negligible. Thus, for reinforced concrete, one expects ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}\approx 1$ and ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}\approx 0$ . As for the parameter $\alpha$ , it makes it possible to adjust the contribution of bending to the damage threshold. The parameter ${\alpha}_{c}$ makes it possible to modulate the evolution of the damage in compression.

### 2.2 Generalised stresses

According to the usual procedure, the generalised stresses (normal forces and moments) are defined by the derivatives of the free-energy density with respect to the generalised strains, $\epsilon$ and $\kappa$ :

$N=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial \epsilon }$ ;    $M=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial \kappa }$

In the present application, the generalised stresses are computed in the frame of the eigenvectors of the generalised strains and they are written as (see the computation in the appendix):

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}{\tilde{N}}_{i}& =& \frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}=& {\lambda}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz}).{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})+2{\mu}_{m}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}.{\xi}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j}),& i=\mathrm{1..2}\\ {\tilde{M}}_{i}& =& \frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}}=& {\lambda}_{f}\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ).{\xi}_{f}(\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ),{d}_{j})+2{\mu}_{f}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}.{\xi}_{f}({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i},{d}_{j}),& i=1,2\end{array}
$$

It is easy to verify (see the appendix) that the generalised stresses $N$ and $M$ , defined from the expressions , share respectively the same eigenvectors as the generalised strains $\epsilon$ and $\kappa$ . If these eigenvectors are denoted respectively as ${Q}_{m}$ and ${Q}_{f}$ , one can write:

$$
N={Q}_{m}\tilde{N}{Q}_{m}^{T} \quad \text{et} \quad M={Q}_{f}\tilde{M}{Q}_{f}^{T}
$$

where $\tilde{N}$ and $\tilde{M}$ are the diagonal matrices composed of the eigenvalues defined in . It is important to note that the eigenvectors for the membrane part ${Q}_{m}$ and for bending ${Q}_{f}$ are completely independent.

Likewise, the pinching stresses are defined as the dual variable of ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ :

$$
{\sigma}_{zz}=\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}={\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{1,}{d}_{2}).(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})+2{\mu}_{m}{\varepsilon}_{zz}
$$

on which the plane-stress condition will be imposed: ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ .

### 2.3 Thresholds and evolution of the damage

In order to be able to define a damage threshold in the framework of the assumption of a *generalised standard material* (see , ), the thermodynamic forces associated with the variables ${d}_{1}$ and ${d}_{2}$ are introduced:

$$
{Y}_{j}=-\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial {d}_{j}}={Y}_{j}^{m}+{Y}_{j}^{f}
$$

where

$$
{Y}_{j}^{m}(\epsilon ,{d}_{j},{\varepsilon}_{zz})=\frac{1}{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}^{2}.{G}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}^{2}.{G}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})))
\text{en notant} \quad {G}_{m}(x,{d}_{j})=(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})H(x)+(\frac{{\alpha}_{c}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}){(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}{{({\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{j})}^{2}})H(-x)\in [0,1]
\text{de telle sorte que :} \quad \frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})}{\partial {d}_{j}}=-\frac{{G}_{m}(x,{d}_{j})}{2{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}
\text{et} \quad {Y}_{j}^{f}(\kappa ,{d}_{j})=\frac{\alpha}{{(\alpha +{d}_{j})}^{2}}{Y}_{j}^{f,0}(\kappa )
\text{avec} \quad {Y}_{j}^{f,0}(\kappa )=(1-{\gamma}_{f})(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\kappa )}^{2}H({(-1)}^{(j+1)}.\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ))+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{2}H({(-1)}^{(j+1)}.{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}))
$$

The damage thresholds are defined by:

$$
{f}_{{d}_{j}}={Y}_{j}(\epsilon ,\kappa ,{d}_{j},{\varepsilon}_{zz})-{k}_{{0}_{j}}\le 0
$$

where ${k}_{{0}_{j}}$ are threshold constants. These thresholds define the convex domain of reversibility in the space of $(\epsilon ,\kappa )$ .

In principle the threshold constants ${k}_{{0}_{1}}$ and ${k}_{{0}_{2}}$ could be different, but according to the assumption made on the symmetry of the plate with respect to the middle surface, the two values are the same: ${k}_{{0}_{1}}={k}_{{0}_{2}}={k}_{0}$ *.*

One sees in  that the parameter $\alpha$ regulates the contribution of bending to the initial damage threshold, since:

${f}_{{d}_{j}}({d}_{j}=0)={Y}_{j}^{m}(\epsilon ,{d}_{j}=0)+\frac{1}{\alpha}{Y}_{j}^{f,0}(\kappa )-{k}_{0}$

The evolution law of the damage variables ${d}_{1}$ and ${d}_{2}$ is defined by the normality rule to the thresholds , for which one can define the pseudo-potential of dissipation $D(\delta )$ :

$$
\begin{array}{}{\dot{d}}_{j}=\eta \frac{\partial {f}_{{d}_{j}}}{\partial {Y}_{j}},\mathrm{avec}\eta \ge 0\\ \iff {Y}_{j}\in \partial D({\dot{d}}_{j})\iff D({\dot{d}}_{j})-D(\delta )\ge {Y}_{\mathrm{j.}}({\dot{d}}_{j}-\delta ),\forall \delta \ge 0\end{array}
$$

The damage values ${d}_{1}$ and ${d}_{2}$ are perfectly determined by the following conditions:

$$
\textrm{si}
{f}_{{d}_{j}}<0
\textrm{si}
{f}_{{d}_{j}}=0
$$

The evolution of the damage variables is therefore obtained with the aid of the consistency condition; the functions $\xi (x,d)$ being convex, the hardening moduli $-{f}_{,{d}_{j}}$ are positive (the coefficients satisfying ${\gamma}_{.}\in [0,1]$ ):

$$
{\dot{d}}_{j}=-\frac{{[{f}_{,Y}.{Y}_{,\epsilon }.\dot{\epsilon}]}_{+}}{{f}_{,{d}_{j}}}
$$

One observes that in damaging pure uniaxial membrane loading ($\dot{{d}_{1}}=\dot{{d}_{2}}\ge 0$ ), the thermodynamic force is expressed: ${Y}^{m}(d)=-{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h\frac{{\varepsilon}^{2}.{\xi}_{m,d}(d)}{2}$ , ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h$ being the uniaxial membrane elastic stiffness. The consistency condition is then written:

$$
\dot{{f}_{d}}={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h\left(-\epsilon \dot{\epsilon}{\xi}_{m,d}-\frac{{\epsilon}^{2}\dot{d}{\xi}_{m,\mathrm{dd}}}{2}\right)=0
$$

Whence: $\dot{{d}_{m}}=-\frac{2\dot{\epsilon}{\xi}_{m,d}}{\epsilon {\xi}_{m,\mathrm{dd}}}$ and therefore the damaging pure uniaxial membrane loading law is:

$$
\dot{N}={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h({\xi}_{m,d}\dot{d}\epsilon +{\xi}_{m}\dot{\epsilon})={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h\dot{\epsilon}\left({\xi}_{m}-2\frac{{\xi}_{m,d}^{2}}{{\xi}_{m,\mathrm{dd}}} \right)={\gamma}_{m}{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h\dot{\epsilon}
$$

which makes it possible to interpret the role of the parameter ${\gamma}_{m}$ : the slope being constant, which is the justification of the algebraic form of the function ${\xi}_{m}$ .

### 2.4 Numerical integration

Unlike the majority of non-linear anelastic constitutive laws, the one presented here does not require the discretisation of the evolution equations of the internal variables. A direct implicit discretisation method in time is adopted.

The variables ${d}_{j}$ can be computed directly from the consistency condition, . The only time one refers to the “damage rate” is in order to verify that it is positive ${\dot{d}}_{j}\ge 0$ . For an incremental analysis this condition is expressed by ${d}_{j}^{n}\ge {d}_{j}^{n-1}$ at the time step $n>1$ , therefore without reference to a particular time-integration scheme.

Let us place ourselves at a given time instant ${t}_{n}$ of the loading path. An elastic prediction step is first carried out (elasticity tensor evaluated with the damage variables ${d}_{j}^{n-1}$ frozen at the previous step), whence $({\epsilon}^{n},{\kappa}^{n})$ . A new ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{n0}}$ is then computed:

${\sigma}_{zz}^{n}=0\Rightarrow {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{n0}}({\epsilon}^{n},{d}_{1}^{n-1},{d}_{2}^{n-1})=-\frac{{\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}({\epsilon}^{n}),{d}_{1}^{n-1},{d}_{2}^{n-1})}{2{\mu}_{m}+{\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}({\epsilon}^{n}),{d}_{1}^{n-1},{d}_{2}^{n-1})}\mathrm{tr}({\epsilon}^{n})$

see .

One then computes ${f}_{{d}_{j}}^{\mathrm{n0}}({\epsilon}^{n},{\kappa}^{n},{d}_{j}^{n-1},{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{n0}})$ in order to check the damage thresholds. If ${f}_{{d}_{j}}^{\mathrm{n0}}\le 0$ , the damage does not evolve: ${d}_{j}^{n}={d}_{j}^{n-1}$ and ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{n}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{\mathrm{n0}}$ , and the generalised stresses are computed according to .

When ${f}_{{d}_{j}}^{\mathrm{n0}}>0$ , the damage may evolve and one must solve the equations:

${f}_{{d}_{j}}^{n}={Y}_{j}({\epsilon}^{n},{\kappa}^{n},{d}_{j}^{n},{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{n})-{k}_{0}=0$

Which corresponds to the solution of the non-linear equations, for given $\epsilon ,\kappa$ :

The tangent operator of this non-linear system is defined as:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{{\mathrm{dR}}_{{d}_{j}}}{d({d}_{j})}& =& \frac{\partial {R}_{{d}_{j}}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}{\partial {d}_{j}}+\frac{\partial {R}_{{d}_{j}}}{\partial {d}_{j}}\\ & =& \frac{1}{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{2}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz}){G}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}{\partial {d}_{j}})\\ & +& \frac{1}{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}^{2}\frac{\partial {G}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})}{\partial {d}_{j}}{\varepsilon}_{zz}+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}^{2}\frac{\partial {G}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})}{\partial {d}_{j}}))\\ & -& \frac{2}{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{3}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}^{2}{G}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}^{2}{G}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})))\\ & -& \frac{2\alpha (1-{\gamma}_{f})}{{(\alpha +{d}_{j})}^{3}}(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}\mathrm{tr}{(\kappa )}^{2}H({(-1)}^{(j+1)}\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ))+{\mu}_{f}\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}^{2}H({(-1)}^{(j+1)}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}))\end{array}
$$

where:

$$
\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}{\partial {d}_{j}}=-\frac{{\lambda}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})}{(2{\mu}_{m}+{\lambda}_{\mathrm{m.}}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j}))}\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})}{\partial {d}_{j}}
$$

and:

$$
\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}}{\partial {d}_{j}}(x,{d}_{j})=-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}}{{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}H(x)+\frac{{\alpha}_{c}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})}{{({\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{j})}^{2}}H(-x) \right)=-\frac{{G}_{m}(x,{d}_{j})}{2{(1+{d}_{j})}^{2}}
$$

One verifies that one does indeed have $\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}}{\partial {d}_{j}}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})<0$ as expected.

### 2.5 Tangent stiffness operator

Since the principal objective of a global model is to propose a simplified approach to the modelling of a complex material, such as reinforced concrete, it is essential that its numerical performance be optimal. Thus, in order to make the model suitable for analyses with implicit schemes in time, whether in quasi-statics or in transient dynamics, the computation of the consistent tangent stiffness becomes indispensable in order to have a quadratic and robust convergence of the global Newton iterative process.

The essential part of the computation of the model is carried out in the frame of the eigenvectors of the generalised strain tensors (and of the generalised stresses, cf. ), the tangent stiffness therefore also being expressed in the same frame. The transformation necessary in order then to be able to use it in the assembly of the global stiffness matrix is specified in the following chapter.

In order to simplify the writing, a vector of generalised stresses (membrane force, bending moment), $\Sigma$ , and a vector of generalised strains (extension, curvature), $E$ , are defined as:

$\Sigma ={({\tilde{N}}_{1}{\tilde{N}}_{2}{\tilde{M}}_{1}{\tilde{M}}_{2})}^{T}$

$E={({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{1}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{2}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{1}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{2})}^{T}$

The tangent stiffness operator $C$ is defined by the relation in the real evolution:

$d\Sigma =C\cdot dE$

It can be computed as the sum of two contributions, the one corresponding to a non-evolution of the damage and the one due to the evolution of the damage. These contributions may be named: *elastic* contribution and *dissipative* contribution:

$$
C=\underset{{C}_{e}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\frac{d\Sigma }{dE}\mid }_{{\dot{d}}_{j}=0}}}+\underset{{C}_{d}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\frac{d\Sigma }{dE}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{j}}=0}}}
$$

Moreover, account is taken of the structure of the tensor $C$ composed of the normal-force–extension and moment–curvature contributions and of their couplings. More particularly the tensors ${C}_{e}$ and ${C}_{d}$ have the following form:

$$
{C}_{e}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{e}^{\mathrm{mm}}& 0\\ 0& {C}_{e}^{\mathrm{ff}}\end{array}\right)
$$

One sees from  that the coupling of moments/extension and of membrane forces/curvature is introduced only through the dissipative part. This coupling has a physical justification, since any cracking perpendicular to the middle surface of the plate affects both the membrane constitutive behaviour and the bending constitutive behaviour.

The sub-matrices ${C}_{e}^{\mathrm{mm}}$ , ${C}_{e}^{\mathrm{ff}}$ , ${C}_{d}^{\mathrm{mm}}$ , ${C}_{d}^{\mathrm{mf}}$ , ${C}_{d}^{\mathrm{ff}}$ are given in the principal frame by the following expressions:

$$
{({C}_{e}^{\mathrm{mm}})}_{ij}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{j}}\mid }_{\dot{{d}_{k}}=0}=\frac{2{\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{k})}{2{\mu}_{m}+{\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{k})}+2{\mu}_{m}{\xi}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{j},{d}_{k}){\delta}_{ij}
{({C}_{e}^{\mathrm{ff}})}_{ij}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{M}}_{i}}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{j}}\mid }_{\dot{{d}_{k}}=0}={\lambda}_{f}{\xi}_{f}(\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ),{d}_{k})+2{\mu}_{f}{\xi}_{f}({\tilde{\kappa}}_{j},{d}_{k}){\delta}_{ij}
$$

$$
{({C}_{d}^{\mathrm{mm}})}_{ij}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {\tilde{\epsilon}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{k}}\cdot \frac{d({d}_{k})}{d{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}
{({C}_{d}^{\mathrm{ff}})}_{ij}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{M}}_{i}}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{M}}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{k}}\cdot \frac{d({d}_{k})}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}
{({C}_{d}^{\mathrm{mf}})}_{ij}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}={\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{k}}\cdot \frac{d({d}_{k})}{\partial {\tilde{\kappa}}_{j}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}
$$

where

$$
\frac{\partial {\tilde{N}}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{k}}={\lambda}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon )+{\varepsilon}_{zz})\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})}{\partial {d}_{k}}+{\lambda}_{m}{\xi}_{m}(\mathrm{tr}(\epsilon ),{d}_{j})\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}{\partial {d}_{k}}+2{\mu}_{m}{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}({\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i},{d}_{j})}{\partial {d}_{k}}
\text{et} \quad \frac{\partial {\tilde{M}}_{i}}{\partial {d}_{k}}={\lambda}_{f}\mathrm{tr}(\kappa )\frac{\partial {\xi}_{f}(\mathrm{tr}(\kappa ),{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})}{\partial {d}_{k}}+2{\mu}_{f}{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}\frac{\partial {\xi}_{f}({\tilde{\kappa}}_{i},{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})}{\partial {d}_{k}}
\text{avec} \quad \frac{\partial {\xi}_{f}}{\partial {d}_{k}}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})=-\alpha \frac{(1-{\gamma}_{f})}{{(\alpha +{d}_{k})}^{2}}H({(-1)}^{(k+1)}x)
$$

In addition to the expressions , one resorts to the equations  in order to determine $\frac{\partial {\xi}_{m}}{\partial {d}_{k}}(x,{d}_{1,}{d}_{2})$ .

The derivatives ${\frac{d({d}_{k})}{d{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}$ and ${\frac{d({d}_{k})}{d{\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}}\mid }_{{f}_{{d}_{k}}=0}$ are computed by differentiating the equation ${R}_{{d}_{k}}=0$ with respect to ${d}_{k}$ , ${\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}$ and ${\tilde{\kappa}}_{i}$ respectively, (see ). When both damage mechanisms are activated, one is led to solve the following systems:

$$
\left(\begin{array}{cc}{A}_{11}& {A}_{12}\\ {A}_{21}& {A}_{22}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}{c}\frac{d({d}_{1})}{d{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}\\ \frac{d({d}_{2})}{d{\tilde{\epsilon}}_{i}}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{B}_{1}^{m}\\ {B}_{2}^{m}\end{array}\right)

with ${A}_{\mathrm{jk}}=\frac{\partial {Y}_{j}}{\partial {d}_{k}}$ , ${B}_{k}^{m}=\frac{-\partial {Y}_{k}}{\partial \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \tilde{N}}{\partial {d}_{k}}$ , ${B}_{k}^{f}=\frac{-\partial {Y}_{k}}{\partial \kappa }=\frac{\partial \tilde{M}}{\partial {d}_{k}}$ ;

with:

$$

and $\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}}{\partial {d}_{k}}$ given by . The matrix $({A}_{\mathrm{jk}})$ is indeed invertible, cf. section numref:r7.01.32-integration-numerique .

### 2.7 Computation of the dissipation

By definition, the dissipation power density during damage is:

$\dot{D}=\sigma :\dot{\varepsilon}-{\dot{\Phi}}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}=-\sum_{j=1,2}\frac{\partial {\Phi}_{\mathrm{ed}}^{S}}{\partial {d}_{j}}{\dot{d}}_{j}=\sum_{j=1,2}{Y}_{j}{\dot{d}}_{j}$

In this expression, the definition of ${Y}_{j}$ has been used. In the damaging phase, the threshold functions always satisfy ${f}_{{d}_{j}}={Y}_{j}-{k}_{0}\equiv 0$ . Consequently, the cumulative dissipation can be computed as:

$$
D=\int\dot{D}\mathrm{dt}={k}_{0}({d}_{1}+{d}_{2})
$$

It has been shown that the cumulative dissipation of the damage process is directly linked to the internal variables. It is sufficient to sum the two contributions and to multiply them by the threshold constant ${k}_{0}$ .

The computation of the dissipation is carried out by the options DISS_ELGA and DISS_ELNO of CALC_CHAMP. These fields have a single component named ENDO.

### 2.8 Internal variables of the model

The model requires two internal variables, ${d}_{1}$ and ${d}_{2}$ (corresponding to the variables $\mathrm{V1}$ and $\mathrm{V2}$ of *Code_Aster* ), which represent the damage on the upper-face side and on the lower-face side, respectively. The distinction between the upper and lower faces is made through the orientation of the local frame at each Gauss point. Thus, the lower face is damaged with $\dot{{d}_{2}}>0$ for positive curvatures, and the upper face with $\dot{{d}_{1}}>0$ for negative curvatures, which follows directly from the definition of ${\xi}_{f}$ , (cf. section r7.01.32-energie-libre).

In any case the choice of the orientation of the frame at a Gauss point does not affect the final result as regards the displacements and the rotations. There may be an impact on the interpretation of the damage and of the generalised stresses if the local orientations are not consistent in a structure. It is strongly recommended to ensure this consistency by supplying the keyword ANGL_REP with the nautical angles of the local frame, (see [u4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01)):

AFFE_CARA_ELEM(COQUE = _F(ANGL_REP = $(a,b)$ ))

In addition to the variables $\mathrm{V1}$ and $\mathrm{V2}$ , one also introduces $\mathrm{V3}$ and $\mathrm{V4}$ , of binary value ($0$  or $1$ ), which indicate the instantaneous evolution of $\mathrm{V1}$ and $\mathrm{V2}$ . More precisely, $\mathrm{V3}$ equals $1$ when $\mathrm{V1}$ evolves and $0$ otherwise. Likewise, $\mathrm{V4}$ equals 1 when $\mathrm{V2}$ evolves and $0$ otherwise.

One also introduces $\mathit{V5}$ , $\mathit{V6}$ and $\mathit{V7}$ , whose role is to measure the relative loss of stiffness of the reinforced-concrete slab in a rational manner, for example by visualisation at each material point:

$\mathrm{V5}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{1+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{d}_{1}}{1+{d}_{1}}+\frac{1+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{d}_{2}}{1+{d}_{2}}\right)$ $\mathrm{V6}=1-\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{{\alpha}_{c}+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}{d}_{1}}{{\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{1}}+\frac{{\alpha}_{c}+{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}{d}_{2}}{{\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{2}}\right)$ and $\mathrm{V7}=1-\mathrm{Max}\left(\frac{1+{\gamma}_{f}{d}_{1}}{1+{d}_{1}},\frac{1+{\gamma}_{f}{d}_{2}}{1+{d}_{2}}\right)$

respectively in tension, in compression and in bending. These variables will always lie between $0$ and the respective $1-{\gamma}_{.}$ , and will always be increasing, being zero in the absence of damage. These variables are more “meaningful” than the variables $\mathrm{V1}$ and $\mathrm{V2}$ .

One introduces the internal variables $\mathit{V8}$ , $\mathit{V9}$ , $\mathit{V10}$ and $\mathit{V11}$ , in order to facilitate the post-processing of the reinforcements. These variables depend on the limit strains EPSI_ELS (SLS limit) and EPSI_LIM (ULS limit), which are supplied via DEFI_MATERIAU/GLRC_DM.

- $V8$ : ACIXELS: ratio of the steel strain in the X direction (maximum between the lower and upper layers) to the strain EPSI_ELS
- $V9$ : ACIXELU: ratio of the steel strain in the X direction (maximum between the lower and upper layers) to the strain EPSI_ELU. One recalls that the model GLRC_DM does not model the yielding of the steels and is therefore not predictive in this range.
- $V10$ : ACIYELS: ratio of the steel strain in the Y direction (maximum between the lower and upper layers) to the strain EPSI_ELS
- $V11$ : ACIYELU: ratio of the steel strain in the Y direction (maximum between the lower and upper layers) to the strain EPSI_ELU. One recalls that the model GLRC_DM does not model the yielding of the steels and is therefore not predictive in this range.

For the concrete, one introduces the internal variables $\mathit{V12}$ and $\mathit{V13}$ .

- $V12$ : BETSUP: ratio of the smallest principal strain (in compression) of the concrete on the upper face to the limit compressive strain of the concrete EPSI_C.
- $V13$ : BETINF: ratio of the smallest principal strain (in compression) of the concrete on the lower face to the limit compressive strain of the concrete EPSI_C.

In order to plot the maxima of the strains, one introduces $\mathit{V14}$ , $\mathit{V15}$ and $\mathit{V16}$ :

- $V14$ : TRAMAX : maximum tensile strain over time
- $V15$ : COMMAX : maximum compressive strain over time
- $V16$ : FLEMAX: maximum bending strain over time

In order to assess the validity of the parameters, the following error analyses are proposed:

- $V17$ : ERRCOM: error expressed as a percentage between the areas of the theoretical compression curve (defined by EPSI_C and FCJ ) and the approximate curve ( NYC, GAMMA_C ) between 0 and COMMAX
- $V18$ : ERRFLE: error expressed as a percentage between the areas of the theoretical bending curve (reinforced-concrete section analysis) and the approximate curve ( MFY, GAMMA_F) between 0 and FLEMAX

### 2.9 Use of the model GLRC_DM in thermomechanical situations

It is assumed that the temperature distribution is affine through the thickness: this is admissible for a plate in a stationary thermal situation. The thermal expansion coefficient is assumed to be that of the concrete, which is clearly predominant in the section: it is therefore used directly in the membrane and bending situations of the plate.

It is also assumed that the material coefficients and parameters do not depend on the temperature in the range studied.

This affine temperature distribution through the thickness therefore results in a membrane thermal strain and a thermal curvature change, whence a simple shift of the membrane strains and of the curvatures as a function of the wall temperatures of the plate.

Thus, once this shift has been applied, there is no direct impact on the expression of the constitutive law nor on the analysis of the irreversible evolutions. Two tests verify the results obtained, cf. r7.01.32-verification .

The model GLRC_DM can therefore be used in stationary thermomechanical situations without modification, within this set of assumptions.

## 3 Parameters of the law

The damageable reinforced-concrete slab model GLRC_DM therefore needs parameters characteristic of elasticity, supplemented by 6 parameters in order to describe the damage constitutive behaviour: ${k}_{0}$ , in order to define the elastic limit, $\alpha$ in order to determine the participation of bending (see r7.01.32-contraintes-generalisees ), ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}$ , ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}$ and ${\alpha}_{c}$ , ${\gamma}_{f}$ in order to describe the non-linear response. All these parameters can be identified from monotonic pure uniaxial tension and bending tests. Some of them are replaced in the *code_aster* data set by more “meaningful” parameters, see below in paragraph r7.01.32-bilan-identification-parametres .

It is possible to proceed either from simple analytical estimates (which give the orders of magnitude) or from a calibration on a response curve supplied by another constitutive model, possibly incorporating compromises.

The procedure is described in the paragraphs below and a summary is given in paragraph r7.01.32-bilan-identification-parametres .

### 3.1 Identification of the linear-elastic constitutive parameters

In this model it is assumed that the reinforced-concrete medium is homogenised and the user is left to choose (compute or measure) the parameters: ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}$ (effective Young's modulus in membrane), ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}$ (effective Young's modulus in bending), ${\nu}_{m}$ (effective Poisson's ratio in membrane) and ${\nu}_{f}$ (effective Poisson's ratio in bending). The following relations are applied in order to determine the Lamé coefficients ${\lambda}_{m}$ , ${\mu}_{m}$ and ${\lambda}_{f}$ , ${\mu}_{f}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{\lambda}_{m}=\frac{{\nu}_{m}{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h}{(1+{\nu}_{m})(1-2{\nu}_{m})}& ,& {\mu}_{m}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h}{2(1+{\nu}_{m})}\\ {\lambda}_{f}=\frac{{\nu}_{f}{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{f}^{2})}& ,& {\mu}_{f}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{24(1+{\nu}_{f})}\end{array}
$$

The relations above are not interchangeable by *F↔M*  for the membrane and bending parameters, since in membrane the relations correspond to the general case (3D elasticity) and the plane-stress condition is treated within the formulation of the model, whereas for bending one has placed oneself from the outset in 2D elasticity with plane stress. In the elastic range one thus has:

$\begin{array}{}{N}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h}{1-{\nu}_{m}^{2}}({\nu}_{m}.\mathrm{tr}\epsilon .{\delta}_{\alpha \beta }+(1-{\nu}_{m}){\epsilon}_{\alpha \beta })\\ {M}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{f}^{2})}({\nu}_{f}.\mathrm{tr}\kappa .{\delta}_{\alpha \beta }+(1-{\nu}_{f}){\kappa}_{\alpha \beta })\end{array}$

$\alpha$ and $\beta$ being indices ranging from 1 to 2.

By default ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}=E$ and ${\nu}_{m}={\nu}_{f}=\nu$ , where $E$ and $\nu$ are the elastic coefficients supplied in the command file under the keyword ELAS. On the other hand, since reinforced concrete is not a homogeneous material, the effective value of ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}$ may differ from ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}$ . Consequently, the user is left the possibility of introducing values ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}$ and ${\nu}_{f}$ (EF and NUF under the factor keyword GLRC_DM) different from $E$ and $\nu$ , which in that case serve only to describe the membrane elasticity.

The plane-stress condition for the membrane ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ is satisfied in the manner described in paragraph r7.01.32-seuils-evolution-endommagement .

**Note:**

The identification of the elastic parameters ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}$ , ${\nu}_{m}$ , ${E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}$ and ${\nu}_{f}$ of the model from the characteristics of the concrete and of the steels rests on two loading cases: pure tension and pure bending.

Consider the following characteristics for the concrete: Young's modulus ${E}_{b}$ , Poisson's ratio ${\nu}_{b}$ , slab thickness $h$ , and for the steels: Young's modulus ${E}_{a}$ , Poisson's ratio ${\nu}_{a}$ , total cross-section per linear metre (for the two layers, assumed symmetric through the thickness and identical in both directions) ${S}_{a}$ , relative position of a layer through the thickness ${\chi }_{a}\in \left]0,1\right[$ .

One thus obtains from the uniaxial test in **pure elastic tension** :

$$
\begin{array}{}{N}_{xx}={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h{\epsilon}_{xx}={E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\epsilon}_{xx}+\frac{{E}_{b}h}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}({\epsilon}_{xx}+{\nu}_{b}{\epsilon}_{yy})\\ {N}_{yy}=0={E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\epsilon}_{yy}+\frac{{E}_{b}h}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}({\epsilon}_{yy}+{\nu}_{b}{\epsilon}_{xx})\end{array}
$$

Whence (keywords E and NU):

$$
{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}={E}_{a}\frac{{S}_{a}}{h}+{E}_{b}\cdot \frac{{E}_{b}h+{E}_{a}{S}_{a}}{{E}_{b}h+{E}_{a}{S}_{a}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}
$$

One observes that this identification produces an error on the in-plane elastic shear stiffness of the slab, a case for which the steels do not contribute (they are grids of welded bars), which makes the homogenised constitutive behaviour orthotropic and not isotropic. Indeed, one obtains with the values :

$$
{G}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}}{2(1+{\nu}_{m})}=\frac{{E}_{b}}{2(1+{\nu}_{b})}\cdot \frac{{E}_{b}^{2}{h}^{2}+2{E}_{a}{E}_{b}h{S}_{a}+{E}_{a}^{2}{S}_{a}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}{{E}_{b}^{2}{h}^{2}+{E}_{a}{E}_{b}h{S}_{a}(1-{\nu}_{b})}\ne \frac{{E}_{b}}{2(1+{\nu}_{b})}
$$

If one prefers to ensure primarily the identification on the case of **in-plane elastic shear**  of the slab, and on the case of the response in the pure-tension direction (thus accepting the error on the orthogonal Poisson effect), one obtains:

$$
{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}={E}_{b}+\frac{{E}_{a}{S}_{a}(1-{\nu}_{b})}{h}
$$

Care will be taken that this rough identification (not thermodynamically admissible with respect to the pure-tension test) does not give unrealistic values of ${\nu}_{m}$ .

Next, one obtains from the uniaxial test in **pure elastic bending** :

$$
\begin{array}{}{M}_{xx}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12}{\kappa}_{xx}=\frac{1}{4}{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{h}^{2}{\chi }_{a}^{2}{\kappa}_{xx}+\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}({\kappa}_{xx}+{\nu}_{b}{\kappa}_{yy})\\ {M}_{yy}=0=\frac{1}{4}{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{h}^{2}{\chi }_{a}^{2}{\kappa}_{yy}+\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}({\kappa}_{yy}+{\nu}_{b}{\kappa}_{xx})\end{array}
$$

Whence (keywords EF and NUF):

$$
{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}=\frac{3}{h}{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}+{E}_{b}\cdot \frac{{E}_{b}h+3{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}}{{E}_{b}h+3{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}
$$

One also observes that this identification produces an error on the anticlastic elastic bending stiffness ${M}_{xy}$ of the slab (coefficient ${G}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{24(1+{\nu}_{f})}$ instead of ${G}_{b}^{f}=\frac{{E}_{b}{h}^{3}}{24(1+{\nu}_{b})}$ ), a case for which the steels do not contribute.

### 3.2 Identification of the damageable elastic constitutive parameters

The way in which the linear-elasticity parameters are obtained having been presented in paragraph r7.01.32-parametres-elastique-lineaire , it is proposed to compute the damage parameters of the model from three tests: a pure-tension test, a pure-compression test and a monotonic uniaxial pure-bending test.

In this way one obtains the values of the threshold ${k}_{0}$ , of the three parameters relating to the membrane effects (${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}$ , ${\alpha}_{c}$ and ${\gamma}_{\mathit{mc}}$ ) independently of the two parameters relating to the bending effects ($\alpha$ , ${\gamma}_{f}$ ).

#### 3.2.1 Tension parameters (keywords NYT,GAMMA_T)

##### 3.2.1.1 Threshold of onset of damage in tension NYT

In particular, for **pure uniaxial** elastic **tension** at the onset of damage one can write the value of the threshold, cf. :

${f}_{{d}_{j}}={Y}_{j}^{m}-{k}_{0}={\epsilon}_{D}^{2}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(1-2{\nu}_{m})}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}+{\nu}_{m}^{2}\frac{1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}}{{\alpha}_{c}}))-{k}_{0}=0$

${\epsilon}_{D}$ being the elastic strain at the onset of damage, one then having ${\epsilon}_{yy}=-{\nu}_{m}{\epsilon}_{D}={\varepsilon}_{zz}$ and ${\xi}_{m}(x,0,0)=1$ , whence:

$$
{k}_{0}=\frac{{\lambda}_{m}{(1-2{\nu}_{m})}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+2{\mu}_{m}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}+{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})/{\alpha}_{c})}{4{({\lambda}_{m}(1-2{\nu}_{m})+2{\mu}_{m})}^{2}}{N}_{D}^{2}
{k}_{0}=\frac{{N}_{D}^{2}}{4{E}_{\mathrm{éq}}^{m}h(1+{\nu}_{m})}\cdot ((1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})/{\alpha}_{c})
$$

having

${N}_{D}=({\lambda}_{m}(1-2{\nu}_{m})+2{\mu}_{m}){\epsilon}_{D}={E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h{\epsilon}_{D}$

**Note**

One recalls that ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}\le 1$ (cf. ) and that* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}\le 1$ so that the damage does indeed result in a loss of stiffness. One also observes on  that one cannot have both ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}=1$ and ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}=1$ , for then ${k}_{0}=0$ (the model has no elastic domain), or else one would have to give ${N}_{D}=\infty$ (keyword NYT).

Continuing the analysis carried out in r7.01.32-parametres-elastique-lineaire , just at the onset of damage, the longitudinal stress in the concrete is:

${E}_{b}{\epsilon}_{D}\frac{1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{m}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}$

so that one can express the threshold ${N}_{D}$ (keyword NYT) with the cracking limit of the concrete ${\sigma}_{b}^{t}$ in tension, assuming the local criterion ${\sigma}_{xx}\le {\sigma}_{b}^{t}$ to be valid:

$$
{N}_{D}={\sigma}_{b}^{t}\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{m}h}{{E}_{b}}\cdot \frac{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}{1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{m}}
$$

##### 3.2.1.2 Stiffness-degradation parameter in tension GAMMA_T

The parameter ${\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}$ is equal to the ratio of the slope corresponding to the stiffness of the damaged phase, supplied by the keyword PENTE/TRACTION in DEFI_GLRC, to the slope corresponding to the elastic stiffness.

As a reminder (see [u4.42.06](doc:u4.42.06)), three methods named RIGI_ACIER, PLAS_ACIER and UTIL are available. These three slope computations make it possible to set up three different calibration methods according to the material properties supplied for tension. In the case where the elastic limit of the steels is not known, the calibration methods RIGI_ACIER, i.e. post-elastic slope equal to the slope of stiffness recovery of the steels, and UTIL, i.e. post-elastic slope intersecting the slope of stiffness recovery of the steels at a maximum strain whose value is imposed by the user, are accessible (this method is not suitable for maximum strains smaller than the trough of the reference curve, see figure r7.01.32-fig-3-2). In the case where the yield limit of the steels is known, it is possible to use the calibration method at the plasticity limit of the steels (PLAS_ACIER). The different calibration methods are illustrated by the following figures.

##### 3.2.1.3 Case of pure uniaxial distortion

Let us check the effect of these parameters on the onset of damage following a loading of elastic **pure distortion** ${\epsilon}_{xy}=\tilde{{\epsilon}_{1}}=-\tilde{{\epsilon}_{2}}$ , with ${\epsilon}_{xx}={\epsilon}_{yy}=0$ . Thus: ${N}_{xy}=\tilde{{N}_{1}}=-\tilde{{N}_{2}}=2{\mu}_{m}{\epsilon}_{xy}$ . The damage-onset threshold with the model GLRC_DM is reached for the shear force:

${N}_{xy}^{D}=2\frac{\sqrt{2{\mu}_{m}{k}_{0}}}{\sqrt{1+{\alpha}_{c}-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{\alpha}_{c}}}=\frac{{N}_{D}}{1+{\nu}_{m}}\cdot \sqrt{\frac{(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})/{\alpha}_{c}}{1+{\alpha}_{c}-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}{\alpha}_{c}}}$

It may be useful to compare this prediction with that obtained with the concrete model ENDO_ISOT_BETON [r7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04), with the cracking limit of the concrete ${\sigma}_{b}^{t}$ in tension:

${{N}_{xy}^{D}}_{\mathrm{EIB}}=2{\sigma}_{b}^{t}h\sqrt{\frac{(1-{\nu}_{b})(1+2{\nu}_{b})}{{(1+{\nu}_{b})}^{2}}}=2{N}_{D}\frac{{E}_{b}(1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{m})}{{E}_{\mathrm{éq}}^{m}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}\cdot \sqrt{\frac{(1-{\nu}_{b})(1+2{\nu}_{b})}{{(1+{\nu}_{b})}^{2}}}$

**Note**

In any combined situation (compression+shear, etc.) in pure membrane, the expression of the first-damage threshold ${Y}_{j}^{m}={k}_{0}$ of the model GLRC_DM, cf. , as a function of the membrane forces ${N}_{xx},{N}_{xy}\mathrm{...}$ , is made up of the same monomials as the “usual” plane-stress criterion of the concrete material considered not to resist beyond ${\sigma}_{b}^{t}$ . This comes from the choice of the formulation of the model GLRC_DM in membrane, in direct descent from the model ENDO_ISOT_BETON.

#### 3.2.2 Compression parameters (keywords NYC,GAMMA_C, ALPHA_C)

In compression, three parameters are to be determined: ${N}_{C}$ , ${\alpha}_{c}$ and ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}$ . Three equations are used in order to arrive at them.

The first comes to us from the formulation of the model. If one considers a **pure uniaxial compression** test, the value of the first-damage threshold, cf. , is written, denoting by ${N}_{C}$ the corresponding normal force:

${k}_{0}=\frac{{N}_{C}^{2}}{4{E}_{\mathrm{éq}}^{m}h(1+{\nu}_{m})}\cdot ((1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})\frac{1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}}{{\alpha}_{c}}+{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}))$

One must therefore necessarily have the relation:

$\frac{{N}_{C}^{2}}{{N}_{D}^{2}}=\frac{{\alpha}_{c}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})}{(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})+{\alpha}_{c}{\nu}_{m}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})}$

Thus, one obtains the expression of ${\alpha}_{c}$ as a function of ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}$ , ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}$ , and ${N}_{D}$ , ${N}_{C}$ :

$$
{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}=1-{\alpha}_{c}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})\cdot \frac{{N}_{D}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})-{N}_{C}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}}{{N}_{C}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})-{N}_{D}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}}
\iff {\alpha}_{c}=\frac{(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}})}{(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})}\cdot \frac{{N}_{C}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})-{N}_{D}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}}{{N}_{D}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})-{N}_{C}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}}
$$

**Note:**

*It is necessary that* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}\le 1$ *, just as* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}\le 1$ , *cf.*  *. One also recalls, cf.*  *, that one cannot have both* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}=1$ *and* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}=1$ *. From*  *, one obtains the necessary condition, whatever* ${\alpha}_{c}$ :

$\mid {N}_{C}\mid \le {N}_{D}\frac{\sqrt{(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m})}}{{\nu}_{m}}$

*Equality in the relation above leads to* ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}=1$ *.*

*For reinforced concrete, having* ${\nu}_{m}\approx 0,2$ *, this condition is written:* $\mid {N}_{C}\mid <5,2{N}_{D}$ *.*

The second equation comes from an additional condition that one imposes: one aims at the same maximum damage in tension and in compression. 𝑑𝑚𝑎𝑥 therefore corresponds to the greatest loss of stiffness, both in tension (denoted 𝜉𝑡_𝑚𝑖𝑛) and in compression (denoted 𝜉𝑐_𝑚𝑖𝑛).

Finally, one introduces a variable ${N}_{\mathit{CU}}$ which corresponds to the ultimate compressive strength of the section, from the height h of the shell, and from the value of the mean compressive strength ${F}_{\mathit{CJ}}$ and ${ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}$ the corresponding strain.

${N}_{\mathit{CU}}=h{F}_{\mathit{CJ}}+{E}_{a}{S}_{a}\cdot {ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}$

The compression threshold ${N}_{C}$ is then linked to the ultimate compression limit ${N}_{\mathit{CU}}$ via the equation:

$$
{N}_{C}=\frac{{N}_{\mathit{CU}}-{\gamma}_{\mathit{mc}}{\mathit{hE}}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}}{1-{\gamma}_{\mathit{mc}}}
$$

One aims at the same maximum damage in tension and in compression. ${d}_{max}$ must therefore make it possible to reach the greatest loss of stiffness, both in tension (denoted ${\xi}_{{t}_{min}}$ ) and in compression (denoted ${\xi}_{{c}_{min}}$ ) . In compression, the maximum damage makes it possible to reach the compression peak ${N}_{\mathit{CU}}$ . In tension, it makes it possible to reach the yielding point of the steels.

$$
\frac{{\alpha}_{c}+{\gamma}_{\mathit{mc}}{d}_{max}}{{\alpha}_{c}+{d}_{max}}={\xi}_{{c}_{min}}=\frac{{N}_{\mathit{CU}}}{{\mathit{hE}}_{\mathit{eq}}^{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}}
$$

$$
\frac{1+{\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}{d}_{max}}{1+{d}_{max}}={\xi}_{{t}_{min}}=\frac{{S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}}{{\mathit{hE}}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{tmax}}}=\frac{{S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}}{{N}_{D}+({S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}-{N}_{D})/{\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}}
$$

The solution of equation  gives the value of the maximum damage ${d}_{max}$ .

${d}_{max}=\frac{{S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}-{N}_{D}}{{N}_{D}\ast {\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}}$

In the case where the value obtained for ${d}_{max}$ is positive, one obtains:

${\alpha}_{c}=\frac{{d}_{max}{N}_{C}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathit{mc}})}{h{E}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}-{N}_{\mathit{CU}}}$ one must necessarily have ${N}_{\mathit{CU}}<h{E}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}$

and

$\frac{{d}_{max}{N}_{C}}{h{E}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{C1}}-{N}_{\mathit{CU}}}=\frac{({N}_{D}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}-{N}_{C}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m}))}{(1-{\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}})({N}_{C}^{2}{\nu}_{m}^{2}-{N}_{D}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{m})(1+2{\nu}_{m}))}$

This last equation is a cubic equation ${N}_{C}$

In the case where ${d}_{max}<0$ , one modifies the computation of ${\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}$ , by imposing ${\gamma}_{\mathit{mt}}=0$

and equation  is modified by

$\frac{1}{1+{d}_{max}}=\frac{{S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}}{{\mathit{hE}}_{m}{ϵ}_{\mathit{tmax}}}$

with ${ϵ}_{\mathit{tmax}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}}{{E}_{a}}$

then one has ${d}_{max}=\frac{{\mathit{hE}}_{m}}{{S}_{a}{\sigma}_{\mathit{ya}}}-1$ .

#### 3.2.3 Bending parameters (keywords MYF,GAMMA_F)

##### 3.2.3.1 Options RIGI_ACIER and PLAS_ACIER

In pure uniaxial elastic **bending** a single damage mechanism is activated, according to its sense, positive or negative. Here one chooses the positive bending, for which one always has ${f}_{{d}_{1}}>{f}_{{d}_{2}}$ . The maximum value of the elastic curvature ${\kappa}_{xx}$  at the onset of damage is denoted ${\kappa}_{D}$ (${\kappa}_{yy}=-{\nu}_{f}{\kappa}_{xx}$ ), such that only the threshold ${f}_{{d}_{1}}=0$ can be reached, whereas ${f}_{{d}_{2}}<0$ for every point of this loading path, cf. :

${f}_{{d}_{1}}={Y}_{1}^{f}-{k}_{0}={\kappa}_{D}^{2}\frac{(1-{\gamma}_{f})}{\alpha}(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}{(1-{\nu}_{f})}^{2}+{\mu}_{f})-{k}_{0}$

whence:

$$
\alpha =(1-{\gamma}_{f})\frac{{\lambda}_{f}{(1-{\nu}_{f})}^{2}+2{\mu}_{f}}{2{({\lambda}_{f}(1-{\nu}_{f})+2{\mu}_{f})}^{2}}\frac{{M}_{D}^{2}}{{k}_{0}}
$$

having

${M}_{D}=({\lambda}_{f}(1-{\nu}_{f})+2{\mu}_{f}).{\kappa}_{D}=\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{3}}{12}{\kappa}_{D}$

Since the reinforced-concrete plate is assumed symmetric with respect to the middle surface, one only needs to carry out the identification for the positive bending (the negative bending giving the same value).

Continuing the analysis carried out in paragraph r7.01.32-parametres-elastique-lineaire , just at the onset of damage, the longitudinal stress in the concrete at the wall of the plate is (one knows that the damage then progresses immediately over a good part of the thickness of the section):

${E}_{b}{\kappa}_{D}h\frac{1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{f}}{2(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}$

so that one can express the threshold ${M}_{D}$ with the cracking limit of the concrete ${\sigma}_{t}^{b}$ :

$$
{M}_{D}={\sigma}_{t}^{b}\frac{{E}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{f}{h}^{2}}{6{E}_{b}}\cdot \frac{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}{1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{f}}
$$

This value is used for the RIGI_ACIER and PLAS_ACIER parameter settings of the operand PENTE/FLEXION of DEFI_GLRC.

The parameter ${\gamma}_{f}$ is equal to the ratio of the slope corresponding to the stiffness of the damaged phase, supplied by the keyword PENTE/FLEXION in DEFI_GLRC, to the slope corresponding to the elastic stiffness. The options RIGI_ACIER and PLAS_ACIER correspond to the constitutive behaviour presented for tension in paragraph r7.01.32-parametre-raideur-traction .

In order to represent more faithfully the constitutive behaviour of reinforced concrete in bending over a more restricted range of curvature, two methods are introduced for the identification of the bending parameters.

These two methods are based on the prior identification of the theoretical curvature–bending curve of the section. This theoretical curve is determined from the theory of the plane section.

The response of the section is computed by integrating the responses over the height of the section.

The response assumptions for each material are the following:

- Non-linear response of the concrete defined by the tension limit SYT, the compression limit SYC and the parameter c which determines the value of the slope of the tensile constitutive behaviour of the concrete after cracking. One retains c=5, that is, the slope after cracking equals -0.2 ${p}_{\mathit{elas}}$ , where ${p}_{\mathit{elas}}$ is the elastic slope. This is a compromise assessed from numerical tension tests with the law ENDO_ISOT_BETON for classes of concrete commonly used in engineering. One adds the assumption that the compression limit is greater than the tension limit (in absolute value) $\mid \mathit{SYC}\mid >\mid \mathit{SYT}\mid$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.32/1000020000000121000000E60DB9E8780833C564.png)

*Constitutive behaviour of the concrete for the computation of the curvature–moment curve*

- Elastoplastic response of the steel defined by the parameter SY

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.32/100002000000011C000000E3BE7337C27FD6DF35.png)

*Constitutive behaviour of the concrete for the computation of the curvature–moment curve*

In order to determine the curvature–bending response, one starts from the assumption of a zero axial-force resultant. For a given curvature, one determines the value of the strain at the neutral axis in order to obtain a zero axial-force resultant. This determination is carried out by making assumptions on the possible states (cracking of the concrete, compression limit reached, yielding of the steels, etc.). It does not call upon a numerical integration with a precision criterion, which makes its use safer and less costly.

The moment is then determined from the values of the strain at the neutral axis and of the curvature.

**Note:**

In the case of the modelling of the shell section proposed, the bending modulus obtained is:

${E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{f}=\frac{3}{h}{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}+{E}_{b}$

It therefore differs from the modulus which takes the Poisson's ratio into account .

In order to ensure the consistency of the formulae and to obtain the same results in elastic bending , one uses a modified Young's modulus of the concrete in the computation of the section response via the integration through the height.

$\tilde{{E}_{b}}={E}_{b}\cdot \frac{{E}_{b}h+3{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}}{{E}_{b}h+3{E}_{a}{S}_{a}{\chi }_{a}^{2}(1-{\nu}_{b}^{2})}$

Two options are available for the determination of the bilinear approximation of the moment–curvature curve .

##### 3.2.3.2 Option RIGI_INIT

For the identification option RIGI_INIT, one defines the threshold bending moment corresponding to the onset of damage in the following way:

- Starting from the initiation point of the first crack ${M}_{D}$ , one searches for the smallest moment on the elastic curve for which the deviation from the theoretical curve exceeds 5%. This moment is retained for the value $\mathit{MYF}$ .
- The damage slope in pure uniaxial bending is then defined as the slope of the tangent to the theoretical moment–curve and passing through the defined threshold point.
- The parameter ${\gamma}_{f}$ is then defined as the ratio of the damaging and elastic slopes.

**Note:**

For sections with little reinforcement, one may obtain a negative damaged slope. ${\gamma}_{f}<0$ . In that case an alarm message is displayed, one imposes a slope ${\gamma}_{f}={10}^{-3}$ and the threshold moment $\mathit{MYF}$ is reduced in order to satisfy the condition of tangency to the theoretical curve.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.32/10000200000001470000010A32CBFFD8AFBDE20C.png)

*Bilinear approximation in bending with the option RIGI_INIT*

For the implementation of this option, the following steps are carried out:

- For each curvature, in an interval of curvatures $\left[{\kappa}_{D},{\kappa}_{L}\right]$ , one determines the theoretical moment $M$ and the tangent $\mathit{dM}/d\kappa$ to the curve ($d\kappa =({\kappa}_{L}-{\kappa}_{D})/1000$ ). This tangent is obtained by finite difference.
- Among the computed values, one selects the first curvature for which the deviation from the curve is greater than 5%.
- In order to obtain the damage slope, one selects the limit curvature ${\kappa}_{R}$ for which one minimises the difference between $\mathit{dM}/d\kappa$ and the damage slope.

##### 3.2.3.3 Option UTIL

For the identification option UTIL, the user defines a target curvature value via the keyword KAPPA_FLEX. One then determines the value of the threshold curvature and moment ${\kappa}_{s}$ and $\mathit{MYF}$ so as to minimise the area between the theoretical curve and the bilinear curve.

**Note**

For sections with little reinforcement, one may obtain a negative damage slope. ${\gamma}_{f}<0$ . In that case an alarm message is displayed, one imposes a slope ${\gamma}_{f}={10}^{-3}$ and the threshold moment $\mathit{MYF}$ is defined at the cracking limit of the concrete, i.e. ${M}_{D}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.32/100002000000015A0000010D56655B1B49756BCA.png)

*Bilinear approximation in bending with the option UTIL*

For the implementation of this option, the following steps are carried out:

- For each curvature, in an interval of curvatures $\left[{\kappa}_{D},{\kappa}_{\mathit{flex}}\right]$ (1000 points), one determines the theoretical moment $M$ and one computes the area under the theoretical curve by the rectangle method, as well as the area under the bilinear curve.
- One evaluates the area under the curve of the bilinear approximations which guarantee the elastic slope computed beforehand and which intersect the theoretical curve for $\kappa ={\kappa}_{\mathit{flex}}$ : the threshold point is moved along the elastic slope and the damaged slope is adjusted in order to pass through the theoretical curve.
- One retains the bilinear approximation which minimises the difference between the area under its curve and that of the theoretical curve.

#### 3.2.4 Uniaxial tension–bending cases

Let us check the effect of these parameters on the onset of damage following a loading combining concomitant monotonic uniaxial **tension** and monotonic uniaxial **bending**. The thresholds are written, for each damage variable:

$\begin{array}{}{k}_{0}\ge {Y}_{1}=\\ {\epsilon}_{xx}^{2}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(1-2{\nu}_{m})}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}+{\nu}_{m}^{2}\frac{1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}}{{\alpha}_{c}}))+{\kappa}_{xx}^{2}{\nu}_{f}^{2}{\mu}_{f}\frac{(1-{\gamma}_{f})}{\alpha}\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{}{k}_{0}\ge {Y}_{2}=\\ {\epsilon}_{xx}^{2}(\frac{{\lambda}_{m}}{4}{(1-2{\nu}_{m})}^{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}})+\frac{{\mu}_{m}}{2}(1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mt}}+{\nu}_{m}^{2}\frac{1-{\gamma}_{\mathrm{mc}}}{{\alpha}_{c}}))+{\kappa}_{xx}^{2}\frac{(1-{\gamma}_{f})}{\alpha}(\frac{{\lambda}_{f}}{2}{(1-{\nu}_{f})}^{2}+{\mu}_{f})\end{array}$

One readily checks that ${Y}_{1}<{Y}_{2}$ : the first damage appears on the variable ${d}_{2}$ as expected. Using the results  and , the threshold ${Y}_{2}={k}_{0}$ is therefore written:

$$
\frac{{N}_{xx}^{2}}{{N}_{D}^{2}}+\frac{{M}_{xx}^{2}}{{M}_{D}^{2}}=1
$$

It thus defines the elastic domain predicted by the model GLRC_DM in the positive quadrant $({N}_{xx},{M}_{xx})$ in an elliptical form.

Continuing the analysis carried out in paragraph r7.01.32-parametres-elastique-lineaire , just at the onset of damage, the longitudinal stress in the concrete is:

$\frac{{E}_{b}}{1-{\nu}_{b}^{2}}({\epsilon}_{xx}(1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{m})+{\kappa}_{xx}\frac{h}{2}(1-{\nu}_{b}{\nu}_{f}))$

Comparing this result with the cracking limit of the concrete ${\sigma}_{t}^{b}$ , one finds that one obtains an elastic domain in the positive quadrant $({N}_{xx},{M}_{xx})$ of polygonal form:

$\frac{{N}_{xx}}{{N}_{D}}+\frac{{M}_{xx}}{{M}_{D}}=1$

One knows that this incipient damage is followed immediately, in a 3D model of the reinforced-concrete plate, by the appearance of a damaged zone over a good part of the thickness.

This difference in prediction with the model GLRC_DM is inherent in the choice made in  of an elasto-damageable plate energy in membrane–bending, combined with the threshold .

As this difference can vary between 0% and 30%, it is suggested to lower the numerical values of ${N}_{D}$ and ${M}_{D}$ defined previously by 10%.

## 7 Appendix: Eigenvalues of the strain tensor

One considers an orthonormal basis ${({e}_{i})}_{i=1,2,3}$ of the three-dimensional Euclidean space, and a tensor $\varepsilon$ of order 2, symmetric, hence diagonalisable. One denotes by ${\varepsilon}_{j}^{i}$ the mixed components of the tensor $\varepsilon ={\varepsilon}_{j}^{i}.{e}_{i}\otimes {e}^{\text{*}j}$ in the basis ${({e}_{i})}_{i=1,2,3}$ . One uses the Einstein convention on the repeated mixed indices.

One denotes: ${Q}_{k},{\eta}_{k}$ the triplet of normalised eigenvectors and associated eigenvalues of the problem:

$$
\varepsilon .{Q}_{k}={\eta}_{k}{Q}_{k}\iff {\varepsilon}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{e}_{i}={\eta}_{k}{({Q}_{k})}^{i}{e}_{i}\text{pour}k=1,2,3
$$

**Note**

One notes that: $(\varepsilon +\xi \text{Id}).{Q}_{k}=({\lambda}_{k}+\xi ).{Q}_{k},\forall \xi \in ℝ$ , hence adding to $\varepsilon$ any diagonal tensor does not modify the principal directions of $\varepsilon$ .

One knows that the eigenvectors ${({Q}_{k})}^{j}{e}_{j}$ form an orthonormal basis (principal frame):

$$
{({Q}_{k})}_{i}{e}^{\text{*}i}.{({Q}_{l})}^{j}{e}_{j}={\delta}_{kl}.{\delta}^{{i}_{j}}\Rightarrow {({Q}_{k})}_{j}{({Q}_{\ell})}^{j}={\delta}_{kl}
$$

Let us differentiate these two relations:

$$
{(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}+{\varepsilon}_{j}^{i}.{(d{Q}_{k})}^{j}=d{\eta}_{k}{({Q}_{k})}^{i}+{\eta}_{k}{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}\text{pour}k=1,2,3
$$

$$
{(d{Q}_{k})}_{j}.{({Q}_{l})}^{j}+{({Q}_{k})}_{j}.{(d{Q}_{l})}^{j}=0
$$

Let us project equation  onto the eigenvector ${({Q}_{l})}^{i}{e}_{i}$ and use equation :

$$
\begin{array}{}{(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}+{\varepsilon}_{j}^{i}.{(d{Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}=d{\eta}_{k}{({Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}+{\eta}_{k}{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}\text{pour}k,l=1,2,3\\ \iff {(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}+{\eta}_{l}.{(d{Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{j}=d{\eta}_{k}.{\delta}_{kl}+{\eta}_{k}{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}\text{pour}k,l=1,2,3\\ \iff {(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}=d{\eta}_{k}.{\delta}_{kl}+({\eta}_{k}-{\eta}_{l}).{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}\text{pour}k,l=1,2,3\end{array}
$$

Whence:

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{}d{\eta}_{k}={(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{k})}_{i}\text{pour}k=1,2,3\\ ({\eta}_{k}-{\eta}_{l}).{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}={(d\varepsilon )}_{j}^{i}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}\text{pour}k\ne l=1,2,3\end{array} \right.
$$

Let us denote by ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{j}^{i}$ the mixed components of a tensor in the basis ${({Q}_{k})}_{k=1,2,3}$ . Then:

$$
\left\{  \begin{array}{}d{\eta}_{k}={(d\tilde{\varepsilon})}_{k}^{k}\text{pour}k=1,2,3(\text{pas de sommation sur}k)\\ ({\eta}_{k}-{\eta}_{l}).{(d{Q}_{k})}^{i}{({Q}_{l})}_{i}={(d\tilde{\varepsilon})}_{l}^{k}\text{pour}k\ne l=1,2,3\end{array} \right.
$$

One obviously checks on the trace

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.32/Object_306.svg)

of the strain tensor (which is independent of the chosen frame):

$$
\sum_{k=1,2,3}d{\eta}_{k}=\mathrm{tr}(d\tilde{\varepsilon})=\mathrm{tr}(d\varepsilon )=d(\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )
$$

Consider the free-energy density of isotropic elasticity:

$$
\phi (\varepsilon )=\frac{1}{2}\lambda {(\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )}^{2}+\mu \sum_{k=1,2,3}{({\eta}_{k})}^{2}
$$

then the state law gives the stress tensor:

$$
\sigma ={\phi }_{,\epsilon }(\varepsilon )=\lambda (\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )\frac{d\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon }{d\varepsilon }+\mu \sum_{k=1,2,3}{\eta}_{k}\frac{d{\eta}_{k}}{d\varepsilon }=\lambda (\mathrm{tr}\varepsilon )\mathrm{Id}+2\mu \sum_{k=1,2,3}{\eta}_{k}.{({Q}_{k})}^{j}{({Q}_{k})}_{i}.{e}_{j}\otimes {e}^{\text{*}i}
$$

Applying the remark made above, the principal frame of the stress tensor $\sigma$ is therefore identical to that of the strains $\varepsilon$ .

The principal stresses are therefore naturally in the principal frame ${({Q}_{k})}^{j}{e}_{j}$ :

$$
{s}_{k}={\tilde{\sigma}}_{k}^{k}=\lambda \text{tr}(\varepsilon )+2\mu {\eta}_{k}
$$
