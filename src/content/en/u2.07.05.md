# u2.07.05 Setting up an analysis by dynamic substructuring

**Summary:**

The purpose of this document is to help users and to give them advice for carrying out a study involving the dynamic substructuring technique.

One first presents the general approach for setting up the technique.

One then describes the sequences of operations required in order to obtain usable results. The presentation is illustrated by three examples treating a modal analysis, a transient-response analysis and a harmonic-response analysis.

## 1 What does dynamic substructuring consist of?

Dynamic substructuring consists in partitioning the structure into several substructures in order to reduce the size of the numerical model and to be able to study each substructure independently. The overall model is then obtained by assembling the models of the different substructures.

Each substructure is projected onto a judiciously chosen basis of vectors. This projection makes it possible to reduce the size of the model of each substructure and consequently the size of the model of the assembled structure.

## 2 Why use dynamic substructuring?

The growing complexity of mechanical structures makes it difficult to estimate their dynamic behaviour under an imposed loading. The numerical modelling of the complete structure would require a significant investment, both in terms of memory space and of analysis cost.

In addition to the reduction in the size of the model, the dynamic substructuring technique makes the modelling of the global structure much more modular.

## 3 A short reminder of the methodology

One recalls here only the fundamental principles of the technique. For more detail, the reader is invited to consult the reference document [R4.06.02](doc:r4.06.02).

Projecting the substructure onto a reduced basis necessarily introduces an approximation into the results obtained. The role of the modeller is to choose a basis rich enough to be able to capture the dynamic behaviour of the substructure. The quality of the results depends on the choice of this projection basis. The techniques most used for the choice of this basis are the Craig-Bampton technique and the Mac Neal technique. To these two techniques is added the use of interface modes, which is a variant of these two techniques in which one carries out a condensation of the connection degrees of freedom.

- The Craig-Bampton technique consists in choosing, as projection basis, normal modes and constraint modes. The normal modes are eigenmodes of the substructure in which one fixes all the nodes of the interfaces with the adjacent substructures. A constraint mode is defined by the static deflected shape obtained by imposing a unit displacement on one connection degree of freedom, the other connection degrees of freedom being fixed. The projection basis contains normal modes and all the constraint modes associated with all the connection degrees of freedom.
- The Mac Neal technique consists in choosing, as projection basis, normal modes and attachment modes. In this case, the normal modes are eigenmodes of the substructure with a free interface. An attachment mode is defined by the static deflected shape obtained by imposing a unit force on one connection degree of freedom, the other connection degrees of freedom being free. The projection basis contains normal modes and all the attachment modes associated with all the connection degrees of freedom.
- Use of interface modes: one can observe that the dimension of the projection basis can become very large if the number of connection degrees of freedom is large. One then defines the notion of interface mode, which consists in carrying out a condensation of the behaviour of the structure onto the interface degrees of freedom and in solving the associated eigenvalue problem. This makes it possible to express the behaviour of the structure reduced to the interface. One limits oneself to keeping, as basis vectors, the first modes computed, in addition to the normal modes. Indeed, if one remains within the framework of low-frequency dynamics, the whole set of constraint modes (or attachment modes) is not necessary to represent the dynamics of the problem. Nevertheless, it is recommended to check a posteriori the relevance of the basis chosen, by verifying for example the continuity of the displacements at the interfaces.

One can list below the different steps of the approach:

- For each substructure:

  - Computation of the projection basis: normal modes and connection modes or interface modes
  - Computation of the generalised matrices of the substructure: projection of the mass and stiffness matrices of the substructure onto the reduced basis
- Assembly of the substructures by the formulation of the continuity of the displacement at the interfaces and the vanishing of the work of the connection forces. One thus obtains a generalised model of the assembled structure
- Computation of the response in generalised coordinates
- Recovery of the results onto the physical basis
- checking of the results obtained
- post-processing (stress analysis, energy analysis, ...)

## 4 Setting up in Code_Aster

### 4.1 Notion of dynamic macro-element

A dynamic macro-element is a reduced (generalised) model of a substructure. It comprises:

- the projection of the model (mass and stiffness matrices) of the substructure onto the reduced basis,
- the information on the interfaces where the adjacent substructures will be connected and the connection matrices associated with these interfaces.

Normally, one builds as many dynamic macro-elements as there are substructures. In the case where one is in the presence of substructures which differ only by their position in space, a single macro-element is sufficient. One then applies a rotation and/or a translation of the macro-element at assembly.

### 4.2 The course of a study

- For each substructure: creation of the dynamic macro-element

  - Computation and assembly of the mass and stiffness matrices and of the loading of the substructure: (CALC_MATR_ELEM, ASSE_MATRICE, CALC_VECT_ELEM, ASSE_VECTEUR)
  - Computation of the projection-basis vectors: normal modes, static modes, interface modes (CALC_MODES, MODE_STATIQUE, DEFI_BASE_MODALE)
  - Definition of the interfaces (DEFI_INTERF_DYNA)
  - Projection of the matrices of the substructure onto the basis vectors (MACR_ELEM_DYNA)

The chaining of these operations can be carried out with a single call to the operator CREA_ELEM_SSD. This operator makes it possible to create a dynamic macro-element by supplying only the information on the model and the interfaces. It computes the projection-basis vectors (normal modes and connection modes), defines the interfaces and projects the stiffness and mass matrices onto the computed basis. It chains the following operators: CALC_MATR_ELEM, ASSE_MATRICE, CALC_MODES, MODE_STATIQUE, DEFI_INTERF_DYNA, DEFI_BASE_MODALE, MACR_ELEM_DYNA.

- Assembly of the different substructures

  - Definition of the generalised model of the assembled structure: connection between substructures and numbering of the degrees of freedom of the system (DEFI_MODELE_GENE, NUME_DDL_GENE)
  - Assembly of the generalised matrices of the substructures (ASSE_MATR_GENE, ASSE_VECT_GENE)

These operations of assembly of dynamic macro-elements can be carried out by using the operator ASSE_ELEM_SSD. It defines the generalised model and assembles the generalised matrices of the different substructures. It chains the following operators: DEFI_MODELE_GENE, NUME_DDL_GENE, ASSE_MATR_GENE.

- Computation of the response on the generalised model (CALC_MODES, DYNA_VIBRA)
- Recovery of the results in physical coordinates of the substructure or on a skeleton mesh (REST_SOUS_STRUCT)
- A posteriori checking of the results by verifying the continuity of the displacement field at the interfaces (POST_RELEVE_T, TEST_TABLE) and the vanishing of the work of the connection forces (CALC_CORR_SSD)

### 4.3 Practical advice

- For the standard cases: use the operator CREA_ELEM_SSD for the creation of the dynamic macro-element and the operator ASSE_ELEM_SSD for the assembly of the macro-elements. This makes the writing of the command file significantly easier.
- For the non-standard cases (particular projection basis), it is sometimes necessary to go back to the elementary operators. The current limitations of CREA_ELEM_SSD and ASSE_ELEM_SSD are:

  - conservative structure (without damping)
  - projection basis: normal modes + connection modes (Craig-Bampton, Mac Neal, the first n interface modes)
  - All the components of the degrees of freedom of the interface nodes are considered for the computation of the connection modes (the keyword MASQUE, which makes it possible to select the active components of the interface nodes, is not active for CREA_ELEM_SSD)
  - The normal modes are computed by using the operator CALC_MODES; the options on the dimension of the mode-search subspace are those taken by default by this operator.
- If one is in the presence of a point interface or of an interface with a small number of degrees of freedom, the use of the classical methods (Craig-Bampton or Mac Neal) is recommended.
- The interface modes are appropriate for distributed (non-point) interfaces. The quality of the results depends on the number of interface modes taken into account in the projection basis. An a posteriori check of the results is always indicated. This check can be done by verifying the continuity of the displacement field at the interfaces and/or the vanishing of the work of the connection forces. One must increase the number of interface modes if one observes a separation at the interfaces or if the work of the connection forces is not negligible.
- Choose carefully the normal modes which serve as projection-basis vectors. The subspace generated by these basis vectors must contain at least the deflected shape of the substructure after assembly. One cannot, for example, observe the torsion of the substructure if one does not put a torsion shape among the basis vectors.
- For an interface of Mac Neal type, one must make sure that the substructure is held somewhere in order to avoid rigid-body motion.

## 5 Some examples of analysis

One presents three cases to illustrate the setting up of the technique.

The first case corresponds to a standard modal analysis with use of the operators CREA_ELEM_SSD and ASSE_ELEM_SSD.

The second case corresponds to a transient-response analysis. One carries out a double projection. The first projection is executed at the creation of the dynamic macro-element and the second projection is carried out on the generalised model of the assembled structure for the response analysis.

The third case corresponds to a harmonic-response analysis on a dissipative structure. One describes the chaining of the elementary operators required for the creation of the dynamic macro-elements and the assembly of the generalised matrices of the substructures.

### 5.1 An example of a modal analysis

It is modelling e of the test case SDLS106 [V2.03.106](doc:v2.03.106).

In this test case, one wishes to compute the eigenmodes of a structure composed of two substructures defined in two different frames. The association of these two substructures is intended to produce a plate in a condition of simple support on its edges.

One can sketch it as follows:

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape1.gif)

The assembled structure is a rectangular plate supported on its edges.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape3.gif)

To arrive at this assembled structure, one carries out a rotation and a translation of substructure 2. Indeed, the two substructures must be expressed in the same frame.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape4.gif)

Rotation of -90 degrees about DZ    +    Translation along DY

One is going to present the steps for the creation of the dynamic macro-element of substructure 1; the dynamic macro-element of substructure 2 is created in an analogous way.

Definition of the boundary conditions of substructure 1:

One wishes to use a basis of Craig-Bampton type, so one must clamp the degrees of freedom of the interface, which are here associated with the node group GAUCHE. The other conditions correspond to the boundary conditions of the substructure.

CHARGE_1=AFFE_CHAR_MECA( MODELE=MODELE1,DDL_IMPO=(

_F( GROUP_NO = 'BLOQUE', DX = 0.0, DY = 0.0, DZ = 0.0),

**_F( GROUP_NO = 'GAUCHE', DX = 0.0, DY = 0.0, DZ = 0.0,**

**DRX = 0.0, DRY = 0.0, DRZ = 0.0)** ,

_F( GROUP_NO = 'BAS', DX = 0.0, DY = 0.0, DZ = 0.0),

_F( GROUP_NO = 'HAUT', DX = 0.0, DY = 0.0, DZ = 0.0)))

- Creation of the dynamic macro-element:

One uses the operator CREA_ELEM_SSD for the creation of the dynamic macro-element,

One has here a distributed interface; one wishes to use **interface modes** as connection modes. Under the factor keyword BASE_MODALE, one chooses a basis of type RITZ and one enters the number of interface modes to be used.

The factor keyword CALC_FREQ gives the numbers of normal modes to be used.

MACEL1 = CREA_ELEM_SSD(

MODELE = MODELE1,

CHARGE = CHARGE_1,

CHAM_MATER = CHAMAT1,

CARA_ELEM = PARAM1,

INTERFACE = _F( NOM = 'GAUCHE',

TYPE = 'CRAIGB',

GROUP_NO = 'GAUCHE'),

BASE_MODALE = _F( **TYPE = 'RITZ'** ,

**NMAX_MODE_INTF = 20,** ),

CALC_FREQ = _F(NMAX_FREQ = 6,),

)

One creates in the same way the dynamic macro-element of substructure 2.

- Assembly of the different substructures:

One carries out the assembly of the substructures by using the operator ASSE_ELEM_SSD

The factor keyword RESU_ASSE_SSD makes it possible to assign a name to the result concepts (model, numbering of the generalised degrees of freedom, generalised matrices).

The factor keyword SOUS-STRUCT makes it possible to name the different substructures. It is in this factor keyword that one assigns the possible rotation and translation of the macro-element (one first carries out the rotation ANGL_NAUT and then the translation TRANS).

The keyword LIAISON makes it possible to define the interfaces between substructures. In the case where one uses interface modes as connection modes, one must specify REDUIT under the keyword OPTION.

The mesh-cell group GROUP_MA_MAIT_* drives the displacement field at the interface. If the interfaces are not compatible (the nodes are not facing one another), it is advisable to choose as master mesh cell the finest mesh cell. Otherwise, by default, the mesh cells of substructure 1 are defined as master.

The choice ELIMINE under the keyword METHODE means that one numbers the generalised degrees of freedom by taking the connection equations into account by the constraint-elimination method.

ASSE_ELEM_SSD(

RESU_ASSE_SSD = _F(

MODELE = CO('MODEGE'),

NUME_DDL_GENE = CO('NUMEGE'),

RIGI_GENE = CO('RIGGEN'),

MASS_GENE = CO('MASGEN'),

),

SOUS_STRUC = (

_F(NOM = 'CARRE1',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACEL1,),

_F(NOM = 'CARRE2',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACEL2,

**ANGL_NAUT = (-90.,0.,0.),),**

TRANS = (0.,0.5,0.),

),

LIAISON = (

_F(SOUS_STRUC_1 = 'CARRE1',

INTERFACE_1 = 'GAUCHE',

GROUP_MA_MAIT_1 = 'CALCUL',

**OPTION = 'REDUIT',**

SOUS_STRUC_2 = 'CARRE2',

INTERFACE_2 = 'BLOQUE',),

),

VERIF = _F( STOP_ERREUR = 'OUI',

PRECISION = 1.E-6,

CRITERE = 'RELATIF'),

METHODE = 'ELIMINE',

),

- Computation of the eigenmodes of the assembled structure:

One carries out the modal analysis on the assembled generalised matrices. The modal shapes obtained are expressed in generalised coordinates.

RESGEN=CALC_MODES( MATR_RIGI=RIGGEN,

MATR_MASS=MASGEN,

OPTION = 'PLUS_PETITE',

CALC_FREQ=_F( NMAX_FREQ=10,),

SOLVEUR_MODAL=_F( METHODE='SORENSEN',),

VERI_MODE=_F(PREC_SHIFT=5.0E-3,

STOP_ERREUR='NON',

STURM='NON',

SEUIL=9.9999999999999995E-01),

STOP_BANDE_VIDE='NON',

)

- Recovery onto the physical basis:

For the exploitation of the results, it is necessary to recover them onto the physical basis.

MODCOT1=REST_SOUS_STRUC(RESU_GENE=RESGEN,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

SOUS_STRUC='CARRE1',);

MODCOT2=REST_SOUS_STRUC(RESU_GENE=RESGEN,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

SOUS_STRUC='CARRE2',);

- Checking of the quality of the results:

The quality of the results obtained can be checked by verifying the continuity of the displacements at the interface. This makes it possible to validate the number of interface modes chosen.

In this test case, one compares the displacement of node N9 of substructure 1 with that of node N70 of substructure 2. These two nodes are facing one another.

T1_DZ=POST_RELEVE_T(ACTION=_F(INTITULE='CARRE1',

NOEUD=('N9'),

RESULTAT=MODCOT1,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

NOM_CMP='DZ',

OPERATION='EXTRACTION',

),);

T2_DZ=POST_RELEVE_T(ACTION=_F(INTITULE='CARRE2',

NOEUD=('N70'),

RESULTAT=MODCOT2,

NOM_CHAM='DEPL',

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

NOM_CMP='DZ',

OPERATION='EXTRACTION',

),);

F_DZ=FORMULE(NOM_PARA=('DZ_C1', 'DZ_C2',),

VALE='abs (DZ_C1 - DZ_C2)')

TC_DZ=CALC_TABLE(TABLE=T1_DZ,

ACTION=(_F(OPERATION='RENOMME',

NOM_PARA=('DZ', 'DZ_C1'),),

_F(OPERATION='COMB',

TABLE=T2_DZ,

NOM_PARA=('NUME_ORDRE',),),

_F(OPERATION='RENOMME',

NOM_PARA=('DZ', 'DZ_C2'),),

_F(OPERATION='OPER',

NOM_PARA='ECART',

FORMULE=F_DZ),

),

TITRE='Comparaison DZ ssd 1 / ssd 2')

TEST_TABLE(

TABLE = TC_DZ,

NOM_PARA = 'ECART',

TYPE_TEST = 'SOMM',

VALE = 0.,

CRITERE = 'ABSOLU',

PRECISION = 3.E-3,

REFERENCE = 'ANALYTIQUE',)

One computes here the sum of the absolute values of the differences. The keyword PRECISION makes it possible to define the maximum difference tolerated.

One can also verify the vanishing of the work of the connection forces by using the operator CALC_CORR_SSD. This run is quite a heavy consumer of CPU time.

CALC_ENR=CALC_CORR_SSD(MODELE_GENE=MODEGE,

RESU_GENE=RESGEN,

SHIFT=1.,

UNITE=6)

A summary of the results obtained is deposited, by default, in the message file. One can enrich the basis if one observes that the work of the connection forces is not negligible. One can take as a model modelling a of the test case SDLS122 [V2.03.122](doc:v2.03.122) for the setting up of this enrichment of the projection basis.

### 5.2 An example of a transient-response analysis

It is modelling e of the test case SDLD102 [V2.01.102](doc:v2.01.102).

The substructures are composed of discrete elements. The first substructure is loaded by a point force.

One uses the same model and the same mesh for the definition of the two substructures.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape5.gif)

The complete structure is obtained by assembling the two substructures.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape6.gif)

Definition of the boundary conditions of the substructure:

One wishes to use a basis of **classical Craig-Bampton** type; the node at the interface must be fixed (node $\mathit{N3}$ for substructure 1 and node $\mathit{N1}$ for substructure 2).

CHARGE_L=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=MODELE,

DDL_IMPO=(

_F(NOEUD='N2',

DY=0.,

DZ=0.,

DRX=0.,

DRY=0.,

DRZ=0.,),

**_F(NOEUD='N1',**

**DX=0.,),**

**_F(NOEUD='N3',**

**DX=0.,)** ,),);

- Creation of the dynamic macro-elements:

One applies the loading $\text{FX}$ on this substructure 1; one must then enter a numbering for the degrees of freedom of the substructure. This numbering will be used subsequently when defining the loading on the assembled model.

MACEL1 = CREA_ELEM_SSD(

MODELE=MODELE,

CARA_ELEM=PARAM,

CHARGE=CHARGE_L,

CHAM_MATER = CHAMAT,

**NUME_DDL=CO('NUMDDL_L')** ,

INTERFACE=_F(NOM='DROITE',

**TYPE='CRAIGB'** ,

NOEUD='N3',),

BASE_MODALE = _F( **TYPE = 'CLASSIQUE'** ,),

CALC_FREQ = _F(NMAX_FREQ = 1,),

)

Creation of the dynamic macro-element of substructure 2:

MACEL2 = CREA_ELEM_SSD(

MODELE=MODELE,

CARA_ELEM=PARAM,

CHARGE=CHARGE_L,

CHAM_MATER = CHAMAT,

INTERFACE=_F(NOM='GAUCHE',

**TYPE='CRAIGB'** ,

NOEUD='N1',),

BASE_MODALE = _F( **TYPE = 'CLASSIQUE'** ,),

CALC_FREQ = _F(NMAX_FREQ = 1,),

)

- Assembly of the different substructures:

ASSE_ELEM_SSD(

RESU_ASSE_SSD = _F(

MODELE = CO('MODEGE'),

NUME_DDL_GENE = CO('NUMEGE'),

RIGI_GENE = CO('RIGGEN'),

MASS_GENE = CO('MASGEN'),

),

SOUS_STRUC=(_F(NOM='COTE1',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA=MACEL1,),

_F(NOM='COTE2',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA=MACEL2,

TRANS=(0.2,0.,0.),),),

LIAISON=_F(SOUS_STRUC_1='COTE1',

INTERFACE_1='DROITE',

SOUS_STRUC_2='COTE2',

INTERFACE_2='GAUCHE',),

VERIF=_F(STOP_ERREUR='OUI',

PRECISION=1.E-6,

CRITERE='RELATIF',),

);

- Computation of the transient response of the assembled structure:

Definition and assembly of the loading vector:

CHARTRAN=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(MODELE=MODELE,

FORCE_NODALE=_F(NOEUD='N2',

FX=1.,),);

V_ELEM=CALC_VECT_ELEM(OPTION='CHAR_MECA',

CHARGE=CHARTRAN,);

The loading must be expressed in the same numbering as the projection vectors (numbering of the degrees of freedom of substructure 1).

V_ASSE=ASSE_VECTEUR(VECT_ELEM=V_ELEM,

**NUME_DDL=NUMDDL_L** ,);

Assembly of the loading vector on the generalised model (loading applied on the substructure COTE1):

FGEN=ASSE_VECT_GENE(NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

CHAR_SOUS_STRUC=_F( **SOUS_STRUC='COTE1'** ,

VECT_ASSE=V_ASSE,),);

Definition of the projection basis for the transient-response analysis (in the majority of cases, the basis is composed of eigenmodes of the system):

RESGEN=CALC_MODES(MATR_RIGI=RIGGEN,

MATR_MASS=MASGEN,

OPTION='PLUS_PETITE',

CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=3,),

TITRE='CALCUL DES MODES PROPRES DE LA STRUCTURE GLOBALE',

);

Numbering of the degrees of freedom of the analysis basis:

NUMEMODE=NUME_DDL_GENE(BASE=RESGEN,

STOCKAGE='DIAG',);

Projection of the assembled matrices onto the analysis basis:

MASPRO=PROJ_MATR_BASE(BASE=RESGEN,

NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEMODE,

MATR_ASSE_GENE=MASGEN,);

RIGPRO=PROJ_MATR_BASE(BASE=RESGEN,

NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEMODE,

MATR_ASSE_GENE=RIGGEN,);

Projection of the loading onto the analysis basis:

VECPRO=PROJ_VECT_BASE(BASE=RESGEN,

NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEMODE,

VECT_ASSE_GENE=FGEN,);

By default, the displacement and the velocity are zero at the initial time instant. One shows below how to enter the initial condition of the system.

One creates the field on the mesh of the substructure with the same numbering as that of the macro-element.

CHAMP_0 = CREA_CHAMP( OPERATION='AFFE', TYPE_CHAM='NOEU_DEPL_R',

MAILLAGE=MAILLA,

**NUME_DDL=NUMDDL_L** ,

AFFE=_F( TOUT = 'OUI',

NOM_CMP = ( 'DX', 'DY','DZ','DRX','DRY','DRZ', ),

VALE = (0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.,))

)

Projection of the field onto the assembled generalised model:

VGEN_0=ASSE_VECT_GENE(NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

**CHAR_SOUS_STRUC=_F(SOUS_STRUC='COTE1',**

**VECT_ASSE=CHAMP_0,)** ,);

Second projection of the field onto the response-analysis basis:

DEPINI=PROJ_VECT_BASE( **BASE=RESGEN** , VECT_ASSE_GENE= **VGEN_0** ,

NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEMODE,

TYPE_VECT='DEPL')

VITINI=PROJ_VECT_BASE( **BASE=RESGEN** , VECT_ASSE_GENE= **VGEN_0** ,

NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEMODE,

TYPE_VECT='VITE')

The transient-response analysis can be carried out by using the command DYNA_VIBRA. One uses the solution technique by projection onto an analysis basis.

Computation of the transient response on the projected assembled model:

TRANGEN1=DYNA_VIBRA( **TYPE_CALCUL='TRAN'** , **BASE_CALCUL='GENE'** ,

SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='EULER',),

MATR_MASS=MASPRO,

MATR_RIGI=RIGPRO,

INCREMENT=_F(INST_INIT=0.,

INST_FIN=82.,

PAS=0.01,),

ETAT_INIT=_F( DEPL = DEPINI,

VITE = VITINI,),

ARCHIVAGE=_F(PAS_ARCH=10,),

EXCIT=_F(VECT_ASSE=VECPRO,

COEF_MULT=1.,),);

- Recovery onto the physical basis:

One can recover the results directly on the substructure or on a visualisation mesh called a skeleton mesh, which must be defined beforehand.

Creation of the visualisation mesh:

SQUEL=DEFI_SQUELETTE(MODELE_GENE=MODEGE,

SOUS_STRUC=(_F(NOM='COTE1',

GROUP_MA='RESSORT'

),

_F(NOM='COTE2',

GROUP_MA='RESSORT'

),),);

Recovery of the analysis basis onto the visualisation mesh:

MODGLO=REST_SOUS_STRUC(RESU_GENE=RESGEN,

TOUT_ORDRE='OUI',

SQUELETTE=SQUEL,);

Recovery of the response onto the visualisation mesh (for a solution with double projection, do not forget to specify the recovery of the analysis basis onto the substructure or onto the skeleton):

TRAN1=REST_GENE_PHYS(RESU_GENE=TRANGEN1,

**MODE_MECA=MODGLO** ,

LIST_INST=LIST_R,

INTERPOL='LIN',

TOUT_CHAM='OUI',

NOEUD='NO3',);

One therefore recalls that, for a time-response analysis, one carries out a double projection. A first projection of the substructure onto the basis composed of normal modes and connection modes, and a second projection of the reduced assembled model onto the response-analysis basis.

### 5.3 An example of a harmonic-response analysis

In the majority of cases, a harmonic-response analysis is carried out on a dissipative model. The operators CREA_ELEM_SSD and ASSE_ELEM_SSD treat only conservative structures. One details in this example the underlying elementary operations, required for carrying out a harmonic-response analysis by dynamic substructuring.

The test case SHLV301A is used to illustrate the presentation. It is a bar of rectangular cross-section modelled with quadratic solid elements. One applies a sinusoidal pressure on a lateral face of the bar.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape7.gif)

The assembled structure:

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.05/Shape8.gif)

Definition and assignment of the material:

MAT=DEFI_MATERIAU( ELAS=_F( RHO = 7.8E03, NU = 0.3, E = 2.1E11,

AMOR_ALPHA = 1.6E-5, AMOR_BETA = 16.))

CHMAT=AFFE_MATERIAU( MAILLAGE=MAYA,

AFFE=_F( TOUT = 'OUI',

MATER = MAT))

Description of the loading:

PRESSION=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(

MODELE=POVOL,

PRES_REP=_F( GROUP_MA = 'PRESSION',

PRES = 50.E4))

Description of the boundary conditions (one wishes to use a projection basis of **Mac Neal** type: the nodes of the interface are free):

FIXAT1=AFFE_CHAR_MECA(

MODELE=POVOL,

DDL_IMPO=(

_F( GROUP_NO = 'COND1', DY = 0.),

_F( GROUP_NO = 'CONDZG', DZ = 0.),

_F( NOEUD = 'N26', DX = 0.),

_F( GROUP_NO = 'DROITE', DX = 0.)))

- Creation of the dynamic macro-elements:

Creation of the elementary matrices of substructure 1:

K_ELEM1=CALC_MATR_ELEM( MODELE=POVOL,

CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

OPTION='RIGI_MECA',

CHARGE=( FIXAT1, )

)

M_ELEM1=CALC_MATR_ELEM( MODELE=POVOL,

CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

OPTION='MASS_MECA',

CHARGE=( FIXAT1, )

)

A_ELEM1=CALC_MATR_ELEM( MODELE=POVOL,

CHAM_MATER=CHMAT,

OPTION='AMOR_MECA',

RIGI_MECA=K_ELEM1,

MASS_MECA=M_ELEM1,

CHARGE=( FIXAT1, )

)

Creation of the elementary vector of the loading for substructure 1:

V_ELEM1=CALC_VECT_ELEM( OPTION='CHAR_MECA',

CHARGE=PRESSION )

Assembly of the elementary matrices and vector:

NUM1=NUME_DDL( MATR_RIGI=K_ELEM1)

K_ASSE1=ASSE_MATRICE( MATR_ELEM=K_ELEM1,

NUME_DDL=NUM1)

M_ASSE1=ASSE_MATRICE( MATR_ELEM=M_ELEM1,

NUME_DDL=NUM1)

A_ASSE1=ASSE_MATRICE( MATR_ELEM=A_ELEM1,

NUME_DDL=NUM1)

V_ASSE1=ASSE_VECTEUR( VECT_ELEM=V_ELEM1,

NUME_DDL=NUM1)

Computation of the normal modes:

MODES1=CALC_MODES( MATR_RIGI=K_ASSE1,

MATR_MASS=M_ASSE1,

CALC_FREQ=_F( NMAX_FREQ = 20) )

Definition of the interface for a basis of Mac Neal type:

INTERF1=DEFI_INTERF_DYNA( NUME_DDL=NUM1,

INTERFACE=_F( NOM = 'DROITE',

**TYPE = 'MNEAL'** ,

GROUP_NO = 'DROITE'),

INFO=2)

Definition of the projection basis:

BAMO1=DEFI_BASE_MODALE( CLASSIQUE=_F( INTERF_DYNA = INTERF1,

MODE_MECA = MODES1,

NMAX_MODE = 20),

INFO=2)

Creation of the dynamic macro-element:

One specifies that one wishes to take the damping into account by entering the damping matrix to be projected.

MACRO1=MACR_ELEM_DYNA( BASE_MODALE=BAMO1,

**MATR_AMOR=A_ASSE1**  )

One builds in the same way the dynamic macro-element of substructure 2.

- Assembly of the different substructures:

Definition of the generalised model: one assigns a name to each substructure and one specifies the interfaces between the different substructures

MODEGE=DEFI_MODELE_GENE( SOUS_STRUC=( _F( NOM = 'COTE1',

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACRO1,

TRANS = (0., 0., 0.,),

ANGL_NAUT = (0., 0., 0.,),),

_F( NOM = 'COTE2',

TRANS = (1., 0., 0.,),

ANGL_NAUT = (0., 0., 0.,),

MACR_ELEM_DYNA = MACRO2)),

LIAISON=_F( SOUS_STRUC_1 = 'COTE1',

SOUS_STRUC_2 = 'COTE2',

INTERFACE_1 = 'DROITE',

INTERFACE_2 = 'GAUCHE'))

Definition of a numbering of the degrees of freedom of the assembled generalised system:

NUMEGE=NUME_DDL_GENE( MODELE_GENE=MODEGE)

Assembly of the generalised matrices of the different substructures:

RIGGEN=ASSE_MATR_GENE( NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

OPTION='RIGI_GENE' )

MASGEN=ASSE_MATR_GENE( NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

OPTION='MASS_GENE' )

AMOGEN=ASSE_MATR_GENE( NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

OPTION='AMOR_GENE' )

Assembly of the generalised loading vector:

VECTGEN=ASSE_VECT_GENE( NUME_DDL_GENE=NUMEGE,

CHAR_SOUS_STRUC=(

_F( SOUS_STRUC = 'COTE1',

VECT_ASSE = V_ASSE1),

_F( SOUS_STRUC = 'COTE2',

VECT_ASSE = V_ASSE2)))

- Computation of the harmonic response of the assembled structure:

DYNGEN=DYNA_VIBRA( **TYPE_CALCUL='HARM'** , **BASE_CALCUL='GENE'** ,

MATR_MASS=MASGEN,

MATR_RIGI=RIGGEN,

MATR_AMOR=AMOGEN,

SOLVEUR=_F( METHODE='LDLT', ),

FREQ=F,

TOUT_CHAM='OUI',

EXCIT=_F( VECT_ASSE = VECTGEN,

COEF_MULT = 1.))

- Recovery onto the physical basis:

One carries out here a recovery onto substructure 1 (COTE1):

DYNHAR1=REST_SOUS_STRUC( RESU_GENE=DYNGEN,

**SOUS_STRUC='COTE1'** )

One therefore recalls that, for a harmonic-response analysis, as for the modal analysis, one carries out the analysis directly on the generalised model of the assembled structure without doing a second projection onto an analysis basis.
