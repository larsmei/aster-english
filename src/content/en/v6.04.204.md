# v6.04.204 SSNV204 – Isotropic cyclic drained compression test on sand d

Summary

We have the following four modellings:

- Modelization A: A isotropic cyclic compression calculation is made in pure mechanics (equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) with Hujeux's law. The calculated solutions are compared with results from elements GEFDYN finite code from the École Centrale Paris;
- Modelization B: monotonous isotropic compression calculation in pure mechanics (equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) on an orthotropic linear elastic material. Solutions are calculated by degenerating Hujeuxvers' law a linear orthotropic elastic behavior and are compared to a true orthotropic linear elastic calculation;
- Modelization C: A cyclic isotropic traction calculation is made in pure mechanics (equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) with the Hujeux law. The purpose of this Modelling is to test the traction mechanisms complementary to the Hujeux law. The calculated solutions are compared with results from elements GEFDYN finite code from the École Centrale Paris;
- Modelization D: A cyclic isotropic compression calculation is performed in pure mechanics (equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) as for Modelling A. The purpose of this Modelling is to test the possibility of taking into account mechanical properties that depend on the coordinates of the mesh in a nonlinear calculation with Hujeux's law.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.204/100000000000051400000456D838525F86D79D41.png)

The test is performed on a single finite element isoparametric cubic $\mathrm{CUB8}$. The length of each edge is 1. The different facets of this cube are mesh cells groups named $\mathrm{HAUT}$, $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$, $\mathrm{ARRIERE}$, $\mathrm{DROIT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$. The mesh-cell group $\mathit{SYM}$ also contains the mesh cells $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ groups; the mesh-cell group $\mathrm{COTE}$ groups of mesh cells $\mathrm{ARRIERE}$ and $\mathrm{DROIT}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The elastic properties are:

- isotropic compressibility module: $K=516200\mathrm{kPa}$
- shear module: $\mu =238200\mathrm{kPa}$

The anelastic properties (Hujeux) are derived from the document provided by the École Centrale Paris:

- power of non-linear elastic law: ${n}_{e}=0.4$
- $\beta =24$
- $d=2.5$
- $b=0.2$
- friction angle: $\varphi =33°$
- Dilatance angle: $\psi =33°$
- critical pressure: ${P}_{\mathit{c0}}=-1000\mathit{kPa}$
- Reference pressure: ${P}_{\mathrm{ref}}=-1000\mathrm{kPa}$
- elastic radius of isotropic mechanisms: ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{s}={10}^{-3}$
- elastic radius of deviatory mechanisms: ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{d}=5.{10}^{-3}$
- ${a}_{\mathrm{mon}}={10}^{-4}$
- ${a}_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0.008$
- ${c}_{\mathrm{mon}}=0.2$
- ${c}_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0.1$
- ${r}_{\mathrm{hys}}=0.05$
- ${r}_{\mathrm{mon}}=0.9$
- ${x}_{m}=1$
- $\text{dila}=1$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

An isotropic compression test consists of imposing an equal variation in load on each side of the sample.

In the model under consideration, the cubic element represents one eighth of the sample. The boundary conditions are therefore:

- The symmetry conditions:

  - ${u}_{z}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{BAS}$ group
  - ${u}_{x}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ group
  - ${u}_{y}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ group
- The loading conditions:

  - ${P}_{n}=1$ on mesh cells $\mathrm{COTE}$ and $\mathrm{HAUT}$ groups

The load is carried out in three phases:

1. isotropic compression loading between $t=-10$ and $t=0$ where the pressure on the mesh cells $\mathrm{COTE}$ and $\mathrm{HAUT}$ groups varies between $p=-100\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p=-300\mathrm{kPa}$.
2. isotropic traction load between $t=0$ and $t=10$ , where pressure varies between $p=-300\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p=-100\mathrm{kPa}$ .
3. isotropic compression loading between $t=10$ and $t=20$ where pressure varies between $p=-100\mathrm{kPa}$ and $p=-340\mathrm{kPa}$.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

$A$ Modelling is three-dimensional and static nonlinear, 3D.

The first step is to preconsolidate the sample to $p=-300\mathrm{kPa}$ (the first phase of the calculation), which is $100$ time step between $t=-10$ and $t=0$. This phase involves the monotonous isotropic mechanism of Hujeux's law.

The isotropic traction phase from $p=-300\mathrm{kPa}$ to $p=-100\mathrm{kPa}$ (2nd phase of the calculation) is conducted in $100$ time step between $t=0$ and $t=10$. In this second phase, the automatic subdivision of the time step is activated to manage non-convergence situations of local integration. This phase allows to process the passage between monotonous and cyclic isotropic mechanisms and then to follow the mixed hardening of the cyclic mechanism.

The new isotropic compression phase from $p=-100\mathrm{kPa}$ to $p=-340\mathrm{kPa}$ (3rd calculation phase) takes place in $100$ time step between $t=10$ and $t=20$ moments. The automatic subdivision of the time step is again activated to manage the passages of cyclic/cyclic and cyclic/monotone mechanisms. The new cyclic consolidation mechanism created follows a mixed nutring, and then when the mechanism is switched to monotonous, it isotropically collapses.

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

Model C is three-dimensional and static nonlinear, (3D).

The first step is to make an elastic preconsolidation ($\mathit{ELAS}$ ) from the sample to $p=-100\mathit{kPa}$ (first phase of the calculation). This preconsolidation takes place in $1$ time step between $t=-10$ and $t=0$ . This phase is purely elastic.

The isotropic traction phase, controlled on the required displacement, up to ${u}_{x}={u}_{y}={u}_{z}=5\mathit{mm}$ (imposed displacement on the $\mathit{HAUT}$, $\mathit{DROIT}$, $\mathit{ARRIERE}$ faces) is carried out in $100$ time step between $t=0$ and $t=5$. The maximum displacements imposed correspond to a deformation of $0.5\text{\%}$. During this second phase, the automatic subdivision of the time step is activated to manage the non-convergence situations of local integration. This phase allows the passage between the monotone and cyclic isotropic mechanisms to be processed and followed by the mixed nutring of the cyclic mechanism until reaching a state of stress close to the tensile state for the material. This test ensures that the perfect plastic mechanisms controlling the Hujeux model's tensile are correctly activated.

The next phase of isotropic compression up to ${u}_{x}={u}_{y}={u}_{z}=-5\mathit{mm}$ (3rd phase of calculation) takes place in $100$ time step between $t=5$ and $t=10$ moments. The automatic subdivision of the time step is again activated to manage the passages of traction mechanisms to isotropic and cyclic/monotone cyclic mechanisms. The new cyclic consolidation mechanism created follows a mixed nutring, then when the mechanism is switched to monotonous, it isotropically collapses.
