# v6.08.106 SSND106: Multiple rotations in large deformations, isotropic hardening

**Summary:**

This test modeled a point material subjected to four $45°$ rigid body tensile-rotation cycles, with an constitutive law elastoplastic Von isotropic hardening applications in large deformations (formulation with GDEF_LOG logarithmic deformation). The invariance of the equivalent stress of Von bets during the rotation phases is checked, and its values during the tensiles by comparing with the Modelling of SIMO_MIEHE.

The modellings $A$ and $B$ treat the problem in flat deformations, either in 3D ($A$) or in D_PLAN ($B$).

The modellings $C$ and $D$ handle the problem in flat constraints, either in 3D ($C$) or in C_PLAN ($D$).

## 1 Reference problem

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.106/10000200000004530000015FCDEB098627D618FE.png)

### 1.1 Geometry

> Figure 1: Reference problem (for $90°$ rotation)

A element of $1000\mathrm{mm}$ cubic material is considered to be on the side, subject alternately to a traction force and then to an overall rotation of $45°$. It undergoes a total of 4 traction/rotation cycles.

### 1.2 Material data

The constitutive law isotropic-plastic isotropic hardening type of bets is considered here: VMIS_ISOT_LINE. The table below lists the parameters used; in order to strengthen comparison, the parameters used result in identical constitutive laws in both cases (linear isotropic hardening).

| Young's modulus: | $200000\mathrm{MPa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $0,3$ |
| Limit of elasticity | $200\mathrm{MPa}$ |
| Linear nutting module | $2000\mathrm{MPa}$ |

## 7 Summary of the results

The results obtained are satisfactory, both in $\mathrm{3D}$ and $\mathrm{2D}$ . There is a maximum difference below $0,2\text{\%}$ between SIMO_MIEHE (taken as reference) and the formalism GDEF_LOG.

This test also validates the smooth operation of GDEF_LOG in flat constraints.

In addition, for all modellings, the equivalent stress remains constant during the rotation phases.
