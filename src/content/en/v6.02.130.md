# v6.02.130 SSNL130 – Unformable plate on a spring mat

**Summary:**

The objective is to test and validate the possibilities of the [AFFE_CARA_ELEM](doc:u4.42.01) command, option RIGI_PARASOL in $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$ and affected by `DIS_CHOC` or `DIS_CONTACT` behavior.

This test case modeled a plate, considered unformable, placed on a carpet of springs.

- The springs are modeled by `DIS_T` (`K_T_D_L`), which allows to impose boundary conditions at the ends of the springs that are not tied to the solid.
- `DIS_CHOC` or `DIS_CONTACT` behaviors allow for unilateral spring behaviour, leaving a possibility of plate release from the spring mat.

The same problem can also be modelled by elements d-interfaces with the `CZM_ELAS_MIX` behavioral relationship, which then represent a continuous distribution of springs (modelization E).

The F Modelling allows to validate the grid method, option RIGI_GRILLE.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A rectangular plate of $a$ width and $b$ length, supported by a carpet of springs.

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.130/PlaqueTapisRessort.svg)

*: Schematic of the plate and springs in the $(y,z)$ plane.*

Dimensions: $a = 1.00 m$, $b = 2.00 m$, $ep = 0.30 m$

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus : $2.0E+11 Pa$

Poisson's ratio : $0.3$

Stiffness overall spring mat : $K = 10000.0 N/m$

Stiffness density of the spring mat: $k = \frac{K}{a.b} = 5000 N/{m}^{2}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The loading is a pressure of the $P = {p.(y-b)^{2}}$ shape, with $p = {5N /{m}^{4}}$

Displacements imposed on the ends of springs not connected to the plate:

- In the time interval $[0,1]$ the displacement is imposed to 0.0 following ${DX}$ , ${DY}$ and ${DZ}$ ,
- In the time interval $[1,2]$ the displacement is imposed to 0.0 following ${DX}$ and ${DY}$.
- Next ${DZ}$ it is imposed by the ${Dz} = (t-1.0) * 0.5E-02$ function.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Not applicable for static analysis.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation of the continuous problem

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.130/PlaqueTapisRessortDeplace.svg)

*: Plate and springs after loading.*

The problem solving is to calculate the vertical displacements of the corners of the plate and the position of the point of the delamination vis-à-vis the spring mat.

The equilibrium equations are as follows:

- Effort resulting from loading:
${Fp} = \underset{s}{\iint }{P.ds} ={p.a.}\frac{{b}^{3}}{3}$
- The point $A$ due to loading:
${{Mp}_{A}} = \underset{s}{\iint }{P.y.ds} = {a.p.}\frac{{b}^{4}}{12}$

The plate is considered rigid, its displacement is of the form $z(y)={U}_{a}(1-\frac{y}{{y}_{0}})$. With ${U}_{a}$ the vertical displacement of the point $A$ and ${y}_{0}$ the position of the detachment.

- Spring reaction effort: ${Fr}=\underset{s}{\iint }\frac{K}{a.b}{U}_{a}(1-\frac{y}{{y}_{0}}) ds =K.{U}_{a}\frac{{y}_{0}}{2b}$
- Point $A$ spring reaction time: ${{Mr}}_{A}=\underset{s}{\iint }\frac{K}{a.b}{U}_{a}(1-\frac{y}{{y}_{0}})y{ds}=K.{U}_{a}\frac{{y}_{0}^{2}}{6b}$

The equations (balance of effort and moments) are solved by the following result: ${y}_{0}=\frac{3b}{4}$, ${U}_{a}=-\frac{8p.a.{b}^{3}}{9K}$ is derived from ${U}_{b}=-\frac{{U}_{a}}{3}$

### 2.2 Method of calculating the discretized problem

In this analysis the spring mat is no longer considered continuous. Springs are regularly distributed. As previously the vertical displacements of the corners of the plate and the position of the stripping line vis-à-vis the spring mat will be calculated.

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.130/PlaqueDiscretRessortDeplace.png)

*: Schematic of the plate and springs after loading.*

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.130/PlaqueDiscretRessortPlan.png)

*: Discretization of the plate in the $(x,y)$ plane*

The v6.02.130-fig-Place DiscreteRessortPlan identifies the springs according to their stiffness. This stiffness is calculated by the RIGI_PARASOL option of the [AFFE_CARA_ELEM](doc:u4.42.01) command. The allocation of the values is based on the area of the area they affect. If $K$ is the overall stiffness of the spring mat, we have:

${k4}=\frac{{K}}{{nx}{ny}}$, ${k2}={k3}=\frac{{k4}}{2}=\frac{{K}}{2{nx}{ny}}$, ${k1}=\frac{{k4}}{4}=\frac{{K}}{4{nx}{ny}}$

The equilibrium equations are as follows:

- Spring reaction effort: ${\mathit{Fr}}_{(j)}={U}_{a}\cdot \left[{K}_{x}^{'}+{K}_{x}^{''}\cdot \sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-j\frac{b}{\mathit{ny}{y}_{0}}\right)\right]$
- Spring reaction time along line ${AB}$: ${{Mr}}_{(j)}={U}_{a}\cdot {K}_{x}^{''}\cdot \sum_{j=1}^{n}(1-j\frac{b}{{ny}{y}_{0}})\cdot j\frac{b}{{ny}}$
with:

  - ${K}_{x}^{'}=(2{k1}+{k2}({nx}-1))$
  - ${K}_{x}^{"}=(2{k3}+{k4}({nx}-1))$
  - $n \frac{b}{ny} \le {y}_{0} \le (n+1)\frac{b}{ny}$

The solution of equations (balance of effort and moments) gives the solution of balance:

$$
\left \lbrace \begin{array}{l}
{U}_{a}=\frac{p.a.{b}^{3}.{ny}.(3.ny-8.n-4)}{6.K.(1+n+{n}^{2})} \\
{y}_{0}=\frac{b.n.(1+n).(3.ny-8.n-4)}{3.ny.(ny+2.n.(ny-2)-4.{n}^{2})}
\end{array} \right.
$$

where $n$ and ${y}_{0}$ must meet the following conditions:

- ${n}\frac{b}{ny} \le {y}_{0} \le (n+1)\frac{b}{{ny}}$
- $0 \le {y}_{0} \le b$
- Integer $n$

### 2.3 Quantities and reference results

The quantities tested will be vertical displacements at the 4 corners of the plate.

### 2.4 Uncertainties about the solution

None, the solution is analytical.

## 5 Modelling E

### 5.1 Features of modellings

The plate is not modelled as it is subject to rigid body movement. The spring mat is modelled by elements interface `3D_INTERFACE_S`. The interface behavior relationship is `CZM_ELAS_MIX` in which the density of stiffness in traction is zero (de-collar without stiffness) and the density of stiffness in compression is simply $k$, without any other treatments. Also, a tangential stiffness (equal to $k$) is added to prevent any transverse movement.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

The spring mattress is cut into 20 x 1 elements interface.

## 6 Modelling F

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics

The plate is modeled by elements `DKT`. Springs are modeled by `SEG2` affected by `DIS_TR` Modelling
whose characteristics are calculated by the RIGI_GRILLE grid method.

Dimensions: a = 1.00 m, b = 2.00 m, ep = 0.30 m.

- Young's modulus : 2.0E + 11 Pa
- Poisson's ratio : 0.3
- Overall stiffness of the spring mat in all directions: K = 1.0E + 8 N/m

The loading and verification protocol is as follows:

- the centre of the plate is placed with a force equivalent to the overall stiffness in each of the 5 directions (DX, DY, DZ, DRX, DRY),
- the resulting displacement must be quasi-unitary.

The DX, DY and DZ components are not correlated; a first calculation simultaneously processes the DX, DY and DZ directions;
A second calculation deals exclusively with the DRX direction; a third calculation deals exclusively with the DRY direction.

## 7 Summary of the results

Using discrete elements, assigned to nodes or segments, with material of the type `DIS_CONTACT` and used with [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03) (behavior `COMP_INCR` relation `DIS_CHOC` or `DIS_CONTACT`) allows to model a unilateral behavior of springs.

In ${2D}$ as in ${3D}$, using the RIGI_PARASOL keyword of the [AFFE_CARA_ELEM](doc:u4.42.01) command allows you to assign to the springs stiffeners proportional to the length or surface of the elements to which they are connected.

Since the behaviour is unilateral, it is necessary to make several iterations to find the position of equilibrium. It is also possible to encounter convergence problems related to a loss of precision, due to poor conditioning of the matrix of stiffness during iterations. The stiffness springs can be canceled from one iteration to the other.

With regard to elements d'interface modelling, the continuous solution is obtained without error. Here too, several iterations are necessary to find the point plate detachment. Ideally, we should also guard against the energy-free mode in which the plate takes off everywhere (it could lead to a non-invertible matrix, even if it was not observed because in the natural state, the compression stiffness, not zero, is preferred).
