# v6.08.103 SSND103 - Validation of a bilinear constitutive law on a discreet element (application to bolted assemblies)

**Summary:**

The objective of this test case is to validate a bilinear constitutive law on a discreet element.

This constitutive law was developed in a study in which the behaviour of the screw d-a bolted assembly is modelled by a discrete element affected by the same behavior.

The constitutive law DIS_BILI_ELAS requires in argument the two apparent screw stiffeners (elements of the contact assembly or not) as well as the value of the pre-locking force imposed on the screw.

It is verified that the gradients of the load curves obtained when the discrete is applied correspond to the apparent stiffeners for different temperatures and direction of stress and that the slope break corresponds to the pre-stressed force imposed.

In theory, a load (force or displacement) is being imposed on a discrete element represented by a node as in *Figure* **a** .

In practice, a blocking condition must be introduced on this node to avoid any movement of solid body and give a physical meaning to its stiffness.

Therefore, the mesh will be composed of two nodes, with the stiffness assigned to the segment formed by their junction (see *Figure* **b** ).

*N.B.*: If this segment has a nonzero length (i.e. both nodes are not confused), its direction sets the direction of the discrete.

- $\mathrm{N1}$ is called the node to which the blocking conditions will be applied.
- $\mathrm{N2}$ is the node on which mechanical loadings will be imposed.
- $\mathrm{Disc}$ l的element is called discrete SEG2 type, which is affected by the constitutive law to be validated.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material data of an elastic steel: $E={2.10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$ , $\nu =0.3$ is arbitrarily chosen to assign to the discreet element .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A d-condition is imposed on the node $\mathrm{N1}$ .

A ${F}_{P}$ pre-locking effort is imposed on the discrete l-l-element.

A proportional load is imposed while moving to the node $\mathrm{N2}$ .

A constant temperature field is imposed over time.

It is considered that the discreet element can only work in translation, and is assigned DIS_T Modelling.

## 2 reference solution

The discrete element is assigned, via the bilinear constitutive law, two ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ stiffeners, which are the temperature functions of the ${K}_{i}={\alpha}_{i}-{\beta}_{i}\dot{T}$ shape.

We arbitrarily fix here:

${\alpha}_{1}=2\dot{{\alpha}_{2}}={2.10}^{8}{\mathrm{N.m}}^{-1}$

${\beta}_{1}=2\dot{{\beta}_{2}}={4.10}^{6}{\mathrm{N.m}}^{-1}$

It is checked that, whatever $U$ displacement or ${F}_{P}$ stress force is imposed, a $F$ nodal reaction is obtained from the discrete checking $\Delta F={K}_{i}.\Delta U$ elastic law, where $i=1$ if $F\le {F}_{P}$ and $i=2$ if $F>{F}_{P}$.

![Figure](/figures/v6.08.103/Shape3.gif)

Nodal reactions of the discrete are noted for displacements (${U}_{1}<{U}_{2}$ ) on both sides of the slope fracture.

The slope failure for ${U}_{P}={F}_{P}/{K}_{1}$ results directly in the following reference solutions:

${F}_{1}={K}_{1}.{U}_{1}$ et ${F}_{2}={F}_{P}+{K}_{2}.({U}_{2}-{U}_{P})$

either ${F}_{2}={F}_{P}.(1-{K}_{2}/{K}_{1})+{K}_{2}.{U}_{2}$

## 6 Summary of the results
