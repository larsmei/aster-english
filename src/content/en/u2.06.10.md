# u2.06.10 Carrying out a civil-engineering study under seismic loading

**Summary:**

The aim of this document is to give advice for carrying out civil-engineering studies under seismic loading. It presents the use of the tools available in *Code_Aster* for modelling reinforced-concrete structures under earthquake loading. One should first consult
[u2.03.07](doc:u2.03.07).

The methods of analysis in non-linear transient dynamics are first recalled. The finite-element modellings and the concrete and steel constitutive models available are then described. Finally, the various stages to be carried out within the framework of a non-linear seismic dynamic study are presented (preliminary studies, solution of the analysis and post-processing).

## 1 Introduction

Within the framework of the seismic reassessment of civil-engineering buildings, the objective of numerical simulations is to gain a better understanding of their non-linear behaviour. This document presents the tools available in *Code_Aster* in order to model reinforced-concrete structures under seismic loading. For this particular need, it supplements the information described in [U2.03.07](doc:u2.03.07).

The aim of this document is to provide the user with methodological advice and rules of good practice to be followed during the various stages of his or her analysis of reinforced-concrete structures under seismic loading. The following points are addressed in turn:

- the methods of analysis in non-linear transient dynamics,
- the choice of the finite-element modelling,
- the choice of the constitutive models,
- the data setup of the problem,
- the analyses prior to the non-linear transient dynamic analysis,
- the solution of the non-linear transient dynamic analysis,
- the post-processing,
- the existing studies.

However, the regulatory design analysis by the spectral method [U2.06.09](doc:u2.06.09) is not addressed in this documentation.

## 2 Methods of non-linear transient dynamic analysis

This document does not detail the general advice for carrying out a non-linear transient dynamic analysis. It is nevertheless important to stress the specific character of such analyses. The use of the dynamic operator DYNA_NON_LINE ([U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01), [U2.06.13](doc:u2.06.13) and [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05)) requires additional precautions relative to a non-linear static analysis STAT_NON_LINE ([U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03), [U2.04.01](doc:u2.04.01) and [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)).

It is in particular important to note that, the time evolution to be represented being of large size, one has a problem that is often costly in CPU time. One is therefore led to set up modellings that are not too large.

Furthermore, certain options available in non-linear statics are not permissible in dynamics. This is in particular the case of the piloting of the loading, which makes it possible to help the convergence of a static analysis. In dynamics, the loading history being "real", one can no longer use this type of method as it stands.

### 2.1 Operator DYNA_NON_LINE

The operator DYNA_NON_LINE ([U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01), [U2.06.13](doc:u2.06.13) and [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05)) makes it possible to compute the dynamic evolution of a structure whose material and/or geometry has a non-linear behaviour.

The equations of dynamics are solved with:

- a finite-element discretisation on the mesh in the "physical" basis,
- implicit or explicit time-integration methods for the solution of the time problem,
- Newton–Raphson integration methods for the solution of the non-linear incremental problem associated with the mechanical equilibrium. This method consists in iterating on the solution with a tangent operator.

In dynamics, unlike statics, there cannot exist a strictly non-invertible solution operator. This is due to the presence of the mass matrix in this operator. However, this in no way guarantees the convergence of the analysis. The most frequent problems concern the solution of the constitutive behaviour.

Advice on the use of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE making it possible to improve the convergence of the algorithms is given in paragraph § 7.2.

### 2.2 Choice of the time-integration scheme

The user can currently choose, in DYNA_NON_LINE, between four time schemes:

**Implicit schemes:**

- a non-dissipative implicit scheme: the Newmark average-acceleration scheme (or trapezoidal rule),
- a dissipative implicit scheme: the HHT scheme (which introduces high-frequency numerical dissipation),

**Explicit schemes:**

- a non-dissipative explicit scheme: the central-difference scheme,
- a dissipative explicit scheme: the Tchamwa–Wielgosz scheme (which introduces high-frequency numerical dissipation).

One should refer to [14], [U2.06.13](doc:u2.06.13) and [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05) for a detailed account of the rules for using these various time-integration methods.

Within the framework of civil-engineering studies under seismic loading, it is recommended to use the classical implicit average-acceleration scheme, which introduces no numerical dissipation.

It will be seen in paragraph § 7.2 that one may be led, in the event of numerical instability of the analyses (high-frequency oscillations), to use an HHT-type scheme.

Moreover, in the event of severe non-convergence, one may try to continue the analysis by using an explicit scheme. However, the operator DYNA_NON_LINE is oriented towards implicit approaches. It is not optimised (vectorised) for explicit solutions. It is therefore currently recommended to use an explicit scheme with the greatest caution.

### 2.3 Choice of the time step of the analysis

#### 2.3.1 Implicit schemes

The time step to be chosen must satisfy a certain number of conditions [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05), [U2.06.13](doc:u2.06.13):

- the time step must be sufficiently small to represent correctly the time sampling of the loading;
- it is advised, for reasons of accuracy ("Shannon"-type criterion on the cut-off frequency), to choose a time step such that: $\Delta t\le \frac{1}{(10\mathrm{\ast }{f}_{max})}$ , with ${f}_{\max}$ the highest frequency to be represented;
- furthermore, it may be of interest to determine an approximation of the CFL condition (Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy, [5]) of the problem in order to have a lower bound on the time step to be used. The stability time step of the CFL condition is given by: ${t}_{c}=L/c$ with $L$ the characteristic length of the smallest element of the mesh and $c$ the speed of the one-dimensional elastic compression waves given by $c=\sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}}$ . The use of a time step markedly smaller (by more than one order of magnitude) than the CFL condition has no physical meaning and may be a source of high-frequency numerical oscillations. One will in particular take care, when subdividing the time step, that the time step used remains close to the CFL condition (it should be noted that the stability time step is generally very small).

In practice, in the non-linear regime, it is necessary to check the low sensitivity of the response obtained for analyses with different time steps.

When one has a converged result for a time step $\Delta {t}_{1}$ , one will check the stability of this result by taking a time step less than or equal to $0,1\times \Delta {t}_{1}$ *.* If the response is identical, the time step $\Delta {t}_{1}$ is satisfactory.

#### 2.3.2 Explicit schemes

These schemes, unlike the implicit schemes, are conditionally stable. One must imperatively use a time step smaller than the stability time step (CFL condition). Otherwise, the analysis may diverge (one observes, for example, abnormally high accelerations). The stability time step is determined at the beginning of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE. The option ‘STOP_CFL’=OUI makes it possible to ensure that the CFL condition is never exceeded.

**Note**

*The computation of the CFL condition is not programmed for all the elements (in particular the discrete elements are ignored); the CFL condition estimated by Code_Aster may therefore be larger (less penalising) than the real CFL condition, with the risks of abrupt divergence that follow from this.*

## 3 Choice of the finite-element modelling

### 3.1 Introduction

One seeks to represent two large families of reinforced-concrete structural elements present in classical civil-engineering buildings:

- the beams and the columns,
- the rafts, the floors (slabs) and the walls.

Three broad categories of finite-element representations are distinguished:

**Local representation (solid elements)**

In this classical finite-element approach, the constituent materials (steel and concrete) are modelled separately. Solid finite elements are used for the concrete and the associated constitutive models are written in 3D. The discrete modelling of the steels with bar finite elements creates stress singularities, which is very penalising as soon as one enters the non-linear constitutive-behaviour regime, cf. [21]. This type of approach makes it possible to obtain a detailed description of the non-linear phenomena involved, but its application to the whole of an industrial-type structure may prove difficult (prohibitive CPU time and memory size). Moreover, the use of local constitutive models leads to penalising non-convergence problems.

**Semi-global representation**

The modellings used are of multilayer beam and shell type, and an integration is carried out through the thickness of the element. The constituent materials are still modelled separately. One may distinguish:

- the multilayer shells supported by shell-type finite elements. Simplifying assumptions associated with shell theory are adopted (the displacement fields vary linearly through the thickness of the shell, the transverse stress ${\sigma}_{zz}$ is zero). The associated constitutive models are written in 2D plane stress;
- the multifibre beams supported by beam-type finite elements. Simplifying assumptions associated with Euler beam theory are adopted (the sections remain straight and perpendicular to the mean fibre). The associated constitutive models are written in 1D for the concrete fibres and for the steel fibres.

The main advantage of this type of modelling is that it is much less costly in CPU time and in memory size than a classical local representation. It furthermore makes it possible to represent in a relatively realistic manner the structure to be studied. Post-processings are available in order to access the quantities of engineering interest.

**Global representation**

In this approach, the global behaviour of the reinforced concrete is modelled (the constituent elements are no longer considered separately). The supporting finite elements are single-layer structural elements (beams, shells). The specific associated constitutive models are written in global variables (generalised forces, generalised strains). Global modellings are generally developed for specific applications (for one type of constituent element of a structure, for one type of loading, …). This type of representation is very inexpensive in CPU time and in memory size, but the data of the modelling require an identification that must be carried out with care. Moreover, this identification is generally valid only for one class of loadings.

### 3.2 Description of the local modelling (solid elements)

#### 3.2.1 Modelling of the concrete

Modelling by means of classical solid finite elements is not addressed in this document, given that it presents no specific features of its own in the case of concrete, cf. also
[u2.03.07](doc:u2.03.07). One should refer to the general documentation of *Code_Aster* for any modelling problem.

#### 3.2.2 Modelling of the reinforcement

One may use the following two types of modelling to represent the reinforcement bars of the reinforcement:

- the bar elements (BARRE, [U3.11.01](doc:u3.11.01)). These line finite elements in 3D space transmit only axial forces and strains. On the other hand, in dynamics, the inertia forces appear in all directions. The use of the BARRE elements implies modelling all the reinforcement bars one by one. This modelling is to be used when the reinforcement is complicated and when one seeks to represent in a very fine manner the geometric position of the reinforcement bars; however, one will take care of the difficulties reported in [21];
- the membrane grid elements (GRILLE_MEMBRANE, [U3.12.04](doc:u3.12.04)). This GRILLE_MEMBRANE modelling makes it possible to represent the reinforcement layers with a single reinforcement direction working in membrane only. The supporting finite elements are surface elements (TRIA3, QUA4, …). The notion of offset does not exist for this modelling. It is therefore necessary to position the reinforcement bars at the correct location when meshing the structure. In order to represent a complete reinforcement layer (reinforcement in two orthogonal directions), one duplicates the first GRILLE_MEMBRANE layer and one defines a second layer orthogonal to the first.

**Note**

*It has been shown that the mixing of solid elements (concrete) and bar elements (steel) can introduce singularities that are a source of non-convergence* *with respect to the mesh-cell size and in the solution of the non-linear equilibrium* *(application of a point loading)*  [21] *. It is therefore preferable, if the problem allows it, to use the grid elements to represent the reinforcement bars.*

#### 3.2.3 Modelling of the connection between the concrete and the reinforcement

It is recommended to make the nodes of the steel and concrete mesh cells coincide in order to ensure the continuity of the displacements at the interface. This makes it possible not to increase the size of the problem, because one thus avoids introducing connection relations between the steel and concrete mesh cells in order to ensure the bond. It is necessary to match correctly all the concrete nodes located along the reinforcement bar with a steel node.

The reinforced-concrete structure is then represented by the superposition of the BARRE or GRILLE_MEMBRANE elements used for the steels and of the 3D solid elements used for the concrete.

It should be noted that this modelling strategy implies that the steel–concrete connection is perfect. This is reductive of the true phenomenology of the behaviour of reinforced-concrete sections, for which the steel–concrete slip (delayed by the use of ribbed or high-bond reinforcement bars) produces the transition towards a regime described by the "tension stiffening effect", for which one obtains a response of the section that is much more resistant than that predicted by the contribution of the steels alone once the concrete is cracked.

More realistic modellings of the connection between the steels and the concrete are available in *Code_Aster:*

- In 2D: JOINT_BA, [R7.01.21](doc:r7.01.21). This one is not suited to seismic applications on a complete building. It may be used to study locally a steel–concrete connection (a pull-out test, for example).
- In 3D: cohesive law CZM_LAB_MIX, [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11), [7]. It makes it possible to model the slip of the steel reinforcement bars relative to the concrete. The slip surface is discretised by interface finite elements, see [R7.02.13](doc:r7.02.13).

### 3.3 Description of the semi-global multilayer-shell modelling

#### 3.3.1 Modelling of the concrete

The concrete is modelled by means of plate or shell elements of *Code_Aster* (DKT, DST, Q4G, COQUE_3D, COQUE_SOLIDE). This document does not return to the formulation of these elements. One should refer to [U2.02.01](doc:u2.02.01) for their use.

It is simply recalled that, given that the analyses carried out are non-linear, a layer-integration method is used for these elements. For each layer, a three-point Simpson method is used, at the middle of the layer and on the upper and lower skins of the layer. For $N$ layers the number of integration points through the thickness is $2N+1$ . For the tangent stiffness, one computes for each layer, in plane stress, the contribution to the membrane, bending and membrane–bending coupling stiffness matrices, as well as the contribution to the internal generalised forces. These contributions are added and assembled in order to obtain the total tangent stiffness matrix.

In order to treat the material non-linearities, it is recommended to use from 3 to 5 layers through the thickness, for a number of integration points equal to 7, 9 and 11 respectively.

#### 3.3.2 Modelling of the reinforcement

One may use the following two types of modelling to represent the reinforcement bars of the reinforcement:

- the bar elements (BARRE, cf. § 3.2.2);
- the grid elements (GRILLE_EXCENTRE, [U3.12.04](doc:u3.12.04)). This GRILLE_EXCENTRE modelling makes it possible to represent the reinforcement layers with a single reinforcement direction, with an offset. One thus defines the position of the reinforcement layer relative to the mid-plane of the reinforced-concrete shell. In order to represent a complete reinforcement layer (reinforcement in two orthogonal directions), one duplicates the first GRILLE_EXCENTRE layer and one defines a second layer orthogonal to the first. One meshes only one shell, which one duplicates in *Code_Aster* in order to create the mesh-cell groups corresponding to the reinforcement bars (operator CREA_MAILLAGE). All the mesh-cell groups are supported on the same nodes.

**Note**

*In this semi-global multilayer-shell representation, it is not possible to model the transverse reinforcement bars.*

#### 3.3.3 Modelling of the connection between the concrete and the reinforcement

As in § 3.2.3, it is recommended to make the nodes of the steel and concrete mesh cells coincide and thus to ensure the continuity of the displacements at the interface. This makes it possible not to increase the size of the problem, because one thus avoids introducing connection relations between the steel and concrete mesh cells in order to ensure the bond. If one uses GRILLE_EXCENTRE elements for the reinforcement bars, the steel and concrete nodes coincide naturally, because the mesh cells are duplicated and are therefore supported on the same nodes.

The reinforced-concrete structure is represented by the superposition of the GRILLE_EXCENTRE modellings used for the reinforcement bars and the shell modelling used for the concrete.

### 3.4 Description of the semi-global multifibre-beam modelling

The multifibre-beam modelling (element POU_D_EM, [U3.11.07](doc:u3.11.07) and [R3.08.08](doc:r3.08.08)) is based on the solution of a beam problem whose heterogeneous cross-section is divided into several fibres. Each fibre is endowed with a uniaxial constitutive behaviour corresponding to the material constituting it, while the kinematics is defined by the extension coming from the extension proper (axial strain) and from the change in curvature of the beam itself. The cross-section may be of arbitrary shape and is described by means of a 2D mesh.

### 3.5 Description of the global modelling

#### 3.5.1 General points

In this type of representation, the structural elements implemented have only a single fibre or layer in the cross-section. The associated homogenised constitutive models are written in global variables (generalised forces, generalised strains) without passing through local laws. Most of the developments existing in the literature concern beams. In *Code_Aster*, however, this type of global reinforced-concrete beam element is not developed.

It should be noted that these global modellings are very specific (to one type of constituent element of a structure, to one type of loading, …) and make it difficult to represent in a sufficiently fine manner the behaviour of complex industrial structures. Furthermore, the data of the modelling require an identification that must be carried out with care.

#### 3.5.2 Modelling of a reinforced-concrete plate

The modelling of a reinforced-concrete plate available in *Code_Aster* is similar to that which was first developed in the Europlexus analysis code [8] in order to treat fast-dynamics problems for civil-engineering structures.

The support elements are degenerate DKTs with a single integration point through the thickness (DKTG elements). This modelling (Figure 3.5.2-a) uses global variables ($N$ , membrane forces; $M$ , bending moments; , $\epsilon$  generalised strains and $\kappa$ , curvatures) coming from a global constitutive model (for reinforced concrete, the model GLRC_DM). This model makes it possible to simulate the behaviour of reinforced-concrete plates under cyclic loading.

The constitutive model GLRC_DM is described in more detail in paragraph § 4.4.5 and the constitutive model DHRC in paragraph § 4.4.7.

### 3.6 Modelling of the connections

It is possible to connect different modellings to one another according to the zones of the structure. The various conceivable modelling approaches are not described in detail here. A few modelling points are nevertheless specified.

#### 3.6.1 Shell–shell connection

The connection between two perpendicular shells can be made directly even if, strictly speaking, the transmission of the torsion forces is not exact (the rotation degree of freedom that generates the torsion is not transmitted).

If one wishes to represent the connection between two perpendicular shells in a more accurate manner, it is possible to do so by means of linear relations (so as not to count twice the volume at the intersection of the two shells). For this, one must use the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA, LIAISON_COQUE, [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) and [U2.02.01](doc:u2.02.01).

The multilayer shell – global shell connection is natural, because the finite elements concerned have the same degrees of freedom.

#### 3.6.2 3D–beam and shell–beam connection

There exists in *Code_Aster* an operator making it possible to connect a solid part to a beam (AFFE_CHAR_MECA, LIAISON_ELEM, OPTION=‘3D_POU’, [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03) and [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01)), or a shell to a beam (AFFE_CHAR_MECA, LIAISON_ELEM, OPTION=‘COQ_POU’, [R3.03.06](doc:r3.03.06), [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01) and [U2.02.01](doc:u2.02.01)).

These connections (3D_POU and COQ_POU) make it possible to connect two parts of meshes that extend one another. It is not intended to model, for example, a connection between a shell and a beam that comes to connect perpendicularly. One therefore sees that **column–floor connections cannot be modelled in this manner**.

In the case of a column–floor connection, if one seeks to transmit the torsion forces correctly, it is recommended to make the connection by means of shells only (cf. § 3.6.1, above) and possibly to extend the column with beams by means of a shell–beam connection (Figure 3.6.2-a).

Another possibility, used in statics, consists in adding fictitious beams in the plane of the shell in order to transmit the forces coming from the column. This solution is to be used with caution in dynamics, because one must impose very small masses on the fictitious beams. If nodes of these beams are not attached to the shell, one risks a disturbance of the solution (ill-conditioned mass matrix).

#### 3.6.3 Column–floor connection

It is recommended to use the option ‘PLAQ_POUT_ORTH’ of the keyword LIAISON_ELEM of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA, [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01). One must have provided beforehand for the definition of the trace of the beam cross-section in the mesh of the floor, with a named mesh-cell group. It is recommended to place the end node of the column at the geometric centre of gravity of the trace of the beam cross-section in the floor. One may consult the test case SDLX301 [V2.05.301](doc:v2.05.301): column–floor building.

Indeed, proceeding in this way makes it possible to respect as well as possible the assumptions of validity both of beam theory and of plate theory. Plate theory does not allow the application of a point force (this creates a singularity that is admittedly manageable in elasticity but that creates difficulties in the non-linear regime).

#### 3.6.4 3D–shell connection

It is generally recommended to extend the shell fictitiously into the 3D solid in order to ensure the kinematic conditions between the two parts.

#### 3.6.6 Modelling of non-linear connections (frame joints; damping device...) by means of discrete elements

One should consult the user's documentation on discrete elements [U2.02.03](doc:u2.02.03).

One may represent the behaviour of a column–beam, column–floor or other connection by discrete elements DIS_T (translation) and DIS_TR (translation and rotation) [U3.11.02](doc:u3.11.02) and by linear constitutive models (linear characteristics given in the operator AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01)) or non-linear constitutive behaviour (material parameters defined with the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), and called via the operand RELATION in the operator COMPORTEMENT[U4.51.11]).

In order to ensure seismic protection, it is proposed to place damping devices within the civil-engineering structure or between two buildings. These devices are designed to exert unidirectional forces on connections that one may consider as point connections. However, as their anchorage is in general distributed, one may take advantage of the various structural connections presented in the paragraphs above. One will choose two-node discrete elements in translation DIS_T. For example, one may use:

- DIS_ECRO_CINE [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17): makes it possible to model in a global manner (elastic limit, non-linear hardening, ultimate load) the non-linear behaviour of frame joints in reinforced-concrete or steel frames (beam–column connection, beam–beam, beam–floor, or even wall–floor over all the intersection nodes between the plate meshes of the wall and of the floor...), but assuming a total decoupling between points along the edge line. One may consult the test case SSND102 [V6.08.102](doc:v6.08.102).
- DIS_VISC [R5.03.17]: makes it possible to model dampers with unidirectional forces with a behaviour of non-linear generalised Zener type, with a power law. One may consult the test case SSND101 [V6.08.101](doc:v6.08.101).

These modellings can be used within a static analysis or a transient dynamic analysis on a "physical" basis.

It is also possible to refer directly to a special constitutive law assigned to a pair of linked nodes (localised non-linearities), within a transient dynamic analysis on a modal basis (operators DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03)). For example, one may use:

- DIS_VISC [R5.03.17]: dampers with unidirectional forces with a behaviour of non-linear generalised Zener type, with a power law. One may consult the test case SDND107 [V5.01.107](doc:v5.01.107).
- ANTI_SISM [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03): dampers with unidirectional forces with a non-linear coupled displacement–velocity behaviour. This law results from a formulation coming from reference [bib22]; one may consult the test case SDND120 [V5.01.120](doc:v5.01.120).

### 3.7 Other structural elements that can be modelled

#### 3.7.1 Modelling of a liner

The liner is a metal shell placed on the inner skin of the containment, guaranteeing leaktightness in the event of an accidental leak. In order to model it, two possibilities exist:

- when the concrete is modelled by solid elements (local 3D representation), the liner is modelled directly by a shell at the real position,
- when the concrete is modelled by shells (multilayer or global), the liner is modelled by a shell offset relative to the mid-surface of the concrete shell.

#### 3.7.2 Modelling of the prestress

The steel prestressing cables are tensioned in order to compress the concrete of the civil-engineering structure. The methodology for implementing the prestress in *Code_Aster* is not presented here. One should refer to [U2.03.06](doc:u2.03.06), which describes in detail the carrying out of a study with prestressing cables. One will simply note that one may use DEFI_CABLE_BP [U4.42.04](doc:u4.42.04) with either a 3D solid modelling or a shell modelling of DKT or DKTG (GLRC) type. Moreover, it is not necessary to make the nodes of the cables and the concrete nodes coincide. The command DEFI_CABLE_BP indeed also makes it possible to create kinematic connections that will link the nodes of the cable with the concrete nodes of the surrounding mesh cell. On the other hand, this generates a large number of Lagrange multipliers, which will make the analysis heavier. There is therefore a compromise to be found between ease of producing the mesh and cost of the analysis. The introduction of too large a number of kinematic relations may become problematic for a transient seismic study that is already costly in time.

### 3.8 Which type of modelling to adopt?

#### 3.8.1 Introduction

The choice of the finite-element modelling is closely linked to the constitutive models associated with it. It is therefore necessary to study the models that can be used (concrete in particular) for seismic studies before answering this question fully. In the following chapter 4, the steel and concrete models available in *Code_Aster* are described and a summary (Table 4) is drawn up of the various possible modelling strategies.

In order to choose a modelling suited to a given problem, it is necessary to answer the following questions:

- **what type of structure is one seeking to model?**
- beam, column / slab, wall,
- simple / complex geometry,
- **what is the size of the problem?**
- **what type of loading is imposed?**
- membrane (tension / compression),
- bending,
- shear,
- **what types of results is one seeking to analyse?**
- global quantities (displacements, support forces, floor spectra, …),
- local quantities (stresses in the concrete, strains in the steels, …).

The advantages and disadvantages associated with each type of modelling are presented below. These questions are answered for each type of modelling.

**Note**

*It is possible to connect different modellings to one another according to the zones of the structure (multilayer shells, global shells, multifibre beams, …).*

#### 3.8.2 Local modelling (solid elements)

**Advantages:**

- it makes it possible to represent finely complex geometries of the frame-joint type, or zones that one seeks to model with accuracy (including the whole of the longitudinal and transverse reinforcement),
- it makes it possible to represent all types of loading,
- it makes it possible to access the global and local quantities.

**Disadvantages:**

- it is more costly in CPU time and in memory size than the semi-global and global modellings,
- the mixing of solid elements (concrete) and bar elements (steel) can introduce singularities that are a source of non-convergence,
- delocalisation is not available with DYNA_NON_LINE (cf. § 4.4.4).

#### 3.8.3 Semi-global multilayer-shell modelling

**Advantages:**

- it is suited to the modelling of thin shell-type structures (slab and wall),
- it makes it possible to represent all types of loading,
- it makes it possible to reduce the size of the problem relative to a modelling with solid elements.

**Disadvantages:**

- it does not make it possible to represent the transverse reinforcement finely,
- certain features are not available for this type of modelling (large transformations, delocalisation (cf. § 4.4.4), …).

#### 3.8.4 Semi-global multifibre-beam modelling

**Advantages:**

- it is suited to the modelling of thin beam-type structures,
- it makes it possible to reduce the size of the problem relative to a modelling with solid elements,
- it is inexpensive in CPU time and in memory size,
- it can be combined with the use of other structural elements (multilayer shells, …).

**Disadvantages:**

- it does not make it possible to represent the transverse reinforcement,
- it is not suited to shear-type loadings,
- it is rather suited to relatively simple structures. However, one may consider simulating the global behaviour of relatively complex reinforced-concrete structures (see the CAMUS 2000 benchmark, § 9.2.3).

#### 3.8.5 Global-shell modelling

**Advantages:**

- it is suited to the modelling of thin shell-type structures (slab and wall),
- it is inexpensive in CPU time and in memory size,
- it can be combined with the use of other structural elements (multilayer shells, …).

**Disadvantages:**

- it does not make it possible to represent finely the mechanical phenomena and the local responses (stresses, …). The behaviour of the structure is "homogenised".
- the implementation of global constitutive models requires an identification of the parameters that may be difficult.

## 4 Choice of the constitutive model

### 4.1 Introduction

Concrete is a complex material made up of grains of very different scales: centimetres for the aggregates, millimetres for the sands, tens of microns for the cements… Each of these constituents has different mechanical properties, and the interfaces between constituents lead to significant heterogeneities in the material. Moreover, the placing of the concrete during construction is liable to generate non-uniform spatial distributions of the constituents.

Furthermore, although these phenomena are not taken into account here, it is important to note that concrete is a multiphase material (presence of water and of vapour in the interstices) and an ageing one. It undergoes over time phenomena of thermohydration, of drying and of creep, for example.

The sensitivity of the responses is notable according to the choice of the threshold parameters of the concrete, as well as of the post-peak behaviour. It is therefore important to calibrate as well as possible the parameters of the constitutive law, in particular in order to calibrate the value of the cracking moment.

The value of carrying out a cyclic bending–membrane response analysis of a reinforced-concrete element representative of the section (of the wall, of the slab…) before launching the study of the reinforced-concrete building is emphasised, in order to check that the parameters chosen are well suited.

#### 4.1.1 Experimental observations

**Tension**

The behaviour in tension is of brittle type. One observes an abrupt decrease in the stress when the tensile strength at failure is reached (Figure 4.1.1-a). The order of magnitude of the tensile strength is about 10 times smaller than that of the compressive strength. The behaviour is quasi-linear and reversible up to failure. The cracking develops in the direction orthogonal to the loading.

For the cyclic behaviour in tension, one observes:

- a loss of stiffness during the cycles (decrease in the elastic modulus in the event of reloading),
- an appearance of irreversible strains when one unloads from a non-linear state.

**Compression**

The behaviour of concrete in compression is of ductile type. Observation of the stress–strain curve in compression (Figure 4.1.1-b) makes it possible to distinguish 3 phases:

- up to stress levels reaching about 40% of the maximum stress at the peak (${\sigma}_{c}$ ), the behaviour is quasi-elastic;
- from 40 to 100% of ${\sigma}_{c}$ , the behaviour becomes progressively non-linear. In the vicinity of the peak the behaviour is strongly anelastic. The cracking develops in the direction parallel to the loading. One observes a phenomenon of volumetric dilation (increase in Poisson's ratio). In the event of unloading, irreversible strains appear;
- beyond the peak, the behaviour observed becomes softening: the post-peak slope becomes negative.

For the cyclic behaviour in compression, one observes:

- a loss of stiffness during the cycles (decrease in the elastic modulus in the event of reloading);
- an appearance of irreversible strains when one unloads from a non-linear state;
- hystereses of the loading–unloading cycles.

**Cyclic**

Observation of the stress–strain curve in cyclic tension–compression (Figure 4.1.1-c) highlights two important aspects:

- the asymmetry of the thresholds in tension and in compression (${\sigma}_{c}=10{\sigma}_{t}$  approximately);
- the closure of the cracks. Stiffness is recovered in compression when the cracks are closed (unilateral effect).

#### 4.1.2 Specific features of seismic studies

In seismic studies, it is first of all imperative to represent correctly the cracking of the concrete in tension:

- abrupt decrease in the post-peak stress,
- decrease in the unloading modulus,
- appearance of irreversible strains.

Given the cyclic nature of seismic loadings, it is then essential to take into account the unilateral aspect of concrete:

- asymmetry of the thresholds,
- closure of the cracks (recovery of stiffness).

Finally, depending on the stress level reached in compression in the study, it is necessary to represent correctly the ductile non-linear behaviour of concrete in compression:

- non-linear increase in the stress up to the peak, then softening,
- decrease in the unloading modulus,
- appearance of irreversible strains.

The models capable of representing (more or less accurately) these phenomena are the following:

- ENDO_ISOT_BETON [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04),
- ENDO_ORTH_BETON [R7.01.09](doc:r7.01.09),
- MAZARS_UNIL[R7.01.08],
- GLRC_DM[R7.01.32].

Other concrete models exist in *Code_Aster* but their use is not recommended. This is the case, for example, of the models of Mazars [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08), Double Drücker-Prager [R7.01.03](doc:r7.01.03) and BETON_REGLE_PR[U4.43.01] (non-linear elastic and therefore non-dissipative). These models do not make it possible to simulate the closure of the cracks and are for this reason not suited to the case of cyclic loading.

### 4.2 De Borst algorithm

Before detailing the various models available, it is recalled that there exists in *Code_Aster* a general method for integrating the non-linear 3D constitutive models in plane stress (Deborst method, [R5.03.03](doc:r5.03.03)). This consists in making the stress ${\sigma}_{zz}$ zero in the solution of the problem.

The De Borst method has furthermore been generalised to 1D models. A uniaxial-stress assumption is imposed (${\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{zz}=0$ ). As a result, the 3D constitutive models can be used for the multifibre-beam modellings.

The keyword RESI_CPLAN_RELA makes it possible to relax the convergence criterion of the method (checking of the plane-stress condition). It is advised, for seismic studies, to keep RESI_CPLAN_RELA at ${10}^{-6}$ (default value). Moreover, it is recommended to take a value of RESI_CPLAN_RELA identical to that of the relative residual on the equilibrium RESI_GLOB_RELA. If the convergence criterion is not satisfied, the analysis stops (or there is subdivision of the time step).

One may use the keyword ITER_CPLAN_MAXI in order to satisfy better the plane-stress condition. By default this parameter is set to 1 (a value sufficient for the non-softening models). If one observes convergence problems during the iterations due to the De Borst algorithm, it is recommended to increase the value of this parameter. One may take, for example, ‘ITER_CPLAN_MAXI’ equal to 5 or 10. If the convergence criterion is not satisfied after the defined number of iterations, the analysis stops (or there is subdivision of the time step).

The choice of the value of ITER_CPLAN_MAXI depends on the solution operator used in Newton's method (cf. § 7.2.3):

- if one uses the tangent matrix, one may first take the default value ITER_CPLAN_MAXI=1. In the event of non-convergence, one may test a higher value;
- if one uses the elastic matrix or the unloading matrix, it is recommended to take directly a higher value of ITER_CPLAN_MAXI (it is advised to perform between 5 and 10 iterations), otherwise the plane-stress condition is liable not to be satisfied.

#### 4.4.1 Model ENDO_ISOT_BETON

This three-dimensional model ([R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04) and Error: Reference source not found ]) is based on damage theory (Figure 4.4.1-a and Figure 4.4.1-b), the damage variable being scalar (isotropic damage). The main advantages of this model are its simplicity (two or three input parameters) and its thermodynamically based theoretical formulation.

**Determination of the parameters of the model:**

One has 3 parameters for the model ENDO_ISOT_BETON [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) in addition to the classical elastic parameters $E$ (Young's modulus) and $\nu$ (Poisson's ratio):

- SYT is the maximum stress in simple tension. The value of this parameter is a classical experimental datum for concrete. SYT is in general of the order of 1 to 4 MPa (Figure 4.4.1-a);
- D_SIGM_EPSI is the slope of the post-peak curve in tension (Figure 4.4.1-a). This parameter plays a predominant role in the response of the structure. It is necessary to use a sufficiently steep post-peak slope in order that the propagation of the damage be correctly represented by the analysis. However, the use of a realistic post-peak slope leads to significant convergence problems. Indeed, the more softening the behaviour of the material, the more difficult the convergence becomes. The value of this parameter is approximately -0.2 to -0.6 times Young's modulus. In practice, one must often reduce the value of this parameter in order not to have non-convergence of the analysis;
- SYC is the elastic limit in simple compression. It is recalled that the model ENDO_ISOT_BETON is quasi-linear in compression (no non-linearity in compression). The use of this optional parameter makes it possible to make the damage threshold dependent on the confinement [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04). For stresses lower than SYC, one has a realistic linear behaviour of the concrete. Beyond SYC (elastic limit), the model is no longer representative of the behaviour of the concrete. SYC is equal to about 10 times the peak tensile stress SYT, in absolute value. If one does not use this parameter, the elastic limit in compression is taken of the order of SYT, which is too low. The use of this parameter is therefore advised, all the more so as it does not a priori aggravate the convergence problems.

**Internal variables:**

- $\mathrm{V1}$ : value of the damage,
- $\mathrm{V2}$ : damage indicator (0 for the elastic regime (zero damage), 1 if damaged, 2 if failed (damage equal to 1)).

**ENDO_ISOT_BETON makes it possible to model:**

- the brittle behaviour of concrete in tension;
- the unilateral behaviour of concrete (closure of the cracks by a recovery of stiffness).

**ENDO_ISOT_BETON does not make it possible to model:**

- the non-linear behaviour in compression;
- the anisotropy of the material;
- the irreversible strains in compression and in tension;
- the hysteresis loops in loading–unloading.

#### 4.4.3 Model MAZARS

This model is based on damage theory [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08). Written in three dimensions, it is available in *Code_Aster* in 1D, 2D, 3D, AXIS, plane stresses. A variant of this model has been developed specifically for civil-engineering studies: MAZARS_UNIL. Two variables CRITSIG and CRITEPS correspond to the ELS and ELU criteria classically used in civil engineering.

- For the 1D behaviours (multifibre beams), a behaviour MAZARS_UNIL is associated with each concrete fibre of the beam. It corresponds to the 1D formulation of the unilateral Mazars model (Mu model). The main characteristics of this model are the following:

◦    use of two variables, one associated with the tensile damage, the other with the compressive damage.

◦    management of the opening of the cracks and of their closure by introducing a restoration of the damaged stiffness.

- For behaviours other than 1D, the model corresponds to that described in [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08), which cannot account for the closure of the cracks in the case of alternating loading.

**Determination of the parameters of the model:**

The parameters of the model must be identified from the classical material characteristics of the concrete. One should refer to [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08), which gives the elements necessary for the calibration of the parameters.

In practice, the use of the command DEFI_MATER_GC makes it possible to define rapidly the material parameters from the characteristics classically obtained during a compression test.

**MAZARS_UNIL** **in 1D** **(Mu model)** **makes it possible to model:**

- the brittle behaviour of concrete in tension;
- the non-linear behaviour in compression;
- the unilateral behaviour of concrete (closure of the cracks by a recovery of stiffness);
- the hysteresis loops in loading–unloading.

MAZARS_UNIL **in a modelling other than 1D:**

- the brittle behaviour of concrete in tension;
- the non-linear behaviour in compression;
- the unilateral behaviour of concrete (closure of the cracks by a recovery of stiffness);
- the hysteresis loops in loading–unloading.

#### 4.4.4 Localisation related to damage and non-local models

The local damage constitutive models ENDO_ISOT_BETON and ENDO_ORTH_BETON or MAZARS may lead to a localisation of the strains. One then observes a concentration of the damage in a band one mesh cell thick. Moreover, the energy dissipated by the damage becomes zero when the width of this band tends towards 0. The localisation phenomenon implies a dependence of the response of the structure on the mesh.

In order to be certain of the presence or otherwise of localisation, it is therefore advised, if one observes significant concentrations of damage, to repeat the analysis by remeshing more finely the suspect zone. If one finds that the damage is still concentrated on the first band of elements (reduction of the damaged zone), there is indeed a localisation problem.

Furthermore, methods of delocalisation of the constitutive models make it possible to avoid these problems of mesh dependence of the solution. There exists in *Code_Aster* a delocalisation method available for the models ENDO_ISOT_BETON and ENDO_ORTH_BETON: the modelling GRAD_VARI ([R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01)).

This non-local modelling is currently not available in *Code_Aster* in non-linear dynamics (DYNA_NON_LINE), because the inertia operator is not computed. Moreover, it is not available either for multilayer shells (only for the 3D or D_PLAN modellings).

#### 4.4.5 Model GLRC_DM

The model GLRC_DM[R7.01.32] is a "global" reinforced-concrete model based on a formulation by heuristic homogenisation for reinforced-concrete plates and shells. It is a damage model formulated in terms of generalised strain/stress relations (membrane extension, curvature, distortion and membrane forces, bending moments, shear forces). It has 2 coupled damage variables describing the damage by bending–extension (one for each face of the plate), and therefore the anisotropy induced by the damage. It can be used in thermomechanical situations (affine temperature distribution through the thickness of the reinforced-concrete section).

As one homogenises the behaviour of the concrete and of the steel, the global model is not softening. As a result, one avoids most of the non-convergence problems. Moreover, one thus avoids the problems of localisation of the damage. One should refer to [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32) for the detailed description of the model.

The parameters of the model are the following [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01):

**Determination of the parameters of the model:**

The four main parameters to be identified are:

- the elastic threshold SYT and the post-elastic slope in membrane GAMMA_T;
- the elastic threshold SYF and the post-elastic slope in bending GAMMA_F.

In order to recalibrate the model more finely, one may furthermore take a Young's modulus and a Poisson's ratio in bending (EF and NUF) different from those taken in membrane. Indeed, as reinforced concrete is not a homogeneous material, the effective value of EF may be different from E (apparent stiffness in bending). Consequently, the user is left the possibility of introducing a value of EF (under the keyword GLRC_DM) independent of E.

For the identification, two methodologies exist. The first identification methodology consists in recalibrating the GLRC_DM parameters relative to the model DKT - ENDO_ISOT_BETON (concrete) + GRILLE - VMIS_CINE_LINE (reinforcement bars) on an elementary test in tension–compression, then on an elementary test in bending.

*Tension–compression:*

It is advised to make use of the test case SSNS106A [V6.05.106](doc:v6.05.106), which treats the case of cyclic tension–compression in order to identify the parameters SYT and GAMMA_T. One obtains a response curve presented in Figure 4.4.5-a.

*Bending:*

It is advised to make use of the test case SSNS106B [V6.05.106](doc:v6.05.106), which treats the case of cyclic bending in order to identify the parameters SYF and GAMMA_F. One may furthermore possibly recalibrate the parameters EF and NUF (elastic parameters of the reinforced-concrete plate in bending). One obtains a response curve presented in Figure 4.4.5-b.

In order to recalibrate the parameters of the model GLRC_DM, the user may wish to retain, in the global approach relative to a more realistic modelling:

- the initial elastic stiffness (and therefore the initial natural frequency),
- the elastic limit,
- the energy dissipation (or damping) defined by the area of the response curve over one cycle,
- the maximum strain reached,
- the degradation of the stiffness.

- $V6$ : relative reduction of stiffness in compression
- $V7$ : relative reduction of stiffness in bending

In addition one introduces the "internal variables" $V8$ , $V9$ , $V10$ and $V11$ , in order to facilitate the post-processings on the steel reinforcement bars. These variables depend on the limit strains EPSI_ELS (ELS limit) and EPSI_LIM (ELU limit), which are supplied via DEFI_MATERIAU/GLRC_DM. See [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32).

One also introduces the "internal variables" $V12$ , $V13$ , $V14$ , $V15$ and $V16$ in order to access easily the extreme strains over time in tension and in compression in the concrete, on the upper face and on the lower face. See [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32).

Finally one introduces the "internal variables" $V17$ and $V18$ in order to assess the validity of the parameters that have been chosen.

#### 4.4.6 Model GLRC_DM coupled to elastoplasticity (VMIS_CINE_LINE)

In order to take into account the elastoplastic phenomenology of the behaviour and thus to represent better the hystereses of the cyclic response of a reinforced-concrete structure, *Code_Aster* has a numerical platform for coupling damage and elastoplastic models [22]. The model GLRC_DM is coupled with a von Mises model (VMIS_CINE_LINE) for the membrane part only (it is supplemented by an elastic model in bending) [R7.01.19](doc:r7.01.19).

To summarise, the constitutive model GLRC_DM + VMIS_CINE_LINE makes it possible to represent the damage in membrane–bending and the plasticity in membrane.

It is recommended to proceed step by step in the use of the model GLRC_DM coupled to membrane elastoplasticity: one will first carry out an analysis with the simple model GLRC_DM and then one will implement this more complex model.

In the operator DYNA_NON_LINE, one uses the operand RELATION_KIT. The keyword associated with the couplings of concrete constitutive behaviours is 'KIT_DDI'. This keyword makes it possible to add the two anelastic strain terms defined by the constitutive laws GLRC_DM and VMIS_CINE_LINE existing in COMPORTEMENT:

```text
COMPORTEMENT = _F( RELATION = ’KIT_DDI’

RELATION_KIT = (‘GLRC_DM’, ‘VMIS_CINE_LINE’))
```

### 4.5 Which type of constitutive model to adopt?

#### 4.5.1 Introduction

The choice of the constitutive model is closely linked to the modelling adopted. In Table 4, the various possible modelling strategies are summarised.

In order to choose the constitutive model suited to our problem, it is necessary to answer the following questions:

- **what is the maximum loading level that one wants to apply to the structure (damage level) and what is the robustness of the constitutive model of the concrete?**
- low damage: little risk of non-convergence;
- high damage: risks of non-convergence becoming high;
- **which mechanical phenomena does one want to represent?**
- damage of the concrete (tension / compression);
- damage of the concrete and plasticity of the steels and of the concrete.

The advantages and disadvantages associated with each type of constitutive model are presented below. These questions are answered for each type of model.

#### 4.5.2 Model ENDO_ISOT_BETON

**Advantages:**

- it is a simple 3D constitutive model of concrete,
- the identification of the parameters is immediate.

**Disadvantages:**

- one represents the damage of the concrete in tension only,
- one finds significant non-convergence problems when the damage becomes high.

**Note**

*The model* *ENDO_ISOT_BETON used in plane stress via the Deborst method for multilayer shells is in principle more robust than its three-dimensional version. In practice, however, one observes convergence problems when the damage becomes too high in the structure.*

#### 4.5.3 Model ENDO_ORTH_BETON

**Advantages:**

- it is a 3D model making it possible to represent finely the damage of the concrete in tension and in compression.

**Disadvantages:**

- one finds significant non-convergence problems when the damage becomes high,
- the determination of the parameters requires a relatively complex preliminary calibration.

**Note**

*The model* *ENDO_ORTH_BETON used in plane stress via the Deborst method for multilayer shells is in principle more robust than its three-dimensional version. In practice, however, one observes convergence problems when the damage becomes too high in the structure.*

#### 4.5.4 Model MAZARS_UNIL [R7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08)

**Advantages:**

- In 1D (used with the multifibre beams [R5.03.09](doc:r5.03.09)):

◦    as the model is written in 1D, the non-convergence problems are reduced in an important manner;

◦    it makes it possible to represent finely the mechanical phenomena in the longitudinal direction (tension–compression and bending); the model is capable of accounting for the phenomenon of closure of the cracks.

- In 2D, 3D, C_PLAN:

◦    makes it possible to represent the shear phenomena, and makes it possible to translate the phenomenon of closure of the cracks (restoration of stiffness).

#### 4.5.5 Model GLRC_DM [R7.01.32](doc:r7.01.32)

Number of parameters: steel: 3, concrete: 8, geometry: 5, method: 2. But these parameters are supplied in Code_Aster from "engineer" data and from assumptions on the post-elastic strain range.

**Advantages:**

- the softening behaviour of the concrete is no longer modelled. As a result, one avoids most of the non-convergence problems;
- likewise, as one homogenises the behaviour of the concrete and of the steel, one avoids the localisation problems;
- the model GLRC_DM + plasticity makes it possible to model the residual strains (in membrane only).

**Disadvantages:**

- the identification of the parameters requires a recalibration exercise by comparison with a more accurate model of ENDO_ISOT_BETON type.
- Steel reinforcement layers necessarily identical in x–y, upper–lower.
- no bending–membrane coupling

#### 4.5.6 Model DHRC [R7.01.37](doc:r7.01.37)

Number of parameters: steel: 3, concrete: 10, steel–concrete slip: 1, geometry: 11.

**Advantages:**

- Arbitrary steel reinforcement layers,
- Native anisotropy,
- Steel–concrete slip consecutive to damage, hence a contribution to the dissipation and modelling of the "tension-stiffening effect" phenomenon,
- native bending–membrane coupling
- Tool for automated identification by numerical FE homogenisation of parameters in Salomé_Méca from "engineer" data.

**Disadvantages:**

- CPU: more costly than GLRC_DM: 30 to 100%,
- avenues for improvement are expected on the tests for characterising the steel–concrete connection: at this stage of the state of the art, this restriction weakens the predictive capability of this modelling;
- Industrialisation and validation on representative structures (experimental campaigns) still to be completed.

## 5 Data setup of the problem

### 5.1 Mesh

#### 5.1.1 General advice

The discretisation of the mesh must be suited to the wavelength of the phenomena that one wishes to represent. A cut-off frequency and therefore an element size can be established by means of a prior modal analysis of the structure.

In many studies, the non-linear analyses are carried out following a linear study. In this case it is necessary to adapt the linear mesh by refining the zones that become non-linear, and also the zones where high levels of bending moment are liable to occur. In order to remesh, one may use the software HOMARD by means of the macro-command MACR_ADAP_MAIL [U7.03.01](doc:u7.03.01). It is in particular possible to remesh part of a mesh by simply indicating the mesh-cell group that one wants to refine.

It is strongly advised to carry out a study of the sensitivity of the results to the mesh. In practice, it is advised, when creating the mesh, to provide for several mesh refinements.

#### 5.1.2 Local solid modelling and multilayer shell

It is advised to make the steel and concrete nodes coincide in order to reduce the size of the problem. The elements BARRE, GRILLE_MEMBRANE or GRILLE_EXCENTRE representing the reinforcement bars must have their nodes coincident with those of the solid or shell elements of the concrete (cf. § 3.2.3 and 3.3.3).

Concerning the modelling of the connections, one should refer to § 3.6.

### 5.2 Initial conditions

The preloading of the reinforced-concrete structure (self-weight and other static loadings…) is necessary in the case of the use of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE. It is done by means of the operator STAT_NON_LINE by imposing only the loading due to the self-weight and to the other loadings. The dynamic loading is applied in continuation of the static self-weight analysis, while retaining this latter loading.

Furthermore, one may possibly take into account the construction sequence of the structure. Taking this sequence into account makes it possible to simulate a pre-cracking appearing during construction. Moreover it may be necessary, even in linear elasticity, in order to represent a state of non-zero initial stresses in the structure (obtained during construction by modification of the static redundancies).

These methods are currently little used for the analysis of civil-engineering works. It is nevertheless recommended to make use of the work carried out on the problem of excavation [U2.04.06](doc:u2.04.06) and of the layered construction of an embankment ([U2.04.07](doc:u2.04.07)).

### 5.3 Boundary conditions

The boundary conditions are to be defined in the classical manner, as in any study.

The seismic analysis of a civil-engineering work resting on a flexible foundation may nevertheless require the soil–structure interaction (ISS) to be taken into account. One should refer to [U2.06.07](doc:u2.06.07) for the description of the methods for taking the ISS into account. One may in particular represent the ISS by means of foundation impedances recalibrated about a linear behaviour of the structure founded on the soil.

### 5.4 Seismic loading

This paragraph takes up the elements detailed in [U2.06.09](doc:u2.06.09).

It is recalled that the structure is said to be single-supported if all the supports on which the structure rests are subjected to the same excitation. The structure is said to be multi-supported if there exist at least two supports that are not subjected to an identical seismic excitation.

#### 5.4.1 Single support

In this case, there are no stresses induced by the differential displacements of the anchorages. One therefore uses a relative-displacement approach and one applies either the accelerogram (case 1) or the displacement (case 2) to the structure (operator CALC_CHAR_SEISME, [U4.63.01](doc:u4.63.01)).

**1st case**

One has the seismic signal a(t), acceleration as a function of time in the three directions $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ – Use of CALC_CHAR_SEISME.

The construction of the "loading" concept used by AFFE_CHAR_MECA proceeds as follows:

- assembly of the assembled mass matrix ${M}_{\mathrm{ass}}$ : operator ASSEMBLAGE;
- construction of the assembled vectors (${V}_{{\mathrm{ass}}_{X}}$ , ${V}_{{\mathrm{ass}}_{Y}}$ and ${V}_{{\mathrm{ass}}_{Z}}$ ), serving as the basis of the loading (1 per direction of the earthquake): operator CALC_CHAR_SEISME with the keyword MONO_APPUI =’OUI’;
- construction of the "loading" concept (1 per direction of the earthquake): operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA with the keyword VECT_ASSE.

This case is the most common in studies of civil-engineering works under earthquake loading.

**2nd case**

One has the seismic signal $d(t)$ , displacement as a function of time in the three directions $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ .

The construction of the "loading" concept used by AFFE_CHAR_MECA is immediate: operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA with the keyword DDL_IMPO.

#### 5.4.2 Multi-support

In this case, the differential displacements of the anchorages induce secondary stresses. It is no longer possible to use a relative-displacement approach. One must therefore carry out the solution in the absolute frame. The loading must be imposed in the form of displacements at the anchorages of the structure.

Relative to the single-support analysis, the 1st method proposed is no longer suited. The use of CALC_CHAR_SEISME in multi-support and in transient non-linear analysis (DYNA_NON_LINE) supplies results whose validity is not guaranteed, because CALC_CHAR_SEISME operates by elastostatic recovery (static modes).

**1st case**

One has the seismic signal $a(t)$ , acceleration as a function of time in the three directions $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ – Use of the Fourier transforms.

It is necessary to construct $d(t)$ , displacement as a function of time, from $a(t)$ . The method proposed consists in using the Fourier transforms in order to integrate the signal:

- computation of the acceleration $a(\omega )$ as a function of the angular frequency: carrying out a Fourier transform of $a(t)$ in each direction;
- computation of the displacement $d(\omega )$ as a function of the angular frequency: multiplication of $a(\omega )$ by ${\omega}^{2}$ ;
- computation of the displacement $d(t)$ as a function of time: carrying out an inverse Fourier transform of $d(\omega )$ ;
- construction of the "loading" concept used by AFFE_CHAR_MECA: operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA with the keyword DDL_IMPO.

**Note**

*One should refer to*  [14] *for the precautions to be taken in carrying out the Fourier transforms: checking of the causality principle (return to zero of the signal in order to perform the FFT) and absence of drift of the displacements.*

**2nd case:**

One has the seismic signal $d(t)$ , displacement as a function of time in the three directions $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ .

The construction of the "load" concept used by AFFE_CHAR_MECA is immediate: operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA with the keyword DDL_IMPO.

#### 5.4.3 General precautions on the time signal

The time evolution as a function of time $a(t)$ or $d(t)$ used as input to the analysis must be as regular as possible. The time step that defines the sampling must therefore be sufficiently fine. If one calls ${f}_{\max}$ the maximum frequency of the spectrum of the signal, one must have a sampling frequency at least 5 to 10 times greater than ${f}_{\max}$ .

Initially, the structure being considered as subjected only to the permanent loadings (weight, ...), it is better to have a displacement, a velocity and an acceleration induced by the earthquake imposed as zero at the supports. If one does not respect these conditions, one risks observing initial oscillations of the response. In practice, in order to respect this, it is sufficient that the seismic signal imposed at the supports in displacement be zero over at least the first two time steps. It will therefore be appropriate to add manually zero values at the beginning of the loading files.

At the end of the analysis, it is recommended also to return to imposed zero displacement conditions over a certain period. This makes it possible to ensure the return to rest of the structure. This is indispensable (causality condition) if one wants to carry out a correct spectral analysis by FFT of the responses.

**Note**

*The ASN guide*  [15] *recommends using a set of* $N$ *accelerograms (* $N=5$ *minimum) representative of the design seismic motion for the design of civil-engineering works. One therefore carries out* $N$ *non-linear transient analyses in order to analyse the variability of the results as a function of the accelerograms used. One defines the quantity to be retained for the design as a function of the number of accelerograms used, of the mean and of the standard deviation of the absolute values of the results (one uses for this a confidence-interval estimator of Student–Fisher type,*  [15] *,* *of the type mean of the non-linear transient responses + k.sigma, with k depending on the number* $N$ *of realisations (5, 10…).). Certain quantities of interest are more sensitive than others to the variabilities;* *moreover the* *non-linearities may be localised and large parts of the structure may remain in the linear regime.* *One notes that within the linear framework,* *the ASN guide*  [15] *recommends using a simple average of the transient responses.*

### 5.5 Damping

#### 5.5.1 Definition

It is recommended to use Rayleigh damping [R5.05.04](doc:r5.05.04) and [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05) in the direct transient solution by means of DYNA_NON_LINE.

The elementary damping matrix ${C}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ is expressed as a linear combination of the elementary mass matrices ${M}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ and stiffness matrices ${K}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ :

${C}_{\mathrm{elem}}=\alpha {K}_{\mathrm{elem}}+\beta {M}_{\mathrm{elem}}$

with $\alpha$ and $\beta$  the Rayleigh coefficients. The part $\beta {M}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ corresponds to the low-frequency damping and the part $\alpha {K}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ to the high-frequency damping (Figure 5.5.1-a).

The damping ratio ${\zeta}_{n}$ for the nth mode of the system considered is defined by:

${\zeta}_{n}=\frac{\alpha {\omega}_{n}}{2}+\frac{\beta}{2{\omega}_{n}}$

with the angular frequency ${\omega}_{n}$ associated with the nth mode.

This Rayleigh damping is calibrated on two modes and damping ratios $({\omega}_{1,}{\zeta}_{1})$ and $({\omega}_{2,}{\zeta}_{2})$ relevant for the analysis of the structure studied. When one knows the values of the dampings for these two frequencies, one can compute $\alpha$ and $\beta$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}\alpha & =& 2\frac{{\omega}_{1}}{{\omega}_{1}^{2}-{\omega}_{2}^{2}}{\zeta}_{1}-2\frac{{\omega}_{2}}{{\omega}_{1}^{2}-{\omega}_{2}^{2}}{\zeta}_{2}\\ \beta & =& 2\frac{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}^{2}}{{\omega}_{2}^{2}-{\omega}_{1}^{2}}{\zeta}_{1}-2\frac{{\omega}_{1}^{2}{\omega}_{2}}{{\omega}_{2}^{2}-{\omega}_{1}^{2}}{\zeta}_{2}\end{array}$

In practice, one often considers the case where the two dampings are equal ${\zeta}_{1}={\zeta}_{2}=\zeta$ , and one then obtains the formulae to be used for the computation of $\alpha$ *and* $\beta$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}\alpha & =& \frac{2}{{\omega}_{1}+{\omega}_{2}}\zeta \\ \beta & =& 2\frac{{\omega}_{1}{\omega}_{2}}{{\omega}_{1}+{\omega}_{2}}\zeta \end{array}$

It is then appropriate to enter the values $\alpha$ *and* $\beta$ by means of the command DEFI_MATERIAU, keywords AMOR_ALPHA/AMOR_BETA.

Figure 5.5.1-a presents the graphical description of Rayleigh damping for two frequencies $({f}_{1,}\zeta )$ and $({f}_{2,}\zeta )$ between which the damping is relatively uniform.

#### 5.5.2 Non-linear case

For the computation of ${C}_{\mathrm{elem}}$ , one takes the stiffness matrix used in Newton's method (RIGI_MECA). If one uses the updated tangent matrix ${K}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{\tan}$ in DYNA_NON_LINE (NEWTON=_F(MATRICE='TANGENTE', REAC_ITER=1,),), the damping matrix is then defined by:

${C}_{\mathrm{elem}}=\alpha {K}_{\mathrm{elem}}^{\tan}+\beta {M}_{\mathrm{elem}}$

The damping matrix $C$ then does not remain constant during the analysis. Indeed, when the structure becomes strongly damaged, ${K}^{\tan}$ decreases and may even become negative. As a result, the interpretation of the effect of proportional damping in the non-linear regime is delicate (variation of the dissipation introduced over time).

The user sets the damping parameters over the range in which the structure is elastic.

#### 5.5.3 Other advice in the non-linear regime

In the non-linear regime, part of the dissipative phenomena are modelled by means of the non-linear constitutive models of concrete and of steel. However, other dissipative phenomena are not modelled by the constitutive behaviours of the materials, which nevertheless dissipate the energy supplied by the excitation throughout the transient and at every material point. One therefore introduces, as in a linear elastic analysis, a conventional viscous damping matrix in the analysis in order to represent these phenomena, cf. [33].

One may choose with *Code_Aster* a damping matrix of Rayleigh type or a modal damping matrix, cf.
[u2.06.03](doc:u2.06.03).

It is recommended to use a maximum damping value of 2% on the first mode, and of 5% at most for the cut-off frequency, in order to comply with the prescriptions of the ASN guide [15]). This value is much lower than that of 5 to 7% used in the linear regime for design analyses. It leads in general to overestimating the peaks of the floor spectra, including when the loading exceeds the design earthquake several times over, cf. [18].

It should be noted that in multi-support (cf. 5.4.2) the solution of the problem is carried out in the absolute frame (${U}_{\mathrm{absolu}}={U}_{\mathrm{relatif}}+{U}_{\mathrm{entraînement}}$ ). In this case, it is indispensable not to introduce damping on the part corresponding to the entrainment velocity. The damping matrix must be taken proportional to the stiffness matrix only (${C}_{\mathrm{elem}}=\alpha {K}_{\mathrm{elem}}$  in the method recommended by the ASN [15]). The choice of the modal damping matrix, by specifying a zero damping value on the rigid modes, is also suitable. Another possibility consists in clamping one of the supports and in describing the motion of the other supports relative to it (this amounts to cancelling the entrainment motion). In this case, one may use a classical Rayleigh damping.

For the discrete elements DIS_T or DIS_TR [U3.11.02](doc:u3.11.02), one defines directly the damping matrix for each degree of freedom in AFFE_CARA_ELEM (keyword DISCRET [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01)). This remark is important in the case where one models added masses by means of discrete elements. It is then indispensable to associate a damping matrix with them, otherwise these added masses are not damped.

It is important to produce the balance of the energies during the transient over the whole of the system: by means of the keyword ENERGIE of DYNA_NON_LINE. This makes it possible to check the distribution of the kinetic energy, of the strain energy, of that supplied to the structure by the loading at each time instant, of that dissipated in viscous form, of that (called hysteretic) dissipated by the non-linear constitutive models. The remainder of the balance is to be assigned to the time-integration scheme, if the latter is dissipative.

When one uses an explicit time-integration scheme (DIFF_CENT or TCHAMWA), it is imperative to use a damping proportional to the mass matrix only. The use of a complete Rayleigh damping leads to a drop in the stability time step and therefore to a very significant increase in the computation time.

## 6 Analyses prior to the non-linear transient dynamic analysis

Before carrying out the non-linear transient analysis, it is advised to proceed step by step by increasing the "difficulties". This makes it possible to validate independently the various elements of the modelling.

### 6.1 Analysis of the mass

It is advised, after having carried out the finite-element modelling, to compute the mass of the structure in order to ensure that the geometric and material characteristics (densities) are correct. This is all the more important as the structure is complex and as the number of different materials is high.

One uses the operator POST_ELEM [U4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22) and the keyword MASSE_INER.

### 6.2 Modal analysis

It is recommended, before carrying out a transient analysis, to carry out a modal analysis of the structure. This makes it possible to check the quality of the modelling (global stiffness, boundary conditions). If, moreover, one has the first natural frequencies measured experimentally, one will be able to compare the finite-element modelling with the real structure and possibly recalibrate the finite-element modelling.

The computation of the eigenmodes of a structure is not detailed in this document. For this, one should refer among others to [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02) (operator CALC_MODES).

It is nevertheless recalled that, for the seismic analysis of a linear structure, one must at least retain all the modes whose natural frequencies are lower than the cut-off frequency of the seismic spectrum (generally of the order of $33\mathrm{Hz}$ , beyond which there is no significant dynamic amplification). In practice, one often contents oneself with retaining only the modes that contribute significantly to the response. One then retains only the modes whose unit effective mass in one direction is greater than 1‰. A second selection criterion consists in also ensuring that, for all of these retained modes, the cumulative unit effective mass in each direction is little different from the total mass of the structure. An acceptability threshold of 95% is commonly accepted for the spectral method; a threshold of 90% is indicated for the transient method. If this second criterion is not satisfied with the modes lower than $33\mathit{Hz}$ , one extends the modal basis beyond this frequency until this threshold is reached.

The sum of the effective modal masses is in fact equal to the total mass that works on the modal basis chosen. In other words, this total working mass is equal to the total mass minus the mass contributions that are carried by clamped degrees of freedom (which therefore do not work on the modal basis). Thus, for example, on a single-degree-of-freedom mass–spring system with a mass ${M}_{1}$ at the top and another mass ${M}_{2}$ at the level of the raft, the working mass will be equal to ${M}_{1}$ and the total mass to ${M}_{1}+{M}_{2}$ . Consequently, the unit effective modal mass for the single mode of the system will be equal to . The total cumulative value will therefore have the same value and, depending on the ratio of ${M}_{1}$ and ${M}_{2}$ , one will therefore not necessarily be able to reach 90% of the total mass ${M}_{1}+{M}_{2}$ , even by considering all the modes (one has only a single mode in this example). In practice, the finer and more realistic the FE model is, the smaller the difference between the working mass and the total mass will be.

One must therefore pay attention to the particular cases where a non-negligible part of the mass is concentrated in this zone of blocked degrees of freedom. Indeed, if this is the case, the dynamic modes computed will never make this zone work and therefore the cumulative effective modal mass will never be able to reach 100% of the total mass (which takes into account the mass at the level of the blocked degrees of freedom).

One will therefore tend to take too many modes, which is conservative, but at the expense of the CPU time.

In order to control this, one must compute the total mass excluding the blocked degrees of freedom: one can do this with POST_ELEM by specifying a GROUP_MA that would contain the whole building except the blocked surface.

Then, it will suffice to apply a rule of three between the cumulative effective mass and the ratio between the total mass and the total mass on the blocked degrees of freedom.

Example: $\text{Cumul\_corrigé\_unitaire}=\text{Cumul\_unitaire}\times \text{Mtot / Mtot\_sans\_DDL\_bloqués}$

With meshes of solid elements, the underestimation of the cumulative value is small and will decrease as one refines further. With surface meshes of a raft or, worse, with a point mass at the raft, the underestimation may become significant.

In the field of reinforced-concrete structures, one generally finds that the modellings have a global stiffness higher than that measured experimentally. The first natural frequency computed may overestimate the measured frequency by more than 20%. Various phenomena may be put forward in order to explain this difference:

- boundary conditions (clamping on the shaking table for a mock-up), the soil–structure interaction (interface dynamic stiffness) and connections (slab–wall built-in connection) that are imperfect under the experimental conditions;
- a significant heterogeneity of the concrete material and a possible debonding between the reinforcement bars and the concrete under the experimental conditions, leading to variations of Young's modulus in the structure;
- initial geometric imperfections.

In order to recalibrate the natural frequencies, several methods are generally used:

- the reduction of Young's modulus of the concrete (the reduction relative to the experimental value of Young's modulus possibly reaching more than 20%). This artificial recalibration leads to a decrease in all of the following natural frequencies. The following modes are therefore poorly represented by the modelling;
- the modification of the boundary conditions. Taking into account non-perfect clampings between the structure and the support frame or between the various structural elements (wall–floor connection, for example).

These more or less conventional modifications are not very satisfactory. The impossibility of modelling properly the eigenmodes of a reinforced-concrete structure has important consequences for the ability of a numerical seismic simulation to predict design margins.

### 6.3 Linear elastic transient dynamic analysis

It is recommended, before carrying out a non-linear transient analysis, to carry out a linear elastic transient analysis. For this, it is sufficient, starting from the command file of the non-linear transient analysis DYNA_NON_LINE, to define elastic materials: COMPORTEMENT (RELATION = ’ELAS’).

This makes it possible to see, for example, whether the damping introduced is indeed taken into account for all the finite elements of the modelling.

### 6.4 Other analyses

#### 6.4.1 Push-over analysis (monotonic non-linear quasi-static)

This consists in applying to the structure a monotonically increasing displacement in order to load it in a manner equivalent to a seismic loading (push-over or progressive pushover). This loading corresponds to a distributed inertia force, corresponding to the mass distribution of a chosen vibratory mode, often the fundamental mode. One may also choose to take, in place of this mode, the static solution under the action of a uniform entrainment acceleration in the structure, in the direction of the seismic motion.

One loads the structure until the creation of the cracks and the plastification of the reinforcement bars which, at the extreme, may lead to the ruin of the structure. This loading case constitutes a sort of envelope curve of the cyclic static non-linear response (Figure 6.4.2-a).

The main advantage of this analysis is to analyse the behaviour of the structure for high levels of non-linearity (damage), which makes it possible to apprehend a certain redistribution of the forces, the ductility of the structure – its capacity to dissipate mechanical energy. One may also highlight possible convergence problems in the concrete constitutive model used. The computational effort is therefore much smaller than for a true non-linear transient dynamic analysis.

This analysis requires, on the other hand, that one be able to define a relevant push-over type loading. Moreover, its implementation may be costly in time.

Once the non-linear static analysis has been carried out, one represents on a diagram the dependence of a characteristic displacement at a given control point (for example the displacement of the centre of gravity of the roof of the building) as a function of a characteristic force (for example the global shear force at the base), in the direction of the earthquake. One then considers this response as being that of an equivalent non-linear single-degree-of-freedom oscillator. One first converts the characteristic displacement into a spectral displacement for the mode considered at the given control point. Then one converts the global force at the base into a spectral pseudo-acceleration, by means of the effective modal mass of the chosen mode. This conversion of the capacity curve produces a *capacity spectrum*, which one often agrees to smooth in the form of perfect elastoplasticity, with a plateau.

One may therefore then compare this capacity spectrum with the response spectrum of the seismic signal, represented in the form of a *demand curve* in the form of a pseudo-acceleration–displacement diagram obtained from the seismic response spectrum. The response spectrum is plotted for a value of reduced damping compatible with the strain level reached in the non-linear regime. The comparison then makes it possible to determine the *performance point* of the structure studied by intersections of the capacity and demand curves, for the value of the reference angular frequency at this performance point; the latter is thus established in an iterative manner.

Of course this method, which assumes that the mode studied is predominant, completely neglects the combination due to the coupling of the dynamic modes of the structure, their variation during the damage, the effects of cycling and of unloading… Moreover, the result of this method depends on the choice of the control point in the structure, which affects the capacity spectrum. There exists no criterion making it possible to estimate the error committed by this simplified method.

#### 6.4.2 Cyclic static analysis

One may carry out a cyclic static non-linear analysis by applying at the supports of the structure the imposed displacement coming from the seismic signal (Figure 6.4.2-a).

The main advantage of this analysis is to analyse the behaviour of the structure for levels of non-linearity (damage) close to those encountered within the framework of the non-linear dynamic analysis, and to highlight possible convergence problems in the concrete models used.

This analysis requires that one have this time evolution in displacement (cf. § 5.4). Moreover, its implementation may be costly in time.

## 7 Solution of the non-linear transient dynamic analysis

### 7.1 Introduction

In this chapter, one places oneself within the framework of an analysis in small perturbations, without impact. The only non-linearities are of material origin. This corresponds to the most frequent civil-engineering studies. One could nevertheless model geometric non-linearities (large displacements, large strains) or frictional contact.

All of the keywords cited in this chapter are attached to the operator DYNA_NON_LINE. One should refer to [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) and [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) for more details.

### 7.2 Help with the convergence of the analysis

#### 7.2.1 Time evolution of the loading

The command DEFI_LIST_INST makes it possible to carry out an automatic subdivision of the time step when Newton's algorithm does not converge.

Furthermore, it is recalled that it is necessary, for a given analysis, to test several time steps in order to analyse the stability of the results as a function of the time step chosen (cf. § 2.3).

#### 7.2.2 Convergence criteria

The keyword CONVERGENCE makes it possible to define the value of the relative residual on the equilibrium (RESI_GLOB_RELA). By default, this is ${10}^{-6}$ . In order to facilitate the convergence of the analysis, one may relax this convergence criterion. One must nevertheless do so with caution, the use of too large a convergence criterion being liable to lead to results far from the real solution. It is advised not to use a RESI_GLOB_RELA greater than ${10}^{-4}$ .

There furthermore exists an absolute residual on the equilibrium (RESI_GLOB_MAXI). Initially, this criterion is used when the loading and the support reactions become zero (for example in the case of a total unloading). In this case, one passes automatically from the relative criterion to the absolute criterion.

When one uses a damage constitutive model, one observes in certain cases a divergence of the absolute residual whereas the relative residual remains small. This is why it is advised, during the analyses, to check that RESI_GLOB_MAXI remains small. If it reaches high values, it is recommended to repeat the analysis by imposing a maximum value on the global residual by defining a RESI_GLOB_MAXI for the analysis. It is advised to use the default value of ${10}^{-6}$ .

Under ITER_GLOB_MAXI, one may modify the maximum number of iterations performed in order to solve the global problem at each time instant (10 by default). If one sees that, during the Newton iterations, the convergence of the constitutive model is slow, one may increase the value of this parameter.

Under ITER_GLOB_ELAS, one may modify the maximum number of iterations performed with the unloading matrix when one uses the keyword PAS_MINI_ELAS of the factor keyword NEWTON (see § 7.2.3) in order to solve the global problem at each time instant (25 by default).

#### 7.2.3 Newton's algorithm

The keyword NEWTON makes it possible to specify the matrix used for the global iterations of Newton's method (Figure 7.2.3-a). One may use either the elastic matrix (MATRICE=’ELASTIQUE’), or the tangent matrix (MATRICE=’TANGENTE’). Moreover, in this latter case one may pass automatically from the tangent matrix to the unloading matrix when the time step is, or becomes (through the subdivision), smaller than a minimum step (PAS_MINI_ELAS). For the damage models the unloading matrix is identified with the secant matrix (Figure 7.2.3-a). This option may be useful when the automatic subdivision of the time step is not sufficient to make an analysis converge. For the damage models (concrete), the tangent matrix may become singular and it may be preferable to use the unloading matrix in order to converge.

As a first step, it is recommended to use the tangent matrix. In order to optimise the convergence, it is strongly advised to update this tangent matrix as often as possible, the best strategy being, if the size of the problem allows it, to update it at every iteration (MATRICE=’TANGENTE’, REAC_ITER= 1, [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03)). This is all the more advised if one has a modelling using the De Borst method (cf. 4.2).

If convergence problems appear (singular tangent matrix), one may choose to switch to the unloading matrix (activation of the keyword PAS_MINI_ELAS). As the convergence with the unloading matrix is slower than that with the tangent matrix, the keyword ITER_GLOB_ELAS (cf. § 7.2.2) makes it possible to define a maximum number of iterations specific to the use of the unloading matrix. For the damage constitutive models, the unloading matrix (which corresponds to the secant matrix) depends on the strain state reached. It is therefore necessary to update this matrix by means of the keyword REAC_ITER_ELAS.

In the case where the convergence problems persist, it may be of interest to carry out an analysis with the elastic matrix in order to see how the Newton iterations proceed in this case.

**Note**

The line search theoretically makes it possible to improve the convergence of Newton's method [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01). However, it is not operational with the operator *DYNA_NON_LINE.*

#### 7.2.4 Material non-linearities

The keyword COMPORTEMENT makes it possible to define the non-linear constitutive models implemented in DYNA_NON_LINE. In the event of non-convergence of the analysis, one may be led to modify the parameters associated with the De Borst algorithm (cf. § 4.2, for a multilayer-shell analysis).

#### 7.2.5 Time-integration scheme

DYNA_NON_LINE has various implicit time schemes (NEWMARK and HHT) and explicit ones (DIFF_CENT and TCHAMWA) described briefly in § 2.2.

In seismic studies, it is recommended to use the implicit average-acceleration method, because it does not introduce artificial numerical damping: SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='NEWMARK', FORMULATION='DEPLACEMENT', ALPHA=0.25, DELTA=0.5,).

If one observes parasitic high-frequency oscillations in the response of the structure, it is advised to use an HHT scheme, which makes it possible to introduce a significant numerical damping for the high frequencies with almost no impact on the low frequencies: SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='HHT', FORMULATION='DEPLACEMENT', ALPHA= alph, MODI_EQUI=‘OUI’,). By default, it is advised to take ALPHA= -0.3. One will note that the numerical damping is proportional to the time step, cf. [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05); the introduction of a time-step subdivision therefore reduces the numerical damping.

In the event of persistent non-convergence and if all the other methods of help with convergence have been tested, one may try to continue the analysis by using the explicit central-difference scheme: SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='DIFF_CENT', FORMULATION='ACCELERATION',).

It is nevertheless recommended to opt for this explicit scheme with the greatest caution. Indeed, such an analysis has important specific features, in particular as regards the time step of the analysis, which must satisfy the CFL condition [5] of time is in general of the order of 10-5 to 10-6 second.

This analysis strategy will be more relevant when *Code_Aster* has a method for switching from an implicit time scheme to an explicit one (and the converse). This method will in due course make it possible to change time-integration scheme during the solution of a non-linear transient dynamic problem. When the structure is in a phase of relatively regular evolution one uses an implicit scheme, which makes it possible to have relatively large time steps. Then, during the phases strongly disturbed by the damage of the concrete, one switches to an explicit scheme which could make it possible to get past the difficulties (snap-back). One may then switch back to an implicit scheme if the remainder of the response becomes more regular again.

### 7.3 Chaining of the analyses

Starting from the static solution under self-weight, one carries out the seismic analyses one after another. The final state of the preceding analysis $(n)$ serves as the initial state of the following analysis $(n+1)$ :

ETAT_INIT=_F( CRITERE= 'RELATIF', EVOL_NOLI = chargement de pesanteur,).

Before continuing the analysis $n+1$ , it is necessary to check that the initial state supplied by analysis n does indeed correspond to the structure at rest: the acceleration field must be very small throughout the structure. When the seismic loading applied ends with a weak phase that does indeed allow the structure to return to rest (signal not truncated), one may chain the analyses directly.

Otherwise, in order to make the acceleration field zero throughout the structure at the end of analysis n (elimination of a parasitic acceleration field), it is advised to bring the structure back to the complete state of rest by applying to the structure a high fictitious damping over a small time interval (a few tenths of a second, for example). For this, it is necessary to create fictitious materials possessing a high elementary damping. Following the seismic analysis, one carries out another dynamic analysis (DYNA_NON_LINE) with the static loading only and using the materials with high damping. At the end of this analysis the acceleration field is necessarily quasi-zero and one may use it as the initial state of the following analysis $(n+1)$ .

## 8 Post-processing

### 8.1 Analysis of the global behaviour of the structure

#### 8.1.1 Maximum displacements and forces

The analysis of the evolution of the displacements and of the reactions at the supports (shear forces, normal forces and global bending moments) supplies important elements for apprehending the global behaviour of a structure.

**Notes**

*It is practical to use the keyword* *OBSERVATION in order to save all of the results for a given point and not for the whole structure.*

#### 8.1.2 Determination of the oscillator spectrum

The determination of the oscillator spectrum of an accelerogram $a(t)$ is available via the operator CALC_FONCTION[U6.62.04] with the keyword SPEC_OSCI: it is obtained by numerical integration of Duhamel's equation by the method of Nigam [R5.05.01](doc:r5.05.01). This command supplies the absolute pseudo-acceleration spectrum and, on request, the pseudo-velocity spectrum or the relative displacement spectrum.

In general, the user must always check that the frequency range used for the computation of the oscillator spectrum is consistent with the frequency content of the input signal. This is all the more crucial if one seeks to obtain, for example, the asymptotic value of the pseudo-acceleration. In certain cases, the default frequency list of the keyword SPEC_OSCI may be too restrictive.

#### 8.1.3 Natural frequencies

The analysis of the evolution of the natural frequencies of a structure during loading makes it possible to have a good indication of its state of damage. The cracking phenomena decrease the global stiffness of the structure and one observes a drop in frequency. If one determines the natural frequencies and the damping of the damaged structure, one may then carry out a spectral seismic response analysis on it.

The determination of the natural frequencies of the damaged structure is possible by means of the keyword MODE_VIBR of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01). This is a method making it possible to carry out a vibratory modal analysis (natural frequencies and associated modes) on the global stiffness and mass matrices of DYNA_NON_LINE. As one must compute the natural frequencies of the structure for a damaged elastic stiffness matrix, it is necessary to have returned the structure to rest before carrying out the computation of the natural frequencies by the operator MODE_VIBR. One should refer to the test case SDNV106 [V5.03.106](doc:v5.03.106) for more details.

Another possibility consists in carrying out, after the seismic loading and after the return of the structure to rest, a "white noise" type loading at a low level of excitation by means of *Code_Aster*. One then carries out a Fourier analysis of the response obtained in order to determine the natural frequencies of the damaged structure.

#### 8.1.4 Balance of dissipated energies

The computation of the energies over all or part of the structure is done by the operator POST_ELEM
[u4.81.22](doc:u4.81.22): the kinetic energy, the elastic strain energy, the total strain energy, the work of the external forces, the dissipation energy for the plate elements of the DKTG family with the global reinforced-concrete constitutive laws GLRC_DM and DHRC. However, the viscous damping energy is not currently computed, nor the strain energy for softening laws such as ENDO_ISOT_BETON. Moreover, the computation of the work of the external forces is carried out from part of the nodal forces and does not take into account the inertia or damping forces.

The energy balance is therefore to be carried out with caution.

One may also, with the keyword ENERGIE of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE, compute the energy balance over the whole structure, including the viscous damping energy. It is stored in the table of name PARA_CALC. It is extracted with the command RECU_TABLE [U4.71.02](doc:u4.71.02).

### 8.2 Analysis of the local behaviour of the structure

#### 8.2.1 Introduction

The options for computing fields cited in this chapter are defined in CALC_CHAMP [U4.81.04](doc:u4.81.04).

**Concrete**

The analysis of the contour maps of damage makes it possible to apprehend the local behaviour of a structure. One may determine the maximum levels of stress and of cracking reached at certain points of the structure.

**Note**

*If one observes that only a few finite elements are very strongly damaged, it may be a phenomenon of localisation of the damage due to the softening constitutive model used. This phenomenon leads to a mesh dependence of the response. In order to determine whether there is localisation or not, it is recommended to repeat the analysis by remeshing more finely the suspect zone. If one observes that the damage again takes place on a single band of mesh cells (reduction of the damaged zone), it is because one has localisation.*

*It should be noted that the models for delocalisation of the constitutive laws by regularisation of the strain are not yet currently available in non-linear dynamics in Code_Aster (cf. §*  4.4.4 *).*

**Reinforcement**

The analysis of the contour maps of the cumulative plastic strains in the reinforcement bars makes it possible to determine the level of plastification reached in the steels.

#### 8.2.2 Local modelling (solid elements)

There is no specific feature related to the post-processing in this case. It is therefore not detailed here.

**Concrete**

One computes directly the stresses at the nodes by element SIGM_ELNO.

**Reinforcement**

Likewise, the membrane grid elements (GRILLE_MEMBRANE) or the bar elements (BARRE) are post-processed by computing the associated stresses SIGM_ELNO.

**Note**

*Under HPP, one computes directly the strains at the nodes by element* *EPSI_ELNO from the displacements.*

#### 8.2.3 Multilayer plate and shell elements

**Concrete**

For the multilayer shells, one post-processes the stresses by element at the nodes (SIGM_ELNO) from the stresses at the integration points of each layer (SIEF_ELGA) computed during a non-linear analysis. If one wishes to obtain these stresses in a particular layer and position, one must use the operator POST_CHAMP.

These stresses are computed in the local frame of the shell defined by the user in the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM. One may post-process in the same manner the internal variables (VARI_ELNO).

**Reinforcement**

The offset grid elements (GRILLE_EXCENTRE) or the bar elements (BARRE) are post-processed simply by computing the associated stresses SIGM_ELNO.

#### 8.2.4 Multifibre beam elements

For the multifibre beams, one does not currently have an option for computing the stresses in the fibres directly in CALC_CHAMP. One may nevertheless recover the stresses in the fibres by using the operands for localisation of a field in the operator RECU_FONCTION:

```text
/ NOEUD = no, [noeud]

/ GROUP_NO = grno, [gr_noeud]

/ MAILLE = ma, [maille]

/ GROUP_MA = grma, [gr_maille]

/ POINT = nupoint,[I]

/ SOUS_POINT = nusp, [I]
```

The integer nupoint specifies the number of the Gauss point whose value one wants to recover (case of the cham_elem "at the GAUSS points").

The integer nusp specifies the number of the sub-point, that is to say of the fibre, on which one reads the stresses (cf. the mesh of the cross-section § 3.4).

One may thus analyse the stresses and the strains in the concrete fibres and in the steel fibres.

#### 8.2.5 "Global" plate and shell elements

In this modelling, one does not have directly the stresses and the strains through the thickness of the shell. It is nevertheless possible to reconstruct the strain field (but not the stress field) for a given through-thickness position from the fields of generalised forces and strains.

As the strain field is considered linear in the section, one has:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{x}(z)={\varepsilon}_{x}^{\mathrm{tot}}+z{\kappa}_{x}^{\mathrm{tot}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{y}(z)={\varepsilon}_{y}^{\mathrm{tot}}+z{\kappa}_{y}^{\mathrm{tot}}\end{array}$

with ${\varepsilon}_{x}^{\mathrm{tot}}$ , ${\varepsilon}_{y}^{\mathrm{tot}}$ , ${\kappa}_{x}^{\mathrm{tot}}$ and ${\kappa}_{y}^{\mathrm{tot}}$ the generalised strains and ${\varepsilon}_{x}(z)$ and ${\varepsilon}_{y}(z)$ the strains through the thickness of the global shell.

#### 8.2.6 Discrete elements carrying linear or non-linear constitutive laws

In this modelling, one has directly the displacements (or even rotations) of the end nodes of the discrete elements, but also the forces passing through them. Moreover, in the case of a non-linear constitutive law, *cf.* [R5.03.17](doc:r5.03.17), one has access to the values of various internal variables useful for analysing the detailed functioning (anelastic contribution, energy dissipated, etc).

### 9.2 Examples of studies carried out

#### 9.2.1 Floor of P4 type

**Description of the test** [2]

Tests were carried out at the CEA / EMSI (Saclay) in order to characterise the non-linear behaviour of the floors of the BAS-BL 1300 MWe building subjected to a vertical earthquake. The mock-up designed was dimensioned so as to be representative of a real floor. The tests were carried out on the Azalée shaking table of the CEA at Saclay. The experimental programme consists of 9 *runs* of increasing acceleration levels. One studies the non-linear behaviour of the main slab in bending.

**Modelling carried out** [25] **,** [26]

One adopts a multilayer DKT plate modelling for the concrete and a modelling of GRILLE type for the reinforcement bars of the reinforcement. The model ENDO_ISOT_BETON is used in plane stress thanks to the De Borst approach. Starting from the static solution under self-weight, one carries out the seismic analyses chained one after another. The final state of the preceding analysis serves as the initial state of the following analysis. One analyses the damage undergone by the reinforced-concrete slab for the acceleration levels reached.

The comparison with the experimental results shows that the modelling carried out makes it possible to represent correctly the non-linear behaviour of the slab, in particular the state of cracking at the end of the loadings. The comparison of the time responses of the vertical displacements at the centre of the slab is very satisfactory. The residual deflection of the cracked slab under self-weight at the end of the highest loadings studied is well determined by the analysis.

#### 9.2.2 SAFE shear walls – T5

**Description of the test** [27]

The SAFE programme (Reinforced Structures of Low Slenderness, CCR-ISPRA, 1998) concerns a series of 13 pseudo-dynamic tests on shear walls. Unlike a dynamic test on a shaking table, the pseudo-dynamic method is a hybrid numerical/experimental method that combines the computation of the displacement of the structure (here one degree of freedom of horizontal translation) and the measurement of the force used by a control system to impose this displacement. The differences between each series of tests are essentially linked to the proportion of reinforcement of the walls, to the initial frequency of the test (from which one determines the mass to be applied at the top of the wall) and to the mean vertical load that one seeks to keep constant during the test. Each series generally comprises at least 3 tests, carried out by varying the intensity of the reference accelerogram.

**Modellings carried out** [11] **,** [23] **,** [24]

Two modelling approaches were carried out: 3D representation by solid finite elements (HEXA), and multilayer shells (DKT). 3 studies were conducted: monotonic static (push-over), alternating static and transient dynamic (DYNA_NON_LINE).

The study with a local modelling (concrete represented by means of solid elements) shows that one rapidly encounters enormous numerical difficulties both in statics and in non-linear dynamics. The constitutive models implemented are not very robust for this type of problem.

The global modelling makes it possible to represent in a satisfactory manner the behaviour of the wall for the lowest loading level (static push-over, alternating static, seismic dynamic). One does not encounter numerical difficulties. The model GLRC_DM makes it possible to supply the static envelope of the experimental cycles (push-over). In dynamics, the comparison of the time responses of the displacements at the top of the wall is very satisfactory. However, the irreversible strains are not modelled in this study. This is why this study is currently being continued with the model GLRC_DM coupled to membrane elastoplasticity.

#### 9.2.3 CAMUS 2000 mock-up

**Description of the test** [4]

The CAMUS 2000 mock-up is a structure at 1/3 scale made up of two walls and five reinforced-concrete floors. The base of the structure is very strongly reinforced in order to ensure the anchorage to the shaking table. The total height of the mock-up is $5,10m$ . Lateral strengthening systems are added to the structure in order to increase its strength. Additional masses are placed on the floors in order to represent the required state of stress at the foot of the structure. The total mass of the mock-up is 36 tonnes. The test programme consists of 3 seismic *runs* of increasing excitation levels. The loading is bidirectional horizontal.

**Modellings carried out** [4]

The analyses were carried out by means of the multifibre-beam modelling (POU_D_EM) and of the constitutive model LABORD_1D for the concrete.

The results obtained by the analysis in terms of global quantities (displacements, generalised forces, …) are relatively close to those obtained experimentally. The comparison of the in-plane bending moments for the first storey of the right-hand wall and for the various loading levels shows that one has a good agreement between the analysis and the test. The multifibre-beam modelling of *Code_Aster* implemented is suited to simulating this type of simple structure under seismic loading.

## 10 Conclusions

The methodology for carrying out a civil-engineering study under seismic loading is presented. One places oneself within the framework of non-linear transient dynamics (operator DYNA_NON_LINE).

Various types of finite-element modelling are available in order to represent the reinforced-concrete structural elements (beam, column, slab, wall, …). One may use a local representation, a semi-global one (multilayer shell, multifibre beam) and a global shell.

Non-linear constitutive models are furthermore available for the concrete (ENDO_ISOT_BETON, ENDO_ORTH_BETON, MAZARS_UNIL, GLRC_DM and GLRC_DM coupled to plasticity) and for the steel (VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_CINE_LINE), suited to seismic applications.

The implementation of a non-linear seismic study is presented (data setup of the problem, preliminary analyses to be carried out before the non-linear dynamic analysis, advice in order to improve the convergence of the analyses and post-processings available).

This document provides the user, for each stage of his or her analysis, with methodological advice and rules of good practice to be followed.
