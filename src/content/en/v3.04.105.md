# v3.04.105 SSLV105 - Centrifugal raid of a rotating beam

**Summary:**

Test of Mechanical structures in linear static analysis.

The geometry is that of an slender beam that is rotated around one of its ends. Five modellings are considered: elements 3D (HEXA20), multifibre Timoshenko beams (POU_D_TGM), d-Euler beams (POU_D_E) and Timoshenko beams (POU_D_T) with constant or variable cross-section. The rotational inertia forces (such as the SSLV104 test) are tested here with centrifugal stiffening.

The reference solutions (analytical and numerical) take into account the additional rigidity term due to rotation. The results are identical to those corresponding to the reference solutions.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is composed of a space-oriented slender beam that is carried by the $(1,1,1)$ vector axis.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.105/100000000000044B000003052782C1430B32DFFF.jpg)

As regards the section of the beam, two cases are considered:

- Constant square section, dØaire $4.0{10}^{-4}{m}^{2}$ (modellings A, B, C and D),
- Circular cross section with homothetic variation, initial $2.0{10}^{-2}m$ radius and final $4.0{10}^{-2}m$ radius (modelization E).

In all the above cases, the length of the beam is $0.5m$ .

### 1.2 Material properties

$E=2.{10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$

$\nu =0$

$\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Clamped beam, free in rotation around an axis perpendicular to its largest dimension and passing through the centre of the clamped face.

Contact details of the rotation vector: $(1,0,–1)$ .

Rotation speed: $\omega =3000\mathrm{rd}/s$ .

The important value of the rotation speed is nothing physical.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation methods used for reference solutions

In the beam local marker, the equation for displacement ${U}_{x}$ (without neglecting elongation) is given by:

$\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\left(ES(x)\frac{\partial {U}_{x}}{\partial x}\right)+\rho S\left(x\right){\omega}^{2}\left(x+{U}_{x}\right)=0.\text{ }$

The boundary conditions associated with this equation sew: $\begin{array}{}{U}_{x}(0)=0\\ \frac{\partial {U}_{x}}{\partial x}(L)={\sigma}_{xx}(L)=0\end{array}$ .

#### 2.1.1 Case of a constant cross-section – analytical solution

In the case of a constant cross-section (modellings A, B, C and D), the above equation may be simplified as follows:

$\frac{{\partial}^{2}{U}_{x}}{\partial {x}^{2}}+\frac{\rho}{E}{\omega}^{2}(x+{U}_{x})=0$ .

$\alpha =\sqrt{\frac{\rho {\omega}^{2}}{E}}$ is installed. By integrating the previous differential equation, the beam marker is obtained:

${U}_{x}(x)=\frac{\sin(\alpha x)}{\alpha \cos(\alpha L)}-x$ ${U}_{y}={U}_{z}=0$

In the global marker, the total point displacement of the beam is therefore written:

$\begin{array}{}{U}_{x}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\frac{\sin(\alpha r)}{\alpha \cos(\alpha L)}-r)\\ {U}_{y}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\frac{\sin(\alpha r)}{\alpha \cos(\alpha L)}-r)\\ {U}_{z}(X,Y,Z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}(\frac{\sin(\alpha r)}{\alpha \cos(\alpha L)}-r)\end{array}$ ,

with $r=\sqrt{{X}^{2}+{Y}^{2}+{Z}^{2}}$ .

#### 2.1.2 Case of a variable section – Digital solution

In the case of the variable section beam of model E, the reference solution is derived from an Aster calculation with a 3D model with a quadratic 19268 elements (TETRA10) and 29122 nodes.

## 8 Summary of the results

Correct operation of the RIGI_ROTA option.

In the case of modellings B, C and D, beams with 8 elements give the solution with a relative error of less than 9.10-5%. With only one element the error is only 0.6 %. In comparison, in the case of Modelling A, the elements 3D with 200 elements give the solution to 0.01%. This is due to the fact that the solution is integrated almost exactly with the beams.

In the case of Modelling E involving a variable section beam, elements beams with 8 elements approach the reference solution with a relative error of 0.1%.

Note the increase in axial displacement compared to the case without stiffening (SSLV104 [V3.04.104](doc:v3.04.104)).
