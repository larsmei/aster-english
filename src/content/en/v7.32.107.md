# v7.32.107 WTNP107 – Modelling drying of a concrete bar with different sorption isotherms

**Summary:**

This test represents the THM Modelling of the drying and heating of a concrete bar. The primary objective of this test is to check the correct consideration of several temperature-dependent sorption isotherms. Moreover, this test is representative of a realistic drying behavior.

The initial saturation of the material is 0.98.A relative humidity of 70% is applied to a edge of the sample as well as a temperature of 60°C and a gas pressure of 5 bar. The disothermal laws are derived from the literature [ 1 , 2 , 3 ].

The sorption curve linking capillary pressure to saturation is given by the following relationship:

$\mathit{Pc}(S,T)=\frac{-{\rho}_{l}({T}_{0}).R.{T}_{0}}{\alpha {M}_{\mathit{vap}}}{({S}^{-1/\beta }-1)}^{1-\beta }\frac{{\gamma}_{\mathit{lv}}({T}_{0})}{{\gamma}_{\mathit{lv}}(T)}.\sqrt{\frac{m({T}_{0})}{m(T)}}$

where ${T}_{0}$ is the reference temperature (20°C) where the parameters $\alpha$ and $\beta$ are evaluated, R is the perfect gas constant, ${M}_{\mathit{vap}}$ is the molar mass of the vapour, and:

$\frac{m(T)}{m({T}_{0})}={10}^{{A}_{d}[{2.10}^{-3}(T-{T}_{0})-{10}^{-6}{(T-{T}_{0})}^{2}]}$ et ${\gamma}_{\mathit{lv}}(T)=0.1558{(1-\frac{T}{647.1})}^{1.26}$

Moreover, relative permeability is given by a Van-Genuchten relationship, such as:

${k}_{\mathit{rl}}(S)={S}^{p}{[1-{(1-{S}^{1/\beta })}^{\beta}]}^{2}$

et

${k}_{\mathit{rg}}(S)={(1-S)}^{p}{[1-{S}^{1/\beta }]}^{2.\beta }$

The constants retained are [ 3 ]: $p=2.91$ $\beta =0.389$ , $\alpha =9.334$ and ${A}_{d}=10.378$

Finally the diffusion in the gaseous mixture [ 3 ] is such that ${D}_{g}(S,T)={D}_{0}(T){\varphi}^{a}{(1-S)}^{b}$ with a and b the Milington coefficients such as a = 2.607 and b = 7 and ${D}_{0}(T)=0.217.{10}^{-4}{(\frac{T}{273})}^{1.88}$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

On the left edge of the sample (AD edge), a capillary pressure is applied which corresponds to a fixed relative humidity (using Kelvin's law) and a temperature:

- HR = 70%(Pc = 48.2 MPa)
- T = 60°C
- Pg = 0.5 MPa (i.e. 0.4 MPa from baseline)
- A zero stream is applied anywhere else

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Initially, S = 0.98 saturation is considered uniformly, which corresponds to ${P}_{c}(x)={P}_{c0}=2.4{10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}$ , a gas pressure ${P}_{g}(x)={P}_{g0}={10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$

and a temperature $T(x)={T}_{0}=20°C$ .

## 5 Modelling C

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics C

Same model as A but with a vapour density exchange term (via relative humidity HR) to test the 'ECHANGE_THM_HR' function.

- 2D D_PLAN_THH2S flat deformation modelling
- Hydraulic Law: ‘LIQU_AD_GAZ_VAPE
- 50 elements $Q8$ refined at the edge of the exchange term to verify the value of the stream at the edge in relation to the analytical solution.

The simulation is performed over a month. The external humidity is fixed to HR=0.7, the exchange term to ${10}^{-3} m/s$.

3 calculations are performed successively: the first (U1) with a saturation vapour pressure pvsat = 2460 Pa, the second (U2) with the same value but defined as a constant function (validation of AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F) and the third (U3) with a saturation vapour pressure dependent on temperature:
$Pvsat ={10}^{(-2.78+ (T-273.)/(31.559+0.1354*(T-273.)))}$

### 5.2 Modelling result C

The capillary pressure values at node 3 (x=0; y=0) are checked as non-regression and the water flow calculated by test case is well equal to:

\left({M}_{w}+{M}_{\text{vp}}\right).n= \alpha.\rho_{vp}.\left( exp\left[ \frac{Pc.M_{H2O}}{\rho_{l}.R.T}\right] -{HR}^{ext} \right)
$$
