# v6.03.011 SSNP011 - Calculation of the critical acceleration coefficient of homogeneous and stratified slopes

**Summary:**

This test case validates the calculation of the critical acceleration coefficient of homogeneous and stratified slopes by the equilibrium limit methods in `CALC_STAB_PENTE`. The test case validates the following features:

- Definition of the hydraulic pressure field by $R_u$ coefficient via keyword `COEF_RU`.
- The methods of direct resolution of critical acceleration coefficient implemented in CALC_STAB_PENTE.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The stability of two slopes in the modellings A and B is studied. One is homogeneous, as shown by the geom-ssnp011-a. The other is stratified, as shown by the geom-ssnp011-b, consisting of 3 layers of soil whose interfaces are almost horizontal with the 22:1 inclination ratio.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.011/mod_a_geom.png)

*Geometry of slope studied in Modelling A[1].*

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.011/mod_b_geom.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

For the homogeneous slope, the mechanical properties of the soil are as follows:

- Density: $2000kg/m^3$
- Friction angle: $30^{\circ}$
- Cohesion : $25kPa$

For the stratified slope, the mechanical properties are shown in Table 1.

**Table 1**: Properties of Laminate Slope Materials

|  | **Top layer** | **Intermediate layer** | **Lower layer** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Density $\rho$ ($kg/m^3$) | 1700 | 1900 | 1900 |
| Cohesion Force $c$ ($kPa$) | 4 | 25 | 15 |
| Friction angle $\varphi$ ($^{\circ}$) | 30 | 15 | 45 |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The following loads shall be applied:

- The gravity with $g=9,81m/{s}^{2}$
- Hydraulic pressure modelled by the uniform coefficient $R_u = 0.5$ in Modelling A.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The exact solution of the critical acceleration coefficient was calculated in the reference article[1] by the finite elements method. The load of the density inertial force applies incrementally to the slope to the collapse, which results in the divergence of the nonlinear calculation with the constitutive law Mohr-Coulomb.

### 2.2 Baseline

The reference results for both modellings are summarized in Table 2.

**Table 2**: Baseline

| **Stage** | **Modelling** | **Critical acceleration coefficient** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Homogenous | A | 0,132 |
| Stratified | B | 0,161 |

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

For the homogeneous slope, the Simplified Bishop method is used to calculate the critical acceleration coefficient. According to the preliminary study, the apses of the ends of the fracture surface are fixed at 30m and 115m, in order to accelerate the calculation. The slippery mass is divided into 8 slices of soil.

### 3.2 Tested quantities and results

The critical acceleration coefficient obtained, as well as the geometric parameters of the critical surface, are tested. The results are shown in Table 3.

The result of the KC from the CALC_STAB_PENTE macro-command gives the difference 0.11%
This proves that the KC Direct Bishop Resolution method is relevant and that the $R_u$ coefficient is correctly taken into account.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

For the stratified slope, the critical surface is obviously non-circular. So we adopt the Morgenstern-Price method to solve the critical acceleration coefficient. In order to speed up the calculation, we initialize the EFWA with an already optimized result and disable the future optimization. Thus in this test case we evaluate the KC of this single critical surface without optimization algorithm. This input surface is determined beforehand by a complete calculation of CALC_STAB_PENTE that converged after 13 iterations EFWA.

### 4.2 Tested quantities and results

The critical acceleration coefficient obtained before and after refinement of the mesh is tested. The results are shown in Table 4.

**Table 4**: Modelling Reference Values B.

| **NUME_RAFF** | **Identification** | **Aster result** | **Reference value** | **Error/Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | KC | 0.16094677307399347 | 0.161 | 0.03% |
| 0 | KC | 0.17268896670181597 | non-regression | 0,0001% |

### 4.3 Summary of the results

The result of the KC from the CALC_STAB_PENTE macro-command gives the difference 0.03%
This demonstrates the relevance of the Morgenstern-Price direct method to calculate the critical acceleration coefficient.
