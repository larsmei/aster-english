# v7.31.113 WTNV113 – Gravitory discharge into saturated porous medium

**Summary:**

The test involves studying the influence of a gravitational flow on the distribution of the fluid pressure of the saturated medium.

Eleven modellings are performed: four two-dimensional modellings (modellings A, B: elements HM_DPQ8, modellings E, F: elements THM_DPQ8) and seven three-dimensional modellings (modellings C and D: elements HM_HEXA20, modellings G and H: elements THM_HEXA20,I: THM_HEXA20D and J: THM_HEXA20S, K: THM_HEXA20S_DIL)

The distinction between modellings A and B (respectively C and D, E and F, G and H) lies in the fluid constitutive law.

Modellings I and J are selective and light Modelling variants of G, they have results that differ from the proposed analytical solution (integration is different), and are therefore non-regression.

The K Modelling is designed to verify the use of the second gradient model in 3D_THMS (similar to J Modelling).

The reference solution is one-dimensional because it depends only on vertical coordinate.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Introduction

The test case study examines the hydraulic behaviour of a porous medium saturated by a single fluid: water in its liquid phase. It is used in *Code_Aster* to model HMou THM by blocking the temperature. The constitutive law of the associated fluid is based on the modellings type LIQU_SATU (modellings A, C, E, G, I, J ) or LIQU_GAZ_ATM type LIQU_GAZ_ATM(modellings B, D, F, H ).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The fluid mass conservation equation is given by the following expression:

> $\frac{{\mathit{dm}}_{i}}{\mathit{dt}}+\text{Div}{M}_{i}=0$ $i$ varying from $1$ to number of constituents (1)

In our example, the model consists of a fluid: liquid water. Equation (1) therefore applies to this component:

> $\frac{{\mathrm{dm}}_{e}}{\mathrm{dt}}+\text{Div}{M}_{e}=0$     (2)

The fluid flow is expressed as:

${M}_{e}={\rho}_{e}{\lambda}_{e}(-\nabla {p}_{e}+{\rho}_{e}g)$     (3)

The fluid mass input is defined by equation (4) where the terms ${N}_{\mathrm{ee}}$ and ${N}_{\mathrm{ea}}$ (equation (5)) depend on the degree of $S$ saturation, $\phi$ porosity, Biot $b$ coefficient, ${K}_{e}$ permeability and ${K}_{s}$ solid matrix elasticity.

> $\frac{{\mathrm{dm}}_{e}}{\mathrm{dt}}={\rho}_{e}{N}_{\mathrm{ee}}\frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{e}}{\mathrm{dt}}+{\rho}_{e}{N}_{\mathrm{ea}}\frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{a}}{\mathrm{dt}}$     (4)
> $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{N}_{\mathrm{ee}}=-\varphi \frac{\partial S}{\partial {p}_{c}}+S(\frac{\varphi}{{K}_{e}}+\frac{b-\varphi }{{K}_{s}}S)\\ {N}_{\mathrm{ea}}={N}_{\mathrm{ae}}=\varphi \frac{\partial S}{\partial {p}_{c}}+(1-S)(\frac{b-\varphi }{{K}_{s}}S)\end{array}$     (5)

The material is saturated, $S=1$ and $\frac{\partial S}{\partial {P}_{c}}=0$ . $\Rightarrow {N}_{\mathrm{ee}}=S(\frac{\varphi}{{K}_{e}}+\frac{b-\varphi }{{K}_{s}}S)$ and ${N}_{\mathrm{ea}}=0$ .

The variational formulation of equation (2), taking into account (3) and (4), is:

$\forall {P}_{e}^{\text{*}}$ checking boundary conditions pressure:

${\int}_{\Omega}{N}_{\mathrm{ee}}\frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{e}}{\mathrm{dt}}{p}_{e}^{\text{*}}+{\int}_{\Omega}{\lambda}_{e}\nabla {p}_{e}.\nabla {p}_{e}^{\text{*}}={\int}_{\Omega}{\lambda}_{e}{\rho}_{e}g.\nabla {p}_{e}^{\text{*}}-{\int}_{\partial \Omega }\frac{{M}_{e}^{\mathrm{ext}}}{{\rho}_{e}}{p}_{e}^{\text{*}}$     (6)

*Discretization*

For the calculation of the analytical solution, one-dimensional $\mathrm{HM}$ Modelling is considered to be a single element-degree (2(HEXA20, DPQ8) discretization. It is specified that any $\mathrm{HM}$ Modelling is $\mathrm{P2P1}$-type, even if the mesh is quadratic, the hydraulic Modelling is linear.

It is assumed in both cases that the gravity is oriented to the negative $z$.

On the other hand, it is assumed that the nonlinearities are low and the $N,\lambda ,\rho$ coefficients are constant, so that the pressure variations are sufficiently small that $N$ and $\rho$ can be assumed to be constant.

- **Linear discretization:**

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.113/1000050A0000159A000015166255A4D6FF4A3E8B.svg)

We'll write:

> $p(z,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{2}{p}^{i}(t){\lambda}_{i}(z)$     (7)

With:

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\lambda}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}-z\\ {\lambda}_{2}=\frac{1}{2}+z\end{array}$     (8)

By introducing the matrices and vectors:

> $\begin{array}{c}[A]=[{A}_{ij}];{A}_{ij}=\underset{-1/2}{\overset{1/2}{\int}}{\lambda}_{i}{\lambda}_{j}\mathit{dz}\\ [B]=[{B}_{ij}];{B}_{ij}=\underset{-1/2}{\overset{1/2}{\int}}\frac{d{\lambda}_{i}}{\mathit{dz}}\frac{d{\lambda}_{j}}{\mathit{dz}}\mathit{dz}\\ \lbrace  {F}_{g}\rbrace  =\lbrace  {F}_{\mathit{gi}}\rbrace  ;{F}_{\mathit{gi}}=\underset{-1/2}{\overset{1/2}{\int}}\frac{d{\lambda}_{i}}{\mathit{dz}}\mathit{dz}\end{array}$

And noting:

> $\lbrace {p}_{e}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{e}^{1}\\ {p}_{e}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$     (10)
> $\lbrace {M}_{e}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{{M}_{e}^{\mathrm{ext}}}_{1}\\ {{M}_{e}^{\mathrm{ext}}}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$     (11)

Equation (6) becomes:

> $\frac{{N}_{\mathit{ee}}}{{\lambda}_{e}}[A]\left\lbrace \frac{{\mathit{dp}}_{e}}{\mathit{dt}}\right\rbrace +[B]\lbrace  {p}_{e}\rbrace  ={\rho}_{e}\lbrace  {F}_{g}\rbrace  -\frac{1}{{\lambda}_{e}{\rho}_{e}}\lbrace  {M}_{e}^{\mathit{ext}}\rbrace$     (12)

The calculation of the $[A]$ and $[B]$ matrices and the $\lbrace  {f}_{g}\rbrace$ vector gives:

> $[A]=\frac{1}{3}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& 1/2\\ 1/2& 1\end{array}\right];[B]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right];\lbrace  F\rbrace  =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-1\\ 1\end{array}\right\rbrace$         (13)

${[A]}^{-1}[B]$ own vectors of: $\lbrace {v}_{1}\rbrace ,\lbrace {v}_{2}\rbrace$ are defined which have the following orthogonality properties:

> ${\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}[A]\lbrace {v}_{j}\rbrace ={\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}[B]\lbrace {v}_{j}\rbrace =0\text{}\mathrm{si}\text{}i\ne j$     (14)

And we're gonna put it down:

> ${a}_{i}={\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}[A]\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace ,{b}_{i}={\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}[B]\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace ,{f}_{i}={\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}\lbrace {F}_{g}\rbrace \mathrm{et}{M}^{i}={\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace }^{T}\lbrace {M}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rbrace$     (15)

We find:

> $\lbrace {v}_{1}\rbrace =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}1\\ 1\end{array}\rbrace ;\lbrace {v}_{2}\rbrace =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-1\\ 1\end{array}\rbrace$     (16)

> $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{a}_{1}=1;{b}_{1}=0;{f}_{1}=0\\ {a}_{2}=\frac{1}{3};{b}_{2}=4;{f}_{2}=-\mathrm{2g}\end{array}$     (17)

$\lbrace {p}_{e}\rbrace$ is then broken down on the basis of $\lbrace {v}_{i}\rbrace$:

> $\lbrace  {p}_{e}\rbrace  =\sum_{i=1}^{2}{\alpha}_{e}^{i}\lbrace  {v}_{i}\rbrace$     (18)

Given the orthogonality properties (14), equation (12) reads:

> $\frac{{N}_{\mathit{ee}}}{{\lambda}_{e}}{a}_{i}\frac{d{\alpha}_{e}^{i}}{\mathit{dt}}+{b}_{i}{\alpha}_{e}^{i}={\rho}_{e}{f}_{i}-\frac{1}{{\lambda}_{e}{\rho}_{e}}{M}_{e}^{i}$     (19)

*Initial conditions*

It is assumed that:

> ${p}_{e}(x,t=0)={p}_{a}^{0}-{p}_{c}^{0}$ space uniforms;

Based on the values of the $\lbrace {v}_{1}\rbrace ,\lbrace {v}_{2}\rbrace$ vectors (equations (16)), it is easy to see that:

> $\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\alpha}_{e}^{1}(t=0)={P}_{a}^{0}-{p}_{c}^{0}\\ {\alpha}_{e}^{2}(t=0)=0\end{array}$     (20)

One takes place in a case where the fluid flow is zero ($\lbrace  {M}_{e}^{\mathit{ext}}\rbrace  =0$ ).

Taking into account (20) , ${f}_{1}=0$ (equations (17)), the solution of the system of equations (19) is:

> $\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\alpha}_{e}^{1}={P}_{a}^{0}-{p}_{c}^{0}\\ {\alpha}_{e}^{2}=\frac{{f}_{2}}{{b}_{2}}{\rho}_{e}(1-\exp(-\frac{{b}_{2}}{{a}_{2}}\frac{{\lambda}_{e}}{{N}_{\mathit{ee}}}t))\end{array}$     (21)

The nodal variables are:

> $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{1}\\ {p}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\alpha}_{1}-{\alpha}_{2}\\ {\alpha}_{1}+{\alpha}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

${\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{1}\\ {p}_{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace }_{\mathit{eau}}=\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{a}^{0}-{p}_{c}^{0}+\frac{{\rho}_{e}g}{2}(1-\exp(-12\frac{{\lambda}_{e}}{{N}_{\mathit{ee}}}t))\\ {P}_{a}^{0}-{p}_{c}^{0}-\frac{{\rho}_{e}g}{2}(1-\exp(-12\frac{{\lambda}_{e}}{{N}_{\mathit{ee}}}t))\end{array}\right\rbrace$     (22)

### 4.3 Remarks

The calculated pressures for the two previous behaviours (THMC=LIQU_SATU (model $A$) and THMC=LIQU_GAZ_ATM (model $B$)) are equal in absolute values. The difference in signs is due to the fact that:

- the pressure $\mathrm{PRE1}$ evaluated in the code is the water pressure for the THMC=LIQU_SATU behavior,
- $\mathrm{PRE1}$ is equal to the capillary pressure for the THMC=LIQU_GAZ behavior. The capillary pressure is equal to the difference between the gas pressure and the liquid pressure. In the particular case where the dry air pressure is the atmospheric pressure (THMC=LIQU_GAZ_ATM), the capillary pressure is the opposite of the liquid pressure.
