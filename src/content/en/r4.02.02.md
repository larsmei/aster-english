# r4.02.02 Elements for linear fluid-structure interaction coupling with an inert fluid

**Summary:**

The elements described here make it possible to carry out computations of the frequencies and eigenmodes of a structure coupled to a fluid. They also make it possible to compute the acoustic response.

After the formulation of the fluid-structure coupling problem, this document describes the approach followed in order to implement the new finite elements.

## 1 Introduction

The vibratory behaviour of a structure is often modified if the structure is in the presence of a fluid: this is what is called vibro-acoustic coupling. One distinguishes the coupling cases into two categories: either the fluid is infinite (this is the case of submerged structures), or the fluid is contained in a bounded medium (this is the case of tanks more or less filled with fluid).

The finite elements described here make it possible to solve coupling problems with a fluid of finite dimension.

## 2 Vibro-acoustic coupling

### 2.1 Presentation

Let an elastic structure defined in a domain ${\Omega}_{s}$ which vibrates in the presence of a perfect, non-weighing, compressible fluid, in isentropic evolution, defined in a domain ${\Omega}_{f}$ . ${\Sigma}_{f}$ being the boundary of the fluid domain ${\Omega}_{f}$ and ${\Sigma}_{s}$ being the boundary of the solid domain ${\Omega}_{s}$ , one denotes by $\Sigma ={\Sigma}_{f}\cap {\Sigma}_{S}$ their common surface. One denotes by ${n}_{s}$ the outward normal to the fluid domain ${\Omega}_{f}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r4.02.02/10000CAC000016390000110EEB1AD8C54E0F0BE2.svg)

At a given instant, the state of the fluid is defined by its (scalar) pressure field $p$ and that of the structure by its (vector) displacement field ${u}_{s}$ . One considers that the coupled system is subjected to small perturbations about its equilibrium state, in which the fluid and the structure are at rest. The fluid-structure interaction problem then consists in solving simultaneously two problems:

- one in the structure subjected, on $\Sigma$ , to a pressure field $p$ imposed by the fluid;
- the other in the fluid subjected to a displacement field $u$ of the wall $\Sigma$ .

**Note:** if the sense of the normal, from fluid towards structure or from structure towards fluid, is of no importance (it is a convention), it is nevertheless imperative that this normal always be oriented in the same sense. It is strongly recommended to keep the orientation convention from the structure towards the fluid for all the modellings of the fluid-structure interface, in order to keep consistency with the other loadings on the structure (see in particular the case of loadings of type ONDE_PLANE).

### 2.2 Formulation of the vibro-acoustic problem

#### 2.2.1 Description of the structure

**Assumption**: the structure is homogeneous and obeys the laws of linear elasticity. In view of this assumption, one can write the following equations governing the state of the structure [bib2].

##### 2.2.1.1 Equation of conservation of momentum

The equation of conservation of momentum is written, in the absence of body forces other than the inertia forces, as follows:

$$
{\rho}_{s}{\ddot{u}}_{s}-div({\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s}))=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{s}
$$

With ${\rho}_{s}$ the density, ${u}_{s}$ the displacements and ${\sigma}_{s}$ the stress tensor.

##### 2.2.1.2 Compatibility relation

One establishes the classical compatibility relation on the strain tensor:

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\nabla {u}_{s}+\nabla {u}_{s}^{T}\right)
$$

Where ${\epsilon}_{s}$ is the (small) strain tensor.

##### 2.2.1.3 Constitutive law in isotropic linear elasticity

One assumes that the solid is linear elastic, hence:

$$
{\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})=C:{\epsilon}_{s}({u}_{s})
$$

$C$ being the elasticity tensor, symmetric. For the isotropic case, one can also write:

$$
{\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})=\lambda \text{tr}({\epsilon}_{s}({u}_{s}))I+2\mu {\epsilon}_{s}({u}_{s})
$$

With $\lambda ` et :math:$mu ` the Lamé coefficients.

##### 2.2.1.4 Boundary conditions

There are boundary conditions on the surface of the structure such that:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})\cdot {n}_{s}={f}_{s}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{N}\\ {u}_{s}=\overline{u}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{U}\end{array}
$$

Where $\partial {\Omega}_{N}$ is the part of the surface of the structure on which the (surface) force conditions ${f}_{s}$ apply and $\partial {\Omega}_{U}$ is the part of the surface of the structure on which the displacement conditions $\overline{u}$ apply.

#### 2.2.2 Description of the fluid

**Assumption:**  the fluid obeys the laws of linear acoustics.

##### 2.2.2.1 Equation of conservation of momentum

The equation of conservation of momentum is written, in the absence of sources:

$$
{\rho}_{0}{\ddot{u}}_{f}-div({\sigma}_{f})=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}
$$

With ${\sigma}_{f}$ the stress tensor of the fluid, ${\rho}_{0}$ the density of the fluid in the state of rest and ${u}_{f}$ the displacement field of the fluid.

##### 2.2.2.2 Equation of conservation of mass

To first order and in the absence of acoustic sources, the equation of conservation of mass is expressed by the relation:

$$
{\dot{\rho}}_{f}+{\rho}_{0}div\left({\dot{u}}_{f}\right)=0
$$

##### 2.2.2.3 Constitutive law

**Assumption:**  the fluid obeys the laws of linear acoustics, hence:

$$
{\sigma}_{f}=-pI
$$

The fluid is assumed to be in barotropic evolution (the pressure $p$ is, for the given fluid, a known function of the density alone):

$$
p={\rho}_{f}{c}_{0}^{2}
$$

where ${c}_{0}$ is the speed of sound in the fluid at rest.

##### 2.2.2.4 Formulation in pressure

One must now combine these equations in order to obtain the equation of state of the fluid; there are several ways of doing so. If one takes the pressure $p$ of the fluid as the state variable, by combining () and (), one obtains:

$$
{\rho}_{0}div({\ddot{u}}_{f})+\Delta p=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}
$$

By differentiating with respect to time equation () and combining it with ():

$$
\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{p}+{\rho}_{0}div\left({\ddot{u}}_{f}\right)=0
$$

If one injects () into (), one obtains the d'Alembert equation:

$$
\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{p}-\Delta p=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}
$$

##### 2.2.2.5 Two-variable formulation

One associates with the pressure variable $p$ an additional variable called the displacement potential of the fluid and denoted $\varphi$ ; one makes the assumption of the absence of a rotational term in the motions of the fluid such that:

$$
{u}_{f}=\nabla \varphi
$$

See [bib1], [bib4], [bib5].

Taking the equation of conservation of momentum () and injecting into it (), after differentiation, which is a rewriting of Bernoulli's equation with ${\dot{u}}_{f}^{2}=0$ (linearity assumption):

$$
\nabla \left(p+{\rho}_{0}\ddot{\varphi}\right)=0
$$

Which gives us a direct relation between the pressure and the displacement potential of the fluid:

$$
\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}{c}_{0}^{2}}p+\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{\varphi}=0
$$

Whence the new form of the wave-propagation equation:

$$
\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{p}+{\rho}_{0}\Delta \ddot{\varphi}=0
$$

##### 2.2.2.6 Formulation in velocity potential

One can also introduce the velocity potential of the fluid, denoted $\psi$ and such that:

$$
{\dot{u}}_{f}=\nabla \psi
$$

Which gives us, as a relation with the pressure $p$ of the fluid:

$$
p=-{\rho}_{0}\dot{\psi}
$$

This last formulation is more natural when one must impose a boundary condition on the boundary in velocity, such as an incident wave.

#### 2.2.3 Description of the fluid-structure interaction

At the fluid-structure interface $\Sigma$ , the fluid being non-viscous, it does not adhere to the wall. One therefore writes the continuity of the normal stresses:

$$
{\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=-p{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

And the continuity of the normal displacements:

$$
{u}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={u}_{f}\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

Since the fluid is considered initially at rest, the continuity of the normal displacements is equivalent to the continuity of the normal velocities:

$$
{\dot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\dot{u}}_{f}\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

And the continuity of the normal accelerations:

$$
{\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\ddot{u}}_{f}\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

By applying () in ():

$$
{\ddot{u}}_{f}=-\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}\nabla p
$$

The equation of continuity of the normal accelerations () is written:

$$
{\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=-\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}\nabla p\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

For the case in which one expresses the equation of state of the fluid by the pressure and the displacement potential of the fluid, the FSI conditions will be:

$$
{\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\rho}_{0}\ddot{\varphi}{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma

{\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=\nabla \ddot{\varphi}\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

And if one uses the velocity potential of the fluid:

$$
{\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\rho}_{0}\dot{\psi}{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma

{\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=\nabla \varphi \cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

These writings make it possible to observe that the sense of the normal ${n}_{s}$ is indeed solely a matter of writing convention. Whatever this orientation, the continuity equations will have strictly the same form.

#### 2.2.4 Radiation condition at the exterior interface

In the case where the structure is surrounded by the fluid, the propagation of the waves in the fluid must respect the radiation conditions at the exterior boundary of this fluid medium $\partial {\Sigma}_{R}$ ; this condition, called the Sommerfeld condition, stipulates that the waves are not reflected by the exterior surface of the fluid. The radiation condition is written:

$$
\underset{r\to \infty }{\lim}{r}^{d-1/2}\left(\nabla p\cdot {n}_{f}+\frac{\dot{p}}{{c}_{0}}\right)=0
$$

This condition being asymptotic, its application to a finite-element modelling is not direct. One will use an approximation of the condition, denoted BGT (after the name of the authors, see [Bib6]). The zero-order approximation is written:

$$
\nabla p\cdot {n}_{f}+\frac{{\rho}_{0}}{{Z}_{C}}\dot{p}=0\text{sur}\partial {\Sigma}_{R}

\textrm{avec}

{Z}_{C}={\rho}_{0}{c}_{0}
$$

This condition is exact for a plane wave. In order to approximate asymptotically the behaviour at infinity of a spherical wave, for a given radius $R$ , one uses a first-order approximation:

$$
\nabla p\cdot {n}_{f}+{\rho}_{0}\left(\frac{1}{{Z}_{C}}\dot{p}+\frac{1}{{Z}_{R}}p\right)=0\text{sur}\partial {\Sigma}_{R}

\textrm{avec}

{Z}_{C}={\rho}_{0}{c}_{0}` et :math:`{Z}_{R}={\rho}_{0}R
$$

#### 2.2.5 Decomposition of the fluid velocity

The velocity of the fluid ${\dot{u}}_{f}$ , which may be written ${\dot{u}}_{f}=\nabla \psi$ , may be decomposed into three components:

$$
\psi ={\psi}^{\mathit{inc}}+{\psi}^{\mathit{rad}}+{\psi}^{\mathit{ref}}
$$

The first term is the incident term, the second the radiation term and the third the reflection term. This decomposition corresponds to the same one on the pressure of the fluid:

$$
p={p}^{\mathit{inc}}+{p}^{\mathit{rad}}+{p}^{\mathit{ref}}
$$

#### 2.2.6 Formulation of the coupled problem

Ultimately, the formulation of the vibro-acoustic problem in terms of displacements for the structure and of pressure in the fluid leads to the equations of the harmonic problem ($P$ ) in the solid medium:

$$
{C}_{ijkl}\cdot {u}_{k,\mathit{lj}}+{\omega}^{2}{\rho}_{S}{u}_{i}=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{S}
$$

The equation of propagation of the waves in the fluid medium:

$$
\Delta p+{k}^{2}p=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}
$$

With the two fluid-structure coupling equations. In stresses:

$$
{C}_{ijkl}\cdot {u}_{k,l}\cdot {n}_{i}=-p{\delta}_{ij}\cdot {n}_{i}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

And in velocities:

$$
{u}_{i}\cdot {n}_{i}=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}{\omega}^{2}}\frac{\partial p}{\partial n}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

Using the decomposition (), one has the condition () at the interface, which is written:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\nabla {\psi}^{\mathit{ref}}\cdot {n}_{s}=-{v}^{\mathit{inc}}\cdot {n}_{s}\\ \nabla {\psi}^{\mathit{rad}}\cdot {n}_{s}={\dot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}\end{array}\text{sur}\Sigma
$$

### 2.3 Finite-element models of the coupled problem

One solves the coupled problem by using the finite-element method from the weak formulation of the problem.

The nodal unknowns are denoted ${U}_{s}$ , $P$ , $\Phi ` et :math:$Psi ` .

Let $({N}_{i}^{f})$ be the finite-element shape functions for the fluid and $({N}_{i}^{s})$ the finite-element shape functions for the structure. One will use the same shape functions for $P$ , $\Phi ` et :math:$Psi ` .

#### 2.3.1 Formulation in u-p

This formulation is the most «natural», because it uses the physical variables of the problem. The set to be solved is therefore:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\rho}_{s}{\ddot{u}}_{s}-div({\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s}))=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{s}\\ {\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})\cdot {n}_{s}={f}_{s}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{N}\\ {u}_{s}=\overline{u}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{U}\\ {\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=-p{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ {\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=-\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}\nabla p\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ \frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{p}-\Delta p=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}\end{array}
$$

The finite-element approximation of the complete problem leads to the following system:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{s}& 0\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& {M}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{P}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{s}& C\\ 0& {K}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ P\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The quantities ${M}_{s}$ , ${K}_{s}$ and ${F}_{s}$ relate to the modelling of the structure and we refer the reader to the appropriate documentations. For the fluid, one has:

$$
{({M}_{f})}_{ij}={\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}.{N}_{i}^{f}{N}_{j}.d\Omega

\textrm{et}

{({K}_{f})}_{ij}={\int}_{{\Omega}_{f}}\nabla {N}_{i}^{f}\cdot \nabla {N}_{j}.d\Omega
$$

And for the fluid-structure coupling:

$$
{(C)}_{ij}={\int}_{\Sigma}{N}_{j}^{f}\left({N}_{i}^{s}\cdot {n}_{s}\right).d\Sigma
$$

The matrices ${M}_{s}$ and ${M}_{f}$ are positive definite; the matrices ${K}_{s}$ and ${K}_{f}$ are positive semi-definite (one must add the boundary conditions in displacement and pressure). The resulting system () is not symmetric.

In order to obtain a fluid mass and stiffness matrix that is rigorously zero, by convention, it is sufficient to set ${\rho}_{0}={c}_{0}=0$ (that is, the coefficients RHO and CELE_R in DEFI_MATERIAU).

For the BGT condition, the system is modified and is:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{s}& 0\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& {M}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{P}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{\rho}_{0}}{{Z}_{C}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{U}}_{s}\\ \dot{P}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{s}& C\\ 0& {K}_{f}+\frac{{\rho}_{0}}{{Z}_{R}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ P\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

One therefore adds a damping term with the matrix $Q$ such that:

$$
{(Q)}_{ij}={\int}_{{\Sigma}_{R}}{N}_{i}^{f}{N}_{j}^{f}.d\Gamma
$$

#### 2.3.2 Formulation in u-p-phi

The previous system being non-symmetric, the search for eigenmodes requires a QEP modal solver, in general less robust than the symmetric modal solvers. That is why one can also employ a formulation of type U-P-PHI.

The set to be solved is therefore:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\rho}_{s}{\ddot{u}}_{s}-div({\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s}))=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{s}\\ {\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})\cdot {n}_{s}={f}_{s}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{N}\\ {u}_{s}=\overline{u}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{U}\\ {\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\rho}_{0}\ddot{\varphi}{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ {\ddot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=\nabla \ddot{\varphi}\cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ \frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}{c}_{0}^{2}}p+\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{\varphi}=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}\\ \frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{p}+{\rho}_{0}\Delta \ddot{\varphi}=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}\end{array}
$$

The finite-element approximation of the complete problem leads to the following system:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{s}& 0& -{\rho}_{0}C\\ 0& 0& {M}_{f}\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& {M}_{f}& -{\rho}_{0}{K}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{P}\\ \ddot{\Phi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{s}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}{M}_{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ P\\ \Phi \end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

The matrices are the same as in the previous paragraph. One sees that the system is symmetric but that it contains zeros on the diagonal. In particular the mass term relative to the variable $p$ is problematic, because it will make the modal computations more delicate. Indeed, if one considers that the eigenvalues of the problem are of the form $\sqrt{(\frac{K}{M})}$ , one sees that the zero term will require:

- either computing the eigenvalues in a frequency band excluding zero, which will in particular require having a good a priori idea of this frequency band (in general, a preliminary computation without coupling with the fluid is used for this);
- or modifying the global system by adding a value on the diagonal which is large enough to make the system invertible, and not too large so as not to disturb the results

Moreover, the presence of the new variable $\Phi$ requires boundary conditions to be defined on this variable.

For the BGT condition, the system is modified and is:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& -\frac{{\rho}_{0}^{2}}{{Z}_{C}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\stackrel{⃛}{U}}_{s}\\ \stackrel{⃛}{P}\\ \stackrel{⃛}{\Phi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{s}& 0& -{\rho}_{0}C\\ 0& 0& {M}_{f}\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& {M}_{f}& -{\rho}_{0}{K}_{f}-\frac{{\rho}_{0}^{2}}{{Z}_{R}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{P}\\ \ddot{\Phi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{s}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}{M}_{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ P\\ \Phi \end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

One sees that the addition of this condition makes the system of order three. Now, there exists no robust scheme for this order; in code_aster, one therefore modifies the system by making it of order two by means of a damping term and using the relation defined in the system of equations , that is $\frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}{c}_{0}^{2}}p+\frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{\varphi}=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}$ . The system then becomes:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{s}& 0& -{\rho}_{0}C\\ 0& 0& {M}_{f}\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& {M}_{f}& -{\rho}_{0}{K}_{f}-\frac{{\rho}_{0}^{2}}{{Z}_{R}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{P}\\ \ddot{\Phi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{\rho}_{0}}{{Z}_{C}}Q& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{U}}_{s}\\ \dot{P}\\ \dot{\Phi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{s}& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{1}{{\rho}_{0}}{M}_{f}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ P\\ \Phi \end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

One notes that the new matrix of order 1 becomes non-symmetric.

#### 2.3.3 Formulation in u-psi

The third formulation proposed in code_aster is that using the variable $\psi$ (velocity potential) for the fluid. The set to be solved is:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\rho}_{s}{\ddot{u}}_{s}-div({\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s}))=0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{s}\\ {\sigma}_{s}({u}_{s})\cdot {n}_{s}={f}_{s}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{N}\\ {u}_{s}=\overline{u}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}_{U}\\ {\sigma}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}={\rho}_{0}\dot{\psi}{n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ {\dot{u}}_{s}\cdot {n}_{s}=\nabla \psi \cdot {n}_{s}\text{sur}\Sigma \\ \frac{1}{{c}_{0}^{2}}\ddot{\psi}-\Delta \psi =0\text{dans}{\Omega}_{f}\end{array}
$$

The finite-element approximation of the complete problem leads to the following system:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{s}& 0\\ 0& -{\rho}_{0}{M}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{\Psi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}0& -{\rho}_{0}C\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& 0\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{U}}_{s}\\ \dot{\Psi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{s}& 0\\ 0& -{\rho}_{0}{K}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ \Psi \end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

This system is symmetric but it contains a velocity term, which in particular requires a quadratic modal solver.

For the BGT condition, the system is modified and is:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{s}& 0\\ 0& -{\rho}_{0}{M}_{f}\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\ddot{U}}_{s}\\ \ddot{\Psi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}0& -{\rho}_{0}C\\ -{\rho}_{0}{C}^{T}& \frac{-{\rho}_{0}^{2}}{{Z}_{C}}Q\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{U}}_{s}\\ \dot{\Psi}\end{array}\right\rbrace +\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{s}& 0\\ 0& -{\rho}_{0}({K}_{f}+\frac{{\rho}_{0}}{{Z}_{C}}Q)\end{array}\right]\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{U}_{s}\\ \Psi \end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{F}_{s}\\ 0\end{array}\right\rbrace
$$

#### 2.3.4 Discretisation by finite elements

If one imposes boundary conditions of imposed-velocity type or imposed wall impedance on the fluid, one is led to solve the following matrix system:

$$
\left[\begin{array}{ccc}K& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{{M}_{f}}{{\rho}_{0}{c}^{2}}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]-{\omega}^{2}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}M& 0& {\rho}_{0}{M}_{\Sigma}\\ 0& 0& \frac{{M}_{\mathrm{fl}}}{{c}^{2}}\\ {\rho}_{0}{M}_{\Sigma}^{T}& \frac{{M}_{\mathrm{fl}}^{T}}{{c}^{2}}& {\rho}_{0}H\end{array}\right]+i{\omega}^{3}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{{\rho}_{0}^{2}}{Z}Q\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{}0\\ i\omega \\ V\end{array}\right]
$$

$\mathrm{Q}$ being the matrix obtained from the bilinear form ${\int}_{{\Sigma}_{z}}{\Phi}^{2}\mathit{dS}$ and $\mathrm{V}$ , the vector obtained from ${\int}_{{\Sigma}_{V}}{\rho}_{0}{v}_{0}\Phi \mathit{dS}$ .

$\mathrm{Q}$ being the matrix obtained from the bilinear form ${\int}_{{\Sigma}_{z}}{\Phi}^{2}\mathit{dS}$ and $\mathrm{V}$ , the vector obtained from ${\int}_{{\Sigma}_{V}}{\rho}_{0}{v}_{0}\Phi \mathit{dS}$ .

## 3 Use

The elements described previously belong, for the fluid part, to the modelling '3D_FLUIDE', '2D_FLUIDE' or 'AXIS_FLUIDE' of the phenomenon MECANIQUE and, for the fluid-structure interface, to the modelling 'FLUI_STRU' or '2D_FLUI_STRU'. In order to define the absorbing fluid elements, which take the BGT condition into account, one must assign the modellings '3D_FLUI_ABSO', '2D_FLUI_ABSO' or 'AXIS_FLUI_ABSO'.

In order to choose the formulation ($u-p$ , $u-p-\phi$ or $u-\psi$ ), one will use the keyword FORMULATION in AFFE_MODELE.

They lead to volume or surface elements, for the fluid part, and to surface or line elements for the fluid-structure interface or for the absorbing elements.

### 3.1 Euler buckling, geometric stiffness

One considers the Euler buckling problem of an elastic solid in interaction with a fluid domain, modelled with the vibro-acoustic assumption. One models fluctuating fields. Euler buckling is therefore treated by searching for the vanishing of the natural vibration frequencies of the coupled system, including the presence of the geometric stiffness associated with the prestressed static state in the solid, controlled by a critical loading parameter to be determined.

One admits at first that the geometric stiffness associated with the initial static state in the fluid is negligible. One therefore proposes to define a zero geometric-stiffness matrix in the fluid domain and on the fluid/structure interfaces. However, this method of analysis neglects the fact that the fluid pressure is a follower loading for the solid, which therefore introduces a non-linear term, which often has notable effects on the critical buckling loads. It is therefore preferable to treat the problem by a non-linear dynamic analysis associated with a stability analysis, by driving the mechanical loading: consult the document U2.06.11.
