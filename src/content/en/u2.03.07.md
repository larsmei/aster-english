# u2.03.07 Overview of the tools available for carrying out structural analyses of civil-engineering concrete

> **Summary.**

The aim of this document is to provide an overview of the modelling possibilities open to the user according to the type of analysis
he or she wishes to carry out in civil engineering. Several choices are always possible, and the purpose of this document is not to take the place of the engineer's own analysis,
but rather to make it easier to choose the modelling options, according to the tools available, and to direct the user towards more specific documents.

## 1 Introduction

Behind the words “civil-engineering analysis” lie both analyses of structures whose dimensions and characteristics
are very varied (specimen, beam, building, ...) but, above all, analyses whose purpose or the information sought are
very different: design of a structure, prediction of long-term ageing, study of the cracking risk,
verification of the seismic resistance of a structure, reassessment of the margins. It is up to the modeller to find the best compromise
between the complexity, the cost of the study and the accuracy or the representativeness of the results sought, by correctly choosing
the type of analysis, the finite-element modelling and the constitutive models, given that a wide choice is available in Code_Aster.

### 1.1 The type of analysis to be carried out

The choice of the operators is made according to the type of phenomenon modelled (thermal, hydraulic, mechanical...) and the type of information
sought (local or global information, steady state or study of the transient, ...), the type of loading (static, dynamic),
and the non-linearities to be modelled (constitutive behaviour, large strains, loading, ...). The choice must be made from among the following operators:

- a linear thermal analysis (THER_LINEAIRE) [R5.02.01](doc:r5.02.01) in order to estimate the temperature of the concrete in the linear cases;
- a non-linear thermal analysis (THER_NON_LINE [R5.02.02](doc:r5.02.02)), when the properties or the boundary conditions are not linear, but also in order to model the thermo-hydration of the concrete or the evolution of drying (under the effect of the moisture gradient);
- a linear static analysis (MECA_STATIQUE [U4.51.01](doc:u4.51.01)) in the cases where the concrete is considered elastic and in the absence of any non-linearity;
- a non-linear static analysis (STAT_NON_LINE, [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)), as soon as one wants to take into account non-linearities of constitutive behaviour
(creep, damage, ...) or of loading (prestress, contact, ...);
- a thermomechanical analysis, by chaining a thermal analysis (linear or not) solved with THER_LINEAIRE or THER_NON_LINE and a static analysis (linear or not)
solved with MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE;
- a coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical analysis of porous-medium type, accessible via STAT_NON_LINE, in particular in order to study the exchanges of water or of gas
through a concrete wall (cf. [R7.01.10](doc:r7.01.10) and [R7.01.11](doc:r7.01.11));
- a vibratory dynamics analysis with the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03), which may be of transient or harmonic type, on a physical basis
or on a modal basis, with the possibility of taking into account certain localised non-linearities of shock or friction type. The operator DYNA_LINE [U4.53.05](doc:u4.53.05)
is also available and makes it possible to call on the same features as DYNA_VIBRA without having to concern oneself with the matrix-assembly stages. Moreover, with DYNA_LINE,
the construction of a relevant projection basis for the solution of the problem on a generalised basis is carried out automatically by the operator
from the information supplied.
- an analysis of spectral-method type by modal synthesis COMB_SISM_MODAL [R4.05.03](doc:r4.05.03), in order to compute the dynamic response to single or multiple imposed motions
and to design a structure;
- a direct transient dynamic analysis with DYNA_NON_LINE [R5.05.05](doc:r5.05.05) if one wants to model localised non-linearities of
friction type, or large displacements, or non-linear constitutive behaviours;
- an analysis of fluid flow in a cracked concrete structure (in 2D), with MACR_ECREVISSE [U7.03.41](doc:u7.03.41).

Finally, note that *Code_Aster* makes it possible to carry out a deterministic analysis, but that procedures have been put in place to make it fairly
easy to carry out mechanico-probabilistic analyses (cf. [U2.08.05](doc:u2.08.05)).

### 1.2 The type of modelling to use

It is possible to use:

- 3D solid elements [R3.01.00](doc:r3.01.00) or 2D surface elements, when this is possible, by making use of simplifying assumptions
such as plane strain, plane stress or axisymmetry;
- plate-type elements (thin for the DKT, DST, Q4G [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) or thick for the Q4GG [R3.07.09](doc:r3.07.09)),
membrane-type elements (GRILLE_MEMBRANE, GRILLE_EXCENTREE, MEMBRANE, [R3.08.07](doc:r3.08.07)) or solid shell elements (COQUE_3D [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04), ...
- line elements by means of a straight Euler beam element (POU_D_E), or Timoshenko-type beams, straight (POU_D_T) (cf. [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01)),
multifibre beams in small or in large displacements (POU_D_EM [R3.08.08](doc:r3.08.08), POU_D_TGM [R3.08.09](doc:r3.08.09)).

These modellings can of course be used on their own or combined.

To help the user make a choice (in particular as regards the use of structural elements), the reader may refer to the document
[U2.02.01](doc:u2.02.01) or to the document [U2.06.10](doc:u2.06.10). Furthermore, the chapter U2.03.07-modele-meca of this document
details how to represent the reinforcement and the prestressing cables, according to the choice made for representing the concrete.

### 1.3 The constitutive law

This is to be chosen according to the phenomena that it is necessary to take into account, while being aware of their limitations,
of the difficulty of identifying realistic material parameters when one starts to seek very fine information,
of the robustness of the law and of the impacts in terms of run time, ... The characteristics of the main laws available
are summarised in this document in order to make the user's choice easier.

The following chapters aim to list the main possible choices, in particular in terms of modelling and of constitutive laws,
as well as the combinations, in order to make the choice easier.

## 3 Possible choices for the hydration analyses

There is a single hydration model in *Code_Aster* which makes it possible to describe the release of heat as well as the hardening, cf. [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12). It is solved via the operator THER_NON_LINE, and the constitutive behaviour 'THER_HYDR'. The model must contain only the concrete, modelled by means of solid or surface elements, preferably linear. The recommended modellings are 3D_DIAG, PLAN_DIAG and AXIS_DIAG (but 3D, PLAN or AXIS are also possible). Sufficiently small time steps must be used at early age (of the order of an hour up to the peak of thermal heating, then the time steps may be increased progressively) in order to obtain a solution of good quality, because an explicit discretisation of the heat source is used.

The temperature and the hydration may then be taken into account in the mechanical analysis in the form of command variables (TEMP and HYDR). In this case, it is recommended to solve the thermal problem with linear elements.

An example of the solution of such a problem is treated in the test case FORMA41.

*Note* **:**

*It is possible not to solve the thermo-hydration problem, and to define a hydration field analytically by means of the commands* CREA_CHAM *and* CREA_RESU *(In this case, the heating due to the thermo-hydration reaction is neglected). Thus one commonly uses the following formula, coming from an LCPC calibration:*

$h=\frac{t}{b+t}$ *, with* $b=1684800\mathit{s.}$ *, if* $t$ *is the time expressed in seconds.*

*(cf. test SSLA103 for an example of data setup).*

## 4 Possible choices for the drying analyses

It is possible to model the drying of the concrete under the effect of the moisture gradient (a simplifying assumption which makes it possible to model only drying by diffusion
and not by advection, for example) with the operator SECH_NON_LINE 1, (cf. [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12)). The model must contain only the concrete,
modelled by means of solid or surface elements, preferably linear (especially if one continues with a mechanical analysis), combined with lumped elements,
that is to say with 3D_DIAG, PLAN_DIAG or AXIS_DIAG (but 3D, PLAN or AXIS are also possible if the temperatures do not vary abruptly).
Five laws are available to represent the evolution of the diffusion coefficient $D$ as a function of the water concentration
(variable SECH expressed in $l/m^3$ for an analysis in SI units) and possibly of the temperature.
The expressions of each of them are given in [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) or [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12).

- SECH_MENSI, in which $D$ is a function of the water concentration;
- SECH_GRANGER, which is equivalent to SECH_MENSI but which takes thermal activation into account (that is to say the acceleration of drying when the temperature increases);
- SECH_BAZANT, in which $D$ is a function of the humidity (related to the water concentration by the sorption function);
- SECH_RFT, which corresponds to the Richards–Fick model in temperature and which models the permeation of water in liquid form and the diffusion of vapour in the gaseous mixture.
- SECH_NAPPE, which makes it possible to define an arbitrary evolution for $D$ in the form of a nappe that is a function of the water concentration and of the temperature.

The drying may then be taken into account in the mechanical analysis in the form of a command variable SECH, which corresponds to the water concentration in the concrete.
The various laws are tested in the test case HSNA100 [V7.20.100](doc:v7.20.100)

**Notes**

> *For more complex cases of drying (for example drying under the effect of a pressure gradient), it is necessary to resort to a
thermo-hydro-mechanical modelling which treats the equations of continuum mechanics by using the theory of porous media, possibly unsaturated,
and by considering that the mechanical, thermal and hydraulic phenomena are fully coupled, cf.* [R7.01.10](doc:r7.01.10) and [U2.04.05](doc:u2.04.05).
> *The identification of the material parameters is carried out from a mass-loss curve. An example is given for the law SECH_GRANGER in the test TTNV101.* [V4.26.101](doc:v4.26.101)

from version 17.4. Previously, one had to use THER_NON_LINE

## 5 Possible modellings for the mechanical analyses (static or dynamic)

A wide choice of modellings, but also of constitutive laws, is available in *Code_Aster* for the mechanical analyses.
It is up to the user to define the most efficient strategy according to the characteristics of the structure studied (containment, tank, slab, beam...),
the loadings applied (thermal, symmetric, 3D...), the information sought (global behaviour, limit load, cracking, creep, ...)
but also the speed of execution expected (complexity of implementation, difficulty of convergence, run time, ...).
In this chapter we are concerned with the modellings that are accessible; the following one concentrates on the constitutive laws.
The aim of this chapter is not to give advice on the choice of the most suitable modelling, but to give a synthetic view
of what it is possible to do in order to make the engineer's choice easier. More precisely, given that, in order to model the concrete alone,
all types of element are usable. Here we only summarise the possible combinations — or at any rate
those classically used — for representing concrete containing steels. In what follows, the entry point of each paragraph corresponds to the choice that is made
for describing the concrete: solid, surface, or by means of structural elements. For the practical details, on carrying out analyses
with the prestressing cables, and in particular the use of the operators DEFI_CABLE_BP and CALC_PRECONT, the reader is invited to consult the document
[U2.03.06](doc:u2.03.06).

### 5.1 Modelling of the concrete by 3D solid elements

The modellings and elements possible for the concrete are:

- the modellings 3D or 3D_SI in the general case, it being known that all the 3D isoparametric elements (hexahedron, tetrahedron, pentahedron, pyramid...) are
usable in linear or in quadratic form for the 3D modelling, whereas the 3D_SI modelling allows the use only of hexahedral elements
(linear or quadratic) and of quadratic tetrahedra. The quadratic elements are more costly but give results of better quality.
The use of under-integration nevertheless makes it possible to reduce the run times. In the case of a chained thermomechanical analysis, it is recommended
to use under-integrated quadratic elements (cf. [u2.01.10](doc:u2.01.10)). Note that, in the case of a local damage analysis, one may
prefer the use of a linear mesh in order to limit the phenomenon of localisation of the damage.
- the modelling 3D_GRAD_VARI if one wants to use a non-local damage model with damage gradient (cf. [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01)).
In this case, the elements must be quadratic.

## 6 Possible constitutive laws for the mechanical analyses (static or dynamic)

### 6.1 Constitutive laws usable for the concrete

The tables below list the main laws developed specifically to describe the concrete, distinguishing the phenomena considered.
The main characteristics of these laws are indicated in order to make it easy to identify the law best suited to the problem considered,
but it is strongly recommended to refer to the reference documentation before any use.

#### 6.1.1 Modelling of the delayed strains

|  | BETON_GRANGER_V [R7.01.01] | BETON_GRANGER [R7.01.01] | BETON_UMLV [R7.01.06] | BETON_BURGER [R7.01.35] | FLUA_PORO_BETON 1 [R7.01.30] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Basic creep | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |
| Desiccation creep | no | no | yes | yes | yes |
| Thermal expansion / Autogenous shrinkage / Desiccation shrinkage | no | no | yes | yes | yes |
| Thermal activation | yes | no | no | yes | yes |
| Influence of “ageing” (hydration, polymerisation, ...) | yes | no | no | no | no |
| Influence of the age of the concrete at the time of loading | no | no | no | no | no |
| Influence of the water content | yes | yes | yes | yes | yes |

Several generations of models are available in *Code_Aster*, but all the models have the objective of describing properly the long-term creep:
they do not seek to reproduce precisely what happens at early age, where the couplings between the phenomena are, it seems, more important.
The most advanced EDF R&D model today is the BETON_BURGER model. One may note that:

- the BETON_GRANGER model leads to a fairly rapid saturation of the creep strains;
- the BETON_UMLV model assumes that the delayed strains evolve in a linear manner in the long term, which generally leads to overestimating them substantially.
Moreover, the apparent Poisson's ratio is not controlled, and may evolve in an abnormal manner, including becoming negative.
- the BETON_BURGER model makes it possible to describe the whole set of behaviours and in particular to recover a logarithmic evolution as a function of time, which seems
to be a very commonly observed phenomenon. On the other hand, it has the drawback of not being linear as a function of the stress;
- the FLUA_PORO_BETON model comes from the work of the LMDC and of the CIH on the behaviour of dams. It is based on a poromechanical formulation and resembles
BETON_BURGER closely in the theory. It belongs to a set of models that can be coupled: Creep + Damage + RAG/RSI.

*Note:*

> *At present, no regulatory creep law is available in the code. However, if the creep strain to be modelled is limited
to a spherical strain (* ${\epsilon}_{\mathit{fl}}=C\times {I}_{d}$ *), it is possible to model it by using a command variable.
If it is proportional to the imposed strain, it is possible to use a variable Young's modulus.*

#### 6.1.2 Modelling of the damage/cracking of the concrete

To describe the degradation of the behaviour, there exist several categories of model. In *Code_Aster*, three types are available:

- softening plasticity models, which introduce irreversible strains,
- damage models, which make it possible to represent a reduction in the stiffness of the material (irreversible or not in the case of “reclosure” of the cracks),
- cohesive-zone models, which make it possible to represent the cracks explicitly, but on condition that the user has introduced
a priori into the mesh the potential cracking paths by means of interface elements.

Recall that, as soon as the model is softening (that is to say that the stress decreases beyond a certain level of strain), the results
are valid only if there is no localisation of the damage or of the plasticity (in other words so long as the degradation remains diffuse).
Indeed, if no specific treatment is carried out, as soon as there is localisation, the solution obtained becomes strongly dependent on the mesh,
both on the size (the smaller the mesh cells, the easier it is to break them) and on the orientation of the mesh cells
(the way in which the structure is meshed orients the direction of propagation of the damage).
Certain models put in place an energy regularisation which consists in adjusting the dissipated volumetric energy as a function of the mesh-cell size,
under the assumption of at most one crack per mesh cell): this technique makes it possible only to limit the dependence on the mesh-cell size but
does not act on the dependence on the orientation of the mesh.
The non-local modelling GRAD_VARI (which brings in the damage gradient) makes it possible, for its part, to regularise the problem by “spreading” the damaged
zone over several mesh cells. However, this implies having meshes that are sufficiently fine (ideally 5 mesh cells in the characteristic length)
in order to obtain a solution independent of the mesh and predictive of the cracking pattern.
The cohesive-zone models, for their part, oblige the user to postulate the potential cracking paths.

Let us also point out that the cracking models are models that are always complicated to implement, and that raise problems of robustness (even if the models
generally manage to reach the stress peak without too much difficulty). They are therefore to be used only when it is necessary, or to be interpreted
with caution (especially on the post-peak stages). For all these questions of robustness, one should refer to the documents
[U2.05.06](doc:u2.05.06) and [U2.05.07](doc:u2.05.07) in order to obtain advice on how to carry out an analysis.

- *The more one seeks to describe the cracking finely and to describe a long cracking path, the more the model must be irreproachable from a mathematical point of view.

computing the difference in displacement on either side of the damaged element in the case of a local model.*
- *The BETON_DOUBLE_DP model is the first model that was developed to describe the softening of the concrete. It has a major drawback,
which is that, because of the shape of the criterion, it is possible, in two- or three-dimensional loading configurations, to exceed the limit tensile stress.
It should be withdrawn in the near future.*

### 6.3 Constitutive models usable for the steel–concrete bond

In most cases, it is sufficient to consider that the bond between the steel and the concrete is perfect. In this case, it is sufficient that the steel mesh cells
and the concrete mesh cells rest on the same nodes. If the meshes are not compatible, then one must create kinematic relations between the nodes
of the two materials (which of course makes the analyses heavier). These kinematic relations are generated automatically by the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP
for a 1D mesh in a 3D mesh. For a 2D mesh embedded in a 3D mesh, it is possible to use AFFE_CHAR_MECA/LIAISON_PROJ in order to
generate these relations.

Nevertheless, it may happen that one wishes to take this phenomenon into account, for example in order to simulate a situation in which the bond between the steel and the concrete
is very degraded, or because one wishes to seek local information such as crack spacings and crack openings.
Models are therefore available in *Code_Aster*, but let us point out that the characterisation of the steel–concrete bond, and consequently the identification
of the parameters of the constitutive laws, remain difficult to obtain.

- For a modelling in AXIS or D_PLAN, the steel and the concrete may be meshed explicitly and connected by a joint element
(modelling AXIS_JOINT or PLAN_JOINT, [U3.13.14](doc:u3.13.14)), to which the constitutive law JOINT_BA [R7.01.21](doc:r7.01.21)
is assigned. However, let us point out that this is a very rich model but complicated to implement, and that it may present results that are not very physical
for large slips (significant recovery of stiffness) (cf. test SSNA112).
- For a modelling of the concrete in 3D, and a representation of the steels, either in 3D or in the form of a MEMBRANE, it is possible to describe the
steel–concrete bond by means of interface elements placed between the 2 materials (modelling 3D_INTERFACE [U3.13.14](doc:u3.13.14))
to which the constitutive law CZM_LAB_MIX is assigned (cf. [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11) and test SSNS110).

Finally, let us point out that it is possible to represent frictional cables in $1D$, by means of the modelling CABLE_GAINE [R3.08.10](doc:r3.08.10)
and of the constitutive law CABLE_GAINE_FROT. The friction model is of Coulomb type (cf. test ZZZZ347E).

## 7 Reinforcement design

For design studies, the Civil Master tool, accessible in the form of a plug-in in salome_meca (internally at EDF), makes it possible to use
easily all the functions dedicated in code_aster from pre-processing to pre-processing.
Here we merely list the various operators existing in code_aster in order to have access to the main features.

Thus, it is possible to compute the reinforcement of a structure for a load case by means of the operator
CALC_FERRAILLAGE [U4.81.42](doc:u4.81.42). The method used is that of Capra and Maury [R7.04.05](doc:r7.04.05) and it
is applicable for plate and shell elements but also for beams. It is possible to use either the rules of BAEL91, or

thanks to the command VERI_FERRAILLAGE [U4.81.46](doc:u4.81.46).

## 8 Miscellaneous

### 8.1 Modelling of the soil–structure interaction and of the structure–soil–structure interaction

Taking into account the soil–structure interaction (SSI) and the structure–soil–structure interaction (I3S) amounts to considering the energy dissipated in the soil and the
coupling effects between the soil and nearby buildings under dynamic loadings (earthquake). In statics, it makes it possible to have boundary conditions that are more realistic than a perfect clamping.

Several methods exist for modelling the interaction between the structure(s) studied and the soil.

- Model the soil explicitly with isoparametric elements (note that, in *Code_Aster*, only the elastoplastic constitutive model known as
“Hujeux” [R7.01.23](doc:r7.01.23) is capable of taking a cyclic loading into account. It makes it possible to consider the behaviour of granular geomaterials,
sandy clays that are normally consolidated or over-consolidated, gravels, ....). In statics, one can simply model the soil as elastic or with a
Mohr-Coulomb law [R7.01.28](doc:r7.01.28).
- Use soil springs for regulatory SSI analyses, in order to take into account the uplift and the reduction in the number of modes of a foundation (cf.
[U2.06.08](doc:u2.06.08)).
- Use MISS3D (cf.
[U2.06.07](doc:u2.06.07) for the classical cases (elastic domain) and [U3.06.21](doc:u3.06.21) for the more complex cases when taking into account
the spatial variability in the seismic analyses [U2.06.12](doc:u2.06.12)).

> It should be noted that MISS3D makes it possible to model both rigid and flexible foundations, heterogeneous soils — with a particular extension to stratified soils
(no account taken of the dip) — and rafts of arbitrary shape, and thus to allow dynamic-interaction analyses with one or more arbitrary structures (I3S).
> The *Code_Aster*–MISS3D chaining is based on a coupling of BEM-FEM type which makes it possible to take the SSI and the I3S into account in the dynamic analyses in seismic analysis.
Nevertheless, the solution being in frequency, only linear problems could be treated until now with this approach.

- Use MISS3D for the complex cases. A new Laplace-time method, which also goes through the computation of the soil impedances with MISS3D, then makes it possible to
carry out non-linear transient analyses (with the use of DYNA_NON_LINE).

### 8.2 Macro to assist with the identification of the material parameters

For a certain number of constitutive laws, the identification of the material parameters can prove relatively complex. Accordingly,
certain macros have been developed to help the users carry out this stage, and even trade-specific tools under salome_meca. These are:

- DEFI_GLRC [U4.42.06](doc:u4.42.06) for the global laws GLRC_DM and GLRC_DAMAGE, which makes it possible to obtain the properties
of the homogenised concrete from the physical and geometric characteristics of the reinforced concrete;
- DEFI_MATER_GC [U4.42.07](doc:u4.42.07) for (i) the laws of MAZARS or MAZARS_UNIL by using the rules coming from BAEL91,
from Eurocode 2 or by supplying classical material characteristics (ii) for the laws ENDO_FISS_EXP, ENDO_LOCA_EXP or ENDO_LOCA_TC;
- the trade-specific tool ARCADE [SU1.18.01] under salome_meca which makes it possible to identify the parameters of the creep law BETON_BURGER_FP
and even to recalibrate them in the case of containment buildings;
- the trade-specific tool for identification of the parameters of the reinforced-concrete law DHRC[SU1.10.01]. Let us point out that the use of this tool
can also make it possible to give the realistic linear response for arbitrary reinforced-concrete plate sections in bending and in membrane,
to check the homogenised properties or to quantify the bending–membrane coupling effects, for example.

Also to be noted, in a closely related area, the existence of the command CALC_ESSAI_GEOMECA [U4.90.21](doc:u4.90.21), which makes it possible
to check the relevance of the parameters of identified soil laws by obtaining the response at a material point for various tests
such as monotonic or cyclic triaxial tests, drained or undrained, oedometer tests, etc.

The reference documentation remains, in any event, an indispensable source of information, it being known that, in addition,
for certain laws, there exist internally at EDF Model Identity Sheets which set out the identification techniques.

## 9 What Code_Aster cannot do

No model is available in a direct manner in *Code_Aster* for representing:

- the damage of the concrete at high temperature (no damage of thermal origin),
- the Transient Thermal Creep (irreversible strain which occurs when the concrete is heated above 100°C while it is subjected to a compressive loading),
- the carbonation of the concrete,
- the cracking of the concrete at early age,
- taking thermal radiation into account in the analyses (one can however use the SYRTHES code),
- the hydration–drying coupling,
- a creep model adapted specifically to early age or to temperatures above 80°C.
- taking drying and hydration into account for the structural elements.

- Practical exercises to put into practice a certain number of notions, capitalised in the form of *code_aster* test cases:

  - FORMA40: to set up a first modelling of reinforced concrete, in 3D or with DKT elements
  - FORMA41: to set up a chained thermo-hydration analysis, then drying, then mechanical
  - FORMA42: to set up the modelling of prestressed concrete (in 3D)
  - FORMA43: to set up a modelling of reinforced concrete with a multifibre beam model
- The *code_aster* documents of type U2, which are very numerous and give practical advice on very varied subjects.
