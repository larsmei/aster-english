# u4.01.06 New features and modifications of version 10

**Summary:**

The purpose of this document is to provide an overall view of the syntax modifications and of the new possibilities of the *Code_Aster* commands introduced during the development of version 10, that is, since version 9.4.10.

For further details, one should consult the documentation of the commands and the histor file of the corresponding sub-version (e.g.: [10.1.7], section *Support/Histor* of the site www.code-aster.org).

## 1 Fracture mechanics

### 1.1 XFEM

Formulations:

- Frictional contact in small/large sliding
- Without contact
- Axisymmetry
- Large rotations

Features:

- Multiple cracking
- Junctions
- Boundary conditions on XFEM nodes
- Prescribed pressure on an XFEM boundary

Scope of use:

- Non-linear constitutive behaviours
- Thermo-mechanics

Crack propagation:

Possibility of multiple cracks, including in 3D: PROPA_FISS

Post-processings: principle of iso-functionality between FEM and XFEM

Standard FE post-processings:

- Cleavage failure probability: POST_BORDET
- Extension to the free mesh: POST_K1_K2_K3
- Improvement of the performance of POST_RCCM

Post-processings exploiting the XFEM framework:

- Plane and non-plane cracks POST_K1_K2_K3
- Extension to the non-linear case CALC_G

### 1.2 Cohesive models

Features:

- Crack initiation and propagation
- Fatigue
- Frictional contact
- Hydro-mechanical coupling

Fields of application:

- Statics and dynamics
- Linear and non-linear
- Brittle and ductile fracture

Family of modellings:

- Joint elements
- Elements with a discontinuity
- Interface elements

Soil–structure

- Extension to a non-linear structure: CALC_MISS
- Response to a seismic motion varying in space: DYNA_ISS_VARI

### 2.3 Modal analysis

Rotating machines:

- Modal analysis of shaft lines

Cracked structures:

- Extension to XFEM
- Computation of the modal $G$ by K_G_MODA

Modal analysis:

Soil–structure interaction:

- Pre-processing, analysis and post-processing CALC_MISS

Constitutive behaviour:

## 3 Thermo-hydro-mechanics

### 3.1 Formulations:

HH and HH2 modellings:

- Pure hydraulics, without thermal, without mechanics
- HH: 2 phases, 1 constituent per phase
- HH2: 2 phases, 2 constituents per phase

Two-phase flows in a porous medium

- SUSHI (Scheme Using Stabilisation and Hybrid Interfaces), finite-volume formulation

Flows in a crack / propagation under fluid pressure

- Joint with hydro-mechanical coupling
- Selective integration *_JHMS

Regularisation suited to porous media

- Modelling with a gradient of volumetric strain in order to avoid localisation problems

Damage model dedicated to heterogeneous materials

- Random distribution of micro-defects (Weibull) D_PLAN_GRAD_SIGM + ENDO_HETEROGENE
- Fracturing of rocks due to gas injection

### 3.2 Constitutive laws

Mechanics

- Cyclic Hujeux law: multi-mechanism law for sands and clays, earth dykes
- Laws of Laigle and Kleine, and viscous Drucker–Prager: viscoplastic laws for rocks (in particular argillites); rocks of storage sites

Hydraulics

- Mualem–Van Genuchten law: hydrodynamic law of unsaturated soils. Account taken of the disappearance/appearance of phases. Rocks of storage sites

## 4 Numerical methods

### 4.1 Non-linear methods

Newton method

- Adaptive time steps, event-driven DEFI_LIST_INST
- Mixed line search: extension to mechanics, thermal, THM

Alternative to Newton: IMPLEX

- Robust for softening problems: approximate solutions, dependence on the time step
- Supports automatic management of the time step

Large strains [Miehe *et al.* ]

- Isotropic and kinematic hardening, anisotropy
- Any constitutive law written under the HPP assumption
- Symmetric matrix, quadratic convergence GDEF_LOG

Mixed formulation $u-p$

- Treatment of incompressibility
- Linear and quadratic elements *_INCO_UP*

### 4.2 Contact

New operator dedicated to contact DEFI_CONTACT

- Data setup common to all the methods
- Better readability
- Significant gains in performance

New methodological documentation to help the user:

- User's guide for contact U2.04.04

## 5 Architecture, ergonomics, performance

### 5.1 Ergonomics

Easy programming of constitutive laws:

- No knowledge of the software architecture required: 1 file to create, 2 files to modify
- See the document [D5.04.01](doc:d5.04.01) “Introducing a new constitutive law”
- Exhaustive description via catalogues of constitutive laws

External programming of constitutive laws by UMAT interfacing

- User constitutive-law definition format of Abaqus
- Ease of compilation thanks to the as_run tool
- Small/large strains

Simplification of the field names

- From 3 items, one goes to 2: SIEF_ELNO_ELGA becomes SIEF_ELNO
- A translator is available

### 5.2 Performance

More efficient and measurable management of memory by display of the consumption

Generalisation of the parallel analysis

- Change one keyword ( *SOLVEUR* ) + specify the number of processors
- Distribution of the elementary computations
- Parallel solution of linear systems
- Iso-functionality with the sequential version
