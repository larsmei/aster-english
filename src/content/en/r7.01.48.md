# r7.01.48 Constitutive model MCC

**Summary:**

This document presents the formulation and the numerical integration of the modified Cam-Clay elastoplastic constitutive model within the framework of Generalised Standard Materials. This model is intended to represent qualitatively the phenomenology of granular soils, taking account of the effects of dilatancy/contractancy, of softening/hardening, as well as of the critical state. The parameters of the constitutive model are seven in total. Three define the isotropic non-linear elastic behaviour; three represent the initial reversibility domain. A combined kinematic-isotropic hardening carried by the volumetric plastic strain is driven by a last parameter.

The constitutive equations are solved according to a variational principle for which the optimality conditions are equivalent to a system of non-linear equations governing the incremental evolution equations of the internal variables. These are solved by employing a direct implicit time-integration method in MFront.

## 1 Introduction

Conceived in the nineteen-sixties to describe the behaviour of normally consolidated clays, the modified Cam-Clay constitutive model represents the essential phenomenology of granular soils under monotonic drained loadings: dilatancy or contractancy and softening or hardening, regimes driven by the evolution of the volumetric plastic strain, as well as the critical state. This internal variable is the only hardening variable of the model.

By means of a state parameterisation in the plasticity criterion by the hardening variable, the modified Cam-Clay model falls within the framework of Generalised Standard Materials. Consequently, a solution of the constitutive equations based on a variational principle may be proposed, making it possible advantageously to ensure the existence and the uniqueness of the incremental evolution problem of the internal variables. The robustness of the numerical integration of the modified Cam-Clay model is thereby greatly strengthened for the analysis of geotechnical structures in Code_Aster.

This document summarises the essential information on the presentation of the modified Cam-Clay model, as well as the solution of its constitutive equations in MFront. It updates the formulation and the numerical solution method of the model CAM_CLAY [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14)_, while remaining faithful to the elements of phenomenology predicted by the latter.

### 5.2 Computation of the algorithmic tangent operator

The algorithmic tangent operator $\mathbb{D}$, computed once the constitutive equations have been solved (block $\texttt{@TangentOperator}$), is obtained by a chain-rule differentiation:

$$
\mathbb{D} = \frac{\partial \Delta \boldsymbol{\sigma}}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}=\mathbb{C}_{n+1} : \frac{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^e}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}
$$

The first term $\mathbb{C}_{n+1}$ denotes the fourth-order elasticity tensor. It results from the expression for the elastic-energy potential  as:

$$
\mathbb{C}_{n+1} = \Upsilon(\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^e_{n+1}) \left(3K\mathbb{J}+2\mu\mathbb{K} - 2\kappa\mu\left(\boldsymbol{I}\otimes\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{d,n+1}^e+\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{d,n+1}^e\otimes\boldsymbol{I}\right)+\frac{\left(2\kappa\mu\right)^2}{K}\boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{d,n+1}^e\otimes \boldsymbol{\epsilon}_{d,n+1}^e\right)
$$

with $\mathbb{J}$ and $\mathbb{K}$ the fourth-order orthogonal projectors, such that for any symmetric second-order tensor $\boldsymbol{a}$:

$$
\mathbb{J}:\boldsymbol{a} = \frac{\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{a})}{3}\boldsymbol{I} \qquad \text{et} \qquad \mathbb{K}:\boldsymbol{a} = \boldsymbol{a}-\frac{\mathrm{tr}(\boldsymbol{a})}{3}\boldsymbol{I}
$$

One notes that $\mathbb{C}_{n+1}$ is not constant because of the non-linearity of the elasticity when $\kappa>0$.

The second term $\cfrac{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^e}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}$ is itself written:

$$
\cfrac{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}^e}{\partial \Delta\boldsymbol{\epsilon}}= J^{-1}_{eel}
$$

where $J^{-1}_{eel}$ represents the first upper-left block of the inverse of the Jacobian matrix in . The proof of this computation will be found in reference .
