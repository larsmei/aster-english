# v6.04.512 SSNV512 – Block cut by a vertical crack connecting between 2 horizontal cracks with X-FEM

**Summary:**

This test validates the approach to join with $\text{X-FEM}$ in case a crack plugs into 2 separate cracks. This is an test case where three cracks are introduced. The first two cracks are horizontal. The third vertical crack plugs into the first two cracks via the JONCTION key word of the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator. The approach is tested with and without contact.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a healthy square in which three interfaces are inserted, in red in Figure 1.1-a. Two interfaces are horizontal. The third interface, vertical, is defined between the first two and connects to them. The dimensions of the structure and the position of the interfaces are given in Figure 1.1-a and are expressed in meters $\text{[m]}$.

The material has an isotropic elastic behaviour, the properties of which are:

Young's modulus : $100\mathit{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : $0.3$

### 2.1 Contactless case

Without contact, each area shall undergo a rigid body movement corresponding to the limit condition imposed on its edge (right or left).

The energy of the structure is therefore:

${E}^{e}=0.$

The analytical solution displacement field is:

$u={u}_{x}(x,y){e}_{x},$

${u}_{x}(x,y)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-\frac{1}{4}\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left[-5,0\right[\times \left]-2,+2\right[,\\ -\frac{1}{2}\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left[-5,+5\right]\times \left]+2,+5\right],\\ +\frac{3}{4}\text{pour}(x,y)\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]-2,+2\right[,\\ +1\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left[-5,+5\right]\times \left[-5,-2\right[,\end{array}$

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\int}_{\Omega}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega .$

On a:

$\mathit{tr}(\sigma )=-(1+\nu )({p}_{x}(y)+{p}_{y}).$

The deformation tensor is obtained by applying the Hooke law:

$\epsilon =\frac{1+\nu }{E}\sigma -\frac{\nu}{E}\mathit{tr}(\sigma )I,$

$\begin{array}{c}\epsilon =-\left(\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu ){p}_{x}(y)}{E}-\frac{\nu (1+\nu ){p}_{y}}{E}\right){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ -\left(\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu ){p}_{y}}{E}-\frac{\nu (1+\nu ){p}_{x}(y)}{E}\right){e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\end{array}$

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}{\int}_{\Omega}\left[(1-\nu ){\left({p}_{x}(y)\right)}^{2}-2\nu {p}_{x}(y){p}_{y}+(1-\nu ){{p}_{y}}^{2}\right]d\Omega$

$\begin{array}{c}{E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}[{\int}_{{\Omega}_{1}\cup {\Omega}_{3}}\left[4(1-\nu )-4\nu +(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{2}}\left[9(1-\nu )-6\nu +(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega \\ +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{4}}\left[(1-\nu )-2\nu +(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega ].\end{array}$

#### 2.2.3 Case 3D

The structure occupies the ${\Omega}_{3D}=\Omega \times [0,1]$ domain. The boundary conditions of the 3D case are the same as the 2D case in the $(X,Y),$ plane a roll condition is imposed in $Z=0$ and the $Z=1$ edge is free of stress. The analytical solution stress tensor is therefore identical to the case of flat stresses ( *cf.* eq 2.2-4):

$\sigma =-{p}_{x}(y){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-{p}_{y}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$

The density of elastic energy is therefore identical to that of the 3D case. The solid is of unit thickness in the direction $Z$ . The expression of the energy of the structure is therefore identical to the case of the flat stresses, but the units are modified. We then have ( *cf.* eq 2.2-5):

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{E}40(7-5\nu )=2,2\text{MJ}\times {\text{m}}^{-1}.$

The $u={u}_{x}{e}_{x}+{u}_{y}{e}_{y}+{u}_{z}{e}_{z}$ analytical displacement field is obtained by integrating deformations:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}={\int}_{-5}^{x}{\epsilon}_{xx}\mathit{dx},\\ {u}_{y}={\int}_{-5}^{y}{\epsilon}_{yy}\mathit{dy},\\ {u}_{z}={\int}_{0}^{z}{\epsilon}_{zz}\mathit{dz},\end{array}$

Because the applied limit conditions are ${u}_{x}(x=-5)=0$ , ${u}_{y}(y=-5)=0$ and ${u}_{z}(z=0)=0$ .

It should be noted that the tensor of deformations is discontinuous. Indeed ( *cf.* eq. 2.2-6):

$\epsilon =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-\frac{2-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-2\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+3\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{3-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-3\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+4\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left]+2,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{2-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-2\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+3\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{1-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+2\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left[-5,-2\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$

The deformation field is observed to be discontinuous through the $y=-2$ and $y=+2$ equation lines. It is therefore necessary to distinguish the cases according to the domain on which one integrates to explain the value of integrals. The expressions of the ${u}_{x}$ and ${u}_{y}$ components are identical to the case of the plane stresses ( *cf.* eq 2.2-7 and 2.2-8) and one has for ${u}_{z}$:

${u}_{z}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{3\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{4\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left]+2,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{3\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{2\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left[-5,-2\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$

Either:

${u}_{z}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{3\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ \frac{4\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left]+2,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ \frac{3\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]-2,+2\right[\times [0,1]\\ \frac{2\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left[-5,+5\right]\times \left[-5,-2\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$ éq 2.2-9

The calculation of the square integral of the displacement standard must therefore again use a domain partition according to the $y=-2$ and $y=+2$ equation interfaces.

So we have:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{3D}}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega ={\int}_{{\Omega}_{1}\times [0,1]\cup {\Omega}_{3}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{2}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{4}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega .$

We finally:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{3D}}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega =\frac{20\left(1999{\nu}^{2}-3685\nu +2800\right)}{3{E}^{2}}.$

D'où:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}=\frac{1}{E}\sqrt{\frac{20\left(1999{\nu}^{2}-3685\nu +2800\right)}{3}}\approx 1,11785807089{\text{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}.$

## 13 Summary of the results

The kinematic opening of a crack connected to several other cracks is possible with $\text{X-FEM}$. However, in some cases these cracks must be linked together via the keyword JONCTION, even if these cracks are not connected to each other.

The approach was validated in $\mathrm{2D}$ for modellings C_PLAN and D_PLAN and elements for QUAD4 and TRIA3. The $\mathrm{3D}$ approach was also validated for elements HEXA8 and TETRA4, with and without contact.
