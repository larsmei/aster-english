# u3.14.06 Modelling 3D_INCO_UPG

**Summary:**

This document describes, for the modelling 3D_INCO_UPG:

- the degrees of freedom carried by the finite elements which support the modelling,
- the associated support mesh cells,
- the supported constitutive laws and loadings,
- the non-linear possibilities,
- the test cases implementing the modelling.

This modelling is based on finite elements suited to the treatment of quasi-incompressible problems. It is indispensable for carrying out limit analyses with the Norton-Hoff law and is also useful for studies presenting high plastic strains for which the classical displacement formulation proves insufficient (oscillation of the stresses). The formulation used is a 3-field formulation: displacement-pressure-swelling [R6.03.05](doc:r6.03.05), usable with all the constitutive behaviours written in incremental form and in elasticity. The modelling 3D_INCO_UPG has volume support mesh cells and accepts the TETRA10, the HEXA20, and the PENTA15.

## 1 Discretisation

### 1.1 Degrees of freedom

| **Modelling** | **Degree of freedom at all the nodes** | **Degree of freedom only at the vertex nodes** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 3D | DX, DY, DZ | PRES(*), GONF |
| --- | --- | --- |

- No kinematic condition can be imposed on the degree of freedom PRES.

### 1.2 Support mesh cell of the stiffness matrices

For the modelling 3D, the support mesh cells of the finite elements may be tetrahedra, hexahedra or prisms.

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Interpolation in displacements** | **Interpolation in pressure and swelling** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 3D | TETRA10 | Quadratic | Linear |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | HEXA20 | Quadratic | Linear |
|  | PENTA15 | Quadratic | Linear |

### 1.3 Support mesh cell of the surface loadings

| **Modelling** | **Mesh cell** | **Interpolation in displacements** |
| --- | --- | --- |

| 3D | TRIA6 | Quadratic |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | QUAD8 | Quadratic |

## 2 Supported loadings

The loadings available under AFFE_CHAR_MECA are the following:

- 'FORCE_ARETE'

Makes it possible to apply line forces to an edge of a volume element.

- 'FORCE_FACE'

Makes it possible to apply surface forces on a face of a volume element.

- 'FORCE_INTERNE'

Makes it possible to apply body forces.

- 'PESANTEUR'

Makes it possible to apply a gravity-type loading.

- 'PRES_REP'

Makes it possible to apply a pressure to a domain of a continuous medium.

## 3 Non-linear possibilities

### 3.1 Constitutive laws

All the constitutive laws usable on mesh cells of continuous media have a physical meaning for these modellings and can be assigned from the moment when they are accessible from COMPORTEMENT in STAT_NON_LINE (Cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)). Or in MECA_STATIQUE (linear elastic in small strains).

Let us point out that one constitutive law is specific to this modelling (dedicated to the limit-load analysis, cf. [R7.07.01](doc:r7.07.01)):

```text
/    'NORTON_HOFF'
```

### 3.2 Strains

The strains available, used in the constitutive relations under the keyword DEFORMATION for the operators STAT_NON_LINE, DYNA_NON_LINE and CALCUL, are (Cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

```text
/     'PETIT'
```

The strains used for the constitutive relation are the linearised strains.

```text
/      'SIMO_MIEHE'
       'GDEF_LOG'
```

Makes it possible to carry out analyses in large plastic strains.

In MECA_STATIQUE, one is in small-strain elasticity.

### 3.3 Newton method

For the solution of the problem by the Newton-Raphson method in the non-linear operators, the elastic matrix is not available. One must therefore use, under the keyword NEWTON for the operators STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE (Cf. [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11)):

```text
/    PREDICTION = ‘TANGENTE’
```

The prediction phase is carried out with the tangent matrix.

```text
/    MATRICE = ‘TANGENTE’
```

The matrix used for the global iterations is the tangent matrix.

**Note:**

The formulation used leads to non-positive matrices and the current solvers are not always able to solve properly the linear systems which are associated with them. In the event of a convergence difficulty, it may therefore be useful to test the other solvers available in the code or the other renumbering methods (cf. [U4.50.01](doc:u4.50.01)).
