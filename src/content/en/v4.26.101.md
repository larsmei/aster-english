# v4.26.101 TTNV101 – Concrete drying – Identification of parameters on the mass loss curve

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to give an example of a simulation of concrete drying and the calculation of mass loss. The mass loss curve is often used to identify parameters of drying laws.

The calculation of the mass loss (water) over time is done. A 3D sample is modelled. The drying law is SECH_GRANGER.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

One quarter of the $70\times 70\times 280\mathrm{mm}$ prismatic sample is considered.

### 1.2 Material properties

In the drying equation:

$\frac{\mathrm{dC}}{\mathrm{dt}}-div\left[D(C,T)\mathrm{grad}C\right]=0$

$D$ diffusion coefficient will be of the SECH_GRANGER form:

$D(C,T)=A\exp(\mathrm{BC})\frac{T}{{T}_{0}}\exp\left[-{\mathrm{QSR}}_{K}(\frac{1}{T}-\frac{1}{{T}_{0}})\right]$

- $A=2,5{10}^{-11}{m}^{2}/h$
- $B=0.12$
- ${T}_{0}=273°K$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Constant uniform temperature field over time of $20°C$

Concentration imposed on the edge of ${C}_{\mathit{eq}}=29,31l/{m}^{3}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The initial water concentration is ${C}_{\mathit{eq}}=106,42l/{m}^{3}$

### 1.5 Transitory

Drying is calculated over a period of $528h$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Quantities and reference results

The reference values are mass loss readings during the drying test from benchmark CONCRACK.

To calculate the water loss of the numerical test piece at a given moment, the following calculations shall be made:

1. for each finite element, the integral of the water concentration on the volume of l-element: $\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV}$, which is equivalent to the volume of water remaining (in l).
2. Somme des quantités d’eau restante $\sum_{\mathit{éléments}}\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV}$ : c’est le volume total d’eau restant dans l’échantillon (en l).
3. La concentration en eau moyenne (en l/m³) en divisant par le volume: ${C}_{\mathit{moy}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathit{éléments}}\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV}}{{V}_{\mathit{tot}}}$
4. Levolume perdu (en l) est: ${C}_{0}{V}_{\mathit{tot}}-\sum_{\mathit{éléments}}\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV}$ et la masse perdue (en kg) est: ${\rho}_{\mathit{eau}}({C}_{0}{V}_{\mathit{tot}}-\sum_{\mathit{éléments}}\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV})$ avec ${\rho}_{\mathit{eau}}=1\mathit{kg}/l$ .
5. La perte de masse (%) est : $100\times \frac{{\rho}_{\mathit{eau}}({C}_{0}{V}_{\mathit{tot}}-\sum_{\mathit{éléments}}\underset{V}{\int}C\mathit{dV})}{{\rho}_{\mathit{beton}}{V}_{\mathit{tot}}}=100\times \frac{{\rho}_{\mathit{eau}}({C}_{0}-{C}_{\mathit{moy}})}{{\rho}_{\mathit{beton}}}$ avec ${\rho}_{\mathit{beton}}=2410\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ .

Steps 2 and 3 are results of POST_ELEM option INTEGRALE command in Code_Aster.

All experimental points and numerical points are compared except the first one that is not relevant.

## 4 Summary of the results
