# v6.02.125 SSNL125 - Traction of a fragile bar: gradient damage

**Summary:**

This test allows verification of fragile damage laws with gradient ENDO_SCALAIRE Modelling A) and ENDO_FISS_EXP (Modelling B) in a one-dimensional, non-homogeneous situation. Because of its 1D character, this problem admits an analytical solution that displays two regimes of boundary layers: one of finite length (existence of a free boundary between the damaged zone and the healthy zone) and the other of infinite length (extension to the boundary of the part).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.125/Shape1.gif)

The structure studied is a long $375\mathrm{mm}$ bar. The problem being purely $\mathrm{1D}$ , its section is without influence.

### 1.2 Material properties

In model A, the material follows a fragile elastic constitutive law (ENDO_SCALAIRE) with a gradient of damage (modelization *_GRAD_VARI).

| ELAS | ENDO_SCALAIRE | NON_LOCAL |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E= $30000\mathrm{MPa}$ NU = $0$ | SY= $3\mathrm{MPa}$ GAMMA= $4$ | C_GRAD_VARI= $1.875N$ PENA_LAGR= $1.5$ |

In model B, the material is governed by an ENDO_FISS_EXP law for which the following parameters are given: E = 30,000 MPa, NU = 0, ft = 3 MPa, fc = 30 MPa (not affecting 1D), Gf = 0.1 N/mm, p=1.5, D=50 mm.

### 1.3 Loading conditions

The left part of the bar ($125\mathrm{mm}$ long) is required to remain rigid (locking the degrees of freedom displacement). The right part of the bar is subject to uniform ${\varepsilon}_{0}$ axial deformation, i.e. a forced displacement with linear spatial distribution. So only one parameter controls the load intensity: the required ${\varepsilon}_{0}$ deformation level.

In the perpendicular directions to the axis of the bar, the movements are blocked: the problem is purely $\mathrm{1D}$ . Moreover, since the Poisson's ratio is zero, no flange stress develops in these directions.

## 2 reference solution

We consider one of the constitutive laws gradient damage [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01) with a free energy density in the form of:

$\Phi (\varepsilon ,a)=A(a)w(\varepsilon )+\omega (a)+c/2{(\nabla a)}^{2}$ , where $0\le a\le 1$ refers to the damage variable and $w(\varepsilon )$ to the elastic strain energy. For ENDO_SCALAIRE law, $\omega (a)=\mathrm{ka}$ and the damage prefactor is written as $A(a)={(\frac{1-a}{1+\gamma a})}^{2}$ . The solution then depends on three material parameters $\gamma$ , $c$ , and $k$ (see [R5.03.25](doc:r5.03.25)).

In the general case, two partial derivative equations must be solved: the equilibrium equation ($\delta \Phi (\varepsilon ,a)/\delta \varepsilon =0$) and the $\delta \Phi (\varepsilon ,a)/\delta a=0$ behavior equation. Getting an analytical solution s is generally delicate, even for one-dimensional structures. To validate this model, however, one attaches to a simpler problem for which the equilibrium equation does not need to be solved, i.e. the displacement field is fixed everywhere. The behavior equation is then controlled by the strain energy elastic $w$ known in all point space. In this case, the semi-analytic solution is obtained by numerical integration of a differential equation with the initial Cauchy conditions.

The ENDO_FISS_EXP law is totally equivalent to the ENDO_SCALAIRE law in a uniaxial traction situation. The analytical solution is therefore identical to the ENDO_SCALAIRE law.

### 2.1 Characterization of the solution

More specifically, a bar with one part of the bar being forced to remain free of deformation is considered, while the other is subject to homogeneous deformation. The damage limit layer that develops at the interface of these two zones is studied. The differential equation of behaviour is as follows in areas where the criterion is met, i.e. where damage is developed:

$c\frac{{\partial}^{2}a}{\partial {x}^{2}}-\frac{\partial A}{\partial a}w-k=0$ , où $\frac{\partial A}{\partial a}(a)=-2(1+\gamma )\frac{(1-a)}{{(1+\gamma a)}^{3}}$ ,             éq 2.1-1

where $x$ is the space variable.

The strain energy elastic $w$ is known in both parts of the bar: $w=0$ on the left and $w({\varepsilon}_{0})=\mathrm{const}$ on the right. The solution is given by a continuous $a\in {C}^{1}(-{L}_{0,}{L}_{1})$ detrievable function.

Three boundary conditionss are available:

$\frac{da}{dx}(-{L}_{0})=\frac{da}{dx}({L}_{1})=0$ et $a(-{L}_{0})=0$                 éq 2.1-2

as well as two interface conditions:

${\left[\mid a\mid \right]}_{x=0}=0{\left[∣\frac{da}{dx}∣\right]}_{x=0}=0$                     éq 2.1-3

In total, we have five boundary conditions for five unknowns. Despite this fact, the problem is "unsure" to be solved numerically, because the boundary conditions are defined at the different points in the space. We proceed in the next process to identify unknowns using the boundary conditions and finally to obtain an ordinary differential equation of type Cauchy-Lipschitz.

### 2.2 Problem resolution in the unloaded area

It is assumed that the left end of the $x=-{L}_{0}$ bar is sufficiently far from the $x=0$ interface to maintain the validity of the hypothesis of a finite length limit layer in the unloaded area. Note then ${b}_{0}>0$ the length ( *a priori* dependent on the ${\varepsilon}_{0}$ deformation level in the loaded area) on which this boundary layer develops.

In part $x\in \left[{L}_{0},-{b}_{0}\right]$ damage does not change and remains null:

$a(x)=0$ on $\left[-{L}_{0},-{b}_{0}\right]$ eq 2.2-1

In the $x\in \left[-{b}_{0},0\right]$ part, the criterion is reached and the damage field is evolving according to the equation [eq 2.1-1], which is considerably simplified in this part of the bar since the elastic strain energy is zero:

$c\frac{{d}^{2}a}{{\mathrm{dx}}^{2}}=k$ on $\left[-{b}_{0},0\right]$ eq 2.2-2

By definition of ${b}_{0}$ , the following conditions are available:

$a(-{b}_{0})=0$ et $\frac{\mathrm{da}}{\mathrm{dx}}(-{b}_{0})=0$                     éq 2.2-4

The damage variable can then be expressed analytically by integrating [eq 2.2-2]:

$a(x)=\frac{k}{\mathrm{2c}}{(x+{b}_{0})}^{2}$ on $\left[-{b}_{0},0\right]$ eq 2.2-5

The values of damage and its derivative at the $x=0$ interface are then known, depending on the unknown ${b}_{0}$:

$a({0}^{-})=\frac{{\mathrm{kb}}_{0}^{2}}{2c}$ et $a'({0}^{-})=\frac{{\mathrm{kb}}_{0}}{c}$                     éq 2.2-6

and damage to the ${a}_{0}$ interface being strictly between $0$ and $1$ , the following frame is derived from the ${b}_{0}$ length :

$0<{a}_{0}<1\Rightarrow 0<{b}_{0}<\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{2c}}{k}}$                     éq 2.2-7

So just take the left side of the bar longer than $\sqrt{\mathrm{2c}/k}$ to make sure you have the damage profile confined to the left.

### 2.3 Problem solving in the loaded area

This part of the bar sees a homogeneous ${\varepsilon}_{0}>0$ deformation associated with an strain energy elastic ${w}_{0}$ . In this area, the boundary layer is no longer bounded and extends asymptotically towards the homogeneous response. It will therefore be assumed that the right end of the $x={L}_{1}$ bar is sufficiently far from the $x=0$ interface to allow for the approximation of a zero derivative for the "infinite" damage field. In the vicinity of the right end of the bar, the behavior equation [eq 2.1-1-o] then reduces to:

$\frac{-\partial A}{\partial a}({a}_{\infty}){w}_{0}=k$                         éq 2.3-1

where ${a}_{\infty}$ is the asymptotic value of the damage field. Considering the expression of $\partial A/\partial a$ ( *cf.* [eq 2.1-1]), the loading level can then be set by the ${a}_{\infty}$ value only:

for ${a}_{\infty}\in \left]0,1\right[,{w}_{0}=\frac{k}{2(1+\gamma )}\frac{{(1+\gamma {a}_{\infty})}^{3}}{(1-{a}_{\infty})}$ eq 2.3-2

On the other hand, the nonlinear differential equation [eq 2.1-1-o] can be written as follows:

$\frac{c}{2}2a'a''=a'(A'(a)w-k)$                     éq 2.3-3

It therefore admits a first integral:

$\forall x\in \left[0,{L}_{1}\right],{\left[\frac{c}{2}{(\frac{\mathrm{da}}{\mathrm{dx}}(s))}^{2}\right]}_{0}^{x}={\left[A(a(s))+ka(s)\right]}_{0}^{x}$             éq 2.3-4

It is recalled that the interface condition [eq 2.1-3] imposes an ${C}^{1}$ connection in $x=0$ , taking into account the expressions linking $a({0}^{+})$ and $a'({0}^{+})$ to the ${b}_{0}$ length of the boundary layer, it is possible to write:

${a}_{0}=a({0}^{+})=\frac{{\mathrm{kb}}_{0}^{2}}{2c}$ et $a{'}_{0}=a'({0}^{+})=\sqrt{\frac{2k}{c}{a}_{0}}$                 éq 2.3-5

In addition, the boundary conditions [eq 2.1-2] ensures the nullity of the $x={L}_{1}$ damage derivative, by making the $a({L}_{1})={a}_{\infty}$ approximation, the first integral [eq 2.3-4] evaluated in $x={L}_{1}$ is:

$A({a}_{0}){w}_{0}+k{a}_{0}-\frac{c}{2}a{'}_{0}^{2}=A({a}_{\infty}){w}_{0}+k{a}_{\infty}$                 éq 2.3-6

The equation [eq 2.3-6] is simplified by [eq 2.3-5], and then reduced to the trinomal of the following second degree of unknown ${a}_{0}$:

$(f({a}_{\infty}){\gamma}^{2}-1){a}_{0}^{2}+2(f({a}_{\infty})\gamma +1){a}_{0}+(f({a}_{\infty})-1)=0$ où $f({a}_{\infty})=A({a}_{\infty})+\frac{k{a}_{\infty}}{{w}_{0}}$     éq 2.3-7

This provides a simple relationship between the ${a}_{\infty}$ loading parameter and the ${a}_{0}$ value of the damage and its $a{'}_{0}$ derivative at the interface.

The resolution of the nonlinear EDO [eq 2.1-1-o] on $\left[0,{L}_{1}\right]$ was achieved by bringing it back to a differential order 1 system, numerically integrated with a Runge-Kutta schema in order 4 (space). The implementation of this schema requires only knowledge of the initial $({a}_{0,}a{'}_{0})$ conditions associated with each ${a}_{\infty}$ load parameter, which is ensured by the [eq 2.3-o] relationship.

### 2.4 Problem solving in the entire bar

To calculate the reference solution on the entire bar, the following diagram is used:

- choice of a loading parameter ${a}_{\infty}\in \left]0,1\right[$
- *Resolution in the section concerned:*
- determination with [eq 2.3-2] of the strain energy elastic ${w}_{0}$ associated
- determination with [eq 2.3-7] of the boundary conditions $({a}_{0,}a{'}_{0})$ game
- digital integration with a Runge-Kutta schema in order 4
- *Resolution in the unloaded part:*
- determination with [eq 2.3-7] and [eq 2.3-5] of the size ${b}_{0}$ of the boundary layer in the unloaded area
- obtain $a(x)$ analytical field in this area with [eq 2.2-5]

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics et du mesh

This is a model axisymmetric (AXIS_GRAD_VARI). The corresponding geometry is a rectangle, i.e. the bar is arranged vertically and its section (without influence) is circular.

The mesh is made up of one element according to the radius. According to the axis, the elements have a size of $2.5\mathrm{mm}$. The mesh thus generated is finally made up of 150 elements quadrangular to 8 nodes.
