# v6.04.246 SSNV246 - Application of a pressure distributed over the lips of a XFEM curved interface through a column

**Summary:**

This is a test to validate the contact facets of XFEM integration elements.

The contact facets used by default are not from the integration elements subs and do not give the possibility to have quadratic 3D contact facets. It is possible to enable the recovery of contact facets from the integration elements subs into the MODI_MODELE_XFEM operator with the DECOUPE_FACETTE='SOUS_ELEMENTS' key word. Linear and quadratic facets in 2D as well as in 3D have the advantage of being in line with the integration elements subs since they are chosen from the sides of the elements subs of the 2D integration and the faces of the elements subs of the 3D integration. This recovery mode also has the following advantage: the nodes coordinates of these facets are not recalculated as is the case by default, since these nodes are recovered during the cutting that is performed in TOPOSE.

In this test, the correct operation of the recovery of these facets is ensured when mechanical pressure is applied to the lips of the interface. The results obtained are compared to an analytical solution. The geometry is two-dimensional, an example of the use of the contact facets from the integration elements subs for a 3D geometry is presented in the test case ssnv247 [v6.04.247](doc:v6.04.247). A "lsn" curve is chosen voluntarily representing the interface to appreciate the performance gain brought by quadratic "facetization".

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of the problem

This is a square on the $L=10m$ side. This bar has an interface type discontinuity (unmesh interface which is introduced into the model via the level-sets thanks to the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator). The square is thus completely crossed by discontinuity (at the approximation of the field of displacement, only Heaviside enrichment is taken into account). The discontinuity is circular of center $O(0,-2)$ and radius $R=9m$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

The following Dirichlet conditions shall be applied:

- on the lower side of the square, the movements are blocked in all directions (${u}_{\text{x}}=0$ and ${u}_{\text{y}}=0$ ),
- On the upper side of the square, the following $x$ moves are blocked ${u}_{\text{x}}=0$ and a square crush is imposed following $y$ , ${u}_{\text{y}}={u}_{\text{y, impo}}=-{1.E}^{-6}$ .

The crack is applied "pressure and contact shear". That is, the forces that would be applied to the crack if the square were not cracked or if there was a perfect adhesion contact. Neuman's conditions are as follows:

- On each of the interface lips, distributed pressure is imposed $p(\theta )={\sigma}_{yy}\ast \mathit{cos²}(\theta )$ via AFFE_CHAR_MECA and the keywordFISSURE dePRES_REP.
- On each of the interface lips, $t(\theta )=-{\sigma}_{yy}\ast \cos(\theta )\sin(\theta )$ distributed shear is imposed via AFFE_CHAR_MECA and the keywordFISSURE of CISA_2D.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is an analytical solution. Taking into account the boundary conditions, the displacements can be obtained from the analytical resolution of the motion quantity conservation equation.

The Poisson's ratio $\nu$ is zero, so the problem is one-dimensional following $y$. The stress tensor is uniform throughout the domain: $\sigma =Eϵ=E\ast {ϵ}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$ .

$\text{Div}(\sigma )=0$ Gold, therefore $\frac{\partial {ϵ}_{yy}}{\partial y}=0$ . Based on boundary conditions applied ${ϵ}_{yy}=\frac{{u}_{\text{y, impo}}}{L}$

Finally, $\sigma =E\ast \frac{{u}_{\text{y, impo}}}{L}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}={\sigma}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$.

At the crack level, ${e}_{r}=-\sin(\theta ){e}_{\text{x}}+\cos(\theta ){e}_{\text{y}}$ and ${e}_{\theta}=-\cos(\theta ){e}_{\text{x}}-\sin(\theta ){e}_{\text{y}}$.

At the point of the $(R,\theta )$ coordinate crack, if there were no cracks, one would:

$\sigma .{e}_{\text{r}}=({e}_{\text{r}}.\sigma .{e}_{\text{r}}){e}_{\text{r}}+({e}_{\text{r}}.\sigma .{e}_{\theta}){e}_{\theta}={\sigma}_{yy}\ast [{({e}_{y}.{e}_{r})}^{2}{e}_{\text{r}}+({e}_{y}.{e}_{r})x({e}_{y}.{e}_{\theta}){e}_{\theta}]$

$\sigma .{e}_{\text{r}}={\sigma}_{yy}\ast (\mathit{cos²}(\theta ){e}_{\text{r}}-\sin(\theta )\cos(\theta ){e}_{\theta})$

Finally, the solution to the problem is:

$\sigma ={\sigma}_{yy}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$

$u(x,y)=\frac{{u}_{\text{y, impo}}}{L}\ast (\frac{L}{2}+y)$

The previous two modellings validate the use of contact facets from the elements integration subs for the application of mechanical pressure and shear to crack lips for linear and quadratic modellings 2D. In particular, the significant gain in precision is seen when using quadratic elements. Even if the discontinuity is curved, the accuracy is excellent with very little elements.

## 5 Conclusion

Both modellings validate the use of contact facets from elements integration XFEM for applying mechanical pressure forces and 2D shearing to the lips of an unmesh crack. They also illustrate the gain in precision obtained with quadratic elements compared to linear elements.
