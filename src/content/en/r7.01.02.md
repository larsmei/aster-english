# r7.01.02 Modelling of prestressing cables

**Summary:**

In order to improve the strength of certain civil-engineering structures, prestressed concrete is used: to this end, the concrete is compressed by means of steel prestressing cables. In *Code_Aster*, it is possible to carry out analyses of such structures: the prestressing cables are modelled either by two-node BARRE elements or by 3-node CABLE_GAINE elements, which are then linked kinematically to the volume or plate elements which constitute the concrete part of the structure. To carry out this analysis, there exist three commands specific to these prestressing cables: DEFI_CABLE_BP, which makes it possible to define the cable geometrically and the tensioning conditions, AFFE_CHAR_MECA, operand RELA_CINE_BP, which makes it possible to transform the information computed by DEFI_CABLE_BP into a loading for the structure, and CALC_PRECONT, which makes it possible to apply the prestress to the structure.

The main specific features of the modelling based on the BARRE or CABLE_GAINE elements in the bonded case are the following:

- The tension profile along a cable can be computed (i) either according to the BPEL 91 regulation [bib1], taking into account the anchorage draw-in, the loss by straight and curved friction, the relaxation of the cables, the creep and the shrinkage of the concrete, (ii) or according to the ETC-C regulation, taking into account the anchorage draw-in, the loss by friction and by relaxation of the cables. In both these cases, the cable/concrete connection is assumed perfect, as for ducts injected with a grout.
- It is possible to define an anchorage zone (instead of an anchorage point) in order to attenuate the stress singularities due to the application of the tension on a single node of the cable (effect of the modelling).
- The behaviour of the cables is elastoplastic, the thermal expansion being able to be taken into account.
- Thanks to the operator CALC_PRECONT, one can simulate the phasing of the tensioning of the cables and the tensioning can be carried out in several time steps in the event of the appearance of non-linearities. Finally, the final tension in the cable is strictly equal to the tension prescribed by the BPEL.
- The cables being modelled by finite elements, their stiffness remains active throughout the analyses.

The main specific features of the modelling based on the CABLE_GAINE elements, in the unbonded cases, are the following:

- It is possible to model bonded, sliding or frictional cable–concrete connections.
- In the bonded case, it is the tension profile computed according to the BPEL91 which is imposed; in the frictional and sliding cases, the profile is obtained by simulating the tensioning of the cable and, if need be, the anchorage draw-in. The other types of loss cannot be taken into account.

The operators DEFI_CABLE_BP and CALC_PRECONT are compatible with all types of solid mechanical finite elements and with the plate elements (DKT, Q4GG) for the description of the concrete medium crossed by the prestressing cables and for the tensioning.

## 1 Preliminaries

Certain civil-engineering structures are made up not only of concrete and of passive steel reinforcements, but also of prestressing cables. The analysis of these structures by the finite-element method then requires the integration not only of the geometric and material characteristics of these cables but also of their initial tension.

The operator DEFI_CABLE_BP has been designed according to the prescriptions of the BPEL 91 regulation, which makes it possible to define the tension in a flat-rate manner. The mechanisms taken into account by this operator are then the following:

- the tensioning of a cable by one or two ends,
- the loss of tension due to the friction developed along the straight and curved paths,
- the loss of tension due to the anchorage draw-in,
- the loss of tension due to the relaxation of the cable,
- the loss of tension due to the shrinkage and the creep of the concrete.

It is also possible to use the prescriptions of the ETC-C to define the tension. In this case, the mechanisms taken into account are the following:

- the tensioning of a cable by one or two ends,
- the loss of tension due to friction and to the in-line losses,
- the loss of tension due to the anchorage draw-in,
- the loss of tension due to the relaxation of the cable.

Finally, the tension in the cable can be obtained by a mechanical analysis using the CABLE_GAINE modelling and the law CABLE_GAINE_FROT, which is inspired by the BPEL 91. In this case, the mechanisms taken into account are the following:

- the tensioning of a cable by one or two ends,
- the loss of tension due to the friction developed along the straight and curved paths,
- the loss of tension due to the anchorage draw-in.

The cables are modelled by two-node BARRE elements or by 3-node CABLE_GAINE elements, which implies adopting an approximate layout in the case of curved layouts. This can be done as close as possible to reality without any major restriction (the cable nodes must lie inside the volume of the concrete elements) with regard to the mesh of the concrete elements. The concrete part of the structure can be modelled by means of any type of $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$ volume element or with the DKT plate elements. The operator DEFI_CABLE_BP has the possibility of creating kinematic conditions between the nodes of BARRE elements and the $\mathrm{2D}$ or $\mathrm{3D}$ elements which do not coincide in space. This has the advantage of simplifying the creation of the mesh and of leaving the user free as regards the arrangement of the elements and their number. For the BARRE elements and the CABLE_GAINE elements in the bonded case, the prestressing-cable / concrete connection is of perfect type, with no possibility of relative slip. In the other cases, the cable can slide in its duct. The operator also makes it possible to define a stress-diffusion cone around the anchorages (only in the case where the cable is bonded) in order to limit there the stress concentrations, very much higher than in reality, which are due to the modelling.

The second main function of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP is to evaluate the profile of the tension along the prestressing cables, taking into account the technological aspects of their implementation. When the cables are installed, the prestress is obtained by means of hydraulic jacks placed at one or two ends of the cables. The tension profile along a cable is affected by the friction (straight and/or curved), by the strain of the surrounding concrete, by the draw-in of the anchorages at the ends of the cables and by the relaxation of the steels.

This tension can then be taken into account as an initial stress state when solving the complete finite-element problem. The problem is that, in this case, under the effect of the tension of the cable, the concrete and cable assembly compresses, leading to a decrease in the tension of the cable. To avoid this problem and to have exactly the tension prescribed by the BPEL or the ETC-C in the structure at equilibrium, the tension must be applied by means of the macro-command CALC_PRECONT. In addition, thanks to this method, it is possible to phase the tensioning of the cables or to impose the loading in several time steps, which can be of interest in the case where the behaviour of the concrete becomes non-linear as early as the cable-tensioning phase.

In the sliding and frictional cases of the CABLE_GAINE element, the tension profile computed by DEFI_CABLE_BP is not used. The tensioning is obtained by a finite-element analysis, still using the macro-command CALC_PRECONT.

## 2 The operator DEFI_CABLE_BP

### 2.1 Evaluation of the characteristics of the cable layout

#### 2.1.1 Interpolation by a cubic spline

The method used to obtain a geometric interpolation of the cables is presented here; this interpolation is indispensable in order to compute precisely the curvilinear abscissa and the angle $\alpha$ used in the prestress-loss formulae.

One begins by constructing an interpolation of the trajectory of the cable (in fact an interpolation of the two projections of the trajectory in the two planes $\mathit{Oxy}$ and $\mathit{Oxz}$ ), then, from these interpolations, one estimates the curvilinear abscissa and the cumulative angular deviation, according to the formulae:

$s(x)=\underset{0}{\overset{x}{\int}}\sqrt{1+{y}^{'2}(x)+{z}^{'2}(x)}\text{dx}$                                                 eq 2.1-1

$\alpha (x)=\underset{0}{\overset{x}{\int}}\frac{\sqrt{{y}^{''2}(x)+{z}^{''2}(x)+{\left[{y}^{''}(x){z}^{'}(x)-{y}^{'}(x){z}^{''}(x)\right]}^{2}}}{1+{y}^{'2}(x)+{z}^{'2}(x)}\text{dx}$                 eq 2.1-2

In order to preserve the topology of the cable (and in particular the ordering of the nodes which make it up) the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP works from mesh cells and mesh-cell groups (rather than from nodes and node groups), so as to be able to compute the quantities by following the sequence of the nodes along the cable.

The interpolation used for the computation of the prestress in the concrete will be a cubic Spline interpolation carried out in parallel on the three spatial coordinates as a function of the curvilinear abscissa. The coordinates of the nodes of the cable are the “real” coordinates, that is the coordinates defined by the mesh of the cable.

All the computations presented within the framework of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP are defined from the real geometry of the structures and from the real positions of the nodes. The computations of the tension at the nodes will be carried out from node to node, in the order given by the topology of the mesh, from the formulae cited above [eq 2.1-1] and [eq 2.1-2].

The computation of the cumulative angular deviation and of the curvilinear abscissa requires the precise computation of the derivatives of the trajectory of the cable, defined in the operator in a discrete manner by the position of the nodes of the cable mesh. Lagrange polynomials present instabilities, in particular for irregular meshes. Moreover, a large number of discretisation points will lead to polynomials of high degree. Furthermore, a small uncertainty on the interpolation coefficients will result in a large error on the results, in terms of derivatives. By choosing a polynomial interpolation of low degree, one will obtain second derivatives that are zero or discontinuous (depending on the degree).

The interest of a cubic interpolation of Spline type is to obtain continuous second derivatives and computation costs of order $n$ , if $n$ is the number of points of the tabulated function to be interpolated, with polynomials of low degree. The principle of this interpolation method is described exclusively in the case of a function of the form $x\to f(x)$ .

It is assumed that an interpolation of the tabulated function is carried out, from the values of the function at the discretisation points ${x}_{1},{x}_{2},\mathrm{...},{x}_{n}$ , and from its second derivative. One can thus construct a polynomial of order 3, on each of the intervals ${x}_{i},{x}_{i+1}$ , whose polynomial expression is the following:

$y=\frac{{x}_{j+1}-x}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}}{y}_{j}+\frac{x-{x}_{j}}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}}{y}_{j+1}+{{\text{Cy}}_{j}}^{''}+{{\text{Dy}}_{j+1}}^{''}$

with:

$C=\frac{1}{6}\left[{(\frac{{x}_{j+1}-x}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}})}^{3}-(\frac{{x}_{j+1}-x}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}})\right]{({x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j})}^{2}$

$D=\frac{1}{6}\left[{(\frac{x-{x}_{j}}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}})}^{3}-(\frac{x-{x}_{j}}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}})\right]{({x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j})}^{2}$

One can readily check that:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{ccc}y({x}_{j})={y}_{j}& \text{et}& {y}^{''}({x}_{j})={{y}_{j}}^{''}\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{ccc}y({x}_{j+1})={y}_{j+1}& \text{et}& {y}^{''}({x}_{j+1})={{y}_{j+1}}^{''}\end{array}\end{array}$

The values of the second derivative at the interpolation points must then be estimated. By writing the equality of the interpolations on the intervals $[{x}_{i-1},{x}_{i}]$ and $[{x}_{i},{x}_{i+1}]$ of the first-order derivative, at the point ${x}_{i}$ , one obtains the following expression:

$\frac{{x}_{j}-{x}_{j-1}}{6}{{y}_{j-1}}^{''}+\frac{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j-1}}{3}{{y}_{j}}^{''}+\frac{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}}{6}{{y}_{j+1}}^{''}=\frac{{y}_{j+1}-{y}_{j}}{{x}_{j+1}-{x}_{j}}-\frac{{y}_{j}-{y}_{j-1}}{{x}_{j}-{x}_{j-1}}$

One thus obtains $(n-2)$ equations relating the values of the second derivatives at the discretisation points ${x}_{1},{x}_{2},\mathrm{...},{x}_{n}$ . By writing the boundary conditions at ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{n}$ on the values of the second derivatives, one obtains an $(n,n)$ system from which one can determine uniquely the value of all the derivatives, and thus obtain the interpolation function. Two solutions then present themselves for the establishment of the boundary conditions:

- fix the value of the second derivative in an arbitrary manner at the points ${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{n}$ , at zero for example,
- assign the real values of the second derivative at these points, if this datum is accessible.

One obtains a system of equations whose unknowns are the $n$ second derivatives of the tabulated function to be interpolated. This linear system has the particular feature of being tri-diagonal, which means that the solution is of order $O(n)$ . In practice the interpolation is decomposed into two stages:

- the first consists in computing the estimated values of the second derivative at the points, an operation which is carried out only once,
- the second consists in computing, for a given value of $x$ , the value of the interpolated function, an operation which can be repeated as many times as one wishes.

Tests carried out on the sine function, over three periods, show that the results depend strongly on the number of points, as well as on the distribution of the points of the curve to be interpolated (expected result), but that even in delicate situations (few points and a very irregular curve) the interpolation does not diverge. In other words, even if the correlation concerning the trajectory of the cable is not very good (interpolation with very few points) the interpolation will be situated approximately within a bracket close to the real trajectory. This case will not arise in practice, but it makes it possible to check the stability of the interpolation method.

For the problem considered here, one cannot always write the trajectory of the cable in the form $[y(x)],[z(x)]$ , in the cases where this curve is not bijective, in particular when the projection of the trajectory in one of the two planes $\mathit{Oxy}$ or $\mathit{Oxz}$ is cyclic or closed (case of a circular concrete structure).

By taking an intermediate variable of the type $u=\int\mid {x}^{'}\mid ` , a parameter which is always increasing and whose increment is identical in absolute value to that of the variable :math:`x$ , one can reduce the problem to expressions $[y(u)]$ which are bijective functions of the variable $u$ . The cubic Spline interpolation described above is then applicable to the function $y(u)$ (as well as to the function $z(u)$ ). In practice, however, this leads to problems of matching of the tangent (angular points) at the points where the variable $x$ changes its direction of variation, and to local irregularities.

The trajectory of the cable is described as a parametric curve. Knowing a set of points of the curve, the most readily accessible parameter is then the curvilinear abscissa. The trajectory of the cable is written in the form $[x(p),y(p)]$ , in the plane $\mathit{Oxy}$ (respectively $[x(p),y(p),z(p)]$ in a three-dimensional space).

The cumulative chord $p$ discretised at the tabulated points of the function that one interpolates, ${P}^{1},{P}^{2,}\mathrm{...},{P}^{n}$ , is computed in the following way:

$p(1)=0$ at the point ${P}^{1}$ ,

$p(k)=p(k-1)$ + distance $({P}^{k-1}{P}^{k})$ at the point ${P}^{k}$

One thus has two curves defined by a set of pairs [x(i),p(i)] and [y(i),p(i)] to which one can apply directly the cubic Spline interpolation presented previously, and which makes it possible to avoid the difficulties encountered previously. The interpolation is carried out for the two coordinates (or three coordinates, in dimension 3), independently of one another.

#### 2.1.2 Method without interpolation

It is possible to compute simply the curvilinear abscissa and the angle $\alpha$ without carrying out an interpolation. This method is obviously less accurate but it is very robust. Moreover, the finer the mesh, the more its accuracy increases. Now, the problems linked to the spline interpolation arise precisely when the mesh is too fine relative to the irregularities that it contains.

This method without interpolation is used when the spline interpolation has failed (see  2.1.3 ).

The computation of the curvilinear abscissae is very simple. It consists in summing the length of the cable mesh cells.

**Computation of the cumulative angular deviation:**

The following example is sufficient to describe the computation of the angular deviation by this method. The mesh cells ${M}_{1}$

, ${M}_{2}$ and ${M}_{3}$ constitute a cable. ${N}_{1}$ is the first node of the cable; the value of its cumulative angular deviation is $\alpha =0$ . The angle $theta$ is the angular deviation between the mesh cells ${M}_{1}$ and ${M}_{2}$ .

At every point of $]{N}_{1}{N}_{2}[$ the cumulative angular deviation is always zero because the vector tangent to the curve at these points is $\overrightarrow{{N}_{1}{N}_{2}}$ . At every point of $]{N}_{2}{N}_{3}[$ , the cumulative angular deviation is equal to $theta$ because the tangent vector at each of these points is $\overrightarrow{{N}_{2}{N}_{3}}$ ( $theta$ is the angle between $\overrightarrow{{N}_{1}{N}_{2}}$ and $\overrightarrow{{N}_{2}{N}_{3}}$ ). It has been decided that the vector tangent to the curve at ${N}_{2}$ is the average of $\overrightarrow{{N}_{1}{N}_{2}}$ and $\overrightarrow{{N}_{2}{N}_{3}}$ . This gives that the cumulative angular deviation at ${N}_{2}$ is $\alpha =\frac{theta}{2}$ .

With the same logic, the cumulative angular deviation at ${N}_{3}$ is $\alpha =theta+\frac{beta}{2}$ , and $theta+beta$ at ${N}_{4}$ .

#### 2.1.3 Check of the spline interpolation

In order to check whether the spline interpolations for the three coordinates of space are correct, one computes the number of changes of variation of the first derivative and the number of changes of sign of the second derivative. If the number of changes of sign is smaller than the number of changes of variation (+ an integer constant fixed at 10), one considers that the interpolation is of good quality. Otherwise, one passes to the method without interpolation.

### 2.2 Determination of the tension profile in the cable according to the BPEL 91

#### 2.2.1 General formula

The operator DEFI_CABLE_BP makes it possible to compute the tension $F(s)$ along the curvilinear abscissa $s$ of the cable. It is determined from the rules of the BPEL 91 [bib1].

Overall, one arrives at the following formulation:

$F(s)=\tilde{F}(s)-\left\lbrace {x}_{\text{flu}}\times {F}_{0}+{x}_{\text{ret}}\times {F}_{0}+r(j)\times \frac{5}{100}\times {\rho}_{1000}\left[\frac{\tilde{F}(s)}{{S}_{a}\times {\sigma}_{y}}-{\mu}_{0}\right]\times \tilde{F}(s)\right\rbrace$     eq 2.2.1-1

where $s$ denotes the curvilinear abscissa along the cable. The parameters introduced in this expression are:

- ${F}_{0}$ initial tension,
- ${x}_{\mathrm{flu}}$ flat-rate rate of loss of tension by creep of the concrete, relative to the initial tension,
- ${x}_{\mathrm{ret}}$ flat-rate rate of loss of tension by shrinkage of the concrete, relative to the initial tension,
- ${\rho}_{1000}$ relaxation of the steel at 1000 hours, expressed in %,
- ${S}_{a}$ area of the cross-section of the cable,
- ${\sigma}_{y}$ elastic limit stress of the steel,
- ${\mu}_{0}$ dimensionless relaxation coefficient of the prestressing steel.

In this formula, ${F}_{0}$ denotes the initial tension at the anchorages (before draw-in), $\tilde{F}(s)$ represents the tension after the losses by friction and by anchorage draw-in have been taken into account, ${x}_{\text{flu}}\times {F}_{0}$ represents the loss of tension by creep of the concrete, ${x}_{\text{ret}}\times {F}_{0}$ the loss of tension by shrinkage of the concrete, $r(j)\times \frac{5}{100}\times {\rho}_{1000}\left[\frac{\tilde{F}(s)}{{S}_{a}\times {s}_{y}}-{m}_{0}\right]\times \tilde{F}(s)$ the losses by relaxation of the steels.

**Note:**

*The introduction into these elements of tension losses is optional. Thus, if one intends to carry out a creep and/or shrinkage analysis of the concrete using an appropriate law with* *STAT_NON_LINE, these elements must not be introduced into the losses computed by* *DEFI_CABLE_BP.*

The evaluation of the losses requires knowledge of the curvilinear abscissa $s$ and of the cumulative angular deviation $\alpha$ computed from the first and second derivatives of the trajectory of the cable. The precise computation of these derivatives requires an interpolation between the passage points of the cable. This interpolation is carried out by means of Splines, which are better than the Lagrange polynomials, which present instabilities, in particular for irregular meshes (cf. previous paragraph).

In what follows each of the mechanisms intervening in the computation of the tension is detailed.

#### 2.2.2 Loss of tension by friction

We begin by computing the tension along the cable taking into account the losses by contact between the cable and the concrete: ${F}_{c}(s)={F}_{0}\exp\left(-f\alpha -\phi s\right)$

where $\alpha$ denotes the cumulative angular deviation and the parameters introduced are:

- $f$ friction coefficient of the cable on the concrete in the curved part, in ${\mathit{rad}}^{-1}$ ,
- $\phi$ friction coefficient per unit length, in ${m}^{-1}$ ,
- ${F}_{0}$ tension applied at one or at both ends of the cable.

#### 2.2.3 Loss of tension by anchorage draw-in

To take the anchorage draw-in into account, the following reasoning is made:

the tension along the cable is affected by the anchorage draw-in over a distance $d$ which is computed by solving a problem with two unknowns: the function ${F}^{\ast }(s)$ which represents the force after draw-in of the anchorage and the scalar $d$ :

| $\Delta =\frac{1}{{E}_{a}{S}_{a}}\underset{0}{\overset{d}{\int}}\left[F(s)-{F}^{\ast }(s)\right]\text{ds}$ $F(s)$ equals ${F}_{0}{e}^{(-\mathrm{f\alpha }-\phis )}$ $\Delta$ is the value of the anchorage draw-in (it is a datum) | ![Figure](images/10000000000003C500000247E5A3ED94766C2591.jpg) |
| --- | --- |

${F}^{\ast }(s)$ , the force after draw-in of the anchorage, is determined from the formula [bib1]:

$\left[F(s).{F}^{\ast }(s)\right]={\left[F(d)\right]}^{2}$ ,

The length $d$ will be determined iteratively by means of the previous integral. Other authors use different relations such as:

$\left[F(s)-F(d)\right]=\left[F(d)-{F}^{\ast }(s)\right]$

For the computation of $d$ , three particular cases may arise:

1. This loss by anchorage draw-in is localised in the anchorage zone. If the cable is curved, and the length of the cable sufficiently short, it may happen that $d$ is greater than the length of the cable. In this case, the loss of prestress due to the anchorage draw-in applies everywhere. One must compute the area comprised between the two curves $F(s)$ and ${F}^{\ast }(s)$ , which must be equal to ${E}_{a}{S}_{a}\Delta$ , and which thus makes it possible to compute ${F}^{\ast }(s)$ .
2. In the case where a tension is applied at each of the two ends of the cable, let us call ${F}_{1}(s)$ the initial tension distribution computed as if the tension were applied only at the first anchorage, and ${F}_{2}(s)$ the initial tension distribution computed as if the tension were applied only at the second anchorage. The value which must be retained at every point of the cable as the initial tension is $F(s)=\mathrm{Max}({F}_{1}(s),{F}_{2}(s))$ .
3. Finally, in the case where *d* is greater than the length of the cable, and when a tension is applied at each of the two ends of the cable (superposition of the two previous cases), the following procedure must be applied:

- computation of ${F}_{1}(s)$ , the initial tension computed as if the tension were applied only at the first anchorage and taking the anchorage draw-in into account (as in particular case 1),
- computation of ${F}_{2}(s)$ , the initial tension computed as if the tension were applied only at the second anchorage and taking the anchorage draw-in into account (as in particular case 1),
- computation of $F(s)=\text{Min}({F}_{1}(s),{F}_{2}(s))$ .

#### 2.2.4 Delayed strains of the steel

The loss by relaxation of the steel, for an infinite time, is expressed in the following way:

$r(j)\times \frac{5}{100}\times {\rho}_{1000}\left[\frac{\tilde{F}(s)}{{S}_{a}\times {\sigma}_{y}}-{\mu}_{0}\right]\times \tilde{F}(s)$

(${\rho}_{1000}$  the relaxation at 1000 hours in %; ${\mu}_{0}$ the relaxation coefficient of the prestressing steel and ${\sigma}_{y}$ the guaranteed value of the maximum load at rupture of the cable).

This relation expresses the loss by relaxation of the cables for an infinite time. The BPEL 91 proposes the following formula: $r\left(j\right)=\frac{j}{j+9.{r}_{m}^{0}}$ where $j$ indicates the age of the structure in days and ${r}_{m}^{0}$ a characteristic radius obtained by taking the ratio of the cross-section of the concrete structure, in ${m}^{2}$ , to the perimeter of the concrete section (in metres).

#### 2.2.5 Loss of tension by instantaneous strains of the concrete

The instantaneous losses are not taken into account in the formula [eq 2.2.1-1] used in *Code_Aster*. What the BPEL calls instantaneous loss of tension is in fact the loss of tension induced in cables already installed by the installation of a new group of cables. To model this phenomenon, the phasing of the prestressing must be represented in the Code_Aster analysis, that is the whole set of cables must not be tensioned at the same moment but successively, by chaining the CALC_PRECONT (see test SSNV164).

### 2.3 Determination of the tension profile in the cable according to the ETC-C

#### 2.3.1 General formula

The operator DEFI_CABLE_BP makes it possible to compute the tension $F(s)$ along the curvilinear abscissa $s$ of the cable according to the rules of the ETC-C [bib4].

The theoretical formula is the following:

$F(s)={F}_{0}-\Delta {F}_{\mu}-\Delta {F}_{\mathit{anc}}-\Delta {F}_{\mathit{el}}-\Delta {F}_{r}-\Delta {ϵ}_{\mathit{cs}}-\Delta {ϵ}_{\mathit{cc}}$

where:

- ${F}_{0}$ is the initial tension applied to the cable
- $\Delta {F}_{\mu}$ are the losses of tension by friction,
- $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{anc}}$ are the losses of tension due to the anchorage draw-in,
- $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{el}}$ are the losses of tension due to the elastic strain of the concrete,
- $\Delta {F}_{r}$ are the losses of tension due to the relaxation of the steels,
- $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{cs}}$ are the losses of tension due to the shrinkage of the concrete,
- $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{cc}}$ are the losses of tension due to the creep of the concrete.

The losses due to the elastic strain are estimated according to the ETC-C as:

$\Delta {F}_{\mathit{el}}(s)=\frac{{A}_{p}{E}_{p}\Delta {\sigma}_{c}(x)}{2E}$

with $E$ the Young's modulus of the concrete, ${A}_{p}$ and ${E}_{p}$ the cross-section and the Young's modulus of the steel, and $\Delta {\sigma}_{c}(x)$ the stress induced in the concrete by the prestress.

They can be estimated by simulating the phasing of the prestressing by means of the operator CALC_PRECONT. These losses are not taken into account in the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP.

The losses of tension due to the shrinkage of the concrete $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{cc}}$ and to the creep of the concrete $\Delta {F}_{\mathit{cs}}$ can be obtained by imposing an equivalent strain field after the tensioning of the cables. Here again, they are therefore not taken into account in the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP.

In the end, the formula implemented in DEFI_CABLE_BP is the following:

$F(s)={F}_{0}-\Delta {F}_{\mu}-\Delta {F}_{\mathit{anc}}-\Delta {F}_{r}$             eq. 2.3.1-1

The 3 types of loss are detailed in the paragraphs below.

#### 2.3.2 Losses of tension by friction

In accordance with the ETC-C, the losses by friction are estimated by the following formula:

${F}_{c}(s)={F}_{0}\left(1-{e}^{-\mu (\alpha +k\text{}s)}\right)$         [eq. 2.3.2-1]

where $\alpha$ denotes the cumulative angular deviation and the parameters introduced are:

- $\mu$ friction coefficient of the cable on the concrete e
- $k$ the in-line loss coefficient $[{m}^{-1}]$
- ${F}_{0}$ tension applied at one or at both ends of the cable.

#### 2.3.3 Losses of tension by anchorage draw-in

> The formula is identical to the BPEL. Refer to § 2.2.3 .

#### 2.3.4 Losses due to the relaxation of the steel

The formula given by the ETC-C is the following:

$\Delta {F}_{r}(s)=0,8\times 0,66{\rho}_{1000}.{\exp}^{9,1\tilde{F}(s)/{F}_{\mathit{prg}}}.{(\frac{\mathit{nh}}{1000})}^{0,75(1-\tilde{F}(s))/{F}_{\mathit{prg}}}.{10}^{-5}\tilde{F}(s)$     eq 23.4-1

where:

- $s$ denotes the curvilinear abscissa along the cable.
- ${\rho}_{1000}$ relaxation of the steel at 1000 hours, expressed in %,
- ${F}_{\mathit{prg}}$ rupture stress in the steel,
- $\mathit{nh}$ the number of hours after the prestressing corresponding to the date at which the losses by relaxation of the steel are computed.

In this formula, $\tilde{F}(s)$ represents the tension after the losses by friction and by anchorage draw-in have been taken into account and, normally, also after the elastic losses have been taken into account.

Two computation options are proposed, corresponding to the choice TYPE_RELAX='ETCC_DIRECT' or TYPE_RELAX='ETCC_REPRISE'.

If the user chooses the option TYPE_RELAX='ETCC_DIRECT', then the tension used to compute the loss due to the relaxation of the steels does not take the elastic losses into account but only the losses by friction and by anchorage draw-in.

If the user chooses the option TYPE_RELAX='ETCC_REPRISE', then the tension used to compute the loss due to the relaxation of the steels takes the 3 types of prestress loss into account. This tension must be supplied by the user (keyword TENSION under MODI_CABLE_ETCC). It will have been obtained in the course of a first analysis which may be described as a “short-term” state, by modelling the losses by friction, by anchorage draw-in and the elastic losses by modelling of the phasing (cf. test SSNV229B for an example of implementation).

### 2.4 Determination of the kinematic relations between steel and concrete

Since the nodes of the cable mesh do not necessarily coincide with the nodes of the concrete mesh, it is necessary to define kinematic relations modelling the perfect bond between the cables and the concrete.

The following paragraphs describe, in order, the spatial geometric considerations making it possible to define the notion of neighbourhood between the nodes of cable elements and of concrete elements, then the method of computation of the coefficients of the kinematic relations.

#### 2.4.1 Definition of the neighbouring nodes

The computation of the coefficients of the kinematic relations requires the determination of the “neighbouring” nodes of each of the nodes of the cable mesh. The diagram which follows symbolises a cable node and a concrete mesh cell:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/10000D0A000069D5000035895F67186A7B745950.svg)

The mesh cell defined by the nodes $1,2,3,4$ contains the cable node. The neighbouring nodes are therefore the vertices $1,2,3,4$ . If the cable node is situated inside an element with $p$ nodes ${P}_{1},{P}_{2},\mathrm{...},{P}_{n}$ , then the nodes ${P}_{1},{P}_{2},\mathrm{...},{P}_{n}$ are called “neighbouring nodes” of the cable node.

Plate elements without offset, and solid elements, are treated in the same way. The computation of the offset of each node of the cable mesh is necessary for the computation of the coefficients of the kinematic relations.

In the case of plate elements, when the cable node is characterised by a non-zero offset, the neighbouring nodes are defined as the set of the vertex nodes of the element which contains the projection of the cable node in the plane tangent to the concrete mesh. In the case where the cable node (or else its projection in the plane tangent to the concrete mesh) belongs to a boundary of an element, it is the vertices of this boundary which form the set of the neighbouring nodes.

#### 2.4.2 Computation of the coefficients of the kinematic relations

Throughout the descriptions which follow, the quantities are systematically expressed in the global frame of the mesh. The kinematic connections are therefore expressed as a function of the degrees of freedom expressed in this basis. The normals and rotation vectors are expressed in the global frame, unless explicitly stated otherwise.

In the finite-element modelling of the cable–concrete structure, the displacement of a material point of the concrete structure can readily be expressed by means of the shape functions of the concrete element or mesh cell whose vertices form the neighbouring nodes, as a function of the displacements of the neighbouring nodes of the “concrete” discretisation. Likewise, a quantity or a displacement of a point of the cable (or of its projection onto the tangent plane of the concrete mesh) is identical to the value of this quantity at the material point of the concrete structure which occupies this same position (perfect bond between the concrete and the steel), and is therefore expressed as a function of the value of this same quantity at the vertices of the element, by means of the shape functions.

If $(x,y,z)$ are the coordinates of the cable node, or those of its projection, and ${N}_{1},{N}_{2},\mathrm{...},{N}_{n}$ the shape functions associated with the concrete nodes ${P}_{1},{P}_{2},\mathrm{...},{P}_{n}$ , vertices of an element of the concrete mesh (or vertices of a boundary of an element of the concrete mesh), and $({x}_{i},{y}_{i},{z}_{i})$ the coordinates of node $i$ , then the interpolation of a variable $u$ on the element is written:

$u(x,y,z)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}(x,y,z).u({x}_{i},{y}_{i},{z}_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}(x,y,z).{u}_{i}$

$u$ being able to be a coordinate, or any other nodal datum.

The kinematic connections make it possible to express the identity of displacement between the node of the cable mesh and the concrete material point which occupies the same position. This corresponds to the hypothesis of a perfect bond between the concrete and the cable.

##### 2.4.2.1 Case where the concrete is modelled by solid finite elements

Taking up the previous notations and considering ${\text{dx}}^{c},{\text{dy}}^{c},{\text{dz}}^{c}$ the displacements of the cable node, and ${\text{dx}}_{j}^{b},{\text{dy}}_{j}^{b},{\text{dz}}_{j}^{b}$ the displacements of the nodes $j$ ($j=1,n$ ) of the concrete structure neighbouring the node of the cable, we obtain the following relations:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{dx}}^{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{c},{y}^{c},{z}^{c}){\text{dx}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{dy}}^{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{c},{y}^{c},{z}^{c}){\text{dy}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{dz}}^{c}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{c},{y}^{c},{z}^{c}){\text{dz}}_{{i}^{b}}\end{array}$

$n$ being the number of nodes of the concrete element neighbouring the node of the cable, or that of one of its boundaries. For each node of the cable, one obtains 3 kinematic relations between the displacements of the nodes of the two meshes, cable and concrete.

##### 2.4.2.2 Case where the concrete is modelled by plate finite elements

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/1000102400002113000021136F8325A8F33AA93B.svg)

Let ${P}_{0}^{c}$ be the initial position of a cable point in the undeformed geometry and let ${P}^{c}$ be the position of this same point after deformation. Let us call ${P}_{0}^{p}$ the projection of ${P}_{0}^{c}$ onto the surface of the middle sheet of the undeformed concrete shell and ${P}^{p}$ the projection of ${P}^{c}$ onto the surface of the middle sheet of the deformed concrete shell. Let $\overrightarrow{{n}_{0}}$ be the normal to the middle plane of the concrete shell at ${P}_{0}^{p}$ and $\overrightarrow{n}$ that at ${P}^{p}$ .

${P}_{O}^{p}$ is given by: $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{0}^{p}\\ {y}_{0}^{p}\\ {z}_{0}^{p}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{0}^{c}\\ {y}_{0}^{c}\\ {z}_{0}^{c}\end{array}\right\rbrace -\left[\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{0}^{c}-{x}_{0}^{b}\\ {y}_{0}^{c}-{y}_{0}^{b}\\ {z}_{0}^{p}-{y}_{0}^{b}\end{array}\right\rbrace \cdot \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{n}_{\mathrm{0x}}\\ {n}_{\mathrm{0y}}\\ {n}_{\mathrm{0z}}\end{array}\right\rbrace \right]\cdot \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{n}_{\mathrm{0x}}\\ {n}_{\mathrm{0y}}\\ {n}_{\mathrm{0z}}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

${P}^{p}$ is given by: $\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{}^{p}\\ {y}_{}^{p}\\ {z}_{}^{p}\end{array}\right\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{}^{c}\\ {y}_{}^{c}\\ {z}_{}^{c}\end{array}\right\rbrace -\left[\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{}^{c}-{x}_{}^{b}\\ {y}_{}^{c}-{y}_{}^{b}\\ {z}_{}^{p}-{y}_{}^{b}\end{array}\right\rbrace \cdot \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{n}_{}x\\ {n}_{}y\\ {n}_{}z\end{array}\right\rbrace \right]\cdot \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{n}_{}x\\ {n}_{}y\\ {n}_{}z\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The point ${P}_{O}^{p}$ belongs to a concrete plate mesh cell whose nodes are denoted ${P}_{1}^{b},{P}_{2}^{b}\text{et}{P}_{3}^{b}$ .

The eccentricity of the cable with respect to the concrete shell is defined as the distance $e=\parallel \overrightarrow{{P}_{0}^{p}{P}_{0}^{c}}\parallel$ and it is assumed that this eccentricity does not vary when the structure deforms: $e=\parallel \overrightarrow{{P}_{0}^{p}{P}_{0}^{c}}\parallel =\parallel \overrightarrow{{P}^{p}{P}^{c}}\parallel$

The displacements of the points of the cable and of its projection are introduced:

$\overrightarrow{{P}_{0}^{c}{P}^{c}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}d{x}^{c}\\ d{y}^{c}\\ d{z}^{c}\end{array}$ $\overrightarrow{{P}_{0}^{p}{P}^{p}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{dx}}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{dx}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{dy}}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{dy}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{dz}}^{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{dz}}_{{i}^{b}}\end{array}$

The “rotation” vector $\overrightarrow{\theta}$ of the plate at the point ${P}^{p}$ and the rotational degrees of freedom of the nodes of the plate are introduced: $\overrightarrow{\theta}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{drx}}^{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{drx}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{dry}}^{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{dry}}_{{i}^{b}}\\ {\text{drz}}^{b}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}_{0}^{p},{y}_{0}^{p},{z}_{0}^{p}){\text{drz}}_{{i}^{b}}\end{array}$

By definition of $\overrightarrow{\theta}$ , one has: $\overrightarrow{n}-\overrightarrow{{n}_{0}}=\overrightarrow{\theta}\wedge \overrightarrow{{n}_{0}}$

One can then write:

:math:``

$\overrightarrow{{P}^{p}{P}^{c}}=e\overrightarrow{n}$

By subtracting these two equations, taking into account the definition of $\overrightarrow{\theta}$ , one finds:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{dx}}^{c}-{\text{dx}}^{p}=e.({\text{dry}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0z}}-{\text{drz}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0y}})\\ {\text{dy}}^{c}-{\text{dy}}^{p}=e.({\text{drz}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0x}}-{\text{drx}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0z}})\\ {\text{dz}}^{c}-{\text{dz}}^{p}=e.({\text{drx}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0y}}-{\text{dry}}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0x}})\end{array}$

By injecting the shape functions into this last equation, one finally has:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{dx}}^{c}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{dx}}_{i}^{b})=e.((\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{dry}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0z}}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{drz}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0y}})\\ {\text{dy}}^{c}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{dx}}_{i}^{b})=e.((\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{drz}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0x}}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{drx}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0z}})\\ {\text{dz}}^{c}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{dx}}_{i}^{b})=e.((\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{drx}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0y}}-(\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p}){\text{dry}}_{i}^{b}).{n}_{\mathrm{0x}})\end{array}$

##### 2.4.2.3 Case where the node of the cable projects onto a node of the concrete mesh

The distance between the projection ${P}_{0}^{p}$ of the cable node ${P}_{0}^{c}$ and a concrete node ${P}_{i}^{b}$ is given by:

$d=\parallel {P}_{0}^{P}{P}_{i}^{b}\parallel =∥(\begin{array}{}{x}^{c}-{x}_{i}^{b}\\ {y}^{c}-{y}_{i}^{b}\\ {z}^{c}-{z}_{i}^{b}\end{array})-\left[(\begin{array}{}{x}^{c}-{x}_{i}^{b}\\ {y}^{c}-{y}_{i}^{b}\\ {z}^{c}-{z}_{i}^{b}\end{array}).{\overrightarrow{n}}_{0}\right].{\overrightarrow{n}}_{0}∥$

If it happens that this distance is zero (in practice less than 10-5), this means that the cable node projects onto the vertex of a concrete mesh cell, and then the kinematic relations simplify:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\text{dx}}^{c}-{\text{dx}}_{i}^{p}=e.({\text{dry}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0z}}-{\text{drz}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0y}})\\ {\text{dy}}^{c}-{\text{dy}}_{i}^{p}=e.({\text{drz}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0x}}-{\text{drx}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0z}})\\ {\text{dz}}^{c}-{\text{dz}}_{i}^{p}=e.({\text{drx}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0y}}-{\text{dry}}_{i}^{p}.{n}_{\mathrm{0x}})\end{array}$

These relations are the general relations in which: ${N}_{j}({x}^{p},{y}^{p},{z}^{p})=0$ if $j\ne i$ .

### 2.5 Treatment of the end zones of the cable

The modelling of a prestressing cable as it is done in *Code_Aster* consists in representing the assembly of cable, passage duct, and all the anchorage parts, solely by means of a sequence of bar elements. The link between the cable elements and the concrete medium is ensured by kinematic conditions on the degrees of freedom of each of the nodes of the cable, and those of the concrete elements crossed.

When the tensioning of the cable is applied, it is observed that the reactions generated at the ends of the cables on the concrete create stress levels very much higher than in reality, and cause damage to the concrete. By way of example, in certain studies, compressive stresses of more than $200\mathit{MPa}$ have been observed, which largely exceeds the experimental value observed ($40\mathit{MPa}$ ). In reality, this phenomenon is not observed thanks to the installation of a stress-diffusion cone (see the drawing below) which distributes the prestressing force over a large surface of the concrete. In the case of the finite-element model, this surface does not exist, since the force is taken up directly by a node.

| ![Figure](images/10055F360000307900002447FE8413C38C64A32B.svg) | ![Figure](images/1004AC7800001DAA00002461DFCC84359ED21171.svg) |
| --- | --- |
| Real situation | FE model without a cone |

This way of modelling has several drawbacks:

- the concentration of this force crushes the concrete,
- the spatial discretisation of the model changes the results.

To remedy this problem, the keyword CONE of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP makes it possible to distribute this prestressing force no longer on one node, but on all the nodes contained in a volume (all the nodes of this volume are linked together to form a rigid solid) bounded by a cylinder of radius $R$ and of length $L$ , representing the equivalent of the zone of influence of the spreading cone of an anchorage (see the figure below).

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/1006464A0000205900002957AF7399D11C980FF9.svg)

The identification and the creation of the kinematic relations between the nodes of the concrete and of the cable are done automatically by the command DEFI_CABLE_BP, where the new data $R$ and $L$ will have to be supplied by the user.

### 2.6 Note: computation of the cable tension as a mechanical loading

The choice has been made to leave the cable elements in the mechanical model supporting the finite-element analysis (linear or not). Consequently, there is no computation of an equivalent force to be transferred to the nodes of the mesh. One simply states that the prestressing cables have a non-zero initial stress state. This stress state is the one deduced from the tension as computed by DEFI_CABLE_BP.

For reasons of simplicity, the data-processing object created by the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP is a table storing values at the nodes of the cable. Consider then two connected elements of the cable:

$\mathrm{e1}$ with vertices $\mathit{N1}$ and $\mathit{N2}$ , and

$\mathrm{e2}$ with vertices $\mathit{N2}$ and $\mathit{N3}$ .

It is assumed that ${l}_{1}$ and ${S}_{1}$ are the length and the cross-section of an element $\mathrm{e1}$ and that ${l}_{2}$ and ${S}_{2}$ are the length and the cross-section of the element $\mathrm{e2}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/1000042E000069D50000314DD3809EA87F9F1CB4.svg)

DEFI_CABLE_BP will compute at the node $\mathit{N2}$ a tension ${T}_{{N}_{2}}$ defined by:

${T}_{{N}_{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\underset{{e}_{1}}{\int}T(s)\text{ds}}{{l}_{1}}+\frac{\underset{{e}_{2}}{\int}T(s)\text{ds}}{{l}_{2}})$

Conversely, for the finite-element computation, the operator STAT_NON_LINE will consider that the initial stress in the element $\mathit{e1}$ is ${\sigma}_{0}^{\mathrm{e1}}=\frac{{T}_{{N}_{1}}+{T}_{{N}_{2}}}{{\mathrm{2S}}_{1}}$

**Note:**

One will always consider that the constitutive law of the cable is of incremental type.

## 3 The macro-command CALC_PRECONT

The purpose of the macro-command CALC_PRECONT is to carry out the tensioning of the cables in the concrete from the data contained in the concept cable_precont produced by DEFI_CABLE_BP.

Two different procedures for tensioning the cables are present in this macro-command. One or the other of these procedures is chosen automatically according to whether the concept cable_precont has been created with the keyword ADHERENT = 'OUI' or 'NON'.

If ADHERENT = 'OUI', the tension profile computed in DEFI_CABLE_BP is transformed into an initial loading by AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

Otherwise, the tensioning is simulated completely by a mechanical analysis by imposing forces on the slip degrees of freedom of the CABLE_GAINE elements from the information contained in the concept cable_precont.

### 3.1 Bonded case: why a macro-command for the tensioning?

It is possible to transform the tension in the cables, computed by DEFI_CABLE_BP, into a loading taken directly into account by STAT_NON_LINE by means of the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA, operand RELA_CINE_BP (SIGM_BPEL=’OUI’). In this case, the tension is taken into account as an initial stress state when solving the complete finite-element problem.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/10000D04000069D500002F21E34579AE525D1920.svg)

The solution of the problem makes it possible to reach a state of equilibrium between the prestressing cable and the rest of the structure after instantaneous strain. Indeed, under the action of the tension of the cable, the assembly of cable(s) and concrete will compress relative to the initial position (cable in tension, mesh undeformed). The length of the cable will therefore decrease, and the initial tension will likewise, as a consequence, decrease. One therefore obtains a final state with a tension in the cable different from the tension computed initially. It is then indispensable to increase proportionally the tension applied *in situ* at the anchorages in order to take this loss into account.

The use of the macro-command CALC_PRECONT makes it possible to avoid this correction phase, by obtaining the equilibrium state of the structure with a tension in the cables equal to the regulatory tension. Moreover, because of the method adopted, it furthermore makes it possible to apply the tension in several time steps, which can be of interest in the event of yielding or of damage of the concrete. It furthermore makes it possible to tension the cables in a non-simultaneous manner and therefore in a manner closer to the reality of construction sites.

In order to benefit from these advantages, the loading is applied in the form of an external loading and not as an initial state, which allows the progressive loading of the structure. Moreover, in order to avoid the loss of tension in the cable, the idea is not to bring the stiffness of the cables into play during the tensioning phase (cf. [bib3]).

The different stages carried out by the macro-command are detailed here.

#### 3.1.1 Stage 1: computation of the equivalent nodal forces

This stage consists in transforming the internal tensions of the cables computed by DEFI_CABLE_BP into an external loading. To this end, a first STAT_NON_LINE is carried out solely on the cables that one wishes to prestress, with the following loading:

- cable clamped
- the tension given by DEFI_CABLE_BP

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/10002E720000691C000016398189F305803B0442.svg)

**Figure 3.1.1-a: Loading at stage 1**

The nodal forces on the cable are computed. These forces are recovered by means of CREA_CHAMP. And the associated loading vector $F$ is constructed.

#### 3.1.2 Stage 2: application of the prestress to the concrete

The following stage consists in applying the prestress to the concrete structure, without bringing the stiffness of the cable into play. To this end, it is assumed for this analysis that the Young's modulus of the steel is zero. One can choose to apply the prestress loading in a single time step or in several time steps if the concrete is damaged.

The loading is therefore the following:

- blocking of the rigid-body motions for the concrete,
- the nodal forces coming from the first analysis on the cable,
- the kinematic connections between the cable and the concrete.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/10001BFC000069D50000230949467E945E2B4EA7.svg)

**Figure 3.1.2-a: Loading at stage 2**

#### 3.1.3 Stage 3: transfer of the external forces into internal forces

Before continuing the analysis in the traditional manner, it is necessary to transform back the external forces which have made it possible to deform the concrete structure into internal forces. This operation is done without modification of the displacements and the stresses of the whole of the structure, since equilibrium has been reached at stage 2: it is a simple device in order to be able to continue the analysis. The loading is therefore the following:

- blocking of the rigid-body motions for the concrete,
- the kinematic connections between the cable and the concrete,
- tension in the cables.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.02/100024C2000069D500003079F709D3377622FA3A.svg)

**Figure 3.1.3-a: Loading at stage 3**

### 3.2 Unbonded case

The value given to the keyword TENSION_INIT of DEFI_CABLE_BP is imposed as a force on the degree of freedom GLIS of the active anchorage nodes of the cable, whereas at the passive nodes this degree of freedom is blocked at zero.

In the case of an ACTIF/ACTIF anchorage, one must proceed in two stages:

1. A force is imposed on the first active anchorage while the degree of freedom GLIS is blocked on the second.
2. The analysis is resumed by imposing the force on the second anchorage; the degree of freedom GLIS of the first is then blocked at zero with a load of type DIDI.

If an anchorage draw-in is specified in DEFI_CABLE_BP, another analysis is launched by imposing on the degree of freedom GLIS a displacement equal to the given anchorage draw-in. This load is also of type DIDI.

To continue the analysis after CALC_PRECONT, it is sufficient to block the degree of freedom GLIS of the anchorage nodes with a DIDI load.

## 4 Modelling procedure for a cable modelled with BARRE

### 4.1 The different stages: standard case

In order to succeed in modelling a prestressed-concrete structure the procedure to be followed is the following:

- model the concrete elements (DKT, Q4GG, 2D or 3D),
- model the prestressing cables by two-node bar elements (BARRE),
- assign to the bar elements the mechanical characteristics of the prestressing cables,
- define the material parameters for the steel as well as BPEL_ACIER and BPEL_BETON or ETCC_ACIER and ETCC_BETON,
- by means of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP compute the kinematic data (kinematic relations between the nodes of the cable and those of the concrete elements) and the static data (tension profile along the cables),
- define the kinematic data as a mechanical loading,
- call the operator CALC_PRECONT,
- solve the problem with the operator STAT_NON_LINE, integrating only the kinematic data and the loadings other than the prestress.

For more practical information, refer to the document [U2.03.06](doc:u2.03.06).

### 4.2 Particular case

If for one reason or another the user does not wish to use the macro-command CALC_PRECONT, it is possible to adopt the following procedure:

- model the concrete elements,
- model the prestressing cables by two-node bar elements (BARRE),
- assign to the bar elements the mechanical characteristics of the prestressing cables,
- by means of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP compute the kinematic data (kinematic relations between the nodes of the cable and those of the concrete elements) and the static data (tension profile along the cables),
- apply these kinematic and static data as a mechanical loading,
- solve the problem with the operator STAT_NON_LINE, integrating all the loadings.

At the end of this analysis, one must determine the correction coefficients to be applied to the initial tensions applied to the cables (at the level of the declaration of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP) making it possible to compensate for the loss by instantaneous strain of the structure.

Once the command file has been modified by these correction coefficients, the modelling of the prestressing cables is accomplished.

Caution: in the case of a chaining of STAT_NON_LINE, from the second call onwards one should include in the loading only the kinematic relations and not the tension in the cables, otherwise this tension will be added at each analysis.

### 4.3 Precautions for use and notes

It is recommended to limit the recourse to a large number of kinematic relations, otherwise the computation time will be made heavier. However, when a node of the bar elements constituting the cables coincides topologically with a concrete node, there is no addition of a kinematic relation.

If a first STAT_NON_LINE is carried out before tensioning the cables, it is preferable to deactivate the cables, either by not taking them into account in the model, or by assigning them a constantly zero tension (constitutive law SANS), and by including in the loading the kinematic relations linking the cable to the concrete.

In the case where a phasing of the prestressing is carried out, one must remember to include the kinematic relations in the loading for the cables already tensioned at the previous stages.

## 5 Modelling procedure for a cable modelled with CABLE_GAINE

In order to succeed in modelling a prestressed-concrete structure the procedure to be followed is the following:

- model the concrete elements (3D),
- model the prestressing cables by three-node 1D elements (CABLE_GAINE), taking care that the elements are not linear, otherwise the losses by friction due to the curvature cannot be evaluated,
- assign to the CABLE_GAINE elements the mechanical characteristics of the prestressing cables,
- define the material parameters for the steel and for the friction law (CABLE_GAINE_FROT), but also BPEL_ACIER and BPEL_BETON (even if, in practice, the parameters are not used in the frictional cases),
- by means of the operator DEFI_CABLE_BP compute the kinematic data (kinematic relations between the nodes of the cable and those of the concrete elements) and the static data (tension profile along the cables),
- define the kinematic data as a mechanical loading,
- call the operator CALC_PRECONT,
- solve the problem with the operator STAT_NON_LINE, integrating only the kinematic data and the loadings other than the prestress, and blocking the degree of freedom GLIS of the anchorage nodes with a loading of TYPE='DIDI'.

For CALC_PRECONT and STAT_NON_LINE, one should preferably use the convergence criterion RESI_REFE_RELA in order to be sure of having reached convergence.
