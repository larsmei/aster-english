# r5.03.08 Integration of the viscoelastoplastic constitutive relations in the non-linear mechanics operators

**Summary**

This document describes, for non-linear viscoelastic constitutive behaviours and viscoelastoplastic constitutive behaviours with a threshold, built on the basis of Lemaître's non-linear viscoelastic model, the ingredients required for the implementation of the non-linear mechanics algorithm, as in STAT_NON_LINE described in [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01). The input data of all these non-linear viscoelastic constitutive relations integrated in *Code_Aster* generally have the same form. Only the way of introducing the main datum (the viscous strain-rate function) varies: it is presented according to the various keywords which allow the user to choose the desired constitutive relation.

These quantities are computed by a semi-implicit or implicit integration method. From the initial state, or from the previous analysis time instant, the stress field resulting from a strain increment is computed.

## 1 Introduction

The implementation of Lemaître's non-linear viscoelasticity model (keyword LEMAITRE) is described here; for certain particular values of the parameters it can reduce to a Norton viscoelastoplastic constitutive relation.

A variant (depending on the fluence) of this Lemaître model has been added, with a view to the modelling of fuel assemblies (keyword LEMAITRE_IRRA). This model has the particular feature of including an additional anelastic strain: the growth strain.

A viscoelastoplastic model with a threshold has been added. It is a material whose behaviour is purely elastic up to a threshold and then, once this threshold has been exceeded, the constitutive relation becomes a particular case of the Lemaître relation (LEMA_SEUIL).

Finally, a viscoelastoplastic model with creep in the logarithm of the fluence has been implemented; it is accessed through the keyword VISC_IRRA_LOG.

For each of these models, the material is assumed to be isotropic (except for the growth strain, which is uniaxial). They can be used in 3D, in plane strain (D_PLAN) and in axisymmetric conditions (AXIS).

The constitutive equations of the models and their implementation in *Code_Aster* are presented in this note.

## 2 Continuous relation

The small-perturbation assumption is adopted and the strain tensor is split into an elastic part, a thermal part, a known anelastic part and a viscous part. The equations are then:

$\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{\text{tot}}={\epsilon}_{e}+{\epsilon}_{\text{th}}+{\epsilon}_{a}+{\epsilon}_{v}\\ \sigma =\mathrm{A}\left(T\right){\epsilon}_{e}\\ {\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}=g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},\lambda ,T\right)\frac{3}{2}\frac{\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\end{array}$

with:

$\lambda$ : cumulative viscous strain $\dot{\lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}:{\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}}$

$\stackrel{~}{\sigma}$ : stress deviator $\stackrel{~}{\sigma}=\sigma -\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\left(\sigma \right)\mathrm{Id}$

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ : equivalent stress ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\stackrel{~}{\sigma}:\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}$

$\mathrm{A}\left(T\right)$ : elasticity tensor

The power density associated with the dissipation of viscoelastic energy is written:

${D}_{\mathit{intr}}=\stackrel{~}{\sigma}\cdot {\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}=\dot{\lambda}.{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$

## 3 Nature of the function g for each of the constitutive relations

### 3.1 Viscoelastic relation LEMAITRE

In this case, $g$ is expressed explicitly ($\sigma$ is a scalar here):

$g\left(\sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)={\left(\frac{1}{K}\frac{\sigma}{{\lambda}^{1/m}}\right)}^{n}$ with $\frac{1}{K}\ge 0,\frac{1}{m}\ge 0,n>0$

The material characteristic data are those supplied under the factor keywords LEMAITRE or LEMAITRE_FO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU.

LEMAITRE= _F(N=n,    UN_SUR_K= $\frac{1}{K}$ , UN_SUR_M = $\frac{1}{m}$ )

The Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ are those supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO. In the case $1/m=0$ , this three-dimensional constitutive relation is of NORTON type. If in addition $n=1$ , this constitutive relation reduces to the linear viscoelastic law of MAXWELL

### 3.2 LEMAITRE relation depending on the fluence (LEMAITRE_IRRA)

This paragraph describes the dependence on the fluence (and its treatment) of the viscoplastic constitutive relation of J. Lemaître, introduced for the modelling of fuel assemblies and applicable to massive 2D and 3D elements and to TUYAU elements, under the keyword LEMAITRE_IRRA.

#### 3.2.1 Formulation of the model

The equations are as follows:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{{\sigma}^{\mathrm{D}}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\begin{array}{cccccc}& & & & & \end{array}\\ \dot{p}={\left[\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}{{p}^{1/m}}\right]}^{n}{\left(\frac{1}{K}\frac{\varphi}{{\varphi}_{0}}+L\right)}^{\beta}{e}^{-\frac{Q}{R(T+{T}_{0})}}\\ \stackrel{\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}\cdot \phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}}{\stackrel{⏞}{({\mathrm{A}}^{-1}(T).\sigma )}}={\dot{\epsilon}}_{\text{tot}}-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{g}-{\dot{\epsilon}}_{\text{th}}\end{array}$

with:

${T}_{0}=273,15°C$

$n>0$, $1/K\ge 0$, $1/m\ge 0$

${\phi }_{0}>0$, $Q/R\ge 0$, $L\ge 0$, $\beta$ any real number

The coefficients are supplied under the keywords LEMAITRE_IRRA and ELAS of DEFI_MATERIAU and $\phi$ is the neutron flux (quotient of the fluence increment, defined by the keyword AFFE_VARC of AFFE_MATERIAU, by the time increment).

The growth law is: ${\epsilon}_{g}(t)=f(T,{\Phi}_{t}){\epsilon}_{g}^{0}$ with ${\epsilon}_{g}^{0}$ the unit uniaxial strain in a frame ${R}_{1}$ given by the user by means of the keyword MASSIF (see [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01) and [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)) and $f(T,{\Phi}_{t})$ is likewise a function defined by the user in DEFI_MATERIAU ([U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01)).

**Notes:**

*The fluence, the time and the flux* ${\Phi}_{0}$ *must be expressed in units such that the ratio* $\Phi /{\Phi}_{0}$ *is dimensionless.* $Q/R$ *is in kelvin.* $T$ *is in* $°C$ *.*

#### 3.2.2 Treatment of the dependence on the fluence

The model described above in fact corresponds to an ordinary Lemaître viscoelastic law, defined by the three coefficients $n$ , $1/K’$ and $1/m$ with:

$\frac{1}{K'}={\left(\frac{1}{K}\frac{\Phi}{{\Phi}_{0}}+L\right)}^{\beta /n}{e}^{-\frac{Q}{\text{nR}(T+{T}_{0})}}$

It is therefore sufficient to compute $1/K’$ and to supply it as a datum to the analysis in place of $1/K$ .

Moreover, in the computation of the elastic stress, the growth strain expressed above is added to the anelastic strains (zero by default), after carrying out the change of frame between the local frame and the frame ${R}_{1}$ .

### 3.3 Relation LEMA_SEUIL

For this behaviour, $g$ is also expressed explicitly (since it is a particular case of the LEMAITRE relation presented above):

If $D\le 1$ then $g\left(\sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)=0$ (purely elastic behaviour)

If $D>1$ then $g\left(\sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)=A\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma \right)\Phi ` with :math:`A\ge 0$ , $\Phi \ge 0$

With: $D=\frac{1}{S}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}(u)\text{du}$

The material data to be entered by the user must be supplied under the keyword LEMA_SEUIL or LEMA_SEUIL_FO of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU:

LEMA_SEUIL = _F(A=A,     S=S)

As for the parameter $\Phi$ , it is the neutron flux which bombards the material (quotient of the fluence increment, defined by the keyword AFFE_VARC of AFFE_MATERIAU, by the time increment).

The Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ are those supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO.

### 3.4 Relation VISC_IRRA_LOG

For this relation, $g$ is not expressed explicitly. The viscoelastoplastic behaviour is represented by a one-dimensional creep test, at constant stress, which involves the time elapsed since the instant at which the stress is applied. The constitutive relation is defined here by the datum of four functions ${f}_{1,}{g}_{1,}{f}_{2,}{g}_{2}$ describing the evolution of the viscous strain with time:

${\varepsilon}_{v}=\lambda ={f}_{1}(t){g}_{1}(\sigma ,T)+{f}_{2}(t){g}_{2}(\sigma ,T)$                     eq 3.4-1

The function $g$ is then computed numerically by eliminating the time $t$ in the following way:

1. for a given triplet $(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)$ , equation [eq 3.2-1] is solved for $t$ by Newton's method (see [bib2]). An approximation of the solution $t(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)$ is found,
2. the value of the function $g$ at $(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)$ is obtained by differentiating equation [eq 3.2-1] with respect to time (see [bib1]):

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}=\dot{\lambda}=g(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)={f}_{1}^{'}(t){g}_{1}(\sigma ,T)+{f}_{2}^{'}(t){g}_{2}(\sigma ,T)$

and by substituting into this new equation the value of $t(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)$ found previously. The following uniaxial formulation is found:

${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{v}=\dot{\lambda}=g(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)={f}_{1}^{'}(t(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)){g}_{1}(\sigma ,T)+{f}_{2}^{'}(t(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)){g}_{2}(\sigma ,T)$

The form of the four functions ${f}_{1,}{g}_{1,}{f}_{2,}{g}_{2}$ is predefined and the user introduces only a few parameters into the command file.

For VISC_IRRA_LOG, one has:

${f}_{1}\left(t\right)=\ln(1+\omega .\Phi .t)$

${g}_{1}(\sigma ,T)=A.\sigma .{e}^{-\frac{Q}{T}}$

${f}_{2}(t)=\Phi .t$

${g}_{2}(\sigma ,T)=B.\sigma .{e}^{-\frac{Q}{T}}$

The parameter $\Phi$ is the neutron flux. It must be entered under the factor keyword AFFE_VARC via the command variable IRRA.

The parameters $A$ , $B$ , $\omega$ and $Q$ are those supplied under the factor keyword VISC_IRRA_LOG of the operator DEFI_MATERIAU.

It will be noted that, for all the functions:

$t$ is expressed in hours

$T$ is expressed in $°C$

$\sigma$ is expressed in $\mathit{MPa}$

Data consistent with these units must therefore be entered in the command file and in the mesh file.

The Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$ are those supplied under the factor keywords ELAS or ELAS_FO.

## 4 Integration of the constitutive relation

### 4.1 Establishment of the scalar equation for the implicit scheme and with constant elastic coefficients

${\epsilon}_{\text{tot}}$ denotes the total strain at the instant $t+\Delta t$ and $\Delta {\epsilon}_{\text{tot}}$ the variation of the total strain during the current time step.

${\epsilon}_{\mathrm{o}}$ denotes the strain at the instant $t+\Delta t$ resulting from thermal expansion and from the anelastic strains (among which the growth strains may appear, cf [§3.2]). One therefore has:

$\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{o}}=\left[\alpha \left(t+\Delta t\right)\left(T\left(t+\Delta t\right)-{T}_{\text{ref}}\right)-\alpha \left(t\right)\left(T\left(t\right)-{T}_{\text{ref}}\right)\right]\mathrm{Id}+{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{a}}\left(t+\Delta t\right)-{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{a}}\left(t\right)$

where $\mathrm{Id}$ is the identity tensor of order 2 in dimension 3.

One sets $\Delta \epsilon =\Delta {\epsilon}_{\text{tot}}-\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{o}}$

Since $\mu$ is assumed constant here, one has the following relation between the deviators of $\Delta \sigma$ and $\Delta \varepsilon$ :

$\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}=2\mu \left(\Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}-\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}\right)$                             eq 4.1-1

Now, the flow law is written implicitly:

$\frac{\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}}{\Delta t}=\frac{3}{2}g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}},T\right)\frac{\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}$                         eq 4.1-2

One therefore has, eliminating $\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}$ between [eq 4.1-1] and [eq 4.1-2]:

$\begin{array}{c}2\mu \Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}=\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}+3\mu \Delta t.g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}},T\right)\frac{\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\\ \left({\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}\right)=\left(1+3\mu \Delta t\frac{g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}},T\right)}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}}\right)\stackrel{~}{\sigma}\end{array}$             eq 4.1-3

Setting ${\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{e}={\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}$ , one therefore has:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=3\mu \Delta t.g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}},T\right)+{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$                 eq 4.1-4

Now, from [eq 4.1-2]:

${\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}=\Delta t.g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}},T\right)$

Whence:

$\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=3\mu {\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}+{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\\ {\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}=\frac{1}{3\mu }\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\right)\end{array}$

Substituting this last expression into [eq 4.1-4], one has:

${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=3\mu \Delta t.g\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{1}{3\mu }\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\right),T\right)+{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$

If one sets, ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e},{\lambda}^{-},T$ and $\Delta t$ being known:

$f\left(x\right)=3\mu \Delta t.g\left(x,{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{1}{3\mu }\left({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-x\right),T\right)+x-{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}$

one can then compute the quantity ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}={\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma \right)}_{\text{eq}}$ as being the solution of the scalar equation: $f\left(x\right)=0$ where $x={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}$ , the convention adopted for the following paragraphs.

In the case of a Lemaître law with threshold, LEMA_IRRA_SEUIL, the previous equations are useful only once the threshold has been crossed. Indeed, below the threshold the behaviour is elastic.

The threshold is discretised implicitly:

$D({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )=\frac{1}{S}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}{\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma \right)}_{\text{eq}}(u)\text{du}$

In the same way as for the integration of the Von Mises elastoplastic constitutive laws, two cases are then distinguished.

$D({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )\le 1$ then $\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}g\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)=0$

$D({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )>1$ then $\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}g\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)=A.\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\right)\phi$

It follows from the equation above that:

$\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}g\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ,\lambda ,T\right)\ne A.\left(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}\right)\phi$ implies $D({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )\le 1$

Now $g$ can take only the value $0$ or $A.(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}})\varphi$ hence

$g({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ,\lambda ,T)\ne A.(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}})\varphi$ implies $D({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma )\le 1$ and $\Delta \sigma =A.\Delta \varepsilon$

that is, $g({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma ,\lambda ,T)=A.(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}{\sigma}_{\text{eq}})\varphi$ then $D({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )>1$

The criterion for crossing the threshold can therefore be written $D({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )>1$

Now $D({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )=\frac{1}{S}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}{({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )}_{\text{eq}}(u)\text{du}$

Discretising time, one then has:

$D({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )=\frac{1}{S}\underset{0}{\overset{{t}^{-}}{\int}}{({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )}_{\text{eq}}(u)\text{du}+\frac{1}{S}\underset{{t}^{-}}{\overset{t}{\int}}{({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )}_{\text{eq}}(u)\text{du}$

$D({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )=\frac{1}{S}({D}^{-}S+\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{-}+{({\sigma}^{-}+A.\Delta \varepsilon )}_{\text{eq}}}{2}(t-{t}^{-}))$

### 4.2 Solution of the scalar equation: principle of the routine ZEROF2

It is easily shown that, if the conditions required in paragraph [§3] on the material characteristics are satisfied, the function $f$ is strictly increasing and the equation $f(x)=0$ admits a unique solution.

If ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=0$ , then the solution is $x=0$ . Otherwise, one has: $f(0)=-{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}<0$

The problem therefore consists in finding, for an arbitrary function $f$ , the solution of the equation $f(x)=0$ knowing that this solution exists, that $f(0)<0$ and that $f$ is strictly increasing.

The algorithm adopted in ZEROF2 is as follows:

- one starts from ${a}_{0}=0$ and ${b}_{0}={x}_{\mathrm{ap}}$ where ${x}_{\mathrm{ap}}$ is an approximation of the solution. If necessary (that is to say if $f({b}_{0})<0$ ), one reduces, by the secant method (${z}_{n}=\frac{{a}_{n}f({b}_{n})-{b}_{n}f({a}_{n})}{f({b}_{n})-f({a}_{n})}$ then ${a}_{n+1}={b}_{n}$ and ${b}_{n+1}={z}_{n}$ ), in one or several iterations, to the case where $f(a)<0$ and $f(b)>0$ :

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.08/1000165E000069D500002DC9EB7503805BCA0BCA.svg)

(In the case of the figure above, this first step was carried out in one iteration: ${a}_{1}={b}_{0}$ and $f({b}_{1})>0$ ).

- one computes ${N}_{d}$ = integer part $(\sqrt{{N}_{\max}})$ where ${N}_{\max}$ is the maximum number of iterations that has been set. The equation is then solved by the secant method, using however the bisection method whenever $n$ is a multiple of ${N}_{d}$ :

If ${N}_{d}$ divides $n$

${z}_{n}=\frac{{a}_{n}+{b}_{n}}{2}$

else

${z}_{n}=\frac{{a}_{n}f({b}_{n})-{b}_{n}f({a}_{n})}{f({b}_{n})-f({a}_{n})}$

end if

$n=n+1$

if $\mid f(z)\mid >\varepsilon$

if $f(z)<0$

${a}_{n+1}={z}_{n}$     ${b}_{n+1}={b}_{n}$

else

${a}_{n+1}={a}_{n}$     ${b}_{n+1}={z}_{n}$

end if

go to 1)

else

The solution is: $x={z}_{n}\to \text{FIN}$

end if

This second part of the algorithm makes it possible to treat, in a reasonable number of iterations, the cases where $f$ is very strongly non-linear, whereas the secant method would have converged too slowly. These cases of strong non-linearity are encountered in particular with the LEMAITRE law, for large values of $\frac{n}{m}$ .

### 4.3 Computation of the stress at the end of the current time step

From [eq 4.1-3], if $x$ is the solution of the scalar equation, setting:

$b(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e})=\frac{1}{1+3\mu \Delta t\frac{g(x,{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{1}{3\mu }({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-x),T)}{x}}=\frac{x}{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}$

one has:

$\stackrel{~}{\sigma}=b\left(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}\right){\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{e}$                         eq 4.3-1

In the case where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=0$ , which is equivalent, from the scalar equation, to $x=0$ , $b$ is extended by continuity. To this end, one sets $y(x)={\lambda}^{-}+\frac{1}{3\mu }({\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-x)$ and $G(x)=g(x\text{,y}(x),T)$ . The derivative of $G$ is expressed as a function of the partial derivatives of $g$ at the point $(x\text{,y}(x),T)$ :

$\text{G'}\left(x\right)=\frac{\partial g}{\partial x}\left(x\text{,y}\left(x\right),T\right)-\frac{1}{3\mu }\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}\left(x\text{,y}\left(x\right),T\right)$

The extension of $b$ by continuity then gives:

$b\left(0,0\right)=\frac{1}{1+3\mu \Delta t\text{G'}\left(0\right)}$

and one has, still in the case where ${\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=0$ , $\tilde{\sigma}=0$ .

Once $\stackrel{~}{\sigma}$ has been computed, one obtains $\sigma ` from the relation (:math:`K$ is assumed constant here):

$\sigma ={\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma =\stackrel{~}{\sigma}+\left(\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\left({\sigma}^{-}\right)+K.\text{Tr}\left(\Delta \epsilon \right)\right).\mathrm{Id}$                 eq 4.3-2

> The internal variables are as follows: $V1$ *:* cumulative plastic strain, $V2$ : cumulative viscoelastic energy-dissipation density.

### 4.4 Semi-implicit scheme

With an implicit numerical scheme [eq 4.1-2], in the case, for example, where $g$ does not depend on $\lambda$ , only the value of the stress at the end of the time step enters through the computation of $\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}$ . This may result in significant numerical errors if the stress varies strongly with time (see [bib2]).

To remedy this and improve the solution, the flow law is discretised in a semi-implicit way:

$\frac{\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}}{\Delta t}=\frac{3}{2}g\left({\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}{2},{T}^{-}+\frac{\Delta T}{2}\right)\frac{\left({\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{2}\right)}{{\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}$         eq 4.4-1

In order to transform, in the most economical way, what was programmed previously (following the implicit formulation [eq 4.1-2]), it is sufficient to divide each member of equation [eq 4.4-1] by 2:

$\frac{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}/2\right)}{\Delta t}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{g\left({\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}{2},{T}^{-}+\frac{\Delta T}{2}\right)}{2}\frac{\left({\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{2}\right)}{{\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}$

and to do the same with relation [eq 4.1-1]:

$\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{2}=2\mu \left(\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}}{2}-\frac{\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}}{2}\right)$

It is observed that this system has the same form as that formed by equations [eq 4.1-1] and [eq 4.1-2], the datum being $\frac{\Delta \epsilon }{2}$ instead of $\Delta \epsilon ` , the unknowns being respectively :math:$frac{Delta sigma }{2}` and $\frac{\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}}{2}$ instead of $\Delta \sigma ` and :math:$Delta {epsilon}_{mathrm{v}}` and the function $\frac{g}{2}$ replacing the function $g$ .

One can therefore use the solution of paragraphs [§4.1] to [§4.3] as well as the corresponding algorithm by introducing $\frac{\Delta \epsilon }{2}$ and by dividing the function $g$ by $2$ . It then remains to multiply the results $\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}$ and $\frac{\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}}{2}$ by $2$ in order to obtain the stress and viscous-strain increments computed by the semi-implicit scheme (the $\Delta \sigma ` and the :math:$Delta {epsilon}_{mathrm{v}}` of equation [eq 4.4-1]).

It will be noted that the computation of the tangent operator is not affected by this modification of the numerical scheme. Indeed, one obviously has:

$\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \left(\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}{\partial \left(\frac{\Delta \epsilon }{2}\right)}$

### 4.5 Taking into account the variation of the elastic coefficients as a function of temperature

If $\mathrm{A}$ is the elasticity tensor, one has:

$\Delta \epsilon =\Delta {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{v}}+\Delta \left({\mathrm{A}}^{-1}.\sigma \right)$

with:

$\Delta \left({\mathrm{A}}^{-1}.\sigma \right)={\mathrm{A}}^{-1}\left({T}^{-}+\Delta T\right).\left({\sigma}^{-}+\Delta \sigma \right)-{\mathrm{A}}^{-1}\left({T}^{-}\right).{\sigma}^{-}$

This is reflected in the equations of [§4.4] by:

$2\mu \left(\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}}{2}\right)-\left({\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{2}\right)=3\mu \Delta t\frac{g\left({\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}},{\lambda}^{-}+\frac{{\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}{2},{T}^{-}+\frac{\Delta T}{2}\right)}{2}\frac{\left({\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}{{\left({\sigma}^{-}+\frac{\Delta \sigma }{2}\right)}_{\text{eq}}}-{\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}.\left(\frac{2{\mu}^{-}+2\mu }{4{\mu}^{-}}\right)$

Setting:

${\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{e}=\left(\frac{2{\mu}^{-}+2\mu }{4{\mu}^{-}}\right){\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \left(\frac{\Delta \stackrel{~}{\epsilon}}{2}\right)$

and

$\text{Tr}\left({\sigma}^{\mathrm{e}}\right)=\left(\frac{3{K}^{-}+3K}{6{K}^{-}}\right)\text{Tr}\left({\sigma}^{-}\right)+3K.\text{Tr}\left(\frac{\Delta \epsilon }{2}\right)$

one reduces exactly to the previous case [§4.4].

## 5 Computation of the tangent operator

In the case where ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}=0$ and $x=0$ , the elasticity tensor is taken as the tangent operator.

Otherwise, this operator is obtained by differentiating equation [eq 4.3-1] with respect to $\Delta \epsilon$ :

$\frac{\partial \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }=\frac{\partial b\left(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}\right)}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }{\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{e}+b\left(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}\right)\frac{\partial {\stackrel{~}{\sigma}}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }$

then by also differentiating [eq 4.3-2] with respect to $\Delta \epsilon$ :

$\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }+K{\mathrm{I}}_{3}\frac{\partial \text{Tr}\left(\Delta \epsilon \right)}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }=\frac{\partial \Delta \stackrel{~}{\sigma}}{\partial \Delta \epsilon }+K.{\mathrm{I}}_{3}^{\mathrm{t}}{\mathrm{I}}_{3}$

It will be noted that, in these equations, the tensors of order 2 and of order 4 are respectively likened to vectors and to matrices. ${\mathrm{I}}_{3}$ is here a tensor of order 2, likened to a vector:

${}^{t}\text{}{\mathrm{I}}_{3}=\left(\mathrm{1,1,1,0,0,0}\right)$

One moreover has:

$\frac{\partial b(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e})}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\partial b}{\partial x}(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e})\frac{\partial x}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }+\frac{\partial b}{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e})\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }$

$\frac{\partial x}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }$ must therefore be computed. To this end, the scalar equation is differentiated implicitly with respect to $\Delta \varepsilon$ .

For simplicity, in the writing of $g$ and of its derivatives the parameter $T$ will henceforth be omitted.

One then has:

$\left[3\mu \Delta t{G}^{'}(x)+1\right]\frac{\partial x}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }+\Delta t\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,y)\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }$

Whence:

$\frac{\partial x}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=\frac{1-\Delta t\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,y)}{1+3\mu \Delta t{G}^{'}(x)}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }$

$\frac{\partial x}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=\frac{1-\Delta t\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,y)}{1+3\mu \Delta t{G}^{'}(x)}\frac{3\mu }{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}$

with the expression for $\text{G'}(x)$ obtained in [§4.3].

The following expression for the tangent operator is finally obtained:

$\frac{\partial \Delta \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=K{I}_{3}^{t}{I}_{3}+2\mu \left[\gamma {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}{}^{t}\text{}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+b(x,{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}^{e})A\right]$

with

$A=\frac{\partial \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }={J}_{6}-\frac{1}{3}{I}_{3}{}^{t}\text{}{I}_{3}$ where ${J}_{6}$ is the identity matrix of rank 6.

$\gamma =\frac{3}{2{({\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e})}^{3}}\left[{\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{e}\frac{1-\Delta t\frac{\partial g}{\partial y}(x,y)}{1+3\mu \Delta t{G}^{'}(x)}-x\right]$

**Note:**

*In the case of the law* *VISC_IRRA_LOG, it is easily verified that:*

$\begin{array}{}{G}^{'}(x)=\frac{1}{{f}_{1}^{'}{g}_{1}+{f}_{2}^{'}{g}_{2}}[{g}_{1}{g}_{1}^{'}({f}_{1}^{{'}^{2}}-{f}_{1}{f}_{1}^{\text{''}})+{g}_{2}{g}_{2}^{'}({f}_{2}^{{'}^{2}}-{f}_{2}{f}_{2}^{\text{''}})+{g}_{1}{g}_{2}^{'}({f}_{1}^{'}{f}_{2}^{'}-{f}_{1}^{\text{''}}{f}_{2})\\ +{g}_{2}{g}_{1}^{'}({f}_{1}^{'}{f}_{2}^{'}-{f}_{1}{f}_{2}^{\text{''}})-\frac{1}{\mathrm{3m}}({f}_{1}^{\text{''}}{g}_{1}+{f}_{2}^{\text{''}}{g}_{2})]\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial \lambda }(x,y,T)=\frac{{f}_{1}^{\text{''}}{g}_{1}+{f}_{2}^{\text{''}}{g}_{2}}{{f}_{1}^{'}{g}_{1}+{f}_{2}^{'}{g}_{2}}\end{array}$
