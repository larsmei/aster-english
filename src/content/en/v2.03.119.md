# v2.03.119 SDLS119 - Plate on supports subject to Ricker acceleration (time-frequency method)

**Summary:**

This non-regression test implements the treatment of the nonlinear problem of detachment of a flexible plate modelled by elements finished hulls and placed on contact springs. These springs support the proper weight of the plate and the compressions generated by the rotation and vertical movement of the plate. Detachments are taken into account and treated by penalization.

The stresses imposed in the form of horizontal drive acceleration are Ricker pulses. In this Modelling, the resolution of the dynamic problem is performed in a linear calculation loop where the nodal forces complement is recalculated over the entire time range, due to the nonlinearity of the detachment. Either a harmonic calculation with a frequency and time-return by Fourier transform in the classical time-frequency method, or a transient calculation on a physical basis in a strictly alternative temporal method, is used.

This test case allows the REST_SPEC_TEMP operator to test, on a strictly linear basis, the REST_SPEC_TEMP operator back in time by Fourier transform inverse of all harmonic evolution by comparing its result with the transient evolution obtained directly by the transient calculation.

The Modelling B performs an identical calculation on a modal basis. The calculation parameters are different (including the step and the calculation time band). This test case allows testing the EXCIT_RESU option in a transitional calculation on a modal basis, and the RESU_GENE projection option of a dyna_transin PROJ_BASE structure.

## 2 Reference solution: Modelling A

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The method used here is the time-frequency [bib1] method where after a first linear step resolved in frequency after transformation of Fourier transient excitations and then general return in time of all the result obtained by transformation of Fourier inverse, the addition of internal nodal forces due to the nonlinearity of the decollation calculated over the entire time range is estimated. A new linear resolution in frequency is then carried out in a new step after transformation of Fourier of this complement added to the initial transient excitations. The solution obtained generates a new complement of nonlinear internal nodal forces and so on. The iterative process is stopped when a standard on the time window of the difference in displacements between successive steps becomes less than a user criterion value.

There is also a purely transient variant of this [bib2] method where only transiently resolved without frequent return of the complement of nonlinear internal nodal forces.

### 2.2 Baseline

The maximum horizontal and vertical displacements recorded during 2 steps of the dynamic calculation at the upper left end of the plate at point $P3$ (see ) are used as reference results.

### 2.3 Additional validation of REST_SPEC_TEMP

The REST_SPEC_TEMP operator is based on a fortran version of the FFT algorithm. It is relevant to check that the same result is found with the FFT (python) of CALC_FONCTION. To do this, we will compare the evolution of the following $X$ and $Y$ displacement of point $\mathit{P3}$ . the difference absolute between the two methods must be negligible (in the order of numerical accuracy).

## 4 N_PUIS option verification: Modelling C

### 4.1 Calculation method used for N_PUIS verification

Modelling C is implemented exclusively to verify the use of the N_PUIS option in REST_SPEC_TEMP. For this Modelling a simple harmonic calculation is performed on a physical basis where the Ricker signal is only imposed. Three harmonic calculations are compared:

- A first calculation that imposes a Ricker wavelet with a time step $\Delta t=0.01s$ up to ${T}_{max}=20.47s$ . Then a harmonic calculation is performed on a physical basis with a $\Delta f=1/({T}_{max}+\Delta t)$ frequency step and up to ${f}_{max}=25\mathit{Hz}$ . To return to time a REST_SPEC_TEMP is applied with N_PUIS = 0;
- A second calculation that imposes a Ricker wavelet with a time step $\Delta t=0.01s$ up to ${T}_{max}=20.47s$ . Then a harmonic calculation is performed on a physical basis with a $\Delta f=1/({T}_{max}+\Delta t)$ frequency step and up to ${f}_{max}=10\mathit{Hz}$ . To return to time a REST_SPEC_TEMP is applied with N_PUIS = 2;
- A final calculation that imposes a Ricker wavelet with an time step $\Delta t=0.01s$ up to ${T}_{max}=9.99s$ . Then a harmonic calculation is performed on a physical basis with a $\Delta f=1/({T}_{max}+\Delta t)$ frequency step and up to ${f}_{max}=50\mathit{Hz}$ . To return in time a CALC_FONCTION(FFT...) is applied with METHODE = ‘COMPLET'. In this case, you cannot use REST_SPEC_TEMP because the input signal is supplied as FFT calculated with CALC_FONCTION(FFT...) with METHODE = ‘COMPLET'. Therefore, you use a CALC_FONCTION(FFT...) with METHODE = ‘COMPLET' also for post-processing (transformed from Fourier inverse).

## 6 Modelling A

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the resolution of the dynamic problem is performed in a linear calculation loop where the nodal complement of forces due to nonlinearity of the delamination is recalculated over the entire time range. Either a harmonic calculation with a frequency and time-return by Fourier transform in the classical time-frequency method is used, or a transient calculation on a physical basis with a strictly alternative temporal method.

### 6.2 Mesh characteristics

The model is composed of 55 nodes (285 ddls), 42 elements (32 elements DKT plates and 10 DIS_T discrete elements).

### 6.3 Calculation parameters

Each transient dynamic calculation is conducted over an $5s$ interval by time step of $0.005s$ archived every 2 steps. Each harmonic calculation is conducted with a step of $1/20.48\mathrm{Hz}$ that allows to restore a $20.48s$ time window sufficient to properly calculate the FFT force of gravity constant over time; the maximum frequency of calculation is $25\mathrm{Hz}$ and the extended frequency is $50\mathrm{Hz}$ to obtain an time step $0.01s$ in the FFT time window.

## 7 Summary of the results

The implementation of this test case can be considered a good application of the time-frequency method and can be used to test, on a strictly linear basis, REST_SPEC_TEMP operator back in time by Fourier transform inverse of any harmonic evolution by comparing its result with the transient evolution obtained directly by a transient calculation on a physical basis.
