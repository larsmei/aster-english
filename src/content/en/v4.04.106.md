# v4.04.106 TPLV106 - Stationary nonlinear thermal in mobile marker

**Summary:**

This elementary test allows a three-dimensional example to be treated as a problem with a variable of space in nonlinear stationary thermal as a moving marker (convection-diffusion problem).

It also allows to check the account of a solid/liquid phase change by *Code_Aster* .

The reference solution is analytical and the differences with the results of *Code_Aster* are less than 1%. The problem is modelled in the plane case.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Either a bar moving at $V$ speed to the right of temperature conditions imposed in $X=0$ and $X=L$ expressed in a fixed reference (relative to the moving bar).

![Figure](/figures/v4.04.106/100026E0000069D50000330E98E799A372599B9D.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

- the thermal conductivity is constant: $K=150W/m°C$
- the enthalpy function is such that:

$\beta (T)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}{C}_{S}T& ;T\le {T}_{1}\\ {C}_{S}T+{C}_{\mathrm{Sl}}(T-{T}_{1})& ;{T}_{1}\le T\le {T}_{2}\\ {C}_{S}T+{C}_{\mathrm{Sl}}({T}_{2}-{T}_{1})+{C}_{l}(T-{T}_{2})& ;{T}_{1}\le T\le {T}_{2}\end{array}$

with the following values:

$\begin{array}{}{C}_{S}={C}_{l}=1/3.{10}^{7}J/{m}^{3}°C\\ {C}_{\mathrm{Sl}}=8.333{10}^{7}J/{m}^{3}°C\\ {T}_{1}=585.°C\\ {T}_{2}=615°C\end{array}$

![Figure](/figures/v4.04.106/10001B3C0000284F000021B181ADDA7F90ED407F.svg)

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Temperatures imposed on the ends

${T}_{0}=200°C$ for $x=0$

${T}_{L}=1000°C$ for $x=L=\mathrm{1m}$

Solid moving speed: $V={10}^{-4}m/s$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference result is of the semi-analytic type. The 1D equation to be solved is as follows:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}V\beta {(T)}_{,x}-K{T}_{,xx}=0\\ \text{avec}{T}_{(x=0)}={T}_{0}\text{et}{T}_{(x=L)}={T}_{L}\end{array}$                         éq 2.1-1

By integrating the equation [eq 2.1-1-o]:

$\frac{V}{K}\beta (T)-\frac{\mathrm{dT}}{\mathrm{dx}}=A$                                 éq 2.1-2

where $A$ is a boundary conditions-dependent constant, $\frac{V}{K}$-speed, and $\beta (T)$-enthalpy function.

This constant will be determined analytically.

The equation [eq 2.1-2] leads to:

$x={\int}_{{T}_{0}}^{T(x)}\frac{\mathrm{dT}}{A+\frac{V}{K}\beta (T)}$                                 éq 2.1-3

who must verify:

$L={\int}_{{T}_{0}}^{{T}_{L}}\frac{\mathrm{dT}}{A+\frac{V}{K}\beta (T)}$                             éq 2.1-4

Knowing ${T}_{0,}{T}_{L},L,V,t$ and $\beta (T)$ , the equation [eq 2.1-4] shall give the value of the $A$ integration constant .

However, it is difficult (or even impossible) to determine this constant analytically, hence the use of a numerical resolution of the equation [eq 2.1-4] to determine $A$ .

With the $({T}_{0,}{T}_{L},{T}_{1,}{T}_{2,}{C}_{S}={C}_{l},{C}_{\mathrm{Sl}}...)$ problem data, we got the (physical) solution of $A$ that takes the $A=-294.9117$ value.

From this constant, the analytical solution of the problem [eq 2.1-1-o] is analytical.
