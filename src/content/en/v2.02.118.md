# v2.02.118 SDLL118 - Axial fluid-elastic excitation beam

**Summary:**

A PVC tube is considered to be centrally located in a cylindrical circular-section chamber and subjected to the action of water axial flow. This physical configuration corresponds to the experimental device of Tanaka et al. [bib1] which is used to measure changes in frequency and damping reduced from the first mode of the tube to the average flow speed.

The purpose of this test case is to validate the MEFISTEAU [R4.07.04](doc:r4.07.04) model resorption to calculate the modal characteristics of a wired structure under confined axial flow, taking into account fluid-elastic excitation.

The specific features to be tested are:

- DEFI_FLUI_STRU [U4.25.01](doc:u4.25.01) operator: definition of parameters for the consideration of fluid-elastic coupling, in the case of a configuration of the "xial flow tube beam" type (factor keyword FAISCEAU_AXIAL),
- CALC_FLUI_STRU [U47.66.02](doc:u47.66.02) operator: calculation of the evolution of modal reduced frequencies and dampings according to the average flow speed, by implementing the MEFISTEAU model.

The numerical results of the Tanaka et al. device simulation are validated by comparison with the experimental results. Given the relatively large uncertainties on the experimental values, the reference results for the non-regression of the code are those obtained numerically when test case was returned.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The tube in question is a hollow cylinder with the following dimensions:

| length | $L=1m$ |
| --- | --- |
| outer diameter | ${\phi }_{\text{ext}}=13\mathrm{mm}$ |
| inner diameter | ${\phi }_{int}=8,8\mathrm{mm}$ |

The tube is placed in the centre of a cylindrical enclosure with circular cross section. The inner diameter of the enclosure is $d=5\mathrm{cm}$.

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.118/10000000000003CD000002088B5D335940973782.png)

The roughness of the tube walls is $\varepsilon ={10}^{–5}m$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The physical characteristics of the PVC material constituting the tube are as follows:

| Young's modulus | $E=2,80.{10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =0,3$ |
| density | $\rho =1500\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |

The water surrounding the tube has the following properties:

| density | ${\rho}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| --- | --- |
| kinematic viscosity | ${\nu}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1,1.10–6{m}^{2}/s$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Both ends of the tube are connected to fixed supports by two metal rods. The relative flexibility of these rods frees the degrees of freedom of tube ends from rotation. Therefore, it can be estimated that the tube supports are of the rotulated-rotulated type, with the metal rods introducing at each end an additional stiffness of rotation.

In addition, these rods allow an axial stress to be applied to the tube, which can thus be prestressed in traction or compression. In practice, two configurations are studied:

- non-prestressed tube: no effort is applied. This configuration corresponds to Modelling A of test case,
- prestressed compression tube by applying an axial stress of $40N$ at one end.

This configuration corresponds to the Modelling of test case B.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The tube is represented by elements straight beams Timoshenko (MECA_POU_D_T), supported by as many mesh cells segments as 2 nodes (SEG2). Two elements MECA_DIS_TR are added to the nodes ends of the tube, allowing to model metal rods by discreet rotation stiffeners.

The elements beam shall be made of the characteristics of circular cross-section:

| external radius | ${R}_{\mathrm{ext}}=6,5{.10}^{–3}m$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thickness | $E=2,1{.10}^{–3}m$ | (see paragraph [§1.1]) |

These elements are also assigned a material of ELAS behavior:

| Young's modulus | $E=2,80.{10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =0,3$ |  |
| density | $\rho =1500\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | (see paragraph [§1.2]) |

Discrete elementss are assigned the same stiffness rotational axis around the two orthogonal axes to the tube's neutral fibre:

|  | ${K}_{r}=6,29\mathrm{Nm}/\mathrm{rad}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |

This stiffness rotation was adjusted to find the eigenfrequency of the first double mode in air.

Degrees of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$ and $\mathrm{DZ}$ nodes $\mathit{N1}$ and $\mathrm{N101}$ ends are blocked to prevent a rigid body movement of the tube (axial translation movement). $\mathrm{DY}$ node $\mathit{N1}$ is also blocked. In addition, in each node, degree of freedom $\mathrm{DRY}$ rotation is blocked, to prevent twisting movement.

The tube is immersed in a cylindrical $2,5\mathrm{cm}$ enclosure of internal radius (see paragraph [§1.1]). The density and kinematic viscosity profiles of the surrounding water are assumed to be constant along the tube:

| density | ${\rho}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kinematic viscosity | ${\nu}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1,1{.10}^{–6}{m}^{2}/s$ | (see paragraph [§1.2]) |

No axial effort is applied to the tube which is therefore not prestressed.

The reduced frequency and damping evolutions of the first double bending mode are calculated for an average flow speed range from $0$ to $8m/s$ , by $1m/s$ step .

An initial reduced depreciation of the 4.8% tube is taken into account.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The total number of nodes used for the mesh is 101.

The mesh cells (SEG2 type) are 100.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 3.3 Calculation steps

The validation of fluid-structure coupling operators for axial flow tube beam configurations is done in two main steps.

The first is to define the parameters for taking into account fluid-structure coupling with the DEFI_FLUI_STRU operator followed by the keyword FAISCEAU_AXIAL.

The second is the calculation of the reduced modal frequency and damping evolutions according to the average flow speed, with the CALC_FLUI_STRU operator and the implementation of the MEFISTEAU model.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

Model B is identical to model A (see paragraph [§3.1]), but this time the tube is prestressed in compression.

$23,7N$ compression axial force is applied to node end $\mathrm{N101}$ . The stress intensity has therefore been adjusted to the experimental value provided for $40N$ , in order to correctly recover the frequency value of the first double mode in air (see paragraphs [§1.2], [§1.3]). This readjustment can be applied by the summary modelling of metal rods for support and compression.

The nodal force is derived from the elementary forces vector, then a assembled vector which is constructed according to the degrees of freedom numbering of the tube. The static deformation due to compression is then obtained by multiplying the assembled vector by the inverse of the structural rigidity matrix. Using this static deformation, a stress field is then calculated at the elements, from which a geometric rigidity matrix is derived. This matrix is then added to the structural rigidity matrix to obtain the rigidity matrix of the compression tube, which is finally used for the calculation of the air modes.

The reduced frequency and damping evolutions of the first double bending mode are calculated for an average flow speed range from $0$ to $8m/s$ , by $1m/s$ step . An initial reduced damping of the tube is taken into account by 4.3%.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The Mesh characteristics of this second model are the same as the A model, i.e.:

101 nodes used and 100 mesh cells type SEG2.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 4.3 Calculation steps

As for model A, the functions to be validated are those of fluid-structure coupling operators for configurations of the "xial flow pipe beam" type (see paragraph [§3.3].

In addition, Modelling B allows testing other features.

The first allows to realize the calculation of a field of displacement to nodes by inversion of the structural rigidity matrix and produces the inverse by a assembled force vector, with the operators FACTORISER and RESOUDRE.

The second allows the calculation of a geometric rigidity matrix using a stress field at elements, with the CALC_MATR_ELEM operator, option RIGI_GEOM.
