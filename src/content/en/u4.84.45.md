# u4.84.45 Macro-command POST_NEWMARK

## 1 Purpose

Check the stability of a 2D embankment structure (dyke/dam) according to the criterion of maximum admissible horizontal displacement under seismic loading, via the Newmark method [1].

The procedure depends, as input, on the result concept of a linear or non-linear transient dynamic analysis, as well as on the position of the potential slip circle and on the associated seismic coefficient.

The macro-command also makes it possible to estimate the static and dynamic factor of safety from a stress field coming from the application of self-weight to the structure, as well as during the dynamic transient.

Depending on the options requested by the user, the macro-command supplies as output a table containing the mean horizontal acceleration of the sliding mass, the horizontal displacement, the factor of safety and the seismic coefficient.

## 3 Description of the macro-command

The macro-command POST_NEWMARK makes it possible to obtain an estimate of the irreversible lateral displacement of a potentially sliding zone of an embankment structure (dam/dyke) via the Newmark method bib1.

The Newmark method is based on the idea that the potentially sliding zone of the structure can be approximated by a block sliding on an inclined plane. During the earthquake, this block slides along the inclined plane when the mean acceleration (${a}_{m}$ ) of the block exceeds a fixed value, called the critical acceleration (${a}_{y}$ ). The method considers that the residual displacement of the sliding block can be obtained by integrating twice the time instants of the mean acceleration exceeding the critical acceleration. This operation is carried out in the following manner:

- obtaining the velocity signal by integration of the mean acceleration exceeding the critical acceleration,
- clipping of the velocity signal at v>0 and then integration in order to obtain the displacement signal.

From a dynamic analysis of finite-element type, the mean acceleration of a potentially sliding zone is defined as the quotient of the resultant of the lateral forces ${F}_{L}$ along the interface between the potentially sliding zone and the rest of the structure and the mass $m$ of this zone:

$$
{a}_{m}=\frac{{F}_{L}}{m}
$$

The critical acceleration is defined as the acceleration which leads to a factor of safety of value 1.0 for the potentially sliding zone. From the critical acceleration, one defines the seismic coefficient ${k}_{y}$ by relating the critical acceleration to the value of the acceleration of gravity $g$ :

$$
{k}_{y}=\frac{{a}_{y}}{g}
$$

The macro-command POST_NEWMARK accepts only 2D meshes and two types of slip zone:

- a simple circular shape, whose position is supplied by the user. In this case, the resultant of the lateral forces is computed from the mesh cells of the mesh on which the dynamic analysis was carried out and of which a part belongs to the circle defined by the user.
- a shape defined from an auxiliary mesh, which must be positioned on the geometrical locus of the mass that slides. In this case, one computes the resultant of the lateral forces on the mesh cells corresponding to the geometrical locus of the auxiliary mesh.

The seismic coefficient ${k}_{y}$ may be supplied by the user as input to the macro-command, if obtained for example from a pseudo-static stability analysis with a non-linear constitutive law incorporating a failure criterion, or by a methodology of limit-analysis type.

The user may also not supply the seismic coefficient ${k}_{y}$ . In this case, it is estimated from the evolution of the factor of safety with the mean acceleration (cf. test case sdlp200). The regression is carried out for a range lying between 0.8 and 1.2 of the value of the factor of safety, and the critical-acceleration value is retained by default conservatively because it is at −3 standard deviations from the mean of the regression.

### 3.1 Computation of the factor of safety in statics and in dynamics

The macro-command POST_NEWMARK makes it possible to estimate the static and dynamic factor of safety of a potentially sliding zone defined from:

- an auxiliary mesh defining the potentially sliding zone, or directly from the data supplied by the user on the position of the slip circle. In this latter case, the macro-command builds the auxiliary mesh directly,
- a static stress field coming from gravity.

For this one uses the definition of the global factor of safety ${F}_{s}^{g}$ of Kulhawy bib2:

$$
{F}_{s}^{g}=\frac{\sum_{i}^{N}(c'+\sigma {'}_{n,s}\tan(\varphi '))\Delta L}{\sum_{i}^{N}{\sigma}_{t,s}.\Delta L}
$$

where $c'$ and $\varphi '$ are the effective cohesion and the effective friction angle of the material, $\sigma {'}_{n,s}$ is the normal stress and ${\sigma}_{t}$ the tangential stress on the slip line, both obtained from a static gravity analysis, $N$ the spatial discretisation of the slip line and $\Delta L$ the length of each discretised element.

For a dynamic analysis, the estimate of the factor of safety takes into account the variations of the normal and tangential stresses along the slip line, via the following formula:

$$
{F}_{s}^{g}=\frac{\sum_{i}^{N}(c'+\sigma {'}_{n,s}\tan(\varphi '))\Delta L+\sum_{i}^{N}(\sigma {'}_{n,d}\tan(\varphi '))\Delta L}{\sum_{i}^{N}{\sigma}_{t,s}.\Delta L+\sum_{i}^{N}{\sigma}_{t,d}.\Delta L}
$$

where $\sigma {'}_{n,d}$ is the normal stress and ${\sigma}_{t,d}$ the tangential stress on the slip line for the dynamic analysis. The user must therefore supply the static stress field coming from an application of self-weight. The macro-command carries out the estimate of the factor of safety for each time step.

## 4 Operands

### 4.1 Operands RAYON, CENTRE_X, CENTRE_Y

```text
◊    RAYON = rayon
```

Radius of the slip circle for which the structure must be checked

```text
♦    CENTRE_X = posx
```

Position along the coordinate X of the centre of the slip circle

```text
♦    CENTRE_Y = posy
```

Position along the coordinate Y of the centre of the slip circle

**Note 1:**
*The command POST_NEWMARK handles only structures modelled according to a 2D geometry. The command stops with a fatal error if the mesh used is 3D.*

**Note 2:**
*The user must check the suitability of the position supplied for the slip circle and of the mesh on which the dynamic analysis was carried out.*

### 4.2 Operand RAFF_CERCLE

```text
◊     RAFF_CERCLE = n,      [I]
```

This operand makes it possible to choose the level of refinement of the mesh of the slip circle which will be used for the computation of the factor of safety. The refinement is controlled by the integer value n, which controls the size of the angular sector $\alpha$ [°] of the mesh cells in the following manner: $\alpha =\frac{45}{{2}^{n}}$ .

### 4.3 Operand RESULTAT

```text
◊    RESULTAT = resultat
```

This operand makes it possible to supply the result concept incorporating the seismic response of the structure.

**Note:**
*In the case of a result of type dyna_trans, the user must beforehand compute the stress field of type SIEF_ELGA. This operation is carried out with the command CALC_CHAMP (see test case zzzz402a).*

### 4.4 Operand KY

```text
◊    KY = ky
```

This operand makes it possible to supply the value of the seismic coefficient obtained for the slip circle from a static stability analysis (e.g. limit analysis) and for which the structure must be checked. If it is not supplied, the macro-command will compute it automatically and the value will be displayed in a column of the table at the output of the macro-command.

### 4.5 Operand POSITION

```text
◊    POSITION = /’AVAL’,    [DEFAUT]
                /’AMONT’
```

This operand makes it possible to distinguish a slip circle on the downstream face or on the upstream face. The macro-command considers by convention the upstream face to the left of the structure and the downstream face to the right. This information makes it possible to compute the factor of safety correctly, because the sign of the shear stresses along the slip circle depends on whether the circle is on the upstream face or on the downstream face.

**Note:**
*The user may consult the test case sslp119 for the use of this operand.*

### 4.6 Operand GROUP_MA_CALC

```text
♦    GROUP_MA_CALC = grma
```

This mandatory operand makes it possible to supply the set of mesh-cell groups on which the dynamic analysis was carried out. These mesh-cell groups are used in order to determine the mesh cells belonging to the slip circle. The use of this operand makes it possible to speed up appreciably the projection stages if the factor of safety is computed by the macro-command.

### 4.7 Operand MAILLAGE_GLIS

```text
◊    MAILLAGE_GLIS = mail
```

This operand makes it possible to supply the auxiliary mesh which will be used as a “patch” for the stability analysis. This mesh must be positioned geometrically on the zone that slides and must have SEG2 or SEG3 mesh cells for the slip line.

### 4.8 Operand GROUP_MA_GLIS

```text
◊    GROUP_MA_GLIS = grma_gls
```

This operand allows the user to supply the surface mesh-cell group grma_gls of the mesh mail on which he or she wishes to define the patch. If the keyword is not supplied, the patch is constituted of all the surface mesh cells of the mesh mail.

### 4.9 Operand GROUP_MA_LIGNE

```text
◊    GROUP_MA_LIGNE = grma_lgn
```

This operand allows the user to supply the line mesh-cell group grma_lgn of the mesh mail defining the slip line. If the keyword is not supplied, the slip line is constituted of all the line mesh cells of type SEG2 and SEG3 of the mesh mail.

### 4.10 Operand RESULTAT_PESANTEUR

```text
◊    RESULTAT_PESANTEUR = resu_pes
```

This mandatory operand makes it possible to supply the result concept incorporating the response of the structure to gravity (static stresses).

**Note:**
*In the case of a result of type evol_elas, the user must beforehand compute the stress field of type SIEF_ELGA. This operation is carried out with the command CALC_CHAMP (see test case zzzz402a).*

### 4.11 Operands CHAM_PHI and CHAM_COHESION

```text
♦    CHAM_PHI = cham_phi

♦    CHAM_COHESION = cham_coh
```

These operands, mandatory if RESULTAT_PESANTEUR is supplied, allow the user to supply the fields at the nodes of type NOEU_NEUT_R which define the material properties ($\varphi '$ and $c'$) necessary for the evaluation of the factor of safety. $\varphi '$ must be stored in the variable X1 and $c'$ in the variable X2.

**Note 1:**
*The user may take inspiration from the test case sslp119a in order to see the sequence of commands to be used for the definition of CHAM_PHI and CHAM_COHESION.*

**Note 2:**
*The value of* $\varphi '$ *must be supplied by the user in degrees*  [°].
*The value of* $c'$ *must be consistent with the modelling carried out.*

### 4.12 Operand CHAM_FS

```text
◊     CHAM_FS = CO(cham_fs)
```

This operand makes it possible to produce, at the output of the macro-command, the field of the local static factor of safety.

### 4.13 Operand NB_ECART_TYPE

```text
◊    NB_ECART_TYPE = nb_ecart_type,        [R]
```

In the case where the critical acceleration is estimated by the macro-command, this operand makes it possible to take into account conservatively the variance of its estimate by subtracting nb_ecart_type * standard deviation from the estimator of the mean of the critical acceleration.

**Note:**
*Choosing NB_ECART_TYPE=0 amounts to not taking the variance into account in the estimate of the critical acceleration.*

### 4.14 Operand METHODE

```text
◊    METHODE = /‘ECLA_PG‘,        [DEFAUT]
               /‘COLLOCATION‘
```

This operand makes it possible to choose the type of projection of the operator PROJ_CHAMP in order to obtain the stresses in the support mesh of the zone that slides, necessary for the computation of the factor of safety (in statics and in dynamics). The default method ‘ECLA_PG’ is more accurate, but nevertheless more costly for large models than the approach via the method ‘COLLOCATION’.

**Note:**
*The choice of the projection method by COLLOCATION implies having beforehand computed the stress field of type SIEF_NOEU. This operation is carried out with the command CALC_CHAMP (see test case zzzz402c)*

### 4.15 Operand VERI_MASSE

```text
◊    VERI_MASSE = /‘NON‘,        [DEFAUT]
                  /‘OUI‘
```

This operand makes it possible to activate the option of checking the computation of the mass of the potentially sliding zone. For this, the macro-command carries out successive refinements via MACR_ADAP_MAIL, so as to estimate the mass of this zone better. For this, two refinement strategies are deployed:

- If the option RAYON is used, one refines a circular zone with a radius 20% larger than that defining the slip circle,
- If the option MAILLAGE_GLISS is used, uniform refinement of the whole mesh (which may potentially be costly).

### 4.16 Operands RESI_RELA and ITER_MAXI

```text
◊     RESI_RELA = resi,         [R]
◊     ITER_MAXI = iter,         [I]
```

In the case where VERI_MASSE=’OUI’, these operands make it possible to control the mesh-refinement process via two criteria:

- RESI_RELA: maximum relative residual between two verification iterations.
- ITER_MAXI: maximum number of iterations.

One stops the process as soon as one of the two criteria is reached.

### 4.17 Operand CHAM_MATER

```text
◊    CHAM_MATER = cham_mat,        [cham_mater]
```

In the case where the dynamic analysis was carried out directly from the mass, stiffness and damping matrices, this operand allows the user to supply a material field if VERI_MASSE=’OUI’.

### 4.18 Keyword MAILLAGE

```text
◊    MAILLAGE = _F(
```

Factor keyword making it possible to supply the name of the mesh objects that the user may have available at the output of the macro-command.

#### 4.18.1 Operands MAILLAGE, NOM_GROUPE

```text
◊    MAILLAGE = CO(mesh_out),
◊    NOM_GROUPE = grpma_out,
```

Operand for recovering as output the mesh mesh_out, equal to the mesh given in resultat enriched with the mesh-cell group grpma_out on which the computation of the irreversible displacements was carried out.

#### 4.18.2 Operand MAILLAGE_MASSE

```text
◊    MAILLAGE_MASSE = CO(mesh_mass_out),
```

Operand for recovering, at the output of the macro-command, the refined mesh used for checking the estimate of the mass (operand VERI_MASSE).
