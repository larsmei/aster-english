# r3.03.07 Follower pressure for the volumetric shell elements

**Summary:**

We present in this document the model used to compute the follower-pressure loading acting on the middle surface of the volumetric shell finite elements corresponding to the COQUE_3D modelling. The discretisation of the loading leads to a nodal vector of the external forces and to a non-symmetric contribution in the tangent stiffness matrix. These finite-element objects are evaluated at each iteration of the Newton algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

Our analysis starts from the weak formulation of equilibrium under a follower-pressure loading activated by the keyword TYPE_CHARGE:’SUIV’ in the command STAT_NON_LINE [U4.32.01](doc:u4.32.01). The difference with respect to a classical geometrically linear analysis is that the pressure acts on the deformed geometry and no longer on the initial geometry. This new geometry is obtained from the transform of the initial middle surface subjected to large displacements and large rotations [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05). The notation is inspired by [R3.07.05](doc:r3.07.05).

This transform can be parametrised exactly like the initial surface by using the reduced coordinates of the associated isoparametric element: the covariant or contravariant frames are constructed at each point of the deformed surface. With this parametrisation, the virtual work of the pressure is written in the deformed configuration by using the associated isoparametric elements. The result is that the integration domain is independent of the displacements, which one uses to express the variation of the virtual work of the external pressure forces with respect to those displacements. This presents an important advantage over the method applied to the pressure which follows the facets of the 3D elements [R3.03.04](doc:r3.03.04). Indeed, this latter method, based on an updated Lagrangian formulation, leads to non-linear terms that are difficult to linearise, coming from the Jacobian transformation with respect to the reference configuration.

The finite-element objects obtained by linearisation, with respect to the incremental displacements, of the virtual work of the external pressure forces are to be updated at each iteration of the Newton algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE. We emphasise the fact that the contribution of the follower pressure to the tangent stiffness matrix is non-symmetric, and we recall that the geometric part of the tangent matrix is already non-symmetric [bib2].

## 2 Kinematics

For the volumetric shell elements $\Omega$ one defines a reference surface $\omega$ , or middle surface, warped (of curvilinear coordinates ${\xi}_{1}{\xi}_{2}$ for example) and a thickness $h({\xi}_{1}{\xi}_{2})$ measured along the normal to the middle surface. The position of the points of the shell is given by the curvilinear coordinates $({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})$ of the middle surface $\omega$ and the elevation ${\xi}_{3}$ with respect to this surface.

One recalls the large transformation undergone by the shell:

${\omega}^{\varphi}$ (the set of points ${P}^{\varphi}$ at ${\xi}_{3}=0$ ) is the transform of the initial middle surface $\omega$ (the set of points $Pà{\xi}_{3}=0$ ).

The position of the point ${P}^{\varphi}$ on the deformed configuration can be established as a function of the position of the initial point $P$ as follows:

${x}_{P}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})={x}_{P}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})+{u}_{P}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})$ .

### 2.1 Parametrisation of the transform of the middle surface

The transform ${w}^{j}$ can be parametrised in a manner similar to the parametrisation of the initial surface. One can thus define the infinitesimal element of the vector tangent to ${w}^{j}$ :

$\begin{array}{}{\text{dx}}_{P}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\frac{\partial {x}_{P}^{\varphi}}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}d{\xi}_{1}+\frac{\partial {x}_{P}^{\varphi}}{\partial {x}_{1}}d{\xi}_{2}\\ {\text{dx}}_{P}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=d{\xi}_{1}{a}_{1}^{\varphi}({x}_{1},{x}_{2})+d{\xi}_{2}{a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\end{array}$

where $\left[{a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2});{a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\right]$ represents a non-orthogonal natural basis $({a}_{1}^{\varphi}.{a}_{2}^{\varphi}\ne 0)$ and a non-unit one $(\parallel {a}_{1}^{\varphi}\parallel \ne 1;\parallel {a}_{1}^{\varphi}\parallel \ne 1)$ tangent to the surface ${\omega}^{\varphi}$ . The two basis vectors can be related to the displacements via the following formula:

$\begin{array}{}{a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\frac{\partial {x}_{P}^{\varphi}}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}=\frac{\partial ({x}_{p}+{u}_{p})}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}\\ {a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\frac{\partial {x}_{P}^{\varphi}}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}=\frac{\partial ({x}_{p}+{u}_{p})}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\end{array}$

which makes it possible to relate them to the vectors of the natural basis attached to the initial surface $w$ by the relations:

$\begin{array}{}{a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})={a}_{1}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})+\frac{\partial {u}_{p}}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}\\ {a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})={a}_{2}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})+\frac{\partial {u}_{p}}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\end{array}$

It is important to note that these vectors are distinct from the vectors obtained by the large rotation $\Lambda$ of the vectors ${a}_{1}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2});{a}_{2}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})$ :

$\begin{array}{}{a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\ne \Lambda ({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2}){a}_{1}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\\ {a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\ne \Lambda ({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2}){a}_{2}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\end{array}$

Indeed, because of the deformation due to transverse shear, the rotated vectors are no longer tangent to ${\omega}^{\varphi}$ . An illustration of this is given by [Figure 3.1-a].

With this parametrisation, the infinitesimal surface-element vector which is perpendicular to ${\omega}^{\varphi}$ can be written:

$d{\omega}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})={a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\times {a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

## 3 Variational formulation

### 3.1 Virtual work

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.07/10001B62000069D5000040E8A0C2E2A98FAB0205.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Volumetric shell. Follower pressure on the initial middle surface and on its transform**

The virtual work of a **follower** pressure $p$ (that is to say, acting on the transformed middle surface and moving with it) can be expressed in the form:

$\delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=-{\int}_{{\omega}^{\varphi}}\delta {u}_{p}.pd{\omega}^{\varphi}$

If one uses the isoparametric surface element corresponding to our volumetric-shell modelling, the surface $d{\omega}^{\varphi}$ is expressed directly as a function of the isoparametric coordinates $d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$ and one obtains the following simple form of the equation above:

$\delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=-{\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}\delta {u}_{p}.p({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2}){a}_{1}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})\times {a}_{2}^{\varphi}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

### 3.2 Tangent operator

As the virtual work of the follower pressure depends on the current configuration, its linear variation $\Delta$ is not zero and must be taken into account. The tangent operator associated with this virtual work is written at iteration $(i+1)$ in the form:

$L\left[\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{(i+1)}\right]=\delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{(i)}+\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{(i)}$

where $\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{(i)}$ is the increment between two iterations of the virtual work of the follower pressure. If the pressure is given in the form:

$p=\lambda {p}_{0}$

$\lambda$ being the load level which is fixed during the iterations (load control $\Delta \lambda =0$ ), one can write:

$\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=-{\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}\delta {u}_{p}.p({a}_{1}^{\varphi}\times \Delta {a}_{2}^{\varphi}-{a}_{2}^{\varphi}\times \Delta {a}_{1}^{\varphi})d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

The incremental variations of the vectors of the local basis tangent to the transform of the middle surface are given by:

$\begin{array}{}{\Delta a}_{1}^{\varphi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}{\Delta u}_{P}\\ {\Delta a}_{2}^{\varphi}=\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}{\Delta u}_{P}\end{array}$

since the initial middle surface does not “move” during the iterations, which implies $\Delta {x}_{P}=0$ .

These calculations finally make it possible to establish the expression of the increment of the virtual work of the follower pressure in the form:

$\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=-{\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}\delta {u}_{P}\cdot P(\left[{a}_{1}^{\varphi}\times \right]\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\Delta {u}_{P}-\left[{a}_{2}^{\varphi}\times \right]\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\Delta {u}_{P})d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

where $\left[{a}_{1}^{\varphi}\times \right]\mathrm{et}\left[{a}_{2}^{\varphi}\times \right]$ are respectively the antisymmetric matrices of the tangent vectors ${a}_{1}^{\varphi}\mathrm{et}{a}_{2}^{\varphi}$ respectively.

**Note:**

In reference [bib2], an integration by parts is carried out on the expression above. It is shown that the tangent matrix can be decomposed into a symmetric part arising from an integration over the domain and an antisymmetric part arising from the integration over the contour. It is also shown that the assembly of the antisymmetric parts of the elementary tangent matrices leads to a zero matrix when the pressure is continuous from one finite element to another, because of the existence of a potential associated with the work of the pressure in that case.

## 4 Discretisation

At the points $P$ of the middle surface, the interpolation of the virtual displacement is written:

$\delta u({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2})=\sum_{I=1}^{\text{NB}1}{N}_{I}^{(1)}({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2}){(\begin{array}{}\delta u\\ \delta v\\ \delta w\end{array})}_{I}$

and the interpolation of the incremental displacement between two iterations is written:

$\delta u({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2})=\sum_{I=1}^{\text{NB}1}{N}_{I}^{(1)}({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2}){(\begin{array}{}\Delta u\\ \Delta v\\ \Delta w\end{array})}_{I}$

We rewrite the two preceding equations in matrix form:

$\begin{array}{}\delta u({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\left[N\right]{\left\lbrace \delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}\\ \Delta u({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\left[N\right]{\left\lbrace \Delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}\end{array}$

where $\left[N\right]$ is the matrix of the translational shape functions on the middle surface, whose expression is:

$\left[N\right]=\left[\text{...}{\left[{N}_{I}^{(1)}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\right]}_{I=1,\text{NB}1}\text{...}{\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]}_{\text{NB}2}\right]$

The shape functions ${N}_{I}^{(1)}\text{et}{N}_{I}^{(2)}$ (used in what follows) are given in the appendix of [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04). The nodes $I=1,\text{NB}1$ are the vertex nodes and the mid-side nodes (for the quadrangle and the triangle). The node $\text{NB}2$ is at the barycentre of the element.

The vector ${\left\lbrace \delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}$ is the nodal vector of the virtual displacements, given by:

${\left\lbrace \delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}=(\begin{array}{}.\\ .\\ .\\ {(\begin{array}{}\delta w\\ \delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \delta {\theta}_{y}\\ \delta {\theta}_{z}\end{array})}_{I}\\ .\\ .\\ .\\ I=1,\mathrm{NB1}\\ \\ {(\begin{array}{}\delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \delta {\theta}_{x}\end{array})}_{\mathrm{NB2}}\end{array})$

The vector ${\left\lbrace \Delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}$ is the nodal vector of the incremental displacements between two iterations.

${\left\lbrace \Delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}=(\begin{array}{}.\\ .\\ .\\ {(\begin{array}{}\Delta u\\ \Delta v\\ \Delta w\\ \Delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \Delta {\theta}_{y}\\ \Delta {\theta}_{z}\end{array})}_{I}\\ .\\ .\\ .\\ I=1,\mathrm{NB1}\\ \\ {(\begin{array}{}\Delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \Delta {\theta}_{x}\\ \Delta {\theta}_{x}\end{array})}_{\mathrm{NB2}}\end{array})$

This discretisation also makes it possible to establish the expression of the derivatives of the incremental displacement of the middle surface with respect to the surface isoparametric coordinates in the form:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}\Delta u({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2})=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}N\right]{\left\lbrace \Delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}\\ \frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\Delta u({\xi}_{1,}{\xi}_{2})=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}N\right]{\left\lbrace \Delta u\right\rbrace }^{e}\end{array}$

where $\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}N\right]\text{et}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}N\right]$ are the derivative matrices of the translational shape functions on the middle surface, whose expressions are:

$\begin{array}{}\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}N\right]=\left[\mathrm{...}{\left[\frac{\partial {N}_{I}^{(1)}}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\right]}_{I=1,\text{NB}1}\mathrm{...}{\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]}_{\text{NB}2}\right]\\ \left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}N\right]=\left[\mathrm{...}{\left[\frac{\partial {N}_{I}^{(1)}}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 1& 0\\ 0& 0& 1\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]\right]}_{I=1,\text{NB}1}\mathrm{...}{\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]}_{\text{NB}2}\right]\end{array}$

One can thus express the virtual work of the follower pressure in the following matrix form:

$\delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=\left\lbrace \delta {u}^{e}\right\rbrace .\left\lbrace {f}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{e}\right\rbrace$

with $\left\lbrace {f}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{e}\right\rbrace$ the nodal vector of the external forces, which can be expressed in the following manner:

$\left\lbrace {f}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}^{e}\right\rbrace ={\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}{\left[N\right]}^{T}({a}_{1}^{\varphi}\times {a}_{1}^{\varphi})d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

It is important to note that, with our parametrisation of the transform of the middle surface, the Jacobian $\det(\left[J({\xi}_{3}=0)\right])$ of this surface is not involved in the computation of the finite-element objects.

One will also note that the pressure is discretised with an isoparametric interpolation of the values at the NB2 nodes:

$p({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2})=\sum_{I=1}^{\text{NB}2}{N}_{I}^{(2)}({\xi}_{1},{\xi}_{2}){p}_{I}$

One can also express the increment between two iterations of the virtual work of the follower pressure in matrix form:

$\Delta \delta {\pi}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}=-\lbrace \delta {u}^{e}\rbrace \lbrace {K}_{T}^{e}{}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}\rbrace \lbrace \Delta {u}^{e}\rbrace$

where $\lbrace {K}_{T}^{e}{}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}\rbrace$ is the contribution to the tangent stiffness matrix of the external forces, which can be expressed in the form:

$\lbrace {K}_{T}^{e}{}_{\begin{array}{}\mathrm{pression}\\ \mathrm{suiveuse}\end{array}}\rbrace ={\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}\left[N\right]p\left[{a}_{1}^{\varphi}\times \right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{2}}N\right]d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}-{\int}_{\left[-1,+1\right]\times \left[-1,+1\right]}\left[N\right]p\left[{a}_{2}^{\varphi}\times \right]\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial {\xi}_{1}}N\right]d{\xi}_{1}d{\xi}_{2}$

**Note:**

*One observes that the finite-element formulations resulting from this approach do not involve the rotational degrees of freedom. The treatment is therefore also valid for the facets of the finite elements of three-dimensional elasticity.*
