# v6.08.110 SSND110 – Validation of monocrystalline laws from Dislocation Dynamics

**Summary:**

A validation of the monocrystalline laws, either in relation to an analytical solution or with an experimental reference, is performed on a reduced point hardware problem.

For all modellings (except C), single crystal charged with a particular orientation is tested, allowing the preferred slip of a single system, at least at the beginning of the load.

For Modelling C, reference is made to experimental results cited by Julien Schwartz in his thesis, which leads to a simulation of a tensile test on a polycrystalline compound of 40 single crystals with MONO_DD_FAT behavior.

Modelling A: This Modelling validates the MONO_DD_CFC behavior

Modelling B: This Modelling validates the MONO_DD_CC behavior

Modelling C: This Modelling validates the MONO_DD_FAT behavior

Modelling D: This Modelling validates the MONO_DD_CC_IRRA behavior

Modelling E: This Modelling validates the MONO_DD_CFC_IRRA behavior.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Reference solution for Modelling A

It is based on [bib.1] and [R5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11). A analytical solution is found under the assumptions:

- $\sigma$ stress tensor is known (stress imposed on a hardware point)
- ${a}_{ij}$ interaction matrix is composed of only 1.

For each slippery system, the solved split is calculated by: ${\tau}_{s}=\sigma :{m}_{s}$

with ${m}_{s}$ the orientation tensor defined by: ${({m}_{s})}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({({n}_{s})}_{i}\cdot {({l}_{s})}_{j}+{({l}_{s})}_{i}\cdot {({n}_{s})}_{j})$ . ${n}_{s}$ designating normal to the sliding plane of the $s$ system and ${l}_{s}$ the sliding direction. The evolution of the plastic slip is given for each $s$ system by:

$\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}\frac{{\tau}_{s}}{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}$ where $\dot{{p}_{s}}=\dot{{\gamma}_{0}}({(\frac{∣{\tau}_{s}∣}{{\tau}_{f}+{\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}})}^{n}-1)$ if $∣{\tau}_{s}∣\ge {\tau}_{0}+{\tau}_{s}^{f}$ , otherwise $\dot{{p}_{s}}=0$

with ${\tau}_{s}^{\mathrm{forest}}(\omega )=\mu C(\omega )\sqrt{\sum_{j=1,12}{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }$ where ${\omega}_{s}$ is connected to ${\rho}_{s}$ dislocation density by: ${\omega}_{s}={b}^{2}\ast {\rho}_{s}$. ${\tau}_{s}$ being known, $\dot{{\gamma}_{s}}$ is therefore only function of ${\omega}_{s}$.

The evolution of ${\omega}_{s}$ is given by the differential equation: $\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\langle \omega \rangle )$ with ${h}_{s}(\omega )=(A\frac{\sum_{j\in \mathrm{forest}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }{\sum_{j=1,12}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }}+BC(\omega )\sum_{j\in \mathrm{copla}(s)}\sqrt{{a}_{\mathrm{sj}}\langle {\omega}_{j}\rangle }-\frac{y}{b}\langle {\omega}_{s}\rangle )$

$C(\omega )=0.2+0.8\frac{\ln(\alpha \sqrt{\sum_{i=1,12}\langle {\omega}_{i}\rangle })}{\ln(\alpha b\sqrt{{\rho}_{\mathrm{ref}}})}$ .

For the chosen orientation, $\text{1-5-9}$ , the Schmid factors, linking the stress tensor to the different ${\tau}_{s}$ solved splits, are , for the 12 octahedral systems of $\text{CFC}$ [R5.03.11](doc:r5.03.11) : $\begin{array}{c}[0.45784855,0.22892428,0.22892428,0.15261618,0.26707832,0.11446214,\\ \text{}0.19840104,0.29760156,0.4960026,0.04578486,0.11446214,0.16024699]\end{array}$

The first active slip system will be number 9 ($\text{A3}$ ), and the second will be number 1 ($\text{B4}$ ). The splits resolved for both systems are:

- $\text{A3}$ system (number 9): ${\tau}_{s}=49,6\mathit{MPa}$
- $\text{B4}$ system (number 1): ${\tau}_{s}=45,785\mathrm{MPa}$

For both systems, ${\tau}_{s}$ being known, it is sufficient to solve the differential equation $\dot{{\omega}_{s}}=\dot{{p}_{s}}{h}_{s}(\langle \omega \rangle )$ to know all the variables. This is done numerically, using the module "odeint" of scipy (see file SSND110A.22).
