# v2.04.302 SDLV302 – Modal substructure analysis: two-wired beam

**Summary:**

This test validates the modal analysis of a structure by the dynamic substructure method. The structure studied is a bi-supported beam deformable to the shear force.

It is modelled by elements hexahedral volumes at 20 nodes (3D Modelling).

The modal analysis is carried out by three methods:

- direct approach;
- Craig-Bampton dynamic substructure approach;
- direct approach with proportional viscous damping.

A particular interest of this test is the presence of linear relationships between several degrees of freedom.

The calculated eigenfrequencies are compared with analytically obtained values for a Timoshenko beam model deformable to shear force and taking into account the rotational inertia of the sections.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

It is permitted that bending in the $\mathit{XY}$ plane and extension along the $X$ axis. The boundary conditions model being volume-densal, the boundary conditions differ somewhat from the boundary conditions model that would be imposed on a beam model.

Movement required:

| En $X=0$ , $Y=h/2$ | $\mathit{DX}=0$ , $\mathit{DY}=0$ |
| --- | --- |
| En $X=L$ , $Y=h/2$ | $\mathit{DY}=0$ |
| En $Z=b/2$ | $\mathit{DZ}=0$ |

To the previous conditions, the planeity constraint of the $X=0$ and $X=L$ sections is added. This constraint can be expressed as follows. ${x}^{T}=(X,Y,Z)$ defines the coordinate vector and ${u}^{T}=(\mathit{DX},\mathit{DY},\mathit{DZ})$ defines the displacement vector; point positions are identified by the $x{'}^{T}={x}^{T}+{u}^{T}=(X',Y',Z')$ vector. $A$, $B$ and $C$ are three unaligned points of the section. point is required to be any $P$:

$∣\begin{array}{cccc}X{'}_{P}& Y{'}_{P}& Z{'}_{P}& 1\\ X{'}_{A}& Y{'}_{A}& Z{'}_{A}& 1\\ X{'}_{B}& Y{'}_{B}& Z{'}_{B}& 1\\ X{'}_{C}& Y{'}_{C}& Z{'}_{C}& 1\end{array}∣=0$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is obtained analytically for a Timoshenko beam, taking into account shear force deformation and rotational inertia of the sections. The theoretical aspects are developed in the reference given in 2.4 .

Set the following adimensional quantities:

${\Omega}_{n}=\frac{\rho A{L}^{4}}{\mathit{EI}}{\omega}_{n}^{2}$ own values

$j=\frac{I}{A{L}^{2}}$ Rotary Inertiality

$g=\frac{\mathit{EI}}{k'AG{L}^{2}}$ shear coefficient

The frequencies of the first bending modes are given by the following expression:

${\Omega}_{n}=\frac{(g+j){\lambda}_{n}^{2}+1-\sqrt{{(g-j)}^{2}{\lambda}_{n}^{4}+2(g+j){\lambda}_{n}^{2}+1}}{2gj}$

with

${\lambda}_{n}=n\pi$ , $n=1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$

The frequency of the extension modes is given by:

${f}_{n}=(\mathrm{2n}-1)\frac{1}{4L}\sqrt{\frac{E}{\rho}}$ , $n=1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics A

![Figure](/figures/v2.04.302/100000000000060200000295E490978F1801EEDF.png)

Figure 3.1-1:Maillage de la géométrie du problème

In this Modelling, the complete structure is meshed using 3D volume elements.

The constraint of flatness is expressed as follows.

- le point A $X=0$ , $Y=h/2$ , $Z=0$ : $\mathit{DX}=\mathit{DY}=0$ , $\mathit{DZ}\ne 0$ ;
- le point B $X=0$ , $Y=h/2$ , $Z=b$ : $\mathit{DX}=\mathit{DY}=0$ , $\mathit{DZ}\ne 0$ ;
- le point C $X=0$ , $Y=h$ , $Z=b/2$ : $\mathit{DX}\ne \mathit{DY}\ne 0$ , $\mathit{DZ}=0$

By neglecting the terms of the second order in the displacements, the flatness constraint results in a linear relationship between $\mathit{DX}=0$ displacements of $A$, $B$, $C$ and $P$ points, any point displacements of the face:

$∣\begin{array}{cccc}{\mathit{DX}}_{P}& {Y}_{P}& {Z}_{P}& 1\\ {\mathit{DX}}_{A}& h/2& 0& 1\\ {\mathit{DX}}_{B}& h/2& b& 1\\ {\mathit{DX}}_{C}& h& b/2& 1\end{array}∣=0$

The condition is written for ${\mathit{DX}}_{A}=0$:

${\mathit{DX}}_{P}={\mathit{DX}}_{C}\times (\frac{2{Y}_{P}}{h-1})$

In $X=L$ , the flatness condition is obtained in a similar way but for any ${\mathit{DX}}_{A}$. The relationship is written:

${\mathit{DX}}_{P}={\mathit{DX}}_{C}\times (\frac{2{Y}_{P}}{h-1})+2\times (1-\frac{{Y}_{P}}{h})\times {\mathit{DX}}_{A}$
