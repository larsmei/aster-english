# r7.01.50 Integration of the elastoplastic constitutive relation INTERF_POU_CINE for the 3D_INTERF_POU elements

**Summary:**

This document describes the integration of the elastoplastic constitutive relation INTERF_POU_CINE. This law concerns only the 3D_INTERF_POU elements [R7.01.49](doc:r7.01.49).

The integration method used is based on a direct implicit formulation. From the initial state, or from the previous analysis time instant, one computes the stress field resulting from a strain increment. One also computes the tangent operator.

## 1 Introduction

The 3D_INTERF_POU element represents the interaction between a reinforcement and its surrounding matrix. Their kinematics are represented there respectively by those of a 2-node beam and of an 8-node hexahedral 3D element. The interaction domain considered (the interface) is a line coincident with the beam domain. The 3D_INTERF_POU element integrates there, at its Gauss points, a phenomenological law returning the **interaction forces between the matrix and the reinforcement** as a function of their **relative displacement**, carried by three components.

This document describes the **INTERF_POU_CINE law with kinematic hardening**. The behaviour is uncoupled in the different directions in elasticity, but coupled in plasticity.

## 2 General notations and hypotheses in elastoplasticity

### 2.1 General notations

The quantities evaluated at the previous time instant $t$ are indexed ${}_{n}$. The quantities evaluated at the current time instant $t+\Delta t$ are indexed ${}_{n+1}$.

The increments are denoted by $\Delta$.

For the computation of the derivatives, one will write: $\dot{\eps}$ derivative of $\eps$ with respect to time.

| $\sig = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} \sigt & \signx & \signy \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the interface forces. |
| --- | --- |
| $\eps = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} \epst & \epsnx & \epsny \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the relative displacements. |
| $\Cel = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} k_t & k_n & k_n \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the elastic stiffnesses. |
| $k_n, k_t$ | elastic stiffnesses in the directions respectively normal and tangential to the reinforcement. |
| $\sigy = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} \sigyt & \sigyn & \sigyn \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the elastic limits. |
| $\sigyn, \sigyt$ | elastic limits in the directions respectively normal and tangential to the reinforcement. |
| $\rsig = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} \rsigt & \rsignx & \rsigny \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the recall interface forces (kinematic hardening). |
| $\ksi = \isigy \left( \sig - \rsig \right)$ |  |
| $\Gpl = \transpose{\langle \begin{matrix} g_t & g_n & g_n \end{matrix} \rangle}$ | vector of the plastic stiffnesses. |
| $g_n, g_t$ | plastic stiffnesses in the directions respectively normal and tangential to the reinforcement. |
| $\lambda$ | plastic multiplier. |
| $t$ | time. |
| $\Delta$ | increment |

### 2.2 Hypotheses of elastoplasticity

The hypotheses of *elastoplasticity* are recalled below:

- decomposition of the strain into an elastic part and a plastic part:

$$
\dot{\eps} = \dot{\eps}^{el} + \dot{\eps}^{pl}
$$

- existence of an elastic domain governed by a linear elastic relation such that:

$$
\dot{\sig} = \Cel \dot{\eps}^{el}
$$

- existence of a *yield surface* bounding this elastic domain:

$$
f (\sig, \rsig) \leq 0
$$

- existence of a *flow rule* giving the direction of plastic flow from a *plastic potential*:

$$
\dot{\eps}^{pl} = \dot{\lambda} \transpose{\frac{\partial \Phi}{\partial \sig}}
$$

- existence of a *hardening function* prescribing the evolution of the internal variables:

$$
\dot{\rsig} = - \dot{\lambda} \vector{h} \left(\sig, \rsig\right)
$$

- application of the *Kuhn-Tucker complementarity conditions*:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}
    f \le 0               \quad \quad & \quad \quad
    \dot{\lambda} \ge 0   \quad \quad & \quad \quad
    \dot{\lambda} f = 0
\end{array}
$$

## 3 General characteristics

The *yield surface* is considered elliptical in the space of the interface forces (spherical in the normalised space). It is symmetric in the two directions normal to the reinforcement (fig. r7.01.50-fig-surface).

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.50/surf_charge.png)

**Yield surface* in the normalised space of the interface forces*

| *Yield surface* | $f (\sig, \rsig) = \transpose{\ksi} \ksi - 1 = 0$ |
| --- | --- |
| *Flow rule* (associated: $\Phi = f$) | $\dot{\eps}^{pl} = \dot{\lambda} \tdfds = 2 \dot{\lambda} \isigy \ksi$ |
| *Hardening function* | $\dot{\rsig} = \Gpl \dot{\eps}^{pl} = 2 \dot{\lambda} \Gpl \isigy \ksi$ |

## 4 Integration of the local constitutive behaviour

### 4.1 Formulation of the time-continuous problem

The solution of a mechanical problem at the scale of the structure requires knowledge of the stress state at each of the Gauss points of the model. In the context of a non-linear constitutive behaviour at a Gauss point, a step of integration of this behaviour is necessary at that point.

This step consists in **updating the local variables** ($\sig$, $\rsig$, $\eps^{pl}$) **at** $\idp{t}$, from their knowledge at $\idn{t}$ and from the knowledge of $\idp{\eps}$, and consistently with the evolution equations of the variables given in the previous section r7-01-50-equations-evolution,

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    \dot{\sig}      & = \Cel \left( \dot{\eps} - \dot{\eps}^{pl} \right)  \\
    \dot{\eps}^{pl} & = 2 \dot{\lambda} \isigy \ksi                       \\
    \dot{\rsig}     & = 2 \dot{\lambda} \Gpl \isigy \ksi
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

while respecting the *Kuhn-Tucker complementarity conditions*:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    f               & \le 0  \\
    \dot{\lambda}   & \ge 0  \\
    \dot{\lambda} f & = 0
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

### 4.2 Time discretisation of the problem

**This time-continuous problem is transformed into a discrete constrained-optimisation problem** by the application of an *implicit Euler scheme*. The non-linear evolution equations thus take the form:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    \sigp       & = \Cel \left( \epsp - \epsp^{pl} \right)  \\
    \epsp^{pl}  & = \epsn^{pl} + 2 \Dlamp \isigy \idp{\ksi} \\
    \rsigp      & = \rsign + 2 \Dlamp \Gpl \isigy \idp{\ksi}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

The *Kuhn-Tucker conditions* take the discrete form:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right)          & \le 0  \\
    \Dlamp                                  & \ge 0  \\
    \Dlamp f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right)   & = 0
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

In order to be solved algorithmically, **these conditions are reformulated in the form of a two-step procedure**:

1. *elastic prediction*, which freezes the plastic flow during the time step, followed, if the *Kuhn-Tucker conditions* are violated by the *elastic prediction*, by a
2. *plastic correction*, bringing back onto the *yield surface* the point representing the stress state

The integration here is indeed carried out with respect to time $t$, but it is easier to reduce the problem to one in $\lambda$.

Let us detail, by way of example, the computation of the expression for $\epsp^{pl}$.

The application of the *implicit Euler scheme* to $\eps^{pl}$ gives:

$$
\epsp^{pl} = \epsn^{pl} + \Dtp \idp{\dot{\eps}}^{pl}
$$

where

$$
\idp{\dot{\eps}}^{pl} = \idp{\dot{\lambda}} \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}
$$

And the application of the scheme to $\lambda$ gives:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
                          & \lamp = \lamn + \Dtp \idp{\dot{\lambda}}          \\
    \Leftrightarrow \quad & \idp{\dot{\lambda}} = \frac{\lamp - \lamn}{\Dtp}  \\
    \Leftrightarrow \quad & \idp{\dot{\lambda}} = \frac{\Dlamp}{\Dtp}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

Replacing $\idp{\dot{\lambda}}$ by this last expression in the equation, we finally obtain:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
                          & \epsp^{pl} = \epsn^{pl} + \Dtp \frac{\Dlamp}{\Dtp} \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}        \\
    \Leftrightarrow \quad & \boxed{\epsp^{pl} = \epsn^{pl} + \Dlamp \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

where

$$
\idp{\left. \tdfds \right|} = 2 \isigy \idp{\ksi}
$$

### 4.3 Elastic prediction

The *elastic prediction* step **assumes the plastic flow to be frozen** during the time step. Thus, all the quantities at the time step $\idp{t}$ which relate to the plasticity state are considered equal to those of the previous time step $\idn{t}$.

$$
\begin{split}\left \lbrace
\begin{align}
    \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{trial}   & := \epsn^{pl}                                               \\
    \idp{\sig}^{trial}         & := \Cel \left( \epsp - \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{trial}   \right)   \\
    \idp{\rsig}^{trial}        & := \idn{\rsig}                                             \\
    \idp{f}^{trial}            & := f \left( \idp{\sig}^{trial}, \idp{\rsig}^{trial} \right)
\end{align}
\right.\end{split}
$$

If $\idp{f}^{trial} \le 0$, then the system is in an elastic regime during the time step and the state $\idp{}$ is fully determined:

$$
\begin{split}\left \lbrace
\begin{align}
    \epsp^{pl}    & = \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{trial}    \\
    \idp{\sig}    & = \idp{\sig}^{trial}         \\
    \idp{\rsig}   & = \idp{\rsig}^{trial}        \\
    \idp{f}       & = \idp{f}^{trial}            \\
    \idp{\Dlam}   & = 0
\end{align}
\right.\end{split}
$$

Otherwise, if $\idp{f}^{trial} \gt 0$, then the elastically predicted state violates the *Kuhn-Tucker conditions*. The system is in a plastic regime. The plastic-correction step will make it possible to bring the system back into a state compatible with the conditions. Determining the state in this regime requires the solution of the equation $\idp{f} = 0$.

### 4.4 Plastic correction

The *plastic correction* step **assumes a plastic regime**:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right)          & =   0  \\
    \Dlamp                                  & \gt 0  \\
    \Dlamp f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right)   & =   0
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

In order to solve the equation $f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right) = 0$, and thus to determine a plastic state compatible with the *Kuhn-Tucker conditions*, it is first necessary to express this equation solely from quantities known at the previous time instant $\idn{}$ and from a single scalar unknown $\Dlamp$, the plastic multiplier expressed at the current time instant $\idp{}$:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    f \left( \sigp, \rsigp \right)  & = f \left( \Dlamp \right)   \\
                                    & = \ksip^2 \cdot \vId - 1    \\
                                    & = \left( \frac{\ksip^{trial}}{\vId + \vA \Dlamp} \right)^2 \cdot \vId - 1
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

where

$$
\ksip^{trial} = \isigy \left( \idp{\sig}^{trial} - \idp{\rsig}^{trial} \right)
$$

**The equation** $f \left( \Dlamp \right) = 0$ **is non-linear. It is solved by means of a local** *Newton method*, **which requires linearising the equation about the current point** $\Dlamp$:

$$
f \left( \Dlamp^{(k+1)} \right) = f \left( \Dlamp^{(k)} \right) + \idp{\left. \frac{d f}{d \left( \Dlam \right)} \right|}^{(k)} d \left( \Dlamp \right) = 0
$$

where

$$
\begin{split}\boxed{
    \left \lbrace
    \begin{align}
        f \left( \Dlamp^{(k)} \right)      & = \left( \frac{\ksip^{trial}}{\vId + \vA \Dlamp^{(k)}} \right)^2 \cdot \vId - 1 \\
        \frac{d f}{d \left( \Dlam \right)} & = - \frac{2 \vA {\ksip^{trial}}^2}{\left( \vId + \vA \Dlamp^{(k)} \right)^3} \cdot \vId
    \end{align}
    \right.
}\end{split}
$$

**The final state at** $\idp{t}$ **is obtained from the converged state** of the local *Newton method*:

$$
\begin{split}\left \lbrace
\begin{align}
    \epsp^{pl}   & = \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}     \\
    \sigp        & = \sigp^{(k+1)}               \\
    \rsigp       & = \rsigp^{(k+1)}              \\
    \Dlamp       & = \Dlamp^{(k+1)}
\end{align}
\right.\end{split}
$$

The final stress state at $\idp{t}$ at the Gauss point is finally returned to the finite element and, from there, by assembly, to the global *Newton-Raphson* loop (at the scale of the structure). The latter also requires the computation of the tangent matrix of the constitutive behaviour for the assembly of the global tangent matrix. This computation is presented in the following part.

Let us detail the computation of $\ksip$:

Let

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    \sigp & = \Cel \left( \epsp - \eps^{pl} \right)        \\
          & = \Cel \left( \epsp - \epsn^{pl} - \left( \epsp^{pl} - \epsn^{pl} \right) \right)    \\
          & = \sigp^{trial} - \Cel \Delta \epsp^{pl}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

$$
\dot{\lambda} \dot{f} = 0
$$

and

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    \rsigp & = \rsign + \Gpl \Delta \epsp^{pl}           \\
           & = \rsigp^{trial} + \Gpl \Delta \epsp^{pl}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

substituted into

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    \ksip & = \isigy \left( \sigp - \rsigp \right)                                                                 \\
          & = \isigy \left( \sigp^{trial} - \rsigp^{trial} - \left( \Cel + \Gpl \right) \Delta \epsp^{pl} \right)  \\
          & = \ksip^{trial} - \isigy \left( \Cel + \Gpl \right) \Delta \epsp^{pl}                                  \\
          & = \ksip^{trial} - \underbrace{2 \sigy^{-2} \left( \Cel + \Gpl \right)}_{\vA} \ksip \Dlamp
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

This expression is manipulated in order finally to obtain $\ksip$:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
                          & \ksip^{trial} = \left( \vId + \vA \Dlamp \right) \ksip                   \\
    \Leftrightarrow \quad & \boxed{\ksip = \frac{\ksip^{trial}}{\vId + \vA \Dlamp}}
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

## 5 Computation of the tangent operator

One seeks the expression of the **consistent tangent matrix** (or algorithmic tangent matrix) at the converged state $\idp{}^{(k+1)}$:

$$
\idp{\Ct}^{(k+1)} = \idp{\left. \frac{d \sig}{d \eps} \right|}^{(k+1)}
$$

One recalls that

$$
\begin{split}\left \lbrace
\begin{align}
    d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}  & = d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)}  \idp{\left. \dfds \right|}^{(k+1)}   \\
    d \rsigp^{(k+1)}           & = \Gpl d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}
\end{align}
\right.\end{split}
$$

because $d \epsn^{pl} = d \rsign = 0$ (constant over the time step).

One also recalls that

$$
\sigp^{(k+1)} = \Cel \left( \epsp - \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)} \right)
$$

whence

$$
d \sigp^{(k+1)} = \Cel \left( d \epsp - d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)} \right)                                             \\
$$

The equation, through $d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}$, depends on $d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)}$, which is unknown. **The** *consistency condition* **makes it possible to determine the value of** $d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)}$. This condition follows from the *Kuhn-Tucker conditions* and is written as follows:

In plastic regime 1, this condition imposes that $d \idp{f}^{(k+1)} = 0$.

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    d f \left( \sigp^{(k+1)}, \rsigp^{(k+1)} \right)  & = \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} d \sigp^{(k+1)} + \idp{\left. \tdfdr \right|}^{(k+1)} d \rsigp^{(k+1)}   \\
                                                    & = \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \Cel \left( d \epsp - d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)} \right) + \idp{\left. \tdfdr \right|}^{(k+1)} \Gpl d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}   \\
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

By replacing $d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)}$ by its expression in the equation $\idp{f}^{(k+1)} = 0$, $d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right) }^{(k+1)}$ can be isolated:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
                            & \idp{f}^{(k+1)} = 0       \\
    \Leftrightarrow \quad   & d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)}  = \frac{ \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \Cel }{ \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \Cel \idp{\left. \dfds \right|}^{(k+1)} - \idp{\left. \tdfdr \right|}^{(k+1)} \Gpl \idp{\left. \dfdr \right|}^{(k+1)} } d \epsp
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

Knowing that $\dfdr = - \dfds$, the expression can be simplified to

$$
d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)}  = \frac{ \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \Cel }{ \idp{\left. \tdfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \left( \Cel + \Gpl \right) \idp{\left. \dfds \right|}^{(k+1)} } d \epsp
$$

By replacing $d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right) }^{(k+1)}$ by the previous expression in the equation, one obtains:

$$
\begin{split}\begin{align}
    d \sigp^{(k+1)} & = \Cel \left( d \epsp - d \idp{\eps^{pl}}^{(k+1)} \right)                                             \\
                    & = \Cel \left( d \epsp - d \idp{\left( \Dlam \right)}^{(k+1)} \idp{\left. \dfds \right|}^{(k+1)} \right)
\end{align}\end{split}
$$

And **finally the consistent tangent matrix** $\Ct$:

In the elastic regime, the tangent matrix simply corresponds to the elastic matrix.
