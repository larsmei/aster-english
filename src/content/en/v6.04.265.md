# v6.04.265 SSNV265 – GTN model: germination under deviator loading

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the integration of the porosity related to germination. A purely deviatoric loading allows the porosity due to the growth of cavities to be cancelled, which allows the display of analytical solutions. The problem is implemented at the scale of the material point (SIMU_POINT_MAT): it is directly the stress $T$ that is imposed by ensuring that it remains in a positive hardening regime.

### 1.1 Geometry

The problem is dealt with at the point hardware scale.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The [R5.03.29](doc:r5.03.29) documentation describing the equations of the model is used; the ratings introduced in the model are also adopted.

Since the stress trace is zero, the traces of the elastic, plastic and total deformations are also zero. And the Jacobian of $J$ transformation remains equal to 1. By noting $d={q}_{1}f$ and ${d}_{0}={q}_{1}{f}_{0}$ , one can express the equivalent stress of GTN:

$$
{T}_{\text{*}}=\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{1-d}
$$

In plastic conditions, the consistency condition establishes the link between the stress and the hardening variable:

$$
{T}_{\mathit{eq}}=(1-d)({R}_{0}+{R}_{H}\kappa )
$$

The elastic deformation is the same as the shear module with $\mu$:

$$
{E}^{e}=\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{2\mu }{D}^{0}
$$

The law of plastic evolution is reduced to:

$$
\dot{{E}^{p}}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\dot{\kappa}}{1-d}{D}^{0}
$$

In particular, the equivalent plastic deformation shall be:

$$
{\dot{E}}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}=\frac{\dot{\kappa}}{1-d}
$$

And finally, the evolution of porosity writes:

$$
\dot{f}=B(\kappa )\dot{\kappa}+{b}_{0}\frac{\dot{\kappa}}{1-{q}_{1}f}{\mathrm{\chi }}_{[{E}_{\mathit{eq}0}^{p};+\infty ]}({E}_{\mathit{eq}});\dot{d}={q}_{1}B(\kappa )\dot{\kappa}+{q}_{1}{b}_{0}\frac{\dot{\kappa}}{1-d}{\mathrm{\chi }}_{[{E}_{\mathit{eq}0}^{p};+\infty ]}({E}_{\mathit{eq}})
$$

In general, the above differential equation does not allow simple analytical solution. Therefore, two cases are distinguished, which will correspond to the A and B versions of the test case.

#### 2.1.1 Case A: ${b}_{0}=0$

In this case, porosity simply states:

$$
f(\kappa )={f}_{0}+\underset{0}{\overset{\kappa}{\int}}B(u)\mathit{du};d(\kappa )={d}_{0}+{q}_{1}\underset{0}{\overset{\kappa}{\int}}B(u)\mathit{du}
$$

And plastic deformation is obtained by integration of ():

$$
{E}^{p}(\kappa )=\frac{3}{2}\underset{0}{\overset{\kappa}{\int}}\frac{\mathit{du}}{1-d(u)}{D}^{0}
$$

#### 2.1.2 Case B: $B(\kappa )=0$

In this case, the differential equation () is with separable variables, easily integrating and leading to:

$$
{\left(1-d(\kappa )\right)}^{2}={\left(1-{q}_{1}{f}_{0}\right)}^{2}-2{q}_{1}{b}_{0}⟨\kappa -{\kappa}_{0}⟩;{\kappa}_{0}={E}_{\mathit{eq}0}^{p}(1-{q}_{1}{f}_{0})
$$

And plastic deformation is again obtained via ().

### 2.2 Baseline

The $\kappa$ values used to obtain the reference results in the A and B cases are chosen so as to remain in the positive hardening field of the model (otherwise, the stress control is not feasible). For the test case A, $\kappa =0.148$ is chosen; for the test case B, $\kappa =0.180$. An increasing stress is then imposed up to the ${T}_{\mathit{eq}}(\kappa )$ value according to ().

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The reference results are obtained very precisely by calculating the integrals which appear in the analytical solution using a trapezoid method, the step of which is very fine.
