# v6.04.193 SSNV193 – Contact with static macroelements

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the use of static macro-elements with unilateral contact in CONTINUE and DISCRETE formulation.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A cube on the $1m$ side is considered to be placed on a rigid plane.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.193/Shape1.gif)

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is elastic isotropic with properties of:

- $E=209000\mathit{MPa}$
- $\nu =0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

On the upper side, a vertical displacement of $-\mathrm{0.1m}$ is imposed. The rigid plane is clamped and a unilateral condition of contact between the cube and the rigid plane is imposed.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The calculation is a calculation of non-regression between different situations. The reference calculation is done without static macro-element.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The displacement on the lower side of the cube, in the middle and the contact reaction is tested. Vertical displacement is necessarily zero (initial contact, rigid plane).

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The displacement is a analytical solution.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

3D modelling is used.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh contains 1128 elements of type HEXA8.

- The first calculation is made with contact in CONTINUE formulation and without macro-element;
- The second calculation is made with contact in CONTINUE formulation and the cube is represented by a macro-element;
- The third calculation is made with contact in DISCRETE formulation and the cube is represented by a macro-element;

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

The displacement and vertical reaction of point $A$ is tested.

### 3.4 Remarks

The three calculations give exactly the same results.

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case shows the possibility of using static macro-elements to represent the rigidity of a structure subjected to unilateral contact, whatever the formulation of the latter (continuous or discreet). There are no restrictions on the conditions of use, potential contact surfaces may belong to the macro-elements. The complete model comparison/macro-elements static shows that the results are identical, both in motion and force.
