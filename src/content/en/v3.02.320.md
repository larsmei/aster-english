# v3.02.320 SSLP320 - Propagation of a X-FEM crack for the requested outlet in Mode I

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to validate the calculation of stress intensity factors (${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$) and the crack propagation path with X-FEM in 2D, within the framework of the linear elasticity.

This test involves a rectangular plate with an opening crack, and is subject to a tensile charge on the lower and upper edges of the plate.

Three methods to manage the propagation of X-FEM cracks are available, each of which is modeled.

Three modellings are considered:

- Modelling A: mesh method,
- Model B: simplex method,
- Modelling C: upwind method,
- Modelling D: geometric method,

The relevance of the results is assessed by comparing stress intensity factors with analytical values.

There is a difference between ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{I}$ theoretically less than 1.13% for the mesh method, 1.12% for the simplex method, 1.13% for the upwind method, 1.1% for the geometric method and 1.1% for the upwind & FMM method.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The 2d structure is a rectangular plate ($\mathrm{LX}=10m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=30m$ ), with an opening crack (see v3.02.320-fig-geometrie-plate-fissuree). The initial crack length is $a=5m$ .

The line in $y=0$ and the line in $y=\mathrm{Ly}$ is called "lower line".

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.320/Shape1.gif)

*Geometry of cracked plate*

The nodes rated $\mathrm{P1}$ and $\mathrm{P4}$ on the v3.02.320-fissuree-plate-geometry-fray is used to impose the boundary conditions, which is explained to the v3.02.320-conditions-limits-loads.

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus:        $E=205{10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$

Poisson's ratio:        $\nu =0$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The load is to apply a load distributed on the lower and upper lines $p=106\mathrm{Pa}$ and in the direction of normal external to the surface.

In order to block rigid modes, the nodes ${P}_{1}$ , ${P}_{2}$ , ${P}_{3}$ and ${P}_{4}$ movements are blocked as follows:

- ${\mathrm{DY}}^{\mathrm{P4}}={\mathrm{DY}}^{\mathrm{P1}}=0$ ;
- ${\mathrm{DX}}^{\mathrm{P4}}=0$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The analytical expressions of stress factors ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ are functions of distributed force $p$ , crack length a, plate width $\mathrm{Lx}$ :

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{I}=p\sqrt{\pi a}f(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})\\ {K}_{\mathrm{II}}=0\end{array}$

where the $f$ function can be determined in several different ways. We choose the one obtained by bib1, and which is true for $\begin{array}{}\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}}<0,6\end{array}$:

$\begin{array}{}f(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})=1,12-0,231(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})+10,55{(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})}^{2}-21,72{(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})}^{3}+30,39{(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})}^{4}\end{array}$

The crack is advanced by the Paris law:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\mathrm{da}}{\mathrm{dN}}=C\Delta {K}^{m}\end{array}$ where a is crack length, $C$ and $m$ are material constants, $\begin{array}{}\Delta K\end{array}$ is the difference between two consecutive FIC s and $N$ is the number of cycles.

With the numerical values of the test:

> No propagation: $0,25m$
> $\mathrm{Lx}$  : $10m$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the mesh method is tested for crack propagation. Level-sets are determined by orthogonal projection on the crack segments.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular "sain" mesh composed of $40\times 101$ QUAD4, respectively, along the $x,y$ axes. The crack is represented by a succession of SEG2, regardless of the mesh of the structure.
