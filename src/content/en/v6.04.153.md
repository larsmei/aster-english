# v6.04.153 SSNV153 - Contact pulley-corde

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a quasi-static analysis of a mechanical problem with friction-free contact. It is a rope placed on a pulley, whose inner surface is clamped, and drawn from two sides with a vertical displacement imposed.

This test, processed in 2D with elements QUAD4 is implemented to validate the smoothing of normal contact surfaces.

This test is based on 2D results proposed by Papadopolous [bib1].

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

**Modelling:** **2D_PLAN for solid elements (QUAD4)**

The pulley is meshed with a regular mesh n-using only elements QUAD4. The same number of nodes is on the inside and outside surface. For the rope the mesh is also regular and includes only elements QUAD4. There are 8 mesh cells in the rope thickness.

The contact is solved using a continuous formulation (`DEFI_CONTACT`, `FORMULATION` = `CONTINUE`), with the following choices:

- the edge of the pulley is considered as the slave interface,
- the edge of the rope is considered the master interface.

## 5 Summary of the results

In this test case, the two contact surfaces are not flat. Normal changes from one mesh cell to another. This discontinuity causes pairing problems that have a great influence on the calculation. Among these problems, we find the problem of dissymmetrization, i.e. finding results that do not respect the symmetry of the problem. For this test case, the problem is perfectly symmetrical (geometrics, boundary conditions and loading) on the $y$ axis. Without normal regularization a dissymmetry appears on the $x$ axis. Indeed, the horizontal displacements of the $A$, $B$ and $C$ points on the y axis then have nonzero values that do not respect the overall symmetry of the problem.

With normal adjustment, the solution obtained using the continuous method, respects perfectly the symmetry. It should be noted, however, that the gibi mesh, used in this test case, does not give a perfectly symmetrical mesh and that rounding problems very slightly damage the quality of the solution.

In Modelling A, the problem is dealt with with the `CONTINUE` method (use of `DEFI_CONTACT`). Integration is done at the nodes level of the mesh. Particular attention should be paid to the choice of potential contact areas in these problems of non-plane surfaces.
In model B, the problem is treated with an increased Lagrangian approach (use of `DEFI_CONT`).
