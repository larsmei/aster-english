# r5.03.04 Elasto-visco-plastic constitutive relations of Chaboche

**Summary:**

This document describes the integration of the elasto-visco-plastic constitutive model of Chaboche with non-linear kinematic and isotropic hardening, with possible taking into account of viscosity. The implemented model has one or two kinematic variables, and takes into account all the variations of the coefficients with temperature, and has a hardening effect on the tensorial backstress variables. This version also makes it possible to model (in an optional manner) the viscous character of the material (Norton viscosity). It is integrated by the solution of a single non-linear scalar equation. This model is available in 3D, plane strain and axisymmetry. The plane-stress modelling uses a method of static condensation (de Borst). One also gives elements for identifying the coefficients of the constitutive relation.

## 1 Elasto-visco-plastic models of Chaboche available in Code_Aster

For the analysis of structures subjected to cyclic loadings, the classical isotropic (linear or not) and linear kinematic hardenings [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) and [R5.03.16](doc:r5.03.16) are no longer sufficient. In particular, one cannot correctly describe the stabilised cycles obtained experimentally on a tensile specimen subjected to an imposed alternating strain or to a tension–compression.

If one seeks to describe precisely the effects of a cyclic loading, it is desirable to adopt more sophisticated modellings (but simple to use) such as the model of Saïd Taheri, for example, cf. [R5.03.05](doc:r5.03.05), or else, if the number of cycles is limited, the model of Jean-Louis Chaboche which is presented here.

In reality, the Chaboche model can be more or less sophisticated. The models developed in *Code_Aster*  comprise either one kinematic variable (VMIS_CIN1_CHAB and VISC_CIN1_CHAB) or two (VISC_CIN2_CHAB and VMIS_CIN2_CHAB), and isotropic hardening.

The choice of using two kinematic variables certainly complicates the model, but makes it possible to identify correctly the uniaxial tests over a wider range of strains [bib2], [bib7]. A certain number of identifications of the parameters of this model have been carried out mainly for the stainless steels A316 and A304 ([bib7], [bib8]).

The models comprise 8 parameters (a single kinematic variable) or 10 (two kinematic variables), introduced in the command DEFI_MATERIAU:

```text

♦ R_0 =        R_0,
◊ R_I =        R_I,    (inutile si B=0)

◊ W =        w ,    (défaut: 0.)

♦ G_0 =        G_0,
◊ A_I =        A_I,    (défaut: 0.)
)

CIN2_CHAB (CIN2_CHAB_FO)                    = _F(
♦ R_0 =    R_0,
◊ R_I =    R_I,

inutile si B=0 ou si effet de mémoire)
◊     B =            b ,    (défaut: 0.)

♦ C1_I =        C1_I,
♦ C2_I =        C2_I,
```

The 8 or 10 parameters are real constants. All these parameters may depend on the temperature (keywords CIN1_CHAB_FO or CIN2_CHAB_FO) and the expected values are of function type.

In the case where one wishes to introduce viscosity in addition (models VISC_CIN1_CHAB and VISC_CIN2_CHAB), one must also supply in the command DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keyword LEMAITRE (or LEMAITRE_FO) the parameters N and UN_SUR_K, which may depend on the temperature.

```text

♦ N =        n,
♦ UN_SUR_K =    1/K
```

The parameter UN_SUR_M of the keyword LEMAITRE (respectively LEMAITRE_FO) must obligatorily be set to zero (respectively to the identically zero function).

It is also possible to take into account a memory effect of the largest plastic strain by means of the models ( VISC_CIN2_MEMO and VMIS_CIN2_MEMO). The keywords to be filled in are:

```text

♦ Q_M =        Qm,
♦ Q_0 =        Q0,
♦ MU =        mu,
♦ ETA =        eta, (défaut: 0.5)
```

In the case of a non-proportional loading, it is necessary to enrich the model, by the data of two additional parameters:

```text
CIN2_NRAD = _F(

◊ DELTA1=:math:`{\delta}_{1}`    (défaut= 1.E+0),

◊ DELTA2=:math:`{\delta}_{2}`    (défaut= 1.E+0),

```

The constitutive laws are accessible in all the commands using the keyword COMPORTEMENT with the following relations:

VISC_CIN1_CHAB, VISC_CIN2_CHAB, VISC_CIN2_MEMO, VISC_CIN2_NRAD, VISC_MEMO_NRAD,VMIS_CIN1_CHAB, VMIS_CIN2_CHAB, VMIS_CIN2_MEMO, VMIS_CIN2_NRAD, VMIS_MEMO_NRAD.

**Note**  : the model VISCOCHAB [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14) also makes it possible to represent the effects described in this document. It furthermore comprises additional recovery and hardening terms. But its use in structural analyses is more costly in computation time (because one must solve, either by the Runge–Kutta method or by the Newton method, a system of 27 equations in 27 unknowns). Moreover, it poses robustness problems when the time step is large, because the Newton method may fail. This leads to numerous subdivisions of the time step.

The models described in this document are optimised, insofar as they lead to solving a single scalar equation, and the solution method used is very robust (method of Brent or secant, cf. [R5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14)); it is therefore a model capable of integrating large time steps rapidly.

In the remainder of this document, one describes the characteristics of the various models. One then presents the detail of their numerical integration in connection with the construction of the consistent tangent matrix. Finally, one also gives a few elements for the identification of the characteristics of the material.

## 2 Description of the models

### 2.1 Description of the models

At every time instant, the state of the material is described by the strain $\varepsilon$ , the temperature $T$ , the plastic strain ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ , the cumulative plastic strain $p$ and the backstress tensor $X$ . The state equations then define, as a function of these state variables, the stress $\sigma ={\sigma}^{H}\text{Id}+\tilde{\sigma}$ (decomposed into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts), the isotropic part of the hardening $R$ and the kinematic part $X$ :

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\sigma )=K\text{tr}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$ with ${\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}=\alpha ({\text{T-T}}^{\text{ref}})\text{Id}$         eq 2.1-1

$\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma -{\sigma}^{H}\text{Id}=2\mu (\tilde{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}^{p})$                                     eq 2.1-2

$R=R(p)$                                                         eq 2.1-3

$X=X(p,{\varepsilon}^{p})={X}_{1}(p,{\varepsilon}^{p})+{X}_{2}(p,{\varepsilon}^{p})$                             eq 2.1-4

where $K,\mu ,\alpha$ and the coefficients of $X(p)$ and $R(p)$ are characteristics of the material which may depend on the temperature. More precisely, they are respectively the bulk and shear moduli, the coefficient of thermal expansion, and the isotropic and kinematic hardening functions. As for ${T}^{\text{réf}}$ , it is the reference temperature, for which one considers the thermal strain as being zero.

**Note:**

*For the model* *VISC_CIN1_CHAB one considers only the single tensorial variable* ${X}_{1}(p)$ *hence* ${X}_{2}(p)=0$ *. This remains valid for all that follows: one will formally describe the two models in the same way, the model* *VISC_CIN1_CHAB being deduced from* *VISC_CIN2_CHAB by assuming* ${X}_{2}(p)=0$ *.*

The evolution of the plastic strain is governed by a flow rule normal to a von Mises plasticity criterion:

$F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)$ with ${A}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}:\tilde{A}}$             eq 2.1-5

> ${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\dot{\lambda}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2}}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}}$                                     eq 2.1-6

$\dot{p}=\dot{\lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}:{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}}$                                                 eq 2.1-7

As for the plastic multiplier $\dot{\lambda}$ , it is obtained by the consistency condition:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}\text{si}F<0\text{ou}\stackrel{}{\mathrm{\dot{}}}F<0& \dot{\lambda}=0\\ \text{si}F=0\text{et}\stackrel{}{\mathrm{\dot{}}}F=0& \dot{\lambda}\ge 0\end{array}$                                     eq 2.1-8

**Note:**

*The evolution of the variables* ${\mathrm{X}}_{1}$ *and* ${\mathrm{X}}_{2}$ *is given by:*

$\begin{array}{c}{X}_{1}=\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}(p){\alpha}_{1}\\ {X}_{2}=\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}(p){\alpha}_{2}\\ {\dot{\alpha}}_{1}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}(p){\alpha}_{1}\dot{p}\\ {\dot{\alpha}}_{2}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{2}(p){\alpha}_{2}\dot{p}\end{array}$                                     eq 2.1-9

The functions $C(p)$ , $\gamma (p)$ and $R(p)$ are defined, in accordance with [bib2] by:

$\begin{array}{c}R(p)={R}_{\infty}+({R}_{0}-{R}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}}\\ {C}_{1}(p)={C}_{1}^{\infty}(1+(k-1){e}^{-\text{wp}})\\ {C}_{2}(p)={C}_{2}^{\infty}(1+(k-1){e}^{-\text{wp}})\end{array}$

$\begin{array}{c}{\gamma}_{1}(p)={\gamma}_{1}^{0}({a}_{\infty}+(1-{a}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}})\\ {\gamma}_{2}(p)={\gamma}_{2}^{0}({a}_{\infty}+(1-{a}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}})\end{array}$

The evolution of these coefficients makes it possible to represent hardening in several ways: classical isotropic hardening (monotonic or cyclic) by $R(p)$ , “hardening” of the coefficients relative to the kinematic terms by $C(p)$ and $\gamma (p)$ . (cf. [bib 11 ]). The exponential expressions are similar to the definition of the non-linear kinematic hardening (eq.2,1,9), and (in their principle) represent a variation of the coefficients from the value indexed by $0$ (for $p=0$ ) up to the value indexed by $\infty$  when $p$ becomes large.

This implies that the coefficients $b$ and $w$ are assumed positive. In the opposite case, an alarm message is issued, because the computed solution risks being non-physical.

The presence of viscosity can be modelled in a simple way [bib2] by replacing the consistency condition [eq 2.1-8] by:

$\dot{p}={(\frac{\langle F\rangle }{K})}^{N}$                                         eq 2.1-10

$\langle F\rangle ` partie positive de :math:`F$ (Macaulay brackets), $K,N$ viscosity characteristics (Norton) of the material. One leaves unchanged all the other equations of the model. One will see that such an introduction of the viscosity entails only minor modifications of the implicit integration algorithm of the constitutive law.

The memory effect consists in replacing the evolution of the isotropic hardening by:

$F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}-{R}_{0}-R(p)$

$\dot{R}=b(Q-R)\dot{p}$

$Q=Q{}_{0}\text{}+({Q}_{m}-{Q}_{0})(1-{e}^{-2\mu q})$

$f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )-q\le 0$ defining a domain characterising the maximum plastic strains, of which $q$ measures the radius and $\xi$ the centre, computed according to a normality law that is to say with the evolution law: $\dot{\xi}=\frac{1-\eta }{\eta}\dot{q}{n}^{\text{*}}$ . The parameter $\eta$ (which does not exist in the initial formulation [bib.2]), makes it possible to take the memory effect partially into account. If it is equal to 0.5, one recovers the initial formulation. If it is equal to 1, $q$ is equal to the norm of the largest plastic strain reached. If it is much smaller than 0.5, the memory effect is taken into account only in part.

**Notes:**

- *The definition of* ${\mathrm{X}}_{1}$ *and* ${\mathrm{X}}_{2}$ in the form [eq 2.1-9]:
- *makes it possible to keep a formulation which takes into account the variations of the parameters with temperature without introducing a term in* $\dot{T}$ as in [bib.4], in the same way as the viscoplastic Chaboche model. These terms are necessary because not taking them into account would lead to inaccurate results [bib4].
- makes it possible to have a writing consistent with the thermodynamic expression of the plastic potential [bib2] (p.221).
- *One notes that the functions* ${C}_{1}(p),{\gamma}_{1}(p),{C}_{2}(p),{\gamma}_{2}(p),R(p)$ *appearing in the preceding equations all three make it possible to model various non-* *linear hardening effects. The introduction of hardening, either at the level of the kinematic part, by* $C(p)$ *, or at the level of the recall term, by the function* $\gamma (p)$ , does not have the same effect on the identification tests [bib2]. The use of a model with $\gamma (p)$ *makes it possible in particular to identify more easily strong cyclic hardenings. Several works of identification of the coefficients of the Chaboche models have moreover been carried out on the basis of the model with a hardening represented by* $\gamma (p)$ ([bib5], [bib6]), in particular for stainless steels.

### 2.3 Insertion of the effect of non-proportionality of the loading

In a manner similar to the model VISCOCHAB, one can insert into VISC_CIN2_CHAB/MEMO the equations expressing the non-proportional effect. The model obtained is named here VISC/VMIS_CIN2_NRAD, or VISC/VMIS_MEMO_NRAD (according as one takes into account or not the memory effect).

$\begin{array}{c}\dot{{\alpha}_{1}}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}(p){\alpha}_{1}\dot{p}\\ {\dot{\alpha}}_{2}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{2}(p){\alpha}_{2}\dot{p}\end{array}$ becomes: $\begin{array}{c}\dot{{\alpha}_{1}}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}(p)({\delta}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}+(1-{\delta}_{1})({\alpha}_{1}:n)n)\dot{p}\\ {\dot{\alpha}}_{2}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{2}(p)({\delta}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}+(1-{\delta}_{2})({\alpha}_{2}:n)n)\dot{p}\end{array}$

with $n=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2}}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2})}_{\mathit{eq}}}$ hence $n:n=1$ and in particular $\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta pn$

It is easy to check that this new expression of the evolution of the internal variables ${\alpha}_{i}$ reduces to the preceding expression in the case where ${\delta}_{i}=1$ , or else in the case of a **radial situation**  , where one can set ${\alpha}_{i}=\xi n$ .

One then has: $\dot{{\alpha}_{i}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}-{\gamma}_{i}\dot{p}({\delta}_{i}\xi n+(1-{\delta}_{i})\xi n)=\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}-{\gamma}_{i}\dot{p}{\alpha}_{i}$ .

## 3 Integration of the constitutive relations

In order to carry out numerically the integration of the constitutive law, one performs a discretisation in time and one adopts an implicit Euler scheme, reputed appropriate for elastoplastic constitutive relations. Henceforth, one will use the following notations: ${A}^{-}$ , $A$ and $\Delta A$ represent respectively the values of a quantity at the beginning and at the end of the time step considered as well as its increment during the step. The problem is then the following: knowing the state at time ${t}^{-}$ as well as the increments of strain $\Delta \varepsilon$ (coming from the prediction phase (cf. reference documentation of STAT_NON_LINE [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01))) and of temperature $\Delta T$ , determine the state of the internal variables at time $t$ as well as the stresses $\sigma$ .

One takes into account the variations of the characteristics with respect to temperature by noting that:

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{\sigma}^{{H}^{-}}+K\text{tr}(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$                         eq 2.2-1

$\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu (\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})={\tilde{\sigma}}^{\varepsilon}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$                     eq 2.2-2

with

${\tilde{\sigma}}^{\varepsilon}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}$

In view of equation [eq 2.2-1], one notes that the hydrostatic behaviour is purely elastic if $K$ is constant. Only the treatment of the deviatoric component is delicate.

In the absence of a viscous term, the discretised consistency relation is:

Elastic regime: $F\le 0$ and $\Delta p=0$

Plastic regime: $F=0$ and $\Delta p\ge 0$

On the other hand, in the presence of viscosity, the consistency condition is replaced by equation [eq2.1‑10] which, discretised, is written:

$\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}={(\frac{\langle F\rangle }{K})}^{N}\mathrm{\iff }\langle F\rangle =K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$

In other words, by setting:

$\tilde{F}=F-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$

the increment of cumulative viscoplastic strain is determined by:

$\begin{array}{cc}\text{Régime élastique :}& \tilde{F}\le 0\text{et}\Delta p=0\\ \text{Régime viscoplastique :}& \tilde{F}=0\text{et}\Delta p\ge 0\end{array}$                                     eq 2.2-3

Finally, by adopting an implicit discretisation, the only difference between the plastic and viscoplastic constitutive laws lies in the form of the yield function $F$ : one observes in it an additional term in the case of viscosity. In fact, incremental plasticity appears as the limiting case of incremental viscoplasticity when $K$ tends towards zero. This convergence has already been described by J.L.Chaboche and G. Cailletaud in [bib3].

In the remainder of this paragraph, one will therefore detail the integration of the viscoplastic law. In order to recover the case of plastic behaviour, it suffices to take $K=0$ in the equations below (one recalls that the user, in order to place himself in this case, must obligatorily remove the keyword LEMAITRE or LEMAITRE_FO from the command DEFI_MATERIAU).

$\tilde{\sigma}-{X}_{1}-{X}_{2}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}({C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}+{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})$

The flow equations [eq 2.1-6] and [eq 2.1-7], once discretised, and the consistency condition [eq 2.2-3] are written (noting that $p=\lambda$ ):

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\Delta p\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}}{{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}}$         eq 2.2-4

$\tilde{F}\le 0\Delta p\ge 0\tilde{F}\Delta p=0$                                 eq 2.2-5

The treatment of the consistency condition (preceding equation) is classical. One begins with an elastic trial ($\Delta p=0$ ) which is indeed the solution if the plasticity criterion is not exceeded, that is if:

${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}({p}^{-}){\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}({p}^{-}){\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\text{eq}}-R({p}^{-})<0$                     eq 2.2-6

In the opposite case, the solution is plastic ($\Delta p>0$ ) and the consistency condition reduces to $\tilde{F}=0$ . In order to solve it, one shows that one can reduce it to a scalar problem by expressing $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ and $\Delta {\alpha}_{1},\Delta {\alpha}_{2}$ as a function of $\Delta p$ . By gathering the equations of the problem coming from the implicit discretisation, one obtains the system of equations:

${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{{e}_{}}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}=R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$         eq 2.2-7

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\Delta p\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}^{{e}_{}}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}}{R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}}$                 eq 2.2-8

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}\Delta p\\ \Delta {\alpha}_{2}=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}\Delta p\end{array}$                         eq 2.2-9

In this writing, one must note well that $p={p}^{-}+\Delta p$ and ${\alpha}_{i}={\alpha}_{{i}^{-}}+\Delta {\alpha}_{i}$ and that ${C}_{i},{\gamma}_{i}$ are functions of $p$ . By considering the last three equations, this linear system in $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ and $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}$ can be solved in order to express these quantities as a function of $\Delta p$ . Indeed, it is equivalent to:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}(R(p)+3\mu \Delta p+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N})=\Delta p(\frac{3}{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})$     eq 2.2-10

$\begin{array}{c}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}(1+{\gamma}_{1}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}\Delta p\\ \Delta {\alpha}_{2}(1+{\gamma}_{2}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}\mathit{Dp}\end{array}$                     eq 2.2-11

By computing ${C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}$ and by replacing them in the expression of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ one obtains an expression of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ as a function of $\Delta p$ only:

$\begin{array}{c}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}=(\frac{{C}_{1}}{1+{\gamma}_{1}\Delta p})\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-(\frac{{C}_{1}{\gamma}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}\Delta p}{1+{\gamma}_{1}\Delta p})={M}_{1}(p)\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{M}_{1}(p){\gamma}_{1}\Delta p{\alpha}_{1}^{-}\\ {C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}=(\frac{{C}_{2}}{1+{\gamma}_{2}\Delta p})\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-(\frac{{C}_{2}{\gamma}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}\Delta p}{1+{\gamma}_{2}\Delta p})={M}_{2}(p)\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{M}_{2}(p){\gamma}_{2}\Delta p{\alpha}_{2}^{-}\\ \text{avec}{M}_{i}(p)=\frac{{C}_{i}(p)}{1+{\gamma}_{i}(p)\Delta p}\end{array}$         eq 2.2-12

By carrying this expression over into the expression of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ one finds:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{1}{(R(p)+(3\mu +{M}_{1}+{M}_{2})\Delta p+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N})}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\Delta p(({C}_{1}-{M}_{1}{\gamma}_{1}\Delta p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+({C}_{2}-{M}_{2}{\gamma}_{2}\Delta p){\alpha}_{2}^{-}))$

which simplifies to:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{1}{D(p)}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\Delta p({M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}))$                     eq 2.2-13

with:

$D(p)=R(p)+(3\mu +{M}_{1}(p)+{M}_{2}(p))\Delta p+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$

It now only remains to replace $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ in the expressions of ${C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}$ in order to express this term as a function of $\Delta p$ by:

$\begin{array}{c}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}=\frac{{M}_{1}}{D}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\Delta p({M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}))-{M}_{1}{\gamma}_{1}\Delta p{\alpha}_{1}^{-}\\ {C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2}=\frac{{M}_{2}}{D}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\Delta p({M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}))-{M}_{2}{\gamma}_{2}\Delta p{\alpha}_{2}^{-}\end{array}$

then to substitute the expression thus obtained as well as $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ as a function of $\Delta p$ into the equation $\tilde{F}=0$ , and one obtains a scalar equation in $\Delta p$ to be solved, namely:

$\tilde{F}(p)={({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}=0$

which simplifies to:

$\tilde{F}(p)=\frac{R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}}{D(p)}{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}=0$         eq 2.2-14

This scalar equation in $\Delta p$ is solved numerically, by a method of searching for a zero of a function (secant method which one describes briefly in appendix 2).

It is normalised in the following way:

$\tilde{\hat{F}}(p)=1-\frac{D(p)}{{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\text{eq}}}=0$                                     eq 2.2-15

Once $\Delta p$ has been determined, one can compute $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ by means of equation [eq 2.2-13] then $\Delta {\alpha}_{1}$ and $\Delta {\alpha}_{2}$ by means of equations [eq 2.2-11]. It only remains to compute the stress tensor, by equations [eq 2.2-1] and [eq2.2-2], and to update the internal variables ${\alpha}_{1}$ and ${\alpha}_{2}$ .

### 3.1 Integration of the terms taking non-radiality into account

The discretisation leads to: ${\Delta \alpha }_{i}={\Delta \varepsilon }^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p\left[{\delta}_{i}({\alpha}_{i}^{-}+\Delta {\alpha}_{i})+(1-{\delta}_{i})(({\alpha}_{i}^{-}+\Delta {\alpha}_{i}):n)n\right]$

Let us compute $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta p-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p({\delta}_{i}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{\beta}_{i}+{\delta}_{i}\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n+(1-{\delta}_{i})\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{\beta}_{i}+(1-{\delta}_{i})(\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n))$

having set ${\alpha}_{i}^{-}:n=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{\beta}_{i}$ . One can therefore express $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n$ as a function of $\Delta p$ and ${\beta}_{i}$

$\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n(1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p)=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta p(1-{\gamma}_{i}{\beta}_{i})$ that is $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n=\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta p(1-{\gamma}_{i}{\beta}_{i})}{(1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p)}$

One can therefore express $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}$ solely as a function of $\Delta p$ and ${\beta}_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}:n$ and propagate these modifications into the solution method used previously:

$\Delta {\alpha}_{i}(1+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p(1-{\delta}_{i})({\alpha}_{i}^{-}:n)n-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p(1-{\delta}_{i})(\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n)n$

By using the expression of $\Delta {\alpha}_{i}:n$ as a function of $\Delta p$ and ${\beta}_{i}$ ,

$\Delta {\alpha}_{i}(1+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p(1-{\delta}_{i})\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}{\beta}_{i}n-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p(1-{\delta}_{i})\frac{\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta p(1-{\gamma}_{i}{\beta}_{i})}{(1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p)}n$

$\Delta {\alpha}_{i}(1+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}-{\gamma}_{i}(1-{\delta}_{i})\frac{{\beta}_{i}+\Delta p}{1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$

$\Delta {\alpha}_{i}(1+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p)=\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}{N}_{i}(\Delta p,{\beta}_{i})-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}$ with

${N}_{i}(\Delta p,{\beta}_{i})=\frac{1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}-{\gamma}_{i}(1-{\delta}_{i}){\beta}_{i}}{1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p}$

Here again, one can check that if ${\delta}_{i}=1$ , one recovers the equations without the non-radiality effect.

In order to continue to solve, one must compute:

${C}_{i}\Delta {\alpha}_{i}={M}_{i}{N}_{i}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p{\delta}_{i}{M}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}$ with ${M}_{i}=\frac{{C}_{i}}{(1+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p)}$

So that the computation of the plastic-strain increment is similar to the classical case:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\Delta pn$ with $n=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2})}_{\mathit{eq}}}$ .

By using the expressions computed previously as well as the expression of the criterion:

${({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}-2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}\Delta {\alpha}_{1}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}\Delta {\alpha}_{2})}_{\text{eq}}=R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$ one has:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}(R(p)+3K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}+\Delta p(3\mu +{M}_{1}{N}_{1}+{M}_{2}{N}_{2}))=\frac{3}{2}\Delta p({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})$

hence $n=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}}{D}$ with

$D(\Delta p;{\beta}_{1};{\beta}_{2})=R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{\text{1/N}}+\Delta p(3\mu +{M}_{1}{N}_{1}+{M}_{2}{N}_{2})$

**Note** : here again, one can check that in the case where one does not take into account the non-radial effect, ${\delta}_{i}=1$ , which entails $N=1$ . One indeed recovers the classical expression of the normal $n$ .

In the present case; there are 3 scalar unknowns: $\Delta p$ , ${\beta}_{1}$ , ${\beta}_{2}$ . In fact, it is possible to express ${\beta}_{1}$ and ${\beta}_{2}$ as a function of $\Delta p$ by noting that:

$n=\sqrt{(\frac{3}{2})}\frac{\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}}{{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\mathit{eq}}}$ . One can therefore determine $n$ as a function of $\Delta p$ only, then compute directly ${\beta}_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}{\alpha}_{i}^{-}:n$ , which then become explicit functions of $\Delta p$ . In order to solve, it suffices to replace the expressions above in the criterion (which amounts to writing $n:n=1$ ):

$\tilde{\hat{F}}(p)={(\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\mathit{eq}}-D(\Delta p;{\beta}_{1}(\Delta p);{\beta}_{2}(\Delta p))=0$

### 3.2 Integration of the memory effect

In the case of the memory effect, the function $R(p)$ is no longer known explicitly, but through the system of equations:

1 eq: $f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )-q=\frac{2}{3}\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi ):({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}-q\le 0$

6 eq: $\Delta \xi =(1-\eta )H(F)\langle {\text{n:n}}^{\text{*}}\rangle \Delta p{n}^{\text{*}}=(1-\eta )\frac{\Delta q}{\eta}{n}^{\text{*}}$

With

$\Delta R=b(Q-R)\Delta p$ $Q=Q{}_{0}\text{}+({Q}_{m}-{Q}_{0})(1-{e}^{-2\mu ({q}^{-}+\Delta q)})$ ${n}^{\text{*}}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi }{{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}$

Knowing $\Delta p$ , one begins by computing $f({\varepsilon}^{p},{\xi}^{-},{q}^{-})$ .

If this quantity is negative, then the solution of the system governing the memory effect is: $\mathrm{\Delta q}=0,\mathrm{\Delta \xi }=0$ .

In the opposite case, knowing $\Delta p$ , one must find $\Delta q$ and $\Delta \xi$ such that:

$f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-}-\Delta \xi )-{q}^{-}-\Delta q=0$

$\Delta \xi =\frac{(1-\eta )}{\eta}\Delta q{n}^{\text{*}}=\frac{(1-\eta )}{\eta}\Delta q\frac{3}{2}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p-}+\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-}-\Delta \xi }{\frac{3}{2}({q}^{-}+\Delta q)}$

*Because* $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{p}}=\frac{1}{D(p)}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\Delta p({M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}))$ can be computed explicitly from $\Delta p$ .

There remains:

$\Delta \xi (1+\frac{(1-\eta )\Delta q}{\eta ({q}^{-}+\Delta q)})=\frac{(1-\eta )}{\eta}\Delta q\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p-}+\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-}}{({q}^{-}+\Delta q)}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }$ $\Delta \xi (\eta {q}^{-}+\Delta q)=(1-\eta )\Delta q({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\Delta \xi =\frac{(1-\eta )\Delta q({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})}{\eta {q}^{-}+\Delta q}$

by carrying over into the equation of the threshold surface: $f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=0$

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-}-\Delta \xi )-{q}^{-}-\Delta q=0=\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})\mid (1-\frac{(1-\eta )\Delta q}{\eta {q}^{-}+\Delta q})\mid -{q}^{-}-\Delta q=0\\ \mathrm{\iff }\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})\mid \eta ({q}^{-}+\Delta q)\mid -({q}^{-}+\Delta q)({\mathit{\eta q}}^{-}+\Delta q)=0\text{si}\eta {q}^{-}+\Delta q>0\end{array}$

which makes it possible to compute $\Delta q$ explicitly from $\Delta p$ :

$\Delta q=\eta \frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{p}}-{\xi}^{-})-\eta {q}^{-}$

It then remains to modify the isotropic-hardening function by computing:

$Q=Q{}_{0}\text{}+({Q}_{m}-{Q}_{0})(1-{e}^{-2\mu ({q}^{-}+\mathit{\Delta q})})$ then $\Delta R=b(Q-R)\Delta p$

One can therefore use the solution of the scalar equation in $\Delta p$ ( eq 2.2-14) by using the expressions above.

**Notes:**

- In [bib2] one also finds the expression *:* $\text{dq}=\eta H(f)\langle {\text{n:n}}^{\text{*}}\rangle \text{dp}$ *.*

This last equation results from the expression in rate of the multiplier. In the implicit discretisation carried out here, it is not used for the solution (since then the system would comprise more equations than unknowns). Moreover, the 3 equations given in [bib2] are redundant: indeed, knowing $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ one must determine a tensorial variable $\Delta \xi$ and a scalar variable . $\Delta q$  Now we have a tensorial equation and two scalar equations.

This is due to the fact that the equation $\text{dq}=\eta H(f)\langle {\text{n:n}}^{\text{*}}\rangle \text{dp}$ comes from the consistency condition $\mathit{df}=0$ (which is specified in [bib2]) but is not used for the implicit solution of the problem. $\text{df}({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi }{{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}d{\varepsilon}^{p}-\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi }{{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}d\xi -\text{dq}=n:{n}^{\text{*}}\text{dp}-{n}^{\text{*}}:{n}^{\text{*}}\text{dq}-\text{dq}=n:{n}^{\text{*}}\text{dp}-2\text{dq}=0$

It would be useful for an explicit solution, by expressing the derivatives with respect to time of all the unknowns sought.

- an interesting criterion, given in [bib2], makes it possible to adjust the parameters of the memory effect . Indeed, by considering a simple tension–compression loading, one must find $q=\frac{1}{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{{p}_{\max}}$ (by choosing $\eta =\frac{1}{2}$ ). For a material point under uniaxial loading, the (uniform) fields have as components: $\sigma =\sigma (\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})$ ${\varepsilon}^{p}=p(\begin{array}{ccc}1& 0& 0\\ 0& -\frac{1}{2}& 0\\ 0& 0& -\frac{1}{2}\end{array})$ In this case, during the first uniaxial loading in the direction $x$ : $\begin{array}{c}{\xi}^{-}=0\\ {q}^{-}=0\\ \Delta q=\eta {\varepsilon}_{x}^{p}\end{array}$ In this case, $q=\frac{1}{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{{p}_{\max}}$ , implies that $\eta =\frac{1}{2}$  and $\Delta \xi =\frac{1}{2}({\varepsilon}^{p})$ Moreover, in the case of a symmetrical tension–compression cycle (in plastic strain), one obtains, during the first symmetrical unloading (with $\eta =\frac{1}{2}$ ): $\begin{array}{c}{\xi}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}^{{p}_{\max}}\\ {q}^{-}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{{p}_{\max}}\\ \Delta q=\eta (\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p})-{q}^{-})=\eta (\mid {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{{p}_{\min}}-{\xi}^{-}\mid -\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{{p}_{\max}})=\frac{1}{2}\mid {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{{p}_{\min}}\mid \end{array}$ $q={q}^{-}+\Delta q={\varepsilon}_{xx\max}^{p}=\frac{1}{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p}$             $\Delta \xi =\frac{(1-\eta )\Delta q({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})}{\eta {q}^{-}+\Delta q}=-\frac{1}{2}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{xx\max}^{p}$ $\xi ={\xi}^{0}+\Delta \xi =0$ which does indeed correspond to the expected result (cf. [bib2]): domain $F=0$ centred on the origin, and of radius the half-amplitude of plastic strain.

### 3.3 Computation of the tangent stiffness

In order to allow a solution of the global problem (equilibrium equations) by a Newton method [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01), it is necessary to determine the consistent tangent matrix of the incremental problem.

This matrix is classically composed of an elastic contribution and of a plastic contribution:

$\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }-2\mu \frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \varepsilon }$                             eq 2.3-1

with ${\sigma}^{e}=\sigma +2\mu \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ , which gives back in particular ${\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}$

One immediately deduces that in the elastic regime (classical or pseudo-unloading), the tangent matrix reduces to the elastic matrix:

$\frac{\delta \sigma }{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }$                                         eq 2.3-2

For this, one once more adopts the convention of writing symmetric tensors of order 2 in the form of vectors with 6 components. Thus, for a tensor $a$ :

$a={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{a}_{xx}& {a}_{yy}& {a}_{zz}\end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}\sqrt{2}{a}_{xy}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{xz}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{yz}\end{array}\right]$                     eq 2.3-3

If one furthermore introduces the hydrostatic vector $1$ and the deviatoric projection matrix $P$ :

$1={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}1& 1& 1\end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$                                             eq 2.3-4

$P=\text{Id}-\frac{1}{3}1\otimes 1$                                                 eq 2.3-5

where $\otimes$ is the tensor product

Then the consistent tangent stiffness matrix is written for an elastic behaviour:

$\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{e}}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=K1\otimes 1+2\mu P$                                             eq 2.3-6

On the other hand, in the plastic regime, the variation of the plastic strain is no longer zero.

One differentiates with respect to $\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}$ , knowing that one has:

$\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \varepsilon }=\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}.\frac{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}{\delta \varepsilon }=2\mu \frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}.P$                     eq 2.3-7

| $s$ | space of symmetric tensors |
| --- | --- |
| $P$ | projector onto the deviators |

In order to compute $\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$ , one uses the expression of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ as a function of ${\tilde{\sigma}}_{e}$ and $p$ :

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{1}{D(p)}(\frac{3}{2}\Delta p{\tilde{\sigma}}_{e}-\Delta p({M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}))$

which is written in the form:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=A(p){\tilde{\sigma}}_{e}+{B}_{1}(p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{B}_{2}(p){\alpha}_{2}^{-}$

Therefore:

$\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=A(p)\text{Id}+{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}\otimes \frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}+\frac{\delta {B}_{1}(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\otimes {\alpha}_{1}^{-}+\frac{\delta {B}_{2}(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\otimes {\alpha}_{2}^{-}$

The quantities of the type $\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$ are computed by means of: $\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta p}\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$

Finally, it only remains to compute the variation of $p$ : $\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$

One uses for this: $\tilde{F}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})=0$

$\tilde{F}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}_{e})=\frac{R(p)+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}}{D(p)}{({\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{M}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}=0$

${\tilde{F}}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})\delta p=-{\tilde{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=-\frac{{\tilde{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}{{\tilde{F}}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}$         eq 2.3-8

The detail of the computations is given in appendix 1.

The initial tangent matrix, used by the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, is obtained by adopting the behaviour of the previous step (elastic or plastic, indicated by an internal variable $\xi$ equal to 0 or 1) and by making $\Delta p$ tend towards zero in the preceding equations.

## 4 Principle of the identification of the parameters of the model.

In the simplest case (a single kinematic variable, ${\gamma}_{1}=\text{cste},{C}_{1}=\text{cste}$ $,R(p)={\sigma}_{y}$ ) the coefficients of the model ${\gamma}_{1},{C}_{1}$ can be identified on a simple uniaxial tensile test, or else on a cyclic-hardening curve.

Indeed in the uniaxial case, the model reduces in 1D to [bib2]:

$\begin{array}{c}{\text{dX}}_{1}={C}_{1}d{\varepsilon}^{p}-{\gamma}_{1}{X}_{1}\xi d{\varepsilon}^{p},\xi =\pm 1\\ \mid \sigma -{X}_{1}\mid ={\sigma}_{y}\end{array}$

which one can integrate (under monotonic loading) in the following manner:

$\begin{array}{c}{X}_{1}=\xi \frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}}+({X}_{1}^{0}-\xi \frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}})\exp(-\xi {\gamma}_{1}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\varepsilon}_{0}^{p})),\xi =\pm 1\\ \sigma =\xi {\sigma}_{y}+{X}_{1}\end{array}$

the asymptote of the tensile curve of which makes it possible to obtain $\frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}}$ by:

${\varepsilon}^{p}\to \infty $ ${X}_{1}\to \xi \frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}}$ hence $\sigma \to \xi ({\sigma}_{y}+\frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}})$

and the slope at the origin of which supplies ${C}_{1}$ (if ${X}_{1}^{0}=0$ ) :

${\varepsilon}^{p}\to 0$ ${\dot{X}}_{1}\to {C}_{1}-{y}_{1}{X}_{1}^{0}\xi$ ${X}_{1}^{0}={C}_{1}-{y}_{1}{X}_{1}\xi$

For a model with two kinematic variables, without isotropic hardening, a tensile curve still makes it possible to recover these relations:

${\varepsilon}^{p}\to \infty ` $\sigma \to \xi ({\sigma}_{y}+(\frac{{C}_{1}}{{\gamma}_{1}}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{\gamma}_{2}}))$ and the slope at the origin is equal to ${C}_{1}+{C}_{2}$

But apart from these simple cases a numerical identification is necessary in order to obtain the parameters. One will be able to carry out this identification for example on tension–compression tests at imposed strain. (cf.  10 ).

**Tangent constitutive matrix**

In order to obtain the tangent behaviour in the elastoplastic case, one must compute $\frac{d\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{d\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}}$ [eq 2.3-7].

One uses for this the expression of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ as a function of $\tilde{{\sigma}^{e}}$ and $p$ , which is written in the form:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3\Delta p}{\mathrm{2D}(p)}{\tilde{\sigma}}_{e}+{B}_{1}^{\text{*}}(p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{B}_{2}^{\text{*}}(p){\alpha}_{2}^{-}$

with

${B}_{i}^{\text{*}}(p)=-\Delta p\frac{{M}_{i}(p)}{D(p)}$

${M}_{i}(p)=\frac{{C}_{i}(p)}{1+{\delta}_{i}{\gamma}_{i}(p)\Delta p}$

$D(p)=R(p)+(3\mu +{M}_{1}(p){N}_{1}(p,{\beta}_{1})+{M}_{2}(p){N}_{2}(p,{\beta}_{2}))\Delta p+K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$

One recalls the following definitions:

$\begin{array}{c}R(p)={R}_{\infty}+({R}_{0}-{R}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}}\\ {C}_{i}(p)={C}_{i}^{\infty}(1+(k-1){e}^{-\text{wp}})\\ {\gamma}_{i}(p)={\gamma}_{i}^{0}({a}_{\infty}+(1-{a}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}})\end{array}$

hence:

$\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=\frac{3\Delta p}{\mathrm{2D}(p)}\text{Id}+\frac{\delta (\frac{3\Delta p}{\mathrm{2D}(p)})}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\otimes {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+\frac{\delta {B}_{1}^{\text{*}}(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\otimes {\alpha}_{1}^{-}+\frac{\delta {B}_{2}^{\text{*}}(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}\otimes {\alpha}_{2}^{-}$

The quantities of the type $\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$ are computed by means of: $\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=\frac{\delta A(p)}{\delta p}\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$

These various terms are expressed by:

- $\frac{\delta (\frac{3\Delta p}{\mathrm{2D}(p)})}{\delta p}=\frac{3}{2}I(p)$ with $I(p)=\frac{1}{D(p)}-\frac{{D}^{'}(p)}{{D}^{2}(p)}\Delta p$
- $\frac{\delta {B}_{i}^{\text{*}}(p)}{\delta p}=-\frac{{M}_{i}^{'}(p)}{D(p)}\Delta p-{M}_{i}(p).I(p)={H}_{i}(p)$

Let us detail the computation of ${D}^{'}$ :

- In the case of the memory effect, it suffices to modify the term ${R}^{'}(p)$ . Since $R={R}^{-}+\Delta R={R}^{-}+b\frac{(Q(\Delta p)-{R}^{-})}{1+b\Delta p}\Delta p={R}^{-}+\frac{b\Delta p}{1+b\Delta p}({Q}_{M}+({Q}_{0}-{Q}_{M}){e}^{-2\mu q}-{R}^{-})$ ${R}^{'}(p)=\frac{b}{1+b\Delta p}(\frac{Q-{R}^{-}}{1+b\Delta p}-2\mu \Delta p(Q-{Q}_{M})\frac{\partial \Delta q}{\partial \Delta p})=\frac{b}{1+b\Delta p}(\frac{Q-{R}^{-}}{1+b\Delta p}-\mathrm{2\mu \Delta p}({Q}_{0}-{Q}_{M})\frac{\partial \Delta q}{\partial \Delta p})$ or $\Delta q=\eta (\frac{2}{3}{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})-{q}^{-})$ hence $\frac{\partial \Delta q}{\partial \Delta p}=\eta \frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-}}{{J}_{2}({\varepsilon}^{p}-{\xi}^{-})}\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\partial \Delta p}$ and $\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta \Delta p}=\frac{\delta (\frac{3\Delta p}{\mathrm{2D}(p)})}{\delta \Delta p}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+\frac{\delta {B}_{1}^{\text{*}}(p)}{\delta \Delta p}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+\frac{\delta {B}_{2}^{\text{*}}(p)}{\delta \Delta p}{\alpha}_{2}^{-}=\frac{3}{2}I(\Delta p){\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{H}_{1}^{\text{*}}(\Delta p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{H}_{2}^{\text{*}}(\Delta p){\alpha}_{2}^{-}$
- In the case of non-proportionality (${\delta}_{1}\mathrm{\ne }1$  or ${\delta}_{2}\mathrm{\ne }1$ ), a few derivatives are modified:

${M}_{i}^{'}(p)=\frac{{C}_{i}^{'}(p)}{1+{\delta}_{i}{\gamma}_{i}(p)\Delta p}-\frac{{C}_{i}(p)}{{(1+{\delta}_{i}{\gamma}_{i}(p)\Delta p)}^{2}}({\gamma}_{i}^{'}{\delta}_{i}\Delta p+{\gamma}_{i}{\delta}_{i})$

${D}^{'}={R}^{'}+\frac{K}{N\Delta t}{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{\frac{1}{N}-1}+3\mu +{M}_{1}{N}_{1}+{M}_{2}{N}_{2}+\Delta p({M}_{1}^{'}{N}_{1}+{M}_{2}^{'}{N}_{2}+{M}_{1}{N}_{1}^{'}+{M}_{2}{N}_{2}^{'})$

with ${N}_{i}^{'}=\frac{1+{\gamma}_{i}^{'}({\delta}_{i}\Delta p+({\delta}_{i}-1){\beta}_{i})+{\gamma}_{i}({\delta}_{i}+({\delta}_{i}-1){\beta}_{i}^{'})-{N}_{i}({\gamma}_{i}+{\gamma}_{i}^{'}\Delta p)}{1+{\gamma}_{i}\Delta p}$

It remains to compute: $\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$

One therefore uses, according to [eq 2.3-8] : $\frac{\delta p}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=-\frac{{\tilde{F}}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}{{\tilde{F}}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}$

$\tilde{F}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})={S}_{\text{eq}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})-R(p)-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}=G(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})-R(p)-K{(\frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t})}^{1/N}$

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{c}\frac{\mathit{\delta p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=-\frac{{G}_{,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})}{{G}_{,p}(p,{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e})-{R}_{v}^{'}(p)}=-\frac{\frac{3}{2}\frac{{R}_{v}(p)}{D(p)}\frac{S}{{S}_{\text{eq}}}}{\frac{3}{2}\frac{S}{{S}_{\text{eq}}}:{S}_{,p}-{R}_{v}^{'}(p)}=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\frac{{R}_{v}}{{\mathit{DS}}_{\text{eq}}}(A{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{B}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{B}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}{\frac{3}{2}\frac{S}{{S}_{\text{eq}}}:{S}_{,p}-{R}_{v}^{'}(p)}\\ =-\frac{3}{2}\frac{{L}_{1}(p).{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{L}_{21}(p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{L}_{22}(p){\alpha}_{2}^{-}}{{L}_{3}(p)}\end{array}\\ \end{array}$

Finally, $\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}$ is put in the form:

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\delta \Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}}{\delta {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Delta p}{D(p)}\text{Id}+\frac{3}{2}({I}_{S}(p){\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{I}_{\mathit{a1}}(p){\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{I}_{\mathit{a2}}(p){\alpha}_{2}^{-})\otimes {\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}\\ +({H}_{s}^{1}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{H}_{\mathit{a1}}^{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{H}_{\mathit{a2}}^{1}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})\otimes {\alpha}_{1}^{-}\\ +({H}_{s}^{2}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}+{H}_{\mathit{a1}}^{2}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}+{H}_{\mathit{a2}}^{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})\otimes {\alpha}_{2}^{-}\end{array}$

with:

$\begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{cc}{I}_{S}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}I(p).\frac{{L}_{1}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}& {I}_{\mathit{a1}}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{I(p){L}_{21}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}\\ & {I}_{\mathit{a2}}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{I(p){L}_{22}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}\end{array}\\ {H}_{s}^{1}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{1}(p).{L}_{1}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}{H}_{\mathit{a1}}^{1}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{1}(p){L}_{21}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}{H}_{\mathit{a2}}^{1}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{1}(p){L}_{22}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}\\ {H}_{s}^{2}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{2}(p).{L}_{1}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}{H}_{\mathit{a1}}^{2}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{2}(p){L}_{21}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}{H}_{\mathit{a2}}^{2}(p)\text{=-}\frac{3}{2}\frac{{H}_{2}(p){L}_{22}(p)}{{L}_{3}(p)}\end{array}$

**Solution of the equation f(p) = 0**

It is a matter of solving a non-linear scalar equation by seeking the solution in a confidence interval. For this, one proposes to couple a secant method with a control of the search interval. Let the following equation to be solved:

$f(x)=0$ , $x\in \left[a,b\right]$ , $f(a)<0$ , $f(b)>0$                         eq A2-1

The secant method consists in constructing a sequence of points ${x}^{n}$ which converges towards the solution. It is defined by recurrence (linear approximation of the function by its chord):

${x}^{n+1}={x}^{n-1}-f({x}^{n-1})\frac{{x}^{n}-{x}^{n-1}}{f({x}^{n})-f({x}^{n-1})}$                     eq A2-2

Moreover, if ${x}^{n+1}$ were to leave the interval, then one replaces it by the bound of the interval in question:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\text{si}{x}^{n+1}<a\text{alors}{x}^{n+1}:=a\\ \text{si}{x}^{n+1}>b\text{alors}{x}^{n+1}:=b\end{array}$                                 eq A2-3

On the other hand, if ${x}^{n+1}$ is in the current interval, then one updates the interval:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}\text{si}{x}^{n+1}\in \left[a,b\right]\text{et}f({x}^{n+1})<0\text{alors}a={x}^{n+1}\\ \text{si}{x}^{n+1}\in \left[a,b\right]\text{et}f({x}^{n+1})>0\text{alors}b={x}^{n+1}\end{array}$             eq A2-4

One considers that one has converged when $f$ is sufficiently close to 0 (tolerance to be entered). As for the first two points of the sequence, one can choose the bounds of the interval, or else, if one has an estimate of the solution, one can adopt this estimate and one of the bounds of the interval.

**Note:**

*This method works well if there is a single solution in the interval* $\left[a,b\right]$ *. Without this being formally proved, one can note that* $f(0)>0$ *.*

*One then seeks* $b$ *such that* $f(b)<0$ *.*

*One starts for this from* $b=\frac{{({\tilde{s}}^{\underline{e}}\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}^{-}-\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}^{-})}_{\text{eq}}-R({p}^{-})}{\mathrm{3m}}$

*If* $f(b)>0$ *, one multiplies* $b$ *by 10 and one tests whether* $f(b)>0$ *, and so on, until one finds a value* $b$ *such that* $f(b)<0$ *.*

*One is then sure that there is at least one solution on* $\left[a,b\right]$ *.*
