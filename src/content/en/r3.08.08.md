# r3.08.08 Multi-fibre beam element (straight) POU_D_EM

**Summary:**

This document presents the multi-fibre beam elements based on the solution of a beam problem for which each cross-section of a beam is divided into several fibres. Each fibre then behaves as an Euler beam. Several materials may be assigned on a single finite-element support (SEG2), which avoids having to duplicate the mesh cells (steel + concrete, for example).

The beams are straight (element POU_D_EM). The cross-section may be of arbitrary shape, described by a “fibre mesh”, see [u4.26.01](doc:u4.26.01).

The assumptions adopted are the following:

- Euler assumption: the transverse shear is neglected (this assumption is verified for large slendernesses),
- the multi-fibre beam elements take into account the effects of thermal expansion, of drying and of hydration (right-hand-side terms) and, in a simplified manner, torsion. The normal-force/bending coupling is treated naturally, by integration in the cross-section of the uniaxial responses of the constitutive models associated with each group of fibres. An enrichment of the axial strain, solved by local condensation in the case of non-linear behaviours, allows good numerical results, whatever the evolution in the cross-section of the material centre of gravity of the cross-section.

## 2 Introduction

The analysis of structures subjected to a dynamic loading requires constitutive models capable of representing the non-linearities of the material.

Numerous analytical models have been proposed. They may be classified into two groups:

- detailed models based on solid mechanics and their description of the local behaviour of the material (microscopic approach)
- models based on a global modelling of the behaviour (macroscopic approach).

In the first type of models, one may find the classical finite-element models as well as the “fibre”-type models (having a beam-type element as support).

Whereas the “classical” finite-element models are powerful tools for the simulation of the non-linear behaviour of the complex parts of structures (joints, assemblies, …), their application to the whole of a structure may prove impractical because of a prohibitive computation time or of the memory size necessary to carry out this analysis. On the other hand, a multi-fibre beam modelling (r3.08.08-fig-poutre-beton-arme) possesses the advantages of the simplifying assumptions of an Euler–Bernoulli beam kinematics while offering a practical and efficient solution for a complex non-linear analysis of composite structural elements such as those that one may encounter, for example, in reinforced concrete.

Moreover, this “intermediate” modelling is relatively robust and inexpensive in computation time because of the use of 1D non-linear constitutive models.

## 3 Element of beam theory (reminders)

One takes up here the elements developed within the framework of the Euler beam elements (see [r3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01)).

A beam is a solid generated by a surface of area $S$ whose geometrical centre of inertia $G$ describes a curve $C$ called the mean fibre or neutral fibre. The area $S$ is the right section (transverse cross-section) or profile, and one assumes that if it is evolving, its evolutions (size, shape) are continuous and progressive when $G$ describes the mean line.

For the study of beams in general, one makes the following assumptions:

- the right section of the beam is undeformable,
- the transverse displacement is uniform over the right section.

These assumptions make it possible to express the displacements of an arbitrary point of the cross-section, as a function of the displacements of the corresponding point located on the mean line, and as a function of an increment of displacement due to the rotation of the cross-section about the transverse axes.

The discretisation into “exact” beam elements is carried out on a line element with two nodes and six degrees of freedom per node. These degrees of freedom are the three translations $u,v,w$ and the three rotations ${\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},{\theta}_{z}$ (r3.08.08-fig-element-poutre).

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/elements_poutre.png)

*Beam element*

Given that the strains are local, one builds at each vertex of the mesh a local basis depending on the element on which one is working. The continuity of the displacement fields is ensured by a change of basis, bringing the data back into the global basis.

In the case of straight beams, one traditionally places the mean line on the x axis of the local basis, the transverse displacements thus taking place in the plane $(y,z)$ .

Finally, when one stores quantities linked to the degrees of freedom of an element in a vector or an elementary matrix (therefore of dimension $12$ or ${12}^{2}$ ), one first stores the variables for vertex $1$ then those of vertex $2$ . For each node, one first stores the quantities linked to the three translations, then those linked to the three rotations. For example, a displacement vector will be structured in the following manner:

$\underbrace{u_1, v_1, w_1, \theta_{x_1}, \theta_{y_1}, \theta_{z_1}}_{\text{sommet 1}} \qquad \underbrace{u_2, v_2, w_2, \theta_{x_2}, \theta_{y_2}, \theta_{z_2}}_{\text{sommet 2}}$

## 5 Straight multi-fibre beam element

One describes in this chapter the obtaining of the elementary stiffness and mass matrices for the straight multi-fibre beam element, according to the Euler model. The stiffness matrices are computed with the options 'RIGI_MECA' or 'RIGI_MECA_TANG', and the mass matrices with the option 'MASS_MECA' for the consistent matrix, and the option 'MASS_MECA_DIAG' for the diagonalised mass matrix.

One presents here a generalisation  in which the reference axis chosen for the beam is independent of any geometrical, inertial or mechanical consideration. The element works for an arbitrary cross-section (heterogeneous and without symmetry) and is therefore suited to a non-linear evolution of the behaviour of the fibres.

One also describes the computation of the nodal forces for the non-linear algorithms: 'FORC_NODA' and 'RAPH_MECA'.

### 5.1 Reference beam element

The r3.08.08-fig-element-reference-element-reel shows us the change of variable carried out in order to pass from the real finite element (r3.08.08-fig-element-poutre) to the reference finite element.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/element_reference_element_reel.png)

*Reference element vs Real element*

One will then consider the displacement field continuous at every point of the mean line with respect to the displacement field discretised in the following manner:

$$
{U}_{s}=[N].\lbrace U\rbrace
$$

The index $s$ denotes the quantities attached to the mean fibre.

By using the shape functions of the reference element, the discretisation of the variables ${u}_{s}\left(x\right),{v}_{s}\left(x\right),{w}_{s}\left(x\right),{\theta}_{\text{sx}}\left(x\right),{\theta}_{\text{sy}}\left(x\right),{\theta}_{\text{sz}}\left(x\right)$ becomes:

$$
\left(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{s}\left(x\right)\\ {v}_{s}\left(x\right)\\ {w}_{s}\left(x\right)\\ {\theta}_{\text{sx}}\left(x\right)\\ {\theta}_{\text{sy}}\left(x\right)\\ {\theta}_{\text{sz}}\left(x\right)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{cccccccccccc}{N}_{1}& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{2}& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& {N}_{3}& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{4}& 0& {N}_{5}& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{6}\\ 0& 0& {N}_{3}& 0& -{N}_{4}& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{5}& 0& -{N}_{6}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& {N}_{1}& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{2}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& -{N}_{3,x}& 0& {N}_{4,x}& 0& 0& 0& -{N}_{5,x}& 0& {N}_{6,x}& 0\\ 0& {N}_{3,x}& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{4,x}& 0& {N}_{5,x}& 0& 0& 0& {N}_{6,x}\end{array}\right)\cdot \left(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}\\ {v}_{1}\\ {w}_{1}\\ {\theta}_{x1}\\ {\theta}_{y1}\\ {\theta}_{z1}\\ {u}_{2}\\ {v}_{2}\\ {w}_{2}\\ {\theta}_{x2}\\ {\theta}_{y2}\\ {\theta}_{z2}\end{array}\right)
$$

With the following interpolation functions, and their useful derivatives:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{N}_{1}=1-\frac{x}{L}& ;& {N}_{1,x}=-\frac{1}{L}\\ {N}_{2}=\frac{x}{L}& ;& {N}_{2,x}=\frac{1}{L}\\ {N}_{3}=1-3\frac{{x}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}+2\frac{{x}^{3}}{{L}^{3}}& ;& {N}_{3,xx}=-\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}+12\frac{x}{{L}^{3}}\\ {N}_{4}=x-2\frac{{x}^{2}}{L}+\frac{{x}^{3}}{{L}^{2}}& ;& {N}_{4,xx}=-\frac{4}{L}+6\frac{x}{{L}^{2}}\\ {N}_{5}=3\frac{{x}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}-2\frac{{x}^{3}}{{L}^{3}}& ;& {N}_{5,xx}=\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}-12\frac{x}{{L}^{3}}\\ {N}_{6}=-\frac{{x}^{2}}{L}+\frac{{x}^{3}}{{L}^{2}}& ;& {N}_{6,xx}=-\frac{2}{L}+6\frac{x}{{L}^{2}}\end{array}
$$

### 5.2 Determination of the stiffness matrix of the multi-fibre element

#### 5.2.1 General case (Euler beam)

Let us consider an Euler beam, straight, oriented in the direction $x$ , subjected to distributed forces ${q}_{x},{q}_{y},{q}_{z}$ (r3.08.08-fig-poutre-euler).

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/100000000000023A000000A0BB31259EF785E008.png)

*3D Euler beam*

The displacement and strain fields then take the following form when one writes the displacement of an arbitrary point of the cross-section as a function of the displacement $({U}_{s})$ and of the rotation ${\theta}_{s}$ of the mean line:

$$
u(x,y,z)={u}_{s}(x)-y{\theta}_{\mathit{sz}}(x)+z{\theta}_{\mathit{sy}}(x)
$$

$$
v(x,y,z)={v}_{s}(x)
$$

$$
w(x,y,z)={w}_{s}(x)
$$

$$
{\epsilon}_{xx}={u}_{x}^{'}(x)-y{\theta}_{\mathit{sz}}^{'}(x)+z{\theta}_{\mathit{sy}}^{'}(x)
$$

$$
{\epsilon}_{xy}={\epsilon}_{xz}=0
$$

**Note**

Torsion is treated globally by admitting an elastic assumption, separately; one does not compute ${\epsilon}_{yz}$ here. $f’(x)$ denotes the derivative of $f(x)$ with respect to $x$ .

By introducing the equations  and  into the principle of virtual work one obtains:

$$
{\int}_{{V}_{0}}{\sigma}_{xx}.\delta {\epsilon}_{xx}{\text{dV}}_{0}={\int}_{0}^{L}\left({\mathit{\delta u}}_{s}\left(x\right){q}_{x}+{\mathit{\delta v}}_{s}\left(x\right){q}_{y}+{\mathit{\delta w}}_{s}\left(x\right){q}_{z}\right)\text{dx}
$$

${q}_{x},{q}_{y},{q}_{z}$ denoting the applied line forces. Which gives, by using the equation :

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{0}^{L}\left(N{\mathit{\delta u}}_{s}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{x}{\mathit{\delta \theta }}_{\text{sx}}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{y}{\mathit{\delta \theta }}_{\text{sy}}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{z}{\mathit{\delta \theta }}_{\text{sz}}^{'}\left(x\right)\right)\text{dx}\\ \hfill ={\int}_{0}^{L}\left({q}_{x}{\mathit{\delta u}}_{s}\left(x\right)+{q}_{y}{\mathit{\delta v}}_{s}\left(x\right)+{q}_{z}{\mathit{\delta w}}_{s}\left(x\right)\right)\text{dx}\end{array}
$$

with:

$$
N={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{M}_{y}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}z{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{M}_{z}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}-y{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}
$$

**Notes**

The torsional moment ${M}_{x}$ is not computed by integration but computed directly from the torsional stiffness (see the equation ).

The beam theory associated with an elastic material gives: ${\sigma}_{xx}=E{\epsilon}_{xx}$

#### 5.2.2 Case of the multi-fibre beam

One now assumes that the cross-section $s$ is not homogeneous, materials with different mechanical characteristics.

Without adopting any particular assumption on the intersection of the axis $x$ with the cross-section $s$ or on the orientation of the axes $Y,Z$ , the relation between the “generalised” stresses and the “generalised” strains ${\mathrm{D}}_{s}$ becomes  :

$$
{\mathrm{F}}_{s}={\mathrm{K}}_{s}\cdot {\mathrm{D}}_{s}
$$

with:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{F}}_{s}={\left(N,\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{M}_{y},\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{M}_{z},\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{M}_{x}\right)}^{T}\\ {\mathrm{D}}_{s}={\left({u}_{s}^{'}\left(x\right),\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\theta}_{\text{sy}}^{'}\left(x\right),\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\theta}_{\text{sz}}^{'}\left(x\right),\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\theta}_{\text{sx}}^{'}\left(x\right)\right)}^{T}\end{array}
$$

The matrix ${K}_{s}$ may then be put in the following form:

$$
{\mathrm{K}}_{s}=\left(\begin{array}{cccc}{K}_{s11}& {K}_{s12}& {K}_{s13}& 0\\ & {K}_{s22}& {K}_{s23}& 0\\ & & {K}_{s33}& 0\\ \text{sym}& & & {K}_{s44}\end{array}\right)
$$

with:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{K}_{s11}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}\text{EdS}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s12}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}\text{Ezds}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s13}=-{\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}\text{Eyds}\\ {K}_{s22}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}{\text{Ez}}^{2}\text{dS}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s23}=-{\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}\text{Eyzds}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s33}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}S}{\text{Ey}}^{2}\text{ds}\end{array}
$$

where $E$ may vary as a function of $y$ and $z$ . Indeed, it may be that in the plane modelling of the cross-section, several materials coexist. For example, in a reinforced-concrete cross-section, there is both concrete and reinforcement.

The discretisation of the cross-section into fibres makes it possible to compute the integrals of the equations . The computation of the coefficients of the matrix ${\mathrm{K}}_{s}$ is detailed in r3.08.08-discretisation-section-fibres-ks.

**Note**

The torsion term ${K}_{s44}={\text{GJ}}_{x}$ is given by the user with the aid of the data of* ${J}_{x}$ , with the aid of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM.

The introduction of the equations  to  into the principle of virtual work leads to:

$$
{\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}0}^{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}L}{\mathit{\delta D}}_{s}^{T}.{K}_{s}.{D}_{s}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{dx}-{\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}0}^{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}L}\left({\mathit{\delta u}}_{s}\left(x\right)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{x}+{\mathit{\delta v}}_{s}\left(x\right)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{y}+{\mathit{\delta w}}_{s}\left(x\right)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{z}\right)\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\text{dx}=0
$$

The generalised strains are computed by (${D}_{S}$  is given at the equation ):

$$
{\mathrm{D}}_{s}=\mathrm{B}\left\lbrace \mathrm{U}\right\rbrace
$$

With the following matrix $\mathrm{B}$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
B = \begin{bmatrix} N_{1,x} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & N_{2,x} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & -N_{3,xx} & 0 & N_{4,xx} & 0 & 0 & 0 & -N_{5,xx} & 0 & N_{6,xx} & 0 \\ 0 & N_{3,xx} & 0 & 0 & 0 & N_{4,xx} & 0 & N_{5,xx} & 0 & 0 & 0 & N_{6,xx} \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & N_{1,x} & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & N_{2,x} & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$

The discretisation of the space $\left[0,L\right]$ with elements and the use of the equations  makes the equation  equivalent to the solution of a classical linear system:

$$
\mathrm{K}.\mathrm{U}=\mathrm{F}
$$

The stiffness matrix of the element (r3.08.08-fig-poutre-multifibre) and the vector of the resultant forces are finally given by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{K}}_{\text{elem}}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}0}^{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}.\mathrm{B}\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}\text{dx}\\ \mathrm{F}={\int}_{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}0}^{\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}L}{\mathrm{N}}^{T}.Q\phantom{\rule{1em}{0ex}}\text{dx}\end{array}
$$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/100000000000048500000255FD5575173B5A09B3.png)

*Multi-fibre beam – Computation of ${\text{K}}_{\mathit{elem}}$*

With the vector $Q$ which depends on the external loading: $\mathrm{Q}={\left({q}_{x}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{y}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}{q}_{z}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}0\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}0\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}0\right)}^{T}$ .

If one considers that the distributed forces ${q}_{x},{q}_{y},{q}_{z}$ are constant, one obtains the following nodal-force vector:

$$
\mathrm{F}={\left(\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{x}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{y}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{z}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}0\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}-\frac{{L}^{2}{q}_{z}}{12}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{L}^{2}{q}_{y}}{12}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{x}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{y}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{\mathit{Lq}}_{z}}{2}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}0\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{L}^{2}{q}_{z}}{12}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\frac{{L}^{2}{q}_{y}}{12}\phantom{\rule{1.5em}{0ex}}\right)}^{T}
$$

#### 5.2.3 Discretisation of the cross-section into fibres – Computation of ${K}_{s}$

The discretisation of the cross-section into fibres makes it possible to compute the different integrals which intervene in the stiffness matrix, and the other necessary terms.

The geometry of the fibres gathered into groups of fibres, via the operator DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE [u4.26.01](doc:u4.26.01) contains in particular the characteristics (Y, Z, AIRE) for each fibre. One may provide at most 10 fibre groups maximum per beam element.

Thus, if one has a cross-section which comprises $n$ fibres one will have the following approximations of the integrals:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{K}_{s11}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{S}_{i}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s12}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{z}_{i}{S}_{i}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s13}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{y}_{i}{S}_{i}\\ {K}_{s22}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{z}_{{i}^{2}}{S}_{i}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s23}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{y}_{i}{z}_{i}{S}_{i}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{K}_{s33}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{E}_{i}{y}_{{i}^{2}}{S}_{i}\end{array}
$$

with ${E}_{i}$ the initial or tangent modulus and ${S}_{i}$ the cross-section of each fibre. The stress state is constant per fibre.

Each fibre is also located with the aid of ${y}_{i}$ and ${z}_{i}$ the coordinates of the centre of gravity of the fibre with respect to the axis of the cross-section defined by the keyword 'COOR_AXE_POUTRE' (see the command DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE [u4.26.01](doc:u4.26.01)).

The numbering of the fibres depends on the choice of the keyword 'FIBRE' or 'SECTION' (see the command DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE [u4.26.01](doc:u4.26.01)).

$$

with the following terms ${K}_{s11},{K}_{s12},{K}_{s13},{K}_{s22},{K}_{s33},{K}_{s23},{K}_{s44}$ given at the equation .

#### 5.2.5 Integration in the non-linear case (RIGI_MECA_TANG)

When the behaviour of the material is non-linear, in order to allow a correct integration of the internal forces (see r3.08.08-calcul-forces-internes), it is necessary to have at least two integration points along the beam. One has chosen to use two Gauss points.

The integral of ${\mathrm{K}}_{\mathit{elem}}$  is computed in numerical form:

$$
{\mathrm{K}}_{\mathit{elem}}={\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{{\rm K}}_{s}.\mathrm{B}\text{dx}=j\sum_{i=1}^{2}{w}_{i}\mathrm{B}{\left({x}_{i}\right)}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}\left({x}_{i}\right).\mathrm{B}\left({x}_{i}\right)
$$

- where ${x}_{i}$ is the position of the Gauss point $i$ in a reference element of length 1, that is to say: $(1\pm 0,57735026918963)/2$ ;

#### 5.3.2 Discretisation of the cross-section into fibres - Computation of ${M}_{s}$

The discretisation of the cross-section into fibres makes it possible to compute the different integrals which intervene in the mass matrix. Thus, if one has a cross-section which comprises $n$ fibres one will have the following approximations of the integrals:

$$

with ${\rho}_{i}$ and ${S}_{i}$ the density and the cross-section of each fibre. ${y}_{i}$ and ${z}_{i}$ are the coordinates of the centre of gravity of the fibre defined as previously.

### 5.5 Strain-enriched formulation

With the displacement interpolations of the equation , the generalised axial strain is constant and the curvatures are linear (see equations ,  and ):

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{s}(x)=\frac{{u}_{2}-{u}_{1}}{L}\\ {\chi }_{\text{ys}}(x)=-\left(-\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}+\frac{12x}{{L}^{3}}\right){w}_{1}+\left(\frac{\mathrm{6x}}{{L}^{2}}-\frac{4}{L}\right){\theta}_{\mathit{y1}}-\left(-\frac{12x}{{L}^{3}}+\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}\right){w}_{2}+\left(\frac{\mathrm{6x}}{{L}^{2}}-\frac{4}{L}\right){\theta}_{\mathit{y2}}\\ {\chi }_{\text{zs}}(x)=\left(-\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}+\frac{12x}{{L}^{3}}\right){v}_{1}+\left(\frac{\mathrm{6x}}{{L}^{2}}-\frac{4}{L}\right){\theta}_{\mathit{z1}}+\left(-\frac{12x}{{L}^{3}}+\frac{6}{{L}^{2}}\right){v}_{2}+\left(\frac{\mathrm{6x}}{{L}^{2}}-\frac{4}{L}\right){\theta}_{\mathit{z2}}\end{array}\right.
$$

If there is no coupling between these two strains (elastic case, with the reference mean line which passes through the barycentre of the cross-section), this poses no problem. But in the general non-linear case, there is a shift of the neutral axis, and the terms ${K}_{s12}$ and ${K}_{s13}$ of ${\mathrm{K}}_{s}$ (equations  and ) are not zero, there is coupling between the moments and the normal force. One then has an incompatibility in the approximation of the axial strains of a fibre:

$$
\epsilon ={\epsilon}_{s}(x)-y{\chi }_{\text{zs}}(x)+z{\chi }_{\text{ys}}(x)
$$

A means of eliminating this incompatibility is to enrich the axial strain field:

$$
{\epsilon}_{s}(x)\mapsto {\epsilon}_{s}(x)+\tilde{{\epsilon}_{s}}(x);\tilde{{\epsilon}_{s}}(x)=\alpha .G(x);G(x)=\frac{4}{L}-\frac{8x}{{L}^{2}}
$$

for $x\in \left[-\frac{L}{2},\frac{L}{2}\right]$

where $G(x)$ is an enriched strain which derives from a “bubble” function in displacement and $\alpha$ the enrichment degree of freedom. The variational basis of such an enrichment is supplied by the Hu-Washizu principle  which may be presented in the same manner as the method of incompatible modes [bib6].

#### 5.5.1 Method of incompatible modes

The regular generalised displacement field ${\mathrm{U}}_{s}$ is defined by the equation . The generalised strains ${\mathrm{D}}_{s}$ and the generalised stresses ${\mathrm{F}}_{s}$ by the equation .

The Hu-Washizu principle consists in writing the weak form of the equilibrium equations, but also of the computation of the strains and of the constitutive law, in projection onto the three virtual fields (generalised displacements ${\mathrm{U}}_{s}^{\text{*}}$ , generalised strains ${\mathrm{D}}_{s}^{\text{*}}$ and generalised stresses ${\mathrm{F}}_{s}^{\text{*}}$ ):

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{0}^{L}\frac{{\mathit{dU}}_{s}^{\text{*}}}{\text{dx}}\cdot {F}_{s}\text{dx}-{W}_{\text{ext}}=0\\ {\int}_{0}^{L}{F}_{s}^{\text{*}}\cdot \left(\frac{d{U}_{s}}{\text{dx}}-{D}_{s}\right)\text{dx}=0\\ {\int}_{0}^{L}{D}_{s}^{\text{*}}\cdot \left({F}_{s}-{K}_{s}\cdot {D}_{s}\right)\text{dx}=0\end{array}\right.
$$

One introduces the enrichment of the real strains, and one chooses to decompose the virtual strain field into a “regular” part coming from the virtual displacement field and an enriched part:

$$
{D}_{s}=\frac{{\mathit{dU}}_{s}}{\text{dx}}+{\bar{D}}_{s}
$$

$$
{D}_{s}^{\text{*}}=\frac{{\mathit{dU}}_{s}^{\text{*}}}{\text{dx}}+{\bar{D}}_{s}^{\text{*}}
$$

One carries the equation  into the second equation of the system , which justifies the “enrichment” by the orthogonality:

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{F}}_{s}^{\text{*}}.{\mathrm{D}}_{s}\mathit{dx}=0
$$

The last equation of the system  decomposes into two since one has two independent virtual fields in the equation

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}\frac{{\mathit{dU}}_{s}^{\text{*}}}{\text{dx}}.\left({F}_{s}-{K}_{s}.{D}_{s}\right)\text{dx}=0
$$

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}{\overline{\text{D}}}_{s}^{\text{*}}\cdot \left({\text{F}}_{s}-{\text{K}}_{s}\cdot {\text{D}}_{s}\right)\mathit{dx}=0
$$

Finally, the method of incompatible modes consists in choosing the stress space orthogonal to the space of the enriched strains, so that the equation  is automatically verified and the equation  therefore simply gives:

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}{\bar{\mathrm{D}}}_{s}^{\text{*}}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}.{\mathrm{D}}_{s}\mathit{dx}=0
$$

If one returns to the strong formulation of the constitutive law in the equations  and , the system  becomes:

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}\frac{{\mathit{dU}}_{s}^{\text{*}}}{\text{dx}}.{F}_{s}\text{dx}-{W}_{\text{ext}}=0 \quad {\int}_{0}^{L}{{\overline{\text{D}}}_{s}}^{\text{*}}\cdot {\text{F}}_{s}\mathit{dx}=0 \quad {\text{F}}_{s}={\text{K}}_{s}\cdot {\text{D}}_{s}
$$

**Note**

Here one enriches only the axial strain of an Euler-Bernoulli beam element, with a continuous function, therefore $\bar{\mathrm{D}}={\left(\begin{array}{cccc}\bar{{\epsilon}_{s}}& 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right)}^{T}$ .

#### 5.5.2 Numerical implementation

From the finite-element point of view, one may write the displacements and the strains in matrix form, with the enriched part:

$$
{\mathrm{B}}_{s}=\mathrm{N}.(\mathrm{U})+\mathrm{Q}.(\alpha ) \quad \mathrm{D}=\mathrm{B}.(\mathrm{U})+\mathrm{G}.(\alpha )
$$

where $\mathrm{N}$ and $\mathrm{B}$ are the classical matrices of the interpolation functions and of their derivatives (see the equations  and ) and:

$$
\mathrm{Q}={\left(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{4x}{L}-\frac{4{x}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}& 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right)}^{T} \quad \text{et} \quad \mathrm{G}={\left(\begin{array}{cccc}\frac{4}{L}-\frac{8x}{{L}^{2}}& 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right)}^{T}
$$

$$
{\int}_{0}^{L}\mathrm{G}(x)\text{dx}=0
$$

**Note**

$\mathrm{G}$ has been chosen so that the element always passes the “patch test” (zero strain energy for a rigid-body motion) :

After classical manipulations of passage from the continuous to the discrete, the system of equations , written for the whole of the structure, is approximated by:

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{A}_{e=1}^{{N}_{\text{elem}}}\left({F}_{int}-{F}_{\text{ext}}\right)=0\\ {h}_{e}=0\text{}\forall e\in [1,{N}_{\text{elem}}]\end{array}\right.
$$

with:

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{F}}_{int}={\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{\mathrm{F}}_{s}\text{dx}={\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}.\left(\mathrm{B}.{\mathrm{U}}_{s}+\mathrm{G}.\alpha \right)\text{dx}\\ {\mathrm{F}}_{\text{ext}}={\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{N}}^{T}.\mathrm{f}\text{dx}\\ {h}_{e}={\int}_{0}^{L}{\mathrm{G}}^{T}.{\mathrm{F}}_{s}\text{dx}\end{array}\right.
$$

${A}_{e=1}^{{N}_{\text{elem}}}$ denotes the assembly over all the elements of the mesh; $\mathrm{f}$ is the distributed axial load on the beam element. The system of equations  is non-linear, it is solved in an iterative manner (see STAT_NON_LINE).

At iteration $(i+1)$ , with $\Delta {\mathrm{U}}^{(i)}={\mathrm{U}}^{(i+1)}-{\mathrm{U}}^{(i)}$ and $\Delta {\alpha}^{(i)}={\alpha}^{(i+1)}-{\alpha}^{(i)}$ , the linearisation of the system gives (Newton correction iterations):

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{A}_{e=1}^{{N}_{\text{elem}}}\left(\left({\mathrm{F}}_{int}^{(i+1)}-{\mathrm{F}}_{\text{ext}}^{(i+1)}\right)+{\mathrm{K}}_{e}^{(i)}.\Delta {\mathrm{U}}^{(i)}+{\mathrm{X}}_{e}^{(i)}\Delta {\alpha}^{(i)}\right)=0\\ {h}_{e}^{(i+1)}+{\mathrm{X}}_{e}^{(i)T}.\Delta {\mathrm{U}}^{(i)}+{H}_{e}^{(i)}\Delta {\alpha}^{(i)}=0\forall e\in [\mathrm{1,.}\mathrm{..},{N}_{\text{elem}}]\end{array}\right.
$$

with:

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{K}}_{e}^{(i)}={\int}_{L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}^{(i)}.\mathrm{B}\text{dx}\\ {\mathrm{X}}_{e}^{(i)}={\int}_{L}{\mathrm{B}}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}^{(i)}.\mathrm{G}\text{dx}\\ {H}_{e}^{(i)}={\int}_{L}{\mathrm{G}}^{T}.{\mathrm{K}}_{s}^{(i)}.\mathrm{G}\text{dx}\end{array}\right.
$$

The second equation of the system  is local. It makes it possible to compute the enrichment degree of freedom $\alpha$ independently on each element. One computes it by a local iterative method (iterations $(j)$ for a fixed displacement ${d}^{(i)}=\Delta {\mathrm{U}}^{(i)}$ ):

$$
{\alpha}_{(j+1)}^{(i)}={\alpha}_{(j)}^{(i)}-{\left({H}_{e(j)}^{(i)}\right)}^{-1}{h}_{e(j)}^{(i)}
$$

Thus, when one has converged at the local level, one has:

$$
{h}_{e}({d}^{(i)},{\alpha}^{(i)})=0
$$

And one may operate a static condensation in order to eliminate $\alpha$ at the global level.

$$
{\mathrm{K}}_{e}^{(i)}={\mathrm{K}}_{e}^{(i)}-{\mathrm{X}}_{e}^{(i)}{\left({H}_{e}^{(i)}\right)}^{-1}{\mathrm{X}}_{e}^{(i)T}
$$

From a practical point of view, this technique makes it possible to treat the enrichment at the elementary level without disturbing the number of global degrees of freedom. It is implemented at the level of the elementary routine charged with computing the options FULL_MECA, RAPH_MECA and RIGI_MECA_TANG.

- In the particular case set out here, ${H}_{e}^{(i)}$ is a real number, therefore very easy to invert.
- Likewise, ${h}_{e}$ and $\alpha$ are also real numbers.
- The computation of ${K}_{e}^{(i)}$ is explained in r3.08.08-integration-cas-non-lineaire, the other quantities of the equation  are computed according to the same technique.
- Likewise the computation of ${\mathrm{F}}_{int}$ is explained in r3.08.08-calcul-forces-internes, ${h}_{e}$ in the equation  is computed according to the same technique.

**Notes**

#### 5.5.3 Taking the eccentricity into account

For an elastic behaviour, the enrichment of the axial strains makes it possible to take correctly into account the coupling between the normal force and the bending moments, and to make the response of the beam independent of the position chosen for the reference axis (see keyword COOR_AXE_POUTRE in the operator DEFI_GEOM_FIBRE, [u4.26.01](doc:u4.26.01)). It thus makes it possible to treat the case of eccentric beams.

The numerical implementation of the enrichment of the axial strains presented in r3.08.08-implantation-numerique concerns the non-linear analyses with STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE, for the options FULL_MECA, RAPH_MECA and RIGI_MECA_TANG. Indeed, the determination of $\alpha$ is done by local iterations at the elementary level (equation ).

In the case of analyses with the option RIGI_MECA (MECA_STATIQUE, modal analysis, non-linear analyses with an 'ELASTIQUE' prediction), one may determine $\alpha$ explicitly as a function of the eccentricities of the elastic centre with respect to the reference axis:

${e}_{y}=\frac{{\int}_{S}\mathit{Ey}\mathit{dS}}{{\int}_{S}E\mathit{dS}}=\frac{{K}_{\mathit{S13}}}{{K}_{\mathit{S11}}}$ and ${e}_{z}=\frac{{\int}_{S}\mathit{Ez}\mathit{dS}}{{\int}_{S}E\mathit{dS}}=\frac{{K}_{\mathit{S12}}}{{K}_{\mathit{S11}}}$

One may then treat the static condensation in order to eliminate $\alpha$ at the level of the elementary stiffness matrix. These computations have been done analytically and the matrix is implemented explicitly in *Code_Aster* (modification of some twenty terms if ${e}_{y}$ and/or ${e}_{z}$ are not zero).

Given that the displacement is enriched, the mass matrix (see r3.08.08-matrice-masse-elment-multifibre) is modified. As for the stiffness matrix, the terms modified in the case where ${e}_{y}$ and/or ${e}_{z}$ are not zero have been computed analytically and programmed explicitly.

The enrichment of the strains also modifies the computation of the options DEGE_ELNO  and EPSI_ELGA .

### 5.6 Non-linear constitutive models that can be used

The models supported are on the one hand the 1D constitutive relations of type VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_CINE_LINE and VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, CORR_ACIER [r5.03.09](doc:r5.03.09) for the steels, on the other hand the model MAZARS_UNIL [r7.01.08](doc:r7.01.08) dedicated to the uniaxial behaviour of concrete in cyclic loading. One may thus have several materials per multi-fibre beam element.

Furthermore, if the behaviour used is not available in 1D, one may use the other 3D laws with the aid of the method of R.DeBorst [r5.03.09](doc:r5.03.09). For example, one may treat: GRAN_IRRA_LOG, VISC_IRRA_LOG. However in this case, one can treat only a single material per multi-fibre beam element.

**Note**

The internal variables, constant per fibre, are stored in the sub-points attached to the integration point considered.

Access to the post-processing of the quantities defined at the sub-points is done via the MED3.0 format, of Salomé.
## 6 Assembly-skeleton element

The assembly-skeleton element is a generalisation of the multi-fibre beam element presented in the previous section. This element consists of an element having a beam kinematics, but in which batches of fibres are gathered into sub-beams which represent the guide tubes. These sub-beams are themselves multi-fibre, as shown in r3.08.08-fig-section-multipoutres . This element is accessible only within the framework of the kinematics of Euler beams.

The assumptions adopted are the following:

- the Euler assumption: the transverse shear is neglected (this assumption is verified for large slendernesses);
- the element carries the usual displacement and rotation degrees of freedom of beams as well as three additional degrees of freedom modelling the rotation of the grid linking kinematically the guide tubes;
- the rotations in the whole of the beams are all the same;
- the skeleton element takes into account the effects of thermal expansion, of gravity and, in a simplified manner, torsion.

### 6.1 Kinematics of the finite element

One considers here a structural element describing the skeleton of a fuel assembly made up of ${N}_{p}$ guide tubes, here considered as three-dimensional beams. These guide tubes are linked kinematically to one another by the grids of the fuel assembly, here considered as a rigid body. From these two assumptions, it is possible to describe the whole kinematics of this assembly of beams with the aid of nine degrees of freedom, as indicated on r3.08.08-fig-element-assemblage-combustible :

- three degrees of freedom representing the displacement of the grid: $({u}_{x},{u}_{y},{u}_{z})$
- three degrees of freedom describing the rotations of the guide tubes: $({\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},{\theta}_{z})$
- three degrees of freedom modelling the rotation of the grid linking the guide tubes: $({\omega}_{x},{\omega}_{y},{\omega}_{z})$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/10000201000002BC000003E64C49C02630BFC7BA.png)

*Diagram of the fuel-assembly element*

The position of a guide tube $i$ with respect to the centre of the assembly skeleton is described with the aid of a pair of real numbers $({Y}^{i},{Z}^{i})$ . By using the usual degrees of freedom (three displacements and three rotations) of a three-dimensional Euler beam and by assuming that the rotations in all the beams are the same, the kinematics of the guide tube $i$ is written:

$$
{u}_{x}^{i}={u}_{x}\left(x\right)-{Y}^{i}.{\omega}_{z}\left(x\right)+{Z}^{i}.{\omega}_{y}\left(x\right)
{u}_{y}^{i}={u}_{y}\left(x\right)-{Z}^{i}{\omega}_{x}\left(x\right)
{u}_{z}^{i}={u}_{z}\left(x\right)+{Y}^{i}{\omega}_{x}\left(x\right)
{\theta}_{x}^{i}={\theta}_{x}(x)
{\theta}_{y}^{i}={\theta}_{y}(x)
{\theta}_{z}^{i}={\theta}_{z}(x)
$$

Moreover, with a view to using beams with a non-linear behaviour, the cross-section of the beams is itself described with the aid of fibres (numbered from $1$ to ${N}_{f}$ ) and the multi-fibre beam theory is used here. Thus, for each beam $i$ (varying from $1$ to ${N}_{p}$ ), the axial displacement of its fibre $j$ is a function of the mean displacement of the cross-section of the beam, to which one adds the displacement imposed by the rotation of the cross-section. Since beam theory imposes that the cross-section of the beams remains straight, the axial displacement field of the fibre $j$ of a guide tube $i$ is written:

$$
{u}_{x}^{i,j}={u}_{x}^{i}-\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{z}\left(x\right)+\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{y}\left(x\right)
$$

By injecting into it the first equation :

$$
{u}_{x}^{i,j}={u}_{x}\left(x\right)-{Y}^{i}.{\omega}_{z}\left(x\right)+{Z}^{i}.{\omega}_{y}\left(x\right)-\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{z}\left(x\right)+\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{y}\left(x\right)
$$

Where the quantity $({y}^{i,j},{z}^{i,j})$ corresponds to the position of the fibre $j$ of a beam $i$ with respect to the centre of the assembly of beams. The axial strain field of the fibre is subsequently determined by:

$$
{\epsilon}_{xx}^{i,j}=\frac{\partial {u}_{x}^{i,j}}{\partial x}={u}_{x}^{'}\left(x\right)-{Y}^{i}.{\omega}_{z}^{'}\left(x\right)+{Z}^{i}.{\omega}_{y}^{'}\left(x\right)-\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{z}^{'}\left(x\right)+\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right).{\theta}_{y}^{'}\left(x\right)
$$

The principle is to rely on the existing multi-fibre beams by passing from the degrees of freedom of the assembly-skeleton element to the $i$ fields of “generalised multi-fibre beam displacements” constituting it, by using (). By proceeding thus (i.e. by treating all the beams individually), one determines a “local generalised displacement field” ${U}_{i}$ for each of the beams $i$ . One thus comes back, for each of the beams, to the determination of the fields with the aid of the six “classical” degrees of freedom along the element.

Thus, one determines the generalised strains ${D}_{i}$ on each of the beams $i$ with ${D}_{i}=B.{U}_{i}$ (cf. [r3.08.08](doc:r3.08.08)). Once this is done, one may:

- determine the uniaxial strains on the fibres in the same way as for the Euler multi-fibre beams;
- obtain the stresses by the constitutive law;
- determine the generalised forces on the beam $i$ as for an Euler multi-fibre beam from the stresses;
- assemble the elementary matrix of the assembly skeleton from the matrices of each sub-beam;
- finally one must construct the generalised forces of the assembly element from the generalised forces on the beams $i$ . It is a matter of separating the moments caused by the rotation of the beams and those caused by the rotation of the grid.

The implementation of this finite element is intended as an encapsulation of the multi-fibre Euler beam model.

The discretisation of the skeleton element is carried out on a line element with two nodes and nine degrees of freedom per node. These degrees of freedom are the three translations $(u,v,w)$ and the six rotations relative to the beams $({\theta}_{x},{\theta}_{y},{\theta}_{z})$ and to the grids $({\omega}_{x},{\omega}_{y},{\omega}_{z})$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.08/element_squelette.png)

*Skeleton element*

Given that the strains are local, one builds at each vertex of the mesh a local basis depending on the element on which one is working. The continuity of the displacement fields is ensured by a change of basis, bringing the data back into the global basis. In the case of straight beams, one traditionally places the mean line on the axis $x$ of the local basis, the transverse displacements thus taking place in the plane $(y,z)$ .

Finally, when one stores quantities linked to the degrees of freedom of an element in a vector or an elementary matrix (therefore of dimension $18$ or ${18}^{2}$ ), one first stores the variables for vertex $1$ then those of vertex $2$ . For each node, one first stores the quantities linked to the three translations, then those linked to the six rotations. For example, a displacement vector will be structured in the following manner:

$\underbrace{u_1, v_1, w_1, \theta_{x_1}, \theta_{y_1}, \theta_{z_1}, \omega_{x_1}, \omega_{y_1}, \omega_{z_1}}_{\text{sommet 1}} \qquad \underbrace{u_2, v_2, w_2, \theta_{x_2}, \theta_{y_2}, \theta_{z_2}, \omega_{x_2}, \omega_{y_2}, \omega_{z_2}}_{\text{sommet 2}}$

### 6.2 Determination of the stiffness matrix

The stiffness matrix of the skeleton element rests directly on an assembly of the elementary matrices of each beam of the bundle of the assembly. One will therefore refer to the documentation relating to the straight multi-fibre beam element concerning the computation of these matrices. The stiffness matrices are computed with the options 'RIGI_MECA' or 'RIGI_MECA_TANG'.

The skeleton element carries two nodes (indexed 1 and 2) each provided with nine degrees of freedom describing the three fields $(U,\theta ,\omega )$ . The whole of these degrees of freedom come to generate displacements/rotations on the multi-fibre sub-beams which for their part have twelve degrees of freedom. In order to differentiate the degrees of freedom of the skeleton element and of the sub-beams, one uses respectively capitals / lower case. For the displacements:

$$
{u}_{{x}_{1}}={U}_{{x}_{1}}-{\omega}_{{z}_{1}}{Y}^{i}+{\omega}_{{y}_{1}}{Z}^{i} \quad \text{et} \quad {u}_{{x}_{2}}={U}_{{x}_{2}}-{\omega}_{{z}_{2}}{Y}^{i}+{\omega}_{{y}_{2}}{Z}^{i}
{u}_{{y}_{1}}={U}_{{y}_{1}}-{\omega}_{{x}_{1}}{Z}^{i} \quad \text{et} \quad {u}_{{y}_{2}}={U}_{{y}_{2}}-{\omega}_{{x}_{2}}{Z}^{i}
{u}_{{z}_{1}}={U}_{{z}_{1}}+{\omega}_{{x}_{1}}{Y}^{i} \quad \text{et} \quad {u}_{{z}_{2}}={U}_{{z}_{2}}+{\omega}_{{x}_{2}}{Y}^{i}
$$

For the rotations:

$$
{\theta}_{{x}_{1}}={\Theta}_{{x}_{1}} \quad \text{et} \quad {\theta}_{{x}_{2}}={\Theta}_{{x}_{2}}
{\theta}_{{y}_{1}}={\Theta}_{{y}_{1}} \quad \text{et} \quad {\theta}_{{y}_{2}}={\Theta}_{{y}_{2}}
{\theta}_{{z}_{1}}={\Theta}_{{z}_{1}} \quad \text{et} \quad {\theta}_{{z}_{2}}={\Theta}_{{z}_{2}}
$$

If one denotes by ${K}^{i}$ the matrix of size 12x12 corresponding to the beam $i$ , one may compute for example the normal force ${f}_{x}^{i}$ on the beam in this way:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{f}_{x}^{i}={K}_{1,1}^{i}.{u}_{{x}_{1}}+{K}_{1,2}^{i}.{u}_{{y}_{1}}+{K}_{1,3}^{i}.{u}_{{z}_{1}}\\ {K}_{1,4}^{i}.{\theta}_{{x}_{1}}+{K}_{1,5}^{i}.{\theta}_{{y}_{1}}+{K}_{1,6}^{i}.{\theta}_{{z}_{1}}+\\ {K}_{1,7}^{i}.{u}_{{x}_{2}}+{K}_{1,8}^{i}.{u}_{{y}_{2}}+{K}_{1,9}^{i}.{u}_{{z}_{2}}\\ {K}_{1,10}^{i}.{\theta}_{{x}_{2}}+{K}_{1,11}^{i}.{\theta}_{{y}_{2}}+{K}_{1,12}^{i}.{\theta}_{{z}_{2}}\end{array}
$$

This force is expressed as a function of the “local” degrees of freedom; one may also express it as a function of the “global” degrees of freedom, that is to say those of the skeleton element:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{f}_{x}^{i}={K}_{1,1}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{x}_{1}}-{\omega}_{{z}_{1}}{Y}^{i}+{\omega}_{{y}_{1}}{Z}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,2}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{y}_{1}}-{\omega}_{{x}_{1}}{Z}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,3}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{z}_{1}}+{\omega}_{{x}_{1}}{Y}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,4}^{i}.{\theta}_{{x}_{1}}+{K}_{1,5}^{i}.{\theta}_{{y}_{1}}+{K}_{1,6}^{i}.{\theta}_{{z}_{1}}+\\ {K}_{1,7}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{x}_{2}}-{\omega}_{{z}_{2}}{Y}^{i}+{\omega}_{{y}_{2}}{Z}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,8}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{y}_{2}}-{\omega}_{{x}_{2}}{Z}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,9}^{i}.\left[{U}_{{z}_{2}}+{\omega}_{{x}_{2}}{Y}^{i}\right]+\\ {K}_{1,10}^{i}.{\theta}_{{x}_{2}}+{K}_{1,11}^{i}.{\theta}_{{y}_{2}}+{K}_{1,12}^{i}.{\theta}_{{z}_{2}}\end{array}
$$

The generalised forces on the element are defined for the normal force by simple summation:

$$
{F}_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{f}_{x}^{i}
$$

The tangent matrix is obtained by differentiating the forces with respect to the degrees of freedom. One therefore has, for example:

$$
\frac{\partial {F}_{x}}{\partial {U}_{{x}_{1}}}=\frac{\partial \left(\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{f}_{x}^{i}\right)}{\partial {U}_{{x}_{1}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}\frac{\partial {f}_{x}^{i}}{\partial {U}_{{x}_{1}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{K}_{1,1}^{i}
$$

And:

$$
\frac{\partial {F}_{x}}{\partial {\omega}_{{x}_{1}}}=\frac{\partial \left(\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{f}_{x}^{i}\right)}{\partial {\omega}_{{x}_{1}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}\frac{\partial {f}_{x}^{i}}{\partial {\omega}_{{x}_{1}}}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}\left(-{Z}^{i}.{K}_{1,2}^{i}+{Y}^{i}.{K}_{1,3}^{i}\right)
$$

One therefore sees that the first term of this matrix of the skeleton element is nothing other than the sum of the first terms of the matrix of the “sub-beams” making up the assembly. More generally, the elementary skeleton matrix is a linear combination of the terms of the tangent matrices of the sub-beams.

One will not detail here the content of the whole of the matrix (18x18) whose useful stored part contains 171 terms. The ordering of the degrees of freedom is the following:

$$
({U}_{{x}_{1}},{U}_{{y}_{1}},{U}_{{z}_{1}},{\theta}_{{x}_{1}},{\theta}_{{y}_{1}},{\theta}_{{z}_{1}},{\omega}_{{x}_{1}},{\omega}_{{y}_{1}},{\omega}_{{z}_{1}},{U}_{{x}_{2}},{U}_{{y}_{2}},{U}_{{z}_{2}},{\theta}_{{x}_{2}},{\theta}_{{y}_{2}},{\theta}_{{z}_{2}},{\omega}_{{x}_{2}},{\omega}_{{y}_{2}},{\omega}_{{z}_{2}})
$$

And that of the forces:

$$
({F}_{{x}_{1}},{F}_{{y}_{1}},{F}_{{z}_{1}},{M}_{{x}_{1}},{M}_{{y}_{1}},{M}_{{z}_{1}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{x}_{1}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{y}_{1}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{z}_{1}},{F}_{{x}_{2}},{F}_{{y}_{2}},{F}_{{z}_{2}},{M}_{{x}_{2}},{M}_{{y}_{2}},{M}_{{z}_{2}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{x}_{2}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{y}_{2}},{\mathit{MG}}_{{z}_{2}})
$$

### 6.3 Determination of the internal forces of the element

A constitutive law is used, as within the framework of multi-fibre beam theory, in order to determine stresses on the fibres. One therefore has:

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}=f\left({\epsilon}_{xx}^{i,j},\alpha \right)
$$

Where $i$ is a functional of the strain of the fibre and of internal variables $\alpha$ describing the constitutive law. The normal force in each of the fibres is subsequently obtained by integration of the stresses over the cross-section. Since the latter are constant per fibre:

$$
{f}_{x}^{i,j}={\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{s}^{i,j}
$$

where ${s}^{i,j}$ is the area of the fibre $j$ of a beam ${N}_{p}$ .

One may thus reconstruct the generalised forces of a usual multi-fibre beam from the forces in the fibres constituting it, as done for the multi-fibre beam elements [r3.08.08](doc:r3.08.08). For the normal forces $i$ :

$$
{N}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}
$$

and for the shear forces $i$ :

$$
{V}_{y}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{y}^{i,j}
\textrm{et}
{V}_{z}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{z}^{i,j}
$$

The bending moments of the beam $i$ are written:

$$
{M}_{y}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right)
\textrm{et}
{M}_{z}^{i}=-\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right)
$$

Finally, the torsional moment ${M}_{x}^{i}={G}_{x}^{i}.{\theta}_{x}^{i}$ is computed directly from the stiffness ${G}_{x}$ on the assumption of a purely elastic behaviour. These forces therefore make it possible to establish an equilibrium in the different guide tubes constituting the skeleton.

By introducing the equation  into the principle of virtual work, one obtains:

$$
{\int}_{{V}_{0}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\delta {\epsilon}_{xx}^{i,j}.{\mathit{dV}}_{0}=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\left({N}^{i}.\delta {u}_{x}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{y}^{i}.\delta {\theta}_{y}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{z}^{i}.\delta {\theta}_{z}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{gy}^{i}.\delta {\omega}_{y}^{'}\left(x\right)+{M}_{gz}^{i}.\delta {\omega}_{z}^{'}\left(x\right)\right).\mathit{dx}
$$

With the normal force for the beam $i$ :

$$
{N}^{i}={\int}_{S}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\mathit{dS}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{s}^{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}
$$

The bending moment with respect to $y$ :

$$
{M}_{y}^{i}={\int}_{S}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right).\mathit{dS}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right).{s}^{i,j}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.\left({z}^{i,j}-{Z}^{i}\right)
$$

And the bending moment with respect to $z$ :

$$
{M}_{z}^{i}=-{\int}_{S}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right).\mathit{dS}=-\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right).{s}^{i,j}=-\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.\left({y}^{i,j}-{Y}^{i}\right)
$$

The contribution of the beam $i$ to the bending moment of the grid with respect to $y$ is written:

$$
{M}_{g,y}^{i}={\int}_{S}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{Z}^{i}.\mathit{dS}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{s}^{i,j}.{Z}^{i}=\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.{Z}^{i}={N}^{i}.{Z}^{i}
$$

And the bending moment of the grid with respect to $z$ is written:

$$
{M}_{g,z}^{i}=-{\int}_{S}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{Y}^{i}.\mathit{dS}=-\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{\sigma}_{xx}^{i,j}.{s}^{i,j}.{Y}^{i}=-\sum_{j=1}^{{N}_{f}}{f}_{x}^{i,j}.{Y}^{i}=-{N}^{i}{Y}^{i}
$$

These last two terms feed the generalised grid forces set out hereafter.

The forces in the beams are assembled in order to describe the total force in the skeleton element. The normal force and the shear forces are obtained by summing the individual forces on the beams: these forces are computed by summation of the forces on each beam of the assembly bundle.

One therefore has the normal and shear forces such that:

$$
N=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{N}^{i} \quad {V}_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{V}_{y}^{i} \quad {V}_{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{V}_{z}^{i}
$$

And the torsional and bending moments of the beams:

$$
{M}_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{M}_{x}^{i} \quad {M}_{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{M}_{y}^{i} \quad {M}_{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{M}_{z}^{i}
$$

The beams being rigidly linked to one another by the grids, the normal and shear forces in the beams generate, because of the eccentricity of the guide tubes with respect to the centre of the beam assembly, bending moments. The equilibrium of the forces having to be respected in the sense of the assembly skeleton, three new generalised forces are thus introduced and consist of moments applied to the rigid body. They represent the dual of the additional degrees of freedom presented in the description of the kinematics of the structure.

The torsional and bending moments of the grids therefore take the values:

$$
{M}_{g,x}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{F}_{z}^{i}.{Y}^{i}-\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{F}_{y}^{i}.{Z}^{i} \quad {M}_{g,y}=\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{N}^{i}.{Z}^{i} \quad {M}_{g,z}=-\sum_{i=1}^{{N}_{p}}{N}^{i}.{Y}^{i}
$$

Thus, it is possible to describe the equilibrium in the skeleton with the aid of these nine generalised forces.
