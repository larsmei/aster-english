# v7.20.101 FORMA30 - Hollow thermal elastic cylinder

**Summary:**

This $\mathrm{2D}$ axisymmetric quasi-static test allows you to illustrate the questions related to the thermo-elastoplastic modellings on a simple case:

- for thermal calculation, it highlights the effects of maximum exceedance, instability of the explicit diagram and shows the contribution of the diagonalization of the thermal mass matrix,
- For mechanical calculation, it highlights the stresses due to incompatibility of thermal deformations, even if the cylinder is free, then the incremental aspects of the calculation with STAT_NON_LINE. It also shows the influence of the reference temperature and the temperature of definition of the coefficient of thermal expansion.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure studied is a cylinder slice, modeled in axisymmetric, (cf. HPLA100)

![Figure](/figures/v7.20.101/10006098000069D500005FE9EE2D3CCC8AF7DBBA.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is homogeneous isotropic, linear thermoelastic.

$E={2.10}^{5}M/{\mathit{mm}}^{2}$ ; $\nu =0.3$

The expansion coefficient is a function of the temperature:

$\alpha ={10}^{-5}°{C}^{-1}$ for $T=100°C$ , $\alpha ={10}^{-4}°{C}^{-1}$ for $T=0°C$

The reference temperature is $0°C$ . The thermal coefficients are:

$\lambda =1W/mK$ , $\rho {C}_{p}=1000\mathit{MJ}/{m}^{3}K$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and thermal calculation loads

The cylinder is subjected to exchange on its internal edge with a fluid that passes abruptly from $100°C$ to $0°C$:

- zero stream on $\mathit{AB}$ edges , $\mathit{BC}$ , $\mathit{CD}$
- on the edge $\mathit{AD}$ , convective exchange condition, with:

$H=100W/{\mathit{mm}}^{2}/°C$

$\mathit{Text}=100°C$ at $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , then $0°C$ at $t=\mathrm{0.01s}$ , and then kept constant.

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and mechanical calculation loads

**Conditions of symmetry**

Unbridled case: zero displacement following $\mathit{Oy}$ along the $\mathit{AB}$ side.

Bracket case: zero displacement following $\mathit{Oy}$ along the AB and $\mathit{CD}$ sides.

Loading: thermal expansion.

#### 4.1.1 Thermal calculation

To improve the results of Modelling A, thus to overcome these exceedances of the maximum temperature (see [R3.06.07](doc:r3.06.07)), several solutions are possible:

- time step can be increased, which is not always compatible with the correct apprehension of the speed of the transient (as in the present case),
- or refine the mesh, which is a good solution, but expensive in time calculation,
- The diagonalization of thermal mass matrices, i.e. AXIS_DIAG Modelling, can be used. The curves marked with circles on the figures [Figure 5.1-a] and [Figure 5.1-b] above are obtained. The temperature is always below $100°C$. It is the simplest solution.

If an explicit diagram ($\mathit{THETA}=0$) is used, there is a clear instability for large time step (cross-marking curve in Figure 5.1-a above).

In conclusion, for thermal calculation, an $\mathit{THETA}$ greater than or equal to 0.5 must be used to have a stable diagram whatever the time step. In addition, a time step small enough to grasp the transient must be used, but not too small to avoid oscillations. If they appear, either the mesh must be refined or the AXIS_DIAG Modelling (or PLAN_DIAG, or 3D_DIAG) used.

#### 4.1.2 Free expansion thermoelastic calculation

The calculation is performed with MECA_STATIQUE, using thermal expansion as the only load. With boundary conditions of the unbridled case: zero displacement following $\mathit{Oy}$ along the $\mathit{AB}$ side.

For mechanical calculation, it will be enough to calculate at $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , and $t=\mathrm{11s}$ for example.

The $t=\mathrm{0s}$-time stresses are zero, as the temperature field is uniform ($T=200°C$) and remains compatible. However, the resulting deformations are not zero since the reference temperature is equal to $200°C$.

At $t=\mathrm{11s}$ , or any other positive mechanical moment, we see the so-called thermal compatibility stresses. Indeed, the temperature field is no longer uniform but varies according to $r$ . This produces incompatible deformations, which therefore generate stresses, even for an unbridled cylinder. This situation occurs even for a linear temperature field relative to the radius. On the other hand (see exposed) a linear temperature field relative to the global coordinates does not produce a constraint for an unbridled structure.

#### 4.1.3 Thermoelastic calculation with flange

The calculation with MECA_STATIQUE of the bride case shows the contribution of the flange to the stresses ($\mathit{SIYY}$ in particular): at the moment $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , the reference temperature being equal to $0°C$ , the uniform temperature field causes a uniform stress state $\mathit{SIYY}$ of $\mathrm{200MPa}$ , and at $t=\mathrm{11s}$ , the stress state is different from the unbridled case.

This Modelling is correct, but is limited to linear behaviors.

#### 4.1.4 Thermoplastic calculation with flange

We are looking to do the same calculation as before, but this time with STAT_NON_LINE, with COMPORTEMENT=_F(RELATION=的ELAS) so as not to complicate the problem (another behavior would lead to the same observations).The list of moments provided to STAT_NON_LINE is: $t=\mathrm{0s}$ and $t=\mathrm{11s}$ .

Since the calculation is incremental, the 0-minute moment is considered as the initial moment. It is therefore not calculated, and at the next moment ($t=\mathrm{11s}$ ) the solution due to the increase in load (thermal here) between $\mathrm{0s}$ and $\mathrm{11s}$ is calculated. It is then found that the solution obtained (movements, stresses) is different from the calculation with MECA_STATIQUE. It is logical and consistent with the definition of the incremental calculation, but it is a trap for use. To be remembered: implicitly, STAT_NON_LINE in increment assumes that at the initial moment, the structure is non-stressed, not distorted. This implies that the temperature field must be uniform and equal to the reference temperature.

This is not the case here: at $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , $\mathit{TREF}=0°C$ , and $T=200°C$ . By not calculating this thermal expansion, we assume here that at $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , there is no deformation, and no stress.

#### 4.1.5 Thermoplastic calculation with flange and addition of initial conditions

The list of moments is changed: for example, a preliminary moment is added $t=–\mathrm{1s}$. At this moment, a uniform temperature field is defined, equal to the reference temperature. For this purpose, CREA_CHAMP commands are used, then CREA_RESU to enrich the thermal results data structure with this uniform field. The mechanical calculation is then performed, providing the list of moments: $t=–\mathrm{1s}$ , $t=\mathrm{0s}$ , and $t=\mathrm{11s}$

It is then found that the $t=\mathrm{0s}$ moment is well calculated, and that the stresses are identical to the case calculated with MECA_STATIQUE.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test is related to thermoplasticity formation. It shows the usefulness of choosing DIAG Modelling (diagonized thermal mass matrix) for thermal calculations, and illustrates in incremental thermomechanical (STAT_NON_LINE command) how to properly take into account the initial state.
