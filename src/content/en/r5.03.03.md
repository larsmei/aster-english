# r5.03.03 Taking into account the plane-stress hypothesis in non-linear constitutive behaviours

**Summary:**

This document describes a general method for integrating non-linear constitutive models (elastoplastic, viscoplastic, damaging, ...) in plane stresses.

This is carried out by a static condensation method due to R. de Borst.

This method makes it possible to use the C_PLAN modelling, or the modellings COQUE_3D, DKT and TUYAU, for all the incremental constitutive models of (STAT/DYNA)_NON_LINE available in axisymmetric conditions or in plane strains.

## 1 Introduction

One presents here a general method for integrating non-linear constitutive models (plasticity, viscoplasticity, damage) in plane stresses. If the chosen behaviour is not integrated analytically in plane stresses, the method of R. De Borst is activated automatically for the modellings C_PLAN, DKT, COQUE3D and TUYAU.

## 2 Difficulty of integrating non-linear constitutive behaviours in plane stresses

The modelling C_PLAN (as well as the modellings COQUE_3D, DKT, TUYAU) assumes that the local stress state is plane, that is to say that ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ , $z$ representing the direction of the normal to the surface. The stress and strain tensors therefore have the following form (in C_PLAN):

$\varepsilon =(\begin{array}{ccc}{\varepsilon}_{xx}& {\varepsilon}_{xy}& 0\\ {\varepsilon}_{xy}& {\varepsilon}_{yy}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array})$

$\sigma =(\begin{array}{ccc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{xy}& 0\\ {\sigma}_{xy}& {\sigma}_{yy}& 0\\ 0& 0& {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array})$                                     eq.2-1

**Note:**

*For the shells, one must add terms due to transverse shear (* ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{xzn}}{\sigma}_{yz}$ *), but these are treated elastically and do not enter into the solution of the local behaviour.*

This hypothesis implies that the corresponding strain is a priori indeterminate (unlike the other two-dimensional modellings, in which one makes a hypothesis directly on ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ ). It can be determined only with the aid of the constitutive relation. Now, the condition ${\sigma}_{zz}=0$ is not innocuous for the integration of the behaviour, in which one computes a stress increment $\Delta \sigma$ as a function of the strain increment $\Delta \varepsilon$ supplied by the Newton algorithm. In the case of linear elasticity, taking this condition into account is simple and makes it possible to find:

${\varepsilon}_{zz}=-\frac{\nu}{1-\nu }({\varepsilon}_{xx}+{\varepsilon}_{yy})$

But if the behaviour is non-linear, $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}$ cannot be computed solely from $\Delta u$ and is not deduced simply from the other components of the strain tensor. Taking this hypothesis into account must then be done (when it is feasible) in a manner specific to each behaviour, and very often leads to additional solution difficulties: this is the case in particular for the von Mises behaviour with isotropic hardening [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02). Because of this, many constitutive models are not available in plane stresses.

The method presented here has the great advantage of requiring no particular development in the integration of the behaviour in order to satisfy the plane-stress hypothesis. It can be used as soon as the constitutive model is available in axisymmetric conditions or in plane strains.

## 3 Principle of the treatment of plane stresses by the De Borst method

### 3.1 Reminder of the Newton method

In non-linear statics one is led to solve the following equation ([cf. R5.03.01]):

$R(u)={f}^{int}(u)-{f}^{\text{ext}}(t)\to 0$ ,                             eq. 3.1-1

where ${f}^{\text{ext}}$ is the external force and ${f}^{int}$ the internal force, defined as,

${f}^{\int(u)}=\underset{V}{\int}{B}^{T}\sigma (\varepsilon (u))\mathrm{dV}$ .                         eq. 3.1-2

By using the finite-element method (FEM), the internal forces, ${f}^{int}$ , are obtained from the stress field, which is itself determined, by a constitutive law, from the strain field, the discretised strain tensor, $\varepsilon$ , being defined as:

$\varepsilon =Bu$ .

According to the Newton method one solves the system in [eq. 3.1-1] with the following iterative process:

1. ${R}^{(n)}=R({u}^{(n)})$
2. $\Delta {u}^{(n+1)}=\underset{{K}^{{(n)}^{-1}}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{-(\frac{\partial {R}^{(n)}}{\partial {u}^{(n)}})}}{R}^{(n)}=-{K}^{{(n)}^{-1}}{R}^{(n)}$
3. ${u}^{(n+1)}={u}^{(n)}+\Delta {u}^{(n)}$ , repeat 1) until ${R}^{(n)}<\text{tolérance}$ .

The index $(n)$ means that the variable concerned corresponds to the $n$ -th iteration, called *global,* since it concerns the computation of the displacement field, unlike the process called *local* , which makes it possible to compute the component ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ so that the stresses are plane and which will be detailed below.

The stiffness matrix, ${K}^{(n)}$ , is computed as:

${K}^{(n)}(u)=\underset{V}{\int}{B}^{T}\underset{{D}^{(n)}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{(n)}}{\partial {u}^{(n)}}}}B\mathrm{dV}=\underset{V}{\int}{B}^{T}{D}^{(n)}B\mathrm{dV}$ ,

where ${D}^{(n)}$ is the tangent matrix corresponding to the constitutive law used. In order to extend the Newton method to the use of arbitrary constitutive laws, in [bib1] one proposes to condense the value of ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ so that ${\sigma}_{zz}<\text{tolérance}$ in the converged state. One presents here two variants of this approach: in the original approach, the variables ${u}^{(k+1)}$ and ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(k+1)}$ are corrected simultaneously for each global iteration, whereas in the modified approach a local iterative process is added, so that the condition ${\sigma}_{zz}<\text{tolérance}$ is satisfied for each value of ${u}^{(k+1)}$ and not only for the converged state. It is of interest to use the modified approach in particular when the convergence on the global equilibrium is faster than the convergence for the satisfaction of the plane stresses. In certain cases, but not always, the modified approach can reduce the number of global iterations and thus speed up the analyses. In terms of use, the original approach corresponds to the value of the parameter ITER_CPLAN_MAXI = 1 and the modified approach to ITER_CPLAN_MAXI > 1.

In addition to the computation of ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ at the local level, which leads to modifying the local stresses, the approach known as DEBORST consists above all in acting at the level of the stiffness matrix,

${\hat{K}}^{(n)}(u)=\underset{V}{\int}{B}^{T}{\hat{D}}^{(n)}B\mathrm{dV}$ ,

where $D$ has been replaced by $\hat{D}$ . The computation of $\hat{D}$ as well as of ${\varepsilon}_{zz}$ is detailed below.

### 3.2 Original approach

The idea of the method due to R. de Borst [bib1] consists in treating the plane-stress condition not at the level of the constitutive law but at the level of equilibrium. One thus obtains, in the course of the iterations of the global solution algorithm of STAT_NON_LINE, stress fields which tend towards a plane-stress field as the iterations proceed:

${\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}\to 0$

where $n$ denotes the number of the Newton iteration.

One therefore obtains the plane-stress condition not exactly, but in an approximate manner, at convergence of the Newton iterations, for each computed increment. One checks, as specified below, that the component above is less than a given tolerance.

The method consists in decomposing the fields (of strains or of stresses) into a purely plane part (specified by a “hat”) and a component along $z$ . One then brings out explicitly the “ $zz$ ” components in the expression of the strain and stress tensors:

$\varepsilon =(\begin{array}{c}\hat{\varepsilon}\\ {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array})$     and    $\sigma =(\begin{array}{c}\hat{\sigma}\\ {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array})$ .

as well as in the expression of the tangent operator:

${D}^{(n)}=\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{(n)}}{\partial {\varepsilon}^{(n)}}=(\begin{array}{cc}{D}_{11}^{(n)}& {D}_{12}^{(n)}\\ {D}_{21}^{(n)}& {D}_{22}^{(n)}\end{array})$ .                            eq 3.2-1

Finally, at each Newton iteration one corrects the strain values,

${\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}={\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}+\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}$         and    ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}+\Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}$ .

By using [eq. 3.2-1] one can write,

$(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \hat{\sigma}\\ \Delta {\sigma}_{zz}\end{array})={D}^{(n)}(\begin{array}{c}\Delta \hat{\epsilon}\\ \Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}\end{array})$ , with $\Delta {\sigma}_{zz}={\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}-{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}$ ,

and one obtains:

${\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}\approx {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}+{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}+{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}\Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}\to 0$ ,                    eq 3.2-2

From [eq. 3.2-2] one can condense $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}$ as,

$\Delta {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}=-\frac{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}+{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}$ .                                eq 3.2-3

It is exactly the condensation in [eq 3.2-3] which allows us to use the 2D framework for the solution with the FEM. Finally, one seeks to correct the 2D components (denoted by the index 1), both for the stresses and for the tangent operator, so that the plane-stress condition is satisfied.

The plane-stress algorithm is entirely local, applied within the framework of a global finite-element architecture corresponding to a 2D modelling equivalent to that of plane strain. One places oneself at a Gauss point, where one knows the value of the strain at iteration (n+1),

${\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}={\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}+\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}$ , and one must compute the values of the stress ${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}$ and of the tangent operator, ${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}$ .

Algorithm 1:

1. Update ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}$ ,

${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}-\frac{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}+{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}$

1. Compute the intermediate stresses and the tangent operator ${\sigma}^{(n+1)}$ , ${D}^{(n+1)}$ by the 3D constitutive law, imposing ${\varepsilon}_{xz}={\varepsilon}_{yz}=0$ ,

${\sigma}^{(n+1)}=\sigma ({\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)},{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)})$     and    ${D}^{(n+1)}=D({\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)},{\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)})$

1. Compute the final stress using the intermediate correction of ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(n+1)}$ ,

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(n+1)}=-\frac{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}$                             eq 3.2-4

which allows us to write the final stress ${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}$ as:

${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}={\sigma}^{(n+1)}+{D}_{12}^{(n+1)}\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(n+1)}={\sigma}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{12}^{(n+1)}{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}$

1. Compute the final tangent operator:

${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}={D}_{11}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{12}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}$ .

**Note 1**: *In 2) the stress* $\sigma$ *is computed as a tensor in 3D,*

$\sigma =(\begin{array}{cccccc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{yy}& {\sigma}_{zz}& {\sigma}_{xy}& {\sigma}_{xz}& {\sigma}_{yz}\end{array}{)}^{T}$ *.*

*On the other hand, one uses only* $\sigma =(\begin{array}{cccc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{yy}& {\sigma}_{zz}& {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}{)}^{T}$ *, the components* ${\sigma}_{xz}$ *and* ${\sigma}_{yz}$ *not needing to be extracted. Thus,* $D$ *is of size* $4\times 4$ *.*

**Note 2**: *In 3) the expression of* $\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(n+1)}$ *is obtained by supposing that the extrapolated value of* ${\sigma}_{zz}^{n+2}\approx 0$ *,*

${\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+2)}\approx {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}+{D}_{22}^{(n+1)}\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(n+1)}=0$ *.*

**Note 3**: *In 4) one computes the tangent operator as:*

${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}=\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}+\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}={D}_{11}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{12}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}$ *,*

*where* $\delta$ *means the total derivative, unlike* $\partial$ *which means a partial derivative. In general, this tangent operator is not consistent with respect to* ${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}$ *,*

${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}\ne \frac{\delta {\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}$ *.*

*The consistent tangent operator can be obtained in the following manner:*

$\frac{\delta {\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}=\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{12}}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}\frac{\delta {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}$ *,*

*where*

> $\frac{\delta {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}=\frac{\partial {\sigma}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}+\frac{\partial {\delta}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}={D}_{{}_{11}}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{12}}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}$

> $\frac{\delta {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}=\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}+\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}\frac{\partial {\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}}{\partial {\hat{\epsilon}}^{(n+1)}}={D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}$ *.*

*Finally, one obtains:*

> $\frac{\delta {\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}={D}_{{}_{11}}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{12}}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{12}}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}({D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}})$ *.*

*The operator used,* ${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}$ *, tends towards the consistent tangent operator upon convergence of the plane-stress criterion, since* ${D}^{(n+1)}\to {D}^{(n)}$ *, when* ${\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}\to 0$ *, which therefore leads to:*

${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}\stackrel{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}\to 0}{\to }\frac{\delta {\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}}{\delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)}}$ *.*

### 3.3 Modified approach

In the modified algorithm one proposes to introduce an additional loop with respect to the process described above in order to satisfy the plane stresses better for each global Newton iteration, ($n$ ). This new loop encompasses points 2) and 3) of the algorithm presented in 3.2. Thus the new algorithm is written as:

Algorithm 2:

1. Update ${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}$ ,

${\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n)}-\frac{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(n)}+{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n)}\Delta {\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n)}}$

1. Initialise the loop

${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k=0,n+1)}={\varepsilon}_{zz}^{(n+1)}$

*Start of the loop* $k=0,{K}_{\max}$

1. Compute the intermediate stresses and the tangent operator ${\sigma}^{(k,n+1)}$ , ${D}^{(k,n+1)}$ by the 3D constitutive law,

${\sigma}^{k,(n+1)}=\sigma ({\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)},{\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)})$     and    ${D}^{(k,n+1)}=D({\hat{\varepsilon}}^{(n+1)},{\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n)})$

1. Compute the intermediate correction of ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}$ ,

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}=-\frac{{\sigma}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(k,n+1)}}$     and    ${\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k+1,n+1)}={\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}+\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}$ .

*End the loop* if $\mid {\sigma}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}\mid <{\sigma}_{\text{tol}}$ or if $k={K}_{\max}$ .

(With the parameter ${\sigma}_{\text{tol}}$ one defines the tolerance on the value of ${\sigma}_{zz}$ .)

1. Assign to the 2D stress tensor the converged values:

${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}={\sigma}^{(k,n+1)}+{D}_{12}^{(k,n+1)}\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}={\sigma}^{(k,n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{12}^{(k,n+1)}{\sigma}_{zz}^{(k,n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(k,n+1)}}$

In principle, the second term of the equation above can be omitted, if the parameter of the convergence criterion, ${\sigma}_{\text{tol}}$ , is chosen sufficiently small.

1. Compute the final tangent operator:

${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}={D}_{11}^{(n+1)}-\frac{{D}_{12}^{(n+1)}{D}_{{}_{21}}^{(n+1)}}{{D}_{{}_{22}}^{(n+1)}}$ .

**Note 4**: *In the modified version, the tangent operator,* ${\hat{D}}^{(n+1)}$ *, is consistent with respect to* ${\hat{\sigma}}^{(n+1)}$ *, unlike the operator of the original version (see note 3), since* $\mid {\sigma}_{zz}^{(n+1)}\mid <{\sigma}_{\text{tol}}$ .

## 4 Practical aspects of use

This method is used automatically as soon as the chosen behaviour is not available in plane stresses, for modellings C_PLAN or of shell type: COQUE_3D, DKT, TUYAU. In practice, this increases (automatically) by 4 the number of internal variables of the behaviour.

In order to converge well, it is advisable to update the tangent matrix (if possible, at every iteration: REAC_ITER = 1, or every $n$ iterations, with $n$ small).

This method therefore allows great flexibility of use with respect to the behaviours: it is sufficient that a behaviour be available in axisymmetry or in plane strain for it also to be usable in plane stresses.

As for all the integrations of non-linear constitutive models, it is strongly advisable to give a small convergence criterion (leave the default value at ${10}^{–}6$ .).

The advantage of the modified approach is a better satisfaction of the plane-stress condition at each Gauss point ($\mid {\sigma}_{zz}^{\text{mod}\text{if},(n)}\mid \text{<<}\mid {\sigma}_{zz}^{\text{orig},(n)}\mid$ for each $n$ ). In certain cases, it is indispensable in order to make an analysis converge, in particular for the softening constitutive laws.

On the other hand, because of an additional loop the modified procedure is more costly, especially because the loop includes the call to the “3D constitutive law” module. Nevertheless, the extra cost due to the heavier local computations can be compensated by a gain in the number of global Newton iterations, which is most often lower for the modified algorithm. This gain in the number of global iterations is not guaranteed, which has the consequence that the additional iterative loop is not activated by default (ITER_CPLAN_MAXI=1). One has also observed that, as soon as one chooses ITER_CPLAN_MAXI > 1, it is preferable to use ITER_CPLAN_MAXI > 5.
