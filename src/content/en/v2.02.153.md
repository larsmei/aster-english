# v2.02.153 SDLL153 - Eigenmodes of a thin elastic hose under different boundary conditions

**Summary:**

This test represents a calculation of the eigenmodes of a thin elastic cylindrical hose under different boundary conditions.

It allows to validate elements Modelling finished:

- COQUE_3D with mesh cells TRIA7 and QUAD9.
- DKT with mesh cells TRIA3 and QUAD4.

The obtained modes are compared to a digital reference solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.153/100002010000019A000000EFE4218732F55AD697.png)

Fig. 1. Diagram of pipe geometry

Average radius: $R=0.050m$

Pipe thickness: $h=0.0025m$

Length: $L=1m$

$\vec{x}$ , $\vec{\theta}$ and $\vec{z}$ vectors represent the axial, circumferential and radial directions of the pipe.

The $\vec{x}$, $\vec{\theta}$ and $\vec{z}$ directions are rated by $u$, $v$ and $w$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The Material properties components of the pipe are:

| $E=2.{10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\mu =0.3$ | Poisson's ratio |
| $\rho =7800\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | Density by volume |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

> The eigenmodes are calculated under five different boundary conditions:

- FF: both ends of the pipe are free;
- SF: one end is free and the other is in simple support;
- SS: both ends are in single support;
- CS: one single end support and the other clamped;
- CC: both ends are clamped.

> where F, S and C represent *only-supported* , *clamped* and *free* in English.
> No load.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

> Not applicable

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method and reference results

For the free vibrations of a thin cylindrical pipe, the reference solution retained is that obtained in [bib1] by Haar wavelet discreetization method (HWDM). Goldenveizer-Novozhilov hull theory is adapted to formulate this problem. Theoretically the solution consists of infinite terms in Haar wavelet series. However, finite terms are practically considered in the current calculation and the convergence of results is shown in [bib7] with J=7 (maximum level of resolution).

For the case described in the previous paragraph, the reference frequencies for eigenmodes are given in [bib1] via a frequency parameter, $\lambda$ , as follows ($m$ and $n$ are the wave numbers in the axial and circumferential directions, numerical results are noted in the columns HWDM):

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.153/1000020100000200000001247D449B30DC795E9D.png)

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.153/1000020100000204000000FC72C6B12442DD1656.png)

Fig. 2. Reference results in [bib1]

This frequency parameter $\lambda$ is defined by:

$\lambda =\omega R\sqrt{\rho (1-{\mu}^{2})/E}$

where $\omega$ is the eigenfrequency (unit: rad/s).

Therefore, we can obtain a frequency array in Hz ($f=2\pi \omega$ ) with geometry and Material properties for our case:

## 7 Summary of the results

When comparing simulations between COQUE_3D and DKT, DKT gives more accurate results than COQUE_3D.

Whether it is with COQUE_3D or DKT, it is observed in this test case that the results are very sensitive to the quality of the mesh in triangle (TRIA3/7)

Note:

The first mode ($n$=1 and $m$=1) is still not available regardless of the boundary conditions.
