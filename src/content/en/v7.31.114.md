# v7.31.114 WTNV114 - Water flow over saturated porous medium

**Summary:**

The hydraulic behaviour of a saturated porous medium is studied. Nine modellings are performed: two two-dimensional modellings (modellings A and B) and seven three-dimensional modellings (modellings C, D, E, F, G, H, I).

The distinction between modellings is in the constitutive law fluid (LIQU_SATU for modellings A, C, G, H, I and LIQU_GAZ_ATM for modellings B, D, E, F).

This test consists of applying a water flow on the upper side of the model and studying the effect of this flux on the distribution of fluid pressure in the saturated medium. This is an evolutionary problem.

The first eight models studied are 2D planes (HM_DPQ8) and 3D density (HM_HEXA20 or HM_PYRAM13) with linear behavior. The last model is HM-XFEM.

The reference solution is one-dimensional because it depends only on vertical coordinate.

## 1 Reference problem

The test case study examines the hydraulic behaviour of a saturated porous medium consisting of a single fluid: water in its liquid phase. It is used in the Modelling of HM in *Code_Aster*. The constitutive law of the associated fluid is based on the modellings type LIQU_SATU (modellings A and C) or LIQU_GAZ_ATM(modellings B and D).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is one-dimensional because it depends only on vertical coordinate.

The conservation equation of the ${m}_{l}$ water body is given by the following expression:

$$
\frac{d{m}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}+D{M}_{l}=0
$$

with ${M}_{l}$ the water flow such that, if one neglects gravity:

${M}_{l}={\rho}_{l}{\lambda}_{l}(-\nabla {p}_{l})$ with ${p}_{l}$ liquid pressure, ${\rho}_{l}$ its density and ${\lambda}_{l}$ its hydraulic conductivity such as ${\lambda}_{l}=\frac{{K}_{i}}{\mu}$. ${K}_{i}$ is intrinsic permeability and $\mu$ the viscosity of the liquid.

By considering a solid unformable, one can write that

$\frac{d{m}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}={\rho}_{l}\frac{\phi }{{K}_{l}}\frac{d{p}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}$ with $\phi$ porosity and ${K}_{l}$ compressibility of water. We note $N=\frac{\phi }{{K}_{l}}$ .

The equation then becomes:

${\rho}_{l}N\frac{d{p}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}+D({\rho}_{l}{\lambda}_{l}(-\nabla {p}_{l}))=0$

The following formula is used for variation:

$$
{\int}_{\Omega}N\frac{d{p}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}.\stackrel{ˆ}{{p}_{l}}+{\int}_{\Omega}{\lambda}_{l}\nabla {p}_{l}\nabla \stackrel{ˆ}{{p}_{l}}=-{\int}_{\partial \Omega }\frac{{M}^{\mathrm{ext}}}{{\rho}_{l}}\stackrel{ˆ}{{p}_{l}}
$$

with ${M}^{\mathrm{ext}}$ the external loading.

The solution is set in the one-dimensional case and a single element level 1 discretization is adopted, since in THM Modelling the hydraulic part is treated on linear elements. It is also assumed that the nonlinearities are small in this case and that the $N$ and ${\rho}_{l}$ coefficients are constant, which assumes a relatively small variation in pressure.

Or a linear element:

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.114/Object_103.svg)

The pressure is then written on the basis of the functions of forms such as:

${p}_{l}(z,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{i=2}{p}^{i}(t){\lambda}_{i}(z)$

with

${\lambda}_{1}(z)=0,5(1+\mathrm{2z})$

${\lambda}_{2}(z)=0,5(1-\mathrm{2z})$

The following matrices are then introduced:

$\left[A\right]=\left[{A}_{ij}\right];{A}_{ij}={\int}_{-0,5}^{0,5}{\lambda}_{i}{\lambda}_{j}\mathrm{dz}$

$\left[B\right]=\left[{B}_{ij}\right];{B}_{ij}={\int}_{-0,5}^{0,5}\frac{\partial {\lambda}_{i}}{\partial z}\frac{\partial {\lambda}_{j}}{\partial \mathrm{dz}}\mathrm{dz}$

which leads to

$\left[A\right]=\frac{1}{6}\left[\begin{array}{cc}2& 1\\ 1& 2\end{array}\right]$ et $\left[B\right]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& -1\\ -1& 1\end{array}\right]$

The following are usually noted:

$\lbrace {p}_{l}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}^{1}\\ {P}_{l}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$ $\lbrace {M}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{{M}^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{1}\\ {{M}^{\mathrm{ext}}}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

Since we are injecting a flow of water Q on the upper side, we have

$\lbrace {M}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rbrace =\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\\ -Q\end{array}\right\rbrace$

The equation becomes $\frac{N}{{\lambda}_{l}}\left[A\right]\lbrace \frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}\rbrace +\left[B\right]\lbrace {p}_{l}\rbrace =\frac{-1}{{\lambda}_{l}\rho }\lbrace {M}^{\mathrm{ext}}\rbrace$

Considering that for short time variations Dt, the evolution of p is almost linear, it can be written that

$\lbrace \frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}\rbrace =\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}^{1}-{p}_{0}^{1}\\ {P}_{l}^{2}-{p}_{0}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

And as we leave here from an initial state of zero pressure, we will have

$\lbrace \frac{{\mathrm{dp}}_{l}}{\mathrm{dt}}\rbrace =\frac{1}{\Delta t}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}^{1}\\ {P}_{l}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace$

Finally, the system of two unknown equations is obtained:

${p}_{l}^{1}(\frac{N}{{\lambda}_{l}\Delta t}+6)-{\mathrm{6p}}_{l}^{2}=\frac{-\mathrm{2Q}}{{\lambda}_{l}{\rho}_{l}}$

${p}_{l}^{2}(\frac{N}{{\lambda}_{l}\Delta t}+6)-{\mathrm{6p}}_{l}^{1}=\frac{-\mathrm{4Q}}{{\lambda}_{l}{\rho}_{l}}$

The result is:

$\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{P}_{l}^{1}\\ {P}_{l}^{2}\end{array}\right\rbrace ={\left[\begin{array}{cc}\frac{N}{{\lambda}_{l}\Delta t}+6& -6\\ -6& \frac{N}{{\lambda}_{l}\Delta t}+6\end{array}\right]}^{-1}.\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{-\mathrm{2Q}}{{\lambda}_{l}{\rho}_{l}}\\ \frac{-\mathrm{4Q}}{{\lambda}_{l}{\rho}_{l}}\end{array}\right\rbrace$
