# v6.04.160 SSNV160 - Hydrostatic testing with CAM_CLAY, MCC and CSSM behavior models

**Summary:**

This hydrostatic compression test validates CAM_CLAY [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14), MCC [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48) and [r7.01.48](doc:r7.01.48) elastoplastic laws.
CSSM [r7.01.44](doc:r7.01.49), specific to soil behaviour, with a non-linear elastic regime and hardening plastic regime under this load.

The load is controlled by the average stress and produces elastic and plastic deformations only in volume.
Modellings C and D use CAM_CLAY. Modelling G uses MCC. Modelling F uses CSSM.
For each of the two models, the calculated quantities is compared to an reference solution.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

In Modelling C, the hydrostatic test is performed with a stress state initiated at $\boldsymbol{\sigma}=-P_{trac}\boldsymbol{I}$. $p$ is then increased to $p_1=9$ MPa, before performing a discharge to $P_{trac}$.

In Modelling D, a first elastic calculation is performed up to $p_1=100$ kPa. $p$ is then increased up to $p_2=800$ Pa, finally performing a discharge up to $p_1$.

In Modelling G, $p$ is increased up to $10$ MPa before discharge to $10$ kPa.

In H Modelling, $p$ is increased up to $100$ kPa before discharge to $0.1$ kPa.

> **Note:**
> In Modelling C, an initial compressibility is given as a material parameter, $K_{cam}=6.5$ MPa,
therefore, it is not necessary to initialize the stress field (see [r7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14)).
However, in Modelling D, the initial compressibility value is zero ($K_{cam}=0$ Pa).
It is therefore necessary to initialise the stress state, because in the expression of the hydrostatic stress of the CAM_CLAY law,
for zero density deformation, the stress is not zero.
To initialize this stress, a purely elastic calculation is initially performed by changing the pressure from $0$ to $p_1=100$ kPa.
This calculation is based solely on the stress field at Gauss points.
This stress field resulting from the elastic calculation is considered to be the initial state of the hydrostatic stress required for the CAM_CLAY law of the following calculation.
