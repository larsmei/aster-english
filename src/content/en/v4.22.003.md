# v4.22.003 TTNL03 - Thermo-hydration. Simulation of an adiabatic trial

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the thermo-hydratant behaviour THER_HYDR, simulating an adiabatic test:

A sample of fresh concrete is immersed in a calorimeter, the intake being operated with heat release, it is to recover the field of temperature and hydration over time.

The temperature and degree of hydration are uniform in the sample. The temperature measured in the calorimeter will therefore be reference solution, the hydration being determined by analytical integration of the law of evolution.

2 modellings are proposed: three-dimensional and axisymmetric.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A zero heat flux is imposed on all sides of the solid. The loading is initiated only by a heat source dependent on $\Delta Q={Q}_{0}\Delta h$ hydration.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Initial temperature is $20.9°C$

### 1.5 Time Discretion

The explicit integration of hydration requires fine temporal discretization until the end of hydration:

From $t=0$ to $t=\mathrm{20h}$, $\Delta t=7,5\min$ is 160 steps.

From $t=\mathrm{20h}$ to $t=\mathrm{60h}$, $\Delta t=1h$ is 40 steps.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The temperature reference solution is given by the temperatures measured at each moment during the adiabatic test.

The reference solution for the degree of hydration is calculated analytically based on the temperatures measured by integrating the law of evolution of the degree of hydration $\mathrm{hy}$:

$\Delta \mathrm{hy}=A(h){e}^{\frac{-\mathrm{Ar}}{(T+273.15)}}\Delta t$ , $T$ being expressed in $°C$

## 7 Summary of the results

The error obtained with respect to reference solution is about 1% in terms of temperature and hydration. Let us note that the problem was treated with relatively small time step for the hydration phenomenon which lasts several tens of hours. This refinement is necessary because of the explicit integration of hydration.

The results with and without diagonalization of the thermal mass matrix are identical because the observed phenomenon does not contain a very restrictive transient phase.
