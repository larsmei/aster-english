# v6.04.515 SSNV515 – Traction test with Rankine law

*Summary*

A simple tensile test is performed with *the Rankine law* . The calculated solutions are compared to an analytical solution. Three modellingss are proposed:

- 0D Modelling with SIMU_POINT_MAT;
- 3D modelling;
- 2D axisymmetric modelling;

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.515/10000000000005B30000047DFCD6776F6FFADCA9.jpg)

The single tensile test is performed on a single finite element isoparametric cubic shape $\mathit{CUB}4$. The length of each edge is 1. The different facets of this cube are mesh cells groups named $\mathrm{HAUT}$, $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$, $\mathrm{DERRIERE}$, $\mathrm{DROIT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$. The mesh-cell group *SYM* also contains the mesh cells $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ groups; the mesh-cell group $\mathrm{COTE}$ groups of mesh cells $\mathrm{DERRIERE}$ and $\mathrm{DROIT}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The elastic properties are:

- Young's modulus: $E=1\mathit{MPa}$
- Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0,25$

The tensile limit is equal to ${\sigma}_{t}=1\mathit{kPa}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The single tensile test consists of imposing a vertical elongation on the test piece while maintaining the lateral pressure constant and equal to the initial isotropic stress ${P}_{0}=10\mathit{kPa}$

In the model under consideration, the cubic element represents one eighth of the sample, so the limit conditions are as follows:

- The symmetry conditions:

  - ${u}_{z}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{BAS}$ group
  - ${u}_{x}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ group
  - ${u}_{y}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ group
- Side pressure conditions:

  - ${P}_{n}={P}_{0}=10\mathit{kPa}$ on mesh cells $\mathit{DROIT}$ and $\mathit{ARRIERE}$ groups
- The loading conditions:

  - ${u}_{z}=+1$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{HAUT}$ group

The load is carried out in 30 time step between $t=0$ and $t=30$ during which the displacement imposed on the mesh-cell group $\mathrm{HAUT}$ varies from ${u}_{z}=0$ to ${u}_{z}=0.3$ (total vertical deformation of $30\text{\%}$).

### 1.4 Results

The solutions are post-processed at point $C$ , in terms of:

- vertical stress ${\sigma}_{zz}$;
- horizontal deformation ${{\varepsilon}}_{xx}$;
- ${e}^{P}=\Vert {e}^{P}\Vert$ deviator plastic deformation standard

They are compared to an analytical solution (described in the next paragraph) in terms of maximum *difference between* $t=0$ *and* $t=20$ .

## 2 analytical solution

First, introduce the following notes:

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{c}A=K+\frac{4}{3}G\\ B=K-\frac{2}{3}G\\ C=2\left(K+\frac{G}{3}\right)\end{array}\right.
$$

With $K=\frac{E}{3\left(1-2\nu \right)}$ and $G=\frac{E}{2\left(1+\nu \right)}$ compressibility and shear modules, respectively.

$C$ is the Hooke ealsticity tensor, and we will have ${{\varepsilon}}_{yy}={{\varepsilon}}_{xx}$ hypothesis:

$$
C.d{\varepsilon}=\left\{ \begin{array}{c}Bd{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}+Cd{{\varepsilon}}_{xx}\\ Bd{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}+Cd{{\varepsilon}}_{xx}\\ Ad{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}+2Bd{{\varepsilon}}_{xx}\end{array}\right.
$$

Note to simplify the vertical stress at the moment $+$ ${\sigma}^{+}={\sigma}_{zz}^{+}$ , so that the Rankine criterion is written:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}\le {\sigma}_{t}
$$

The following were also mentioned:

$$
\left\{   \begin{array}{l}   \sigma^{\text{préd}} = \sigma^{-} + n.C.d\varepsilon    \\
\sigma^{+}  = \sigma^{-} + n.C.(d\varepsilon - d\lambda n) = \sigma^{\text{préd}} - \underbrace{d\lambda n.C.n}_{\Delta\sigma_{C}} \end{array} \right.
$$

With $n=\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 1\end{array}\right)$ and where:

$$
d\lambda =\frac{{⟨{\sigma}^{\mathit{préd}}-{\sigma}_{t}⟩}_{+}}{A}
$$

According to the associated flow law, we also:

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{c}d{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}^{P}=d\lambda =d{{\varepsilon}}_{v}^{P}\\ {e}^{P}=\frac{2}{3}d\lambda \end{array}\right.
$$

Like $n=\left(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 1\end{array}\right)$, we get:

$$
\Delta {\sigma}_{C}=d\lambda A
$$

The combination of equations (), () and () gives us the ${\sigma}_{zz}^{+}$ stress. Equations () and () give us the standard of ${e}^{P}$ deviator plastic deformation.

Let's try to get the expression of horizontal *elastic* deformation ${{\varepsilon}}_{xx}^{\text{élas}}$ .

Laterally, we have the condition ${\sigma}_{xx}^{+}={P}_{0}$, i.e.:

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}^{\text{préd}}-\Delta {\sigma}_{xx,C}={P}_{0}
$$

With $\Delta {\sigma}_{xx,C}=d\lambda B$

The equation () is then used to obtain the following:

$$
{\sigma}_{xx}^{-}+Cd{{\varepsilon}}_{xx}^{\text{élas}}+Bd{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}-Bd\lambda ={P}_{0}
$$

Hence the horizontal elastic deformation increment:

$$
d{{\varepsilon}}_{xx}^{\text{élas}}=\frac{{P}_{0}-{\sigma}_{xx}^{-}+B\left(d\lambda -d{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}\right)}{C}
$$

## 6 Modelling D

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics

The D Modelling is performed on a 3D material point with STAT_NON_LINE. The difference from Modelling B is the calculation of the initial state by a thermal loading. To bring the sample to the initial isotropic stress ${P}_{0}=10\mathit{kPa}$ , the sample is brought from 20° to 30° celcius. The movements of the sample are blocked, so that the thermal expansion brings the sample into compression.

${\sigma}_{0}=\frac{E}{9\left(1-2\nu \right)}\alpha \Delta T={P}_{0}$

The following value of the thermal expansion coefficient: $\alpha =\frac{9\left(1-2\nu \right){P}_{0}}{E\Delta T}$
