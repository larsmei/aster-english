# u2.05.05 Structural analysis in vibratory fatigue

## 1 Purpose

The purpose of this document is to describe the implementation of a structural analysis in vibratory fatigue. More precisely, it consists in estimating the maximum admissible vibration amplitude of a structure subjected to a static loading (known) and to a dynamic loading (unknown).

## 2 Industrial context

The purpose of this document is to describe the implementation of a structural analysis in vibratory fatigue. The studies of particular interest are those on rotating machines (turbine blades, for example). These components are loaded by a static loading (centrifugal force linked to the rotation of the machine) and by a dynamic loading (vibrations induced by the fluid flow). The life of the structure depends both on the dynamic part (amplitude of the stress variations) and on the static part (mean stress).

The static loading is generally well known; the dynamic part, for its part, is difficult both to measure and to compute. The classical tools for life assessment, based on the evolution of the stresses with time, therefore cannot be used directly (see the note below).

Conversely, it may be of interest to estimate the maximum variation amplitude admissible by the structure, for one or several vibration eigenmodes. This amplitude can then be compared with the vibrations measured on site (BVM measurements, for example).

The procedure for such a study is the following:

- computation of the stress linked to the static loading;
- computation of the stress associated with the eigenmode considered;
- application of the fatigue criteria in order to compute, at each node of the mesh, the maximum admissible vibration for unlimited endurance of the structure.

The last step can be carried out with the operator CALC_FATIGUE (TYPE_CALCUL = 'FATIGUE_VIBR'). The principle of the computation, its implementation in Code_Aster and an industrial example are described in the following chapters.

**Notes** :

*A certain number of assumptions are introduced for the computation, cf. the following paragraph. The aim here is to estimate, conservatively, an acceptable vibration level. If the time history of the stresses in the structure is known, the classical features of the operators* *CALC_FATIGUE or* *POST_FATIGUE (with uniaxial or multiaxial criteria, cycle-counting methods, …) must be used.*

*Outside turbines, the methodology presented here could also be applied to the vibratory analysis of piping lines (the mean stress then corresponding to the internal pressure), or to wind turbines.*

*The computation requires prior knowledge of the stress at fracture and of the endurance limit of the material.*

## 3 Presentation of the computation method

### 3.1 Framework of the computation and assumptions

One places oneself in the framework of a decomposition of the stress in the structure on a modal basis:

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{total}}(t)={\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}{\alpha}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}\cos({\omega}_{i}t+{\phi }_{i})$ ,

denoting by ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}$ the stress linked to the static loading and by ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ the stresses associated with the eigenmodes of the structure. ${\omega}_{i}$ and ${\phi }_{i}$ are respectively the angular frequency (known) and the phase shift (unknown) of mode i.

The classical computation would consist in evaluating the damage, the modal contributions and the phase shifts being known. Here, conversely, one seeks to estimate the maximum modal contributions associated with unlimited endurance of the structure.

It is not possible to identify in a general way the maximum contribution of each of the modes. One is therefore led to introduce three simplifying assumptions: uniaxial fatigue criterion; a priori imposition of the relative weight of the modes; maximisation of the amplitude.

**Uniaxial fatigue criterion:** the use of a uniaxial fatigue criterion (Wöhler method) amounts to assuming that the principal directions of the static loading and of the dynamic loading are the same. This assumption seems admissible for the usual structures aimed at (blades, piping lines, …); it induces a conservatism which is no doubt excessive in the general case. Throughout the remainder, the notation $\sigma$ will correspond to the norm of the stress (von Mises or Tresca).

It should be noted that a multiaxial approach (with criteria of Crossland or Dang Van type) would also be possible. These criteria do however require knowledge, in addition to the tensile endurance limit of the material, of the endurance limit in pure shear. This datum is often unavailable for the materials of power plants, and the feature would then have been difficult to use.

**Relative weight of the modes:** it is assumed that the relative weight of the various eigenmodes considered is known. This relative weight can for example be estimated from on-site measurements. Only a limited number of eigenmodes $N$ is also considered (in practice, one will most often have $N<4$ or 5). In other words: ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{total}}(t)={\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}+\alpha \sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}\cos({\omega}_{i}t+{\phi }_{i})$ .

The coefficient $\alpha$ is the parameter which one seeks to compute.

**Maximisation of the amplitude:** in the expression for the stress above, the phase shifts ${\phi }_{i}$ are unknown. The alternating stress ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}$ , defined as the half-amplitude of the variation of the stress, is then computed as follows: ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}=\alpha \sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ . This definition of ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}$ is conservative.

### 3.2 Computation of the admissible vibrations

Under the assumptions introduced above, the computation reduces to identifying the coefficient $\alpha$ associated with unlimited endurance of the structure.

The static stress ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}$ corresponds here to a mean stress, generally non-zero. It is necessary to take this stress into account in the Wöhler fatigue curve. This is classically done by means of the Haigh diagram [r7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01), either with the Goodman line or with the Gerber parabola.

Denoting by ${S}_{l}$ the endurance limit and by ${S}_{u}$ the fracture limit of the material, one has:

- with the Goodman line:

${S}_{\mathit{alt}}^{\max}={S}_{l}(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{stat}}}{{S}_{u}})$ , that is $\alpha ={S}_{l}(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}}{{S}_{u}})/\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ .

- with the Gerber parabola:

${S}_{\mathit{alt}}^{\max}={S}_{l}(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{stat}}^{2}}{{S}_{u}^{2}})$ , that is $\alpha ={S}_{l}(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{stat}}^{2}}{{{S}_{u}}^{2}})/\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathit{mod}}^{i}$ .

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.05/100004A600001E6300000E5E50450299226B2025.svg)

*Haigh diagram*

This computation is carried out for all the nodes or Gauss points of the structure. The zones where $\alpha$ is smallest correspond to the zones which limit the life of the structure.

To pass from the coefficient $\alpha$ to the admissible vibration amplitude, an additional operation must be carried out. This operation is described in u2.05.05-interpretation-resultats.

## 4 Implementation of the computation

### 4.1 Computation of the static and dynamic stresses

Estimation of the admissible vibration level requires the prior computation of the static and dynamic stresses:

- the result corresponding to the static loading (centrifugal force, pressure, …) can be computed with MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE. A single time step must be present in the result data structure. The computation of the stresses ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}$ from the displacement field is carried out by means of the command CALC_CHAMP (option SIEQ_ELGA or SIEQ_ELNO according to whether one wishes to compute the damage at the nodes or at the Gauss points).
- The computation of the $N$ eigenmodes considered can be carried out with the command CALC_MODES. The computation of the modal stresses ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ is carried out by means of the command CALC_CHAMP (option SIEQ_ELGA or SIEQ_ELNO according to whether one wishes to compute the damage at the nodes or at the Gauss points).

### 4.2 Definition of the material properties

Two material parameters are required for the computation:

- the value of the fracture limit of the material ${S}_{u}$ . This parameter must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [u4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) (factor keyword RCCM, operand Su).
- the endurance limit of the material ${S}_{l}$ . This parameter corresponds to the first point of the Wöhler curve (operator DEFI_MATERIAU, keyword FATIGUE, operand WOHLER).

### 4.3 Call to CALC_FATIGUE

The use of CALC_FATIGUE (TYPE_CALCUL = 'FATIGUE_VIBR') requires the following parameters to be supplied:

- the static and modal stresses computed beforehand;
- the material properties;
- the list of the modes to be considered (NUME_MODE) and their relative weight (FACT_PARTICI), corresponding to the coefficients ${({\beta}_{i})}_{1\le i\le N}$ ;
- the choice of the manner of taking the mean stresses into account (Gerber or Goodman: operand CORR_SIGM_MOYE);
- the choice of the place at which the damage is computed: at the nodes (OPTION='DOMA_ELNO_SIGM') or at the Gauss points (OPTION='DOMA_ELGA_SIGM').

### 4.4 Interpretation of the results

On exit from the operator CALC_FATIGUE, one has a field (at the nodes or at the Gauss points) of the admissible value of $\alpha$ : the zones where $\alpha$ is smallest correspond to the zones which limit the life of the structure.

To pass from the coefficient $\alpha$ to the admissible vibration amplitude, an additional operation must be carried out. Suppose for example that one is interested in the amplitude of the displacements at a given point, which will be denoted $\partial \tilde{u}$ . This point may correspond for example to the position of a sensor, or to the zone of maximum vibration amplitude.

One denotes by ${\tilde{u}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ the displacement at the point of interest associated with mode i. The admissible vibration amplitude at the point of interest is then:

$\partial \tilde{u}=\min(\alpha )\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\tilde{u}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$

This computation is illustrated in the example below.

The minimum value of $\alpha$ can be obtained in three different ways:

- either by post-processing the result field in Aster (POST_RELEVE_T, OPERATION='EXTREMA');
- or by visualising the result field;
- or in the message file. The information is printed as follows:

```text
Amplitude de vibration maximale admissible par la structure : 1.318438
```

### 4.5 Notes and advice

A few notes may be made on this feature:

- The result of the computation is necessarily very sensitive to the quality of the stress computation. The user must therefore make sure that the fineness of the mesh is sufficient. The use of quadratic elements is indispensable.
- Likewise, it is advisable to carry out the computation at the Gauss points, the interpolation for the passage from Gauss points to nodes being a source of inaccuracy.
- The critical zones most often correspond to geometric singularities. The user will have to check whether the singularity corresponds to a physical reality or to too coarse a discretisation. It should be noted that the use of remeshing (software Homard, operator MACR_ADAP_MAIL) does not make it possible to converge towards a stable non-zero solution if the initial geometry is discretised too coarsely.

## 5 Example

### 5.1 Description of the analysis case

The vibratory-fatigue post-processing will be illustrated on the case of the blades of blade stages 5–12 of the low-pressure turbines of PWR plants. The blades are fixed to the disc by fingers and pins. The outside diameter of the blading is of the order of 2 metres.

The material properties adopted for this study are the following:

- endurance limit: ${S}_{l}=400\mathit{MPa}$
- fracture limit: ${S}_{u}=800\mathrm{MPa}$ .

The static loading corresponds to the rotation of the wheel at $1500\mathit{tours}/min$ . Three of the first modes correspond respectively to tangential bending ($f=100\mathit{Hz}$ ), to axial bending ($f=\mathrm{180 }\mathit{Hz}$ ) and to torsion ($f=360\mathit{Hz}$ )

**Note**

The material properties and the loadings used here do not correspond to the real case.

### 5.2 Results obtained

One is interested here in the maximum admissible vibration amplitude at the blade tip (node group 'NO_CAPT', corresponding to the on-site position of the BVM sensor), so that the life in the first finger of the first blade of the blade packet is infinite (mesh-cell group called 'DOI_1_1').

- *Results from CALC_FATIGUE:*

The call to CALC_FATIGUE, for a computation of the damage at the Gauss points for the first mode of the structure, is made as follows:

> DMG_MOD1= CALC_FATIGUE( TYPE_CALCUL = 'FATIGUE_VIBR',
> OPTION = 'DOMA_ELGA_SIGM',
> HISTOIRE = _F( RESULTAT = PRECONT,
> MODE_MECA = MODE,
> NUME_MODE=1,
> FACT_PARTICI = 1,),
> DOMMAGE = 'WOHLER',
> CORR_SIGM_MOYE = 'GOODMAN',
> MATER = MATE,
> )

Then, in order to extract the minimum coefficient $\alpha$ on the zone of interest:

> IMPR_RESU(FORMAT='RESULTAT', RESU=_F(CHAM_GD=DMG_MOD1,GROUP_MA='DOI_1_1'),);

The results obtained, for the first three modes of the structure and for a combination of the first two modes, are given in the following table.

|  | Mode 1 (tangential bending) | Mode 2 (axial bending) | Mode 3 (torsion) | Mode 1 + 0,5 Mode 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum static stress | 685 MPa |  |  |  |
| Maximum modal stress | 13 MPa | 51 MPa | 27 MPa |  |
| ${\alpha}_{\mathrm{Gerber}}^{\min}$ | 1.8 | 0.8 | 1.3 | 1.5 |
| ${\alpha}_{\mathrm{Goodman}}^{\min}$ | 3.3 | 1.5 | 2.3 | 2.8 |

- *Use of the results:*

An additional operation must be carried out in order to pass from the coefficient $\alpha$ to the admissible vibration amplitude at a given node. The node considered here corresponds to the position of one of the displacement sensors.

This operation can for example be carried out by simple Python loops:

> # Paramètres du calcul (les mêmes que dans CALC_FATIGUE)
> fact_partici = [1., 0.5]
> nume_mode = [1,2]
> nbmode = len(nume_mode)
> # EXTRACTION DES DEPLACEMENTS DE LA TABLE
> TDEP = POST_RELEVE_T(ACTION=_F( GROUP_NO='NO_CAPT',

> INTITULE='Depl. modal',
> RESULTAT=MODE,
> NOM_CHAM='DEPL',
> TOUT_CMP='OUI',
> OPERATION='EXTRACTION',) ,) ;

> DX = [None]*nbmode
> DY = [None]*nbmode
> DZ = [None]*nbmode
> tdepl = TDEP.EXTR_TABLE()
> for i in range(nbmode) :

> dmod = tdepl.NUME_MODE==nume_mode[i]
> DX[i] = dmod['DX'].values()['DX']
> DY[i] = dmod['DY'].values()['DY']
> DZ[i] = dmod['DZ'].values()['DZ']

> # EXTRACTION DU COEFFICIENT AMIN
> TDMG12=POST_RELEVE_T(ACTION=_F( GROUP_NO='ELTS',

> INTITULE='(MODE 1) + 0,5 * (MODE 2)',
> CHAM_GD=D_MOD12,
> TOUT_CMP='OUI',
> OPERATION='EXTREMA',) ,) ;

> AMIN = TDMG12.EXTR_TABLE()
> AMIN = AMIN.EXTREMA=='MIN'
> AMIN = AMIN['VALE'].values()['VALE']
> # CALCUL DU DEPLACEMENT MAXIMAL ADMISSIBLE
> DXmax = 0.
> DYmax = 0.
> DZmax = 0.
> for i in range(nbmode) :

> DXmax = DXmax + AMIN[0] * fact_partici[i] * abs(DX[i][0])
> DYmax = DYmax + AMIN[0] * fact_partici[i] * abs(DY[i][0])
> DZmax = DZmax + AMIN[0] * fact_partici[i] * abs(DZ[i][0])

> Dtot = sqrt(DXmax*DXmax + DYmax*DYmax +DZmax*DZmax ).
