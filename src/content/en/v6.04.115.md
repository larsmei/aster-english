# v6.04.115 SSNV115 - Corrugated sheet in nonlinear behaviour

**Summary:**

This problem validates the constitutive law elastoplastic with Von criterion isotropic linear nutring for modellings [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) plates and [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04) volume hulls where membrane and bending effects are also important.

The geometry of the model respects 3 constraints:

- the thickness is low to respect the thin shell hypothesis,
- the problem must be flat deformation according to $\mathrm{Oz}$,
- $\mathrm{Oy}$ curvature is chosen to make both "flexion" and "membrane" meaningful.

There is no analytical solution. Modelling A (2D D_PLAN) serves as a reference. The test has no physical significance and the values of the displacements obtained are very important in relation to the dimensions of the initial structure. This test is therefore more a test of non-regression and of inter-modellings comparison.

The results (on displacement) differ from 2 to 3% between the modellings plate and the 2D reference. This difference reduces to 0.5% between the modellings volume shells and the 2D reference.

## 5 Modelling B

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.115/Object_8.svg)

We're looking for an independent $z$ movement; one "range" of triangular elements is sufficient.

**Cutting**: 20 quadrangles=> 40 DKT triangles. DKT Modelling.

The thickness of the elements is divided into 17 layers for the nonlinear [R3.07.03](doc:r3.07.03) calculation. Each layer has 3 points of integration into the upper skin layer, in the middle of each layer and in the lower skin layer. The model studied here therefore includes 15 points of integration into the thickness of the plate.

## 9 Summary of the results

The reference solution *Aster* 2D flat deformation is found to be adequate for modellings in bulk hulls. the difference on point movements of deflection on the initial geometry is less than 1%. the difference with linear inshell modelling is about 1.5% on the deflection maximum sheet metal estimate. This difference becomes more important for modellings in elements plates that do not take into account the curvature of corrugated sheet metal. The relative error on the deflection maximum estimate does not seem to be wanting to go down below 3%, even by increasing the number of layers to improve the integration of plasticity in the thickness of l的element. As such, it is noted that an increase in the number of layers in the thickness allows to improve the $\mathrm{DX}$ displacement estimate at point, where deflection is maximum without taking into account the quality of the various models without improving the difference between the two, and the difference in the total of the models being investigated.
