# v7.31.149 WTNV149 – Contact at a cohesive interface junction for hydromechanical cases

**Summary:**

This is an analytical validation test of the correct consideration of contact in the presence of a junction of cohesive interfaces for the hydromechanical case coupled with the extended finite elements (XFEM) method. The test being purely mechanical, all degrees of freedom associated with the fluid phase are blocked at 0. Modelling A is two-dimensional while Modelling B is three-dimensional.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry of the problem

This block is a block of $\mathit{LZ}=10m$ height, $\mathit{LX}=10m$ length and $\mathit{LY}=2m$ width. This block has two cohesive interface types of discontinuities (unmesh interface introduced into the model as a level-set curve thanks to the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator). The first one is identified by the normal level-set of ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}=Z-\mathrm{0.5X}-0.2$ equation and crosses the block entirely in the horizontal direction. The second interface is identified by the normal level-set of ${\mathit{lsn}}_{2}=Z+\mathrm{0.5X}+0.2$ equation. It connects to the lower lip of the first interface. The second interface therefore exists only in the part of the block such as ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}<0$. The junction curve between the two interfaces checks ${\mathit{lsn}}_{1}={\mathit{lsn}}_{2}=0$ and has for $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}X=-0.4\\ Z=0\end{array}$ equation. The domain is thus cut into 3 blocks, a lower block, a higher block and an intermediate block between the two interfaces. The $A(6,0,5.7)$ points, $A'(6,2,5.7)$ $B(6,0,4,3)$ , $B'(6,2,4.3)$ , $C(4,0,4.7)$ and $C'(4,2,4.7)$ will be used for the $C'(4,2,4.7)$ tested.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

*Case 2D*

The conditions of Dirichlet are:

- the following $x$ moves are blocked in the right edge and imposed equal to $U={10}^{-4}m$ on the left edge of the domain,
- the following $y$ moves are blocked on the lower side and imposed equal to $-U=-{10}^{-4}m$ on the upper edge of the domain.

* 3D cas*

The conditions of Dirichlet are:

- the following $x$ moves are blocked in the right edge and imposed equal to $U={10}^{-4}m$ on the left edge of the domain,
- the following $z$ moves are blocked on the lower side and are imposed equal to $-U=-{10}^{-4}m$ on the upper edge of the domain,
- the following $y$ moves are blocked throughout the domain.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The bar is subjected to isotropic compression. The interfaces that cross the block are cohesive so the bar should behave as an uncracked bar. Moreover, the material is elastic and is expected to observe the isotropic compression of an elastic material.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The movements are then given by:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}=(x)=U(1-\frac{x}{\mathit{LX}})\\ {u}_{y}(y)=0\\ {u}_{z}(z)=-U\frac{z}{\mathit{LZ}}\end{array}$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

None, it's a analytical solution.

## 5 Conclusion

For D_PLAN_HM Modelling and 3D_HM Modelling, the results are consistent with analytical solution. For the multi-Heaviside cohesive elements HM-XFEM, the following features are now validated:

- MODI_MODELE_XFEM
- POST_CHAM_XFEM

In the presence of a junction of cohesive interfaces, the cracked bar behaves in compression as an uncracked bar. The contact is therefore well considered in each interface branch and at the junction level.
