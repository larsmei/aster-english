# v6.04.177 SSNV177 - Willam Test with ENDO_ORTH_BETON Law

**Summary:**

The Willam test applied to the constitutive law anisotropic unilateral for the behaviour of ENDO_ORTH_BETON concrete (see [R7.01.09](doc:r7.01.09)) developed in [Bib 1] is presented here. It is a tensile-switch test that allows the rotation of the clean damage marker in the case of an unproportional load.

## 1 Presentation of Willam's test

The objective of the test proposed by Willam et al. (see [Bib 2]) is to observe the model's response when the proper load marker turns. This test is purely theoretical and it is unlikely that experimental results will ever be available for this test, given the difficulties associated with the study of the fracture of the tensile concrete. Its major interest is to compare the influence of the description of damage anisotropy on the material response to the isotropic models.

This test is the succession of two loading phases:

  - First phase: single traction. $\Delta {\varepsilon}_{xx}$ is imposed up to nonlinearity, i.e. the beginning of traction damage.
  - Second phase: starting from the deformation state at the end of the first phase, two tensile loads and one shear load are imposed in the tensile loading plane with the following proportionality coefficients:

$(\Delta {\varepsilon}_{xx},\Delta {\varepsilon}_{yy},\Delta {\varepsilon}_{xy})=(0.5,0.75,0.5)$ .

Figure 1-a shows the evolution of the different stress components as a function of ${\varepsilon}_{xx}$ deformation. Four phases can be distinguished in the behaviour:

1. Phase 1 is the elastic phase under uniaxial loading.
2. Phase 2 is the growth of damage in a particular direction. The stress ${\sigma}_{xx}$ decreases as damage first becomes less on the $x$ axis. The stress ${\sigma}_{yy}$ does not increase linearly, indicating that the proper damage marker turns.
3. Phase 3 begins when the damage is total in one direction. The proper damage marker is then blocked as shown in Figure 1-b, which shows the evolution of the angle between the clean damage marker and the initial mark (as well as the angle between the proper deformation mark and the initial mark).
4. Phase 4 then begins when damage in the orthogonal direction to the senitiated blocked direction is observed, and the stress of the growth of damage is softened.

**Figure** 1-a **Strengthening constraints for Willam test**

Figure 1-b also shows that the proper damage marker does not rotate in the same way as the proper deformity marker. This is due to the fact that the law of evolution of damage is not written in relation to deformations but in relation to thermodynamic forces, which depend on both the state of deformation and damage. Since the Willam test is a theoretical test, it is difficult to make a precise judgement on this prediction of the ENDO_ORTH_BETON model.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.177/1000000000000318000002648D1D91417A0AA77F.jpg)

**Figure** 1-b **Evolution of angles of proper markers of deformations** **and damage** **from the initial mark**

The behaviour obtained with our model for the Willam test appears to be characteristic of what can be expected from anisotropic models (see [Bib 3]). The main difference, compared to Carol's results [1999], is the blocking of the clean marker which is observed with the ENDO_ORTH_BETON model when damage is complete in a direction, which does not appear in [Bib 3].

### 2.1 Geometry and boundary conditions

L的element used is a tetrahedron to a gauss point. There is therefore no problem of homogeneity of the fields in the element.

The blocking conditions and linear relationships between the nodes to be applied are summarized in [Figure 3]. The $\mathit{N0N1}$ , $\mathit{N0N2}$ , and $\mathit{N0N3}$ edges are 1.

Given the geometry of the element, blocking conditions and linear relationships, deformation is directly related to the displacements of the nodes:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\mathit{DX}(\mathit{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{yy}=\mathrm{DY}(\mathrm{N2})$

${\varepsilon}_{zz}=\mathrm{DZ}(\mathrm{N3})$

${\varepsilon}_{xy}=\mathrm{DX}(\mathrm{N2})=\mathrm{DY}(\mathrm{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{xz}=\mathrm{DX}(\mathrm{N3})=\mathrm{DZ}(\mathrm{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{yz}=\mathrm{DY}(\mathrm{N3})=\mathrm{DZ}(\mathrm{N2})$

With the required deformation, it is sufficient to impose the appropriate nodes displacements.

### 4.4 Tested quantities and results

The non-regression test is performed on the value of the rotation angles of the proper marks of the deformation and the damage tensor.

For this purpose, the deformation (EPSI_ELGA) and damage (VARI_ELGA) fields are extracted at moment 2, and the matrices (in python) are created for the deformation and damage tensors. Then, the Python LinearAlgebra library is used to calculate the own vectors of the matrices associated with the deformation and damage tensors. Finally, the rotation angle of these vectors is calculated from the initial marker.

## 5 Summary of the results

The Willam test is a simple test to compare the influence of the description of ENDO_ORTH_BETON damage to the material response to isotropic models. It also allows to observe the model response when the proper load marker turns. Thus, on the tested values, it is noted that the damage does not rotate in the same way as the deformation tensor. Moreover, the angle of rotation of the tensor damage reaches a plateau when the damage is complete in one direction, which can be associated with the creation of a macro-crack.
