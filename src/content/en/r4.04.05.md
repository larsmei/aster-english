# r4.04.05 Elasto-viscous constitutive model META_LEMA_ANI with account taken of the metallurgy for the cladding tubes of the fuel rod

**Summary:**

With a view to carrying out 3D analyses of the fuel cladding in an accidental situation of APRP type, the MMC department has formulated, for Zircaloy tubes, an elastoviscous constitutive model, without a threshold, anisotropic, and taking into account the effect of the alpha–beta phase transformation on the mechanical behaviour.

This model, available in *Code_Aster* under the name META_LEMA_ANI, and its solution algorithm are described here. It is available in 3D, plane strain and axisymmetry.

The Hill anisotropy matrix may be supplied either in Cartesian coordinates or in the cylindrical frame associated with the tube. To date, it has been assumed that the axial axis $z$ of the cylindrical frame associated with the tube corresponded to that of the global frame. Consequently, if several tubes are to be modelled, or if the axis of the tube does not correspond to that of the global frame, the model is not correct. In the long term, this restriction ought to be lifted.

The rate equations are integrated numerically by an implicit Euler scheme. The system of equations obtained is solved by the Newton method.

**Contents**

## 1 Context

During the first phase of a design-basis accident of APRP type (loss of primary coolant accident), the fuel rods are subjected to a rapid rise in temperature, to phase transformations of the material and to changes in the mechanical boundary conditions. The study of an APRP accident requires a good knowledge of the behaviour of the cladding of the fuel rods during the first phase, known as “ballooning–burst”. In particular, it is necessary to have a good idea of the total strain of the cladding.

Models of phase transition, of mechanical behaviour and of failure have been developed, and identified on the basis of experimental tests.

For the metallurgical part, Zircaloy undergoes metallurgical transformations between $700°C$ and $1000°C$, in which it passes from a phase of hexagonal close-packed structure (cold alpha phase, or $\alpha$) to a phase of cubic structure (hot beta phase, or $\beta$). The equations governing the kinetics of the transformations on heating ($\alpha$ transforms into $\beta$) and on cooling ($\beta$ transforms into $\alpha$) will be found in [R4.04.04](doc:r4.04.04).

For the mechanical part, the model is written in one dimension, in the circumferential direction of the tube. Even if this model is consistent with the experiments, it cannot be used to carry out 3D finite-element analyses. Furthermore, zirconium alloys exhibit an anisotropic behaviour, at least in the $\alpha$ phase, which cannot be taken into account in a 1D model. From the point of view of the loading, the tube undergoes an azimuthal temperature gradient, leading to gradients of circumferential strain, but also of axial strain, which it is important to take into account in order to obtain a response consistent with experiment.

This is why the MMC department has formulated a 3D model to describe the behaviour of Zircaloy cladding in the case of an analysis of APRP type. The Norton laws describing the 1D behaviour of the material in the various domains $(\alpha ,\alpha -\beta ,\beta )$ are replaced by Lemaitre laws, with account taken of the anisotropy of the $\alpha$ phase. The $\beta$ phase is isotropic. The $\alpha -\beta$ domain is assumed to present an anisotropy proportional to the proportion of the $\alpha$ phase present.

The numerical implementation of this model, available in *code_aster* under the name META_LEMA_ANI, and its solution algorithm are described here. It is available in 3D, plane strain and axisymmetry.

## 2 The relation META_LEMA_ANI in Code_Aster

### 2.1 General points

The model presented is elastoviscous, without a threshold (the yield limit is zero), with account taken of the metallurgical transformations of this material (described in document R4.04.04) and with account taken of the anisotropy of the phase. The viscosity is described by a law of Lemaitre type.

The model is introduced into *Code_Aster* in 3D, plane strain (D_PLAN) and axisymmetry (AXIS) under the name META_LEMA_ANI.

Account is taken of the anisotropy by a fourth-order tensor (Hill matrix $M$) affecting the evolution laws of the viscous strain and the equivalent stress (von Mises stress in the Hill sense).

The rate equations are integrated numerically by an implicit Euler scheme. The system obtained is solved by the Newton method.

### 2.2 Restriction on the use of the model

The equations of the model may be written either in Cartesian coordinates or in the cylindrical frame associated with the tube $(1={e}_{r},2={e}_{\theta},3=z)$. This is because the coefficients of the Hill matrix, $M$, are known in this frame.

At the level of the implementation in *code_aster*, a change of variables of the tensor fields is performed in this case (another choice would have been to apply the change of variable to the Hill tensor $M$, but it is simpler to proceed in the opposite way)

- For a 3D analysis or an analysis in plane strain, the stress tensor known in the global frame $(1=x,2=y,3=z)$ is transformed into the local frame $(1={e}_{r},2={e}_{\theta},3=z)$;
- For a 2D, axisymmetric analysis, the stress tensor known in the global frame $(1={e}_{r},2=z,3={e}_{\theta})$ is computed in the local frame $(1={e}_{r},2={e}_{\theta},3=z)$; in this case, the change of variables is simple, since it consists only in interchanging the indices 2 and 3.

**Limitation: it has been assumed that the axis** $z$ **of the cylindrical frame associated with the tube corresponded to that of the global frame.** **If several tubes are to be modelled, or if the axis of the tube does not correspond to that of the global frame,** **it is at present necessary to use a script stored in the test case hsnv134b in order to take this difference into account.**

### 2.3 Use

In the operator STAT_NON_LINE, this mechanical model is accessed by using the keyword RELATION='META_LEMA_ANI' in the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT.

The material data relating to the model META_LEMA_ANI are supplied in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU by using the factor keywords META_LEMA_ANI.

**Note**: **the Hill matrices for the phases** $\alpha$ **and** $\beta$ **are given in the cylindrical frame** $(1={e}_{r},2={e}_{\theta},3=z)$ **, even for a 2D axisymmetric analysis in which the indices 2 and 3 are interchanged.**

### 2.4 Internal variables

The internal variables of the model META_LEMA_ANI are:

$V1\to \mathit{VN}$: the components of the symmetric tensor of the elastic strains ($N$ equals 6 in 3D and 4 in 2D)

$\mathit{VN}+1$: cumulative viscous strain $p$

$\mathit{VN}+2$: proportion of beta phase ${Z}_{\beta}$

$\mathit{VN}+3$: thermal strain ${\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}$

$\mathit{VN}+4$: equivalent Hill stress ${A}_{\mathit{eq}}$

$\mathit{VN}+5,+6,+7$: viscous stresses ${\sigma}_{v1}$, ${\sigma}_{v2}$ and ${\sigma}_{v3}$, of the pure $\alpha$ phase, the $\alpha \beta$ phase and the $\beta$ phase respectively

$\mathit{VN}+8$: phase-change indicator (0 or 1)

$\mathit{VN}+9$: time instant at which the temperature equals TDEQ (initialised to 0 at the start of the analysis)

$\mathit{VN}+10$: time instant at which the temperature equals TFEQ (initialised to 0 at the start of the analysis)

## 4 Presentation of the model META_LEMA_ANI

In what follows, the equations of the model are presented in a “generic” frame (1,2,3), which represents either the Cartesian frame (Ox,Oy,Oz) or the cylindrical frame $(1={e}_{r},2={e}_{\theta},3=z)$ associated with the cladding of axis $z$.

### 4.1 Metallurgical phases

From a purely metallurgical point of view, Zircaloy comprises two phases, the cold phase $\alpha$ and the hot phase $\beta$, which may be present simultaneously, while satisfying the condition

, where

![Figure](/figures/r4.04.05/Object_26.svg)

and

![Figure](/figures/r4.04.05/Object_27.svg)

represent the proportions of phase $\alpha$ and of phase $\beta$, respectively.

From a mechanical point of view, three phases are considered for the material parameters of the mechanical model: phase 1 = pure $\alpha$ phase, phase 2 = $\alpha \beta$ mixture and phase 3 = pure $\beta$ phase. This is why three indices appear in the equations below. The three phases are distinguished in the following way:

- If $0\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,01$ then phase 3 is the $\beta$ phase
- If $0,01\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,1$ then phase 3 is the $\beta$ phase and phase 2 is the mixed $\alpha \beta$ phase (linear rule of mixtures)
- If $0,1\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,9$ then phase 2 is the mixed $\alpha \beta$ phase
- If $0,9\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,99$ then phase 1 is the $\alpha$ phase and phase 2 is the mixed $\alpha \beta$ phase (linear rule of mixtures)
- If $0,99\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 1,00$ then phase 1 is the $\alpha$ phase

### 4.2 Equations of the model

The strains are partitioned into elastic ${\epsilon}^{e}$, thermal ${\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}$ and viscous ${\epsilon}^{v}$ parts:

$$
\epsilon ={\epsilon}^{e}+{\epsilon}^{\mathit{th}}\mathit{Id}+{\epsilon}^{v}
$$

For the stress–strain relation, the deviatoric part is separated from the spherical part:

$$
\sigma =\stackrel{~}{\sigma}+\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{\mathit{pp}}\mathit{Id}
$$

And one has:

$$
\stackrel{~}{\sigma}=2\mu \left(\stackrel{~}{\epsilon}-{\epsilon}^{v}\right)
$$

With the flow rule for the viscous strain such that:

$$
{\dot{\epsilon}}^{v}=\dot{p}\frac{M:\sigma }{{\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}}
$$

with the equivalent stress in the Hill sense defined by:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}=\sqrt{\sigma :M:\sigma }
$$

The Hill anisotropy matrix, $M$, has the form:

$$
{M}_{\left({e}_{r},{e}_{\theta},{e}_{z}\right)}=\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}{M}_{11}& {M}_{12}& {M}_{13}& 0& 0& 0\\ {M}_{12}& {M}_{22}& {M}_{23}& 0& 0& 0\\ {M}_{13}& {M}_{23}& {M}_{33}& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& {M}_{44}& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& {M}_{55}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& {M}_{66}\end{array}\right]
$$

With:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{M}_{11}+{M}_{12}+{M}_{13}+0\\ {M}_{12}+{M}_{22}+{M}_{23}+0\\ {M}_{13}+{M}_{23}+{M}_{33}+0\end{array}
$$

In the isotropic case, one has:

$$
{M}_{11}={M}_{22}={M}_{33}=1
{M}_{12}={M}_{13}={M}_{23}=-\frac{1}{2}
{M}_{44}={M}_{55}={M}_{66}=\frac{3}{4}
$$

The terms of this matrix depend on the phase distribution, with:

$$
M=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{M}^{3}\text{si}0\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,01\hfill \\ {M}^{2}={Z}_{\alpha}{M}^{1}+(1-{Z}_{\alpha}){M}^{3}\text{si}0,01\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,99\hfill \\ {M}^{1}\text{si}0,99\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 1\hfill \end{array}
$$

The equivalent strain rate is given by:

$$
\dot{p}={\left(\frac{{\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{\mathit{ap}}^{m}}\right)}^{n}{e}^{-Q/T}
$$

Or, equivalently:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}=a{({e}^{Q/T})}^{1/n}{p}^{m}{\dot{p}}^{1/n}={\sigma}_{v}
$$

The rule of mixtures is applied to the viscous stress ${\sigma}_{v}$:

$$
{\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}={\sigma}_{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{f}_{i}({Z}_{\alpha}){\sigma}_{v,i}
$$

With:

$$
{f}_{1}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\text{si}0\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,9\hfill \\ \frac{{Z}_{\alpha}-0,9}{0,09}\text{si}0,9\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,99\hfill \\ 1\text{si}0,99\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 1\hfill \end{array}
{f}_{3}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\text{si}0\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,01\hfill \\ \frac{0,1-{Z}_{\alpha}}{0,09}\text{si}0,01\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,1\hfill \\ 1\text{si}0,1\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 1\hfill \end{array}
{f}_{2}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\text{si}0\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,01\hfill \\ 1-\frac{0,1-{Z}_{\alpha}}{0,09}\text{si}0,01\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,1\hfill \\ 1\text{si}0,1\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,9\hfill \\ 1-\frac{{Z}_{\alpha}-0,9}{0,09}\text{si}0,9\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 0,99\\ 0\text{si}0,99\le {Z}_{\alpha}\le 1\hfill \end{array}
$$

where $\left({a}_{i},{Q}_{i},{n}_{i},{m}_{i}\right)$ are material parameters associated with the three metallurgical phases.
