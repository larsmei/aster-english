# v9.01.101 SZLZ101 - Damage Calculation/RAINFLOW Method

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to calculate damage from a load history in stress.

From a simple loading history defined by DEFI_FONCTION, the elementary cycles are extracted by the RAINFLOW [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01) cycle counting method, then the elementary damage associated with each cycle is calculated by interpolation on the Wöhler curve of the material.

Various possibilities are tested for introducing the Wöhler curve and for taking into account the elasto-plastic concentration coefficient.

Finally, the total damage to the piece is determined by cumulating all the elementary damage by the Miner linear rule.

This example is a validation test of POSTDAM software developed by the REME Department, provided in the validation manual of version 1.0 of this software.

The results provided by the POST_FATIGUE operator are quite identical to those provided by POSTDAM software.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The analysis is to determine the damage to a piece in an point to which the load history is supplied in stress.

From a simple loading history defined by DEFI_FONCTION, the elementary cycles are extracted by the RAINFLOW [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01) cycle counting method, then the elementary damage associated with each cycle is calculated by interpolation on the Wöhler curve of the material.

Various possibilities are tested for introducing the Wöhler curve and whether or not an elastoplastic concentration coefficient is taken into account:

- The Wöhler curve is defined as:

$\begin{array}{cc}{S}_{\mathrm{alt}}=\text{contrainte alternée}=1/2({E}_{C}/E)\Delta \sigma & X={\text{LOG}}_{10}({S}_{\mathrm{alt}})\\ N={10}^{\mathrm{a0}+\mathrm{a1X}+{\mathrm{a2X}}^{2}+{\mathrm{a3X}}^{3}}& D=\lbrace \begin{array}{}1/N\text{si}{S}_{\mathrm{alt}}\ge {S}_{I}\\ 0.\text{sinon}\end{array}\end{array}$

with:

${E}_{C}$ = Young's modulus associated with the fatigue curve of the material,

$E$ = Young's modulus used to determine stresses,

$\mathrm{a0}$ material constants , $\mathrm{a1}$ , $\mathrm{a2}$ and $\mathrm{a3}$ ,

and ${S}_{I}$ the endurance limit of the material.

- The Wöhler curve is defined in the same form and also takes into account an elasto-plastic concentration coefficient defined by:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{e}=1& \mathit{si}& \Delta \sigma <3{S}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1+(1-n)/(\Delta \sigma /3{S}_{m}-1)/(n(m-1))& \mathit{si}& 3{S}_{m}<\Delta \sigma <3m{S}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1/n& \mathit{si}& 3m{S}_{m}<\Delta \sigma \end{array}$

où

${S}_{m}$ is the maximum permissible stress,

and $n$ and $m$ two material-dependent constants.

- The Wöhler curve is defined as the Basquin analytical form: $D=A{S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\beta}$

Finally, the total damage to the piece is determined by cumulating all the elementary damage by the Miner linear rule.
