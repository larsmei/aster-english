# Introduction to code_aster

> **Warning.**

In this document the philosophy and the fields of application of code_aster are described, without developing in detail the functionalities available.

This document is a first contact with code_aster and has therefore been written with a concern for concision. It is not intended to list all the modellings or types of analysis possible with code_aster, and it does not replace the presentation brochure, which gives a more exhaustive overview.

All the information, provided here or in the various manuals, is given in order to describe, with the greatest possible precision, the content of code_aster. It does not aim to provide training in the numerical modelling of the behaviour of mechanical structures. code_aster is only the implementation of methods described and demonstrated in various works to which the engineer will have to refer, in addition to the reference documentation, if necessary. The code_aster manuals assume that training in solid mechanics and in the finite-element method has been acquired elsewhere.

## 1 Study of the mechanical behaviour of structures

### 1.1 A general-purpose code

code_aster is a general-purpose code for the study of the mechanical behaviour of structures.

The priority field of application is the mechanics of deformable solids: this justifies the number of functionalities attached to the mechanical phenomenon. However, the study of the mechanical behaviour of industrial components first requires the modelling of the loadings to which they are subjected, or of the physical phenomena that modify the parameters of this behaviour (internal or external fluid, temperature, metallurgical phase changes, forces of electromagnetic origin...). For these reasons, code_aster offers several possibilities of "chaining" the mechanical phenomenon with thermal analysis or acoustics, or with other software, as well as a "kit" for building coupled thermo-hydro-mechanical problems.

Although code_aster can be used for many structural-analysis problems (general-purpose code), it was developed in particular to allow the study of components of equipment or of machines used in the field of the production and transmission of electricity. Priority was thus given to the modelling of metal structures, geomaterials and reinforced-concrete structural components. Very few modellings are available for composite materials or elastomers.

Non-linear analyses, in mechanics as well as in thermal analysis, are at the heart of code_aster: their efficient treatment required the development of high-performance algorithms that are relatively simple to use, even if the aim is not to make them work as a "black box". For complex studies, it is therefore necessary to understand the nature of the operations carried out by the code, in order to be able to drive them optimally: one then refers to the theoretical notes giving the details of the modellings and of the methods, collected in the Reference manual.

### 1.2 Analysis methodology with code_aster

A structural analysis carried out with code_aster consists of the chaining of a certain number of commands described within a "command file" in text format. The engine and the interpreter of this command file is the PYTHON scripting language. It is therefore possible to use all the functionalities provided by PYTHON. See in particular the documents [U1.03.01](doc:u1.03.01), [U1.03.02](doc:u1.03.02) and the examples of use [U1.05.00](doc:u1.05.00) and [U1.05.01](doc:u1.05.01). Each command (for example reading of the mesh, assignment of the material data, linear static analysis) produces a "result concept", which defines a data structure that the user can handle and reuse in the subsequent commands of the analysis.

The syntax of all the commands is described and commented on in the U4 and U7 manuals of the User's manual.

In order to simplify the user's task, there exist global commands that group the appropriate chaining of operations for a certain number of analysis cases (for example linear statics — command MECA_STATIQUE, non-linear statics — command STAT_NON_LINE, non-linear thermal analysis — command THER_NON_LINE, etc.). Some were developed directly in an integrated manner; others are Python macro-commands that manage the calls to various unitary commands (such as ASSEMBLAGE, which computes and assembles the mass, damping and stiffness matrices of a structure).

There also exist macro-commands specially dedicated to certain applications.

At the end of an analysis, it is often possible to enrich the computer object containing the "result concept" obtained, by carrying out other analyses a posteriori: for example, from the displacement field and the stresses at the Gauss points obtained in a mechanical analysis, one can compute the strain field, the stress field interpolated at the nodes, etc.

These post-processing operations are carried out by the operators CALC_CHAMP, POST_CHAMP, POST_ELEM, MACR_LIGNE_COUPE, POST_RELEVE_T, etc.

### 1.3 Phenomena, modellings, finite elements and constitutive behaviours

#### 1.3.1 Notions

In code_aster, the PHENOMENE (command AFFE_MODELE) is a family of physical problems that use similar unknowns and conservation equations: for example, the mechanical phenomenon calls on displacement unknowns, the thermal phenomenon on temperature unknowns. Depending on the modelling used, the number of unknowns of this type may vary (for example, at each node only one temperature unknown is needed in 3D, but 3 unknowns are used for shells).

In code_aster, the MODELISATION (command AFFE_MODELE) describes the way in which the continuous equations governing a given phenomenon are discretised, with the help of any complementary assumptions (plane strain, beam model, shell model...). In mechanics, for example, one finds the modellings 3D, plane strain, plane stress, shells, plates, Euler beams, Timoshenko beams, pipes, etc. Each of the modellings uses a specific set of degrees of freedom: for example the displacements in the three directions of space for the 3D continuum modellings, three displacements and three rotations for shells, etc.

The PHENOMENE/MODELISATION pair makes it possible to assign, in a one-to-one manner, a finite-element type to each type of geometric mesh cell.

In code_aster, a "finite element", for a given PHENOMENE/MODELISATION pair, is the set consisting of:

- The geometric nature of the supporting mesh cell (representing a piece of volume or of boundary: hexahedron, tetrahedron, triangle, quadrangle, segment...);

- The choice of the interpolations for the geometry and for the unknowns (shape functions);

- The computation "options" that the element can compute (the operations for which the computation of the appropriate integrals has been programmed: for example, elementary stiffness term, elementary surface-force term, elementary mass term...).

- The numerical integration schemes (quadrature formulae of Gauss type, for example).

The constitutive behaviour is fundamentally a physical notion linked to the properties of the material. It is then expressed mathematically. For example, in mechanics, the constitutive relation is the relation that links the stress field to the strain field. During an analysis, the constitutive relation is computed at each integration point (or Gauss point).

code_aster distinguishes between the characteristics of the material — commands DEFI_MATERIAU and AFFE_MATERIAU — and the constitutive relation (keyword COMPORTEMENT).

#### 1.3.2 The phenomena available in code_aster

code_aster is a piece of software that mainly performs solid and structural mechanics, but, in order to complete the representation of the operating environment of mechanical components, the choice was made to include in code_aster functionalities allowing the modelling of phenomena frequently associated with the mechanical phenomenon, such as thermal analysis or acoustics.

#### 1.3.2.1 The mechanical phenomenon

The mechanical phenomenon is modelled in order to reach two main objectives:

*Firstly*:

The determination of the internal state, in particular of the stress state at each point of a structure, under various loadings representing the operating conditions. Knowledge of this stress state makes it possible to continue the analysis of the mechanical behaviour:

- in order to check compliance with the construction rules particular to each type of structure, notably the Design or Construction Rules (RCC...);

- in order to analyse the harmfulness of defects and of their possible propagation: defects inherent in the manufacturing process of the component or of the structure (inclusions, geometric imperfections...) or resulting from the operating conditions (cracking, erosion...);

- for the study of the behaviour under cyclic loading and of the fatigue analysis;

- for the prediction of the admissible loads, with evolution of the internal state.

*Secondly*:

The determination of the deformed configuration induced by a permanent loading (static) or resulting from a slow evolution (quasi-static) or a faster evolution (dynamic) of the loadings or of the boundary conditions. Knowledge of this deformed configuration, and possibly of the corresponding velocities and accelerations, makes it possible to continue the analysis of the mechanical behaviour:

- for the vibratory or acoustic behaviour;

- for the transmission of the loadings to other structures or components;

- for the analysis of the interactions with neighbouring structures, in order to determine the operating anomalies or the wear parameters that may result from them.

The levels of modelling involved in the study of this phenomenon are:

| The representation of the structure from the geometric shape, with several possible modes of representation that may coexist: * the continuum corresponding to a three-dimensional geometry, or a two-dimensional one with various assumptions (plane stress, plane strain, complete axisymmetry or axisymmetry adapted to the decomposition of the loadings into Fourier modes), * structural elements corresponding to a mid-surface medium, a mean-fibre medium or a discretised medium (beams, plates, shells, pipes,… ). |
| --- |
| The representation of the behaviour of the materials, possibly different, at every point of a structure, with constitutive relations making it possible to represent various conditions of use. Numerous constitutive relations are available: linear and non-linear elasticity, non-linear hyperelasticity, viscoelasticity, elasto-plasticity, elasto-visco-plasticity, damage. The parameters of the constitutive relations may in general depend on variables known as "command variables", such as the temperature, the metallurgical state, the degree of hydration or of drying of the concrete, the fluence (neutron irradiation), etc. |
| The representation of the boundary conditions and of the loadings[1]_ , for which functionalities are available making it possible to represent, at every point of the structure, in the global frame or in a frame defined by the user: * Dirichlet conditions: imposed displacement, linear relations between displacement components; * Neumann conditions: imposed point force, surface and line loadings, making it possible in particular to represent pressure loadings; * Volume loadings, making it possible in particular to represent gravity and the centrifugal forces of bodies in rotation. * Unilateral and bilateral contact conditions and dry friction (Coulomb). These boundary conditions and loadings may depend on time (or on frequency) and on one or more space variables. |

The non-linearities taken into account in the mechanical phenomenon are the constitutive non-linearities, the geometric non-linearities (large displacements and large rotations, large strains, buckling) and the contact/friction non-linearities.

#### 1.3.2.2 The thermal phenomenon

It makes it possible to determine the thermal response of solid media in the steady regime (stationary problem) or in the transient regime (evolution problem). One can model solid conduction, convective exchange, heat exchange between walls, and radiation to infinity. The thermal phenomenon may include the modelling, on heating or on cooling, of the metallurgical phase change of steels, which makes it possible to simulate heat-treatment or welding operations (the identification of the behaviour is based on the experimental TRC diagrams).

By analogy with the equations solved, the thermal phenomenon can also be used to model hydration (the unknown is the degree of hydration) or the drying of concrete (the unknown is the water concentration).

#### 1.3.2.3 The acoustic phenomenon

The acoustic phenomenon is modelled in order to reach two main objectives:

- The study of acoustic propagation in a closed medium, corresponding to the Helmholtz equation in a compressible fluid, for propagation domains of complex topology. Knowledge of the pressure field makes it possible to continue the acoustic analysis in order to determine the field of sound levels (expressed in $mathit{dB}$ ) and the fields of active and reactive acoustic intensity.

- The study of coupled 3D vibro-acoustic problems, corresponding to the vibratory behaviour of a structure in a bounded domain of compressible, inviscid fluid.

#### 1.3.3 The coupled phenomena available in code_aster

So that there is no ambiguity, a distinction will be made between:

- The chaining of two phenomena (or weak coupling): a prior study of the first phenomenon, whose results are used as data for the second;

- The full coupling (or strong coupling) of several phenomena: simultaneous solution of the phenomena with equations that are effectively coupled.

#### 1.3.3.1 The chainings internal to code_aster

The chaining may be carried out inside code_aster, or between code_aster and external software.

The chainings currently carried out within code_aster are the following:

- Thermal – mechanical: all the mechanical characteristics of the materials may depend on the temperature, and the algorithms available for the mechanical phenomenon make it possible to exploit the results of a prior thermal analysis (anelastic strains: thermal expansions, concrete shrinkage...) carried out on a possibly different mesh;

- Thermal – metallurgical: after a thermal analysis, it consists in computing the proportions of the various metallurgical phases of the steels;

- Thermal – metallurgical – mechanical: taking into account four mechanical effects of the metallurgical transformations (phase-change strain, modification of the mechanical characteristics, transformation plasticity, hardening recovery);

- Electrical – mechanical: the electrical coupling is limited to taking into account the Laplace forces induced by short-circuit currents in electrical cables;

- Fluid – mechanical: assignment of a pressure field on a wall, deduced from a fluid-mechanics analysis.

#### 1.3.3.2 The thermo-hydro-mechanical couplings

Saturated or unsaturated porous media (geomaterials, soils, concrete) must be studied by coupling the three equations of mechanics, of thermal analysis and of hydraulics. The user chooses the constitutive behaviours to be used from a kit of thermo-hydro-mechanical models known as THM. One can thus choose whether or not to take the effect of temperature into account, and whether to represent one or two pressures. The choice of each of the constitutive behaviours associated with the phenomena retained is also made within this framework.

#### 1.3.3.3 The couplings for fluid-structure interaction

Three types of coupling are available in the field of fluid-structure interaction:

- The computation of the eigenmodes of a structure containing (or immersed in) a fluid at rest (with or without a free surface);

- The computation of the vibrations of a structure in a flow, and the estimation of the resulting damage by vibratory fatigue or wear;

- The taking into account of a boundary condition of infinite-fluid-domain type.

### 1.4 Analysis methods

In order to implement the various modellings, several analysis methods are available, corresponding to different processes for applying the loadings.

#### 1.4.1 Static mechanics

**Static analysis**: it corresponds to permanent loadings, for the treatment of steady-state thermal analysis and of thermo-mechanics. For linear analyses, the results obtained can be combined linearly, according to need, and can be used to describe the initial state of an evolution process.

**Quasi-static analysis**: for all mechanical processes in which inertia effects can be neglected, implicit incremental algorithms are available to solve the non-linear constitutive equations, taking evolving loadings and boundary conditions into account.

#### 1.4.2 Thermal

**Steady-state analysis**: in linear and non-linear thermal analysis, computation of the thermal equilibrium state.

**Transient analysis**: in linear and non-linear thermal analysis, with possible taking into account of metallurgical effects for metals and of hydration and drying for concretes, as well as for thermo-hydro-mechanical problems while neglecting inertia effects on the mechanical part.

#### 1.4.3 Dynamic mechanics

For processes in which the effects of inertia and of propagation must be taken into consideration, one speaks of dynamic analysis.

Analysis in the physical basis is the solution of the equations in the classical basis of the physical degrees of freedom.

Analysis in the modal basis consists of a prior computation of the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the structure, representing the "vibratory" state of the system. The equations to be solved are then projected onto a basis of eigenvectors: the number of degrees of freedom of the system to be solved is proportional to the size of the modal basis used.

For these two types of analysis, in the physical basis or in the modal basis, the response computation can be carried out in the time domain or in the harmonic domain (in the linear case).

For seismic analysis, the problem can also be formulated as an imposed motion in a relative frame (without the entrainment motion).

Linear dynamic analyses can be carried out including the second-order effects, on the stiffness, of the initial static stresses computed beforehand (geometric stiffness, centrifugal stiffening).

For non-linear problems, two analysis methods are available:

- Analysis by modal recombination with localised non-linear boundary conditions, for problems with shock (operator DYNA_VIBRA);

- Non-linear dynamic analysis in the physical basis (operator DYNA_NON_LINE).

#### 1.4.4 Substructuring

Substructuring consists in grouping several finite elements within a macro-element and in "condensing" the whole of their stiffness onto the (fewer) degrees of freedom of this macro-element.

The solution of the global problem is then limited to the determination of the unknowns carried by the macro-elements, then to the computation of the unknowns carried by each "small" element, independently, within each of the macro-elements.

The advantages of this method are the savings in time and memory, when the complete structure is formed of elements reproduced several times by translation or rotation.

In dynamics, the modal analysis and the computation of the harmonic or transient response can be carried out in classical dynamic substructuring by the Craig-Bampton and Mac Neal methods, or by the so-called interface-mode method.

For structures presenting cyclic symmetry, the available methods make it possible to compute the eigenmodes of the global structure from the dynamic behaviour of a base sector.

## 2 The solution method

For the solution of the various problems mentioned, the main method of spatial discretisation currently implemented in code_aster is the finite-element method.

### 2.1 A parameterised implementation of the finite-element method

A particular effort has been made to parameterise the implementation of the finite-element method. The computation options required by each analysis method (static, quasi-static, dynamic) and by each phenomenon (mechanical, thermal, acoustic) are treated globally for the whole structure, whatever the modellings retained for a particular study.

Among the possibilities offered by this architecture, let us mention:

- The independence between the discretisation topology (MAILLAGE) and the interpolation properties of the finite elements assigned to these mesh cells (MODELE), hence the diversity of the modellings that can be used on one and the same mesh;

- The diversity of the constitutive relations and of the material properties that can be used in one and the same model;

- The treatment of the boundary conditions and of the loadings by specific boundary finite elements, so as to allow their location without ambiguity, in particular for continua;

- A systematic procedure making it possible to treat the dependence of the material properties and of the boundary conditions on various parameters (temperature, time, space variable...) by means of a FONCTION or a FORMULE defined in Python.

- Data structures making it possible to use all the modellings with the various solution algorithms.

### 2.2 An extended finite-element library

The finite-element library is parameterised so as to allow the assignment, to the various recognised mesh cells, of the discretised formulations of the available phenomena.

#### 2.2.1 Continua

A continuum means a portion of a three-dimensional or two-dimensional structure, treated as a volume.

The 3D modellings are the simplest forms of continuum, because they call on no additional assumption. In the 2D modellings, one equation is removed, but assumptions must be added: for example plane strain or plane stress in mechanics, and axisymmetry in thermal analysis and in mechanics.

#### 2.2.2 Structural components

Structural elements are built by integrating assumptions on the three-dimensional kinematic behaviour (representing more or less well the phenomena of bending, torsion, shear, warping...). They can be classified into three categories:

1. Mid-surface elements (plates, shells): each type of element rests on assumptions about the variation of the unknowns through the thickness, which makes it possible to compute the value at every point from the value taken on the mid-surface (and possibly on the lower and upper faces in thermal analysis);

1. Mean-fibre elements (bars, beams, pipes, cables): for each cross-section, the assumptions relate the value of the unknowns at every point to the value taken on the mean fibre;

1. Discrete elements (masses, springs, dampers...): they make it possible to introduce, on point mesh cells or on segments, characteristics expressed in an arbitrary frame.

These components require the use of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM in order to define their characteristics.

#### 2.2.3 Connections between modellings

The implementation retained for the finite-element method makes it possible to treat structures modelled with different types of mechanical element (continua or structural elements). The connection of finite elements relying on different degrees of freedom, at one and the same node, can be made by writing linear relations adapted to the nature of the connection. A particular methodology has been developed in order to transmit the force resultants as correctly as possible (in the least-squares sense). One can thus represent satisfactorily the connection between a 3D medium and beams, plates, shells or pipes, as well as the shell–beam, shell–pipe and beam–pipe connections.

#### 2.2.4 Discontinuous media

There also exist elements that take discontinuities into account (e.g.: crack)

1. By the level-set method (XFEM elements), with modelling of contact and of friction;

1. By the CZM methods (Cohesive Zone Models), with modelling of contact on closure and of damage on opening;

1. Elements with an internal discontinuity;

#### 2.2.5 Heterogeneous modellings

Homogenisation techniques make it possible to represent, at lower cost, an array of tubes immersed in an incompressible fluid, multilayer composite shells, or multi-fibre beams.

## 3 Solvers and linear algebra

Concerning the methods for numbering the unknowns, for storing the assembled matrices and for solving the linear systems on which the various algorithms rely, several methods are available today: direct or iterative solver, sequential or parallel.

Natively in code_aster:

- Direct solvers: multifrontal method (OpenMP-parallelised) and ${mathit{LDL}}^{T}$ method.

- Iterative solver: preconditioned conjugate gradient.

These methods are associated with algorithms for renumbering the degrees of freedom, making it possible to optimise the memory size required to store the matrices.

Externally, it is possible to use the following solvers:

- MUMPS: direct solver, MPI-parallelised, out-of-core;

- PeTSC: library of iterative solvers, MPI-parallelised;

For modal computation: Lanczos and Sorensen methods, inverse-power methods, for real and complex problems, symmetric or not.

## 4 The study tools

### 4.1 Supplements and operations on the mesh

The notion of mesh used by code_aster is reduced to a very simple one: a list of the nodes and of their coordinates, a list of the mesh cells and of their topology (connectivities). To these entities is added the notion of node groups and of mesh-cell groups. These groups make it possible to assign various modelling characteristics (finite elements, materials, boundary conditions, loadings…) and to drive the processing of the results (selective extraction of components).

The user can create node groups or mesh-cell groups at any moment in the course of the analysis, by means of logical or geometric criteria. One can also modify the data structure containing the mesh: change of frame, addition of extra nodes on mesh cells, creation of new mesh cells or mesh-cell groups, deletion of mesh cells, etc. The addition and the removal of material can therefore be modelled simply.

The construction of a complex mesh can only be done by using a tool better suited to this purpose, such as Salome and its built-in meshers (Netgen, BLSurf, Hexotic, etc.) or GMSH.

### 4.2 Material-data catalogue

A material-data catalogue under quality assurance (AQ) gives access to the values of the parameters of constitutive laws for various materials commonly used in studies. The material characteristics can be included directly in the command file by means of a specific operator. For the free version, all the catalogue machinery is available but the database is empty.

### 4.3 Processing and exploitation of the results

#### 4.3.1 Operations on fields

The computed fields can be used in all sorts of algebraic combinations. In linear analysis, one can thus, for example, deduce the response to a complex loading from the responses to the unit loadings into which it is decomposed.

#### 4.3.2 Value extraction

Operations for extracting the result fields are available on nodes or on mesh cells. It is also possible to define an arbitrary observation path, independent of the initial mesh. Various computations are proposed on the extracted fields (mean, standard deviation, tensor invariants, transformation into local axes, etc.). For time or frequency evolutions, it is possible to extract the deformed shape at a time instant (a frequency) or the response of a particular quantity (grandeur).

#### 4.3.3 Printout of the results

The results can be printed in an easily readable form, or in the format of the visualisation tools (MED, I-DEAS, GMSH). code_aster communicates with Salomé by means of the MED format.

One can also plot curves in various formats (PostScript or other image formats) using the xmgrace plotter.

### 4.4 Quality control of the results

Numerous functionalities make it possible to check the quality of the results of a study, or to make the study easier to carry out.

#### 4.4.1 Error estimators and adaptive mesh

Two categories of error estimator are available. Coupled with the HOMARD refinement/coarsening software (internal chaining within code_aster by means of macro-commands), they make it possible to adapt the mesh in the course of the analysis in order to reach a given accuracy, for an optimal computational cost.

#### 4.4.2 Checking the quality of a modal basis

Criteria for checking the quality of a modal basis make it possible to ensure that the number of eigenmodes retained correctly represents the phenomena that one wishes to study.

#### 4.4.3 Use of incompatible meshes

Projection operators make it possible to continue, on a second mesh, an analysis carried out on a first mesh. One can thus use different meshes in thermal analysis and in mechanics (including, for example, a crack block in the structure only at the time of its in-service analysis, after having computed, on a simpler mesh, the residual stresses due to its manufacturing process).

#### 4.4.4 Automatic cutting of the time step and piloting of the loading

In the event of non-convergence of the global solution algorithm, the user can request that the code itself start a cutting of the time steps in order to allow convergence. An event-management mechanism is also available.

Furthermore, in order to facilitate the convergence of the analyses, it is also possible to pilot the progressive application of the loading by the value of a degree of freedom or of a strain (continuation methods).

#### 4.4.5 Unloading and loss-of-radiality indicators

These indicators make it possible, a posteriori, to check the validity of the assumptions made about the non-linear behaviour of a structure, and the relevance of the chosen mode of application of the loading (load step).

## 5 Dedicated tools

### 5.1 Definition and operating procedure

Dedicated tools are tools very closely linked to the business of the operator of equipment for the production and distribution of electricity, and using code_aster as the solver. Dedicated tools may have a more or less strong integration with code_aster. Two cases are distinguished:

- Integration into the code_aster command file as a macro-command (including the creation of the mesh from simple geometric data and a call to an external mesher);

- Production, by a separate tool (a stand-alone pre- and post-processor), of command files driving the code_aster analysis, and processing, in that tool, of the result files recovered.

### 5.2 The modes of exchange

code_aster can receive, as data, files coming from analyses previously carried out by external software. It can also export its results in a format that can be exploited by other tools. For certain types of analysis (for example soil–structure or soil–fluid–structure interaction with the MISS3D software), both types of chaining can be activated.

Exchanges with other software are currently made either in the MED format, or in a format specific to the chained software. Several code_aster commands allow the reading or the writing of the objects to be transmitted (result fields, matrices, loadings...). In certain cases (MISS3D), macro-commands make a chained analysis easier to set up.

### 5.3 The software interfaced with code_aster

The meshing software interfaced with code_aster is salome_meca, Gibi (a subset of CASTEM2000), I‑DEAS or GMSH. For the visualisation of the results, one can use salome_meca, I-DEAS or GMSH.
