# v7.33.101 WTNA101 – Non-drained triaxial test with softening Drucker Prager-like behavior

**Summary:**

This test case allows a non-drained triaxial test to be implemented on two different modellingss when calculating non-linearly. This allows to highlight the effect of negative, parabolic or linear nutring, in the case of 3D_HM models.

Modelling A:

- DRUCK_PRAGER model with linear negative nutring for $2\mathit{MPa}$ containment.
- 3D_HM Modelling with mesh cells HEXA20.

Modelling B:

- DRUCK_PRAGER model with parabolic negative nutring for $2\mathit{MPa}$ containment.
- 3D_HM Modelling with mesh cells HEXA20.

Modelization C:

- DRUCK_PRAG_N_A model with parabolic negative nutring for $2\mathit{MPa}$ containment.
- 3D_HM Modelling with mesh cells HEXA20.

## 1 Reference problem

![Figure](/figures/v7.33.101/Shape1.gif)

### 1.1 Geometry

Cubic geometry:

> $O(0.,0.,0.)$ Centre

Point $A(0.5,0.5,0.5)$

> Side Cube $1m$

### 1.2 Material properties

    - Elastic

      - $E=5800.0\mathrm{E6}\mathrm{Pa}$     Young's modulus
      - $\rho =2500{\mathrm{kg.m}}^{-3}$ Density
      - $\nu =0.3$                 Poisson's ratio

    - DRUCK_PRAGER with linear negative nutring

      - $\alpha =0.33$ Pressure Dependence Coefficient
      - ${p}_{\mathrm{ultm}}=0.01$ Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation
      - ${\sigma}^{Y}=2.57\mathrm{E6}\mathrm{Pa}$ Plastic Constraint
      - $h=-2.00\mathrm{E8}\mathrm{Pa}$ Nutrient module

    - DRUCK_PRAGER with parabolic negative nutting

      - $\alpha =0.33$ Pressure Dependence Coefficient
      - ${p}_{\mathit{ultm}}=0.01$ Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation
      - ${\sigma}^{Y}=2.57E6\mathit{Pa}$ Plastic Constraint

      - ${\sigma}_{\mathit{ultm}}^{Y}=0.57E6\mathit{Pa}$ Ultimate Constraint
    - DRUCK_PRAG_N_A with parabolic negative nutting

      - $\alpha =0.33$ Pressure Dependence Coefficient
      - ${p}_{\mathrm{ultm}}=0.01$ Ultimate cumulative plastic deformation
      - ${\sigma}^{Y}=2.57\mathrm{E6}\mathrm{Pa}$ Plastic Constraint
      - ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{ultm}}^{Y}=0.57\mathrm{E6}\mathrm{Pa}$ Ultimate Constraint
      - $\beta =0.33$ Initial dilatance coefficient

    - Hydraulic behaviour: saturated liquid

      - $\mathrm{Pre1}=1\mathrm{Pa}$ Reference fluid pressure
      - ${\rho}_{\mathrm{pre1}}=1000{\mathrm{kg.m}}^{-3}$ Volumetric mass of water
      - $\mathrm{Poro}=0.14$ Initial porosity
      - ${\rho}_{\mathrm{vh}}=2400{\mathrm{kg.m}}^{-3}$ Homogenized density
      - $\mathrm{bio}=1$ Biot coefficient
      - ${K}_{\mathrm{intrinsèque}}=1E-18{m}^{2}$ Intrinsic Permeability
      - $\frac{1}{{K}_{l}}=0$ Liquid incompressible
      - $\mathrm{vi}=1.0E-3\mathrm{Pa.s}$ Viscosity

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Boundary conditions and loadings are applied in two steps:

- Step $A$ : $t\in [0,1.]$

**boundary conditions**

  - Pressure at nodes $\mathrm{PRE1}=0.$
  - Symmetry, imposed displacements on the faces of the cube belonging to the planes

$X=-0.5$     $\mathrm{DX}=0$

$Y=-0.5$     $\mathrm{DY}=0$

$Z=-0.5$     $\mathrm{DZ}=0$

**Charges**

  - $p={2.10}^{6}\mathrm{Pa}$ compression is applied progressively to the faces of the cube belonging to the planes: $X=0.5$ , $Y=0.5$ and $Z=0.5$

![Figure](/figures/v7.33.101/Shape2.gif)

    - Step B: $t\in ]1,2.]$

From the stress state obtained at the moment $t=1.s$ , the following conditions shall be applied on the faces of the cube:

**Movements**

  - For the face of the $Z=0.5$ plane the $\mathrm{DZ}$ displacement shall be progressively applied, following a ramp:

![Figure](/figures/v7.33.101/Shape3.gif)

  - For the faces belonging to the $X=-0.5,Y=-0.5,Z=-0.5$ planes symmetry conditions shall be applied.

Loadings: the loads applied are constant:

      - Face belonging to $X=0.5$ $p={2.10}^{6}\mathrm{Pa}$ plane
      - Face belonging to $Z=0.5$ $p={2.10}^{6}\mathrm{Pa}$ plane
