# v6.04.219 SSNV219 – 3D contact manufactured solutions method and large deformations

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to verify the Modelling of 3D contact in large deformations using the method of manufactured solutions.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A cube on the $1m$ side is considered.

### 1.2 Material properties

For modellings A, B, C and D:

| $E=1\mathit{MPa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\nu =0.15$ | Poisson's ratio |

For modellings E, F, G, H, I, J, K, L and M:

| $E=1\mathit{MPa}$ | Young's modulus |
| --- | --- |
| $\nu =0.$ | Poisson's ratio |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

On the $\text{HAUT}$ edge, a displacement is imposed (see v6.04.219-solution-reference).

On the $\text{BORDX}$ , $\text{BORDMX}$ , $\text{BORDY}$ , $\text{BORDMY}$ , $\text{BORDZ}$ and $\text{ESCLAVE}$ edges, pressure is imposed (see v6.04.219-solution-reference).

Throughout the field, a volume force is imposed (see v6.04.219-solution-reference).

The $\text{MAITRE}$ surface of a paraboloid nature is described by the equation:

$$
Z=-0.2\times (1.+{X}^{2}+{Y}^{2})-0.3
$$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

None

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution analytical is given by:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\mathit{Ux}& =& 0.2\times {Z}^{2}\times X\times Y\\ \mathit{Uy}& =& 0.2\times {Z}^{2}\times X\times Y\\ \mathit{Uz}& =& -0.2\times (1.+{X}^{2}+{Y}^{2})\times (1+0.01\times Z)-0.01\mathrm{\ast }Z-0.3\end{array}
$$

Dirichlet, Neumann and source terms are obtained by the Bib1 manufactured solutions method.

The $\underline{\underline{F}}$ transformation gradient is first determined:

$$
\underline{\underline{F}}=\underline{\nabla}\underline{U}+\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Id}}}
$$

Knowing the normal $\underline{N}={[0,0,-1]}^{T}$ at the $\text{ESCLAVE}$ surface in the undeformed configuration, we obtain its expression in the configuration distorted by the Nanson formula:

$$
\underline{n}=\frac{{\underline{\underline{F}}}^{-T}\underline{N}}{\parallel {\underline{\underline{F}}}^{-T}\underline{N}\parallel }
$$

Knowing the Hooke $\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{A}}}}$ tensor and the Green-Lagrange $\underline{\underline{E}}$ tensor, we calculate the second Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{S}}$ tensor:

$$
\underline{\underline{E}}=\frac{1}{2}({\underline{\underline{F}}}^{T}\cdot \underline{\underline{F}}-\underline{\underline{\mathrm{Id}}})
$$

$$
\underline{\underline{S}}=\underline{\underline{\underline{\underline{A}}}}:\underline{\underline{E}}
$$

It is recalled that the second tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{S}}$ allows undeformed configuration forces per unit of undeformed surface:

$$
\frac{d\underline{{f}_{0}}}{\mathrm{dA}}=\underline{\underline{S}}\cdot \underline{N}
$$

As we are looking to determine the forces in distorted configuration, we will determine the first tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{\Pi}}$

$$
\underline{\underline{\Pi}}=\underline{\underline{F}}\cdot \underline{\underline{S}}
$$

Volume forces ${\underline{f}}_{\mathrm{vol}}$ can be determined as follows:

$$
{\underline{f}}_{\mathrm{vol}}=\text{-div}\underline{\underline{\Pi}}
$$

Knowing the normal in initial configuration on the different faces and the first tensioner of Piola-Kirchhoff $\underline{\underline{\Pi}}$ , we can calculate the surface forces in distorted configuration:

$$
{\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}=\underline{\underline{\Pi}}\cdot \underline{N}
$$

On the $\text{BAS}$ surface that is in contact, one needs a special treatment. Indeed, normal efforts are taken into account by the contact:

$$
\begin{array}{ccccc}{\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}& =& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{contact}}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& p\ast \underline{n}& +& {\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}\end{array}
$$

Where $p$ is the contact pressure. It can be determined by the expression:

$$
p=(\underline{\underline{\Pi}}\cdot \underline{N})\cdot \underline{n}
$$

Therefore, it is only necessary to apply tangential efforts. They are calculated by the expression:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}{\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{t}}^{\text{BAS}}& =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}-{\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}\\ & =& {\underline{f}}_{\text{surf}}^{\text{BAS}}-({\underline{f}}_{{\text{surf}}_{n}}^{\text{BAS}}\cdot \underline{n})\underline{n}\end{array}
$$

With regard to contact efforts, * it is absolutely essential to construct the manufactured solution so that they check the equations of contact* bib2, namely:

$$
\begin{array}{ccc}\text{gap}(\underline{U})& \text{}\ge \text{}& 0\\ p& \text{}\le \text{}& 0\\ p\cdot \text{gap}(\underline{U})& =& 0\end{array}
$$

This verification is done after having calculated the pressure and displacement associated with the manufactured solution in an analytical way, usually with a formal calculation tool (in this case, it is the Python *sympy* module). They must then be visualized, in order to verify *retrospectively* that the solution that was checked well. In the case of this test, we represented pressure and leap of motion analytically in v6.04.219-fig-pressure and v6.04.219-fig-game-gap figures. We notice that they check $p<0$ and $\text{gap}(\underline{U})=0$ , which is characteristic of a fully contactant surface, and conforms to .
