# r5.03.16 Elastoplastic constitutive relation with linear kinematic hardening and non-linear isotropic hardening. 3D modellings and plane stress.

**Summary:**

This document describes an elastoplastic constitutive law with mixed hardening, linear kinematic and non-linear isotropic. The equations to be solved in order to integrate this constitutive relation numerically are specified, as is the consistent tangent matrix.

This constitutive behaviour can be used for the modellings of 3D and 2D continuous media (AXIS, C_PLAN, D_PLAN), and for the modellings DKT, COQUE_3D and TUYAU.

## 1 Introduction

When the loading path is no longer monotonic, isotropic and kinematic hardenings are no longer equivalent. In particular, one may expect to have simultaneously a kinematic part and an isotropic part. If one seeks to describe precisely the effects of a cyclic loading, it is desirable to adopt sophisticated modellings (but simple to use) such as the Taheri model, for example, see [R5.03.05](doc:r5.03.05). On the other hand, for less complex loading paths, one may wish to include only a linear kinematic hardening, all the non-linearities of the hardening being carried by the isotropic term. This makes it possible to describe a traction curve precisely, while still representing phenomena such as the Bauschinger effect [ 1 ] (see for example the figure).

The hardening characteristics are then given by a traction curve and a constant, called the Prager constant, for the linear kinematic hardening term. They are introduced in the command DEFI_MATERIAU:

| Linear isotropic hardening | Non-linear isotropic hardening |
| --- | --- |
| DEFI_MATERIAU / ECRO_LINE SY: elastic limit D_SIGM_EPSI: slope of the traction curve PRAGER: (C: Prager constant ) | defi_materiau traction: (SIGM: traction curve prager: (C: Prager constant ) |

These characteristics may also depend on the temperature, provided that one then uses the factor keywords ECMI_LINE_FO and ECMI_TRAC_FO in place of ECRO_LINE and TRACTION. The use of these constitutive laws is available in the commands STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE:

| Linear isotropic hardening | Non-linear isotropic hardening |
| --- | --- |
| STAT_NON_LINE COMPORTEMENT: RELATION:‘VMIS_ECMI_LINE’ | STAT_NON_LINE COMPORTEMENT: RELATION:‘VMIS_ECMI_TRAC’ |

In the remainder of this document, one describes precisely the combined-hardening model. One then presents the detail of its numerical integration in connection with the construction of the consistent tangent matrix. Finally, a uniaxial tension-compression test illustrates the identification of the material characteristics.

## 2 Description of the model

At any instant, the state of the material is described by the strain $\varepsilon$ , the temperature $T$ , the plastic strain ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ and the cumulative plastic strain $p$ . The state equations then define, as a function of these state variables, the stress $\sigma ={\sigma}^{H}\text{Id}+\tilde{\sigma}$ (decomposed into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts), the isotropic part of the hardening $R$ and the kinematic part $X$ , also called the back-stress:

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\sigma )=K\text{tr}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})\text{avec}{\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}=\alpha ({\text{T-T}}^{\text{réf}})\text{Id}$     [eq 2-1]

$\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma -{\sigma}^{H}\text{Id}=2\mu (\tilde{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}^{p})\text{où}\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon -\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}(\varepsilon )\text{Id}$     [eq 2-2]

$R=R(p)$     [eq 2-3]

$X=C{\varepsilon}^{p}$     [eq 2-4]

where $K,\mu ,\alpha ,R$ and $C$ are material characteristics which may depend on the temperature.

More precisely, they are respectively the bulk and shear moduli, the mean thermal expansion coefficient (see [R4.08.01](doc:r4.08.01)), the isotropic hardening function and the Prager constant. As for ${T}^{\text{réf}}$ , it is the reference temperature, for which the thermal strain is zero.

$K,\mu$ are related to Young's modulus $E$ and to Poisson's ratio by:

$\begin{array}{}\mathrm{3K}=3\lambda +2\mu =\frac{E}{1-2\nu  }\\ 2\mu =\frac{E}{1+\nu  }\end{array}$

**Note:**

Concerning the kinematic part of the hardening [eq], one observes that it is linear in this model. Moreover, one must take care of the fact that in certain references one calls the Prager constant $\mathrm{2C}/3$ and not $C$ . Likewise, for the isotropic hardening function, the elastic limit is included in it by $R(0)={\sigma}^{y}$ , certain references treating it separately.

The evolution of the internal variables ${\varepsilon}^{p}\text{et}p$ is governed by a normal flow law associated with a plasticity criterion $F$ :

$F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}-R$     [eq 2-5]

with ${A}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}\cdot \tilde{A}}$

${\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\dot{\lambda}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\dot{\lambda}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}$     [eq 2-6]

$\dot{p}=\dot{\lambda}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}\cdot {\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}}$     [eq 2-7]

As for the plastic multiplier $\dot{\lambda}$ , it is obtained by the following consistency condition:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\text{si}F\text{<}0\text{ou}\dot{F}\text{<}0& \dot{\lambda}=0\\ \text{si}F=0\text{et}\dot{F}=0& \dot{\lambda}\text{>=}0\end{array}$     [eq 2-8]

## 3 Integration of the constitutive relation

In order to carry out the numerical integration of the constitutive law, one performs a discretisation in time and adopts an implicit Euler scheme, reputed to be appropriate for elastoplastic constitutive relations. Henceforth, one will use the following notations: ${A}^{-},A\text{et}\Delta A$ represent respectively the values of a quantity $A$ at the beginning and at the end of the time step considered, as well as its increment during the step. The problem is then the following: knowing the state at time ${t}^{-}$ as well as the increments of strain $\Delta \varepsilon$ and of temperature $\Deltat$ , determine the state at time $t$ as well as the stresses $\sigma$ .

First, one takes into account the variations of the characteristics with respect to the temperature by noting that:

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{\sigma}^{{H}^{-}}+K\text{tr}(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$     [eq 3-1]

$\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu (\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})$     [eq 3-2]

$X=\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}^{-}+C\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$     [eq 3-3]

In view of equation [eq], one observes that the hydrostatic behaviour is purely elastic. Only the treatment of the deviatoric component is delicate. In order to lighten the writings to come, one introduces ${\tilde{s}}^{e}$ , the difference $\tilde{\sigma}-X$ in the absence of an increment of plastic strains, so that:

$\tilde{\sigma}-X=\underset{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}-\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}^{-}+2\mu \Delta \tilde{e}}}-(2\mu +C)\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$     [eq 3-4]

The flow equations [eq] and [eq] and the consistency condition [eq] are written, once discretised and noting that $p=\lambda$ :

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\Deltap \frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}$     [eq 3-5]

$F\text{<=}0\Deltap \text{>=}0F\Deltap =0$     [eq 3-6]

The treatment of the consistency condition [eq] is classical. One begins with an elastic trial ($\Deltap =0$ ) which is indeed the solution if the plasticity criterion is not exceeded, that is if:

$F={s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-R({p}^{-})\text{<=}0$     [eq 3-7]

Otherwise, the solution is plastic ($\Deltap \text{>}0$ ) and the consistency condition [eq] reduces to $F=0$ . In order to solve it, one begins by showing that one can reduce the problem to a scalar problem by eliminating $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ . Indeed, taking [eq] and [eq] into account, one observes that $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ is collinear with ${\tilde{s}}^{e}$ because:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Deltap }{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}\left[{\tilde{s}}^{e}-(2\mu +C)\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}\right]$     [eq 3-8]

Moreover, according to [eq], the norm of $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ is:

${(\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})}_{\text{eq}}=\frac{3}{2}\Deltap$     [eq 3-9]

One therefore immediately deduces the expression for $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ as a function of $\Deltap$ :

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\Deltap \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}$     [eq 3-10]

It now only remains to replace $\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$ by its expression [eq] in equation [eq]; one obtains:

$\tilde{\sigma}-X={\tilde{s}}^{e}\left[1-\frac{\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\Delta p}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\right]$

and, substituting $\tilde{\sigma}-X$ into the equation $F=0$ , one reduces the problem to a scalar equation in $\Delta p$ to be solved, namely:

$\mid {s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\Deltap \mid -R({p}^{-}+\Deltap )=0$     [eq 3-11]

Insofar as the function $R$ is positive, which will be assumed henceforth, there exists a solution $\Delta p$ of this equation, characterised by:

$\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\Delta p+R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)={s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}$     [eq 3-12]

where $0<\Delta p<\frac{2}{3}\frac{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}{2\mu +C}$

Note that in the interval specified in [eq], the solution is unique. For details of the solution of this equation, one should refer to [R5.03.02].

The particular case of plane stress is studied in § 6 .

## 4 Computation of the tangent stiffness

In order to allow a solution of the global problem (equilibrium equations) by a Newton method, it is necessary to determine the consistent tangent matrix of the incremental problem. To this end, one once again adopts the convention of writing symmetric tensors of order 2 in the form of vectors with 6 components. Thus, for a tensor $a$ :

$a={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}{a}_{xx}& {a}_{yy}& {a}_{zz}\end{array}\begin{array}{ccc}\sqrt{2}{a}_{xy}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{xz}& \sqrt{2}{a}_{yz}\end{array}\right]$     [eq 4-1]

If one furthermore introduces the hydrostatic vector $1$ and the deviatoric projection matrix $P$ :

$1={}^{t}\text{}\left[\begin{array}{cccccc}1& 1& 1& 0& 0& 0\end{array}\right]$     [eq 4-2]

$P=\text{Id}-\frac{1}{3}1\otimes 1$     [eq 4-3]

Then the consistent tangent stiffness matrix is written, for an elastic behaviour:

$\frac{\partial s}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=K1\otimes 1+2\mup$     [eq 4-4]

and for a plastic behaviour:

$\frac{\partial s}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=K1\otimes 1+2\mu (1-\frac{3\mu \Deltap }{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})P+9{\mu}^{2}(\frac{\Deltap }{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}-\frac{1}{{R}^{'}(p)+\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)})(\frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\otimes \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})$     [eq 4-5]

The initial tangent matrix, used by the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, is obtained by adopting the behaviour of the previous step (elastic or plastic, indicated by an internal variable $x$ equal to 0 or 1) and by taking $\Deltap =0$ in equation [eq].

RIGI_MECA_TANG *is the operator linearised with respect to* **time** (cf. [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01) , [R5.03.05](doc:r5.03.05) ) *and corresponds to what is called the rate problem; in the present case, the linearisation with respect to* $\Deltau$ *, at* $\Deltau =0$ *, supplies the same expression.*

One now proposes to demonstrate expression [eq]. By differentiating [eq] and [eq] at fixed temperature, one immediately obtains:

$\delta \sigma =\left[K1\otimes 1+2\mup \right]\delta \varepsilon -2\mu \delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$     [eq 4-6]

If the behaviour regime is plastic, the incremental flow law [eq] then supplies:

$\delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\deltap \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}+\frac{3}{2}\Deltap \delta (\frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})$     [eq 4-7]

As for $\mathrm{dp}$ , it is obtained by differentiating the implicit equation [eq]:

$\left[\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)+{R}^{'}(p)\right]\deltap =\delta {s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}$     [eq 4-8]

Finally, it only remains to supply the variations of ${\tilde{s}}^{e}$ :

$\delta {\tilde{s}}^{e}=2\mu \delta \tilde{\varepsilon}{\mathrm{ds}}_{\text{eq}}^{e}=3\mu \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\cdot \delta \tilde{\varepsilon}\delta (\frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})=\frac{1}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}(2\mu -3\mu \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\otimes \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})\cdot \delta \tilde{\varepsilon}$     [eq 4-9]

By then replacing [eq], [eq] and [eq] in [eq], one indeed obtains expression [eq].

This expression is formally identical to that defined in R5.03.02: [eq] and is written:

$\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }=K1\otimes 1+2\mu (1-\frac{3\mu \xi \Deltap }{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})(\text{Id}-\frac{1}{3}1\otimes 1)+9{\mu}^{2}\xi (\frac{\Deltap }{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}-\frac{1}{{R}^{'}+\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)})(\frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}}\otimes \frac{{\tilde{s}}^{e}}{{s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}})$

with $\xi =1$ if $\Delta \varepsilon$ leads to plastic flow, and $\xi =0$ otherwise.

Using [eq], one finds:

$\frac{\partial s}{\partial \Delta \varepsilon }={\lambda}^{\text{*}}\overrightarrow{1}\otimes \overrightarrow{1}+2{\mu}^{\text{*}}\text{Id}-\xi \frac{9{\mu}^{2}}{H(p)}(1-\frac{{R}^{'}.\Delta p}{R(p)})\frac{1}{{R}^{'}+\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)}(\frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{R(p)}\otimes \frac{{\sigma}^{\text{dev}}}{R(p)})$

with ${\lambda}^{\text{*}}=K-\frac{2\mu }{3}\frac{G(\Delta p)}{H(\Delta p)}2{\mu}^{\text{*}}=2\mu \frac{G(\Delta p)}{H(\Delta p)}$

for the option FULL_MECA: ${\sigma}^{\text{dev}}=\tilde{\sigma}-X$

for the option RIGI_MECA_TANG: ${\sigma}^{\text{dev}}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}-{X}^{-}$

with $H(\Delta p)=1+\frac{\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\xi \Delta p}{R(p)}$

and $G(\Delta p)=1+\frac{3}{2}C\xi \frac{\Delta p}{R(p)}$

## 5 Identification of the material characteristics

Consider a uniaxial tension-compression test, [Figure]. One proposes to show how it makes it possible to identify the Prager constant and the isotropic hardening function. In such a test, the various tensors have fixed directions, that is:

$\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma \Delta X=X\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}{\varepsilon}^{p}D$     [eq 5-1]

with $D=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}2/3& & \\ & -1/3& \\ & & -1/3\end{array}\right]$

As long as the loading is monotonic, and therefore in the tension phase, one immediately obtains the following relations:

$p={\varepsilon}^{p}X=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}{s}^{t}=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}+R({\varepsilon}^{p})$     [eq 5-2]

The Prager constant is determined by the first plastic flow in compression, since one has:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{A}^{t}=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}_{A}^{p}+R({\varepsilon}_{A}^{p})\\ {\sigma}_{A}^{c}=\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}_{A}^{p}-R({\varepsilon}_{A}^{p})\end{array}\Rightarrow C=\frac{{\sigma}_{A}^{t}+{\sigma}_{A}^{c}}{3{\varepsilon}_{A}^{p}}$     [eq 5-3]

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.16/10001B2600003B88000042257C975E0B81367401.svg)

**Figure** 5-a **:** Uniaxial tension-compression test

The hardening curve ${\sigma}^{t}=F({\varepsilon}^{p})$ is deduced from the traction curve ${\sigma}^{t}=F(\varepsilon )$ supplied by the user under the keywords ECRO_LINE ( (SY and D_SIGM_EPSI (linear hardening)) or else TRACTION (arbitrary hardening). It finally makes it possible to obtain the isotropic hardening function by [eq]:

$R({\varepsilon}^{p})={s}^{t}({\varepsilon}^{p})-\frac{3}{2}C{\varepsilon}^{p}$

For the effective computation of R(p), following document R5.03.02, one takes advantage of the linearity (ECMI_LINE) or of the piecewise linearity of the traction curve (ECMI_TRAC):

ECMI_LINE:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}^{t}=F({\varepsilon}^{p})={\sigma}_{y}+\frac{E.{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}p\\ R(p)={\sigma}_{y}+(\frac{E.{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}-\frac{3}{2}C)p={\sigma}_{y}+{R}^{'}.p\\ \end{array}$     [eq 5-4]

Equation [eq] then becomes:

$\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\Deltap +{\sigma}_{y}+{R}^{'}.(p+\Deltap )={s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}$     [eq 5-5]

ECMI_TRAC:

$\begin{array}{}{\sigma}^{t}=F({\varepsilon}^{p})={\sigma}_{i}+\frac{{\sigma}_{i+1}-{s}_{i}}{{p}_{i-1}-{p}_{i}}(p-{p}_{i}),\text{pour}{p}_{i}\text{<=}p\text{<=}{p}_{i+1}\\ R(p)={\sigma}_{i}+\frac{{\sigma}_{i+1}-{\sigma}_{i}}{{p}_{i-1}-{p}_{i}}(p-{p}_{i})-\frac{3}{2}\text{Cp}={\sigma}_{i}-\frac{{\sigma}_{i+1}-{\sigma}_{i}}{{p}_{i-1}-{p}_{i}}{p}_{i}+{R}^{'}.p\end{array}$     [eq 5-6]

## 6 Particular case of plane stress: computation of \Delta p

One must add to equations [eq] to [eq] the plane-stress condition ${s}_{33}=0$ , which adds an unknown (the corresponding strain):

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{s}^{{H}^{-}}+K\text{tr}(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$     [eq 6-1]

$\tilde{\sigma}=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}+2\mu (\Delta \tilde{\varepsilon}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p})$     [eq 6-2]

$X=\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}^{-}+C\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}$     [eq 6-3]

${\sigma}_{33}=0$     [eq 6-4]

Then, equation [eq] becomes:

$\tilde{\sigma}-X=\frac{\mu}{{\mu}^{-}}{\tilde{\sigma}}^{-}-\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}^{-}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{c}-(2\mu +C)\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{y}={\tilde{\sigma}}^{e}-(2\mu +C)\Delta {\epsilon}^{p}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{y}$     [eq 6-5]

where $\Delta {\tilde{\epsilon}}^{c}$ is entirely determined by the elastic behaviour:

$\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{33}^{c}=\frac{-\nu }{1-\nu }(\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{11}^{c}+\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{22}^{c}),\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{11}^{c}=\Delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{11}},\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}_{22}^{c}=\Delta \tilde{{\varepsilon}_{22}}$

and $\Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{y}=\left[\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \Deltay \end{array}\right]$ is unknown. One also assumes that ${\sigma}_{13}={\sigma}_{23}={\varepsilon}_{13}={\varepsilon}_{23}=0$ .

One still has:

$\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\frac{3}{2}\Deltap \frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}$     [eq 6-6]

$\mathrm{F}={\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-R\left(p\right)\le 0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\Delta p\ge 0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};F\Delta p=0$     [eq 6-7]

Elastic trial:

- If

$F={s}_{\text{eq}}^{e}-R({p}^{-})\text{<=}0$     [eq 6-8]

then

$\tilde{\sigma}={\tilde{s}}_{e}$ $\Deltap =0$ $\Deltay =0$     [eq 6-9]

${\sigma}^{H}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{\sigma}^{{H}^{-}}+K\text{tr}(\Delta {\varepsilon}^{c}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$     [eq 6-10]

- Otherwise, the solution is plastic: $\Delta p\text{>}0$ , $\Delta Y\ne 0$ . One can still reduce the problem to a scalar problem in $\Deltap$ .

Taking [eq] and [eq] into account, one observes that $\tilde{\sigma}-X$ is collinear with ${\tilde{s}}^{e}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{y}$ because:

$(\tilde{\sigma}-X)(1+\frac{\frac{3}{2}(2\mu +C)\Delta p}{R(p)})=(\tilde{\sigma}-X)H(\Delta p)=\left[{\tilde{s}}^{e}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{y}\right]$     [eq 6-11]

Therefore:

$({\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}-{X}_{33})H(\Delta p)=\left[{\tilde{s}}_{33}^{e}+\frac{4}{3}\mu \Delta Y\right]$     [eq 6-12]

We shall express equation [eq] as a function of $\Delta p$ only. According to [eq]:

${\sigma}_{33}=0={\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}+{\sigma}^{{}^{H}}={\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}+{\sigma}_{e}^{H}+K.\Deltay$     [eq 6-13]

with ${\sigma}_{e}^{H}=\frac{K}{{K}^{-}}{\sigma}^{{H}^{-}}+K\text{tr}(\Delta {\varepsilon}^{c}-\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}})$

Using [eq], [eq] and the incompressibility of the plastic strains, one can show that:

${\tilde{s}}_{33}^{e}+{\sigma}_{e}^{{}^{H}}=-\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}_{33}^{-}$     [eq 6-14]

Then:

${\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}={\tilde{s}}_{33}^{e}-\mathrm{K.}\Delta Y+\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}_{33}^{-}$     [eq 6-15]

Since, according to [eq]:

${X}_{33}=\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}_{33}^{-}+C.\Delta {\varepsilon}_{33}^{p}=\frac{C}{{C}^{-}}{X}_{33}^{-}+C.\frac{3}{2}\Delta p\frac{{\tilde{\sigma}}_{33}-{X}_{33}}{R(p)}$     [eq 6-16]

with $G(\Deltap )=1+\frac{3}{2}C\frac{\Deltap }{R(p)}$

$\Deltay .(\frac{4}{3}\mu +K\frac{H(\Deltap )}{G(\Deltap )})=\left[{\tilde{s}}_{33}^{e}(\frac{H(\Deltap )}{G(\Deltap )}-1)\right]$     [eq 6-18]

${(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}H(\Deltap )=R({p}^{-}+\Deltap )H(\Deltap )={\left[{\tilde{s}}^{e}+2\mu \Delta {\tilde{\varepsilon}}^{y}\right]}_{\text{eq}}$     [eq 6-19]

## 7 Meaning of the internal variables

The internal variables of the model at the Gauss points (VARI_ELGA) are, for all the modellings:

- VARI_1= $\text{p}$ : the cumulative plastic strain (positive or zero)
- VARI_2 = $\xi$ : equal to 1 if the Gauss point has undergone plastic flow during the increment, or 0 otherwise.

$\text{X}$ : back-stress tensor:

For the 3D modelling:

- VARI_3 = ${X}_{11}$
- VARI_4 = ${X}_{22}$
- VARI_5 = ${X}_{33}$
- VARI_6 = ${X}_{12}$
- VARI_7 = ${X}_{13}$
- VARI_8 = ${X}_{23}$

For the modellings D_PLAN, C_PLAN, AXIS

- VARI_3 = ${X}_{11}$
- VARI_4 = ${X}_{22}$
- VARI_5 = ${X}_{33}$
- VARI_6 = ${X}_{12}$

For the shell modellings (DKT, COQUE_3D), in the local frame and at each integration point of each layer:

- VARI_3 = ${X}_{11}$
- VARI_4 = ${X}_{22}$
- VARI_5 = ${X}_{33}$
- VARI_6 = ${X}_{12}$
