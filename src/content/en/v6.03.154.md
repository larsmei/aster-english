# v6.03.154 SSNP154 – Contact validation Benchmark NAFEMS 1: cylinder roller contact

**Summary:**

This is the first test case in a NAFEMS benchmark for the validation of contact and frangling. Benchmark references are obtained with Abaqus and MARC codes.

This test modeled Hertz contact with and without friction between a massive and a cylinder of different stiffeners gridded with different finesses.

A analytical solution exists for the case without friction.

This test includes 6 modellings for testing:

- linear and quadratic elementss,
- ‘CONTINUE' contact treatment formulations without and with friction.
- contact treatment formulations ‘DISCRETE

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is modelled on flat deformations.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.154/100000000000019B000002580744CFEAB0C9D0D2.png)

$B$ is noted the point on the upper side of the block belonging to the symmetry plane.

### 1.2 Material properties

**Bloc**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $70000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

**Cylinder**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $210000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

The friction coefficient between the block and the cylinder is $\mu =0,1$ **.**

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The structure being symmetrical and subject to a load respecting symmetry, only half is represented. Therefore, $\mathit{DX}=0$ is applied on the plane of symmetry.

The block is clamped at its base:

- $\mathit{DX}=0$
- $\mathit{DY}=0$

The cylinder shall be subjected to a point force on its top:

- $\mathit{FY}=35\mathit{kN}$ , i.e. $\mathit{FY}=17500N$ for the half-structure

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is derived from results obtained with Abaqus and MARC codes in a NAFEMS benchmark for the [bib1] contact-frost validation.

For modellings without friction, a comparison with Hertz analytical solution is made.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Vertical displacement of point $B$ (according to $y$ ) (external reference).

Contact pressure at point $B$ (external reference). The raised contact pressure is that extrapolated from the stresses in the volume.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Important (average code).

### 2.4 Bibliographic reference

[1] A. KONTER. *Advanced Finish Element Contact Benchmarks* . NAFEMS, 2006.

## 9 Summary of the results

This test validates the contact-frotting relative to an analytical solution, but also compared to references given by commercial calculation codes (Abaqus and MARC).

A good agreement between the analytical results and those obtained by *Code_Aster* is observed, and a good agreement with the reference results for friction calculations is observed.

It should be noted that the continuous and discreet formulations give identical results with the following restrictions:

- continuous calculation is easier to perform because the vertical rigid body movement is automatically blocked
- the calculation with friction on a quadratic mesh could not be conducted in discrete formulation

In quadratic, the contact pressure obtained from degree of freedom LAGS_C in continuous formulation has much less oscillations than linear.
