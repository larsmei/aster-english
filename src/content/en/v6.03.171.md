# v6.03.171 SSNP171 - Closing of a flexing crack

**Summary**

This test allows the XFEM method to be evaluated with contact when cracks are closed.

A 2D bending test piece is considered, with a central crack. The bending of the test piece causes a partial closing of the crack. On the crack bottom remaining at the opening, the stress factor ${K}_{I}$ is tested. The theory predicts that the contact factor corrects the ${K}_{I}$ factor of about $8\text{\%}$.

To gauge the influence of the contact when closing, seven modellings are envisaged:

- Modelling A: 2D Modelling and linear elements, with interpenetration of crack lips.
- Modelling B: 2D Modelling and linear elements, with contact between crack lips.
- Modelization C: 2D Modelling and quadratic elements, with interpenetration of crack lips.
- Modelization D: 2D Modelling and quadratic elements, with contact between crack lips.
- Modelling E: 3D Modelling and linear elements, with interpenetration of crack lips.
- Modelling F: 3D Modelling and linear elements, with contact between crack lips.
- G Modelling: 3D Modelling and quadratic elements, with interpenetration of crack lips.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The $\mathrm{2D}$ structure is a rectangular test piece ($\mathit{LX}=10$ , $\mathit{LY}=20$ ), with a straight and horizontal central crack []. The crack half length is constant ($a=1$ ).

The 3D structure corresponds to an extrusion in the Z direction of the 2D test piece. The test piece becomes parallelepipedic in dimensions: $\mathit{LX}=10$ , $\mathit{LY}=20$ , $\mathit{LZ}=1$ . The $a=1$ length crack in the plane (X,Y) is also extruded following Z.

The nodes rated $\mathit{NOEUD1}$ , $\mathit{NOEUD2}$ on it are used to impose the boundary conditions, which are explained in paragraph [§ 1.3 ].

Figure 1.1-1: géométrie de la plaque fissurée 2D

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus:            $E={10}^{6}\mathit{Pa}$

Poisson's ratio:            $\nu =0$

Density: $\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Problem 2D(modellings A, B, C, D) *:*

The load consists of applying a linear force density, set back on the lower and upper edges of the test piece [].

On the upper edge a force density is applied: ${F}_{1}(X,Y)=X$

On the lower edge a force density is applied: ${F}_{2}(X,Y)=-X$

This load exerts a compression on half of the test piece (half left), a tensile on the other half of the test piece (half right). This follows a bending of the test piece [].

This load will require:

- the closing of the crack, in the vicinity of the $\mathit{P1}=(-a,0)$ crack bottom, contained in half of the compression test piece;
- opening of the crack, near the $\mathit{P2}=(a,0)$ crack bottom, contained in half of the tensile test piece (see [])

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.171/100000000000016D0000006A0D9713365E3496B2.png)

Figure 1.3-1: Fissure partiellement fermée

In order to block rigid modes, the nodes $\mathit{NOEUD1}$ , $\mathit{NOEUD2}$ moves are blocked as follows:

- ${\mathit{DX}}^{1}={\mathit{DY}}^{1}=0$
- ${\mathit{DY}}^{2}=0$ .

This block is applied given the displacement field []. Indeed, the displacement is strictly zero at the nodes considered, because they belong to the symmetry plane of the test piece, around which the test piece is bending.

3D problem(modellings E, F, G,) *:*

The 3D problem takes over all the boundary conditions of the 2D problem by adding only direction Z:

- the forces on the top face ${F}_{1}(X,Y,Z)=X$ ,
- the forces on the lower face ${F}_{2}(X,Y,Z)=-X$ ,
- Blocking of ${\mathit{DX}}^{1}={\mathit{DY}}^{1}={\mathit{DZ}}^{1}=0$ rigid modes, ${\mathit{DX}}^{2}={\mathit{DY}}^{2}=0$ and a 3rd point in the symmetry plane for the last ${\mathit{DZ}}^{3}=0$ rigid mode.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

#### 2.1.1 ModelizationsA, C, E, G

The stress factor ${K}_{I}$ is tested against the contactless analytical value.

For a bending test piece and a straight crack $[-a\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}},\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}a]$ abscisse , the analytical value of ${K}_{I}$ without closing the crack is determined by the formula of Bui [ 7 ]:

${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}\underset{-a}{\overset{a}{\int}}-t\sqrt{\frac{a-t}{t+a}}\mathit{dt}$

where $a$ is the half-length of the crack.

From the above integral, the following simplified expression of ${K}_{I}$ is calculated:

${K}_{I}=\frac{{a}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$

Indeed, in the integral, several changes of variables are made:

$t\to \mathit{at}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{a}^{2}\underset{-1}{\overset{1}{\int}}-t\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{t+1}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to t-1$ on a ${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{a}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{2}{\int}}-(t-1)\sqrt{\frac{2-t}{t}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to \mathrm{2t}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{\mathrm{2a}}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}-(\mathrm{2t}-1)\sqrt{\frac{1-t}{t}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to {t}^{2}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{\mathrm{2a}}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}-2({\mathrm{2t}}^{2}-1)\sqrt{1-{t}^{2}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to \sint$ on a ${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{\mathrm{2a}}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{2}}{\int}}\mathrm{2cos}\mathrm{2t}{\cos}^{2}t\mathit{dt}$

After developing the product of $\cos$ :

${K}_{I}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi a}}{\mathrm{2a}}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{2}}{\int}}(\frac{1}{2}+\cos\mathrm{2t}+\frac{1}{2}\cos\mathrm{4t})\mathit{dt}$

Hence the final expression (after integration):

${K}_{I}=\frac{{a}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\pi}}{2}$

The half length being unit ($a=1$ ), the stress intensity factor is tested:

${K}_{I}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\approx 0,88629$

By construction, there is no mode II. So we test: ${K}_{\mathit{II}}=0$

On the other hand, the rate of return of analytical energy is:

$G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})\approx 7,853982E-7$

#### 2.1.2 Modelization B, D, F

For a bending test piece and a straight crack $[-a\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}},\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}a]$ abscisse , the analytical value of ${K}_{I}$ with crack closure is determined by the formula of Bui [ 7 ]:

${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi (a-c)}}\underset{c}{\overset{a}{\int}}t\sqrt{\frac{t-c}{a-t}}\mathit{dt}$

where $c$ is the point abscisse for which the crack begins to open (see [])

and $a$ is the half-length of the crack.

It is shown analytically [ 7 ] that $c=-\frac{a}{3}$ is abscissa

So, ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}\underset{-a/3}{\overset{a}{\int}}t\sqrt{\frac{t+a/3}{a-t}}\mathit{dt}$

From the above integral, the following simplified expression of ${K}_{I}$ is calculated:

${K}_{I}={(\frac{2}{3}a)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{(\pi )}$

Indeed, in the integral, several changes of variables are made:

$t\to \mathit{at}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\underset{-1/3}{\overset{1}{\int}}t\sqrt{\frac{t+1/3}{1-t}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to t-\frac{1}{3}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\underset{0}{\overset{4/3}{\int}}(t-\frac{1}{3})\sqrt{\frac{t}{4/3-t}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to \frac{4}{3}t$ on a ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\frac{4}{9}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}(\mathrm{4t}-1)\sqrt{\frac{t}{1-t}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to {t}^{2}$ on a ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\frac{4}{9}\underset{0}{\overset{1}{\int}}({\mathrm{4t}}^{2}-1)\frac{{\mathrm{2t}}^{2}}{\sqrt{1-{t}^{2}}}\mathit{dt}$

$t\to \sint$ on a ${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\frac{4}{9}\underset{0}{\overset{\pi /2}{\int}}({\mathrm{4sin}}^{2}t-1)({\mathrm{2sin}}^{2}t)\mathit{dt}$

After developing the $\sint$ powers:

${K}_{I}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2\pi a}}{a}^{2}\frac{4}{9}\underset{0}{\overset{\pi /2}{\int}}(2-\mathrm{3cos}\mathrm{2t}+\cos\mathrm{4t})\mathit{dt}$

Hence the final expression (after integration):

${K}_{I}={(\frac{2}{3}a)}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{(\pi )}$

The half length being unit ($a=1$ ), the stress intensity factor is tested:

${K}_{I}={(\frac{2}{3})}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{(\pi )}\approx 0,9648017$

By construction, there is no mode II. So we test: ${K}_{\mathit{II}}=0$

On the other hand, the rate of return of analytical energy is:

$G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})\approx 9,308423E-7$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

We test ${K}_{I}$ , ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and $G$ .

#### 2.2.1 modellings A, C, E, F, G

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the extended elements finite method ($\text{X-FEM}$ ) is used. The finished elements are linear.

A elements X-FEM enrichment radius is defined as ${R}_{\mathit{ENRI}}=0.5$ crack bottom. This enrichment radius allows for the more accurate capture of the singular asymptotic solution at the bottom of the crack. The relatively wide radius size (25% of the crack length) does not introduce conditioning problems, given the new [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) crack bottom approximation.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Mesh is refined in the centre of the test piece to optimize the calculation of the displacement solution near the crack [].

## 10 Summary of results

When closing a crack, the consideration of lip contact significantly affects the stress strength factor ${K}_{I}$ , compared to the contactless XFEM method.

In this test case, there is a difference from the order of $8\text{\%}$ to the value of ${K}_{I}$ calculated by Aster, from Modelling A (without contact between lips) to Modelling B (with contact). This difference is consistent with theoretical predictions.

Indeed, the theory provides that the difference is:

$\frac{{K}_{I}^{\text{avec contact}}-{K}_{I}^{\text{sans contact}}}{{K}_{I}^{\text{avec contact}}}=\frac{({(2/3)}^{\frac{3}{2}}-1/2)}{{(2/3)}^{\frac{3}{2}}}\approx 8,14413\text{\%}$

The results obtained show that the value of ${K}_{I}$ with contact consideration is well evaluated and higher than that obtained without contact consideration. This result underlines the importance of considering contact-related effects in numerical cracking simulations, as this example illustrates a case where the solution with interpenetration (without contact) is not conservative of point in relation to stress-intensity factors. This may prove to be not without consequence for the study of propagation, based on propagation criteria written according to stress-intensity factors.
