# v6.04.510 SSNV510 - Uniaxial compression of a multi-fissed block

**Summary:**

This test validates the multi-Heaviside approach for elements X-FEM. It is a test case where several laterally compressed interfaces are introduced. The mesh is quite rough so that mesh cells see several cracks. Some nodes also see several cracks. These nodes then contain several Heaviside enrichments. It is verified that these different enrichments are well taken into account at the kinematic level and that the stiffness matrices associated with each area between two interfaces allow to obtain the correct distorted ones. The approach is tested with and without contact.

## 2 reference solution

The Poisson's ratio is zero, so we have to find uniform compression on each of the 4 stages of the structure. In other words, for a given stage, the displacement of the structure is proportional to the position according to $X$ and it depends on the pressure that varies according to $Y$. The displacement according to $Y$ depends only linearly on $Y$ because the pressure imposed ${\mathit{Press}}_{y}$ is constant.

So for all points of the structure the displacement is worth:

${\mathit{Depl}}_{X}(X,Y)=(2-X)\frac{{\mathit{Press}}_{x}(Y)}{E}$                         éq 2.1-1

${\mathit{Depl}}_{Y}(Y)=-Y\frac{{\mathit{Press}}_{y}}{E}$         éq 2.1-2

### 2.1 Calculation of the energy of the structure

Either ${p}_{x}=10\text{MPa}$ and ${p}_{y}=10\text{MPa}$ in contact cases, ${p}_{y}=0\text{MPa}$ in contactless cases. The stress tensor analytical solution is:

$\sigma ={\sigma}_{xx}(y){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-{p}_{y}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$

where:

${\sigma}_{xx}(y)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}0\text{pour}y<\frac{1}{2},\\ -{p}_{x}\text{pour}y\in \left[\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}\right[,\\ -2{p}_{x}\text{pour}y\in \left[\frac{3}{2},\frac{5}{2}\right[,\\ -3{p}_{x}\text{pour}y\in \left[\frac{5}{2},\frac{7}{2}\right[,\\ -4{p}_{x}\text{pour}y\ge \frac{7}{2},\end{array}$

$\Omega =[0,2]\times [0,4]$ is the domain occupied by the solid. The energy of the structure is defined by:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\Omega}\sigma :\epsilon \mathit{dS}$

Since $\nu =0$, we have $\epsilon =\frac{1}{E}\sigma$. Hence:

$\sigma :\epsilon =\frac{1}{E}\left({\sigma}_{xx}{(y)}^{2}+{p}_{y}^{2}\right)$

So we have:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{E}\underset{0}{\overset{2}{\int}}\left(\underset{0}{\overset{4}{\int}}\left({\sigma}_{xx}{(y)}^{2}+{p}_{y}^{2}\right)\mathit{dy}\right)\mathit{dx}$

Either:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{E}\left(\underset{\frac{1}{2}}{\overset{\frac{3}{2}}{\int}}{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{3}{2}}{\overset{\frac{5}{2}}{\int}}4{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{5}{2}}{\overset{\frac{7}{2}}{\int}}9{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{7}{2}}{\overset{4}{\int}}16{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{0}{\overset{4}{\int}}{p}_{y}^{2}\mathit{dy}\right)$

Therefore, for ${p}_{x}$ and ${p}_{x}$ expressed in MPa:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{E}\left(22{p}_{x}^{2}+4{p}_{y}^{2}\right)$

This result is valid for flat stresses and flat deformations. In the 3D case, the thickness chosen is 1 m. The expression of energy is identical, but the units are modified. In flat stresses and flat deformations, with contact:

${E}^{e}=2,6\times {10}^{7}\text{MJ}\times {\text{m}}^{-1}$ ,

3D contact:

${E}^{e}=2,6\times {10}^{7}\text{MJ}$ ,

Of flat stresses and flat deformations, without contact:

${E}^{e}=2,2\times {10}^{7}\text{MJ}\times {\text{m}}^{-1}$ ,

3D, contactless:

${E}^{e}=2,2\times {10}^{7}\text{MJ}$ .

### 2.2 Calculation of the L2 standard of displacement

The analytical solution moving field is

$u=\frac{{\sigma}_{xx}(y)}{E}(2-x){e}_{x}-\frac{{p}_{y}}{E}y{e}_{y},$

The ${L}^{2}$ displacement standard is defined by:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\int}_{\Omega}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}\mathit{dS}$

On a:

${\Vert u\Vert }^{2}=\frac{{{\sigma}_{xx}(y)}^{2}}{{E}^{2}}{(2-x)}^{2}-\frac{{p}_{y}^{2}}{{E}^{2}}{y}^{2}$

So we have:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{{E}^{2}}\underset{0}{\overset{4}{\int}}\left(\underset{0}{\overset{2}{\int}}\left[{{\sigma}_{xx}(y)}^{2}{(2-x)}^{2}-{p}_{y}^{2}{y}^{2}\right]\mathit{dx}\right)\mathit{dy}$

Either:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{{E}^{2}}\underset{0}{\overset{4}{\int}}\left(\frac{8}{3}{{\sigma}_{xx}(y)}^{2}-2{p}_{y}^{2}{y}^{2}\right)\mathit{dy}.$

D'où:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{{E}^{2}}\left[\frac{8}{3}\left(\underset{\frac{1}{2}}{\overset{\frac{3}{2}}{\int}}{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{3}{2}}{\overset{\frac{5}{2}}{\int}}4{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{5}{2}}{\overset{\frac{7}{2}}{\int}}9{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}+\underset{\frac{7}{2}}{\overset{4}{\int}}16{p}_{x}^{2}\mathit{dy}\right)+2\underset{0}{\overset{4}{\int}}{p}_{y}^{2}{y}^{2}\mathit{dy}\right].$

And finally:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}=\frac{1}{{E}^{2}}\left(\frac{176}{3}{p}_{x}^{2}+\frac{128}{3}{p}_{y}^{2}\right).$

Either:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}=\frac{1}{E}\sqrt{\frac{176}{3}{p}_{x}^{2}+\frac{128}{3}{p}_{y}^{2}}.$

This result is valid for flat stresses and flat deformations. In the 3D case, the thickness chosen is 1 m. The expression of the ${L}^{2}$ standard of displacement is identical, but the units are modified.

The following are then, in flat and flat deformations, with contact:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}\approx 1,00664459137{\text{m}}^{2}$

3D contact:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}\approx 1,00664459137{\text{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}$

Of flat stresses and flat deformations, without contact:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}\approx 0,765941686205{\text{m}}^{2}$

3D, contactless:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}\approx 0,765941686205{\text{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}$

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.510/Object_11.png)

## 9 Summary of the results

This test enables the multi-Heaviside to be activated for elements X-FEM. It is shown that one can differentiate the number of cracks "viewed" by mesh cell from that "viewed" by node. In the example of this test case, some nodes see a maximum of 2 cracks while the mesh cells see a maximum of 4. It is also shown that it is possible to take into account contact conditions on interfaces, then the nodes are also enriched by multiple degrees of freedom from Lagrange.

The approach was validated in D_PLAN, C_PLAN and 3D for small and large slips.
