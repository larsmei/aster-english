# v3.02.305 SSLP305 - Slim disc in concentrated load support

**Summary:**

The purpose of the test is to validate the calculation of potential energy in linear elasticity.

A single axisymmetric model is presented.

The reference solution is an analytical.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.305/10000000000003F60000018ACE4B94C55F42C604.png)

Diameter: $\phi =0.5m$

Thickness: $h=0.005m$

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus: $E=2.1\times {10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$

Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0.3$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

- Push on edge ($w=0$ )
- Point $A$ concentrated load: $P=–350N$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Not applicable for static analysis.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

- The value of the axial displacement at the center of the disc (point A) is given by:

${W}_{a}=-\frac{P{\phi }^{2}}{64\pi D}\times \frac{3+\nu }{1+\nu }$

où $D=\frac{E{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}$

- The value of potential energy (at equilibrium) is given by:

${E}_{p}=-\frac{1}{2}P{W}_{a}$

- The absolute value of potential energy per radian is:

${e}_{p}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{P{W}_{a}}{2\pi }$

### 3.4 Remarks

- The value of the load to be supplied is reduced to a sector of 1 radian. Therefore, the value of the potential energy given on the result file corresponds to the deformation of this sector (to the sign next).
- The ENERPOT option actually calculates an strain energy:

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.305/Object_6.svg)

which is identical to the potential energy of the sign:

${E}_{p}=\frac{1}{2}{U}^{T}KU-{U}^{T}F=-\frac{1}{2}{U}^{T}F=-\frac{1}{2}{U}^{T}KU$ (car $\mathrm{KU}=F$ )

## 4 Summary of the results

These good results on displacement and l-strain energy (difference similar to 0.5% with the reference solution analytical) show that the calculation of this energy is correct. To get even closer to the reference value, the mesh should be further discretized.
