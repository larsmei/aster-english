# v2.01.101 SDLD101 - Simple Oscillator under Random Exciting

**Summary:**

A simple oscillator, consisting of a mass connected to a support by a spring and a shock absorber, is subjected to a random excitation transmitted by the support, of the required acceleration type.

This test uses the functionality of stochastic analysis and calculates the power spectral density (DSP) of mass motion from the white noise-like excitement given by its DSP as well.

The movement is calculated according to different options: relative, absolute, differential movement.

The statistical properties of the response are then calculated by moving to all options of random dynamic post-processing.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.101/1000055800000DF400000757BCE9867C7144ED7A.svg)

Excitation is an accelerated seismic movement imposed $\mathit{AX}$ applied to the support in the $\mathit{DX}$ direction.

We're interested in the $m$ mass movement.

### 1.2 Material properties

| Point mass: | $m=100\mathit{kg}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Elastic spring: | $k={10}^{6}N/m$ |
| Modal depreciation: | ${\xi}_{0}=0.05$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The problem is one-dimensional in the $x$ direction, and at 1 degree of freedom: the $m$ mass displacement.

Excitation is a spectral density of power (DSP), constant acceleration between $0.$ and $100\mathit{Hz}$.

It is applied to the support.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is analytic [bib1]. The own pulse of the oscillator is $\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$ ,

either ${\omega}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}=100\mathit{rad}/s$ , and ${f}_{o}=15,9155\mathit{Hz}$ .

In absolute motion, the DSP of the acceleration response noted ${G}_{\ddot{R}\ddot{R}}(\omega )$ is connected to the DSP of the ${G}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}$ acceleration excitation also by:

${G}_{\ddot{R}\ddot{R}}(\omega )=\frac{{\omega}_{0}^{4}+4{\xi}_{0}^{2}{\omega}_{0}^{2}{\omega}^{2}}{{({\omega}_{0}^{2}-{\omega}^{2})}^{2}+4{\xi}_{0}^{2}{\omega}_{0}^{2}{\omega}^{2}}{G}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )$

In relative movement, the following are used:

${G}_{\ddot{R}\ddot{R}}(\omega )={∣(\frac{{\omega}^{2}}{{\omega}_{0}^{2}-{\omega}^{2}+2j{\xi}_{0}{\omega}_{0}\omega })∣}^{2}{G}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )$

In differential movement, one has:

${G}_{\ddot{R}\ddot{R}}(\omega )={G}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )$

### 2.2 Baseline

The DSP of the $0,5,10,15,20\mathit{Hz}$ response is tested in all three cases of motion: absolute, relative and differential.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

## 4 Summary of the results

It is not surprising that the expected results for the random dynamic response are obtained with an accuracy of $\text{0\%}$. Indeed, the DSP responses are not the result of an iterative resolution process, but of an analytical expression involving modal transfer functions. This analytical expression coincides with the reference solution for this problem.

For the post-processing, there is no reference solution.
