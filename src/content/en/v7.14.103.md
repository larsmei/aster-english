# v7.14.103 EPICU03 - Validation of the POST_KCP command correction β in the case of a semi-elliptic defect.

**Summary:**

The POST_KCP control calculates the elastic stress intensity factor and the elasto-plastic stress intensity factor (also known as "plasticly corrected") using the nodes stress counting from mechanical resolution respectively via the influence coefficient method and the so-called β correction method described in the RSE-M annex 5.4.

This test validates the determination of stress intensity factors of a semi-elliptic defect under longitudinal orientation coating at first and circumferential orientation in a second stage at the point of the defect at the base metal side.

The thermo-mechanical transient considered to be spatially uniform at each moment and the thermal and mechanical properties of the coating as well as of the base metal being also spatially invariant, the absence of an effective defect in the mesh of the core zone of the tank, which is inherent in this simplified method of defect harmfulness analysis, allows this test to be carried out by using axisymmetric Modelling of the core zone of the tank.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The geometry studied is that of the core zone of a generic 1300 MWe tank.

### 1.2 Defect

In the simplified fault-harm analysis methodology, the method of calculating the stress strength factor elastic via the influence coefficient method assumes inherently the absence of effective fault modelling in the mesh. In fact, the mesh allows the stress to be calculated initially at the nodes and a post-processing is then applied to calculate the stress intensity factor elastic by the influence coefficient method from these stresses at the nodes (the method is detailed in [R7.02.10](doc:r7.02.10)).

For this test, the under-coating defect considered is semi-elliptic and longitudinal orientation in the first stage and circumferential orientation in a second stage. Its surface-side tip is strictly based on the interface between the coating and the base metal. Its dimensions are as follows (see figure below):

- depth: ${\mathit{prof}}_{\text{def}}=\mathrm{5mm}$
- length: $\mathrm{2c}\mathrm{=}\mathrm{25mm}$

![Figure](/figures/v7.14.103/Shape1.gif)

### 1.3 General description of thermomechanical coupling

The study consists of a thermo-mechanical chaining. The thermal calculation and the mechanical calculation are performed on the same mesh. The mesh used for these two calculations consists only of 2nd order d-elements (mesh called "quadratic"). Given the invariances of the problem, the chosen Modelling is axisymmetric and only one mesh cell is used in the direction of the height of the vessel.

### 1.5 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.5.1 Step 1: Nonlinear thermal calculation

The limit conditions applied to the thermal calculation are summarized in the Figure below and are broken down as follows:

- internal wall temperature,
- thermal insulation in external wall.

![Figure](/figures/v7.14.103/100000000000020A000000EBB34A80969D7BA2A2.png)

An analytical thermal transient $T(t,z,\theta )$ , with an initial temperature of 280°C, is imposed on the inner wall of the tank. This transient describes the temporal evolution of the ${T}_{i}$ temperature in the inner wall of the tank in relation to the $\theta$ azimutal position and the $z$ longitudinal position , thus providing a three-dimensional analytical transient that is quite close to a real transient.

The analytical expression of the transient is provided in Equation {1}.

$T(t,z,\theta )={T}_{\mathit{is}}+{T}_{1}\times {e}^{\frac{-t}{[(\sum_{i=0}^{6}{a}_{k}{\theta}^{k})({t}_{\mathit{rg}}(1-\frac{z}{{H}_{\mathit{cuve}}})+{t}_{\mathit{rgcuve}}\times \frac{Z}{{H}_{\mathit{cuve}}})]}}+{T}_{2}\times \sin({f}_{\mathrm{2nd}}\times t){e}^{\frac{-t}{{t}_{\mathit{r2nd}}}}$             (1)

with the following notation:

- ${T}_{\mathit{is}}$: temperature of the safety injection
- ${T}_{1}$: temperature of the amplitude of the decay between the initial and final temperatures (safety injection temperature)
- ${T}_{2}$: temperature of secondary oscillations around decay
- ${T}_{\mathit{rg}}$ overall response time
- ${T}_{\mathit{rgcuve}}$ overall response time from the bottom of the tank
- ${t}_{\mathit{r2nd}}$ secondary oscillation response time
- ${f}_{\mathrm{2nd}}$ frequency of secondary oscillations
- ${H}_{\mathit{cuve}}$ height of tank

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Baseline

The reference results are derived from a similar calculation using the CUVE1D fault analysis tool. It should be noted that CUVE1D is based on a version prior to the 2016 RSE-M and therefore applies beta revision associated with band defects. In POST_KCP, the user can choose between beta_2D revision for band or beta_3D defects (according to the 2016 RSE-M) for semi-elliptic defects. To reproduce the CUVE1D results in this test case, beta_2D revision is used.

### 2.2 Uncertainty about the solution

The uncertainties on reference solution are estimated at 2%.

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case validates the application of the POST_KCP command for beta correction at the B-point at the base metal side, in the case of a semi-elliptically coated defect of longitudinal orientation and circumferential orientation.
