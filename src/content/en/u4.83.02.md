# u4.83.02 Operator CALC_FATIGUE

## 1 Purpose

Compute a field of fatigue damage undergone by a structure; a critical plane in which the shear is maximum; or a maximum admissible vibration amplitude.

**Analysis of a damage field**: from a history of equivalent stresses (signed von Mises stresses) or of equivalent strains (signed second invariant) computed at the nodes or at the Gauss points, a field of quantity is computed which contains the damage undergone by the structure at each node or at each Gauss point. The elementary loading cycles are extracted by a cycle-counting method (RAINFLOW method); the total damage undergone by the structure is the sum of the damages associated with the elementary cycles.

**Crack-initiation criterion:** in order to compute the damage, a crack-initiation criterion is indispensable. The crack-initiation criteria are supplied by the keyword (such as the Dang-Van criterion…). It is also possible for the user to build a criterion as a formula of predefined quantities.

**Critical plane and maximum shear**: from a stress history computed at the Gauss points or at the nodes, in the case where the loading is periodic, a field of quantity is computed which contains, among other things: the half-amplitude of the maximum shear, the associated normal vector, the number of cycles to failure and the damage corresponding to the Gauss points or to the nodes. If the loading is non-periodic, the field of quantities contains the maximum damage and the associated normal vector at the Gauss points or at the nodes.

**Maximum admissible vibration amplitude**: this option aims to estimate the maximum admissible vibration amplitude of a structure subjected to a static loading (known) and to a dynamic loading (unknown). From the static stress and the modal stresses of the eigenmodes considered, computed at the Gauss points or at the nodes, the maximum vibration amplitude is computed using a uniaxial fatigue criterion.

Produces a concept of type cham_elem or cham_no.

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Keyword TYPE_CALCUL

This keyword makes it possible to compute

- either a field of fatigue damage undergone by a structure, if TYPE_CALCUL = ‘CUMUL_DOMMAGE’;
- or the critical plane in which the shear is maximum, if TYPE_CALCUL = ‘FATIGUE_MULTI’;
- or the maximum vibration amplitude admissible for a structure subjected to a vibratory loading, if TYPE_CALCUL = ‘FATIGUE_VIBR’.

In the first two cases, the loading of the structure is known (time evolution of the stresses or of the strains) and one is interested in the damage or in the associated critical plane.

In the last case, the static loading of the structure is known (typically the centrifugal forces for a turbine blade) but not the dynamic loading (typically the vibration of the blade). The option ‘FATIGUE_VIBR’ then makes it possible to estimate the maximum vibration amplitude admissible for the structure in order to have an unlimited endurance. The principle of the analysis is described in § 22.

### 3.2 Operands common to all the options

#### 3.2.1 Operand MATER

```text
◊    MATER = mater
```

Makes it possible to specify the name of the material mater created by DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01).

The material mater must contain the definition of the Wöhler curve of the material for the damage analysis by the methods 'WOHLER' and 'TAHERI_MIXTE' and the definition of the Manson-Coffin curve of the material for the damage analysis by the methods 'MANSON_COFFIN', 'TAHERI_MANSON' and 'TAHERI_MIXTE'.

For analyses of type ‘FATIGUE_VIBR’, the material must in addition contain the ultimate stress (operator DEFI_MATERIAU, factor keyword RCCM, operand SU).

#### 3.2.2 Operand INFO

```text
◊    INFO            =    /    1            Aucune impression.
/    2
```

Printout of the parameters of the damage analysis (number of order numbers, number of analysis points, type of the damage analysis (stresses, strains), location of the damage (nodes or Gauss points), type of the equivalent component (VMIS_SG or INVA_2SG), method of extraction of the cycles (RAINFLOW) and method of damage analysis (WOHLER or MANSON_COFFIN or TAHERI_MANSON or TAHERI_MIXTE).

- point by point, of the loading history, of the extracted cycles and of the damage value.
- of the damage field.

The printouts are made in the file MESSAGE.

#### 3.3.3 Operand DOMMAGE

In order to compute the damage undergone by a structure, the elementary cycles of the loading history must first be extracted.

Many methods are available for this. The method available in *Code_Aster*  for the damage analysis by the Wöhler or Manson-Coffin method is the cascade range-counting method or Rainflow method [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01).

For the damage analysis by the methods TAHERI_MANSON and TAHERI_MIXTE, the so-called natural counting method is used, which consists in generating cycles in the order of their application.

Once the elementary cycles have been extracted, this operand makes it possible to specify the damage-analysis method for each elementary cycle.


```text
♦    DOMMAGE =    / 'WOHLER'
```

For a loading history of stress type, the number of cycles to failure is determined by interpolation of the Wöhler curve of the material for a given alternating-stress level (each elementary cycle corresponds to a stress-amplitude level $\Delta \sigma =∣{\sigma}_{max}-{\sigma}_{min}∣$ and an alternating stress ${S}_{\mathit{alt}}=1/2\Delta \sigma$ ).

The WOHLER method can be used only for the options 'DOMA_ELNO_SIGM' or 'DOMA_ELGA_SIGM'. In addition, the specified resultat concept must contain respectively the field of symbolic name SIEQ_ELNO or SIEQ_ELGA (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP).

The Wöhler curve of the material must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01), in one of the three possible forms [R7.04.02](doc:r7.04.02):

- function discretised point by point (keyword FATIGUE, operand WOHLER),
- analytical Basquin form (keyword FATIGUE, operands A_BASQUIN and BETA_BASQUIN),
- “current-zone” form (keyword FATIGUE, operands E_REFE, A0, A1, A2, A3 and SL, and keyword ELAS, operand E).

**Note on the fatigue curves:**

*For small amplitudes, the problem of extending the fatigue curve may arise: for example, for the RCC-M fatigue curves beyond 106 cycles, the corresponding stress 180 MPa is regarded as an endurance limit, that is, any stress lower than 180 MPa must produce a zero usage factor, or an infinite allowable number of cycles.*

*In Code_Aster, the endurance limit is fixed at 10 million cycles.*

*The method adopted here corresponds to this notion of endurance limit: if the stress amplitude is lower than the first abscissa of the fatigue curve, a zero usage factor is taken, that is, an infinite allowable number of cycles.*


```text
♦    DOMMAGE =    / 'MANSON_COFFIN'
```

For a loading history of strain type, the number of cycles to failure is determined by interpolation of the Manson-Coffin curve of the material for a given alternating-strain level (each elementary cycle corresponds to a strain-amplitude level $\Delta \varepsilon =∣{\varepsilon}_{\max}-{\varepsilon}_{\min}∣$ and an alternating strain ${E}_{\mathit{alt}}=1/2\Delta \varepsilon$ ).

The MANSON_COFFIN method can be used only for the options 'DOMA_ELNO_EPSI' or 'DOMA_ELGA_EPSI', 'DOMA_ELNO_EPME' or 'DOMA_ELGA_EPME'. In addition, the specified resultat concept must contain respectively the field of symbolic name EPEQ_ELNO, EPEQ_ELGA, EPMQ_ELNO or EPMQ_ELGA (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP).

The Manson-Coffin curve must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) (keyword FATIGUE, operand MANSON_COFFIN).


```text
♦    DOMMAGE =    / 'TAHERI_MANSON'
```

This damage-analysis method applies only to loadings of strain type, that is, for the options 'DOMA_ELNO_EPSI', 'DOMA_ELGA_EPSI', 'DOMA_ELNO_EPME' or 'DOMA_ELGA_EPME'. In addition, the specified result concept must contain respectively the field of symbolic name EPEQ_ELNO, EPEQ_ELGA, EPMQ_ELNO or EPMQ_ELGA (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP).

Let there be $n$ elementary cycles of half-amplitude $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{1}}{2,}\mathrm{...},\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{n}}{2}$ .

The analysis of the elementary damage of the first cycle is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material.

The analysis of the elementary damage of the following cycles is determined by the algorithm described below:

- If $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}}{2}\ge \frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}}{2}$

the analysis of the elementary damage of cycle $(i+1)$ is determined by interpolation on the Manson-Coffin curve.

- If $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}}{2}<\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i}}{2}$

one determines:

$\frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{i+1}}{2}={F}_{\mathrm{NAPPE}}(\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}}{2},\underset{j<i}{\max}(\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{j}}{2}))$

$\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}^{\text{*}}}{2}={F}_{\mathrm{FONC}}(\frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{i+1}}{2})$

where ${F}_{\mathrm{NAPPE}}$ is a nappe introduced under the operand TAHERI_NAPPE.

${F}_{\mathrm{FONC}}$ is a function introduced under the operand TAHERI_FONC.

The damage value of cycle $(i+1)$ is obtained by interpolation of $\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}^{\text{*}}}{2}$ on the Manson-Coffin curve of the material (${\mathrm{Nrupt}}_{i+1}$  = number of cycles to failure for cycle $(i+1)=\text{MANSON\_COFFIN}(\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}_{i+1}^{\text{*}}}{2})$ and ${\mathrm{Dom}}_{i+1}$ = damage of cycle $(i+1)=$ .

The Manson-Coffin curve must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) (keyword FATIGUE, operand MANSON_COFFIN).

**Notes:**

1. *The nappe or the formula introduced under the operand* *TAHERI_NAPPE is in fact the cyclic hardening curve with prestress of the material.*
2. *The function or the formula introduced under the operand* *TAHERI_FONC is in fact the cyclic hardening curve of the material.*
3. *The nappe or the formula introduced under the operand* *TAHERI_NAPPE must have* *'X' and* *'EPSI' as parameters.*
4. *The function or the formula introduced under the operand* *TAHERI_FONC must have* *'SIGM' as parameter.*

In addition, the specified result concept must contain respectively the field of symbolic name EPEQ_ELNO, EPEQ_ELGA, EPMQ_ELNO or EPMQ_ELGA (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP).

**Note:**

*For a periodic loading, the damage analysis is carried out only on the first complete cycle. The first part of the loading history corresponding to the monotonic loading is not taken into account because its purpose is to impose a non-zero mean loading. For elastic constitutive behaviour, the analysis is carried out between the maximum value and the minimum value of the cycle considered. For elastoplastic constitutive behaviour, the analysis is carried out between the first unloading and the second unloading.*

The following table lists the crack-initiation criteria available for two types of loading.


| TYPE_CHARGE= 'PERIODIQUE' | TYPE_CHARGE= 'NON_PERIODIQUE' |
| --- | --- |
| 'MATAKE_MODI_AC' 'DANG_VAN_MODI_AC' 'FORMULE_CRITERE' | 'MATAKE_MODI_AV', 'DANG_VAN_MODI_AV' 'FATESOCI_MODI_AV' 'FORMULE_CRITERE' |

For a constant-amplitude loading, the operand CRITERE makes it possible to specify the criterion which the half-amplitude of the maximum shear must satisfy. For a variable-amplitude loading, the operand CRITERE makes it possible to specify the criterion which the maximum damage must satisfy.

The crack-initiation criteria in Code_Aster may be called by a name for the well-established criteria. It is also possible for the user to build a crack-initiation criterion himself as a formula of predefined quantities.

Notation:

${\text{n}}^{\text{*}}$         :    normal to the plane in which the shear amplitude is maximum;

$\Delta \tau (\text{n})$     :    shear-stress amplitude in a plane of normal $\text{n}$ ;

$\Delta \gamma (\text{n})$     :    shear-strain amplitude in a plane of normal $\text{n}$ ;

${N}_{\max}(\text{n})$     :    maximum normal stress on the plane of normal $\text{n}$ ;

${\tau}_{0}$         :    endurance limit in purely alternating shear;

${d}_{0}$         :    endurance limit in purely alternating tension–compression;

$P$         :    hydrostatic pressure;

${c}_{p}$         :    coefficient used to take into account a possible pre-hardening;

${\sigma}_{y}$         :    yield stress.

**Criterion** MATAKE_MODI_AC

The initial MATAKE criterion is defined by the inequation [eq. 3.12-1]:


$\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})+a{N}_{max}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})\le b$                             eq 3.12-1

where $a$ and $b$ are two constants given by the user under the keywords MATAKE_A and MATAKE_B of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU; they depend on the material characteristics and are equal to:

$a=({\tau}_{0}-\frac{{d}_{0}}{2})/\frac{{d}_{0}}{2}$             $b={\tau}_{0}$

If the user has the results of two tension–compression tests, one alternating and the other not, the constants $a$ and $b$ are given by:

$a=\frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{1}-\Delta {\sigma}_{2})-2{\sigma}_{m}}$ ,

$b=\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})+2{\sigma}_{m}}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{2}$ ,

with $\Delta {\sigma}_{1}$ the loading amplitude for the alternating case $({\sigma}_{m}=0)$ and $\Delta {\sigma}_{2}$ the loading amplitude for the case where the mean stress is non-zero $({\sigma}_{m}\ne 0)$ .

We modify the initial MATAKE criterion by introducing the definition of an equivalent stress, denoted ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})$ :

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})=({c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})+a{N}_{\max}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}}))\frac{f}{t}$ ,

where $f/t$ represents the ratio of the endurance limits in alternating bending and torsion, and must be supplied under the keyword COEF_FLEX_TORS of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU.

**Criterion** DANG_VAN_MODI_AC

The initial DANG VAN criterion is defined by the inequation [eq. 3.12-2]:

$\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})+aP\le b$                                 eq 3.12-2

where $a$ and $b$ are two constants given by the user under the keywords D_VAN_A and D_VAN_B of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU; they depend on the material characteristics. In the case where the user has two tension–compression tests, one alternating and the other not, the constants $a$ and $b$ are equal to:

$a=\frac{3}{2}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{1}-\Delta {\sigma}_{2})-2{\sigma}_{m}}$         $b=\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{(\Delta {\sigma}_{2}-\Delta {\sigma}_{1})+2{\sigma}_{m}}\times \frac{\Delta {\sigma}_{1}}{2}$

with $\Delta {\sigma}_{1}$ the loading amplitude for the alternating case $({\sigma}_{m}=0)$ and $\Delta {\sigma}_{2}$ for the case where the mean stress is non-zero $({\sigma}_{m}\ne 0)$ .

In addition, we define an equivalent stress in the sense of DANG VAN, denoted ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})$ :

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})=({c}_{p}\frac{\Delta \tau }{2}({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})+aP)\frac{c}{t}$

where $c/t$ represents the ratio of the endurance limits in alternating shear and tension, and must be supplied under the keyword COEF_CISA_TRAC of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU.

For further information, consult the document [R7.04.04](doc:r7.04.04).

**Criterion** MATAKE_MODI_AV

The criterion MATAKE_MODI_AV is a development of the MATAKE criterion. Unlike the two previous criteria, this criterion selects the critical plane according to the damage computed in each plane. The plane in which the damage is maximum is the one retained. This criterion is suited to non-periodic loadings, which leads to the use of a cycle-counting method in order to compute the elementary damages. To count the cycles, the RAINFLOW method is used.


Once known, the elementary damages are cumulated linearly in order to determine the damage.

In order to compute the elementary damages, the history of the shear stresses is projected onto one or two axes so as to reduce it to a one-dimensional function of time ${\tau}_{p}=f(t)$ . After extracting the elementary sub-cycles of ${\tau}_{p}$ with the RAINFLOW method, an elementary equivalent stress is defined for every elementary sub-cycle $i$ :

${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{i}(\text{n})=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\mathrm{Max}({\tau}_{\mathrm{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\tau}_{\mathrm{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))-\mathrm{Min}({\tau}_{\mathrm{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\tau}_{\mathrm{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))}{2}+a\mathrm{Max}({N}_{1}^{i}(\text{n}),{N}_{2}^{i}(\text{n}),0))$ eq 3.12-3

with $\text{n}$ the normal to the current plane, ${\tau}_{\mathrm{p1}}^{i}(\text{n})$ and the ${\tau}_{\mathrm{p2}}^{i}(\text{n})$ values of the projected shear stresses of sub-cycle $i$ and ${N}_{1}^{i}(\text{n})$ and the ${N}_{2}^{i}(\text{n})$ normal stresses of sub-cycle $i$ . From ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{i}(\text{n})$ and a fatigue curve, the elementary number of cycles to failure ${N}^{i}(\text{n})$ and the corresponding damage ${D}^{i}(\text{n})=1/{N}^{i}(\text{n})$ are determined. In [eq. 3.12-3] $\alpha$  is a corrective term which makes it possible to use a tension–compression fatigue curve. The constants $a$ and $\alpha$ must be supplied under the keywords MATAKE_A and COEF_FLEX_TORS of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU.

A linear damage cumulation is used. Let $k$ be the number of elementary sub-cycles; for a fixed normal $\text{n}$ , the cumulated damage is equal to:

$D(\text{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}{D}^{i}(\text{n})$                             eq 3.12-4

In order to determine the normal vector ${\text{n}}^{\text{*}}$ corresponding to the maximum cumulated damage, $\text{n}$ is varied; the normal vector ${\text{n}}^{\text{*}}$ corresponding to the maximum cumulated damage is then given by:

$D({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})=\underset{\text{n}}{\mathit{Max}}(D(\text{n}))$

**Criterion**  DANG_VAN_MODI_AV

The approach and the techniques used to compute this criterion are identical to those used for the criterion MATAKE_MODI_AV. The only difference lies in the definition of the elementary equivalent stress, where the hydrostatic pressure $P$ replaces the maximum normal stress ${N}_{\max}$ :

${\sigma}_{\mathit{eq}}^{i}(\text{n})=\alpha ({c}_{p}\frac{\mathit{Max}({\tau}_{\mathit{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\tau}_{\mathit{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))-\mathit{Min}({\tau}_{\mathit{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\tau}_{\mathit{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))}{2}+a\mathit{Max}({P}_{1}^{i}(\text{n}),{P}_{2}^{i}(\text{n}),0))$

The constants $a$ and $\alpha$ are to be supplied by the user under the keywords D_VAN_A and COEF_CISA_TRAC of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of DEFI_MATERIAU.

For further information, consult the document [R7.04.04](doc:r7.04.04).

**Criterion** FATESOCI_MODI_AV

The criterion of FATEMI and SOCIE is defined by the relation:

${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}(n)=\frac{\Delta \gamma (n)}{2}(1+k\frac{{N}_{max}(n)}{{\sigma}_{y}})$

where $k$ is a constant which depends on the material characteristics. Unlike the other criteria, it uses the shear strain instead of the shear stress. In addition, the various quantities which contribute to the criterion are multiplied and not added. The criterion of FATEMI and SOCIE can be used after an elastic or elastoplastic analysis. This criterion selects the critical plane according to the damage computed in each plane. The plane in which the damage is maximum is the one retained.

This criterion is suited to non-periodic loadings, which leads us to use the RAINFLOW cycle-counting method to compute the elementary damages. The elementary damages are then cumulated linearly in order to determine the damage.

In order to compute the elementary damages, the history of the shear strain is projected onto one or two axes so as to reduce it to a one-dimensional function of time ${\gamma}_{p}=f(t)$ . After extracting the elementary sub-cycles with the RAINFLOW method, an elementary equivalent strain is defined for every elementary sub-cycle $i$ :

${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{i}(\text{n})=\alpha {c}_{p}(\frac{\mathit{Max}({\gamma}_{\mathit{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\gamma}_{\mathit{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))-\mathit{Min}({\gamma}_{\mathit{p1}}^{i}(\text{n}),{\gamma}_{\mathit{p2}}^{i}(\text{n}))}{2})(1+a\mathit{Max}({N}_{1}^{i}(\text{n}),{N}_{2}^{i}(\text{n}),0))$ eq 3.12-5

with $a=\frac{k}{{\sigma}_{y}}$ , $\text{n}$ the normal to the current plane, ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{p1}}^{i}(\text{n})$ and ${\gamma}_{\mathrm{p2}}^{i}(\text{n})$ the values of the projected shear strains of sub-cycle $i$ , ${N}_{1}^{i}(\text{n})$ and ${N}_{2}^{i}(\text{n})$ being the two values of the normal stress of sub-cycle $i$ . From ${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{i}(\text{n})$ and a Manson-Coffin curve, the elementary number of cycles to failure and ${N}^{i}(\text{n})$ the corresponding damage ${D}^{i}(\text{n})=1/{N}^{i}(\text{n})$ are determined.

**It will be noted that the shear strains used in the criterion of FATEMI and of SOCIE are distortions** ${\gamma}_{ij}$ ($i\ne j$ ) **. If the shear strains of tensor type** ${ϵ}_{ij}$ ($i\ne j$ ) **are used, they must be multiplied by a factor 2 because** ${\gamma}_{ij}=2{ϵ}_{ij}$ **.**

In equation [eq. 3.12-5] $\alpha$ is a corrective term which makes it possible to use a Manson-Coffin curve obtained in tension–compression. ${c}_{p}$ is a coefficient which makes it possible to take into account a possible pre-hardening.

The constants $a$ and $\alpha$ must be supplied under the keywords FATSOC_A and COEF_CISA_TRAC of the factor keyword CISA_PLAN_CRIT of the command DEFI_MATERIAU.

It is noted that a rigorous approach is to use the Manson-Coffin curve obtained directly in torsion (which is not always available). The use of the Manson-Coffin curve obtained in tension–compression with the corrective term $\alpha$ (which is the ratio of two endurance limits), as programmed in Code_Aster, is therefore an approximation.

Since a linear damage cumulation is used, if $m$ is the number of elementary sub-cycles, then for a fixed normal $\text{n}$ , the cumulated damage is equal to:

$D(\text{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}{D}^{i}(\text{n})$

In order to find the normal vector ${\text{n}}^{\text{*}}$ corresponding to the maximum cumulated damage, $\text{n}$ is varied. The normal vector ${\text{n}}^{\text{*}}$ associated with the maximum cumulated damage is then given by:

$D({\text{n}}^{\text{*}})=\underset{\text{n}}{\mathit{Max}}(D(\text{n}))$

**Criterion**  FORMULE_CRITERE

This type of criterion allows the user to build a criterion as a formula of predefined quantities. This criterion is based on a general relation:

> “Equivalent quantity” = “Life curve”

where the “Equivalent quantity” is a formula supplied under the operand FORMULE_GRDEQ (see 3.4.6) and the “Life curve” is supplied under the operand COURBE_GRD_VIE (see 3.4.7), either by a function (tabulated or a formula, under the operand 'FORMULE_VIE', see 3.4.8), or by a curve name 'WOHLER' or 'MANSON_COFFIN' defined beforehand in DEFI_MATERIAU.

> **Criterion** VMIS_TRESCA

The criterion VMIS_TRESCA is not strictly speaking a fatigue criterion, since it does not make it possible to compute a damage. It determines the maximum amplitude variation of the stress tensor over time. Concretely, the von Mises and Tresca criteria are applied to the tensors which result from the difference of the stress tensor taken at two distinct time instants. By varying these time instants, the maximum values of the von Mises and Tresca criteria can be computed [R7.04.04](doc:r7.04.04).

### 3.5 Operands specific to an analysis of type FATIGUE_VIBR

#### 3.5.1 Principle of the analysis

This option does not aim to compute the damage associated with a known loading, but conversely to estimate the maximum vibratory loading associated with an unlimited endurance of the structure under study. The structures concerned are typically blades, subjected to a known static loading (centrifugal force linked to the rotation of the machine) and to an unknown or poorly known dynamic loading (vibrations induced by the fluid flow).

A fundamental assumption of this option is to consider a uniaxial fatigue criterion (Wöhler method). In other words, it is assumed that the principal directions of the static loading and of the dynamic loading are the same. This assumption seems acceptable for the usual structures concerned (blades, pipe lines, …); it induces a conservatism which is no doubt excessive in the general case.

The procedure of a study with this option is as follows:

- Analysis of the stress linked to the static loading ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}$ with MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE;
- Analysis of the stresses associated with the $N$ eigenmodes considered ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ with CALC_MODES;
- Fatigue analysis with CALC_FATIGUE / TYPE_CALCUL = 'FATIGUE_VIBR'
- Introduction of an assumption on the relative weight of the various eigenmodes considered ${({\beta}_{i})}_{1\le i\le N}$ (corresponds to the operand FACT_PARTICI):

      - ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{total}}(t)={\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}}+\alpha \sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}\cos({\omega}_{i}t+{\phi }_{i})$ ,

where ${\omega}_{i}$ and ${\phi }_{i}$ are respectively the angular frequency (known) and the phase shift (unknown) of mode i. The coefficient $\alpha$ is the parameter which one seeks to compute;

- Retrieval of the material parameters and choice of the damage-analysis criterion (operands CORR_SIGM_MOYE and MATER, cf. § 24). One denotes by $f$ the criterion which the maximum amplitude of stress variation ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\max}$ must satisfy. $f$ depends on the endurance limit ${S}_{l}$ and on the ultimate strength ${S}_{u}$ of the material:
- ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\max}=f({\sigma}_{\mathrm{stat}},{S}_{l},{S}_{u})$
- At all the nodes or Gauss points of the mesh (according to the choice in OPTION):
- Analysis of the amplitude of stress variation: ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}=\alpha \sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ (it should be noted that, since the phase shifts between the modes are not known, the amplitude is defined conservatively as the sum of the amplitude of each of the modes);
- Analysis of the coefficient $\alpha$ corresponding to an unlimited endurance: $\alpha =\frac{f({\sigma}_{\mathit{stat}},{S}_{l},{S}_{u})}{\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\sigma}_{\mathit{mod}}^{i}}$
- Interpretation and use of the result of CALC_FATIGUE: the operator supplies the field (at the nodes or at the Gauss point) of the admissible values of $\alpha$ : the minimum value of $\alpha$ on the mesh makes it possible to compute the maximum admissible vibration amplitude of the structure (the minimum value is displayed in the message file; it can also be recovered by post-processing or visualising the result field); the field makes it possible to locate the zones which limit the life of the structure.


To pass from the coefficient $\alpha$ to the admissible vibration amplitude at a given point $\partial \tilde{u}$ (corresponding, for example, to the position of a sensor), an additional operation must be carried out. One denotes by ${\tilde{u}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$ the displacement at the point of interest associated with mode $i$ ; the admissible vibration amplitude at this point is then:

$\partial \tilde{u}=\min(\alpha )\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\beta}_{i}{\tilde{u}}_{\mathrm{mod}}^{i}$

**Note:**

*If the static stress at a node exceeds the ultimate stress of the material, the admissible vibration amplitude is zero. In this case, an alarm message is issued and the analysis continues on the other nodes.*

##### 3.5.2.4 Operand FACT_PARTICI

```text
♦    FACT_PARTICI    =    list_R
```

Relative weight of each of the modes to be considered. The length of the list must be identical to the length of the list entered under the operand NUME_MODE.

Only the ratio between the various factors supplied is important. If one wishes to pass from the parameter computed by CALC_FATIGUE to a maximum displacement amplitude at a given node, the same coefficients must nevertheless be taken into account (cf. §  22 ).

#### 3.5.3 Operand OPTION

This factor keyword makes it possible to specify where the damage is computed:

- 'DOMA_ELNO_SIGM' for the analysis of the damage at the nodes from a stress field.

The static and modal results (operands RESULTAT and MODE_MECA) must contain the field of symbolic name SIEQ_ELNO (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP), which defines among other things the value of the signed von Mises equivalent stress (component VMIS_SG) computed at the nodes.

- 'DOMA_ELGA_SIGM' for the analysis of the damage at the Gauss points from a stress field.

The static and modal results (operands RESULTAT and MODE_MECA) must contain the field of symbolic name SIEQ_ELGA (which can be computed by CALC_CHAMP), which defines among other things the value of the signed von Mises equivalent stress (component VMIS_SG) computed at the Gauss points.

#### 3.5.4 Operand CORR_SIGM_MOYENNE

```text
♦    CORR_SIGM_MOYE =        /    'GOODMAN' ,
/    'GERBER' ,
```

The structure is subjected to a loading with a non-zero mean stress, the mean stress corresponding to the static stress.

The mean stress ${\sigma}_{m}$ can be taken into account in the Wöhler fatigue curve by means of the Haigh diagram [R7.04.01](doc:r7.04.01). Two corrections are available in order to compute the admissible alternating stress ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\max}$ as a function of the endurance limit ${S}_{l}$ and of the ultimate strength ${S}_{u}$ of the material:

Goodman line:

${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\max}={S}_{l}(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}})$

Gerber parabola:

${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\max}={S}_{l}{(1-\frac{{\sigma}_{m}}{{S}_{u}})}^{2}$

The value of the ultimate strength of the material ${S}_{u}$ must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01) (factor keyword RCCM, operand Su). The endurance limit ${S}_{l}$ corresponds to the first point of the Wöhler curve (operator DEFI_MATERIAU, keyword FATIGUE, operand WOHLER).

#### 3.5.5 Operand DOMMAGE

```text
♦    DOMMAGE =    / 'WOHLER'
```

For the moment, only the Wöhler method is available for vibratory fatigue analyses. This method is based on the analysis of the amplitude of stress variation and on the comparison with the Wöhler fatigue curve of the material.

The Wöhler curve of the material must be introduced in the operator DEFI_MATERIAU (keyword FATIGUE, operand WOHLER). Only the endurance limit ${S}_{l}$ (i.e. the first point of the curve) is actually used in the analysis.
