# u2.06.14 Advice on carrying out Fluid-Structure Interaction analyses

**Summary**

This document presents an overall view of the different approaches available in *Code_Aster* for modelling the effects of fluid-structure interaction in the vibratory analyses of structures. It also recapitulates a number of practical pieces of advice and of precautions to be taken when carrying out Fluid-Structure Interaction analyses.

## 1 The fluid forces acting on a vibrating structure

There exist two families of fluid forces which can act on a vibrating structure in a fluid medium:

1. The **fluid-elastic forces**: these are forces which depend on the motion of the structure; on its displacement, its velocity and its acceleration. Because of this, it is appropriate to take these fluid-elastic coupling effects into account by adding coefficients to the structural stiffness, mass and damping matrices.
2. The **forces independent of the motion** of the structure. These are the forces which act in the cases of turbulent or two-phase flows.

In this document the different operators of *Code_Aster* are presented which make it possible to model each of the two types of fluid forces in the framework of the vibratory analyses of structures.

## 2 Modelling of the fluid-elastic forces

In *Code_Aster*, there exist two different methods for modelling the fluid-elastic forces which represent the fluid-structure coupling forces. The difference between the two methods concerns the nature and the complexity of the physical phenomena that the user wishes to consider and, consequently, the hypotheses adopted when computing the coupling forces.

The **first method** consists in computing added matrices representing the fluid-structure coupling with a **potential approach.** One makes the hypothesis that the **structure** undergoes only **small strains** and that the **fluid** which surrounds it is **perfect**; the effect of shear in the fluid is then considered negligible. For the computation of the added mass, the fluid is considered unsteady. As for the added stiffness and the added damping, these can be computed only in the presence of a flow. In this case, one makes the hypothesis that the **flow** is **potential**; one then neglects any effect linked to the physics of the rotational parts of the flow, which can be important in configurations with separation or at turbulent velocities.

The **second method** for modelling the coupling forces consists in determining the fluid-elastic transfer matrix, particular to a **predefined configuration;** a “configuration” being the whole set: the **structure** and its geometry on the one hand and the **flow** and its properties on the other. The configurations which can be modelled with this method are:

1. Tube bundle under transverse flow
2. Rod under flow through an annular confinement (control-rod cluster)
3. Tube bundle under axial flow
4. Flow between two coaxial shells

Despite its sometimes restrictive hypotheses, the potential method nevertheless remains the most generic from the point of view of its applicability, independent of the configuration of the study. The user is advised to employ the second method only in the case where the case under study corresponds to one of the four configurations currently available.

### 2.1 The “potential” approach for computing the modal basis of a coupled system

In this approach, the computation method consists in determining, first, added fluid mass, stiffness and damping matrices with the help of the operator CALC_MATR_AJOU or the macro-command MACRO_MATR_AJOU. These two operators give as a result added matrices, projected onto the modal basis of the structure in vacuum.

To determine the modal basis of the “wetted” structure, it is sufficient to combine the structural matrices with the generalised added matrices with the help of the operator COMB_MATR_ASSE. It will then be necessary to recompute the eigenmodes with CALC_MODES. The modes of the coupled system must finally be restored onto the physical basis of the structure with the help of the operator REST_GENE_PHYS. ### 2.2 The “predefined configuration” approach for computing the modal basis of a coupled system

The data-setup and computation method for this second approach is different from that of the potential approach. Indeed, the physical phenomena which are modelled here are more complex and the results depend on an additional parameter, the flow velocity. The computation of a modal basis of the coupled system is therefore established for a range of velocities. One therefore obtains with this approach, not a traditional dynamic modal basis (mode_meca), but a fluid-elastic modal basis, which will be called melasflu in *Code_Aster*.

To compute the fluid-elastic basis of a coupled system, the user must first call the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU in order to supply the type of configuration of the study as well as all the characteristics which are associated with it. These characteristics differ according to the configuration but they generally concern the geometry of the structure, the properties of the fluid and of its flow.

#### 2.2.1 Special case of a tube bundle under transverse flow

In the case of the configuration of a tube bundle under transverse flow (FAISCEAU_TRANS), it is possible to compute, by means of the operator FONC_FLUI_STRU, the inertial distribution of the fluid all along the structure. The user can therefore use this information to enrich the structural matrices before carrying out the first modal analysis of the structure. In practice, after the call to FONC_FLUI_STRU, the user must create a fluid-structure *hybrid* material by using the option ELAS_FLUI of DEFI_MATERIAU and assign it to all the finite elements of the structure under transverse flow (AFFE_MATERIAU).

The elementary mass and stiffness matrices can then be computed with the options MASS_FLUI_STRU and RIGI_FLUI_STRU of CALC_MATR_ELEM. After assembly of the elementary matrices, the user will be able to compute the modal basis of the wetted structure, for example with the operator CALC_MODES.

**It is important to note that this taking into account, upstream, of the inertial effect of the fluid is possible only in the case of a tube bundle under transverse flow. It makes it possible subsequently to carry out the whole of the dynamic analysis on a projection basis which corresponds to the modal basis of the wetted structure.**

#### 2.2.2 Case of the other configurations

For the three other configurations, the reference modal basis is determined from the mechanical properties of the structure (purely structural mass and stiffness matrices), without any taking into account at this level of the effects linked to the presence of the fluid.

Once the configuration of the study and the reference projection basis are well defined, the study of the coupled system can continue with a call to the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU. This operator recovers, in addition to the configuration of the study and the projection basis, a list of the velocities which represent the range of flow velocities over which the user wishes to study the dynamic behaviour of the structure.

In the concept resulting from CALC_FLUI_STRU, the user has available, for each flow velocity, a basis modified by the taking into account of all the coefficients of the fluid-elastic coupling. The steps of the analysis for the four configurations are detailed in the following two diagrams:

### 2.3 Vibratory analyses following the obtaining of the modal basis of the coupled system

The modelling of the fluid-elastic forces is practically finished with the obtaining of a dynamic modal basis of the structure taking the coupling effects into account. To continue the study, the user can freely make use of the computed modal basis in order to analyse the behaviour of the coupled fluid-structure system.

**Transient analyses**

In the case where the modelling of the fluid-elastic coupling has been carried out according to the second method, that is to say on the basis of a configuration predefined in the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU, the user must call the operator MODI_BASE_MODALE in order to extract from the melasflu basis a modal basis of type mode_meca for a single flow velocity. The modal basis will then be usable in all the transient-dynamics operators.

In the case where added coefficients have been computed according to the potential method, the user can make direct use of the modal basis obtained by restoration onto the physical basis of the basis of generalised modes (result of the call to REST_GENE_PHYS, see ).

**There currently exists only one time-integration scheme which accepts modal bases of type** melasflu **, it is the ITMI method of the operator** DYNA_VIBRA **.**

**Spectral analyses**

To carry out a spectral dynamic analysis of the coupled system, the user is on the other hand not obliged to extract a mode_meca basis from the wetted melasflu basis. The operator DYNA_SPEC_MODAL, which makes it possible to carry out the spectral analyses, is indeed compatible with modal bases of type melasflu.

## 3 Modelling of the forces independent of the motion of the structure

In *Code_Aster*, it is possible to model several types of fluid forces which can act on a structure without however depending on its motion. Physically, these forces are directly linked to fluctuations in the pressure along the structure, observed in the turbulent regimes of the flow.

It is important to note that most of the models of the turbulent forces which are predefined in *Code_Aster* apply only to well-defined configurations. These are indeed the same configurations which are available in the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU (see paragraph  2.2 ).

The definition of the model of the turbulent forces is done by means of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB. The different types of turbulence spectra which are available are presented in the following table. The type of structure-flow configuration which is generally associated with each model is also specified:

| **SPECTRUM MODEL** | **MEANING** | **CONFIGURATION** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SPEC_LONG_CORR_[1-4] | Turbulence of the **long** **corr**elation-length type for transverse flows | Tube bundle under transverse flow |
| SPEC_CORR_CONV_[1/2] | Turbulence of boundary-layer **conv**ection type for parallel flows | Plate under parallel flow (Corcos) Tube bundle under axial flow (Au Yang) |
| SPEC_CORR_CONV_3 | Turbulence spectrum defined from an analytical function | General configuration |
| SPEC_FONC_FORME | Turbulence spectrum identified by its decomposition onto a family of **shape** **func**tions *Predefined model:* GRAPPE_1 | General configuration *: Control-rod cluster* |
| SPEC_EXCI_POINT | Turbulence spectrum with **exci**tation of **point** force or moment type *Predefined model:* GRAPPE_2 | General configuration *: Control-rod cluster* |

**Table** 3-1 **: Details of the turbulence-spectrum models available in the operator** DEFI_SPEC_TURB

The operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB produces a spectre_sdaster concept which contains all the information necessary for the definition of the turbulent excitation spectrum according to the model chosen. For example, for spectra of correlation-length type (SPEC_LONG_CORR_[1-4]), the user must always supply the transverse velocity profile of the flow, a correlation length, and the coefficients of a pressure-spectrum model. The user can refer to the documentation of the operator DEFI_SPEC_TURB (U4.44.31) for more details on each turbulence model and its input parameters.

It is important for the user to know that the “spectrum” obtained downstream of DEFI_SPEC_TURB is not really “computed” at this stage. One nevertheless has available, in the spectre_sdaster concept, the information on the spectrum making it possible to project it onto the modal basis of the wetted structure by using the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE. The operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE takes care of computing the acceptance integrals corresponding to the spectrum and to the modal basis of the structure. It produces a matrix of inter-spectra of generalised excitations.

In addition to the turbulence-spectrum model (spectre_sdaster) and the modal projection basis, the user must supply, in the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE, information concerning the (frequency) discretisation of the spectrum by using the mandatory keywords FREQ_INIT, FREQ_FIN and NB_POIN. If the modal projection basis is of type melas_flu, it is mandatory at this stage to specify the fluid flow velocity. This makes it possible to extract, internally in the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE, the projection basis which corresponds to the requested velocity. The user also has the option of computing only the auto-spectra of generalised excitations, that is to say only the diagonal terms of the generalised inter-spectrum matrix (OPTION='DIAG'). Finally, it is also possible to carry out the projection of the turbulent excitation onto part of the mesh.

The interspectre concept produced by the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE is then usable in all the operators of *Code_Aster* in order to take into account the effect of the turbulence in a vibratory analysis of the structure. The user can choose one of the two equivalent approaches in order to analyse the vibratory level of the wetted structure under turbulent excitation: the spectral approach and the temporal approach. The precautions to be followed in the implementation of each of the two approaches are specified in what follows.

## 4 Vibratory analysis of a wetted structure: spectral approach

From the modal basis of the wetted structure and the inter-spectrum of modal excitation computed in PROJ_SPEC_BASE, the user can proceed directly to the computation of an inter-spectrum of generalised displacements by means of the operator DYNA_SPEC_MODAL. Here the user also has the obligation to supply the flow velocity if the modal basis is of type melas_flu, and can choose to compute the whole inter-spectral matrix of the generalised displacements or else only the diagonal terms (the auto-spectra).

To restore the generalised results onto the physical basis, the user must then call the operator REST_SPEC_PHYS. It will then be possible, for example, to compute a displacement inter-spectrum on one or several nodes, for the components of interest ($\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ , $\mathit{DRX}$ , etc.). If the aim is to compute the RMS values, the resulting inter-spectra are to be post-processed by the operator POST_DYNA_ALEA.

## 5 Vibratory analysis of a wetted structure: temporal approach

In certain cases, it is of interest to study the vibratory behaviour of a structure under flow by a transient analysis. The idea is to generate, from the inter-spectral matrix of generalised excitation (obtained by PROJ_SPEC_PHYS), a temporal excitation which has the same frequency signature as the original spectrum. This can be done by means of the operator GENE_FONC_ALEA, which makes it possible to generate temporal excitation functions from an inter-spectral matrix (interspectre concept).

Three points of very great importance are to be taken into account when passing from the spectral domain to the temporal domain. They concern the relations which link:

1. The frequency samplings of the operator PROJ_SPEC_BASE and of GENE_FONC_ALEA.
2. The frequency sampling to the total duration of the temporal excitation.
3. The temporal sampling of the excitation to the cut-off (maximum) frequency of the spectrum.

The user interested in carrying out a transient analysis should carefully choose the frequency discretisation of the turbulence spectrum when calling PROJ_SPEC_BASE (keywords FREQ_INIT, FREQ_FIN and NB_POIN). In addition, the input parameters of the operator GENE_FONC_ALEA must be consistent with those of PROJ_SPEC_BASE.

In PROJ_SPEC_BASE, the spectrum is discretised with NB_POIN points between FREQ_INIT and FREQ_FIN. In the operator GENE_FONC_ALEA, the spectrum is re-sampled with a new NB_POIN, different from that of PROJ_SPEC_BASE.

If the option INTERP='OUI' is used in GENE_FONC_ALEA, the spectrum is interpolated for frequencies lying between f = 0 Hz and f = FREQ_FIN. FREQ_FIN is deduced automatically from the maximum frequency in the original spectrum. It is therefore equal to the FREQ_FIN of PROJ_SPEC_BASE.

If the option INTERP='NON' is used, the maximum frequency is recomputed. It is a function of the value of the keyword NB_POIN in GENE_FONC_ALEA.

The user must in every case supply two different values for the keyword NB_POIN in PROJ_SPEC_BASE and GENE_FONC_ALEA. The minimum value of NB_POIN to be supplied in GENE_FONC_ALEA (GFA) can be computed as follows:

> ${{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}\ge \frac{({{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}-1)\times {{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{FIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}}{{{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{FIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}-{{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{INIT}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}}+1$             (1)

For example, if the turbulence spectrum is discretised in PROJ_SPEC_BASE at $2048$ points between $25,5$ and $45,5\mathit{Hz}$ , at least: $\frac{2047\times 45,5}{45,5-25,5}+1=4657$ points will be needed in GENE_FONC_ALEA in order not to lose information on the excitation spectrum even before the generation of the temporal excitation function.

### 5.1 Practical advice on use

It is strongly advised to call GENE_FONC_ALEA with the option INTERP='NON' and to choose an NB_POIN well above the recommended minimum value.

With the option INTERP='NON', the total duration of the temporal excitation can be computed as the inverse of the frequency step in the spectrum of PROJ_SPEC_BASE; it is equal to:

> ${t}_{max}=\frac{1}{\delta f}=\frac{{{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}-1}{{{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{FIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}-{{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{INIT}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}}$                 (2)

In the previous example, the duration of the excitation is equal to ${t}_{max}=102,35s$ .

The temporal sampling of the excitation can be computed from the maximum frequency of the excitation spectrum:

> $\delta t=\frac{1}{2\times {{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{MAX}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}}$

In the case where the option INTERP='NON' is used in GENE_FONC_ALEA, the maximum frequency of the spectrum used for the generation of the temporal excitation is given by the formula:

> ${{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{MAX}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}=\delta f\times {{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}$

It follows that:

> $\delta t=\frac{{t}_{max}}{2\times {{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}}$                                 (3)

In practice, the user who wishes to carry out a transient analysis generally has an idea of the time-integration step and of the total duration of the vibratory analysis. The choice of the different parameters is made in the following order:

1. From the total duration of the desired transient simulation, the user must choose the number of points of the frequency discretisation (NB_POIN) in PROJ_SPEC_BASE according to equation (2).
2. Next, from the total duration of the excitation and the integration step, the user can compute, using equation (3), the number of sampling points (NB_POIN) to be chosen in the operator GENE_FONC_ALEA. One must therefore choose a number equal to or greater than this value,
3. Finally, the user must check that the value of NB_POIN of GENE_FONC_ALEA satisfies the condition in equation (1),

To illustrate the practical application of these 3 steps, an example case is considered in which the turbulence spectrum is defined between $1,0$ and $384,832\mathit{Hz}$ (keywords FREQ_INIT and FREQ_FIN of PROJ_SPEC_BASE). In addition, it is assumed that the transient vibratory analysis is to be carried out over a duration greater than $10s$ and with a minimum integration step of ${10}^{-4}s$ .

According to equation (2), ${{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}={t}_{max}({{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{FIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}-{{\mathit{FREQ}}_{\mathit{INIT}}}^{\mathit{PSB}})+1=3839,32$

An admissible value (a power of 2) is then ${{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{PSB}}=4096$ , which gives a total duration of the excitation ${t}_{max}=10,6687s$

The frequency sampling (NB_POIN) to be chosen in GENE_FONC_ALEA is then computed according to equation (3): ${{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}=53343,65$

An admissible value is then ${{\mathit{NB}}_{\mathit{POIN}}}^{\mathit{GFA}}=65536$ (a power of 2, and it satisfies equation (1)). This choice in GENE_FONC_ALEA with the option INTERP='NON' leads to a temporal sampling of the resulting excitation of: $\delta t=8,139\times {10}^{-5}s$ . This is compatible with the integration time step wanted by the user (${10}^{-4}s$ ).
