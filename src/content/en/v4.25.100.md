# v4.25.100 TTLV100 - Thermal shock in a pipe with exchange condition

**Summary:**

This transient linear thermal test consists of imposing a cold thermal shock on a hollow cylinder assumed infinite with the help of a limit exchange condition.

The impact is modelled by a linear $\Delta T=–269°C$ ramp in $12s$.

The problem is handled in axisymmetric and 3D.

The reference solution is obtained on a fine mesh.

The test is performed on 3 modellings: (TRIA3, QUAD4), (TRIA6, QUAD9) in AXIS_DIAG and (HEXA8, PENTA6) in 3D_DIAG.

The transient linear thermal algorithm is tested when the mass matrix is diagonalized (modelling AXIS_DIAG and 3D_DIAG with ‘的 *mass Dumping* 的).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v4.25.100/1000000000000256000001261087239B6AF2363C.png)

$r(A)=417\mathrm{mm}$

$r(B)=496\mathrm{mm}$

$r(\mathrm{M1})=443.43\mathrm{mm}$

$r(\mathrm{M2})=469.67\mathrm{mm}$

### 1.2 Material properties

$\lambda =19.97W/m°C$

$\rho {C}_{p}=4.89488{10}^{6}J/{m}^{3}°C$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Exchange $\lambda \frac{\partial T}{\partial n}{\mid}_{r={r}_{i}}=h({T}_{\mathrm{ext}}-T(r,t))$

with $\begin{array}{}h=40000W/{m}^{2}°C\\ {T}_{\mathrm{ext}}{(A)}_{t=\mathrm{0s}}=289°C\\ {T}_{\mathrm{ext}}{(A)}_{t=\mathrm{12s}}=20°C\end{array}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

$T(r,0)=289°C$ for all $r$

Time Discretization ($t$ ) :

| 12 | not for | $[0.,12.]$ | either | $\Delta t=1.s$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | not for | $[12.,20.]$ | either | $\Delta t=4.s$ |
| 4 | not for | $[20.,100.]$ | either | $\Delta t=20.s$ |
| 2 | not for | $[100.,200.]$ | either | $\Delta t=50.s$ |
| 2 | not for | $[200.,400.]$ | either | $\Delta t=100.s$ |
| 8 | not for | $[400.,2000.]$ | either | $\Delta t=200.s$ |

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is obtained on a fine mesh with 99 elements quadratic QUAD8 in thickness without diagonal thermal mass matrix option.

### 2.2 Baseline

Temperatures at $\mathrm{M1}$ ($r=443.33$ ) and $\mathrm{M2}$ ($r=469.67$ ) points,

and at different times ($t=12.,100.,600.$ and $2000.$ ).

## 6 Summary of the results

AXIS_DIAG modelling gives quite satisfactory results. Although the mesh only has 3 elements in thickness, the difference on temperatures remains below 0.5%.

3D_DIAG modelling gives satisfactory results. the difference on temperatures also remains below 0.5%.

Despite the violence of the thermal shock, the diagonalization of the mass matrix thus allows to obtain a temperature solution that does not neosculate during the transient.
