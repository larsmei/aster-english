# r3.03.06 Shell–beam connection

**Summary:**

The shell–beam connection is described here. It makes it possible to join two parts of a mesh, one made up of beam elements (or of a discrete element), and the other meshed with shell elements (in order to represent phenomena lying outside beam kinematics). This development therefore operates under assumptions which express that it is the same beam kinematics which is transmitted between the two meshes, on either side of the connection. It takes the form of 6 linear relations linking the displacements of all the nodes of the shell edge to the 6 degrees of freedom of the end node of the beam.

## 1 Assumptions and applications

### 1.1 Assumptions and limitations

The shell–beam connection is described here. It is used to join two meshes, one comprising shell (or plate) elements, the other comprising beam elements. This feature makes it possible to model a slender structure in two parts: one part meshed with classical beam elements, representing beam kinematics and beam constitutive behaviour, and the other part meshed with shell elements, in order to bring out other phenomena (ovalisation, swelling, localised plasticity).

The following assumptions are nevertheless made:

- the area of the transverse section at the end of the shell mesh is identical to the area of the cross-section of the beam element which corresponds to it,
- the centres of gravity are identical,
- the sections are plane and coplanar,
- the normal to the shell section coincides with the axis of the beam.

Limitations:

- ovalisation of the cross-sections is not taken into account in the connection,
- warping is not taken into account.

### 1.2 Intended applications:

#### 1.2.1 Modelling of piping

One of the major applications concerns piping. The bent parts or the branch connections are then meshed with shells, which makes it possible to bring out an ovalisation, a local elastoplastic constitutive behaviour or a swelling in the event of internal pressure. This connection does not transmit the ovalisation of the pipes, since the latter is not modelled in the beam elements. To do so, the shell–pipe connection must be used, or else a sufficient length of straight piping must be meshed with shell elements so that the ovalisation at the connection is negligible.

#### 1.2.4 Application of a loading or of boundary conditions of “beam” type

At the end of a slender structure meshed with shells, it is often useful to impose either a loading of “beam” type, that is a force torsor, or boundary conditions (clamped support) compatible with beam kinematics. The transverse end section of the shell mesh may then be connected to a discrete element, on which this torsor or this clamped support will be applied.

## 2 Application of the 3D–beam connection method. Constraint equations

The approach is identical to that of the beam–3D connection [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03): the connection takes the form of 6 linear relations linking the displacements of all the shell nodes of the connection section (6 degrees of freedom per node, as compared with 3 degrees of freedom per node in 3D) to the 6 degrees of freedom of the beam node. The shell connection section is composed of shell-edge elements (segments). On the transverse connection section, the “shell” displacement field is decomposed into a “beam” part and a “complementary” part. This leads us to define the kinematic connection conditions between beam and shell as the equality of the beam displacement (distributor torsor or kinematic torsor) and of the beam part of the shell displacement field.

As in [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03), one introduces the space $\mathrm{T}$ of the fields associated with a kinematic torsor (defined by two vectors):

$\mathrm{T}=\left\lbrace v\in V/\exists (T,\Omega )\text{tel que}v(M)=\mathrm{T}+\Omega \mathrm{\wedge }\text{GM}\right\rbrace$                     eq 2-1

Here, $G$ represents the centre of gravity of the connection section (which must be identical to that of the beam). For the displacement fields of $\mathrm{T}$ , $\mathrm{T}$ is the translation of the section (or of the point $G$ ), $\Omega$ the infinitesimal rotation, and the fields $v$ are the displacements of the space of admissible displacements *V*  which keep the section $S$ plane and undeformed (the NAVIER-BERNOULLI hypotheses are again used here).

The vector subspace $T$ being of finite dimension (equal to 6) possesses an orthogonal complement for the scalar product defined on $V$ :

${T}^{\perp }=\left\lbrace v\in V/{\int}_{s}v(M).w(M)\text{dS}=0\text{}\forall w\in T\right\rbrace$                         eq 2-2

Or, more explicitly:

${T}^{\perp }=\left\lbrace v\in V/{\int}_{s}v(M)\text{dS}=0\text{et}{\int}_{s}\text{GM}\wedge v\text{dS}=0\right\rbrace$                     eq 2-3

Every field of $V$ decomposes uniquely as the sum of an element of $T$ and of an element of ${T}^{\perp }$ .

$u={u}^{p}+{u}^{s}\text{}{u}^{p}\in T\text{},\text{}{u}^{s}\in {T}^{\perp }$                     eq 2-4

One has in addition the following property:

For every pair of shell fields $(w,v)$ defined on $S$ ,

$\begin{array}{c}w={w}^{p}+{w}^{s}\\ v={v}^{p}+{v}^{s}\end{array}\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}{\int}_{s}v.w\text{dS}={\int}_{s}{v}^{p}.{w}^{p}\text{dS}+{\int}_{s}{v}^{s}.{w}^{s}\text{dS}$             eq 2-5

**Definition:**

*The beam displacement component of a shell field* $u$ *defined on the section is the name given to the component* ${u}^{p}\text{de}u$ *on the subspace* $T$ .

One immediately obtains the characterisation:

${T}_{u}=\frac{1}{\mid S\mid }{\int}_{S}u\text{dS},\text{}{\Omega}_{u}={I}^{-1}({\int}_{s}\text{GM}\wedge u\text{dS})$                 eq 2-7

where $\mid S\mid$ represents the area of the section $S\text{et}I$ the geometric inertia tensor of the surface $S$ , expressed at $G$ .

In other words, one may also say that the calculation of the beam part of a shell field $u$ is carried out by using the orthogonal-projection property, since $T\text{et}{T}^{\perp }$ are orthogonal by definition.

If one writes ${u}^{p}={T}_{u}+{\Omega}_{u}\wedge \text{GM}$ , then:

$({T}_{u},{\Omega}_{u})=\underset{(T,\Omega )}{\text{Argmin}}{\int}_{S}{(u-T-\Omega \wedge \text{GM})}^{2}$                     eq 2-6

The beam component of $u$ may therefore be interpreted as the beam displacement field closest to $u$ in the least-squares sense.

The kinematic connection condition sought between the shell field on $S$ and the elements of the beam displacement torsor at $G$ is given by:

$\mid S\mid T-{\int}_{S}u\text{dS}=0\text{}I(\Omega )-{\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge u\text{dS}=0$                 eq 2-8

Equation [eq 2-8] shows that the situation is identical to the 3D–beam case. The linear relations will have the same form. The only difference comes from the integrals over $S$ *(which here represents a curve corresponding to the shell section, modelled by shell-edge elements). In addition, the shell displacement field involves rotational degrees of freedom.*

In order to translate equation [eq 2-8] into linear relations, the two integrals must be computed:

- mean displacement: ${\int}_{S}u\text{dS}$
- mean rotation: ${\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge u\text{dS}$

## 3 Integrals to be computed. Shell kinematics.

For each node, the program computes the coefficients of the 6 linear relations [eq 2-8] which link:

- the 6 degrees of freedom of the beam node $\text{P}$ (geometrically coincident with the centre of gravity $G$ of the transverse section of the shell mesh)
- with the degrees of freedom of **all**  the nodes of the list of shell-edge mesh cells.

These linear relations are dualised, like all the linear relations arising, for example, from the keyword LIAISON_DDL of AFFE_CHAR_MECA. They are built, as for the 3D–beam connection, from the assembly of elementary terms.

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.06/10002F0C000069D500002796DC6D90EBCCC47F81.svg)

Shell or plate kinematics, linear through the thickness:

$u(M)=u(Q)+(\theta (Q)\wedge n).{y}_{3}$

- $u$ is the displacement vector of the middle surface at $Q$ ,
- $n$ is the vector normal to the middle surface of the shell at $Q$ ,
- $\theta$ is the rotation vector at $Q$ of the normal along the directions ${t}_{1}\text{et}{t}_{2}$ of the tangent plane
- ${y}_{3}$ is the through-thickness coordinate (${y}_{3}\in \left]-\frac{h}{2},\frac{h}{2}\right[$ ).

### 3.1 Computation of the mean displacement over the section S

The aim is to compute the integral ${\int}_{S}u\text{dS}$ , where $u$ is the shell displacement (comprising 6 degrees of freedom per node), and $S$ is the shell edge of the transverse connection section.

The mean displacement over the section $S$ is written:

${\int}_{s}u(M)\text{ds}=h{\int}_{l}u(Q)\text{ds}+{\int}_{l}(\theta (Q)\wedge n)({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\text{ds}$

that is ${\int}_{s}u(M)\text{ds}=h{\int}_{l}u(Q)\text{ds}$

In this expression, the variations of the metric through the thickness of the shell are neglected.

### 3.2 Computation of the mean rotation of the section S

$\begin{array}{}{\int}_{s}\mathrm{GM}\wedge u(M)\text{ds}={\int}_{l}{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}(\mathrm{GQ}+{y}_{3}n(Q))\wedge (u(Q)+\theta (Q)\wedge n(Q).{y}_{3}){\text{dsdy}}_{3}\\ =h{\int}_{l}\mathrm{GQ}\wedge u(Q)\text{ds}+{\int}_{l}\mathrm{GQ}\wedge (\theta (Q)\wedge n(Q))\text{ds}{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3}\\ +{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge u(Q)({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\text{ds}+{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge (\theta (Q)\wedge n(Q)){\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}{y}_{3}^{2}{\text{dy}}_{3}.\text{ds}\end{array}$

that is ${\int}_{s}\mathrm{GM}\wedge u(M)\text{ds}=h{\int}_{l}\mathrm{GQ}\wedge u(Q)\text{ds}+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge (\theta (Q)\wedge n(Q))\text{ds}.$

### 3.3 Computation of the inertia tensor

The inertia tensor is defined by [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03):

$I(\Omega )={\int}_{s}\mathrm{GM}\wedge (\Omega \wedge \mathrm{GM})\text{ds}$

setting: $\mathrm{GM}=\mathrm{GQ}+n(Q).{y}_{3}.$

One obtains: $I(\Omega )=h{\int}_{l}\mathrm{GQ}\wedge (\Omega \wedge \mathrm{GQ})\text{ds}+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge (\Omega \wedge n(Q))\text{ds}$

### 3.4 Implementation of the method

The computation of the coefficients of the linear relations is carried out in two stages:

- computation of elementary quantities on the elements of the list of shell-edge mesh cells (mesh cells of type SEG2 or SEG3):
- one computes the 9 terms:
- ${\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{xds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{yds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{x}^{2}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{y}^{2}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{z}^{2}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{xyds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{xzds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{yzds}$

as well as terms arising from $I(\Omega ):\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}n\wedge (\Omega \wedge n)\text{ds}$

which makes it possible to compute: $\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}({n}_{y}^{2}+{n}_{z}^{2})\text{ds},\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}{n}_{x}{n}_{y}\text{ds},\text{etc}...$

- summation of these quantities over $(S)$ , whence the computation of:
- $A=\mid S\mid$
- position of $G$
- inertia tensor $I$
- knowing $G$ , elementary computation, on the elements of the list of shell-edge mesh cells, of:

${\int}_{\text{elt}}{N}_{i}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{\text{xN}}_{i}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{\text{yN}}_{i}\text{ds};{\int}_{\text{elt}}{\text{zN}}_{i}\text{ds}$ where $\begin{array}{c}\text{GM}=\left\lbrace x,y,z\right\rbrace \\ {N}_{i}=\text{fonctions de forme de l'élément}\end{array}$

(One need only note that, in the present case, the integrals over the edge elements are to be multiplied by the thickness of the shell: $\underset{\text{elt}}{\int}{N}_{i}\text{ds}=h\underset{l}{\int}{N}_{i}\text{dl}$ where $l$ represents the curvilinear abscissa of the middle fibre of the shell-edge element).

In addition, one adds the extra terms coming from: $\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge (\Omega \wedge n(Q))\text{ds}$

Writing $n=\mid \begin{array}{c}{n}_{x}\\ {n}_{y}\\ {n}_{z}\end{array}$ and $\theta =\mid \begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}\\ {\theta}_{y}\\ {\theta}_{z}\end{array}$ in the global frame, one obtains:

$n(Q)\wedge (\theta \wedge n(Q))=\mid \begin{array}{c}({n}_{y}^{2}+{n}_{z}^{2}){\theta}_{x}-{n}_{x}{n}_{y}{\theta}_{y}-{n}_{x}{n}_{z}{\theta}_{z}\\ -{n}_{x}{n}_{y}{\theta}_{x}+({n}_{x}^{2}+{n}_{z}^{2}){\theta}_{y}-{n}_{y}{n}_{z}{\theta}_{z}=A\theta \\ -{n}_{x}{n}_{z}{\theta}_{x}-{n}_{y}{n}_{z}{\theta}_{y}+({n}_{x}^{2}+{n}_{y}^{2}){\theta}_{z}\end{array}$

then:

$\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge (\Omega \wedge n(Q))\text{ds}=\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\sum_{\text{el}}({\int}_{\text{el}}A(s){N}_{j}(s)\text{ds}){\theta}_{j}$

- “assembly” of the terms computed above in order to obtain, at each of the nodes of the edge mesh cells, the coefficients of the terms of the linear relations.

## 4 Use

### 4.1 Modelling

For each connection, the user must define, under the factor keyword LIAISON_ELEM of AFFE_CHAR_MECA:

| $S$ : | the trace of the beam cross-section on the shell: this is done by means of the keywords MAILLE_1 and/or GROUP_MA_1, that is, the user gives the list of line mesh cells (assigned ‘edge’ elements of shell modelling) which geometrically represent this section. |
| --- | --- |
| $\text{P}$ : | a node (keyword NOEUD_1 or GROUP_NO_1) carrying the 6 classical beam degrees of freedom: DX, DY, DZ, DRX, DRY, DRZ |
| $V$ : | the vector defining the axis of the beam, oriented from the shell towards the beam, and defined by its coordinates by means of the keyword AXE_POUTRE: (v1, v2, v3) |

**Note:**

- *the node* $\text{P}$ *may be a node of a beam element or of a discrete element,*
- *the list of shell-edge mesh cells, defined by* *MAILLE or* *GROUP_MA must represent* **exactly** *the cross-section of the beam. This is an important constraint on the mesh.*

### 4.2 Examples and tests

#### 4.2.1 Test SSLX101

This is a straight beam subjected to unit forces at $\text{B}$ (tension, bending moments and torsion). A thin-tube section of thickness $\text{h}\ll \text{R}$ is taken.

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.06/Object_80.svg)

The clamped support at $\text{O}$ is achieved by means of a connection between the edge of the shell and a point element located at $\text{O}$ . This element is clamped (zero translations and rotations).

This makes it possible to obtain, in the shell, a state of stress very close to a “beam” solution: there is no perturbation of the stress field. The solution differs from the analytical solution (strength-of-materials solution, RDM) by 3%, this being due solely to the fineness of the mesh in shell elements.

#### 4.2.2 Bending of a plate

Consider a thin plate, sufficiently long, of length $\mathrm{2L}$ , of width $\text{b}$ , of thickness $\text{h}$ , modelled by a shell element $\text{OA}$ and a beam element on $\text{AB}$ :

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.06/Object_81.svg)

- The 1st connection condition is written:

$bhU(A)=h{\int}_{\text{CD}}U(y)\text{dy}$

the displacement of the point $\text{A}$ (belonging to the beam) is the mean of the displacements of the edge $\text{CD}$ of the plate.

- The 2nd connection condition is written:

$I(\Omega )=h{\int}_{\text{CD}}\mathrm{AQ}\wedge U(Q)\text{ds}+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{\text{CD}}\theta (Q)\text{ds}$

In the case of bending about $y$ , the only non-zero term is: $\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{-\frac{b}{2}}^{b/2}\theta (y)\text{dy}$

Indeed, $h{\int}_{\text{CD}}\mathrm{AQ}\wedge U(Q)\text{ds}=h({\int}_{-\frac{b}{2}}^{\frac{b}{2}}{U}_{z}\text{ydy}).x=0$

For bending about $y$ , the connection is therefore written:

${I}_{y}{\theta}_{y}(A)=\frac{b{h}^{3}}{12}{\theta}_{y}$ since ${\theta}_{y}$ is constant on CD.

This application is implemented in the test SSLX100B: 3D_coque_poutre mixture.
