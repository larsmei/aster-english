# r7.01.47 Damage law ENDO_LOCA_TC

**Summary:**

This document describes the isotropic quasi-brittle constitutive model ENDO_LOCA_TC.
It takes up the fundamentals of the models ENDO_ISOT_BETON [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04) and ENDO_LOCA_EXP [R7.01.42](doc:r7.01.42) and enriches them by introducing:

- a rationalised damage threshold with a view to optimising numerical robustness;
- an effect of damage in compression, so as to bound the stresses there, without however seeking to model the post-peak phase. The threshold stress in compression tends in a monotonic manner towards a limit, the compressive strength.

This document summarises the main information on the model for its use in code_aster, relying on the internal note . The user wishing to have more information on the model (complete description of the equations, mechanical or mathematical properties...) is invited to refer to this reference.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 History

The development of the model ENDO_LOCA_TC results from a progressive approach which now extends over more than 25 years within EDF. It aims to build a hierarchy of models with an increasingly rich phenomenology, but without sacrificing numerical robustness.
Figure r7.01.47-fig-tab_modeles indicates the phenomenological characteristics of some of these models.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.47/tab_modeles.png)

*Characteristics of the numerically robust damage models of Code_Aster*

ENDO_LOCA_TC meets a need brought out in recent studies: to have available a model which is both numerically robust
and able to account for the unilateral effect of damage on the initiation threshold
and on the current stiffness (effect of damage different in compression and in tension) as well as for the bounded character
of the stresses in tension and compression. ENDO_LOCA_TC is a robust local isotropic model without irreversible strains.

### 1.2 Requirements

In order to preserve the balance between phenomenological richness and numerical robustness, a compromise proves necessary in modelling. It is guided by the following minimum expectations, which in fact constitute the requirements of the model ENDO_LOCA_TC.

Phenomenological (physical) expectations:

- (AP1) The stresses remain bounded whatever the strain loading path.
- (AP2) The damage threshold differs according to the direction of loading in order to take account in particular of the contrast between tension and compression.
- (AP3) In the tension domain, the stress threshold corresponds to a Rankine criterion, close to the experimental observations, see .
- (AP4) Beyond a critical strain level in pure tension, the stress becomes zero.
- (AP5) Even for strain paths whose tensile components become high, a stiffness in compression remains (unilateral effect of damage on the stiffness).
- (AP6) In the current reversibility domain (elastic domain for a given mechanical state), any closed strain path leads to a closed stress path (elasticity) and neither produces nor consumes energy (hyperelasticity).
- (AP7) A path of pure slip type (shear in strain, typical of the loadings in the neighbourhood of the reinforcement) leads to a response qualitatively distinct from a path in pure shear stress.

Expectations guaranteeing numerical robustness:

- (AR1) There exists a continuous response in stress whatever the continuous strain path.
- (AR2) The process of numerical integration of the constitutive relation (for a given strain increment) admits a unique solution.
- (AR3) A robust numerical method is available to determine this solution whatever the level of imposed strain.
- (AR4) Only the damage process in tension results in a softening response.
- (AR5) The reversibility domain in strain space is convex, in order to allow the implementation of a piloting technique adapted to damage (PRED_ELAS)

Finally, one will prefer a model whose parameters remain limited in number and whose identification is deduced simply from the usual experimental measurements.

### 1.3 Field of use: local vs. non-local

Damage of a material leads to a decrease of its resistance capacity in terms of stresses that can be supported
: one speaks of softening or negative hardening. This physical characteristic is at the origin of the phenomenon of localisation
of the damage and of the strain in bands of moderate thickness .
In order to treat the localised case, specific models are required: these are the non-local constitutive models or
else the cohesive models (CZM), which make it possible to describe the localised regime properly.
A local model such as ENDO_LOCA_TC must be used in situations of diffuse damage which can appear
thanks to the presence of reinforcement in the concrete or because of an imposed-strain loading such as in thermal analysis.
The field of use of ENDO_LOCA_TC is therefore that of distributed cracks (or crack networks) for
which one can build a mean behaviour per REV (Representative Elementary Volume).

Conversely, one recommends not seeking to model the cracks individually by bands of localised damage one mesh cell thick. If such a result occurs during analyses under code_aster with a local model, its objectivity can be called into question.
A non-local version of ENDO_LOCA_TC is under development which will make it possible to predict the cracking of concrete at the scale of the individual crack.

## 2 Phenomenology & Use

### 2.1 Phenomena covered and modelling choices

The damage model ENDO_LOCA_TC is isotropic. It therefore accounts neither for an initial anisotropy – the initial elasticity is isotropic there, as is the damage threshold – nor for an induced anisotropy:
the damage accumulates without keeping the memory of the loading directions which lead to its evolution, so that the current elasticity and damage threshold remain isotropic.

This isotropic framework nevertheless makes it possible to distinguish tension vs. compression effects. Indeed, among the loading directions (the principal directions of the strains), it is possible to identify those corresponding to positive principal strains (respectively negative):
the directions of tension (resp. compression). This distinction makes it possible to separate the elastic stress (that which one would observe under the same strain in the absence of damage) into two terms, one relating to tension and the other to compression, which will be affected differently by the damage in order to supply the stress actually undergone by the point considered.
At fixed damage, the model is therefore non-linear elastic.

The model accounts for two distinct contributions to the damage. A first, called tensile damage, is driven by the tensile strains and weights only the elastic tensile stress.
For increasing tensile strains, this weighting coefficient (denoted A), initially equal to 1, remains so up to a certain tensile strength threshold, then decreases until it becomes zero (total ultimate damage) for a strain which is sufficiently high but finite, so that only the elastic compressive stress still contributes to the stress.
This means that at ultimate tensile damage, the point no longer resists tensile loadings but only compressive ones.

A second damage component is driven by the compressive strains (more precisely the elastic compressive stress). This is why it is called compressive damage, even if it affects not only the elastic compressive stress but also the tensile one (it therefore weights the whole elastic stress). For increasing compressive strains (in absolute value), this weighting coefficient (denoted C), initially equal to 1, remains so up to a certain threshold of loss of linearity in compression, then decreases and tends asymptotically towards 0 but without ever reaching the zero value. Its evolution as a function of the strain is such that the stresses remain bounded. But unlike tension, they continue to increase (no softening in compression), until they reach an ultimate value, the compressive strength limit.

The model ENDO_LOCA_TC presents no dissipative mechanisms other than the tensile and compressive damages. In particular, it accounts for no irreversible strain.
Finally, it is possible to take account of the influence on the mechanical response of other physical phenomena such as thermal effects or the hydric transfers within the concrete. On the one hand, the temperature can influence the characteristic parameters of the model. And on the other hand, the latter is expressed in mechanical strains, that is the strains of thermal or drying origin are subtracted from the total strain.

### 2.2 Parameters of the model

In addition to the elastic parameters (Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio), the parameters of the model are five in number:

- $f_{t}$ the tensile strength with $f_{t} > 0$
- $p$ a tensile hardening coefficient, with in practice $p\in\left[-0.2,9\right]$
- $\bar{\omega}$ the energy to failure in confined tension normalised by the elastic energy at the initiation of damage  with $\bar{\omega}> \frac{4}{3\pi}\left(p+2\right)^{3/2}$
- $f_{c}$ the (mean) compressive strength with $f_{c} > 2\,f_{t}$
- $\sigma^{0}$ the initiation threshold stress in compression with $2\,f_{t} \leq \sigma^{0} < f_{c}$

In the absence of tests which would allow a more precise recalibration, the fib Model Code () supplies orders of magnitude on the basis of correlations for Young's modulus E (in MPa), Poisson's ratio $\nu$, the fracture energy $G_{f}$ (in N/m) and the tensile strength $f_{t}$ (in MPa) as a function of the compressive strength $f_{c}$ (in MPa):

$$
E = 21500\,\left(\frac{f_{c}}{10}\right)^{\left(1/3\right)} \quad ; \quad  \nu = 0.2 \quad ; \quad  G_{F}=73\,f_{c}^{0.18} \quad ; \quad  f_{t}=0.3\,\left(f_{c}-8\right)^{\left(2/3\right)}
$$

In consistency with the estimation of the secant Young's modulus proposed in Eurocode 2 (), the value of the initiation threshold in compression can be chosen by:

$$
\sigma^{0}=0.4\,f_{c}
$$

The mean inter-crack distance $L_{F}$ can be estimated a priori as a function of the reinforcement and of the simplistic homogenisation scheme illustrated in r7.01.47-fig-nonlocal:

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.47/local_nonlocal.svg)

*Illustration of the principle of equivalence between homogeneous model and cohesive model*

This simplistic 1D homogenisation scheme, described in , makes it possible to pass from the cohesive response of a crack (CZM, deduced from the non-local model) to the mean response of the REV made up of a crack and of a surrounding elastic zone. The responses of the two models are built so that under a given stress $\sigma$ , the elongation of the REV, denoted $\triangle U$, is identical. One sees appear explicitly the role of the mean inter-crack distance $L_{F}$ (i.e. the size of the REV cell) which establishes the link between the elongation $\triangle U$ and the mean strain $\frac{\triangle U}{L_{F}}$ which is the input variable of the local model.

With this value of the mean inter-crack distance $L_{F}$, the normalised consumed energy $\bar{\omega}$ is expressed as a function of the other parameters as follows:

$$
\bar{\omega}=\frac{2E_{C}\,G_{F}}{L_{F}\,f_{t}^2}
$$

With the confined-tension Young's modulus defined by:

$$
E_{c}=\lambda+2\mu=\frac{\left(1-\nu\right)\,E}{\left(1-2\nu\right)\left(1+\nu\right)}
$$

Finally, as regards the hardening coefficient $p$, a value of - 0.2 leads to a quasi-linear (negative) hardening, whereas values lying between 1 and 4 are more representative of the hollowing of the tensile curves presented in , see . Let us note moreover that a hardening coefficient $p=1.5$ corresponds to the response closest to the “mean” bilinear cohesive model introduced in .

### 2.5 Aid to convergence

Damage analyses can be difficult to make converge because there exists a risk of brutal propagation of the damage related to the quasi-brittle character of concrete in tensile damage. Two numerical strategies make it possible to recover a form of continuity of the evolution of the damage with respect to the applied loading.

The first consists in adapting the intensity of the loading independently of its real chronology; one then speaks of load piloting (by a continuation method).
A well-adapted piloting function, named piloting by elastic prediction in , consists in controlling the maximum damage increment in the structure during the current time step.
This method, designated by PRED_ELAS in code_aster, can be used in the absence of command variables (such as a thermal transient) and of loadings depending on physical time, see document [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80).

An alternative numerical strategy consists in introducing a share of viscosity into the evolution of the damage.
It is characterised by a parameter denoted $\tau$ which has the dimension of a time: the higher it is, the more pronounced the viscosity effect. The viscosity will induce a continuous evolution of the damage which will help convergence but it will lead to a delay of the damage. The characteristic viscosity time $\tau$ corresponds to TAU_REGU_VISC in DEFI_MATERIAU. It is optional and requires an advanced knowledge of the model in order to be used.

### 2.6 Internal variables

The two components of the damage, A and C, are stored in the internal variables of the model (more precisely, ENDOTRAC corresponds to $1-A$ and ENDOCOMP to $1-C$, in order to keep the current usage of a damage initially zero which evolves up to 1).
These are the only two “true” internal variables.

Other variables are also placed in the list of internal variables but they correspond to post-processings of the two previous ones.
Thus, the variables A and C can be combined in order to reflect the relative loss of stiffness in the current loading direction (for which loadings both in tension and in compression are generally superimposed): this is the “internal” variable ENDO. In the same vein, ENDOTOT expresses the most penalising loss of stiffness by considering all the loading directions; it is equal to $1\,-\,A\,C$.

Moreover, two other (equivalent) measures of the damage (HISTTRAC and HISTCOMP) are also placed in the list of internal variables.
These are the “raw” damages directly driven by the thresholds in tension and in compression (these are the variables which one will denote later by $a$ and $c$ where HISTTRAC corresponds to $a$ and HISTCOMP to $c-1$), kept essentially for practical numerical reasons.

Finally, the strain energies corresponding to the elastic stresses of tension (ENERTRAC) and of compression (ENERCOMP) are also stored among the “internal” variables of the model. In the presence of viscosity, a last internal variable (SIGMVISC) supplies an estimate of the delay taken by the damage for the current strain level. This is the difference between the current stress and the stress which one would observe in the absence of viscosity, that is for a damage without delay. The difference is evaluated in terms of maximum principal stress.

| Name | Number | Meaning |

| HISTTRAC | V3 | Tensile damage corresponding to a |
| ENERTRAC | V4 | Strain energy corresponding to the elastic tensile stresses |
| ENDOCOMP | V5 | Stiffness in compression equal to $1-C$ with C the compressive damage component |

## 3 Equations of the model

### 3.1 Strain-stress relation

The mechanical state is described by the strain $\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}$, a tensile damage $a\in\left[0,1\right]$ which evolves from 0 (sound) to 1 (broken) and a compressive damage $c$ which evolves from 1 (sound) to $+\infty$ (saturated). The strain energy is defined by:

$$
w \left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},a,c\right)=C\left(c\right)\left[A\left(a\right) w^{+}\left(\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}\right)+ w^{-}\left(\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}\right) \right] \quad ; \quad  w^{+/-}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\lambda\langle trac\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)\rangle^{2}_{+/-}+2\mu\sum_1^3\langle\varepsilon_{i}\rangle^{2}_{+/-}\right]
$$

$w^{+}$ and $w^{-}$ correspond to a partition of the elastic energy $w_{e}$ between the effects of tension and of compression such that:

$$
w_{e}=w^{+}+w^{-}
$$

The stress – strain relation derives from the previous potential:

$$
\sigma=\frac{\partial w}{\partial \tensTwo{ \varepsilon}}=C\left(c\right)\left[A\left(a\right)\tensTwo{ s}^{+}+\tensTwo{ s}^{-} \right]
$$

Where

$$
\tensTwo{ s}^{+}=\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial \tensTwo{ \varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}\right) \quad ; \quad  \tensTwo{ s}^{-}=\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial \tensTwo{ \varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}\right)
$$

$A\left(a\right)$ is the normalised stiffness function in confined tension and it is chosen identical to that of the model ENDO_LOCA_EXP ([R7.01.42](doc:r7.01.42)).

$$
A\left(a\right)=\frac{1-a}{e\left(a\right)} \quad ; \quad  e\left(a\right) = 1-a+\frac{\bar{\omega}}{2}\left[m_{0}a+\left(D_{1}-m_{0}\right)a^{r}\right]
$$

$m_{0}$, $D_{1}$ and r are expressed solely with the help of the parameter $p$ of the law ENDO_LOCA_TC:

$$
m_{0}=\frac{3\pi}{2}\left(p+2\right)^{-3/2}, D_{1}=\frac{3\pi}{4}\sqrt{1+p}\quad ; \quad  r= \frac{2\left(D_{1}-1\right)-m_{0}}{2-m_{0}}
$$

Thus, the parameters introduced are limited to the coefficient $p$ which influences the shape of the post-peak response, the more hollowed the higher $p$ is, and to the normalised energy to failure in confined tension defined by

$$
\bar{\omega}=\frac{\omega}{w_{c}}
$$

Where $\omega$ is the volumetric energy to failure of the concrete in a confined-tension test and $w_{c}$ is the elastic energy at the initiation of damage in confined tension.

The graph of the stiffness function A for the set of values of r7.01.47-tab-para is presented in r7.01.47-fig-evol_A.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.47/evol_A.svg)

*Tensile stiffness function A as a function of the tensile damage a.*

$C\left(c\right)$ is the normalised residual compressive stiffness. It affects the whole stiffness under the effect of compression. It is:

$$
C\left(c\right)=\frac{1}{c}\left[1+\left(\frac{f_{c}}{\sigma^{0}}-1\right)\tanh\left(\frac{c-1}{\frac{f_{c}}{\sigma^{0}}-1}\right)\right]\quad ; \quad f_{c}>\sigma^{0}
$$

Two new parameters are introduced: the compressive strength of the concrete $f_{c}$ and the initiation threshold in compression $\sigma^{0}$. The graph of the function C for the set of values of r7.01.47-tab-para is presented in r7.01.47-fig-evol_C.

### 3.2 Evolution of the tensile damage

The strain relative to the tensile damage is defined by:

$$
\chi_{t}=\langle\frac{s_{3}}{f_{t}}\rangle_{+} \quad ; \quad  s_{3}=\lambda_{3}\left(\tensTwo{ s}\right)
$$

$f_{t}$ is a parameter of the law corresponding to the tensile limit of the concrete. $s_{3}=\lambda_{3}\left(\tensTwo{ s}\right)$ denotes the maximum eigenvalue of the elastic stress tensor $\tensTwo{ s}$ defined as the derivative of the elastic energy density ().

$$
\tensTwo{ s} =\frac{\partial w_{e}}{\partial \tensTwo{\varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)=s^{+}+s^{-}
$$

The strain measure $\chi_{t}$ makes it possible to build the threshold function for the tensile damage, as well as the corresponding evolution equation. The equation governing the threshold function in tension is:

$$
g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{{\varepsilon}},a\right) = \chi_{t}\left(\tensTwo{{\varepsilon}}\right)^{2}-e\left(a\right)^{2}
$$

with $e\left(a\right)$ the damage threshold defined by . The evolution law of the damage in tension is:

$$
g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{{\varepsilon}},a\right) \leq 0\quad ; \quad  \dot{a}\geq0\quad ; \quad \dot{a}\,g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{{\varepsilon}},a\right) = 0
$$

For an initially undamaged state, one has $a\left(0\right)=0$

### 3.3 Evolution of the compressive damage

The evolution of the relative residual stiffness in compression C is governed by a mechanism similar to that of tension.
The strain measure relative to the damage in compression is defined as follows:

$$
\chi_{c}=\langle-\,\frac{s_{1}^{-}}{\sigma^{0}}\rangle_{+} \quad ; \quad  s_{1}^{-}=\lambda_{1}\left(\tensTwo{ s}^{-}\right)
$$

Where $\sigma^{0}\geq 2\,f_{t}$ in order to guarantee certain properties of the model such as the absence of initiation of damage in tension in a compressive loading. $\lambda_{1}\left(\tensTwo{ s}^{-}\right)$ is the smallest eigenvalue of the tensor $\tensTwo{ s}^{-}$ defined in . The evolution of the damage is then governed by means of the following threshold function:

$$
g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},c\right) = \chi_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)-c
$$

The evolution law of the damage in compression is written:

$$
g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},c\right) \leq 0\quad ; \quad  \dot{c}\geq0\quad ; \quad \dot{c}\,g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},c\right) = 0
$$

For an initially undamaged state, one has $c\left(0\right)=1$. Unlike the tensile damage measure a which evolves from 0 to 1,
the compressive damage c evolves from 1 to $+\infty$.

## 4 Numerical implementation

In general, the integration of a constitutive relation consists in determining the time history of the internal variables,
here $t\rightarrow a\left(t\right)$ and $t\rightarrow c\left(t\right)$, and of the stresses $t\rightarrow \tensTwo{\sigma}\left(t\right)$
for a strain loading $t\rightarrow \tensTwo{\varepsilon}\left(t\right)$.
Traditionally, this operation is carried out by proceeding to a discretisation in time of the differential equations which characterise the behaviour and then to the treatment of the (non-linear) algebraic systems which result from it.

### 4.1 Equations discretised in time

For the discretisation in time of the constitutive relation ENDO_LOCA_TC, one adopts an implicit Euler scheme, of order 1.
For a time step $\left[t_{n},t_{n+1}\right]$
and any quantity (grandeur) $X$, one denotes $X_{n}=X\left(t_{n}\right)$, $X_{n+1}=X\left(t_{n+1}\right)$ and $\triangle X =X_{n+1}-X_{n}$.
The discretisation of the stress – strain relation via the scheme retained leads to:

$$
\tensTwo{\sigma}_{n+1}=C\left(c_{n+1}\right)\left[A\left(a_{n+1}\right)\tensTwo {s}_{n+1}^{+}+\tensTwo {s}_{n+1}^{-} \right]
$$

Where

$$
\tensTwo {s}_{n+1}^{+}=\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial \tensTwo{ \varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{ \varepsilon}_{n+1}\right) \quad ; \quad  \tensTwo {s}_{n+1}^{-}=\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial \tensTwo{ \varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo {\varepsilon}_{n+1}\right)
$$

The discretisation of the evolution equation in tension is written:

$$
g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},a_{n+1}\right) \leq 0\quad ; \quad  \triangle a\geq0\quad ; \quad \triangle a \,g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},a_{n+1}\right) = 0
$$

Finally, the evolution equation in compression is written after discretisation:

$$
g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},c_{n+1}\right) \leq 0\quad ; \quad  \triangle c\geq0\quad ; \quad \triangle c_{n+1}\, g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},c_{n+1}\right) = 0
$$

### 4.2 Solution of the algebraic system

The integration of the behaviour consists in determining $a_{n+1}$ and $c_{n+1}$ knowing $a_{n}$, $c_{n}$, $\varepsilon_{n}$
and $\triangle \varepsilon$ and then in computing $\sigma_{n+1}$ via .
It is interesting to note that the decoupling between the evolution laws makes it possible to determine $a_{n+1}$ and $c_{n+1}$ independently.
Let us begin with the computation of the tensile damage at the end of the time step $a_{n+1}$ with the help of the definition of the threshold:

$$
g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},a_{n+1}\right) = \chi_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1}\right)^{2}-e\left(a_{n+1}\right)^{2}
$$

As the function $g_{t}$ is strictly decreasing on [0,1],  it admits a unique solution:

- if $g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},a_{n}\right)\leq 0$ then $a_{n+1}=a_{n}$.
- if $g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},1\right)\geq 0$ then $a_{n+1}=1$ because the damage in tension is bounded.
- otherwise $a_{n+1}$ is the unique solution of $g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1},a_{n+1}\right)= 0$ and lies strictly between $a_{n}$ and 1.

In order to solve the last case, it is necessary to go through the solution of a scalar equation.
As $g_{t}$ is continuously differentiable, one can implement a Newton method with control of the bounds.

As regards the evolution of the damage in compression, the problem  admits a unique solution.
Indeed, as the function $c\rightarrow g_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},c\right)=\chi_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)-c$ is affine,
the solution is written simply:

$$
c_{n+1}=max\left(c_{n},\chi_{c}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n+1}\right)\right)
$$

### 4.3 Tangent matrix

ENDO_LOCA_TC is a constitutive law formulated in mechanical strain.
In order to illustrate well the role of this choice, one considers here by way of example the effect of the temperature $T$
through a thermal strain $\tensTwo{\varepsilon^{th}}$ and a dependence of the parameters of the model on the temperature.
The stress coming from the behaviour-integration process of the previous paragraph can be written formally:

$$
\tensTwo{\sigma^{num}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},T\right)
$$

Where the mechanical strain is defined by:

$$
\tensTwo{\varepsilon}=\triangledown^{s}\vector{u}-\tensTwo{\varepsilon^{th}}
$$

Following , the algorithm for solving the equilibrium equations of Code_Aster relies on a limited expansion
to order 1 of the stresses at each integration point. In the present case, it is written:

$$
\tensTwo{\sigma^{sol}}_{n+1}=\tensTwo{\sigma^{num}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon^{sol}}_{n+1},T_{n+1}\right)\approx\tensTwo{\sigma^{num}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},T_{n+1}\right)+\frac{\partial \tensTwo{\sigma^{num}}}{\partial \tensTwo{\varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},T_{n+1}\right)\bf{:}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon^{sol}}_{n+1}-\tensTwo{\varepsilon}\right)
$$

where $\tensTwo{\varepsilon^{sol}}_{n+1}$  and $\tensTwo{\sigma^{sol}}_{n+1}$  denote respectively the strain and the stress solutions of the problem at the end of the time step.
The mechanical strain $\tensTwo{\varepsilon}$ in the neighbourhood of which the limited expansion is carried out is the mechanical strain at the beginning of the time step $\tensTwo{\varepsilon}_{n}$ in the prediction phase of the algorithm and the last iterate of the mechanical strain in the correction phase.

During the integration of the behaviour this mechanical strain $\tensTwo{\varepsilon}$ is an input datum, just as the temperature at the end of the time step $T_{n+1}$ (chaining and not coupling of the thermal and the mechanical analyses). As for the (consistent) tangent operator, it is the factor of the order-1 term in the limited expansion, namely:

$$
\tensFour{L}=\frac{\partial \tensTwo{\sigma^{num}}}{\partial \tensTwo{\varepsilon}}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon},T_{n+1}\right)
$$

In order to compute this tangent operator, one proceeds by differentiation, with a view to identifying $\tensFour{L}$ via $d\tensTwo{\sigma}=\tensFour{L}\bf{:}d\tensTwo{\varepsilon}$. Starting from the expression of the stress  where  the index n+1 is omitted in order to lighten the notations, one obtains the following equation:

$$
d\tensTwo{\sigma}=dC\left[A\,\tensTwo{s}^{+}+\tensTwo{s}^{-}\right]+C\,dA\,\tensTwo{s}^{+}+C\left[A\frac{\partial^{2} w^{+}}{\partial \tensTwo{\varepsilon}^{2}}+\frac{\partial^{2} w^{-}}{\partial \tensTwo{\varepsilon}^{2}}\right]\bf{:}\,d\tensTwo{\varepsilon}
$$

In this expression, one exploits the $C^{2}$ character of $w^{+}$ and $w^{-}$ by implementing a regularisation of the latter introduced in . The variations of A and C are then computed by differentiating the corresponding consistency equations. In the regime of active tensile damage (otherwise $dA=0$), the equation $g_{t}$ coming from  and  leads to:

$$
dg_{t}=2\chi_{t}d\chi_{t}-2e\left(a\right)e^{'}\left(a\right)da=0
$$

and from there:

$$
dA=A^{'}\left(a\right)da\quad ; \quad da=\frac{\chi_{t}d\chi_{t}}{e\left(a\right)e^{'}\left(a\right)}
$$

One proceeds in the same way in order to evaluate $dC$ in the regime of active compressive damage (otherwise $dC=0$),
via the differentiation of the consistency equation $g_{c}=0$ coming from .

$$
dC=C^{'}\left(c\right)dc\quad ; \quad dc=d\chi_{c}
$$

### 4.4 Piloting PRED_ELAS

In general, the piloting consists in decomposing the external loading (imposed forces and displacements) into two terms, one fixed (FIXE_CSTE, in the vocabulary of Code_Aster) and the other (FIXE_PILO) proportional to the piloting parameter $\eta$ (ETA_PILOTAGE). One then seeks to determine $\eta$ so as to satisfy a piloting equation which depends on the degrees of freedom of the problem and on a parameter which controls the “speed” of advance along the branch of solutions.

In the case of damage models, the choice of the piloting PRED_ELAS introduced in  is well adapted:
the advance parameter is a target damage increment denoted $\triangle a_{cible}$
and the piloting equation ensures that one (integration) point of the structure actually reaches this target damage whereas all the other points damage less (or not at all).
In this way, one adjusts the loading so that the structure damages progressively.

The solution algorithm proposed in  relies on a Newton method
for the solution of the equilibrium equations at the scale of the structure and a technique of nested intervals for the piloting equation (which requires that the reversibility domain be convex).
The only specificity relative to the constitutive model retained is expressed by the solution of the following scalar equation in $\eta$, at the level of each integration point:

$$
g_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{fixe}}+\eta\,\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{pilo}},a_{n}+\triangle a_{cible}\right)=0
$$

Where $\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{fixe}}$ and $\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{pilo}}$ are strains known at this stage and
associated respectively with the fixed and pilotable loadings, $g_{t}$ the threshold function in tension
and finally $a_{n}$ again denotes the damage at the beginning of the current time step.
Given the form of the threshold function, the local piloting equation is also written:

$$
\chi_{t}\left(\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{fixe}}+\eta\,\tensTwo{\varepsilon_{pilo}}\right)=e\left(a_{n}+\triangle a_{cible}\right)
$$

details the computation of the piloting function and of the associated bounds.
