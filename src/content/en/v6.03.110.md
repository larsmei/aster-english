# v6.03.110 SSNP110 - Edge crack in a rectangular, finished elastoplastic plate

**Summary:**

This test is a test case in nonlinear fracture mechanics.

A finished rectangular plate is considered, cracked and subjected to traction loading. The constitutive law used is an elastoplastic law of Von Mises.

This test case includes five modellings in $\mathrm{2D}$:

- A Modelling, in flat stress, under the hypothesis of small deformations (DEFORMATION= 'PETIT' in the STAT_NON_LINE operator);
- Model B, removed in V15, allowed, in flat stresses, to study the influence of the consideration of the second order terms of deformations (DEFORMATION= 'GREEN' in STAT_NON_LINE operator) on the calculation of energy release rate $G$;
- modellings C and D, in flat stress, are designed to test the X-FEM method (non-meshed crack) in non-linear elasticity and elastoplasticity for the calculation of the displacement field;
- Modelling E, in flat deformations, validates the calculation of $G$ for the different methods of definition of the hardening curve;
- F Modelling is identical to C with quadratic elements.

## 4 Modelling C

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a nonlinear elasticity calculation under the hypothesis of small shifts, using the X-FEM method (non-meshed crack). The Modelling is in flat stress.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh is composed of linear d-elements (560 mesh cells QUA4 - 600 nodes). The refinement of the mesh is uniform (18 elements according to $x$ and 31 elements according to $y$).

### 4.3 Tested quantities and results

The $\mathit{DY}$ component of the displacement is tested at both points where the crack leads (points of each of the lips), at the last moment of the calculation (imposed displacement $0,1\mathit{mm}$). This test is performed on the displacement field created by the POST_CHAM_XFEM operator.

The reference value is the solution obtained in model A.

## 5 Modelling D

This is an elastoplasticity calculation based on the small displacements hypothesis, using the X-FEM method (non-mesh crack). The crack opening and closing is represented by activating the contact on the lips.

Compared to model C, nonlinear elasticity is replaced by plasticity. Up to the moment 0.1 the load is monotonous and the results are therefore identical between modellings C and D. In model D, a discharge is carried out and an opposite load is applied, thus activating the contact on the lips of the crack.

### 6.3 Tested quantities and results

The quantity tested is the $G$ restitution rate , calculated for the first integration crown defined in model A and for ten values of the horizontal displacement imposed $\delta$

Two separate sets of calculations are performed:

The first step is to take a linearly hardened constitutive law. Three calculations are performed using the different methods available to define the same hardening curve (TRACTION, ECRO_LINE and ECRO_PUIS). The solutions obtained from these three calculations are compared.

Then a power-shaping constitutive law is selected and the results obtained between ELAS_VMIS_TRAC and ELAS_VMIS_PUIS are compared.

The test is non-regression type.

the three different calculations in linear nutring lead to exactly the same result on $G$;

both power-law hardening calculations lead to very close results (difference less than 0.3%). the difference decreases if the time step of the calculation is reduced.

## 8 Summary of the results

This test case is designed to validate the calculation of the yield rate of the elastoplasticity stresses:

    - modellings A and B ensure that the energy release rate calculation is invariant by the theta method following the integration crowns for constitutive laws elastic-perfectly plastic type. The low contribution of second-order terms to deformation is also noted;
    - Modelling E validates the different methods of definition of the hardening curve.

The modellings C, D and F validate the displacement field calculation for the X-FEM models.
