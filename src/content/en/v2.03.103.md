# v2.03.103 SDLS103 - Coaxial rings under ring flow: inertial coupling between modes

**Summary**

A physical configuration consisting of two coaxial cylindrical hulls is considered in interaction with a fluid flowing into the ring space separating the hulls.

The purpose of the test case is to validate the fluid-structure coupling model developed in the CALC_FLUI_STRU operator for this type of configuration.

The reference solution is supplied by a calculation made with *Code_Aster* implementing the CALC_MATR_AJOU operator.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The configuration studied consists of two 4 m high coaxial cylindrical hulls:

![Figure](/figures/v2.03.103/100014EE000069F000004D1A83A95521F092C3B2.svg)

The inner shell has an average $1m$ radius and a thickness of $1\mathrm{cm}$.

The outer shell has an average $1,10m$ radius and a thickness of $1\mathrm{cm}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material constituting the two hulls is steel. Its physical characteristics are:

| $\rho =7800\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | $E=2.{10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$ | $\nu =0,3$ |
| --- | --- | --- |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The support conditions are the same for both hulls: clamped ends in the lower part ($z=0$ ) and free in the upper part ($z=4m$ ).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is provided by a calculation using *Code_Aster* implementing the CALC_MATR_AJOU operator.

This is done by using an mesh with elements of the structure (internal and external hulls), elements of volume fluid (ring space) and elements of fluid-structure interface. This method is described in full in [bib1].

On the interface elements, the normal zero speed limit condition is imposed, reflecting the condition of non-ingress of the fluid into the hulls.

On the inlet and outlet sections of the annular space, the limit condition of zero potential is imposed, reflecting the null disturbed pressure condition at the ends.

A first modal calculation of the air structure is performed. The CALC_MATR_AJOU operator then calculates the mass matrix added by the fluid, projected on the modal basis of the air structure. This mass matrix can then be recombined with the generalised mass matrix of the air structure, and then solve a new modal problem that leads to the characteristics of the water system at rest. **The results obtained for the eigenfrequencies of the water system at rest constitute the reference solution.**

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

In the case studied, half-hull thicknesses represent 10% of the dimension of the ring game. In the reference calculation made by operator CALC_MATR_AJOU, half-thicknesses are neglected for the definition of the fluid domain. Therefore, a systematic error of the order of 5% on frequencies is expected, due to this approximation.

In addition, the differences in Modelling and resolution between the numerical method implemented in the CALC_MATR_AJOU operator and the analytical model developed in the CALC_FLUI_STRU operator induce an additional difference.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The structure geometry and characteristics of the material constituting the hulls were previously presented.

Concerning the absolute roughness of the walls of the structures, we take a value of 10–5 meters.

The surrounding fluid is water. The values taken for density and kinematic viscosity are respectively

$$
{\rho}_{f}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}
$$

Uncontained flow upstream and downstream of structures is considered.

The fluid-structure coupling problem is solved by the MOCCA_COQUE [bib2] analytical model integrated in *Code_Aster* (operator CALC_FLUI_STRU), for an average zero flow speed: the modal characteristics of the water system at rest are obtained by taking into account the added mass effects.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

Compared to the reference calculation, the Mesh characteristics are similar, with the difference that the fluid domain is no longer represented. To make the two hulls solidary, one adds an mesh-cell group connecting the nodes at the clamped support level.

### 3.3 Steps of calculation

The definition of the characteristics of a physical configuration consisting of two coaxial cylindrical hulls for a fluid-structure coupling calculation is given via the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU factor keyword COQUE_COAX.

The fluid-structure coupling resolution for a configuration of the coaxial hull type and the calculation of the modal parameters (reduced frequencies and damping) and modal deformed water at rest are performed with the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU.

### 4.2 Remarks

The results are consistent with what could be expected, as we can see:

- a systematic error of the order of 5%, due to the non-counting of hull thicknesses for the definition of the fluid domain in the reference calculation;
- a residual difference due to differences in Modelling and resolution between the two CALC_MATR_AJOU and CALC_FLUI_STRU operators.

Modes 1 to 6 are modes for which the structure is strongly coupled with the fluid. In practice, these modes correspond to movements of the inner and outer hulls globally in phase opposition. For these modes, the added mass terms are theoretically proportional to $\frac{{\rho}_{f}\pi {R}^{3}}{H}$ , where $R$ denotes the mean radius and $H$ the thickness of the annular space.

For these first six modes, the results provided by CALC_FLUI_STRU lead to lower eigenfrequencies than those calculated by CALC_MATR_AJOU. Indeed, since $H$ is lower, the added mass terms are larger.

Modes 7 to 12 are modes for which the structure is weakly coupled with the fluid. In practice, these modes correspond to movements of the inner and outer hulls almost in phase. Thus, the added mass terms are theoretically proportional to the mass of water driven, i.e. ${\rho}_{f}\pi RH$.

In this case, it is normal that the results provided by CALC_FLUI_STRU lead to higher eigenfrequencies than those calculated by CALC_MATR_AJOU. Indeed, since $H$ is lower, the added mass terms are smaller.

## 5 Summary of the results

The comparison of the calculated water-at-rest frequencies with CALC_FLUI_STRU and CALC_MATR_AJOU operators is satisfactory. The differences encountered between these two operators is explained by the fact that they use different modelling.
