# v6.04.508 SSNV508 – Flat-stressed block with interface, traction and side compression, for quadratic elements X-FEM

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the distortion of an interface introduced into a very thin rectangular parallelepipedic plate in an X-FEM frame. The structure is pressed in traction and subjected to a linear lateral pressure. The interface is represented by a level set flat and horizontal cutting elements or coinciding with their edges. It involves elements X-FEM [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12) $\text{P2}$ (movement) that have degrees of freedom displacement in each node. The problem is treated in 2D and 3D.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 2D geometry

The structure is a rectangle consisting of two plates of the same material, separated by an interface.

The dimensions of the plate, on which the pressures are applied, are:

${L}_{X}=\mathrm{2m}$ , ${L}_{Y}=1,8m$

The second plate has the following dimensions:

${L}_{X}=\mathrm{2m}$ , ${L}_{Y}’=1,2m$

The position of the reference points is:

|  | $x$ | $y$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | -1 | 0 |
| $B$ | 1 | 0 |
| $C$ | 1 | 1,8 |
| $D$ | -1 | 1,8 |
| $O$ | 0 | 0 |

The structure is a rectangular parallelepiped consisting of two plates of the same material, separated by an interface.

The dimensions of the top plate, on which the pressures are applied, are:

${L}_{X}=\mathrm{2m}$ , ${L}_{Y}=1,8m$ , ${L}_{Z}=0,01m$

The second plate below has the following dimensions:

${L}_{X}=\mathrm{2m}$ , ${L}_{Y}’=1,2m$ , ${L}_{Z}=0,01m$

### 1.3 Material properties

Poisson's ratio: $0.2$

Young's modulus: $1.{10}^{10}N/{m}^{2}$

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

The lower plate $(y<0)$ is blocked by an clamped support of its lower face.

The $\mathit{ABCD}$ plane is blocked in the ${e}_{z}$ direction.

In the 3D case, the $(x=0)$ plane is blocked in the ${e}_{x}$ direction, and in the 2D case, the $(x=0)$ line is blocked in the ${e}_{x}$ direction.

The $(y>0)$ top plate is subjected to a horizontal distributed pressure acting on the $P=\pm (-{5.10}^{4}y+{1.10}^{5})N/{m}^{2}$ side faces (according to the compression principle). It is applied a quadratic traction displacement on the top face $d=-2,5{.10}^{-6}{x}^{2}+{1.10}^{-5}m$ .

## 2 Reference solution: flat stress analysis

### 2.1 Solution 2D

By placing itself in the Cartesian $(x,y)$ marker, the movement of all point $M(x,y)$ from the top plate is written:

$u(x,y)={u}_{x}(x,y)\overrightarrow{{e}_{x}}+{u}_{y}(x,y)\overrightarrow{{e}_{y}}$ **éq 2-1**

**Notes**:

- *The top and bottom plates are separated by the fact that the interface completely separates the plate in two. The bottom plates being clamped at its base, it is therefore completely motionless and the analytical study is carried out only on the top plates.*

The displacement components in the $\lbrace  1,x,y,{x}^{2},{y}^{2},xy,{x}^{2}y,{xy}^{2}\rbrace$ base are broken down:

${u}_{x}(x,y)={I}_{1}+{I}_{2}x+{I}_{3}y+{I}_{4}{x}^{2}+{I}_{5}{y}^{2}+{I}_{6}xy+{I}_{7}{x}^{2}y+{I}_{8}x{y}^{2}$ **éq 2-2**

${u}_{y}(x,y)={J}_{1}+{J}_{2}x+{J}_{3}y+{J}_{4}{x}^{2}+{J}_{5}{y}^{2}+{J}_{6}xy+{J}_{7}{x}^{2}y+{J}_{8}x{y}^{2}$ **éq 2-3**

The problem has a geometrical and loading symmetry with respect to the axis of the ordered ones. This implies:

${I}_{1}={I}_{3}={I}_{4}={I}_{5}={I}_{7}={J}_{2}={J}_{6}={J}_{8}=0$ **éq 2-4**

The local equilibrium equations expressed in the Cartesian benchmark give:

${I}_{8}={J}_{7}=0$ **éq 2-5**

${I}_{6}=-\frac{2(1-\nu )}{1+\nu }{J}_{4}-\frac{4}{1+\nu }{J}_{5}$ **éq 2-6**

Applying the Dirichlet boundary conditions of the upper face $d={d}_{2}{x}^{2}+{d}_{0}$ , we derive from this:

${J}_{4}={d}_{2}=-2,5{.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-7**

${J}_{1}+{J}_{3}{L}_{y}+{J}_{5}{L}_{y}^{2}={d}_{0}$ **éq 2-8**

Applying the limit conditions of Neumann of the $P={p}_{1}y+{p}_{0}$ side edges on the stresses arising from the generalised Hooke law:

${J}_{5}=-\frac{{d}_{2}}{2+\nu }+\frac{{p}_{1}{(1+\nu )}^{2}}{2E(2+\nu )}=-0,5{.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-9**

${I}_{6}=-\frac{{\mathrm{2d}}_{2}(1-\nu )}{1+\nu }{p}_{0}-\frac{{\mathrm{4J}}_{5}}{1+\nu }=-\frac{5}{3}{.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-10**

${I}_{2}=-\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}{p}_{0}-\nu {J}_{3}$ **éq 2-11**

The interface is a free edge, that is, the point all-constrained vector of this surface in the normal direction outside the structure is zero:

${J}_{3}=\frac{\nu {p}_{0}}{E}={2.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-12**

${I}_{2}=\frac{-{p}_{0}}{E}=-{1.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-13**

Therefore, by combining the results and expressions obtained, we draw ${J}_{1}$:

${J}_{1}={d}_{0}-{J}_{3}{L}_{y}-{J}_{5}{L}_{y}^{2}=8,02{.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-14**

The solution is as follows:

${u}_{x}(x,y)=-{1.10}^{-6}(\mathrm{10x}+\frac{5}{3}xy)$ **éq 2-15**

${u}_{y}(x,y)={1.10}^{-6}(8,02+\mathrm{2y}-2,5{x}^{2}-0,5{y}^{2})$ **éq 2-16**

On the interface the following result is given:

${u}_{x}(x,y=0)=-{1.10}^{-5}x$ **éq 2-17**

${u}_{y}(x,y=0)={1.10}^{-6}(8,02-2,5{x}^{2})$ **éq 2-18**

### 2.2 3D solution

> The 3D stress field does not vary in the $z$ direction, which implies that the deformations are also independent of the 3D stress field. The problem can then be reduced to the 2D problem (plane $\mathit{ABCD}$) for the resolution of stresses and deformations.

In the 3D case, the solution on ${u}_{x}$ and ${u}_{y}$ has the following form:

${u}_{x}(x,y,z)={1.10}^{-6}(-\mathrm{10x}-\frac{5}{3}xy+{h}_{x}(z))$ **éq 2-19**

${u}_{y}(x,y,z)={1.10}^{-6}(8,02+\mathrm{2y}-2,5{x}^{2}+{h}_{y}(z))$ **éq 2-20**

> In addition, the deformation on ${e}_{z}$ is written:

${\epsilon}_{zz}(x,y,z)=-\frac{\nu}{1-\nu }(\frac{\partial {u}_{x}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial {u}_{y}}{\partial y})={1.10}^{-6}(2+\frac{2}{3}y)$ **éq 2-21**

With:

${\epsilon}_{zz}(x,y,z)=\frac{\partial {u}_{z}(x,y,z)}{\partial z}$ **éq 2-22**

Thus, by combining the results and expressions obtained, we obtain:

${u}_{z}(x,y,z)={1.10}^{-6}[(2+\frac{2}{3}y)z+g(x,y)]$ **éq 2-23**

According to ${\epsilon}_{xz}=0$ and ${\epsilon}_{yz}=0$, we get:

${h}_{x}(z)={C}_{1}z+{C}_{0}$ **éq 2-24**

${h}_{y}(z)=-\frac{{z}^{2}}{3}+{C}_{3}z+{C}_{5}$ **éq 2-25**

$g(x,y)=-{C}_{\mathrm{1x}}-{C}_{3}y+{C}_{4}$ **éq 2-26**

The $\mathit{ABCD}$ plane is blocked on the ${e}_{z}$ direction, we get:

${u}_{z}(x,y,z=0)=0$ **éq 2-27**

This implies: ${C}_{1}={C}_{3}={C}_{4}=0$ .

The $(x=0)$ plane is blocked on the ${e}_{x}$ direction, we get:

${u}_{x}(x=0,y,z)=0$ **éq 2-28**

This implies: ${C}_{0}=0$ .

In addition, the displacement imposed on the upper surface leads to: ${C}_{5}=0$ .

Finally, we get:

${u}_{x}(x,y,z)=-(\mathrm{10x}+\frac{5}{3}xy){.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-29** ${u}_{y}(x,y,z)=(8,02+\mathrm{2y}-2,5{x}^{2}-0,5{y}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}{z}^{2}){.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-30**

${u}_{z}(x,y,z)=(2+\frac{2}{3}y){\mathit{z.10}}^{-6}$ **éq 2-31**

On the interface the following result is given:

${u}_{x}(x,y=0,z)=-{1.10}^{-5}x$ **éq 2-32**

${u}_{y}(x,y=0,z)=(8,02-2,5{x}^{2}-\frac{1}{3}{z}^{2}){.10}^{-6}$ **éq 2-33**

${u}_{z}(x,y=0,z)={2.10}^{-6}z$ **éq 2-34**

## 8 Modelling Summary of the results

The objectives of this test are met.

- The aim was to demonstrate the feasibility of considering the Heaviside enrichment of conventional form functions on quadratic elements. Only the case of a crack completely crossing the structure was considered (interface).
- The method is validated in $\mathrm{2D}$ , $\mathrm{P2}$ on a mesh quadrangle.
- The method is validated in $\mathrm{3D}$ , $\mathrm{P2}$ on a rectangular parallelepipedic mesh.
- A better solution is obtained with C_PLAN modelling than 3D modelling.
