# v2.01.106 SDLD106 – Spring mass system with harmonic oscillation damping

**Summary**

This test case validates the elemental matrix of a discrete element. The proper frequency of an oscillator to an degree of freedom (mass – spring system), the transient response due to sinusoidal excitation and the forced response due to harmonic excitation is calculated.

The reference results are obtained analytically.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The diagram of the system is shown in the following figure:

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.106/10003C68000069D500005B2800C1BDD7955DD14B.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The Material properties are:

stiffness            $K:4{\pi}^{2}N/m$

mass $M:100\mathit{kg}$

$C=0.4\pi \mathit{Ns}/m$ damping

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The spring is clamped at one of its ends and subjected to $F$ force at the free end. The point mass can only move according to the $x$ direction.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

At the initial moment, the mass is still and is in its equilibrium position.

$x(0)=0m$

$v(0)=0m/s$

## 2 reference solution

The purpose is to calculate the proper frequency of the oscillator, the response due to sinusoidal excitation, and the response due to harmonic excitation.

### 2.1 Calculation of the eigenfrequency

For the validation of the calculation of the eigenfrequency, we consider the system without damping. Thus, the displacement of the free end of the spring is governed by the following relationship:

$M\ddot{x}+Kx=F$                                             (1)

The frequency of this oscillator is:

${f}_{0}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\frac{K}{M}}=\frac{{\omega}_{0}}{2\pi }$ (${f}_{0}$: eigenfrequency, ${\omega}_{0}$: clean pulse)

### 2.2 Calculation of the transitional response

For the validation of the calculation of the transient response, we consider the system without damping.

With the initial condition $x(0)=0$ and $\dot{x}(0)=0$ , if a sinusoidal force $F(t)=F\sin(\Omega t)$ is applied to the free end of the spring, the differential equation solution (1) is:

$x(t)=\frac{F(\sin\Omega t-\frac{\Omega}{{\omega}_{0}}\sin{\omega}_{0}t)}{M({\omega}_{0}^{2}-{\Omega}^{2})}$

For this response calculation to sinusoidal excitation, we chose: $\Omega =1\mathit{rd}/s$ and $F=1N$ .

### 2.3 Calculation of the harmonic response

The purpose of this calculation is to calculate the amortized oscillator response due to harmonic excitation.

The free end of the spring is moved by the following relationship:

$M\ddot{x}+C\dot{x}+Kx=F$                                     (2)

Applying a sinusoidal force $F(t)=F\sin(\Omega t)$ , at the free end of the spring, and adopting the complex notation, obtains the forced response: $\stackrel{ˆ}{x}(\Omega )=\frac{F}{K-{\Omega}^{2}M+jC}$

For this calculation of the harmonic forced response, we chose: $0.5{\omega}_{0}\le \Omega \le 1.5{\omega}_{0}$
