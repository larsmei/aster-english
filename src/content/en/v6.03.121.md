# v6.03.121 SSNP121 – Integration of contact terms in 2D and 3D

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a quasi-static analysis of a mechanical problem with friction-free contact. Here, we are particularly interested in the integration of contact terms ( *patch-test*). The aim is to study two identical blocks, which are gridded differently and are subject to symmetrically imposed displacements.

This test consists of six modellings in $\mathrm{2D}$ (elements linear SEG2):

- Modelling A: METHODE=‘CONTINUE'. The sub-elements integration method also proposed by Bathe [bib1] is used with three sub-elements,
- Modelling B: METHODE=‘CONTRAINTE
- Modelling C: METHODE=‘PENALISATION
- Modelling G: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. A variant of Modelling A where initial activation of contact status is tested.
- Modelling L: METHODE=‘GCP
- Modelling P: METHODE=‘CONTINUE

$\mathrm{2D}$ Modelling (elements linear SEG2 vis-à-vis elements quadratic SEG3):

- Modelling H: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. Contact surfaces are composed of d-elements SEG2 vis-à-vis d-elements SEG3,

Six modellings in $\mathrm{3D}$ (elements quadratic):

- Modelling D: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. Contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements QUAD8 vis-à-vis d-elements QUAD8,
- Modelling E: METHODE=‘CONTINUE
- Modelling F: METHODE=‘CONTINUE'. The contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements TRIA6 vis-à-vis d-elements QUAD8.
- Q: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. Contact surfaces are composed of d-elements QUAD9 vis-à-vis d-elements QUAD9, mesh cells $\mathrm{3D}$ are HEXA27,
- Modelling R: METHODE=‘CONTINUE'. The contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements TRIA6 vis-à-vis d-elements TRIA6, the mesh cells $\mathrm{3D}$ are PENTA18,

and four modellings in $\mathrm{3D}$ (elements linear vis-à-vis quadratic elements):

- Modelling I: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. Contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements QUAD4 vis-à-vis d-elements QUAD8,
- Modelling J: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. The contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements TRIA3 vis-à-vis d-elements TRIA6,
- Modelling K: METHODE=‘CONTINUE. Contact surfaces are composed of d-d-elements TRIA3 vis-à-vis d-elements QUAD8.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.121/10000000000004D60000031ECE154F1D38C1AF9B.png)

Length $a=2m$ .

Width $b=1m$ .

$O$ point mid segment $\mathit{AB}$ (mark origin).

### 1.2 Material properties

**Schedules 1 and 2:**

Poisson's ratio: $0.0$

Young's modulus: $2.{10}^{6}N/{m}^{2}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Plate 1 is blocked:

- On $\mathit{HG}$ $\mathit{DX}=0$ and $\mathit{DY}=0$ .

Plate 2 is subject to a displacement imposed:

- On $\mathit{CD}$: $\mathit{DY}={U}_{0}=–0.1m$ and $\mathit{DX}=0$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution, analytic, can be deduced from a very simple calculation. The Poisson's ratio being zero, the uniaxial deformation is written $\varepsilon ={U}_{0}/\mathit{HD}=–0.1/2.$ The pressure is then $E\varepsilon =–{10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$.

### 2.2 Baseline

The contact pressure is constant and equal to $–{10}^{5}\mathit{Pa}$ over the entire contact surface. Similarly, the vertical displacement (according to $y$) is constant on the contact surface and equal to ${U}_{0}/2=–0.05\text{m}$.

### 2.3 Bibliographic reference

  1. EL-ABBASI and K.J. BATHE: " *Stability and Patch Test Performance of Contact Discretion and a New Solution Algorithm* ", Computers & Structures, 79, 1473-1486, 2001

## 19 Summary of the results

This very simple example (known as *patch-test*) is designed to test a new technique for integrating contact terms based on sub-elements subdivision available only for the formulation ‘CONTINUE'. This technique aims to reduce the magnitude of contact pressure. In the case studied here, the pressure is constant over the entire contact surface (note that the Poisson's ratio is zero).

Thus, with the discrete formulations ‘CONTRAINTE' and ‘PENALISATION', the solution has non-physical oscillations of the order of 6-7 %. Using the ‘CONTINUE' method, with an adapted integration scheme, oscillations disappear almost completely and the results obtained are very close to the reference solution (<0.5%).
