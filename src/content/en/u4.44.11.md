# u4.44.11 Operator DEFI_CONTACT

## 1 Purpose

Assign unilateral contact conditions and friction conditions in mechanics, or unilateral conditions on the degrees of freedom. One details:

- the control of the pairing for the facing boundaries;
- the physical and numerical parameters, useful for the various formulations available.

## 3 Principles

This command makes it possible to describe the zones subjected to unilateral contact conditions with or without friction. The description is made at two levels:

- The global parameters, such as the choice of the formulation or the parameters for controlling the non-linear solution algorithm (fixed-point or Newton loops, parameters specific to the solver);
- The local parameters specific to each contact zone.

The concept produced by `DEFI_CONTACT` is then supplied as a parameter in the simple keyword `CONTACT` of the operators [STAT_NON_LINE](doc:u4.51.03) and [DYNA_NON_LINE](doc:u4.53.01). Each zone comprises two surfaces which may come into contact, and which are described by supplying the mesh-cell groups which constitute them. For parallelism, one is advised to use the centralised partitioning method if one uses the discrete formulation and/or if one uses [DYNA_NON_LINE](doc:u4.53.01) (because of the time schemes).

The sets of mesh cells potentially in contact are skin mesh cells: surface and line mesh cells in dimension 3 (QUAD9, QUAD8, QUAD4, TRIA7, TRIA6, TRIA3, SEG3, SEG2), line and point mesh cells in dimension 2 (SEG3, SEG2 and POI1).

In dimension 2, mesh cells of type POI1 must necessarily be on the slave surface. They are not usable with the CONTINUE formulation.

In dimension 3, mesh cells of type SEG2 or SEG3 must necessarily be on the slave surface. This implies that the definition of a contact between two beam elements is not possible in 3D.

- For the `DISCRETE` formulation, in dimension 3, the treatment of contact with quadratic edge mesh cells of type QUAD8 requires linking the mid-side nodes to the vertex nodes in order to have correct results. This operation is carried out automatically in the code. Nevertheless, for 3D continuum analyses the use of HEXA27 or PENTA18 elements (with QUAD9 faces) is strongly advised. If, however, the use of HEXA20 elements proves necessary, the linear relations written automatically on this occasion may be liable to conflict with boundary conditions (in particular of symmetry); this is why it may be necessary to impose the boundary conditions only on the vertex nodes of the QUAD8 mesh cells concerned (one may use the operator `DEFI_GROUP` to create the ad-hoc node group).
- For the `CONTINUE` formulation, in dimension 3, the use of quadratic edge mesh cells of type QUAD8 or curved TRIA6 (that is, whose mid-side nodes are not aligned with the vertex nodes) may lead to violations of the contact law. More precisely, the contact is then solved on average on each element. In the presence of contact one may therefore observe gaps that are both slightly positive and negative, which may disturb the results near the contact zone, or the restart analyses with an initial state. For this reason, it is advised to use HEXA27 or PENTA18 elements (with QUAD9 faces), or else linear elements. One can transform into HEXA27 or PENTA18 a mesh made up of HEXA20 or PENTA15 mesh cells by means of the operator [CREA_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.02).

**Notes**

The structures studied may undergo large sliding relative to one another. There exist three main types of formulations:

1. The discrete formulations (see [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50)), which correspond to the solution of the discretised unilateral problem (displacement unknowns and nodal forces). This formulation is accessible *via* FORMULATION='DISCRETE'. It is usable with or without Coulomb friction.

1. The continuous formulation (see [R5.03.52](doc:r5.03.52)), which is an augmented-Lagrangian method written by means of the mixed variational statement “displacements / contact pressure / friction multipliers”. This formulation is accessible *via* FORMULATION='CONTINUE'. It is usable with or without Coulomb friction.
2. The unilateral-relation type of formulation (FORMULATION='LIAISON_UNIL'). Close to the discrete formulations, it relies on an algorithm used in contact in order to impose boundary conditions of inequality type on any degree of freedom. This formulation is treated separately in the last part of this document (*cf.* § 3.8).

Before performing an analysis with contact, it is **indispensable** to have read the user's guide for contact [U2.04.04](doc:u2.04.04), which explains by means of examples the role of most of the keywords described below and describes methodologies for different types of studies taking contact into account.

All the methods of solution of contact-friction known as “mesh-based” formulations (discrete formulations [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50) or continuous formulation [R5.03.52](doc:r5.03.52)) rest on a two-stage strategy:

- a pairing operation, which consists in finding which mesh cells and which nodes are potentially in a contact situation;
- a solution operation proper, which consists in solving the unilateral contact problem with or without friction.

Paragraphs 3.1 and 3.2 concentrate on the pairing phase, while paragraphs 3.4 and 3.5 deal with the methods of solution of the problem.

Finally, paragraph 3.6 describes the field dedicated to post-processing, produced by an analysis with contact.

The formulation 'LIAISON_UNIL' is dealt with in paragraph 3.8.

### 3.1 Control of the pairing (mesh-based methods excluding LAC)

One uses the factor keyword `ZONE`. The keywords of this paragraph are valid for the mesh-based formulations (`DISCRETE` and `CONTINUE`), except for the LAC method (see § 3.3).

#### 3.1.1 Operand APPARIEMENT

In the case of the discrete formulations, the pairing may be node-facet (``MAIT_ESCL’`) or nodal (``NODAL’`). For nodal pairing one writes a non-penetration relation between a master node and a slave node, whereas for node-facet pairing one writes this relation between a slave node and its projection onto the nearest master mesh cell (see [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50) for details on the pairing algorithm).

Nodal pairing is reserved for compatible meshes and is available only in the DISCRETE formulation. Master-slave pairing is the only method which makes it possible to take large sliding into account in a precise way.

#### 3.1.2 Operands GROUP_MA_MAIT/GROUP_MA_ESCL

For the mesh-based formulations the user supplies the list of the potential contact mesh cells of the master surface (GROUP_MA_MAIT) and of the slave surface (GROUP_MA_ESCL). These mesh cells must be surface or line mesh cells in dimension 3 (QUAD9, QUAD8, QUAD4, TRIA7, TRIA6, TRIA3, SEG3, SEG2), line or point mesh cells in dimension 2 (SEG3, SEG2 and POI1).

- It is important to check that the connectivity of these mesh cells is such that the normal to the structure is outward (to do this, use [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04)).
- The intersection between the slave surface and the master surface of one and the same zone must be disjoint, or else the common nodes must be excluded
- The slave surfaces must necessarily be pairwise disjoint in the continuous formulation.

**Caution**

In all that follows, one will use the master-slave concept: the nodes of the slave surface cannot “penetrate” the facets (or the nodes) of the master surface. In the case of pairing of type MAIT_ESCL, the master surface is that defined by GROUP_MA_MAIT. In the case of pairing of type NODAL (available only for the discrete formulations), the master surface is that which must comprise the largest number of nodes. If this is not the case, the user is stopped by an error message and invited to interchange the two surfaces.

- It is impossible to mix purely two-dimensional modellings (plane stresses C_PLAN, plane strains D_PLAN and axisymmetric AXIS) with three-dimensional modellings. The master and slave surfaces must be of the same nature (2D/2D or 3D/3D). An error message stops the user in the contrary case. Note that a beam, a plate or a shell is of dimension 3 and that it is therefore possible to perform beam/3D or beam/plate contact.
- In 3D, it is not possible to define contact zones with a slave surface of type SEG2 or SEG3, which excludes the case of a contact between two beam or cable elements.

**Notes**

#### 3.1.3 Operands SANS_GROUP_NO/SANS_GROUP_MA

The operands `SANS_GROUP_NO` and `SANS_GROUP_MA` make it possible to exclude nodes from the slave surfaces, an operation which is recommended when the latter are subjected to boundary conditions in the expected direction of the contact (clamping, for example).

#### 3.1.4 Operands TYPE_PROJECTION/DIRE_APPA

The choice of the master mesh cell paired with a slave node is made by an operation of minimisation of the distance between this node and the master mesh cells. With the default option, TYPE_PROJECTION='ORTHOGONALE', the algorithm used for this orthogonal projection is a classical Newton algorithm.

In very rare cases this algorithm may fail, for example if the projection onto a mesh cell is not unique, which may happen if the master mesh cell is convex. In this case, the user may supply a fixed pairing direction to be used in the algorithm *via* the option `TYPE_PROJECTION='FIXE'`, the direction then being given by a vector in `DIRE_APPA`.

#### 3.1.5 Operands DIST_APPA and TOLE_PROJ_EXT

During the pairing of the current slave node, it is possible to restrict the search domain among the master mesh cells by using the keyword DIST_APPA. If DIST_APPA=-1 (default value), then all the master mesh cells given in the contact zone are liable to be paired with the slave node. If DIST_APPA=val with val a positive real, then only the master mesh cells located, in 3D in the sphere, in 2D in the circle of radius val centred on the slave node, may be paired.

In certain situations, it may be necessary to extend fictitiously the mesh cells of the master surface. Take the case of contact in 2D (the contact surfaces are therefore segments); one places oneself on the edge of the contact surface. The projection of a slave node falls outside the master surface.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.11/Cadre1.gif)

*Projection of a slave node outside a master mesh cell*

One solution then consists in not pairing the node, which amounts to excluding it from the contact:

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.11/Cadre2.gif)

*Exclusion of a slave node*

However, this solution does not take the limit cases into account and may cause untimely interpenetrations if the mesh is not “optimal” (that is, not fine enough, which is difficult to ensure within the framework of large transformations). *Conversely*, one obviously cannot snap back all the nodes projecting outside the master surface.

One has therefore opted for an intermediate solution, by limiting the extension of the master surface liable to cause a snapping back.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.11/Cadre3.gif)

*Operation of `TOLE_PROJ_EXT`*

The size of the snapping-back zone is fixed by the keyword `TOLE_PROJ_EXT`, which takes as argument the value, relative to the reference element, of the extension of the master mesh cell. By default, this value is fixed at 0.50. For example in 2D, this means that any slave node projecting more than 25% to the right or to the left of the length of the master mesh cell will not be snapped back (in the case of a segment, the reference element is of length 2). To forbid snapping back completely, it is sufficient to set `TOLE_PROJ_EXT` negative. This operator is valid in 2D and in 3D (in the latter case, it is the extension of a surface contact mesh cell).

Note
* It is dangerous to deactivate snapping back completely. Apart from the edges of the contact surfaces, there are indeed situations in which points do not project into the interior of any master mesh cell (this is the case for any convex master surface). If all the contact nodes come to be excluded, an alarm is issued. One must then check that this situation is indeed the one expected by the user.

#### 3.1.6 Choice of the normals

##### 3.1.6.1 Type of normal (NORMALE)

It is possible to choose the type of the normal used to write the non-interpenetration conditions:

- the outward normal to the master mesh cell (NORMALE='MAIT', by default);
- the inward normal to the slave mesh cell (NORMALE='ESCL');
- an average between the master and slave normals (NORMALE='MAIT_ESCL').

##### 3.1.6.2 Determination of the master or slave normals (VECT_MAIT/VECT_ESCL)

The determination of the normal (on the slave mesh cell or on the master mesh cell) can be done in several ways:

- automatically: the normal is then computed by the use of the shape functions of the element; this is the option 'AUTO';
- fixed and given directly by the user; this is the option 'FIXE'. One then enters the normal with the keyword MAIT_FIXE or ESCL_FIXE;
- variable and given indirectly by the tangent; this is the option VECT_Y. The user then gives the direction of the **second tangent vector** by the keyword MAIT_VECT_Y or ESCL_VECT_Y. The code then reconstructs the normal from the vector product of the first tangent of the mesh cell and of the vector supplied by the user.

A fixed direction (option VECT_*='FIXE') is necessary if one must evaluate the normal on a mesh cell of type POI1.

A variable direction given by the second tangent (VECT_MAIT='VECT_Y') is used in particular in the case of beams which deform only in a plane. In this case, the use of the option of type VECT_Y makes it possible to update the normal continuously while the beam deforms:

$\vec{N}=\vec{T}\wedge \mathit{VECT}\text{\_}Y$

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.11/1000000000000469000001A44B35F96EC2325206.png)

*Determination of the normal by VECT_Y*

In the example above, T1=(1,0,0) and T2=(-1,0,0), with VECT_Y=(0,1,0), one obtains the desired normal for each beam: N1=(0,0,1) and N2=(0,0,-1).

The orientation of each beam (that is, the vectors T1 and T2) is given by means of the keyword `ORIE_LIGNE` of the operator [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04).

##### 3.1.6.3 Smoothing of the normals (LISSAGE)

The operand LISSAGE makes it possible to smooth the normals to the contact surfaces involved in the computation of the contact matrix. One will denote by $Q$ any node of the contact surfaces (master or slave), by $P$ a node of the slave surface and by $M$ the master node obtained by projection of the node $P$.

The smoothing is done in two stages:

- the first stage of the smoothing consists in performing an average at the node $Q$ of the normals to the mesh cells which contain $Q$;
- the second stage consists in interpolating the normal at $P$ or $M$ from the normals at $Q$ and the shape functions associated with the mesh cell containing $P$ or $M$.

The smoothing takes into account the normal options decided by the keywords VECT_MAIT and VECT_ESCL. Caution: this parameter is global and not zoned.

#### 3.1.7 Modification of the gap

The gap is always computed as being the minimum distance between the slave node and the projection onto the nearest master mesh cell, modulo the options for the choice of this normal. It is however possible to define “hard-set” gap values, for example in order to simulate the presence of a hole or of a bump not represented by the mesh, or in order to take into account the geometric characteristics of the structural elements which use the notion of neutral fibre or of mean surface.

##### 3.1.7.1 Operands DIST_MAIT/DIST_ESCL

These operands make it possible to take into account a fictitious unmeshed gap, or else the thickness of the shells for example (by default the contact relations are written between the meshed parts, that is, between the two middle surfaces).

This gap is taken into account on the master surfaces (DIST_MAIT) or slave surfaces (DIST_ESCL). One counts the distance positively in the direction of the outward normal to the structure (cf. [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50)). A negative value therefore makes it possible to “hollow out” a surface virtually (master as well as slave). Conversely, a positive value makes it possible to “thicken” a surface.

The quantities supplied are necessarily functions of the space or time variables ($X,Y,Z,\mathit{INST}$). If the user wishes a fixed value, a constant function must be defined (see [DEFI_CONSTANTE](doc:u4.31.01)).

##### 3.1.7.2 Operands DIST_POUTRE/DIST_COQUE/CARA_ELEM

Similar to the two previous keywords, the keywords DIST_POUTRE/DIST_COQUE make it possible to introduce a fictitious gap which rests on the description of the structural elements in the concept produced by AFFE_CARA_ELEM, which the user has necessarily supplied:

- for the beam elements (modelling POU_*), the keyword DIST_POUTRE states that the code must take into account an additional gap corresponding to the radius of the **circular** cross-section of the beam.
- for the plate or shell elements (modelling DKT or COQUE_3D, for example), the keyword DIST_COQUE states that the code must take into account an additional gap corresponding to the half-thickness about the middle surface of a shell.

Caution: when one uses these keywords, the fictitious gap is added only on the slave surface. If one models a contact between two structural elements, one will therefore also have to use DIST_MAIT.

### 3.2 Control of the pairing specific to the CONTINUE formulation

#### 3.2.1 Operands SANS_GROUP_NO_FR

These keywords make it possible for the user to exclude from the friction treatment slave nodes carrying boundary conditions potentially in conflict with the imposition of the sticking and sliding conditions. The excluded nodes nevertheless continue to satisfy the contact conditions.

In 2D, no other keyword is necessary: the nodes are excluded from friction.

In 3D, if the user has supplied a vector with 3 components under the keyword DIRE_EXCL_FROT, then the direction indicated by the projection of this vector onto the plane tangent to the contact points is excluded. Sticking or sliding will therefore occur only in the perpendicular direction (in the tangent plane).

If the user does not supply the keyword DIRE_EXCL_FROT in 3D, then this amounts to excluding the 2 orthogonal directions of friction and therefore to no longer solving the friction on the eliminated nodes.

- The exclusion of nodes is possible only with the default integration scheme

**Note**

### 3.3 Control specific to the LAC formulation

One uses the factor keyword ZONE. The keywords of this paragraph are valid for the LAC method.

#### 3.3.1 Operands GROUP_MA_MAIT/GROUP_MA_ESCL

For the LAC formulation the user supplies the list of the potential contact mesh cells of the master surface (GROUP_MA_MAIT) and of the slave surface (GROUP_MA_ESCL). The slave surface must previously have been prepared (cut into patches) by the command CREA_MAILLAGE/DECOUPE_LAC.

- It is important to check that the connectivity of these mesh cells is such that the normal to the structure is outward (to do this, use [MODI_MAILLAGE](doc:u4.23.04)).
- The intersection between the slave surface and the master surface of one and the same zone must be disjoint, or else the common nodes must be excluded
- The slave surfaces must necessarily be pairwise disjoint

**Caution**

#### 3.3.2 Operands APPARIEMENT/TYPE_APPA

The LAC method necessarily uses a pairing of “mortar” type, that is, segment/segment (in 2D) or face/face (in 3D). This pairing is much more costly than a pairing of node/segment type used for the other contact methods. One therefore has the choice between two possibilities:

- Use a fast algorithm, but one which risks causing false pairings (it is easy to check this problem visually). One then chooses `TYPE_APPA = 'RAPIDE'`;
- Use a robust algorithm, but one which is much slower. One then chooses TYPE_APPA    = 'ROBUSTE;

One recommends using the “fast” pairing only for simple cases (connected surfaces).

The parameter `RESI_APPA` makes it possible to adjust the degree of precision of the pairing. It is not recommended to alter it; its use requires great precautions and must be reserved for experts

#### 3.3.3 Operand TYPE_JACOBIEN

This operand is used when the kinematics of the problem is of “large transformations” type. For example, if one performs a simulation with the model “GDEF_LOG”, one must use TYPE_JACOBIEN='ACTUALISE'; otherwise the results (in particular the contact pressures) will be slightly wrong.

#### 3.3.4 Operand CONTACT_INIT

The operand CONTACT_INIT makes it possible to fix the contact status at the initial state.

By default, only the relations with zero gap or else interpenetrated are activated by the algorithm (CONTACT_INIT='INTERPENETRE'). It is nevertheless possible to force the algorithm to activate all the relations without exception (CONTACT_INIT='OUI'). The value CONTACT_INIT='INTERPENETRE' is obligatory in the case of a restart of an analysis with an initial state. It is also possible to deactivate any initial contact by CONTACT_INIT='NON'.

The LAC method uses a specific output data structure, `CONT_ELEM`, defined by element.

### 3.4 Choice and control of the global solution algorithm

#### 3.4.1 Control of the geometric non-linearity

Whatever the formulation employed, it is necessary to treat the geometric non-linearity of the contact-friction problem. It is possible either to neglect it (REAC_GEOM='SANS'), or to solve it in an approximate manner (REAC_GEOM='CONTROLE'), or else exactly (REAC_GEOM='AUTOMATIQUE').

To solve this non-linearity, one in general employs an algorithm of “fixed-point” type (ALGO_RESO_GEOM='POINT_FIXE'). The CONTINUE formulation is also capable of treating the geometric non-linearity within the Newton algorithm itself (ALGO_RESO_GEOM='NEWTON', see § 3.4.4.5).

When the geometric non-linearity is solved by a “fixed-point” loop, a certain number of adjustments are possible.

The operand REAC_GEOM indicates on which geometric configuration the contact problem is treated:

- REAC_GEOM=’AUTOMATIQUE’: one updates the geometry automatically, that is, the number of “geometric updating–iterations” cycles until convergence is not fixed in advance but obeys a geometric convergence criterion. This is the default option, advised in order to solve the pairing non-linearity correctly. It ensures that the contact conditions have been imposed on an (initial) configuration which differs by less than resi_geom from the configuration found (1% by default for the DISCRETE or CONTINUE formulations).
- REAC_GEOM=’SANS’: one works on the initial geometry. This option is valid only under the assumption of small perturbations, or when the normal to the contact surfaces does not change in the course of the analysis.
- REAC_GEOM=’CONTROLE’: when the automatic criterion cannot be satisfied, the user must control the geometric updating himself and, for this, must supply the parameter NB_ITER_GEOM. This is the number of geometric-updating cycles which will be performed per load step. Let us place ourselves at a given load step:

  - The value 1 indicates that, at Newton convergence, one updates the geometry and one passes to the following load step.
  - The value 2 indicates that, at Newton convergence, one updates the geometry and one iterates again until convergence before passing to the following load step.
  - The value $n>2$ indicates that one performs $n$ “geometric updating–convergence” cycles before passing to the following load step.

If you have selected REAC_GEOM=’AUTOMATIQUE’, the parameter ITER_GEOM_MAXI is the maximum tolerated number of geometric-updating cycles. If the criterion on RESI_GEOM is not satisfied after iter_geom_maxi cycles, then one stops in error, or else one cuts the time step if the user has requested it. The documentation [U2.04.04](doc:u2.04.04) provides numerous pieces of advice for overcoming these convergence problems (in particular, activate smoothing).

- If the user chooses a controlled updating with $n>1$ and one detects the necessity of a geometric updating, an alarm will be issued. It is up to the user to decide whether the error committed (necessarily greater than 1%) is acceptable or not. There is indeed a risk of a pairing error (a mesh cell has been paired on a configuration which has moved) and therefore of interpenetration.
- In the case where one solves on the initial geometry or else with a single geometric-updating cycle, there is no warning, because one cannot compute an error. The user must therefore check the validity of the choice in post-processing.
- The value of the geometric convergence criterion is displayed in the table of Newton iterations (column CONTACT CRITERE VALEUR)

**Notes**

#### 3.4.2 Friction

To assign Coulomb friction to the contact zones, use the keyword FROTTEMENT='COULOMB'. This keyword is **global** (valid for all the zones), but it is possible to assign friction zone by zone by adjusting the value of the Coulomb coefficient (one sets this coefficient to 0 when one does not wish friction).

#### 3.4.3 Formulation DISCRETE

##### 3.4.3.1 Choice of the algorithms

To select the type of algorithm in the DISCRETE formulation, one uses the keywords ALGO_CONT and ALGO_FROT. ALGO_FROT is modifiable only if FROTTEMENT='COULOMB'. Not all combinations are possible:

|  | ALGO_FROT='PENALISATION' |
| --- | --- |
| ALGO_CONT='CONTRAINTE' | **Impossible** |
| ALGO_CONT='GCP' | **Impossible** |
| ALGO_CONT='PENALISATION' | **OK** |

The different solution methods are:

- 'CONTRAINTE': default algorithm which treats the exact unilateral contact problem with the active-constraint method (see [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50)). **No friction possible**.
- 'GCP': this is an iterative method close to the ’CONTRAINTE’ method, but which is particularly suited to cases in which the number of contact relations is very high. **No friction possible**.
- 'PENALISATION': the penalised method makes it possible to treat contact problems in an approximate manner, with or without friction, in 2D or 3D.

Although the different algorithms are given by zone, it is not possible to mix several methods in the same DEFI_CONTACT.

##### 3.4.3.2 Dualised methods (CONTRAINTE)

The CONTRAINTE method makes it possible to solve contact problems without friction in an exact manner (no interpenetration is tolerated). It is particularly fast and robust; its convergence is proved. The solution of the system of inequalities resulting from the contact, being based on the explicit construction of a Schur complement, has a use limited to a few hundred contact relations. Beyond that, the memory and computation costs become too large.

`NB_RESOL` is the number of simultaneous solutions for the construction of the Schur complement. Performing several simultaneous solutions makes it possible to handle matrices by blocks. Increasing nb_resol therefore accelerates the construction of the Schur complement, but causes a loss of memory space. nb_resol=10 is a good compromise. This parameter is to be reserved for **experts**.

`STOP_SINGULIER` makes it possible to deactivate the fatal error appearing if the Schur complement is singular following a significant loss of decimals in the factorisation (8 decimals by default). One supplies for this `STOP_SINGULIER=`NON’`. This parameter is to be reserved for **experts**.

One can control the loop on the statuses by giving a multiplying coefficient, `ITER_CONT_MULT`: the maximum number of iterations on the contact status will be equal to the product of iter_cont_mult by the number of slave nodes.

If the analysis stops because the maximum number of contact iterations is exceeded, one can then try to refine the mesh, subdivide the time step or, as a last resort, increase the value of ITER_CONT_MULT.

Note: for the CONTRAINTE method, convergence being proved, this coefficient is fixed hard-coded at 2.

##### 3.4.3.3 Method GLISSIERE

This option is available only for the `CONTRAINTE` method. It makes it possible to activate the bilateral contact mode, also called “slide”, in which two surfaces which are in contact remain “stuck” (that is, with a zero gap) whatever the evolution of the loading. It allows large relative sliding.

Bilateral contact is activated only once the surfaces are effectively in contact (one does not stick *a priori* two separated surfaces if the loading does not imply it).

The operand `ALARME_JEU` makes it possible to trigger an alarm as soon as the algorithm detects that, without the slide method, there would be separation of the two surfaces. Its value is set by default to 0, which alarms the user as soon as the surfaces ought to have separated without the option activated.

##### 3.4.3.4 Method GCP

This method makes it possible to solve contact problems without friction. It solves, with an adjustable precision (possibly very high), the contact conditions by means of Lagrange multipliers. It is in every respect similar to the ’CONTRAINTE’ method, with the difference that it is totally iterative in nature and therefore very economical in memory. In other words, the additional storage cost linked to taking contact into account is small. This specificity has the effect of making it particularly suited to cases involving a large number of contact relations.

The keyword RESI_ABSO makes it possible to adjust the precision of the solution of the contact inequalities (it is a stopping criterion on the value of the gaps for the iterative algorithm). RESI_ABSO (which must be understood as “absolute residual”) represents the maximum level of interpenetration tolerated for the bodies in contact. From a practical point of view, one will begin by choosing a value of the order of ${10}^{-3}$ times the size of the elements in the neighbourhood of the contact surfaces, then one will decrease this value until the results stabilise.

The maximum number of authorised iterations of the projected conjugate-gradient algorithm can be adjusted with the keyword ITER_GCP_MAXI. By default this number of iterations depends on the size of the problem.

Like any iterative solution method, the 'GCP' method can be accelerated by the use of a preconditioner. Only one is available today: a Dirichlet preconditioner (`PRE_COND='DIRICHLET'`). Its use can in certain cases accelerate the solution and appreciably reduce the solution time. By default no preconditioner is activated (`PRE_COND='SANS'`).

The preconditioning phase is carried out by the solution (itself also iterative) of an auxiliary problem. The keyword `COEF_RESI` makes it possible to trigger this preconditioner only when the residual has decreased sufficiently: more precisely, when the initial residual of the algorithm (that is, the initial interpenetration) has been multiplied by coef_resi (coef_resi is therefore smaller than 1).

`ITER_PRE_MAXI` makes it possible to fix the maximum number of iterations of the preconditioner.

The solution method `GCP` requires a phase called linear search. Two variants are available: admissible or non-admissible. One chooses them with the keyword RECH_LINEAIRE (cf. [R5.03.50]).

##### 3.4.3.5 Method PENALISATION

This method is a method of solution of contact/friction by regularisation. It is not exact, in the sense that there is **always** interpenetration when contact is established. If one uses a discrete formulation with penalisation, one must therefore supply the penalisation coefficient(s) (cf. § 3.5.1.1).

#### 3.4.4 Formulation CONTINUE

##### 3.4.4.1 Choice of the algorithms

The continuous formulation has two types of algorithms for solving the contact-friction non-linearities: a fixed-point algorithm, or else a generalised Newton algorithm. Besides the geometric non-linearity (cf. §), this choice can also be made for the contact non-linearity and the friction non-linearity via the keywords `ALGO_RESO_CONT`, `ALGO_RESO_FROT` and `ALGO_RESO_GEOM` (not all choices are possible):

| ALGO_RESO_CONT | ALGO_RESO_FROT | ALGO_RESO_GEOM |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| POINT_FIXE | POINT_FIXE | POINT_FIXE | **OK** |
| NEWTON | POINT_FIXE | POINT_FIXE | **OK** |
| POINT_FIXE | NEWTON | POINT_FIXE | **OK** |
| NEWTON | NEWTON | POINT_FIXE | **OK (by default)** |
| POINT_FIXE | POINT_FIXE | NEWTON | **Impossible** |
| NEWTON | POINT_FIXE | NEWTON | **OK** |
| POINT_FIXE | NEWTON | NEWTON | **Impossible** |
| NEWTON | NEWTON | NEWTON | **OK** |

When one chooses the generalised Newton method for friction, the tangent matrix produced becomes non-symmetric. The generalised Newton method is much faster and less sensitive to the value of the friction coefficient than the fixed-point method. It may in certain cases prove less robust in the treatment of the geometric non-linearity, which is why it is not activated by default in this case. In the case of the solution of a contact-friction problem in generalised Newton, it is often necessary to increase the number of Newton iterations (fixed by default at 10).

Whatever the solution method used (fixed point or Newton), the results obtained are identical.

The option `MATR_DISTRIBUEE=’OUI’` of the factor keyword SOLVEUR of the non-linear operators is forbidden with contact in the continuous formulation.

##### 3.4.4.2 Contact non-linearity

With the default settings (`ALGO_RESO_CONT='NEWTON'`), there is no parameter to supply for the solution of the contact non-linearity.

When one chooses ALGO_RESO_CONT='POINT_FIXE', one can control the loop on the contact statuses in two ways:

- By giving the **absolute** maximum number of contact iterations, ITER_CONT_TYPE='MAXI' then ITER_CONT_MAXI;
- By giving the **relative** maximum number of contact iterations by a multiplying coefficient, ITER_CONT_TYPE='MULT' then ITER_CONT_MULT; in this case, the number of iterations on the contact status will be equal to the product of iter_cont_mult by the number of slave nodes.

In the fixed-point method, the statuses are changed by blocks and the maximum number of iterations is therefore more logically supplied as an absolute value. If the maximum number of contact iterations is reached, one can then try to refine the mesh, subdivide the time step or, as a last resort, increase the value of ITER_CONT_MAXI/ITER_CONT_MULT.

##### 3.4.4.3 Stabilisation of the contact status with an elastic matrix

The keyword CONT_STAT_ELAS makes it possible to activate the use of the elastic matrix (even if MATRICE = “TANGENTE” under NEWTON in STAT_NON_LINE) during the iterations of the correction phase of the Newton algorithm, until the contact statuses are stabilised for cont_stat_elas iterations. By default, this functionality is not active, cont_stat_elas=0. The use of this functionality may be useful when modelling contact with non-linear constitutive behaviours, and when one observes failures of integration of the constitutive law during the stabilisation of the statuses.

##### 3.4.4.4 Non-linearity of the friction threshold

By default the Coulomb problem is solved by a generalised Newton algorithm (ALGO_RESO_FROT='NEWTON'); the only keyword that one can supply is therefore RESI_FROT, in order to define the stationarity criterion of the threshold (the counterpart of RESI_GEOM, *cf.* 3.4.1). An additional column in the convergence table makes it possible to follow the value of the criterion (column *CONTACT NEWTON GENE CRIT. FROT.*).

When one chooses ALGO_RESO_FROT='POINT_FIXE', the Coulomb problem is solved by a succession of fixed points on the Tresca threshold. To control this loop, the mechanism is the same as for the geometric-updating loop (*cf.* 3.4.1; ITER_FROT_MAXI plays the role of ITER_GEOM_MAXI and RESI_FROT plays the role of RESI_GEOM).

When one chooses ADAPTATION=ADAPT_COEF, one activates the possibility of controlling the friction augmentation coefficient (COEF_FROT) by a method of automatic treatment of cycling (see [R5.03.52](doc:r5.03.52)). This option is to be activated in the case of convergence difficulties if one uses the generalised Newton method, in the case where a large number of cycling occurrences takes place. The number of cycling occurrences in the course of an iteration is indicated in the column *CONTACT INFOS CYCLAGES* of the convergence table.

##### 3.4.4.5 Geometric non-linearity

The parameters of the fixed-point loop on the geometry (default parameter) are given in § 3.4.1.

When one chooses ALGO_RESO_GEOM='NEWTON', one switches to the generalised Newton algorithm. The geometric non-linearity is then taken into account at each Newton iteration: one redoes the pairing and one recomputes the local basis (normal and tangents). In this mode, an additional column appears in the convergence table; it corresponds to the evaluation of the geometric criterion (column *CONTACT NEWTON GENE CRIT. GEOM.*).

It is possible to modify the target value of this criterion, just as in POINT_FIXE mode, by means of the parameter RESI_GEOM. However, its definition in generalised Newton implies that it is generally satisfied before the Newton equilibrium criterion. If one really wishes to control it, one must therefore switch to POINT_FIXE mode.

#### 3.4.5 Method without solution

In the case of the mode without solution (see § 3.5.4), the code issues an alarm as soon as it detects an interpenetration. The parameter STOP_INTERP makes it possible to stop the analysis instead of alarming the user.

### 3.5 Local parameters (zone by zone) for control of the solution

#### 3.5.1 Local parameters (zone by zone) of the DISCRETE formulation

The keywords of this paragraph are valid for the DISCRETE formulation. The parameters are defined by the factor keyword ZONE.

##### 3.5.1.1 Method 'PENALISATION'

E_N gives the penalisation coefficient on the interpenetration for the penalised method. It is homogeneous to a ratio of force to length: it is the stiffness of springs placed between the contact surfaces in order to prevent interpenetration. A value of the order of the largest Young's modulus of the solids in contact, multiplied by a characteristic length, is initially recommended. One will increase the value of the coefficient until stable results are obtained.

Moreover, it is possible to control the interpenetration distances and therefore to refine one's choice of coefficient, which is not the case in the presence of friction, since one does not know a priori which are the sliding zones and the non-sliding zones, whereas in the case of contact one can check that the interpenetration distances are small compared with the characteristic dimensions of the contact zones.

There exists, in the DISCRETE formulation, a mechanism of automatic adaptation of the penalisation coefficient E_N (see the command [DEFI_LIST_INST](doc:u4.34.03)).

E_T gives the penalisation coefficient on the sliding for the penalised method. It is homogeneous to a ratio of force to length. It is necessary only when the contact is active. One must increase the value of this coefficient until stable results are obtained, that is, results which depend on it as little as possible.

##### 3.5.1.2 Parameters specific to friction

The parameter COULOMB makes it possible to set the friction coefficient.

In the case of a penalised formulation, the parameter COEF_MATR_FROT, lying between 0 and 1, makes it possible to moderate the destabilising effect of the negative part of the sliding matrix (which is added to the tangent stiffness, *cf.* [R5.03.50](doc:r5.03.50)). The larger this coefficient, the better the convergence when one is close to equilibrium, and the more difficult the solution far from equilibrium. A value of 0.5 is therefore a good compromise. The default value, fixed at 0, ensures better robustness at the cost of a longer computation time.

#### 3.5.2 Local parameters (zone by zone) of the CONTINUE formulation

The keywords of this paragraph are valid for the CONTINUE formulation. The parameters are defined by zone.

##### 3.5.2.1 Operands CONTACT_INIT/SEUIL_INIT

The operands CONTACT_INIT and SEUIL_INIT make it possible to fix, respectively, the contact status at the initial state and the initial sliding threshold.

By default, only the relations with zero gap or else interpenetrated are activated by the algorithm (CONTACT_INIT='INTERPENETRE'). It is nevertheless possible to force the algorithm to activate all the relations without exception (CONTACT_INIT='OUI'). The value CONTACT_INIT='INTERPENETRE' is obligatory in the case of a restart of an analysis with an initial state. It is also possible to deactivate any initial contact by CONTACT_INIT='NON'.

The default value for detecting interpenetration is given by the following formula:

$$
contInit = ar_{min} \, 1.0 \times 10^{-6}
$$

where $ar_{min}$ is the minimum edge computed on the contact zone.

One can also give an absolute value to this criterion by the keyword DIST_MAXI.

As regards friction, the user may specify, if so wished, the value of the initial friction threshold frotInit by the keyword SEUIL_INIT (if an initial adherent contact is desired, for example). The sliding threshold has the dimension of a surface force density. At a point of the contact surface, there will be sliding if the contact pressure $\lambda$ and the friction coefficient $\mu$ satisfy: $\lambda \ge \mu \times frotInit$. The value chosen will apply to all the points of the contact zone concerned. If no value is supplied, then the default behaviour of SEUIL_INIT is such that:

- if it is a restart of an analysis or an analysis with an initial state, then one automatically reconstructs the initial-threshold field of each contact point by using the values of the component LAGS_C of the field DEPL specified in STAT_NON_LINE/ETAT_INIT,
- otherwise, the initial threshold is set to zero and all the points are potentially sliding.

##### 3.5.2.2 Coefficients of the CONTINUE formulation

The continuous formulation is an augmented Lagrangian formulation. There exists a penalised version of it.

If one chooses ALGO_CONT='STANDARD' and ALGO_FROT='STANDARD', the continuous formulation is then equivalent to a classical augmented Lagrangian formulation, whose augmentation coefficients are given, respectively, by COEF_CONT and COEF_FROT.

If one chooses ALGO_CONT='PENALISATION' and ALGO_FROT='PENALISATION', the formulation is then a penalised Lagrangian. One can then modify the penalisation coefficients COEF_PENA_CONT and COEF_PENA_FROT, but only when one chooses ADAPTATION='NON' or ADAPTATION='CYCLAGE'.

In the case where one wishes to use the automatic penalisation mode ADAPTATION='TOUT' or ADAPTATION='ADAPT_COEF', one no longer concerns oneself with the choice of COEF_PENA_CONT, but only with PENE_MAXI. If PENE_MAXI is not specified, then by default PENE_MAXI is equal to $1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{-2}$ times the smallest edge value in the contact zone. The maximum value of the penetration over all the contact zones is given in the column ''CONTACT PENETRATION''. This indicates the quality of the treatment due to the penalised method.

![Figure](/figures/u4.44.11/1000020100000098000001760640BB14F28AC1DF.png)

One can also use mixed modes, ALGO_CONT='STANDARD' and ALGO_FROT='PENALISATION', and conversely.

The penalised method is described, in the case of the discrete method, in § 3.4.3.5 and § 3.5.1.1; COEF_PENA_CONT corresponds there to E_N and COEF_PENA_FROT to E_T.

##### 3.5.2.3 Operand ADAPTATION

The operand ADAPTATION makes it possible to select a method of treatment of cases of difficult convergence for the contact:

- 'NON': there is no treatment;
- 'ADAPT_COEF': one automates the computation of COEF_PENA_CONT and/or COEF_PENA_FROT;
- 'CYCLAGE': frequent changes of contact status may make convergence difficult.
To avoid these untimely changes of status (one speaks of “cycling”), one modifies the computation of the contact and friction matrices. The contact matrix becomes a convex combination of the matrices of the state (pressure, gap, status) of the cycling contact point at the previous iteration and at the current iteration. This mode is available for ALGO_CONT='STANDARD'/'PENALISATION'/'LAC'. Moreover, in this mode, an automatic and definitive switch between the mode ALGO_CONT='STANDARD' and the mode ALGO_CONT='PENALISATION' may occur if the contact statuses do not converge well. In this case, there will be automatic control of the maximum penetration (keyword PENE_MAXI);
- 'TOUT': one combines the mode 'CYCLAGE' with the mode 'ADAPT_COEF';

##### 3.5.2.4 Operand INTEGRATION

The operand INTEGRATION makes it possible to select a numerical integration method for the contact and friction terms. Several methods are implemented:

- AUTO, to let the code choose the most suitable integration scheme (of trapezium or Simpson type);
- GAUSS, for a Gauss quadrature, with the possibility of choosing the degree of the polynomials that the scheme
integrates exactly;
- SIMPSON, for a Simpson scheme ($1/3$), with the possibility of choosing the number of subdivisions of
the reference element;
- NCOTES, for a Newton-Cotes scheme, with the possibility of choosing the degree of the interpolating polynomials.

The choice of the degree of the polynomials that one integrates exactly, or of the number of subdivisions for the Simpson scheme, is made with the keyword ORDRE_INT.

Automatic integration (the default choice) is the most general and the most effective. The choice of an integration scheme is to be reserved for **experts**.

##### 3.5.2.5 Operand GLISSIERE

Unlike the discrete formulation (see § 3.4.3.3), it is possible to activate this option zone by zone.

#### 3.5.3 Method without solution

When RESOLUTION='NON', one performs a check of the interpenetration of two surfaces without imposing the contact conditions (if there is interpenetration, it will not be corrected).

The code issues an alarm as soon as an interpenetration is detected at the end of a loading step. The global parameter STOP_INTERP (§ 3.4.6) makes it possible to stop the analysis instead of alarming the user.

TOLE_INTERP (zone by zone) sets the value of interpenetration tolerated (homogeneous to a length in the unit of the mesh). The value given is absolute (it has no sign). This method is available in the discrete formulation and in the continuous formulation.

- In the mode RESOLUTION='NON', the pairing is done only once at the end of each time step; the notion of a geometric loop no longer exists if all the zones are in mode RESOLUTION='NON'. In this latter case, the option REAC_GEOM, if it is different from 'SANS', therefore has no meaning.
- The fact that the mode RESOLUTION='NON' detects interpenetrations in one of the contact zones does not imply that the real analysis (with effective checking of the unilateral condition) gives the same results. Indeed, the activation of the unilateral condition will necessarily change the deformation kinematics relative to the mode RESOLUTION='NON'. One must therefore be cautious when one mixes zones with solution of the contact and others without solution.
- The nodes interpenetrated in this mode have the status CONT=3 in the field CONT_NOEU

### 3.6 Field CONT_NOEU

To facilitate post-processing, the field CONT_NOEU is produced systematically at the end of an analysis with contact, and it is available in the result data structure (of type evol_noli). It contains the following components, defined at each slave node:

- CONT: frictional-contact indicator

- -1: the node (or the integration point) is not paired (for example outside the master mesh cell, see TOLE_PROJ_EXT)
- 0: no contact
- 1: adherent contact (only if FROTTEMENT='COULOMB')
- 2: sliding contact
- 3: if there is interpenetration (only in mode RESOLUTION='NON');

- JEU: value of the gap
- RN: norm of the normal contact reaction
- RNX: component along $\mathit{DX}$ of the normal contact reaction
- RNY: component along $\mathit{DY}$ of the normal contact reaction
- RNZ: component along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the normal contact reaction
- GLIX: component along ${t}_{1}$ of the tangential sliding (local frame)
- GLIY: component along ${t}_{2}$ of the tangential sliding (local frame)
- GLI: norm of the tangential sliding
- RTAX: component along $\mathit{DX}$ of the tangential sticking force
- RTAY: component along $\mathit{DY}$ of the tangential sticking force
- RTAZ: component along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the tangential sticking force
- RTGX: component along $\mathit{DX}$ of the tangential sliding force
- RTGY: component along $\mathit{DY}$ of the tangential sliding force
- RTGZ: component along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the tangential sliding force
- RX: component along $\mathit{DX}$ of the frictional contact force (RNX+RTAX+RTGX)
- RY: component along $\mathit{DY}$ of the frictional contact force (RNY+RTAY+RTGY)
- RZ: component along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the frictional contact force (RNZ+RTAZ+RTGZ)
- R: norm of the frictional contact force
- I: impulse of the resultant $R$ of the frictional contact force
- IX: impulse of the component along $\mathit{DX}$ of the frictional contact force
- IY: impulse of the component along $\mathit{DY}$ of the frictional contact force
- IZ: impulse of the component along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the frictional contact force
- PROJ_X: coordinates along $\mathit{DX}$ of the projection of the slave node onto the master mesh cell or the master node
- PROJ_Y: coordinates along $\mathit{DY}$ of the projection of the slave node onto the master mesh cell or the master node
- PROJ_Z: coordinates along $\mathit{DZ}$ of the projection of the slave node onto the master mesh cell or the master node

**Note**

In the CONTINUE formulation this field is produced only with the default integration scheme

It is printed as follows, in the form of a table:

```python
MATABLE=POST_RELEVE_T(
    ACTION=_F(INTITULE='INFOS FROTTEMENT',
              GROUP_NO='ESCLAVE',
              RESULTAT=U,
              INST=10.,
              TOUT_CMP='OUI',
              NOM_CHAM='CONT_NOEU',
              OPERATION='EXTRACTION',),)

IMPR_TABLE(TABLE=MATABLE);
```

It should be noted that the quantities corresponding to the contact-friction reactions are nodal forces (in units of force) in the sense of the finite elements, and not contact pressures. In axisymmetric modelling, just as for the option FORC_NODA or REAC_NODA, one must therefore multiply the values obtained by $2\pi$ in order to obtain the resultant (command [CALC_CHAMP](doc:u4.81.02)). The nodal forces have no meaning pointwise (they depend on the fineness of the mesh); only their resultant can be interpreted.

More generally, in order to have access to the contact pressures, one should refer to the user's guide for contact [U2.04.04](doc:u2.04.04), which describes the ways of recovering them when they are produced by the analysis, or of reconstructing them in the contrary case.

### 3.7 Field CONT_ELEM

For the post-processing of the LAC method, the field CONT_ELEM is produced systematically at the end of an analysis with contact. It is available in the result data structure (of type evol_noli) and contains the following components, defined on each slave mesh cell:

- PRES: value of the mean contact pressure
- JEU: value of the integrated gap (it is negative if the node is not paired, positive or zero otherwise)
- CONT: contact indicator

- -1: the mesh cell is not paired
- 0: no contact
- 1: contact

- COEFSURF: intersection surface. This value, lying between 0 and 1, indicates the portion of the slave mesh cell which is effectively intersected by the master mesh cells.
- PRESCOOR: product of COEFSURF and of PRES

### 3.8 Formulation LIAISON_UNIL

The formulation LIAISON_UNIL is usable to define a unilateral condition (inequality) of nodal type on arbitrary degrees of freedom such that:

$\sum{\alpha}_{i}(t){p}_{i}<r(x,y,z,t)$

with:

- ${p}_{i}$ the value of the degree of freedom of a node (displacement, pressure, temperature, etc.) (§ 3.8.1.3)
- ${\alpha}_{i}(t)$ a real function of time (parameter INST) (§ 3.8.1.4)
- $r(x,y,z,t)$ a real function of time or of space (parameters X, Y, Z, INST) (§ 3.8.1.4)

One will note that the parameter ${\alpha}_{i}$ also makes it possible, if its sign is negative, to reverse the sense of the inequality.

#### 3.8.1 Local parameters (zone by zone) of the formulation LIAISON_UNIL

The keywords of this paragraph are valid for the formulation LIAISON_UNIL. The parameters are defined by zone.

##### 3.8.1.1 Operands GROUP_MA/GROUP_NO

The unilateral condition is expressed on the nodes of the mesh given under the keyword GROUP_NO. One may nevertheless give the mesh cells carrying the nodes by means of the keyword GROUP_MA. Unlike the case of “classical” contact, since the conditions are nodal, it is unnecessary to orient the normals to the skin mesh cells in the case LIAISON_UNIL.

##### 3.8.1.2 Operands SANS_GROUP_NO

The keyword SANS_GROUP_NO makes it possible to exclude nodes from the unilateral condition in the same way as for the formulations CONTINUE or DISCRETE (see § 3.1.3).

##### 3.8.1.3 Operand NOM_CMP

NOM_CMP gives the list of the components ${p}_{i}$ (degrees of freedom) on which the unilateral relation is exerted. This may be any degree of freedom carried by the node. For example: PRE, PRE1, PRE2, TEMP, or else DX, DY or DZ.

##### 3.8.1.4 Operands COEF_IMPO and COEF_MULT

COEF_IMPO is the value $r(t)$ imposed on the right-hand side of the unilateral relation. COEF_MULT is the list of the multiplying coefficients $\alpha (t)$ used in front of each of the degrees of freedom. The lengths of the lists COEF_MULT and NOM_CMP must of course be identical.

The coefficients $r(t)$ and $\alpha (t)$ are necessarily **functions**. To define constant coefficients, one should use the command DEFI_CONSTANTE.

##### 3.8.1.5 Operands ALGO_CONT and COEF_PENA

By default, the unilateral-relation condition is imposed by using the method of active constraints. This method assumes that the global matrix is symmetric. In the case where the physics of the problem leads to a non-symmetric matrix, the global matrix is then symmetrised, and this may lead to convergence difficulties.

In the case where one wishes to preserve the non-symmetry of the global matrix during the solution, one can use the penalisation method. The penalisation coefficient COEF_PENA must then be supplied. A good value of the coefficient COEF_PENA is difficult to determine in a systematic way. It depends on the problem and on the physics considered. One cannot give general advice on the value of this parameter; the user is therefore strongly advised to check a posteriori that the unilateral condition is correctly taken into account by viewing the results in the zone concerned. If this is not the case, it is necessary to increase COEF_PENA until this is verified.

#### 3.8.2 Example

One wishes to impose the condition 1.3*PRE1-5.2*PRE2 < 4.0; one will then have:

```text
coef_i = DEFI_CONSTANTE(VALE=4.0)
coef_m1 = DEFI_CONSTANTE(VALE=1.3)
coef_m2 = DEFI_CONSTANTE(VALE=-5.2)
NOM_CMP = (`PRE1’,’PRE2’)
COEF_IMPO = coef_i
COEF_MULT = (coef_m1,coef_m2)
```
