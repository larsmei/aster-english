# 1 **v3.02.114** SSLP114 - Semi-infinite flat fissure

**Summary**

This test validates the calculation of an asymptotic field by the XFEM method. This is to check whether the XFEM Modelling accurately represents the analytical solution, the mechanics of the break. This analytical field is the exact solution for the $I$ opening problem of a flat crack.

The domain is a square plate, cut to the middle by a horizontal crack. A load is imposed on the 4 edges to ensure a strict opening in $I$ mode, conforming to the analytical solution. Limit conditions of type "moving" on the unfungible edges, and of type "force" on the edge cut by the crack.

To validate this approach, we are considering 3 modellings:

- Modelling A: A simple calculation is performed from linear elements (TRIA3) for a horizontal crack
- Modelling B: a simple calculation is performed from quadratic elements (TRIA6) for a horizontal crack
- Modelling C: The crack is tilted to change the reference of analytical formulas. To keep the opening in $I$ mode, the fields (stress and displacement) imposed on the edges are tilted. On the one hand, the impact of the form of the domain on the results is evaluated, since in theory, asymptotic equations do not depend on the geometry of the domain in the reference of the crack. On the other hand, the robustness of the calculation with the degradation of the conditioning is evaluated.
- Modelling D: A simple calculation is performed from linear elements (TRIA3) for a horizontal crack, in flat stresses
- Modelling E: A simple calculation is performed from quadratic elements (TRIA6) for a horizontal crack, in flat stresses

The stress intensity factors $\mathrm{KI}$ , $\mathrm{KII}$ are tested. For $I$ mode, $\mathrm{KII}=0$ and $\mathit{KI}\ne 0$ , $\mathit{KI}$ corresponding to the proportionality coefficient imposed on the solution fields (see paragraph [4 ]).

Similarly, the accuracy of the calculation of the field of displacement calculated on the domain is also verified, in relation to the analytical solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

*Model A*:

The 2D structure is a square unit plate ($\mathrm{LX}=1$ , $\mathrm{LY}=1$ ), with an "inlet" crack on a half length []. The crack is straight, horizontal and $a=0,5$ long. The crack from the right edge is arbitrarily oriented towards the centre.

The edges of the domain are noted in trigonometric direction:

- $\mathit{LIG1}$ is the lower edge.
- $\mathit{LIG2}$ is the right edge.
- $\mathit{LIG3}$ is the top edge.
- $\mathit{LIG4}$ is the left edge.

The 4 edges of the domain are used to impose the boundary conditions. On the edges not cut by crack ($\mathit{LIG1}$ , $\mathit{LIG2}$ **,** $\mathit{LIG3}$ ), limit conditions of Dirichlet are imposed, using the analytical solution on the move (see paragraph [ 4 ]).

Note that the right edge ($\mathit{LIG4}$ ) cut by the crack, generates a singularity at the intersection. A displacement leap corresponding to the opening of the crack is observed. It is difficult to control this moving edge, since the jump condition on the upper lip and the lower lip of the crack is to be explained analytically on the edge elements cut by the crack. This difficulty is bypassed by imposing Neumann conditions on this edge.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Loading is imposed under mixed boundary conditions.

The unfungible edges are checked while moving, the cracked edge is checked in force. Furthermore, the boundary conditions of Dirichlet (on the move) fix the structure and prevent the appearance of rigid modes.

In modellings A and B, the crack only cuts the edge $\mathit{LIG4}$ , Neumann is imposed on this edge and Dirichlet on the other 3 edges (see [] ) .

In Modelling C, the previous approach is generalised. For an inclination greater than 45°, the crack cut either the lower edge ($\mathit{LIG1}$ ) or the upper edge ($\mathit{LIG3}$ ). On these two edges also a Neumann condition is applied. Therefore, a Dirichlet condition is imposed on the remaining edge ($\mathit{LIG2}$ ) to fix the rigid modes(see [...] ). The boundary conditions being imposed symmetrically on the horizontal crack ($\alpha =0$ ), the calculations presented will be valid for $-{135}^{°}<\alpha <{135}^{°}$

The required displacement corresponds to the exact analytical solution:

${U}_{x}=\frac{(1+\nu )}{E}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}{K}_{I}\cos(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

${U}_{y}=\frac{(1+\nu )}{E}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}{K}_{I}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

This solution depends on the polar coordinates related to the crack reference []. In the literature ([ 13 ]), the crack direction supports the $\overrightarrow{X}$ axis. The $\overrightarrow{X}$ axis is oriented from the crack bottom outwards. $\overrightarrow{Y}$ is orthogonal to $\overrightarrow{X}$ , such that $(\overrightarrow{X},\overrightarrow{Y})$ is a direct marker.

![Figure](/figures/v3.02.114/100002010000011A000000FEC952BFBB348CEE7B.png)

Figure 1.3-3: Référentiel local des formules analytiques

Therefore, a basic change is required to adapt the valid analytical equations in the crack reference to a given reference. In particular, since the origin of the mesh cells coordinate marker is chosen at the lower left corner of the domain, the polar coordinates are translated:

$\theta$ is the polar angle: $\theta (x,y)=\mathit{arctan2}(y-0.5,x-0.5)$

$r$ radial distance: $r(x,y)=\sqrt{{(x-0.5)}^{2}+{(y-0.5)}^{2}}$

The analytical solution stress tensor is:

${\sigma}_{xx}=\frac{{K}_{I}}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left(1-\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)\right)$

${\sigma}_{yy}=\frac{{K}_{I}}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left(1+\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)\right)$

${\sigma}_{xy}=\frac{{K}_{I}}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)$

Then the stress tensor is projected to deduce the force density to be applied to the right edge ($\mathit{LIG4}$ ), from normal "outgoing" $-\overrightarrow{X}$ .

The force to apply is: $\overrightarrow{F}=\underline{\underline{\sigma}}.-\overrightarrow{X}$

It comes: ${F}_{x}=-{\sigma}_{xx}$ and ${F}_{y}=-{\sigma}_{xy}$

*Model A*:

Compared to the crack reference, the aster axes are oriented in the same direction. The $\mathit{Ux}$ and $\mathit{Uy}$ reference displacement fields are applied to the LIG1,LIG2, LIG3 edges:

${U}_{x}=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}{K}_{I}\cos(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

${U}_{y}=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}{K}_{I}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

The reference stress tensor is also retained. Therefore, the same force density as explained above is applied to the $\mathit{LIG4}$ edge.

${F}_{x}=-{\sigma}_{xx}$ ${F}_{y}=-{\sigma}_{xy}$

*Model B*:

Same equations as Modelling A.

*Model C*:

The crack is tilted from an angle of $\alpha \epsilon \lbrace  {0}^{°}{,30}^{°}{,60}^{°}{,90}^{°}{,120}^{°}\rbrace$.

This rotation impacts the **polar angle** , **movement field** and **stress strength** . These quantities rotates at an $\alpha$ angle , relative to the fixed marker of the aster code.

$\theta \to \theta -\alpha$

However, this transformation of the polar angle is not valid for all angles, because the definition of the polar angle is not continuous. In the local reference, on both sides of the crack there is an angular jump of $2\pi$: from the lower lip to the upper lip, we go from $-\pi$ to $\pi$.

Therefore, the rotation of the crack must also propagate this angular discontinuity. All angles swept by the crack (greyed area cf. [] and []) during rotation, undergo angular jump of $-2\pi$ or $2\pi$ ,

If $\alpha >0$ (crack rotation is done downwards)

- In the $-\pi <\theta <-\pi +\alpha$ field we have : $\theta \to \theta -\alpha +2\pi$
- In the $\pi >\theta >-\pi +\alpha$ field we have : $\theta \to \theta -\alpha$

If $\alpha <0$ (crack rotation is done upwards)

- In the $\pi >\theta >\pi +\alpha$ field we have : $\theta \to \theta -\alpha -2\pi$
- In the $-\pi <\theta <\pi +\alpha$ field we have : $\theta \to \theta -\alpha$

$\overrightarrow{U}\to {R}_{\alpha}\overrightarrow{U}$ where ${R}_{\alpha}$ is the $\alpha$ angle rotation matrix with ${R}_{\alpha}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos(\alpha )& -\sin(\alpha )\\ \sin(\alpha )& \cos(\alpha )\end{array}\right]$

Therefore,

$\overrightarrow{U}\to \left[\begin{array}{c}\cos(\alpha ){U}_{x}-\sin(\alpha ){U}_{y}\\ \sin(\alpha ){U}_{x}+\cos(\alpha ){U}_{y}\end{array}\right]$

The strain tensor is also affected by rotation:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}\to {R}_{\alpha}\underline{\underline{\sigma}}{R}_{-\alpha }$

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}\to \left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos(\alpha )& -\sin(\alpha )\\ \sin(\alpha )& \cos(\alpha )\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\sigma}_{xx}& {\sigma}_{xy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}& {\sigma}_{yy}\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos(\alpha )& \sin(\alpha )\\ -\sin(\alpha )& \cos(\alpha )\end{array}\right]$

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}\to \left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos(\alpha )& -\sin(\alpha )\\ \sin(\alpha )& \cos(\alpha )\end{array}\right]\left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xx}-\sin(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}& \sin(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xx}+\cos(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}\\ \cos(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}-\sin(\alpha ){\sigma}_{yy}& \sin(\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}+\cos(\alpha ){\sigma}_{yy}\end{array}\right]$

Hence the final writing of the stress tensor:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}\to \left[\begin{array}{cc}\cos{(\alpha )}^{2}{\sigma}_{xx}-\sin(2\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}+\sin{(\alpha )}^{2}{\sigma}_{yy}& -\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\alpha )(-{\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy})+\cos(2\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}\\ -\frac{1}{2}\sin(2\alpha )(-{\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy})+\cos(2\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}& \sin{(\alpha )}^{2}{\sigma}_{xx}+\sin(2\alpha ){\sigma}_{xy}+\cos{(\alpha )}^{2}{\sigma}_{yy}\end{array}\right]$

We check that the rotation retains the tensor symmetry and trace, $\mathit{tr}(\underline{\underline{\sigma}})={\sigma}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{yy}$

In the programming of the command file, the horizontal crack is a special case of inclined crack: all equations depend on any $\alpha$ inclination. To find the Modelling A and B, just do $\alpha =0°$ .

### 1.4 reference solution

We're imposing ${K}_{I}=1$.

In the crack-related repository, the analytical equations become:

${U}_{x}=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}\cos(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

${U}_{y}=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}\sqrt{\frac{r}{2\pi }}\sin(\frac{\theta}{2})(3-4\nu -\cos\theta )$

By linearity of the equations, the stress tensor is affected by the same proportionality coefficient and becomes:

${\sigma}_{xx}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left(1-\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)\right)$

${\sigma}_{yy}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\left(1+\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\sin\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)\right)$

${\sigma}_{xy}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi r}}\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{3\theta }{2}\right)$

By construction, there is no $\mathit{II}$ mode. In other words, we test ${K}_{\mathit{II}}=0$ .

*Model A*:

This Modelling allows the POST_ERREUR operator to be validated by comparing the calculated values of ${L}^{2}$ of the structure's displacement and energy to the analytical values.

#### 1.4.1 Calculation of the L2 standard of displacement

$\Omega$ is the domain occupied by the solid. The ${L}^{2}$ displacement standard is defined by:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\int}_{\Omega}{\parallel u\parallel }^{2}\mathit{dS}.$

On a:

${\parallel u\parallel }^{2}={\left(\frac{1+\nu }{E}\right)}^{2}\frac{r}{2\pi }{K}_{I}^{2}{(\kappa -\cos\theta )}^{2},$

for all point from $\Omega$. So we have:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\left(\frac{1+\nu }{E}\right)}^{2}\frac{{K}_{I}^{2}}{2\pi }{\int}_{\Omega}r{(\kappa -\cos\theta )}^{2}\mathit{dS}.$

> The $\Omega$ domain is represented in Cartesian coordinates by $\Omega =[-a,a]\times [-a,a]$ , where $a=\mathit{LX}/2=\mathit{LY}/2=1/2$ , and may be represented in polar coordinates by:

$\Omega =\left\lbrace (r,\theta )\in ℝ\times \left[\frac{-\pi }{4},\frac{7\pi }{4}\right],r\le \rho (\theta )\right\rbrace ,$

where $\rho$ is defined by:

$\rho (\theta )=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{a}{\cos(\theta )}\text{pour}\theta \in \left[\frac{-\pi }{4},\frac{\pi}{4}\right],\\ \frac{a}{\cos\left(\theta -\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}\text{pour}\theta \in \left[\frac{\pi}{4},\frac{3\pi }{4}\right],\\ \frac{a}{\cos(\theta -\pi )}\text{pour}\theta \in \left[\frac{3\pi }{4},\frac{5\pi }{4}\right],\\ \frac{a}{\cos\left(\theta -\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)}\text{pour}\theta \in \left[\frac{5\pi }{4},\frac{7\pi }{4}\right].\end{array}$

Consider the $f:(r,\theta )\to {r}^{n}g(\theta )$ function. We have:

$I={\int}_{\Omega}f(r,\theta )\mathit{dS}=\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\left(\underset{0}{\overset{\rho (\theta )}{\int}}f(r,\theta )r\mathit{dr}\right)d\theta .$

So:

$I=\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\left(\underset{0}{\overset{\rho (\theta )}{\int}}{r}^{n+1}g(\theta )\mathit{dr}\right)d\theta =\frac{1}{n+2}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}g(\theta ){\left(\rho (\theta )\right)}^{n+2}d\theta .$

On a:

$J=\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}g(\theta ){\left(\rho (\theta )\right)}^{n+2}d\theta ={J}_{1}+{J}_{2}+{J}_{3}+{J}_{4},$

with:

${J}_{1}={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{g(\theta )}{\cos{(\theta )}^{n+2}}d\theta ,$

${J}_{2}={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\overset{3\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{g(\theta )}{\cos{\left(\theta -\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}^{n+2}}d\theta ={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{g\left(\theta +\frac{\pi}{2}\right)}{\cos{(\theta )}^{n+2}}d\theta ,$

${J}_{3}={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{3\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{5\pi }{4}}{\int}}\frac{g(\theta )}{\cos{(\theta -\pi )}^{n+2}}d\theta ={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{g(\theta +\pi )}{\cos{(\theta )}^{n+2}}d\theta ,$

${J}_{2}={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{5\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{7\pi }{4}}{\int}}\frac{g(\theta )}{\cos{\left(\theta -\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)}^{n+2}}d\theta ={a}^{n+2}\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{g\left(\theta +\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)}{\cos{(\theta )}^{n+2}}d\theta ,$

So we finally:

$I=\frac{{a}^{n+2}}{n+2}\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{1}{\cos{(\theta )}^{n+2}}\left[g(\theta )+g\left(\theta +\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+g(\theta +\pi )+g\left(\theta +\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)\right]d\theta .$

We notice that for $n=1$ and $g:\theta \to {(\kappa -\cos(\theta ))}^{2}$ ., we have:

$I={\int}_{\Omega}r{(\kappa -\cos\theta )}^{2}\mathit{dS}.$

It is also noted that:

${(\kappa -\cos\theta )}^{2}={\kappa}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}-2\cos(\theta )+\frac{1}{2}\cos(2\theta ).$

In this case, therefore, the following are found:

$g(\theta )+g\left(\theta +\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+g(\theta +\pi )+g\left(\theta +\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)=4{\kappa}^{2}+2.$

D'où:

${\int}_{\Omega}r{(\kappa -\cos\theta )}^{2}\mathit{dS}=\frac{{a}^{3}}{3}(4{\kappa}^{2}+2)\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{1}{\cos{(\theta )}^{3}}d\theta .$

And finally:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\left(\frac{1+\nu }{E}\right)}^{2}{K}_{I}^{2}{a}^{3}\frac{(2{\kappa}^{2}+1)}{3\pi }\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{1}{\cos{(\theta )}^{3}}d\theta .$

Either:

${\parallel u\parallel }_{{L}^{2}}=\frac{1+\nu }{E}{K}_{I}{a}^{\frac{3}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{(2{\kappa}^{2}+1)}{3\pi }\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{1}{\cos{(\theta )}^{3}}d\theta }\approx 7,6057690825\times {10}^{-6}{\text{m}}^{2}.$

#### 1.4.2 Calculation of the energy of the structure

The energy of the structure is defined by:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\Omega}\sigma :\epsilon \mathit{dS}.$

Since $\nu =0$, we have $\epsilon =\frac{1}{E}\sigma$. Hence:

$\sigma :\epsilon =\frac{1}{E}\left({\sigma}_{xx}^{2}+{\sigma}_{yy}^{2}+2{\sigma}_{xy}^{2}\right).$

Using the expression of the tensor of the constraints given above and near developments, we obtain:

$\sigma :\epsilon =\frac{{K}_{I}^{2}}{E}\frac{1}{2\pi r}\left(2\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}+2\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}\sin{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}\right).$

It is noted that:

$2\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}=1+\cos\theta ,$

$2\cos{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}\sin{\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right)}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\sin{(\theta )}^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(1-\cos(2\theta )).$

So we have:

$\sigma :\epsilon =\frac{{K}_{I}^{2}}{E}\frac{1}{2\pi r}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\cos(\theta )-\cos(2\theta )\right).$

Either:

${E}^{e}=\frac{{K}_{I}^{2}}{E}\frac{1}{4\pi }{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\cos(\theta )-\cos(2\theta )\right)\mathit{dS.}$

We notice that for $n=-1$ and $g:\theta \to \frac{5}{4}+\cos(\theta )-\cos(2\theta )$ ., we have:

$I=\underset{0}{\overset{\rho (\theta )}{\int}}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\cos(\theta )-\cos(2\theta )\right)d\theta .$

In this case:

$g(\theta )+g\left(\theta +\frac{\pi}{2}\right)+g(\theta +\pi )+g\left(\theta +\frac{3\pi }{2}\right)=5.$

D'où:

${\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{r}\left(\frac{5}{4}+\cos(\theta )-\cos(2\theta )\right)\mathit{dS}=5a\underset{\frac{-\pi }{4}}{\overset{\frac{\pi}{4}}{\int}}\frac{1}{\cos(\theta )}d\theta =\mathrm{5a}\left(\ln(3)+2\sqrt{2}\right).$

And finally:

${E}^{e}=\frac{{K}_{I}^{2}}{E}\frac{5a}{4\pi }\left(\ln(3)+2\sqrt{2}\right)\approx 3,50687407712\times {10}^{-6}\text{J}\times {\text{m}}^{-1}.$

## 7 Summary of the results

The modellings A, B, C, D and E show that the XFEM method allows the asymptotic field of the theory to be found for a crack opening in $I$ mode. The displacement field is accurately represented, since in particular, the analytical values of stress intensity factors are found.

In paragraph [ 19 ], the evolution of the displacement field was restored to a wide angle of inclination, thus demonstrating that the calculated displacement field remains invariant in the local crack-related repository: the asymptotic field "suits" the crack movement, according to theory. Therefore, the geometry of the domain in the crack repository, the regularity and "directionality" of the mesh, have no influence on the accuracy of the test case calculations, with linear elements.

Furthermore, there is no evidence of a transition zone between the boundary conditions of Dirichlet and Neumann. For example, in model C, the $\mathit{L2}$ test of the error on the move is performed on the corner of the Neumann loading and Dirichlet condition. The displacement calculated by Aster "paste" to the analytical solution. Should it also be ensured that the same is true for the stress field? Developments in the code_Aster should put in place a calculation of the standard in energy to confirm these observations.

However, there are two limitations to the validation presented above:

- On the one hand, the inclination -to the infinity of the crack is not possible in our model. In model C, the angle of inclination is between $-135°$ and $135°$ because of the limit condition of Dirichlet on one of the edges of the domain. Given the symmetry of the problem, continuing this study on the rest of the trigonometric circle does not seem relevant.
- On the other hand, model B shows that validation is possible with quadratic elementss. However, there is a significant degradation of conditioning.
