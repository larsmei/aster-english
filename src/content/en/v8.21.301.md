# v8.21.301 ADLV301 – Cavity-plate coupling problem

**Summary:**

The objective of this test case is to calculate the pressure fields in a cavity with one of the walls flexible, the other walls being perfectly rigid. The excitation is mechanical and consists of a point normal force applied to the flexible wall.

Vibro-acoustic finished elementss are used (modelization A: 3D_FLUIDE and FLUI_STRU)

The boundary conditions are purely mechanical, allowing to create a deformed on the flexible, distorted wall that is at the source of the acoustic field in the cavity. The transmission between the plate and the cavity field is managed by elements interface "fluid-structure", suitable for Modelling by elements finished vibro-acoustics.

The cavity dimensions are large enough and the plate thickness is small to observe a non-negligible coupling effect. In addition, the excitation is de-activated to create a field of movement on the plate and a three-dimensional acoustic field in the cavity.

An exact analytical solution exists. This test case allows you to validate the consideration of fluid-structure coupling. On the other hand, this test case validates the acoustic pressure calculation in the elements vibro-acoustic finished.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution for the problem of modal representation of the plate is given in [1].

The reference solution for the problem of modal representation of the cavity is given in [2].

The integration of these two models and their mutual interaction leads to a coupled modal solution, the equations of which are described below (see also [3]).

The problem is the evaluation of the dynamic response of an elastic rectangular plate coupled with a parallelepipedic acoustic cavity, subject to a transverse point force. The geometry of the system envisaged is shown on the previous page.

The plate has a thickness $t$ and is made of a material characterized by the usual mechanical constants: Young's modulus $E$ , structural loss factor ${\eta}_{S}$ , Poisson's ratio $\nu$ and density ${\rho}_{S}$ . The fluid is characterized by a density ${\rho}_{F}$ , a speed of sound ${c}_{F}$ and a loss factor ${\eta}_{F}$ .

The point force (amplitude $F$ ) is applied to the position $({x}_{F},{y}_{F})$ .

The clean pulses and the proper modes of the structure are calculated by the following expressions ($m,n=1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$ ):

${\omega}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}={\left(\frac{D}{{\rho}_{S}t}\right)}^{1/2}\left({\left(\frac{m\pi }{{L}_{x}}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{n\pi }{{L}_{y}}\right)}^{2}\right)$ with $D=\frac{E{t}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}$

${\Phi}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}(x,y)={A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}\sin\left(\frac{m\pi x}{{L}_{x}}\right)\sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{{L}_{y}}\right)$

The ${A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}$ coefficient shall be chosen so that the relative mode of ${A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}$ is orthonormalized in relation to the structural mass:

$\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\rho}_{s}{\left({\Phi}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}(x,y)\right)}^{2}t\text{dx}\text{dy}=1$

This leads to the following value for ${A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}$: ${A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}=\frac{2}{{({\rho}_{S}t{L}_{x}{L}_{y})}^{1/2}}$

Clean pulses and sound eigenmodes are calculated by assuming rigid walls for the cavity and lead to the following expressions ($i,j,k=0,1,2,\mathrm{...}$ ):

${\omega}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}=c{\left({\left(\frac{i\pi }{{L}_{x}}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{j\pi }{{L}_{y}}\right)}^{2}+{\left(\frac{k\pi }{{L}_{z}}\right)}^{2}\right)}^{1/2}$

${\Phi}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}(x,y,z)={A}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}\cos(\frac{i\pi x}{{L}_{x}})\cos(\frac{j\pi y}{{L}_{y}})\cos(\frac{k\pi z}{{L}_{z}})$

The ${A}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}$ coefficient is assessed by the condition of normalization in relation to the sound mass:

$\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\frac{1}{{\rho}_{F}{c}_{F}^{2}}{\left({\Phi}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}(x,y,z)\right)}^{2}\text{dx}\text{dy}\text{dz}=1$

and therefore ${A}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}$ is equal to: ${A}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}={\left(\frac{{\rho}_{F}{c}_{F}^{2}}{{L}_{x}^{'}{L}_{y}^{'}{L}_{z}^{'}}\right)}^{1/2}$

with:

$\begin{array}{c}{L}_{x}^{'}\text{}={L}_{x}\text{si}i=0\\ \text{}=0\text{si}i>0\end{array}$ $\begin{array}{c}{L}_{y}^{'}\text{}={L}_{y}\text{si}j=0\\ \text{}=0\text{si}j>0\end{array}$ $\begin{array}{c}{L}_{z}^{'}\text{}={L}_{z}\text{si}k=0\\ \text{}=0\text{si}k>0\end{array}$

The coupled system is described by the following equations:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}{\Lambda}_{S}& {\rm E}\\ {\omega}^{2}{{\rm E}}^{T}& {\Lambda}_{F}\end{array}\right]\left(\begin{array}{c}{X}_{S}\\ {X}_{F}\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}{c}{F}^{S}\\ 0\end{array}\right)$

with:

- ${X}_{S}$ and ${X}_{F}$ are the structural and acoustic modal input factors, respectively;
- ${\Lambda}_{S}=\text{diag}\left\lbrace {\left({\omega}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}\right)}^{2}-{\omega}^{2}\right\rbrace$
- ${\Lambda}_{F}=\text{diag}\left\lbrace {\left({\omega}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}\right)}^{2}-{\omega}^{2}\right\rbrace$
- ${F}^{S}={\Phi}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}({x}_{F},{y}_{F})\cdot F$
- ${{\rm E}}_{\lbrace \mathit{mn}\rbrace \lbrace \mathit{ijk}\rbrace }=\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{L}{\int}}{\Phi}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}(x,y){\Phi}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}(x,y,0)\text{dx}\text{dy}={A}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}{A}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}{c}_{x}{c}_{y}$
- ${c}_{x}=-\frac{{L}_{x}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\cos(m-i)\pi }{2(m-i)}+\frac{\cos(m+i)\pi }{2(m+i)}\right)$
- ${c}_{y}=-\frac{{L}_{y}}{\pi}\left(\frac{\cos(n-j)\pi }{2(n-j)}+\frac{\cos(n+j)\pi }{2(n+j)}\right)$

The coupled dynamic response (moving $\text{u}$ for structure and $\text{p}$ for fluid) can then be calculated by:

$u(x,y)=\sum_{m,n}{\Phi}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}(x,y){X}_{\mathit{mn}}^{S}$

$p(x,y,z)=\sum_{i,j,k}{\Phi}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}(x,y,z){X}_{\mathit{ijk}}^{F}$
