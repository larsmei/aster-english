# v4.24.201 TTNP201-Thermal transfer with time-dependent conductivity

**Summary:**

This test consists of imposing variable conductivity time-dependent during a thermal transfer. A prescribed flow condition is applied to a edge of the wall and the temperature remains constant on the opposite edge. The conductivity increases with time and a temperature jump is observed on the edge where the flow is imposed.

The problem is handled in PLAN, AXIS and 3D.

The reference solution is obtained by the iterative method of Crank-Nicholson.

The consideration of a time-dependent conductivity is tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A wall of thickness $L=2m$ is considered.

![Figure](/figures/v4.24.201/1000000000000426000002FAE3C74AA6C927178A.jpg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The thermal properties are:

- $\rho \mathrm{Cp}=1J/{m}^{3}°C$
- $\lambda (W/m°C)$ varies as follows:

![Figure](/figures/v4.24.201/100002010000055A0000031624CECA3F57638E8A.png)

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

On the $\mathrm{SI}$ edge, a cooling flow of $-1W/{m}^{2}$ is imposed.

On the $\mathrm{SE}$ edge, you will set a temperature of $100°C$.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

At the initial moment, the temperature is uniform and equal to $100°C$.

### 1.5 Time-based discussion

The calculation is performed on $\mathrm{200s}$ with an time step of: $0,1s$ between 0 and $\mathrm{40s}$ then $0,05s$ between $\mathrm{40s}$ and $\mathrm{80s}$ and finally $0,1s$ up to $\mathrm{200s}$.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

You must solve: $\rho \mathrm{Cp}\frac{\mathrm{dT}(x,t)}{\mathrm{dt}}=\lambda (t)\frac{\mathrm{dT}(x,t)}{\mathrm{dx}}$ with the initial condition $T(x,0)=100$ and boundary conditions: $-\lambda (t){\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}\mid }_{x=0}=-1$ and $T(L,t)=100$

This differential equation is solved with an iterative diagram of Crank-Nicholson [1] under scilab.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The temperature is observed at the edge of $x=0$ and in the middle of the $x=\frac{L}{2}$ wall. When conductivity increases between 50 and 55 seconds, the temperature in the middle decreases more rapidly. The lost heat is spread to the edges where warming is observed. This phenomenon is also observed when drying calculation is done when the diffusion coefficient depends on temperature. We see then a re-wetting of the edge.

![Figure](/figures/v4.24.201/10000201000004EC0000031F9C248EBAB65F2C86.png)

For the test case reference, the temperature values in $x=0$ and $x=\frac{L}{2}$ are recorded at different times corresponding to the appearance of the temperature jump at peak and at the end of the calculation.

|  | 50s | 51s | 72s | 200s |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| x=0 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |
| x=L/2 | 0.0000 | Not tested | 0.0000 | 0.0000 |

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The resolution method requires small steps in time and space to correctly capture the temperature peak. Here we used $\mathrm{dx}=0,05m$ and $\mathrm{dt}=0,01s$ .

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

  1. Crank, The mathematics of diffusion, Oxford University Press, second edition 1975.
