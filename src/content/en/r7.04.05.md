# r7.04.05 Computation and verification of reinforcement

**Summary:**

The algorithm for computing the reinforcement densities of reinforced-concrete elements is presented, as implemented in the command [CALC_FERRAILLAGE](doc:u4.81.42).
To this end, the computation algorithm for the line elements `1D` will first be presented, and then that for the surface elements `2D` of plate type will be examined.

The determination of the reinforcement of a section is carried out, according to the option chosen, to Eurocode 2 part 1-1 [bib2] (`CODIFICATION = 'EC2'`) or to BAEL91-99 (`CODIFICATION = 'BAEL'`).

This document also presents, in the section Verification of the reinforcement, the reinforcement-verification method used in the operator [VERI_FERRAILLAGE](doc:u4.81.46).

## 1 Computation of the reinforcement of beams and columns

### 1.1 Notation and equilibrium principles

The internal forces of the `1D` structural elements (beams/columns) are expressed in the local frame of the element, which will be defined here consistently with the documentation of [CALC_FERRAILLAGE](doc:u4.81.82).

Thus, the axis $X$ coincides with the load-bearing axis of the element, while the axes $Y$ and $Z$ lie in the plane of the cross-section and parallel to its sides, in accordance with the figure below.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/10000201000002A300000155C041FD4ADD8B28F7.png)

*Definition of the axes of a line element of beam/column type*

Using a beam of rectangular cross-section, one defines in the command file the two sides of the rectangle ${l}_{y}$ and ${l}_{z}$, which then makes it possible to define the two principal axes of inertia of the section, namely the axes $y$ and $z$.

The forces expressed in the local frame of the element are therefore:

- $N$ for the axial force
- ${V}_{y}$ and ${V}_{z}$ for the shear forces
- ${M}_{y}$ and ${M}_{z}$ for the bending moments
- ${M}_{x}$ for the torsional moment

It should be noted that the moments are expressed with respect to the centre of gravity of the unreinforced section (centre of the geometric section). Furthermore, one will say that the section is subjected to:

- **simple bending** if only one bending moment (${M}_{y}$ or ${M}_{z}$ ) loads the section.
- **combined bending** if a bending moment (${M}_{y}$ or ${M}_{z}$ ) and an axial force $N$ act on the section.
- **biaxial combined bending** if the section is subjected simultaneously to the two bending moments $({M}_{y},{M}_{z})$ as well as to an axial force $N$.
- **pure compression** or **pure tension** if only $N$ acts on the section.

The typical reinforcement field of a beam/column is defined as follows, at the cross-section of the element:

- Four layers of longitudinal reinforcement: ${A}_{s_y, \text{sup}}$, ${A}_{s_y, \text{inf}}$, ${A}_{s_z, \text{sup}}$ and ${A}_{s_z, \text{inf}}$ usually expressed in ${\text{cm}}^{2}$

These sections are carried by the two principal axes of inertia $Y$ and $Z$ of the section

- a density/ratio of transverse reinforcement: $\frac{{A}_{\text{sv}}}{s}$ usually expressed in ${\text{cm}}^{2}/m$

To this end, the reinforcement computation is carried out in two stages:

- computation of the longitudinal reinforcement: equilibrium in combined and/or biaxial bending, with respect to the axial compressive forces $N$, and to the two bending moments along the two axes of inertia of the beam, namely ${M}_{y}$ and ${M}_{z}$.
- computation of the transverse reinforcement: equilibrium under the shear forces ${V}_{y}$ and ${V}_{z}$, and under the torsional moment ${M}_{x}$. It should then be noted, in accordance with the explanations which follow, that these forces may in certain cases induce additional tensile forces along the load-bearing axis $X$ of the element, thus leading to additional longitudinal reinforcement.

These equilibrium computations therefore rest on criteria linked to the design limit state retained, namely:

- ULS: the criterion is that the ultimate strains of the compressed concrete and of the reinforcing steel are not exceeded.
- Characteristic SLS: the criterion is that the limiting compressive stress of the concrete in compression and the limiting stress of the bending steel are not exceeded.
- Quasi-permanent SLS: the criterion is that the maximum crack opening is not exceeded.

#### 1.2.1 Computation of the longitudinal reinforcement in simple or combined bending

##### 1.2.1.1 Definition of the computation hypotheses

At the ULS, the principles of verification of the section and of computation of the reinforcement in combined bending with respect to the internal forces $N$, ${M}_{y}$ and ${M}_{z}$, rest on the following three hypotheses:

- The plane cross-sections remain plane after deformation (Euler-Bernoulli). Consequently, this implies that the strain diagram governing the section at equilibrium in combined bending is linear along

- compressive strain of the concrete limited to ${\strainCmp}_{\text{cu2}}$ (pivot B), in the case of a partially tensioned/compressed section
- compressive strain of the concrete limited to ${\strainCmp}_{\text{c2}}$ (pivot C), in the case of a fully compressed section

The parameters below are therefore defined:

- ${f}_{\text{ck}}$ : characteristic value of the compressive strength of the concrete, which in practice is defined according to the concrete class in Eurocode 2.
- ${f}_{\text{cd}}$ : value of the compressive strength of the concrete retained for the ULS computation, namely ${f}_{\text{cd}}=\frac{{\alpha}_{\text{cc}}{f}_{\text{ck}}}{{\gamma}_{c}}$ , with ${\alpha}_{\text{cc}}=1$ for EC2 and ${\gamma}_{c}$ (safety factor) which depends on the type of ULS considered (1.5 in a persistent situation and 1.2 in an accidental situation).

- $n=2$ if ${f}_{\text{ck}}\le 50 \, \text{MPa}$

##### 1.2.1.2 Equilibrium principle and computation of the steel areas

The hypotheses introduced in the previous paragraph are used to draw the diagram of the Figure which follows and are at the origin of the “three-pivot rule” used to design or verify a reinforced-concrete section.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/1000020100000529000001E61D8909DCDB030BC3.png)

*Design principle in combined bending at the ULS*

In the Figure above, one distinguishes:

- A typical concrete section with a lower reinforcement ${A}_{s, \text{inf}}$ and an upper reinforcement ${A}_{s, \text{sup}}$ , of depth equal to $h$ and an effective depth $d$ , which represents the lever arm of the centre of gravity of the lower reinforcement relative to the upper fibre of the section (*in the case of a negative bending moment, it is then the lever arm of the centre of gravity of the upper reinforcement relative to the lower fibre of the section)* . In practice, one can estimate that $d=h-{c}_{\text{inf}}$ , such that ${c}_{\text{inf}}$ represents the cover (to within half a reinforcement diameter) of the lower reinforcement, that is the distance from the centre of gravity of the lower reinforcement to the lower fibre of the section (*in the case of a negative bending moment, one then considers* $d = h-{c}_{\text{sup}}$ ) .
- The plotting of the limit strain diagrams, in the form of straight lines in view of the Euler-Bernoulli hypothesis, passing through the three pivots A, B and C.

Design at the ULS consists in placing oneself under failure conditions corresponding to the attainment of a limit strain relating either to the steel or to the concrete, so that the strain diagram passes through one of the three points A, B, C shown in the previous figure:

- Pivot A corresponds to the failure of the lower steel (*in the case of a negative bending moment, it is then the upper steel)* , that is when the tensioned steel reaches the limit strain ${\strainCmp}_{\text{ud}}$
- Pivot B corresponds to the failure of the concrete at the upper fibre (*in the case of a negative bending moment, it is then the concrete at the lower fibre)* , that is when the most compressed concrete reaches the limit strain ${\strainCmp}_{\text{cu2}}$
- Pivot C corresponds to the attainment of the maximum strength ${f}_{\text{cd}}$ of the concrete (in accordance with the constitutive law), that is for a strain equal to ${\strainCmp}_{\text{c2}}$. This pivot is used for the study of the fully compressed cases; its position relative to the depth of the section is obtained from the intersection of the diagram relating to the limit of the partially compressed case (neutral axis positioned at the lower fibre ( *in the case of a negative bending moment, it is then the upper fibre*) of the cross-section (BC)) and of the case of pure compression (${\strainCmp}_{\text{c2}}$ over the whole depth of the section). For this last case (pure compression), Eurocode 2 thus prefers to limit the concrete strains to ${\strainCmp}_{\text{c2}}$ rather than ${\strainCmp}_{\text{cu2}}$.

The points A, B, C are called “pivots” since one can sweep all the possible failure configurations of the section by pivoting about one of them.

Indeed, from the definition of the three pivots and from the position of the neutral axis of the strain diagram, it is possible to define failure “domains”, as represented in the following figure:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/10000201000003220000012716733E59D92DFDDB.png)

*Domains of behaviour/equilibrium in combined bending at the ULS.*

**Domain 1**: the strain diagram passes through pivot A, but the neutral axis lies outside the section and above the upper fibre, so that the whole section is in tension.

**Domain 2**: the strain diagram still passes through pivot A, but the neutral axis now lies inside the section. The section is thus partially compressed, so that failure occurs at the tensioned steel.

**Domain 3**: the strain diagram now passes through pivot B, and the neutral axis lies inside the section. The section is therefore still partially compressed, with failure at the upper fibre of the concrete.

**Domain 4**: this is a continuation of the hypotheses of the previous domain, but the strain of the lower steel becomes smaller than the limit of the elastic-plastic behaviour, namely ${\strainCmp}_{\text{yd}}=\frac{{f}_{\text{yd}}}{{E}_{s}}$. The tensioned steel falls into the elastic domain and therefore no longer works in an optimal manner in terms of strength.

**Domain 5**: the neutral axis is again outside the section, but below the lower fibre. The section is now fully compressed, so that the strain diagram passes through pivot C.

#### 1.2.2 Computation of the longitudinal reinforcement in biaxial bending

In this part, the section is subjected both to a bending moment along the axis $Y$ and to another along the axis $Z$, as well as to an axial force (positive in compression).

The case of biaxial bending or biaxial combined bending is more complex to solve. Indeed, the interaction diagram (limit strength surface) is in this case three-dimensional, in accordance with the figure below:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/10000201000005400000043EA4D93982975DD34E.png)

*3D interaction diagram along the axes $N$, $M_y$, $M_z$ in the case of a biaxial combined section*

An exact and rigorous design process (similar to the previous computations) would, given the three-dimensional nature of the internal forces, be complex to develop. One therefore opts for a simplified *verification* procedure.

It will indeed be an *iterative* design method (and therefore not direct), based on the principle of verification of the Bresler inequality developed by Bresler, as presented in §5.8.9 of Eurocode 2. This method will thus make it possible to study separately, and in a perfectly decoupled manner, the two combined bendings loading the section, the first $(N,M_y)$ along the principal axis of inertia $Y$ and the second $(N,M_z)$ along the second principal axis of inertia, $Z$. 1. One restarts with $k_{var}^{B} = k_{var}^A / 2$. As long as $COND_{fiss}(k_{var}^B)=FAUX$.
One updates $k_{var}^A = k_{var}^A / 2$ and restarts until one obtains $COND_{fiss}(k_{var}^B)=VRAI$.

2. One carries out a secant search for $k_{var}$ between the bounds $k_{var}^B$ and $k_{var}^A$
in order to find the zero of the function $f(k_{var}) = max(w_{inf}(k_{var})-w_{max,inf}, w_{sup}(k_{var})-w_{max,sup})$.
One converges if $f(k_{var}) \leq 10^{-6} \times (w_{max,inf}+w_{max,sup})$.
$k_{var}$ is the governing solution at the QP SLS.

The function being strongly non-linear, one stops the secant search and considers it a failure at iteration N if
$k_{var}^N \notin [k_{var}^B, k_{var}^A]$, if $\lvert f(k_{var}^N) - f(k_{var}^{N-1}) \rvert \leq 10^{-6} \times (w_{max,inf}+w_{max,sup})$
or if $N=5$.

3. In the event of failure of the secant search,
one carries out a bisection search for $k_{var}$ between the bounds $k_{var}^{B}$ and $k_{var}^{A}$.
The bounds $[k_{var}^B, k_{var}^A]$ are updated at each iteration as a function of the value of $COND_{fiss}(k_{var})$.
One stops when the interval $\lvert k_{var}^A - k_{var}^B \rvert  \leq 10^{-6}$.
$k_{var}^B$ is the governing solution at the QP SLS.

## 2 Computation of the reinforcement of plates

The computation of the reinforcement of plates can be carried out according to two distinct methods: the Capra-Maury method (which is a generalisation of the `1D` computation to a finite number $N$ of fictitious facets) or the Sandwich method (in accordance with Annex LL of EN-1992-2).

### 2.2 The Sandwich method

#### 2.2.1 Principle of the method

The Sandwich method — initiated in the literature by Gupta and Marti and whose principle was introduced ( but in an incomplete and little detailed manner ) in Annex LL of EN-1992-2 in 2004 — is a method which makes it possible to exploit the `2D` character of a plate element, by taking into account the bidirectional stiffness of the element as well as the plate kinematics (this last point being supported by the additional step of scale transition which will be introduced later in the note).

This method divides the plate into three layers (hence its name):

- The upper and lower layers (of respective thicknesses $t_{\text{sup}}$ and $t_{\text{inf}}$ ) resist the membrane internal forces (axial forces, bending moment, membrane shear and torsion). They therefore take up the forces ${N}_{xx}$, ${N}_{yy}$ and ${N}_{xy}$ as well as the forces ${M}_{xx}$, ${M}_{yy}$ and ${M}_{xy}$.
- The intermediate layer, or concrete core (of thickness $t_{c}$ ), resists the out-of-plane shear. It therefore takes up the forces ${Q}_{x}$ and ${Q}_{y}$ .

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/10000000000003CA0000021B8B8091B7908F7537.png)

*Presentation of the “Sandwich” model (division of the plate into three layers)*

Thus, the first step of the method is to determine, by means of an iterative computation, the optimal thicknesses of the two outer layers and then to deduce from them, by means of the membrane equilibrium equations, the membrane reinforcement (namely the quadruplet $\left( {A}_{\text{x,\text{sup}}},{A}_{\text{y,\text{sup}}},{A}_{\text{x,\text{inf}}},{A}_{\text{y,\text{inf}}}\right)$ in $cm^2.ml$). In a second step, the thickness of the intermediate layer can be deduced ($t_c = h–t_{\text{sup}}–t_{\text{inf}}$) and the transverse reinforcement (namely the pair $\left( {A}_{\text{sv},x},{A}_{\text{sv},y}\right)$ in $cm^2.ml.ml$) will be computed according to the classical rules for the shear design of beams as developed in §6.2 of EN-1992-1.

## 3 Verification of the reinforcement

This section presents the method used for the verification of an existing reinforcement employed in the operator [VERI_FERRAILLAGE](doc:u4.81.46) and the macro-command [POST_VERI_FERRAILLAGE](doc:u4.81.47).
The objective is to verify whether the structure with an existing reinforcement (coming from a reinforcement drawing or from a reinforcement computation) supports a given load by providing a mechanical margin and/or a utilisation ratio of the maximum capacity of the structure under this load.
The method used is inspired by that employed in the RunAéro tool.

### 3.1 Verification of the reinforcement of plates

As for the reinforcement computation of plates, the verification is also based on the Capra-Maury algorithm. The various steps of the method employed are the following:

#### 3.1.1 Loop over the facets

One considers $n$ facets pivoting about the axis normal to each finite element (as presented in the part r7.04.05-calcul-ferraillage-longitudinal).
Each of the facets is defined by its normal vector $\vector{n}(\theta)$ making an angle $\theta$ with the local axis $x$ of the plate.
This makes it possible to assimilate the study of a plate to an equivalent problem of beams (`1D`).

#### 3.1.2 Projection of the reinforcement

The upper and lower reinforcement along $x$ and $y$ of the plate will be projected onto the facet as follows,
making it possible to obtain the reinforcement of the fictitious beam (${A}_{s, \text{sup}}(\theta)$ and ${A}_{s, \text{inf}}(\theta)$):

$$
{A}_{s, \text{sup}}(\theta)
=
{A}_{s_x, \text{sup}} \, {\cos}^{2}\theta
+
{A}_{s_y, \text{sup}} \, {\sin}^{2}\theta
$$

$$
{A}_{s, \text{inf}}(\theta)
=
{A}_{s_x, \text{inf}} \, {\cos}^{2}\theta
+
{A}_{s_y, \text{inf}} \, {\sin}^{2}\theta
$$

It is important to note that the method employed for the computation of the margin does not take into account the transverse reinforcement for out-of-plane shear.

#### 3.1.3 Interaction diagram

In this step, the interaction diagram of each facet will be constructed. The interaction diagram of a reinforced-concrete section represents the set of points $(N, M)$ corresponding to a limit state of the section.
These points are then plotted on a `2D` graph whose abscissa axis represents the axial force and whose ordinate axis represents the bending moment.
Every point of the curve represents a unique combination of bending moment and axial force which the section can resist.
The construction of this diagram is carried out by considering the strain diagrams or the relative stress diagrams over the depth of the section, passing through one of the three pivots A, B or C (see r7.04.05-calcul-ferraillage-1D for further information on the rule and the definition of the three pivots).

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.05/diagramme_elu_3pivots.png)

*Construction of the N-M interaction diagram at the ULS*

****ULS**:**

The procedure for constructing this diagram at the ULS consists in considering successively the strain diagrams pivoting about the three pivots A, B or C (see r7.04.05-equilibre-calcul-sections-acier for further information on the rule and the definition of the three pivots).
For the computation of the points of the diagram, the parabola-rectangle law (see r7.04.05-loibetonelu) is used for the concrete, and the choice is free between an inclined or a horizontal plateau for the steel.
By way of illustration, figure r7.04.05-diagrammeelu shows an example of an interaction diagram for a particular case where the upper and lower steel is symmetric (same quantity and same cover) and a diagram with a horizontal plateau is chosen for the steel.
The construction of the diagram is carried out by dividing it into five domains as follows:

**Domain 1**: One begins with a vertical diagram passing through pivot A.
Then, while maintaining pivot A for the lower steel, one rotates the strain until a zero strain on the upper part of the section.
The neutral axis lies outside the section (above the upper fibre) and the whole section is in tension. The concrete does not work in this domain.
In the example illustrated, the strain in the upper steel always remains greater than the strain corresponding to the elastic limit.
With a horizontal plateau, this leads to the maximum stress $f_{\text{yd}}$ equal to that of the lower steel.
Because of the symmetry of the steel, this leads to a zero moment and to the same force over the whole of domain 1.
This is why domain 1 in red does not appear on the interaction diagram.

**Domain 2**: Next, one continues to pivot about pivot A until pivot B is reached on the upper fibre of the section.
In this domain, the neutral axis is now inside the section and the section is partially compressed.
The part of the concrete in compression therefore works.

**Domain 3**: By fixing pivot B at the upper fibre, one moves the strain of the lower steel to the right until the strain corresponding to the elastic limit of the steel.
The neutral axis is still inside the section and the section remains partially compressed.

**Domain 4**: Still fixing pivot B at the upper fibre, one continues to move the strain of the lower steel, which becomes elastic and then passes into compression.
One stops when the strain of the lower fibre is zero. The neutral axis remains inside the section and the section passes from partially compressed to pure compression.

**Domain 5**: In this domain, one now pivots about pivot C until a uniform compression is reached over the whole section with a strain equal to pivot C $({\strainCmp}_{\text{c2}})$.

This method makes it possible to obtain the upper part of the diagram, above the abscissa axis, corresponding to a tensioned lower section. Because of the symmetry of the problem, the lower part of the diagram is obtained by symmetry with respect to the abscissa axis.

****SLS**:**

At the SLS, a homogenised steel-concrete section is considered, so that the stress diagram is systematically linear (see r7.04.05-dimensionnement-ELS).
To this end, the construction of the interaction diagram at the SLS is carried out in a manner similar to the pivot rule of the strain diagram at the ULS, but applied to the stress diagram.

Two pivots are considered at the SLS: pivot A, where the steel reaches its limit, and pivot B, where the concrete reaches its limit.
In a similar manner, all the limit states are traversed, from uniform tension (pivot A) to uniform compression (pivot B).
One begins by pivoting about pivot A for the lower steel until pivot B is reached on the upper fibre, then one pivots about B until uniform compression.

As at the ULS, this method makes it possible to obtain the upper part of the diagram, above the abscissa axis, corresponding to a tensioned lower section.
Because of the symmetry of the problem, the lower part of the diagram is obtained by symmetry with respect to the abscissa axis.

The number of points of the diagram, often around 600 points, varies as a function of the geometric characteristics (in particular the ratio $\frac{h}{d}$) and of the values of the pivots.

#### 3.1.4 Projection of the forces

The objective of this method is to assess the structural response by following a given loading path.
This loading path can be defined by two types of loading: a reference loading $C_0$, often made up of the permanent loads,
and a study load $C$ (the new loading to be assessed). These loadings (2D internal forces $(N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}, M_{xx}, M_{yy}, M_{xy})$) will be projected onto the facet by means of equations  and  ,
which makes it possible to obtain the reduced `1D` force resultant $(N_0(\theta), M_0(\theta))$ for the reference loading and the resultant $(N(\theta), M(\theta))$ for the study load.
By placing these two points on the interaction diagram corresponding to the facet,
one can determine a third point $C_{\text{Rd}}$ as the intersection point of the half-line $\left[ C_{0C} \right)$ with the diagram, as illustrated in figure r7.04.05-diagramme-calcul-marge.

#### 3.1.5 Computation of the margin

The margin is then computed, for each facet, according to the following formula:

$$
m(\theta)
=
1
-
\frac{C_{0} C}{C_{0} C_{\text{Rd}}}
=
1
-
\frac{\Vert(N (\theta), M(\theta)) - (N_0  (\theta), M_0(\theta))\Vert}
     {\Vert(N_{\text{Rd}} (\theta), M_{\text{Rd}} (\theta)) - (N_0 (\theta),M_0 (\theta))\Vert}
$$

The margin of the plate element is equal to the minimum of the margins over all the facets:

$$
\text{marge}
=
\min \left( m(\theta) ; 0 \le \theta \le 180° \right)
$$

A positive margin indicates that the loading point is inside the diagram, which means that the reinforcement is verified.
The larger the margin, the lower the risk. A negative margin indicates that $C_0 \, C$ is greater than $C_0 \, C_{\text{Rd}}$ and that the element is not verified for the load studied.
