# v7.31.104 WTNV104 – Calculation of clean creep and desiccation creep in 3D Modelling with BETON_AGEING

**Summary:**

This test validates the BETON_AGEING model for concrete creep, in 3D_THHM modelling. The results are compared with analytical solutions.

- Modelling A: clean creep test.
- Modelization B: desiccation creep test.

### 1.3 Loading

For Modelling A, a clean creep test is performed by uniaxially compressing the cube represented on the v7.31.104-cube. During the test, the capillary pressure remains zero (saturated conditions).

For model B, a driesiccation creep test is simulated, and the sample is dried by increasing the capillary pressure. In parallel, the total ** stresses** remain zero throughout the test.

In both modellings, neither the gas pressure nor the temperature varied, remaining at their initial values, equal to $100$ kPa and $293$ K, respectively.

## 2 Reference solution

### 2.1 Clean creep test

This section presents the solution of a clean single compression creep test with the BETON_AGEING model on a saturated sample (the tensor of the actual stresses is then equal to the tensor of the total stresses). The stress state exercised at any time $t$ is written in the Cartesian base $(\tensTwo{e}_x,\tensTwo{e}_y,\tensTwo{e}_z)$:

$$
\stress = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \stressCmp_{zz}(t)
\end{pmatrix}
\quad\text{avec}\quad
\stressCmp_{zz}(t) =
\begin{cases}
\stressCmp_0 \quad &\text{si} \quad t>t_i \\
0 \quad &\text{si} \quad t<t_i
\end{cases}
$$

where $t_i$ is the instant application of the load (the latter being strictly higher than the $t_0$ moment at which the concrete is cast).

The tensor of the resulting deformations is axisymmetric:

$$
\strain = \begin{pmatrix}
\strainCmp_{xx}(t) & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \strainCmp_{xx}(t) & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \strainCmp_{zz}(t)
\end{pmatrix}
$$

Its components, for any $t>t_i$ moment, are given below, depending on the parameters of the BETON_AGEING model (see [R7.01.35](doc:r7.01.35)):

$$
\begin{align}
\strainCmp_{xx}(t) &= \underbrace{-\nu\frac{\stressCmp_0}{E}}_{\text{déf. élastique } \strainCmp^{e}_{xx}} + \underbrace{\frac{\stressCmp_0}{3}\left(\frac{1-\exp(-\frac{k_{s,r}}{\eta_{s,r}}(t-t_i))}{3k_{s,r}}-\frac{1-\exp(-\frac{k_{d,r}}{\eta_{d,r}}(t-t_i))}{2k_{d,r}}\right)}_{\text{déf. de fluage propre réversible } \strainCmp^{fp}_{r,xx}}
+\underbrace{\frac{\stressCmp_0}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3k_{s,i}}-\frac{1}{2k_{d,i}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{t-t_0}{t_i-t_0}\right)}_{\text{déf. de fluage propre irréversible } \strainCmp^{pf}_{i,xx}} \\
\strainCmp_{zz}(t) &= \underbrace{\frac{\stressCmp_0}{E}}_{\text{déf. élastique } \strainCmp^{e}_{zz}} + \underbrace{\frac{\stressCmp_0}{3}\left(\frac{1-\exp(-\frac{k_{s,r}}{\eta_{s,r}}(t-t_i))}{3k_{s,r}}+\frac{1-\exp(-\frac{k_{d,r}}{\eta_{d,r}}(t-t_i))}{k_{d,r}}\right)}_{\text{déf. de fluage propre réversible } \strainCmp^{fp}_{r,zz}}
+\underbrace{\frac{\stressCmp_0}{3}\left(\frac{1}{3k_{s,i}}+\frac{1}{k_{d,i}}\right)\ln\left(\frac{t-t_0}{t_i-t_0}\right)}_{\text{déf. de fluage propre irréversible } \strainCmp^{fp}_{i,zz}}
\end{align}
$$

It should be noted that these deformations are linearly dependent on the stress applied $\stressCmp_0$ since the BETON_AGEING model is indeed linear viscoelastic.

### 2.2 Fluid test of desiccation

The equation governing the flow of desiccation is now presented, in the case of drying, i.e. by monotonously increasing the capillary pressure $p_c$. This evolution is obtained by (see [R7.01.35](doc:r7.01.35)):

$$
\dot{\strain}^{fdess} = -\frac{\dot{h}\stress'}{k^{fd}} \quad \text{avec} \quad
\begin{cases}
d\stress' &= d\stress + \left(dp_{gz}-S_{lq}(p_c)dp_c\right)\tensTwo{B}\\
h &= \exp\left(-\cfrac{\mathcal{M}_{vp}}{RT\rho_{lq}}p_c\right)
\end{cases}
$$

In the situation where total stresses and gas pressure do not vary, and starting from a saturated sample, becomes:

$$
\dot{\strain}^{fdess} = -\left(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{vp}}{RT\rho_{lq}}\exp\left(-\frac{\mathcal{M}_{vp}}{RT\rho_{lq}}p_c\right)\int_0^{p_c}S_{lq}(p)dp\right)\frac{\dot{p}_c}{k^{fd}}\tensTwo{B}
$$

This differential equation can be numerically integrated knowing the liquid saturation-capillary pressure relationship, and results in isotropic deformation when the Biot tensor is isotropic also, i.e. for $\tensTwo{B}=b\tensTwoUnit$.

The boundary conditions symmetry are:

- On the mesh cell *DEVANT* : $DX=0$.
- On the mesh cell *GAUCHE* : $DY=0$.
- On the mesh cell *BAS* : $DZ=0$.

The mesh cell *HAUT* is pressured by $\sigma_{0}=-30$ MPa, following a linear ramp on the $[t_i,t_i+1\,\text{s}]$ interval where $t_i=28$ days. At the same time, the capillary pressure, gas pressure and temperature do not vary. They are imposed on their initial values in each node of the mesh as follows:

- $p_c^{init} = 0$ , $p_c^{ddl} = 0$,
- $p_g^{init} = 100$ kPa, $p_g^{ddl} = 0$,
- $T^{init} = 293$ K, $T^{ddl} = 0$.

The solutions are calculated at point NO8 at $t=365$ days. They are given in the v7.31.104-extensive_results in terms of:

- $\varepsilon_{zz}$ vertical total deformation,
- total lateral deformation $\varepsilon_{xx}$ ($=\varepsilon_{yy}$),
- $\varepsilon^{fp}_{r,zz}$ vertical reversible clean creep deformation,
- $\varepsilon^{fp}_{r,xx}$ ($=\varepsilon^{fp}_{r,yy}$) side reversible clean creep deformation,
- $\varepsilon^{fp}_{i,zz}$ vertical irreversible clean creep deformation,
- $\varepsilon^{fp}_{i,xx}$ ($=\varepsilon^{fp}_{i,yy}$) lateral irreversible clean creep deformation.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The modelling and boundary conditions on the move are identical to those described in model A.
The capillary pressure follows a linear ramp between $t=t_i=28$ days and $t=365$ days from zero to $100$ MPa in each node of the mesh. In parallel, the gas pressure and temperature remain at their initial values:

- $p_g^{init} = 100$ kPa, $p_g^{ddl} = 0$,
- $T^{init} = 293$ K, $T^{ddl} = 0$.
