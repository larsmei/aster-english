# r4.10.07 Computation of the error in constitutive relation in dynamics under a frequency-domain formulation

**Summary:**

This document presents the principle of the computation of the error in constitutive relation (ERC). In this document, the solution of such a problem is restricted to the case of linear dynamics under a frequency-domain formalism.

Several uses may motivate recourse to the solution of an ERC problem in linear dynamics:

- The expansion of measured data onto a numerical model (field reconstruction),
- The use of the ERC functional as a cost function in an updating or identification problem,
- The spatial localisation of defects of a numerical model from measurements.

The operator that solves the ERC is CALC_ERC_DYN [u4.53.41](doc:u4.53.41).

## 2 Introduction to energy functionals: case of the error in constitutive relation

In the framework of simulation in general, and of mechanics in particular, one is often led to evaluate one or more physical quantities governing the system under study from measurements. In general, however, the quantities sought are not only inaccessible by measurement but may present a certain number of pathologies (incomplete, redundant, tainted with errors …).

One therefore enters the framework of the solution of an inverse problem, whose aim is to establish a mathematical link resting on physical laws (a model), in order to recover the quantities sought that reproduce the experimental measurements as well as possible. Often, this takes the form of the solution of a minimisation problem in which a functional (cost function) is used, on the one hand, to quantify the discrepancy between the model and the measurement and, on the other hand, to resolve the potentially ill-posed character of the inverse problem.

In mechanics, and in particular in the family of variational methods (as distinct from probabilistic methods), one classically distinguishes three approaches for the construction of the functionals that make it possible to solve the inverse problem in question:

- methods based on least squares;
- the approach resting on the Maxwell-Betti reciprocity theorem;
- approaches based on the construction of energy functionals.

Unlike least squares and the approach that invokes the reciprocity principle, energy functionals are based on the measure of the distance between the fields of admissible solutions constructed from the measurements and from the model.

These techniques were first introduced by P. Ladevèze with the aim of estimating the quality of a solution obtained by the finite-element method. Thus, in [bib1], the concept of the Error in Constitutive Relation (ERC) was introduced. Since then, numerous applications have appeared, for example [bib2], [bib3] or [bib4].

The introduction of this notion may rest on 2 distinct principles:

- The approach valid for Generalised Standard Materials (GSM) which, for a structure with a given constitutive behaviour, characterised by a free-energy potential $\Phi (\cdot )$ , quantifies the difference between two evolutions associated with force and displacement boundary conditions by the following residual:

$e(ϵ,\sigma )=\Phi (ϵ)+{\Phi}^{\ast }(\sigma )-\sigma :ϵ$

where ${\Phi}^{\ast }(\cdot )$ is the potential defined by the Legendre-Fenchel transform of $\Phi (\cdot )$ . It follows from the expression proposed by Fenchel between two quantities, primal and dual, coming from a given potential, namely:

${e}_{\Pi}(x,y)=\Pi (x)+{\Pi}^{\ast }(y)-x:y$

An expansion of this expression for the discrepancy between two admissible pairs $({x}_{1,}{y}_{1})$ and $({x}_{2,}{y}_{2})$ leads to:

${E}_{\Pi}(x,y)=\langle {x}_{2}-{x}_{1},{y}_{2}-{y}_{1}\rangle$

This error is always positive and becomes zero when the boundary conditions are compatible with the constitutive relation. Moreover, it makes it possible to define a distance that meets the necessary criteria of positivity and symmetry. It gives rise to the better-known expressions of the error in constitutive relation presented later.

- The second approach is based on the stability principle in the sense of Drucker, defined for any pair of evolutions of the structure (starting from the same initial state) by the following inequality:

${\int}_{0}^{T}{\int}_{\Omega}({\sigma}_{2}-{\sigma}_{1}):(\dot{{ϵ}_{2}}-\dot{{ϵ}_{1}})d\Omega \mathit{dt}\ge 0$

If one places oneself in the framework of small perturbations, the response of the structure will be unique for any evolution issuing from the same history of the boundary conditions. This uniqueness property is satisfied for the constitutive laws frequently used (elasticity, plasticity, viscoelasticity, etc.) except for a few “singular” laws such as the damage laws. Indeed, the integration of the variational equation of the principle of virtual power entails the vanishing of the following expression:

${\int}_{0}^{T}{\int}_{\Omega}({\sigma}_{2}-{\sigma}_{1}):(\dot{{ϵ}_{2}}-\dot{{ϵ}_{1}})d\Omega \mathit{dt}+{\int}_{0}^{T}{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho {(\dot{{u}_{2}}-\dot{{u}_{1}})}^{2}=0$

Under the assumption of the Drücker stability condition, this equation makes it possible to write the following inequality:

${\int}_{0}^{T}{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\rho {(\dot{{u}_{2}}-\dot{{u}_{1}})}^{2}\le 0\forall t\in [0,T],\forall x\in \Omega$

which results in the equality of the two velocities and thus the equality of the displacements everywhere in the structure and at every instant t (taking into account the identical initial states for the two evolutions 1 and 2). Next, the strain histories are equal (by differentiation), which will be followed by the equality of the internal stresses (by the constitutive relation).

This assumption has been exploited to define a residual in the sense of an error in constitutive relation between two solutions described as kinematically admissible and statically admissible, respectively. A zero residual means that the two quantities, kinematically admissible and statically admissible, are compatible with the constitutive law.

The constitutive relation therefore seems a good measure of the compatibility of a model with the displacement and force boundary conditions. Thus, numerous studies have been carried out on this approach in various fields (elastostatics, elastodynamics, etc.) based on the construction of a distance between admissible fields (with respect to the force and displacement boundary conditions) in the sense of the constitutive relation.

Suppose, moreover, that one has constructed two solutions ${S}^{1}({\sigma}_{1})$ and ${S}^{2}({ϵ}_{2})$ issuing from two compatible evolutions of the displacement and force conditions respectively. The distance, in the sense of the constitutive relation, which makes it possible to evaluate quantitatively the discrepancy $E$ between these two solutions must satisfy:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}E({S}^{1,}{S}^{2})=E({S}^{2,}{S}^{1})\forall {S}^{1,}{S}^{2}\\ E({S}^{1,}{S}^{2})\ge 0\forall {S}^{1,}{S}^{2}\\ E({S}^{1,}{S}^{2})=0\iff {S}^{1}={S}^{2}\end{array}$

A few classical formulations of the error in constitutive relation are proposed below by way of example.

### 2.1 Classical formulation of the error in constitutive relation: case of elastostatics

The formulation of an energy functional of error-in-constitutive-relation type is particularly simple in the case of elastostatics.

One considers that the laws governing the problem are:

- The equilibrium equation:

$div\sigma =0$

- The boundary conditions:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}u=\stackrel{̃}{u}\mathit{sur}{\delta}_{u}\Omega \\ \sigma \cdot n=\stackrel{̃}{f}\mathit{sur}{\delta}_{f}\Omega \end{array}$

- The constitutive law:

$\underline{\underline{\sigma}}=\underline{\underline{C}}:\underline{\underline{ϵ}}(u)$

Thus, the ERC functional is associated with a problem of searching for admissible fields described by:

Find the statically admissible field ${\sigma}_{s}$ and the kinematically admissible field ${u}_{c}$ minimising:

${\int}_{\Omega}({\sigma}_{s}-ϵ({u}_{c})):{C}^{-1}:({\sigma}_{s}-ϵ({u}_{c}))d\Omega$

under the admissibility constraints:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{c}=\left\lbrace u\in {H}^{1}(\Omega );u=\stackrel{̃}{u}\mathit{sur}{\delta}_{u}\Omega \right\rbrace \\ {\sigma}_{s}=\left\lbrace u\in {H}^{div}(\Omega );\sigma \cdot n=\stackrel{̃}{f}\mathit{sur}{\delta}_{f}\Omega \right\rbrace \end{array}$

In the static case, one can show that the statically and kinematically admissible solution fields are independent. This results in the possibility of their being computed separately as a Neumann problem and a Dirichlet problem respectively.

### 2.2 Modified formulation of the error in constitutive relation: dynamic case

In the case of elastodynamics, it has been proved [bib5] that the “direct” extension of the previous formulation does not respect the spirit of the energy functionals for the dynamically and statically admissible fields because, besides the coupling through the constitutive equation, these fields are also linked by the equilibrium relation, which is now:

$div(\sigma )-\rho \ddot{u}=0$

Thus, an approach known as the “modified ERC” is introduced by Feissel and Allix in [bib3], whose formulation is summarised below:

Find the dynamically admissible field ${\sigma}_{d}$ and the kinematically admissible field ${u}_{c}$ minimising:

${\int}_{0}^{T}{\int}_{\Omega}({\sigma}_{d}-ϵ({u}_{c})):{C}^{-1}:({\sigma}_{d}-ϵ({u}_{c}))d\Omega \mathit{dt}+{\int}_{0}^{T}({\int}_{{\delta}_{u}\Omega }{d}_{u}({u}_{c},\stackrel{̃}{u})\mathit{dS}+{\int}_{{\delta}_{f}\Omega }{d}_{f}(f,\stackrel{̃}{f})\mathit{dS})\mathit{dt}$

under the admissibility constraints:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{c}=\left\lbrace u\in {H}^{1}(\Omega );u=\stackrel{̃}{u}\text{sur}{\delta}_{u}\Omega \right\rbrace \\ {\sigma}_{s}=\left\lbrace u\in {H}^{div}(\Omega );\sigma \cdot n=\stackrel{̃}{f}\text{sur}{\delta}_{f}\Omega \text{et}div(\sigma )-\rho \ddot{u}=0\text{sur}\Omega \right\rbrace \end{array}$

where the terms ${d}_{u}$ and ${d}_{f}$ are positive-definite norms to be defined (typically ${L}^{2}$ norms).

#### 2.2.1 Frequency-domain formulation in a finite-element formalism

The formulation presented below comes from the work in [bib6] and [bib7]. It is suited to cases of structural dynamics treated in the frequency domain and is particularly adapted to problems in an FE formulation in linear dynamics.

Let us place ourselves in the case where one seeks periodic solutions of the dynamic problem cited above, of the form:

$\Re ({u}_{\omega}{e}^{i\omega t})$ and $\Re ({f}_{\omega}{e}^{i\omega t})$

where $\Re (\cdot )$ denotes the real part and $\omega$ the given angular frequency. The equilibrium equation is then written:

$div(\sigma )-{\omega}^{2}\rho {u}_{\omega}=0$

Moreover, one denotes the admissible fields such that:

- ${u}_{c}=u$ is the kinematically admissible field
- $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{d}=C:ϵ(v)\\ {\Gamma}_{d}=-\rho {\omega}^{2}w\end{array}$ are the dynamically admissible fields

The Drüker inequality is now written:

${\xi}_{\omega}^{2}(u,v,w)=\text{}{\int}_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\text{tr}[C:{(ϵ(u)-ϵ(v))}^{\text{*}}(ϵ(u)-ϵ(v))]+\frac{1}{2}\rho {\omega}^{2}{(u-w)}^{\text{*}}(u-w)d\Omega$

where the sign $\text{*}$ denotes the complex conjugate. This expression makes it possible to approach the writing of the error-in-constitutive-relation functional commonly used in linear dynamics under a formulation specific to the FE discretisation.

Indeed, suppose that one is interested in the eigenmode search problem such that:

$([K]-{\omega}^{2}[M])\Psi =0;\Psi \ne 0$

where $[K]$ and $[M]$ are the stiffness and mass matrices respectively.

In this context, the problem of searching for optimal admissible fields in the sense of a functional of modified-ERC type is formulated in the following way:

Find the triplet of admissible fields $(u,v,w)$ which minimises:

${e}_{\omega}^{2}(u,v,w)=\frac{\gamma}{2}{(u-v)}^{\text{*}}[K](u-v)+\frac{1-\gamma }{2}{(u-w)}^{\text{*}}{\omega}^{2}[M](u-w)+\frac{1-\alpha }{\alpha}{(\mathit{Hu}-\widehat{u})}^{\text{*}}[\mathit{Gr}](\mathit{Hu}-\widehat{u})$

under the constraint:

$\left[K\right]v-{\omega}^{2}\left[M\right]\text{w=}0$

Where:

$[K]$ represents a real stiffness matrix

$[M]$ represents a mass matrix

$[H]$ represents an observation matrix

$[{G}_{r}]$ represents a symmetric positive-definite matrix serving as the norm of the errors in the observation space

$\omega =2\pi f$ : excitation angular frequency

$\widehat{u}$ observation of the displacements at the angular frequency $\omega$

$\gamma$ weighting parameter of the errors $(u-v)$ and $(u-w)$

$\alpha$ weighting parameter of the functional, which can be likened to a regularisation term

#### 2.2.2 Obtaining the admissible fields for the frequency-domain formulation in a finite-element formalism

The constrained optimisation problem above can be transformed into an unconstrained problem by introducing the following Lagrangian:

${E}_{\omega}^{2}(u,v,w,\lambda )={e}_{\omega}^{2}(u,v,w)+([K]v-{\omega}^{2}[M]w)\lambda$

Thus, obtaining the triplet $(u,v,w)\text{}$ that minimises the associated constrained problem is achieved by seeking the saddle point of the Lagrangian given by:

$\partial {E}_{\omega}^{2}(u,v,w,\lambda )=0$

which, considering the matrices $[K]$ and $[M]$ to be symmetric, leads to:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{\partial {E}_{\omega}^{2}}{\partial u}=\frac{\gamma}{2}[K](u-v)+\frac{1-\gamma }{2}{\omega}^{2}[M](u-w)+\frac{1-\alpha }{\alpha}{H}^{\text{T}}[\mathit{Gr}](\mathit{Hu}-\widehat{u})=0\\ \frac{\partial {E}_{\omega}^{2}}{\partial v}=\frac{-\gamma }{2}[K](u-v)+[K]\lambda =0\\ \frac{\partial {E}_{\omega}^{2}}{\partial w}=\frac{\gamma -1}{2}{\omega}^{2}[M](u-w)-{\omega}^{2}[M]\lambda =0\\ \frac{\partial {E}_{\omega}^{2}}{\partial \lambda }=[K]v-{\omega}^{2}[M]w=0\end{array}$

By simplifying the equations and seeking to obtain a symmetric system of equations, the search for the fields leads to the solution of the system of linear equations of the form:

$Al=b$

with, for each natural angular frequency ${\omega}_{i}$ :

${A}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}{cc}\gamma \left(\text{K+γ}/\left(1-\gamma \right){\omega}_{i}^{2}M\right)& -\gamma \left(K-{\omega}_{i}^{2}M\right)\\ -\gamma \left(K-{\omega}_{i}^{2}M\right)& \left(-\text{2α}/\left(1-\alpha \right)\right){H}^{T}{G}_{r}H\end{array}\right)$ ; and ${b}_{i}=\left(\begin{array}{c}{0}_{n}\\ \left(-\text{2α}/\left(1-\alpha \right)\right){H}^{T}{G}_{r}{\widehat{u}}_{i}\end{array}\right)$

where the vector of the unknown fields is:

${l}_{i}={\left(\begin{array}{c}u\\ u-v\end{array}\right)}_{i}$

Note that the field $(u-w)$ is obtained directly by combining the second and the third equation of the saddle-point search, leading to:

$(u-w)=\frac{\gamma -1}{\gamma}(u-v)$

## 3 Implementation in Code_Aster

The operator CALC_ERC_DYN [U4.53.41](doc:u4.53.41) has been developed in order to make it possible to solve the problem of searching for the optimal fields $(u,v,w)$ associated with the constrained optimisation problem described in the previous section.

In concrete terms, for a given list of frequencies, it solves the system of linear equations $Al=b$ detailed above, which makes it possible to obtain the fields $u$ and $u-v$ sought. It should be noted that the field $u-w$ is not proposed as an output of the operator, because it results in a trivial way from a proportional ratio with $u-v$ .

This operator also makes it possible to evaluate, if desired, the value of the functional ${e}_{\omega}^{2}$ at the solution triplet $(u,v,w)\text{}$ . This is particularly useful when one wishes to use the functional ${e}_{\omega}^{2}$ in an updating or identification problem.

The operator uses five mandatory keywords:

- the keywords MATR_RIGI and MATR_MASSE correspond respectively to the real assembled stiffness and mass matrices of the model under study;
- the keyword MATR_NORME: this keyword makes it possible to supply the generalised assembled matrix corresponding to the symmetric positive-definite matrix ${G}_{r}$ serving as the norm of the errors in the observation space.
- the keyword MATR_PROJ: this keyword makes it possible to give the projection matrix associated with the operator $H$ . It is a geometric correspondence between the mesh associated with the mass and stiffness matrices on the one hand, and the mesh associated with the observations $\widehat{u}$ on the other hand. The projection matrix requested here must come from an analysis with the operator PROJ_CHAMP [U4.72.05](doc:u4.72.05) with the method 'COLLOCATION'.
- the keyword MESURE: this keyword makes it possible to supply the fields which will be used as the observation $\widehat{{u}_{i}}$ . This concept must contain as many order numbers as the number of frequencies which will be studied when the operator is called (operands FREQ/LIST_FREQ). Furthermore, the dimension of the fields contained in this concept must be consistent with the dimension of the norm matrix supplied under the operand MATR_NORME. In addition, the fields contained must not include degrees of freedom of Lagrange type. To this end, it is advisable to condition this concept by prior use of the operator OBSERVATION [U4.90.03](doc:u4.90.03);
- the keyword CHAMP_MESURE making it possible to define the type of field contained in the measurement;
- the keyword FREQ / LIST_FREQ making it possible to define the frequencies at which the problem will be solved;
- the keywords ALPHA and GAMMA corresponding to the weighting parameters of the functional which can be likened to a regularisation term ($\alpha$ ) and to the weighting parameter of the errors $(u-v)$ and $(u-w)$ ($\gamma$ );
- the keyword EVAL_FONCT making it possible to determine whether the value of the functional for the optimal triplet of fields will be evaluated and stored in the result;
- the keyword SOLVEUR making it possible to choose the type of solver which will be used when solving the problem. In the current version, the solvers available are MUMPS (default) and LDLT.
