# u2.04.01 Advice on the use of STAT_NON_LINE

> **Summary.**

The aim of this note is to give some advice to a user wishing to carry out non-linear analyses with *Code_Aster* using the operator STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03].

General advice is given first. The remedies to be implemented for the main types of problem encountered are then specified. The problems in question are of the non-convergence type, or failure of an algorithm. Reference is also made to the documentation specific to each problem.

## 1 Introduction

The purpose of this document is to help the user solve a quasi-static problem of mechanical or thermo-hydro-mechanical evolution of a structure in the non-linear regime, *via* the operator STAT_NON_LINE. A detailed presentation of the algorithm of this operator is given in its theoretical documentation [R5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01).

The 3 main types of non-linearity are the following:

- non-linearity linked to the constitutive behaviour of the material (for example plastic);
- non-linearity linked to the geometry (for example in large displacements);
- non-linearity linked to frictional contact.

In the 2nd part of this document, the main tools available to help the convergence of the analyses and to improve the quality of the results are set out in detail. The following parts are devoted to advice on use. In the 3rd part, general advice is given, common to the 3 types of non-linearity. In the 4th, 5th and 6th parts, advice dedicated to each of the 3 types of non-linearity is given: material, then geometric, and finally frictional contact. This advice refers to the tools mentioned in the 2nd part.

## 2 Tools to help convergence

In the event of problems, several tools are available:

- subdivision of the time step,
- changing the matrices,
- piloting,
- line search,
- changing the finite-element formulation.

### 2.1 Subdivision of the time step

In general, the smaller the time step, the less non-linear the problem, and therefore the easier it is to solve. Subdivision of the time step is therefore an essential tool which makes it possible to get past the most common difficulties. Subdivision is activated by default in DEFI_LIST_INST [U4.34.03](doc:u4.34.03). It is advisable always to activate it. Moreover, the smaller the time step, the smaller the time-discretisation error.

### 2.2 A judicious choice of the matrices

In Newton's algorithm, it is possible to use different matrices in prediction and in correction and to update them more or less often. These choices are specified in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword NEWTON [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03). The most versatile and robust choice is to use the tangent matrix updated at each Newton iteration and to choose an elastic prediction.

PREDICTION='ELASTIQUE'

MATRICE='TANGENTE' (by default)

REAC_ITER=1

REAC_INCR=1 (by default)

In the event of a convergence problem in getting past a delicate time instant, it may be useful to switch from the tangent matrix to the unloading matrix if the time step becomes too small (that is, when the time step in question has been subdivided many times in succession). The value of the time step below which the unloading matrix is taken is given by the keyword PAS_MINI_ELAS. The update frequency of the unloading matrix is defined by REAC_ITER_ELAS. For a precise definition of the unloading matrix, see the factor keyword NEWTON of [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03).

### 2.3 Piloting

Piloting of the loading is a continuation method for Newton's method. Piloting makes it possible in particular to compute the response of a structure which would present instabilities, of geometric origin (buckling) as well as of material origin (softening). Its use is limited to simulations for which time does not play a physical role, which excludes *a priori* dynamic, or viscous, or thermo-mechanical problems.

### 2.4 Line search

Newton's method supplies an increment of the unknowns, but this increment is valid only in a neighbourhood of the initialisation. The idea of the line search is to use the direction of the increment, while controlling the length of the advance in that direction. The advance step is then chosen by minimisation of a functional. This makes it possible in particular to avoid certain divergences of Newton's algorithm. However, activating the line search is “expensive”. It is recommended to activate it only when needed.

In certain situations, the choice of partitioner can have an influence on the line search. It is therefore advisable either to increase ITER_GLOB_MAXI or to change partitioner. The SCOTCH partitioner tends to behave better than the METIS partitioner in certain cases (test case ssns115b).

### 2.5 A judicious choice of the finite-element formulation

Not all finite elements are equal. Some elements behave better than others in certain situations [U2.01.10](doc:u2.01.10). The choice of the finite elements is made in the operator AFFE_MODELE.

## 3 Advice common to the 3 types of non-linearity

In this part, general advice is given on the solution of non-linear problems.

### 3.1 Management of the list of time instants

The management of the list of time instants is specified via the operator DEFI_LIST_INST [U4.34.03](doc:u4.34.03). By default, subdivision of the list of time instants is activated in the event of an error (in the contact, the constitutive law, the piloting, the factorisation...). It is also possible to trigger the subdivision of the time step when the increment of a quantity exceeds a fixed threshold (DELTA_GRANDEUR). This makes it possible, for example, to ensure that an internal variable does not vary too much over a time step. Another possible cause of subdivision is a failure of the residual to decrease during the Newton iterations (DIVE_RESI). This makes it possible to subdivide in the event of a problem without waiting until the end of the number of Newton iterations allowed.

The so-called manual management of the list of time instants is the classical management: the analysis follows the list of time instants supplied by the user (with the possibility of subdivision). It is also possible to opt for an automatic management of the list of time instants. In this case, the user gives only a few passage time instants (time instants at which the loading changes, for example, or post-processing time instants). According to a first feedback of experience, automatic management of the time step [bib1]:

- is of real interest if the user has no *a priori* knowledge of the time discretisation,
- often makes it possible to save a considerable amount of time for “weakly” non-linear studies, that is studies which converge without advanced techniques (piloting, line search),
- often fails for “strongly” non-linear studies if it is used alone; it appears necessary to combine it with another technique, such as the line search.

This operator also makes it possible to extract from a previous analysis the list of the time instants actually computed and to refine it by a factor of 2. It is then possible to re-launch the same analysis with this new list, twice as fine, in order to control the error due to the time discretisation.

### 3.2 Management of the loadings

The application of a loading in force, or in non-zero imposed displacement, is usually done gradually (by load stages). To this end, it is possible to define a loading of function type (of time), or else to multiply the loading by a ramp-type function (with FONC_MULT under the keyword EXCIT of STAT_NON_LINE), except in the case of piloting.

The loads used for the boundary conditions (of zero imposed-displacement type) or for the contact are applied directly and do not require the definition of a function for a gradual application.

### 3.3 Management of the convergence of the global non-linear algorithm

By default, the global non-linear solution algorithm is based on the Newton–Raphson method. The parameterisation of the convergence of Newton's algorithm is done under the factor keyword CONVERGENCE of STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03).

The convergence of Newton's algorithm is characterised by the specification of a threshold value of the residual. When the value of the residual is below this threshold, the algorithm has converged. By default, it is the relative residual which is taken into account. The threshold is then given by the keyword RESI_GLOB_RELA ($10^{-6}$ by default). **It is strongly inadvisable to modify this convergence threshold**.

When the imposed loading is zero (in the case of total unloading, for example), the relative residual does not work and an attempt is made to switch automatically to an absolute convergence criterion (RESI_GLOB_MAXI). This operation is transparent to the user. In general it is inadvisable to fill in RESI_GLOB_MAXI. When these convergence criteria are not sufficient, it is possible to use finer criteria: a criterion which reasons component by component (RESI_COMP_RELA) or a criterion which reasons from a reference value for each quantity (RESI_REFE_RELA).

### 3.4 Management of archiving

The question of archiving the fields computed implicitly is a less important question than in explicit dynamics, where the number of time steps is generally very high.

Depending on the storage size available, and the number of fields one wishes to store, it is possible to estimate the number of time steps that can be stored.

By default, all the time steps are archived, including the computation time instants newly created by automatic re-cutting of the time step. As this number cannot be determined in advance, it is advisable to archive only the time instants of the initial list of time instants. If this list still contains too many time instants, it is advisable to create a 2nd, reduced list, specific to archiving. If the initial list of time instants contains many time instants and automatic subdivision of the time step is not activated (which is not advisable), archiving can also be limited by an archiving frequency.

It is also possible to reduce the bulk of the resultat data structure by extracting certain fields (operator EXTR_RESU).

### 3.5 Observation and monitoring of certain quantities

It is possible to store in a table certain components of the fields computed on parts of the model at time instants of a list (known as the observation list) generally finer than the list of archived time instants. This feature can be activated in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword OBSERVATION. This makes it possible to reduce the archiving requirements considerably. However, caution is required in the use of observation. Indeed, this feature was designed for a few points in the structure and it is not advisable to make several thousand observations at each time step, for performance reasons.

It is also possible to follow the evolution of certain components of the computed fields during the Newton iterations and to display them in the convergence table. This feature can be activated in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword SUIVI_DDL.

### 3.6 Display of information and performance

A great deal of information is displayed by default in the message and result files (.mess and .resu).

An essential piece of information is the convergence table displayed at each computation time instant. This table is very useful and makes it possible in particular to identify certain convergence anomalies.

The operator STAT_NON_LINE displays the size of the matrix (number of equations of the system).

Depending on the size of the matrix, the use of an iterative solver (such as PETSC) will allow a gain in time. For information, an iterative solver is considered to be faster than a direct solver (the default) from about 200000 equations in 3D. The counterpart of the use of an iterative solver is that robustness is not guaranteed. It should be noted that the use of PETSC requires an MPI version of *Code_Aster* (even if only one processor is used).

The use of parallelism can also be effective if the time spent in STAT_NON_LINE is predominant with respect to the total time of the run. For this, it is sufficient to choose a suitable solver (example: MUMPS, PETSC), an MPI version of *Code_Aster* and to specify the number of processes in Astk. A great deal of advice on parallelism is given in the document [U2.08.06](doc:u2.08.06). Finer information on the times spent in each part of the solution is also displayed, and can serve to parameterise the run better (time, memory, number of processors...). For this, the reader is referred to the documentation [U1.03.03](doc:u1.03.03).

## 4 Material non-linearity

This part deals with the problems most frequently encountered when using non-linear constitutive laws.

### 4.1 Parameters linked to the solution of the constitutive behaviour

Most of the constitutive laws of *Code_Aster* are integrated analytically. In the event of an error in the integration of the constitutive law, there is therefore no numerical parameter on which one can act. The usual solution consists in re-cutting the global time step (or in choosing a finer list of time instants).

Certain constitutive laws are solved locally via an iterative numerical method, for which the user can choose the maximum residual allowed and the maximum number of local iterations. Most of the time, the default parameters (RESI_INTE_RELA = ${10}^{-6}$ and ITER_INTE_MAXI = 20) are sufficient. In the event of an error in the integration of the law, one can, as previously, subdivide the time step, but also allow additional local iterations (ITER_INTE_MAXI). For example, for MONOCRISTAL, it is common to allow 100 or 200 local iterations. The choice of the residual is more delicate. Indeed, it is important that the constitutive behaviour be integrated correctly. This means that it is imperative not to increase the residual beyond the default value (${10}^{-4}$ for example), except for the constitutive behaviours integrated by an explicit algorithm. On the other hand, it is sometimes advisable to tighten the residual (for example RESI_INTE_RELA = ${10}^{-10}$). In fact, depending on the way in which the constitutive behaviour is implemented, the residual does not have the same physical meaning (stress, strain...). One must then refer to the reference documentation of the constitutive law in question.

### 4.2 Management of plastic incompressibility

If the material is incompressible ($\nu >0.45$) or in the case of large plastic strains, oscillations in the stresses or in the trace of the stresses may appear. Certain special finite elements, such as the under-integrated elements or the mixed elements, make it possible to treat these problems [U2.01.10](doc:u2.01.10). If the use of under-integrated elements does not settle the problem, then the use of the 2-field mixed formulation (displacement and pressure) in small strains, and of the 3-field mixed formulation in large strains, is advised. Note that the latter works only on quadratic meshes.

### 4.3 Unloading

In the case of unloading, convergence problems of Newton's algorithm may be encountered. It is advisable to use the elastic prediction (in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword NEWTON). It is also useful to activate the subdivision of the time step in the event of a singular matrix (this is automatic in version 11, but it must be activated by hand in the previous versions *via* STOP_SINGULIER = 'DECOUPE' in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword SOLVEUR).

### 4.4 Plane stresses

The treatment of the plane-stress condition is carried out in the general framework by the Deborst method. It is advisable to update the tangent matrix often (every one to three Newton iterations). In certain cases, convergence is reached for Newton's algorithm, but not for the verification of the plane-stress state, which leads to additional iterations, or even to an excessive re-cutting of the time step. It is then advisable to activate an additional loop in order to satisfy the plane stresses better during the Newton iterations: ITER_CPLAN_MAXI must be chosen at least equal to 5 [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11).

### 4.5 Damage (softening problems)

For the treatment of damage problems, a great deal of advice is given in [U2.05.06](doc:u2.05.06). In general, it is advisable to use piloting, possibly together with the mixed line search.

For certain constitutive laws (ENDO_FRAGILE and ENDO_ISOT_BETON), the IMPLEX method is proposed as an alternative to Newton's method. This method is activated in STAT_NON_LINE under the keyword NEWTON. This method is based on an explicit extrapolation of the internal variables in order to determine the displacements from which the constitutive behaviour is integrated implicitly. The vanishing of equilibrium is not verified. Because of this, it introduces an approximation of the solution but makes it possible to guarantee the robustness of the analysis. An error control and an optimisation of the time steps are possible via the operator DEFI_LIST_INST, by choosing an automatic management of the time step and a mode of computation of the time steps specific to IMPLEX. From a practical point of view, the method imposes an update of the matrix at each increment and a single iteration. Before any use of the IMPLEX method, the document [R5.03.81](doc:r5.03.81) must imperatively be consulted.

### 4.6 Thermo-hydro-mechanical (THM)

THM problems involve quite particular notions. It is advisable to consult the documentation [U2.04.05](doc:u2.04.05). Some general advice for the solution is given here. The initialisation of the fields is a delicate stage to which close attention must be paid (ETAT_INIT). It is necessary to use the updated tangent matrix. In the event of a convergence problem, it can be very useful to activate the line search (mixed, preferably). The line search does not, however, systematically improve convergence; it must therefore be handled with caution, inasmuch as it can increase the CPU cost.

## 5 Geometric non-linearity

This part deals with the problems most frequently encountered during analyses with geometric non-linearities.

### 5.1 Buckling

The operator STAT_NON_LINE makes it possible to compute a stability criterion *via* the keyword CRIT_STAB=_F (TYPE='FLAMBEMENT'). The user's guide for buckling [U2.08.04](doc:u2.08.04) gives a great deal of advice on setting up a stability analysis. Some general advice is given here.

In general, the stability analysis is conducted on updated stiffness matrices. In practice, the calls to CRIT_STAB must be limited for reasons of computation cost, by limiting the time instants on which the stability analysis is carried out. In addition, it is judicious to use CRIT_STAB only on the time intervals where the possibility of instabilities is suspected. The time step should also be refined properly on the approach to this zone. By default, 3 critical loads are computed (NB_FREQ). Often the first one may suffice. One particular point linked to instability is the choice of the piloting technique of the algorithm. Indeed, classical piloting in force is not suitable. On the approach to a limit point, the load increment must be reduced and the maximum number of iterations increased. It is also advisable to use arc-length piloting.

### 5.2 Large strains

The keyword DEFORMATION under COMPORTEMENT makes it possible to define the assumptions used for the computation of the strains. By default, small displacements and small strains are considered (DEFORMATION = 'PETIT'). This means that one remains within the small-perturbation hypothesis: small displacements, small rotations, small strains (lower than about 5%). When this hypothesis is no longer verified, the strain model must be changed. For slender structures (shells, plates, beams), it frequently happens that one is in large displacements and large rotations but small strains. One then uses DEFORMATION = 'GROT_GDEP'.

The treatment of large strains differs according to the type of element and the constitutive law. The large-strain model of Simo and Miehe (DEFORMATION = 'SIMO_MIEHE') is advised for the constitutive relations VMIS_ISOT_LINE, VMIS_ISOT_TRAC, ROUSSELIER and all the constitutive behaviours with isotropic hardening only, associated with a material undergoing metallurgical phase changes (relations META_X_IL_XXX_XXX and META_X_INL_XXX_XXX). For the other constitutive laws, the large-strain model of Miehe and Apel (DEFORMATION = 'GDEF_LOG') is advised, theoretically applicable to any constitutive law for the 3D and 2D modellings. More detailed explanations are to be found in the user's guide for the non-linear constitutive behaviours [U4.51.11](doc:u4.51.11), in the paragraph DEFORMATION.

## 6 Frictional-contact non-linearity

The definition of the frictional-contact load is the subject of a specific operator: DEFI_CONTACT [U4.44.11](doc:u4.44.11). Besides the choice of the entities in contact, this operator makes it possible to choose the type of formulation of the contact problem (discrete or continuous), parameters for the pairing operation and parameters for the solution phase. A great deal of advice on data setup is available in the document [U2.04.04](doc:u2.04.04), in particular on the choice of the master and slave surfaces.

Taking the contact conditions into account, with or without friction, leads to non-linear problems, which can be a source of difficulties in the solution of STAT_NON_LINE. One of the first difficulties is the blocking of the rigid-body modes. Indeed, the frictional-contact conditions do not take part in the blocking of the rigid modes. The rigid modes must therefore be blocked without taking the contact conditions into account (apart from rare exceptions). A poor blocking of the rigid-body modes leads to the appearance of zero pivots during the factorisation of the matrix. Advice is given in the documentation [U2.04.04](doc:u2.04.04). Caution: certain contact formulations require a particular solver. In particular, in the discrete formulation, the CONTRAINTE method requires a direct solver (MULT_FRONT or MUMPS). In general, the use of the line search with contact is not advised. In the event of coupling with other non-linearities, it is advisable to make sure that each non-linearity taken separately converges. If problems appear only when the non-linearities are coupled, it is advisable to work with the updated tangent matrix and an elastic prediction.

A comparative benchmark between *Code_Aster* and other commercial software packages was carried out in 2010 on 5 frictional-contact problems [2]. This benchmark made it possible in particular to bring to light oscillations of the contact pressure for the continuous formulation in the case of incompatible meshes, a defect from which the commercial software packages used also suffer. It is then recommended to extract the contact pressures from the stresses on the boundary, or else to use compatible meshes.
