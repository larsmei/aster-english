# v4.05.305 TPLP305 – Temperature bar with X-FEM adiabatic interface

**Summary:**

This test is the stationary linear thermal equivalent of the X-FEM mechanical SSNV173 [V6.04.173](doc:v6.04.173) test. The objective of this test is to validate two aspects of the elementary calculation as part of the thermal problem resolution with X-FEM [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12):

- integration of a discontinuous quantity through sub-cutting of the element
- the enrichment of form functions by the Heaviside function

A parallelepipedic bar is considered to be crossed by an adiabatic interface, both parts of the bar

Thus, each of these parts is subjected to a temperature imposed on one of its edges.

All elements X-FEM Heaviside enrichment thermals are tested, the results are compared to an analytical solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is a analytical solution.

Since the interface is adiabatic, two thermally insulated solids are on both sides of the interface. Each solid sees a temperature imposed on a part of its edge, and a zero flow on the rest of its edge. The problem being linear and stationary, the temperature is constant in each part of the bar (and equal to the corresponding imposed temperature), and discontinus through the interface:

en 3D: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}T(x,y,z)=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}=10°C,\text{}\forall \text{}(x,y,z)\text{}\in \text{}\left[\frac{-\mathit{Lx}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Lx}}{2}\right]\times \left[\frac{-\mathit{Ly}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Ly}}{2}\right]\times \left[\frac{-\mathit{Lz}}{2},0\right[\\ T(x,y,z)=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}=20°C,\text{}\forall \text{}(x,y,z)\text{}\in \text{}\left[\frac{-\mathit{Lx}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Lx}}{2}\right]\times \left[\frac{-\mathit{Ly}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Ly}}{2}\right]\times \left]0,\frac{\mathit{Lz}}{2}\right]\end{array}$

en 2D: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}T(x,y)=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}=10°C,\text{}\forall \text{}(x,y)\text{}\in \text{}\left[\frac{-\mathit{Lx}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Lx}}{2}\right]\times \left[\frac{-\mathit{Ly}}{2},0\right[\\ T(x,y)=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}=20°C,\text{}\forall \text{}(x,y)\text{}\in \text{}\left[\frac{-\mathit{Lx}}{2},\frac{\mathit{Lx}}{2}\right]\times \left]0,\frac{\mathit{Ly}}{2}\right]\end{array}$

In the modellings presented in the following paragraphs, the temperature field approximation is enriched by a Heaviside function to represent the introduced discontinuity. nodes with support through the interface carry "classical" and "Heaviside" degrees of freedoms: then, they cannot be compared directly with the analytical values obtained above.

In order to test the values of these degrees of freedom, one can set up in case the mesh of the bar is made up of 5 regular $\mathrm{1m}$ side hexahedrons, so the central mesh cell is cut in its middle by the interface. The solution being constant in the $x$ and $y$ directions, one can simply consider the equivalent one-dimensional element represented in Figure .

![Figure](/figures/v4.05.305/10000000000000B1000001081D60EC30015ED6DA.png)

Figure 2.1-1: Élément linéaire 1D équivalent

$\text{N1}$ and $\text{N2}$ are the two nodes of this element, ${\phi }_{1}$ and ${\varphi}_{2}$ associated shape functions, $({T}_{1}^{C},{T}_{1}^{H})$ and $({T}_{2}^{C},{T}_{2}^{H})$ associated degrees of freedom couples. $x$ is also noted as the space variable, $\text{A}$ as the point as the $x={0}^{+}$ and $\text{B}$ as the point as the $x={0}^{-}$. The Heaviside function characterizes either the $x<0$ domain (for the node $\text{N1}$), or the $x>0$ domain (for the node $\text{N2}$). ${\chi }_{-}$ is the characteristic $x<0$ domain function and ${\chi }_{+}$ as the characteristic $x>0$ domain function.

In all point $x$ of the element, the temperature field is expressed by the following relationship:

$T(x)={\phi }_{1}(x){T}_{1}^{C}+{\phi }_{2}(x){T}_{2}^{C}-2{\chi }_{-}(x){\phi }_{1}(x){T}_{1}^{H}+2{\chi }_{+}(x){\phi }_{2}(x){T}_{2}^{H}$

*Note*: the multiplier coefficients "-2" and "+2" are introduced to meet the convention of the definition of enrichment (or even [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12)).

At $\text{N1}$ , $\text{N2}$ , $\text{A}$ and $\text{B}$ points we have:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\text{En N1 :}{\chi }_{+}(x)=1,{\chi }_{-}(x)=0,{\phi }_{1}(x)=1,{\phi }_{2}(x)=0\\ \text{En A :}{\chi }_{+}(x)=1,{\chi }_{-}(x)=0,{\phi }_{1}(x)={\phi }_{2}(x)=1/2\\ \text{En B :}{\chi }_{+}(x)=0,{\chi }_{-}(x)=1,{\phi }_{1}(x)={\phi }_{2}(x)=1/2\\ \text{En N2 :}{\chi }_{+}(x)=0,{\chi }_{-}(x)=1,{\phi }_{1}(x)=0,{\phi }_{2}(x)=1\end{array}$

what leads to the linear system:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}_{1}^{C}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}\text{(N1)}\\ \frac{1}{2}{T}_{1}^{C}+\frac{1}{2}{T}_{2}^{C}+{T}_{2}^{H}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}\text{(A)}\\ \frac{1}{2}{T}_{1}^{C}-{T}_{1}^{H}+\frac{1}{2}{T}_{2}^{C}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}\text{(B)}\\ {T}_{2}^{C}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}\text{(N2)}\end{array}$

admitting the following solution:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}_{1}^{C}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}\\ {T}_{2}^{C}=\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}\\ {T}_{1}^{H}={T}_{2}^{H}=\frac{\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\sup}-\text{}\stackrel{̄}{T}{\rbrace ^{\inf}}{2}\end{array}$ ,

of which digital application amounts to imposing: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{T}_{1}^{C}=20°C\\ {T}_{2}^{C}=10°C\\ {T}_{1}^{H}={T}_{2}^{H}=5°C\end{array}$

| **Identification** | **Reference** |
| --- | --- |
| TEMP on Gauss points below the interface | $10.°C$ |
| TEMP on Gauss points above the interface | $20.°C$ |

**Note:** elements X-FEM contains a significant number of integration points (which can be 480 for thermal elements based on mesh cell HEXA8) due to the sub-elements cutting. Furthermore, as the crack position conditions the result of the sub-cutting procedure, this number is variable for the same element type. Therefore, the TEMP component of the TEMP_ELGA field is not tested at all the elements X-FEM integration points, and it is only tested:

- *in one point integration for enriched elements not being crossed by the interface (the value being constant on the element)*
- *in two integration points for elements enriched through the interface (the first below the interface, the second above), only for modellings A, F, and I (mesh set to **HEXA8 or*QUAD4)*

## 7 Modelling E
