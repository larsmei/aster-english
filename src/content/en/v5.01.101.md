# v5.01.101 SDND101 - Drop a spring mass system with impact

**Summary**

This problem corresponds to a transient analysis by modal recombination of a nonlinear discrete system to an degree of freedom. Nonlinearity consists of a contact with impact on a rigid plane. The mass is launched with a nonzero initial speed against the obstacle. The initial game between the material point and the obstacle is zero. This problem allows testing the impact forces post-processing: impact speed, impact time ...

### 1.2 Material properties

The system consists of a mass m and a spring of stiffness $k$ . The impact stop has an stiffness equal to ${K}_{\mathrm{choc}}$ .

| Mass | $m=100\mathrm{kg}$ |
| --- | --- |
| stiffness | $k={10}^{4}N/m$ |
| Normal shock stiffness | ${K}_{\mathrm{choc}}={10}^{6}N/m$ |

### 1.3 Initial conditions

The system is initially in a stand-down position (${U}_{0}=0$) and has an initial speed $\dot{{U}_{0}}>0$ . An initial speed $\dot{{U}_{0}}=1m/s$ will be selected for the application.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

During the impact phase, the system is solution of the differential equation:

$m.\ddot{u}+\mathit{k.u}+{K}_{c}<u{>}^{+}=0$ with ${u}_{0}=0$ and $\dot{{u}_{0}}=\dot{{U}_{0}}$ .

$<x{>}^{+}$ is the positive value of $x$ .

The analytical solution of this problem is:

$u=\frac{\dot{{U}_{0}}}{{\omega}_{c}}\sin({\omega}_{c}t)$ où ${\omega}_{c}=\sqrt{\frac{k+{K}_{c}}{m}}$ .

Speed cancels for ${t}_{\dot{u}=0}=\frac{\pi}{2{\omega}_{c}}$.

The impact force is then maximum and is ${F}_{\max}={K}_{c}u({t}_{\dot{u}=0})={K}_{c}\frac{\dot{{U}_{0}}}{{\omega}_{c}}$.

By construction, the duration of the shock is ${T}_{\mathrm{choc}}=2{t}_{\dot{u}=0}$.

The system returns to the $u=0$ position with the $-\dot{{U}_{0}}$ speed.

In the $u<0$ domain the system has for $\mathrm{m.}\ddot{u}+\mathrm{k.u}=0$ equation with for initial conditions ${u}_{1}=0$ and $\dot{{u}_{1}}=-\dot{{U}_{0}}$ .

Its solution is $u=-\frac{\dot{{U}_{0}}}{{\omega}_{0}}\sin({\omega}_{0}t')$ where ${\omega}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$ .

Speed cancels for: $t{'}_{\dot{u}=0}=\frac{\pi}{2{\omega}_{0}}$ .

By construction, free flight time is: ${T}_{\mathrm{vol}}=2t{'}_{\dot{u}=0}$ .

The system is therefore periodic with alternatively a ${T}_{\mathrm{choc}}$ phase of shock time where the system describes a sinus arch in the $u>0$ domain and a ${T}_{\mathrm{vol}}$ phase of free flight where the system describes a sinus arch in the $u<0$ domain.

The pulse at each impact is: $I=\underset{0}{\overset{{T}_{\mathrm{choc}}}{\int}}{K}_{c}u(t)\mathrm{dt}=2{K}_{c}\frac{\dot{{U}_{0}}}{{\omega}_{c}^{2}}=\frac{2m\dot{{U}_{0}}}{1+\frac{k}{{K}_{c}}}$ .

### 2.2 Baseline

The reference results are the values of the maximum force moments, the maximum force value, the duration of the impact time, the value of the pulse and the impact speed and the number of elementary impacts for the first two oscillations of the system.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The mass-spring system is modeled by element type POI1 to node $\mathrm{NO1}$ . It is subject to $x$ axis movement . The node $\mathrm{NO1}$ is positioned in $O=(0.0.0.)$ .

A PLAN_Z (two parallel planes separated by a game) obstacle is used to simulate any shock of the mass-spring system against a rigid plane. It is chosen to take the $\mathrm{Oy}$ axis for normal at the crash plane, i.e. NORM_OBST: (0., 1., 0.). To avoid being hindered by the rebound of the oscillator on the symmetrical plane, it is pushed back very far (see [Figure 3.1-a]). Therefore, it is chosen to locate the origin of the obstacle in ORIG_OBS: (-1. 0. 0.).

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.101/10000DB6000069D500002E9D0A911E9037675389.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Modeled Geometry**

The $\mathrm{JEU}$ parameter is still to be set, which gives the half-range between the plans in contact. Here we want a zero real game, where $\mathrm{JEU}:1$. If we want a real game of $j$, we need to impose $\mathrm{JEU}:1+j$ in the case of the figure presented.

Time integration is performed with the algorithm d-Euler and a time step of ${5.10}^{-4}s$. All calculation steps are archived. ${\xi}_{i}$ reduced depreciation is considered to be zero for all calculated modes.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh consists of an node and an mesh cell of the POI1 type.

## 4 Summary of the results

The quantities that is less well represented are the shock time and the shock moment (although better than $\text{1\%}$). This problem is not related to the accuracy of the calculation but only to the fact that an ${5.10}^{-4}s$ integration time step was chosen which over such short periods as $0,03s$ already produces a temporal impreciseness of $\text{1,66\%}$. To complete this synthesis, a convergence test could be performed by reducing the calculation step.
