# v8.01.111 FDLV111 - Absorption of a pressure wave in a fluid column

**Summary:**

The elements fluid-order 1 paraaxials are tested to apply absorbent conditions at the border of a finished mesh elements to simulate infinity in direct transient calculations.

They are used to model an infinite fluid column, in 3D or 2D, in which a pressure wave is created using a piston. An interest is the non-reflection of the wave at the "infinite" end of the column.

The two direct transient operators of *Code_Aster* , DYNA_VIBRA and DYNA_NON_LINE, are tested successively.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The system considered in the 3D case is that of a column of fluid with a square cross section and a piston of the same section animated by a rigid body movement according to the axis of the column. The lateral surface of the column is made up of a rigid, still guide. The absorbent elementss are placed on the opposite side of the piston to simulate the infinite character of the column in this direction. In the 2D case, the principle is identical with a column and piston supposed to be very wide, with only a vertical section Modelling (see diagram).

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The piston is moved zero at the initial moment and the fluid is at rest.

## 2 reference solution

The solution must show the absorption of an acoustic wave by the absorbent surface. The piston movement is a uniform translation according to the $x$ axis. Given the symmetry of the problem around this axis, we will obtain an identical pressure field in all $x=\mathrm{Cte}$ planes. Moreover, the absorbent boundary is orthogonal at this axis. Therefore, we study the absorption of flat pressure waves under normal incidence. The theory [bib1] says that with a fluid order 1 paraaxial boundary, this absorption is perfect. This is what we must check with this reference solution.

Thus, by observing the evolution of the pressure in a given point of the mesh, we will focus on finding in the signal obtained the duration of the excitement and the return to rest after the passage of the wave, characteristic of its absorption.

### 2.1 Baseline

This paragraph gives the results obtained with *Code_Aster* in this configuration. We check that they are satisfactory and take them as a reference for the future.

They concern, for the 3D case, the pressure evolution in a point of the fluid located at $150m$ of the piston in the $x$ direction and in the centre of the section in the $yz$ plane. For the 2D case, the point is located at $40m$ of the piston in $x$ direction and in the middle of the section in the $y$ direction (in 2D, a shorter and refined mesh is taken).

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.111/100002000000017600000111A14690793407CA09.png)

![Figure](/figures/v8.01.111/100002000000017F00000111D52AAFEE50F1E9E0.png)

As expected, the width of the signal measured in both cases is identical to that of the excitation function. Physically, the compression due to the piston's advance is observed, then the depression corresponding to its retreat to its initial position. It is also clear that the return to rest immediately after the wave passage and the absence of a reflected signal at the end of the mesh are noted.

### 2.2 Uncertainties

This is a numerical study result. Qualitative predictions are found. Numerical values are related to the accuracy of the calculation. Only the return to rest is clearly given by the analysis.

### 2.3 Bibliographical references

## 5 Summary of the results

We find by calculation with the two modellings qualitatively, the maximum pressure at the right moments and the return to rest after the wave passage.

The results obtained with the DYNA_VIBRA and DYNA_NON_LINE operators are very similar. The difference is that each time step has the balance state of the second member's effort with the DYNA_NON_LINE operator. However, this difference is minimal, as the time step used with DYNA_VIBRA is small enough.
