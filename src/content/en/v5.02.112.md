# v5.02.112 SDNL112 - Vibration damage to a steam generator hanger

**Summary**

This validation case is intended to verify the non-regression of the functions required for the vibrational damage calculations of the steam generator tubes: vibration fatigue and shock wear on an obstacle. The calculation of the vibration response is carried out by the ITMI method of Temporary Integration by Integral Method implanted in the DYNA_VIBRA operator. To this end, the nonlinear dynamic response of a steam generator tube subjected to external flow is studied. This tube is unstable and comes to impact a game holder.

The effects of fluid-elastic coupling (variation in frequency and damping of the structure) are calculated primarily as a function of the flow rate, then the vibrational response of the structure at a given flow rate, and finally the impact wear or vibrational fatigue of the structure.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure studied is similar to the curved area of a steam generator tube. It is assumed to be clamped at both ends $A$ and $C$; these are supposed to correspond to the passage of the tube in the top interlock plate of a steam generator. At the point $B$ top of the hanger (also called apex), the structure is guided in a game support.

| **External fluid:** | The tube is assumed to be immersed in a diphasic mixture over its entire length; the flow of the mixture is transverse to the hanger in all point. The equivalent density of the mixture is obtained by using the formula: |

${\rho}_{e}=\alpha {\rho}_{\mathit{gaz}}+(1-\alpha ){\rho}_{\mathit{liquide}}$ , where $\alpha$ is the volumetric gas ratio.

This density appears to be between $84\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ and $150\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ . An equivalent density is attributed to the dynamic system when calculating its modal base in fluid at rest; this equivalent density includes the density of the internal fluid, that of the structure and that of the external fluid; the inertial effect of the latter is evaluated by means of an added mass coefficient.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The structure is clamped at $A$ and $C$ points. A game support is positioned at point $B$ ($\mathrm{jeu}=\mathrm{1,20 }\mathrm{mm}$). A random load, distributed, transverse to the tube, is imposed on $(A-C)$. This load is defined, on the one hand by using a speed profile along the excited area, and on the other hand by using an adimensional spectrum of excitement.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The tube is initially at rest, resulting in zero displacements and speeds at the initial moment.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The geometric and material properties of the model are those described above. The tube is modelled using Timoshenko's straight beam elements: POU_D_T. It is broken down into 60 elements over six sections.

Nodes $A$ and $C$ are blocked in the $x$ , $y$ and $z$ directions in translation and rotation.

The damping of the fluid structure at rest is 0.4999 %.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The total number of nodes used for this mesh is 61.

The mesh cells are 60 and SEG2 type.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 3.3 Steps of calculation

The flow velocity profile and parameters taking into account fluid-structure coupling are defined using DEFI_FONC_FLUI and DEFI_FLUI_STRU operators. Modal parameters of the structure are calculated by taking into account the fluid-elastic forces using CALC_FLUI_STRU operator. The definition of random excitation is performed by using DEFI_SPEC_TURB operator. The excitation is projected on the modal basis using PROJ_SPEC_BASE operator. Modal response interspectrums are calculated using DYNA_SPEC_MODAL operator. The self-sensing spectrums of nodes are derived from this using REST_SPEC_PHYS operator.

## 4 Modelling results A

### 4.1 Test values

The tests provide the non-regression of the code and cover eigenfrequencies, reduced damping and the RMS on the move.

### 4.2 Remarks

The calculation is performed with 4 modes and a single flow speed of $4.811m/s$.

## 5 Modelling B

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The geometric and material properties of the model are those described above. The tube is modelled using Timoshenko's straight beam elements: POU_D_T. It is broken down into 60 elements over six sections.

Nodes $A$ and $C$ are blocked in the $X$ , $Y$ and $Z$ directions in translation and rotation.

The fluid damping at rest of the structure is 0.4999 %.

For the calculation of damage, the following fatigue curve (Wöhler type) is considered, giving the half stress amplitude as a function of the number of cycles:

Note that this curve is not the actual fatigue curve for the material under consideration.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

The total number of nodes used for this mesh is 61.

The mesh cells are 60 and SEG2 type.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 5.3 Steps of calculation

The flow velocity profile and parameters taking into account fluid-structure coupling are defined using FONC_FLUI_STRU and DEFI_FLUI_STRU operators.

- The modal parameters of the resting water structure are calculated using the CALC_MODES operator.
- Modal parameters of the structure are calculated by taking into account the fluid-elastic forces using the CALC_FLUI_STRU operator.
- The definition of random excitation is performed using the DEFI_SPEC_TURB operator. The excitation is projected on the modal basis using the PROJ_SPEC_BASE operator.
- Random excitation is calculated over a specified period, using the GENE_FONC_ALEA operator.
- For temporal calculation, excitation functions are recovered and the time integration method is used by the integral method installed in the DYNA_VIBRA operator. This calculation allows the nonlinear dynamic response of a steam generator tube subjected to external flow to be studied.
- The post-processing operator used to evaluate tube fatigue is then POST_FATIGUE.

## 6 Modelling results B

### 6.1 Test values

The tests ensure the non-regression of the code and cover the movements and the cumulation of damage.

### 6.2 Remarks

The calculation is performed with 4 modes over a total simulation time of 1 second. It is performed for a single flow speed ($\mathrm{4,811 }m/s$ ) by direct integration with the integral method. The tested values are the damage and the RMS interval of displacement for 1 and 100 cycles at the node of shock. The calculation time and the number of drawings allowing to define the excitation are too small to obtain a suitable statistical average representation of the random phenomenon. Therefore, this test case is only one test case of non-regression. To obtain more representative average values, it is sufficient to extend the calculation time and the number of drawings.

## 9 Conclusion

The sole purpose of this test case is to verify the non-regression of the GEVIBUS- *Code_Aster* calculation chains when using the DYNA_VIBRA operator jointly with the ITMI method of full-method time integration and post- fatigue and wear analysis operators. In view of the results obtained, the non-regression of *Code_Aster* can be considered to be provided for the moment.
