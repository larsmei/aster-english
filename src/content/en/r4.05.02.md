# r4.05.02 Stochastic approach for seismic analysis

**Summary:**

This document presents a probabilistic analysis method for determining the response of a structure subjected to a random excitation of seismic type, from the cross-spectra of the excitation at the support points of the structure. The response is itself expressed in the form of cross-spectra.

## 1 Introduction

Classically, the response of a structure subjected to a seismic excitation can be computed by two approaches:

- transient dynamic analysis if the excitation is defined by an accelerogram (cf. [R4.05.01]).
- analysis by the classical spectral method if the excitation is defined by an oscillator response spectrum (SRO) (cf. [R4.05.03](doc:r4.05.03)).

Now, a seismic excitation is random by nature. These two methods are not initially designed to take this into account: in one case, many time analyses must be repeated for different excitations and then a statistical average taken (significant analysis cost); in the other case, very conservative hypotheses are made by considering averages (of simple or complete quadratic type, for example) for the maximum of the responses.

A probabilistic analysis method has therefore been developed, also called the "stochastic approach to seismic analysis", based on the computation of the dynamic response expressed as power cross-spectra from the power spectral densities of the excitation. This method has in particular the advantage of taking better account of the correlations between the excitations at the various supports of the structure.

The discussion of the various advantages of this method can be pursued in the reference [bib1].

The principle of the method and the notation adopted are therefore presented, starting from the classical approaches, then, in the third part, the probabilistic analysis proper.

Finally, in the fourth part, the various methods for obtaining the exciting cross-spectrum will be presented.

## 2 Principle of the approach

### 2.1 Statement of the problem considered and general principle

We place ourselves in the case of a **multi-supported structure**, that is to say that the structure has $m$ support degrees of freedom, each being subjected to its own excitation (not necessarily equal everywhere). It is assumed that the structure is represented by a finite-element model comprising $n$ degrees of freedom. The response is sought at a finite (and small) number of $l$ degrees of freedom.

It is assumed that the **excitation quantity is of imposed-motion type** and is expressed by a family of accelerograms ${\mathrm{g}}_{j}(\mathrm{t})$ for each of the support degrees of freedom $j$ , $j=1,m$ .

The absolute motion of the structure is **split** classically into a **rigid-body motion and a relative motion.**

The computation of the response in power cross-spectra is carried out by **modal recombination**.

Following this modal analysis, a random dynamic response analysis is split into three parts:

- definition of the exciting power cross-spectrum,
- computation of the response power cross-spectrum.

These first two parts are the subject of the command DYNA_ALEA_MODAL [U4.53.22](doc:u4.53.22).

The restitution of the response power cross-spectrum on the physical basis is carried out with the command REST_SPEC_PHYS [U4.63.22](doc:u4.63.22).

- computation of statistical parameters from the resulting power cross-spectrum.

This last step is treated by the command POST_DYNA_ALEA [R7.10.01](doc:r7.10.01) [U4.84.04](doc:u4.84.04).

### 2.2 Decomposition of the motion

The following decompositions and projections are detailed in the reference documentation relating to the solution by transient analysis of a seismic analysis [R4.05.01](doc:r4.05.01). Only the main lines are retained here.

Let ${X}_{a}$ be the absolute displacement vector (of dimension $n$ ) of all the degrees of freedom of the structure.

The total response, called **absolute** ${X}_{a}$ , of the structure is expressed as the sum of a **relative** contribution ${X}_{r}$ and of the **entrainment** contribution ${X}_{e}$ due to the anchor displacements (subjected to accelerations represented by an accelerogram ${g}_{j}(t)$ at each of the support degrees of freedom $j$ , $j=1,m$ ).

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.02/Object_6.svg)

Let $M,K$ and $C$ be the mass, stiffness and damping matrices of the problem, restricted to the unsupported degrees of freedom.

The equation of motion is then written in the frame attached to the relative motion:

$\begin{array}{c}{{\begin{array}{}\text{M}\ddot{X}\end{array}}_{r}(t)+\text{C {}\dot{X}}_{r}(t)+{\text{K X}}_{r}(t)\text{= -}\text{M}\ddot{{X}_{e}}(t)+{F}_{\text{ext}}\end{array}$

${F}_{\text{ext}}$ : vector of the external forces

In general the external forces are zero during an analysis of seismic responses.

### 2.3 Decomposition on the modal basis

The computation of the response in power cross-spectra is carried out by **modal recombination** and calls, for imposed motion, on a modal basis which comprises both dynamic modes and static modes.

Let $\Phi =\left\lbrace {\phi }_{i,i=1,n}\right\rbrace$ be the $(n,n)$ matrix of the dynamic modes computed for the associated conservative system, keeping the m supports fixed.

Let $\Psi =\left\lbrace {\psi}_{j,j=1,m}\right\rbrace$ be the $(n,m)$ matrix of the static modes. The mode ${\Psi}_{j}$ corresponds to the deformed shape of the structure under a unit displacement imposed on the support degree of freedom $j$ , the other support degrees of freedom being fixed.

The imposed displacement of the anchors ${X}_{s}(t)$ is related to ${X}_{e}(t)$ by the relation: ${X}_{e}(t)=\Psi {X}_{s}(t)$ .

The components of the acceleration of the anchor points ${\ddot{X}}_{s}(t)$ are the accelerograms ${g}_{j}(t)$ , $j=1,m$ .

One can therefore write $\ddot{{X}_{e}}(t)={\Psi \ddot{X}}_{s}(t)=\sum_{j\text{=1}}^{m}{\psi}_{j}{g}_{j}(t)\text{}$ .

The change of variable ${X}_{r}(t)=\Phi .q(t)$ is carried out, $q(t)$ being the vector of the generalised coordinates. By premultiplying the equation of motion by ${}^{T}\text{}\Phi$ , one obtains — in the absence of external forces other than the seismic excitation — the equation projected onto the basis of the dynamic modes:

${}^{T}\text{}\Phi M\Phi \ddot{q}(t)+{}^{T}\text{}\Phi C\Phi \dot{q}(t)+{}^{T}\text{}\Phi K\Phi q(t)=-{}^{T}\text{}\Phi M\Phi {\ddot{X}}_{s}(t)$

It is assumed that the damping matrix is a linear combination of the mass and stiffness matrices (hypothesis of Rayleigh damping constant over the structure, or Basile hypothesis allowing a diagonal damping). The basis $\Phi$ which orthogonalises the matrices $M$ and $K$ therefore also orthogonalises the matrix $C$ .

Taking this hypothesis into account, the previous equation splits into $n$ decoupled scalar equations in the form:

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.02/Object_22.svg)

for $i=1,n$

where one has denoted:

${\mu}_{i}={}^{T}\text{}{\Phi}_{i}M{\Phi}_{i}$ the modal mass

${k}_{i}={}^{T}\text{}{\Phi}_{i}K{\Phi}_{i}$ the modal stiffness

${\omega}_{i}=\sqrt{\frac{{k}_{i}}{{\mu}_{i}}}$ the modal angular frequency

${\xi}_{i}=\frac{{}^{T}\text{}{\Phi}_{i}.C.{\Phi}_{i}}{2{\mu}_{i}{\omega}_{i}}$ the reduced modal damping"

${p}_{ij}=\frac{{}^{T}\text{}{\Phi}_{i}.M.{\Psi}_{j}}{{\mu}_{i}}$ the modal participation factor of the support $j$ on the dynamic mode $i$ .

The solution ${q}_{i}(t)$ of this equation corresponds to the response of the dynamic mode $i$ to the whole of the seismic excitation.

The problem can further be decomposed by introducing the unknown ${d}_{ij}(t)$ solution of the differential equation: ${\ddot{\mathrm{d}}}_{ij}+2{{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}\dot{\mathrm{d}}}_{ij}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}{\mathrm{d}}_{ij}={g}_{j}(t)$ , this last equation corresponding to the response of the dynamic mode $i$ to the acceleration

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.02/Object_28.svg)

. The relative displacement on the physical basis is then expressed:

${X}_{r}(t)=-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{p}_{ij}{d}_{ij}(t){\varphi}_{i}$

The information on the position of the support point is contained in the modal participation factor.

### 2.4 Harmonic response

The total response of the structure has therefore been decomposed into a relative contribution and a differential contribution due to the displacements of the anchors, such that:

$\begin{array}{}{X}_{a}(t)={X}_{r}(t)+{X}_{e}(t)\\ \text{avec}\\ \lbrace \begin{array}{}{\ddot{X}}_{e}(t)={\Psi \ddot{X}}_{s}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{\psi}_{j}{g}_{j}(t)\\ {X}_{r}(t)\text{= -}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{{p}_{ij}d}_{ij}(t){\varphi}_{i}\text{}\text{où}{d}_{ij(t)}\text{est solution de}\text{}{\ddot{d}}_{ij}+2{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}{\dot{d}}_{ij}+{\omega}_{i}^{2}{d}_{ij}={g}_{j}(t)\end{array}\end{array}$

The solution of this last differential equation by the method of the Fourier transform involves the modal transfer functions ${h}_{i}(\omega )$ such that: . ${h}_{i}(\omega )=\frac{1}{{\omega}_{i}^{2}-{\omega}^{2}+2i{\xi}_{i}{\omega}_{i}\omega }$

One therefore obtains: ${d}_{ij}(\omega )={h}_{i}(\omega ).{g}_{j}(\omega )\text{et}{\ddot{d}}_{ij}(\omega )\text{=-}{\omega}^{2}{h}_{i}(\omega ).{g}_{j}(\omega )$

The total harmonic response of the structure is deduced from the previous formulae by modal recombination.

$\begin{array}{}{\ddot{X}}_{a}(\omega )={\ddot{X}}_{r}(\omega )+{\ddot{X}}_{e}(\omega )\\ {\ddot{X}}_{a}(\omega )={\omega}^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{m}{p}_{ij}{h}_{j}(\omega ){\varphi}_{j}(\omega ){j}_{i}+\sum_{j\text{=1}}^{m}{\Psi}_{j}{g}_{j}(\omega )\end{array}$

The complex matrix $(n,m)$ , called the transfer matrix $H(\omega )$ , is then brought out, as follows:

$H(\omega )={\omega}^{2}P.h(\omega )\Phi +\Psi$

where $P$ is the matrix of the participation factors, $h(\omega )$ the vector of the modal transfer functions ${h}_{i}(\omega )$ .

The total response of the structure is ${\ddot{X}}_{a}(\omega )=H(\omega )\ddot{E}(\omega )$ where $\ddot{E}(\omega )$ is the vector of m lines made up of the Fourier transforms of the accelerations

![Figure](/figures/r4.05.02/Object_40.svg)

at the $m$ support degrees of freedom.

It is seen that this expression determines the response in acceleration. This then requires integrating the response twice in order to obtain the displacement; this problem is presented in [bib4]. One of the additional interests of the method proposed here is to avoid this difficulty.

## 3 The random dynamic response

### 3.1 Reminder on power spectral densities [bib2]

#### 3.1.1 Definitions

Let a probabilistic signal be defined by its probability density ${p}_{x}({x}_{1},{t}_{1},\dots ,{x}_{n},{t}_{n})$ . This probability density makes it possible to compute the moment functions of the signal.

**Moment of order 1, or expectation of the signal:**

${\mu}_{X}(t)=E\left[X(t)\right]=\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}X{p}_{x}(x,t)\text{dx}$

**Moments of order 2, or cross-correlation of two signals:**

${\rho}_{\text{XY}}({t}_{1},{t}_{2})=E\left[X({t}_{1})\overline{Y({t}_{2})}\right]=\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}x\stackrel{ˉ}{y}p(x,{t}_{1};y,{t}_{2})\text{dxdy}$

When the signal is stationary, the cross-correlation depends only on $\tau ={t}_{2}-{t}_{1}$ .

It is written ${R}_{\text{XY}}(t)=E\left[X(t)\overline{Y(t-\tau )}\right]$

**Power spectral density and cross-spectrum**

${S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega )$ , the power cross-spectrum or power cross-spectral density between two stationary probabilistic signals, is defined by the Fourier transform of the cross-correlation function, which is written:

${S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{R}_{\text{XY}}(\tau ){e}^{-i\omega \tau }d\tau$

The inverse formula is written: ${R}_{\text{XY}}(t)=\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega ){e}^{i\omega \tau }d\omega$

${S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega )$ is generally complex and satisfies the symmetry relation: ${S}_{\text{YX}}(\omega )=\overline{{S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega )}$ .

When $X=Y$ , ${S}_{\text{XX}}(\omega )$ is called the **power autospectrum or power spectral density (DSP)**. This function has the property of being real and always positive.

#### 3.1.2 Relations between the DSP and the other characteristics of the signal

**Note:**

*Most of the time, the signal is defined over a limited time, its Fourier transform does not exist, and an estimated Fourier transform over a period of length* $T$ *is then defined by:*

${\stackrel{ˆ}{X}}_{T}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-T/2}{\overset{T/2}{\int}}X(t){e}^{-i\omega \tau }d\tau$ .

The following relations then hold with this estimated Fourier transform:

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{\text{XY}}(\omega )=\underset{T\to +\infty }{\lim}\frac{2\pi }{T}E\left[{\stackrel{ˆ}{X}}_{T}(w)\overline{{\stackrel{ˆ}{Y}}_{T}(\omega )}\right]\\ {S}_{\text{XX}}(\omega )=\underset{T\to +\infty }{\lim}\frac{2\pi }{T}E\left[{\stackrel{ˆ}{X}}_{T}(w)\overline{{\stackrel{ˆ}{X}}_{T}(\omega )}\right]\end{array}$

**Link between the power autospectrum and the power of the signal:**

The power of a signal is equal to its variance. For a centred signal, the variance is: ${\sigma}_{X}^{2}={R}_{\text{XX}}(0)$ .

One therefore has: ${\sigma}_{X}^{2}={R}_{\text{XX}}(0)=\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{S}_{\text{XX}}(\omega )d\omega$ .

### 3.2 The equations of motion

The total response of the structure is determined by the relation: ${\ddot{X}}_{a}(\omega )=H(\omega )\ddot{E}(\omega )$ ,

where $\ddot{E}(\omega )$ is the vector of $m$ lines made up of the excitations represented by the Fourier transforms of the accelerograms ${g}_{j}(t)$ at the $m$ support degrees of freedom,

$H(\omega )$ is the transfer matrix defined by $H(\omega )={\omega}^{2}\text{ph}(\omega )\Phi +\Psi$

where $p$ is the matrix of the participation factors,

$H(\omega )$ the vector of the modal transfer functions ${h}_{i}(\omega )$

$\Phi$ basis of the dynamic modes

$\Psi$ basis of the static modes

it comprises $n$ lines (= number of free degrees of freedom of the structure) and $m$ columns.

#### 3.2.1 "Cross-spectral-excitation" matrix

**NB:**

*This designation "cross-spectral-excitation matrix" is abusive: it means "matrix of the power cross-spectral density of the excitation".*

It is assumed that the seismic excitation can be considered as a stationary signal — taking into account the ratios between the representative times — and centred. This makes it possible to use a certain number of results of probabilistic analysis. Interest is then in the stationary response of the system to a stationary excitation.

${S}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )$ denotes the matrix of the power cross-spectra corresponding to the excitation. Its data are set out in chapter 4.

For the record, it is recalled here that it is computed from Fourier transforms of the accelerations. It is a matrix ($m\times m$ ). The term ij corresponds to the cross-spectrum between the signals ${\ddot{E}}^{i}$ and ${\ddot{E}}^{j}$ , that is to say again between the Fourier transforms of the accelerograms ${g}_{i}$ and ${g}_{j}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Random dynamic response

It has been seen that the power cross-spectrum between two probabilistic signals is the Fourier transform of the cross-correlation function of the two signals. It is applied to the total response of the structure:

${S}_{{{\ddot{X}}_{a}\ddot{X}}_{a}}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{R}_{{{\ddot{X}}_{a}\ddot{X}}_{a}}(\tau ){e}^{-i\omega \tau }d\tau =\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}E\left[{\ddot{X}}_{a}(t){}^{T}\text{}\overline{{\ddot{X}}_{a}(t-\tau )}\right]{e}^{-i\omega \tau }d\tau$

One then works in the time domain in order to express the cross-correlation function of the total response ${R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')$ .

$h(t)$ denotes the impulse response of the system: $h(t)={\text{TF}}^{-1}[H(\omega )]$

and $\ddot{e}(t)$ the inverse Fourier transform of the exciting DSP: $\ddot{e}(t)={\text{TF}}^{-1}[\ddot{E}(\omega )]$

By inverse Fourier transform of the relation: ${\ddot{X}}_{a}(\omega )=H(\omega )\ddot{E}(\omega )$

one has ${\ddot{\mathrm{X}}}_{\mathrm{a}}(t)=\mathrm{h}\times \ddot{\mathrm{e}}(t)=\underset{R}{\int}\mathrm{h}(u)\ddot{\mathrm{e}}(t-u)\text{du}$

$\begin{array}{}{R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')=E\left[{\ddot{X}}_{a}(t){}^{T}\text{}\overline{{\ddot{X}}_{a}(t')}\right]\\ {R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')=E\left[\underset{R}{\int}h(u)\ddot{e}(t-u)\text{du}{}^{T}\text{}\overline{\underset{R}{\int}h(v)\ddot{e}(t'-v)\text{dv}}\right]\\ {R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')=E\left[\underset{R}{\int}\underset{R}{\int}h(u)\ddot{e}(t-u){}^{T}\text{}\overline{\ddot{e}(t'-v)}{}^{T}\text{}\overline{h(v)}\text{dv}\text{du}\right]\end{array}$

The system is assumed deterministic in this analysis, so the impulse response can be taken out of the computation of the mathematical expectation. It follows:

${R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')=\underset{R}{\int}\underset{R}{\int}h(u)E[\ddot{e}(t-u){}^{T}\text{}\overline{\ddot{e}(t'-v)}]{}^{T}\text{}\overline{h(v)}\text{dv}\text{du}$

The excitation is assumed to be a stationary process, so the cross-correlation depends only on the time difference $\tau =t-t'$ :

${R}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(t-t'-u+v)=E[\ddot{e}(t-u){}^{T}\text{}\overline{\ddot{e}(t'-v)}]={R}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\theta )\text{pour}\theta =t-t'-u+v=\tau -u+v$

whence ${R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(t,t')=\underset{R}{\int}\underset{R}{\int}h(u){R}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\theta ){}^{T}\text{}\overline{h(v)}\text{dv}\text{du}={R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(\tau )$ which justifies the approach a posteriori.

This expression is now substituted into the expression of the power spectral density of the response:

${S}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}{R}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}(\tau ){e}^{-i\omega \tau }d\tau =\frac{1}{2\pi }\underset{-\infty }{\overset{+\infty }{\int}}\underset{R}{\int}\underset{R}{\int}h(u){R}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\tau -u+v){}^{T}\text{}\overline{h(v)}{e}^{-i\omega \tau }\text{dv}\text{du}\mathrm{dt}$

By distributing the dummy integration variables, the respective Fourier transforms of $h(u),{R}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(t-u+v),{}^{T}\text{}\overline{h(v)}$ are brought out, and one finally obtains:

${S}_{{\ddot{X}}_{a}{\ddot{X}}_{a}}=H(\omega ).{S}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega ).\overline{{}^{T}\text{}H(\omega )}$

with $H(\omega )={\omega}^{2}\text{P.h}(\omega )\Phi +\Psi$

Taking into account the relations between the Fourier transforms of the displacement, of the velocity and of the acceleration, one also has:

$\begin{array}{}{S}_{{\dot{X}}_{a}{\dot{X}}_{a}}=\frac{-1}{{\omega}^{2}}H(\omega ){S}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )\overline{{}^{T}\text{}H(\omega )}\\ {S}_{{X}_{a}{X}_{a}}=\frac{1}{{\omega}^{4}}H(w){S}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )\overline{{}^{T}\text{}H(\omega )}\end{array}$

These relations make it possible to express the response of the structure by the DSP of the displacement or of the velocity.

**Notes:**

- *According to the expression given to* $H(\omega )$ *, the DSP of the total, relative or differential displacement (respectively of the velocity or of the acceleration) is expressed:*

*absolute motion:* $H(\omega )={\omega}^{2}\text{P. h}(\omega )\Phi +\Psi$

*relative motion:* $H(\omega )={\omega}^{2}\text{P. h}(\omega )\Phi \text{}$

*differential motion (i.e. entrainment):* $H(\omega )=\Psi$

- *It is customary, during an analysis with Code_Aster, to restrict the matrix of the transfer function to the lines of the* $l$ *observation degrees of freedom. This makes it possible to lighten the analyses accordingly as soon as* $l$ *is small compared with* $n$ *.*

### 3.3 Application in Code_Aster

The whole of the spectral approach for the seismic analysis is treated in the command DYNA_ALEA_MODAL [U4.53.22](doc:u4.53.22). The data are grouped under three factor keywords and one simple keyword.

The modal basis is made up of the dynamic modes computed by the command CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02), stored in a concept of type mode_meca recovered by the factor keyword BASE_MODALE, on the one hand; of the static modes computed by the command MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14), stored in a concept of type mode_stat recovered by the simple keyword MODE_STAT, on the other hand. The factor keyword BASE_MODALE also has the arguments which make it possible to determine the frequency band or the modes retained for the analysis and the corresponding dampings.

The data corresponding to the excitation are gathered under the factor keyword EXCIT (cf. paragraph [§4]): the type of excitation in the sense of the GRANDEUR is specified there: excitation in displacement or in force, the node or nodes NOEUD and components NOM_CMP excited, the name of the cross-spectra or autospectra INTE_SPEC, complex functions previously read or computed, respectively by the operators LIRE_INTE_SPEC [U4.36.01](doc:u4.36.01) or CALC_INTE_SPEC [U4.36.03](doc:u4.36.03) and stored in a cross-spectrum table of concept tabl_intsp which apply at each excited degree of freedom.

Under the factor keyword REPONSE are found the data related to the choice of the discretisation.

The command DYNA_ALEA_MODAL supplies the response in the form of a power spectral density on the modal basis. In order to obtain the restitution of the DSPs on the physical basis, REST_SPEC_PHYS [U4.63.22](doc:u4.63.22) will be used, which makes it possible to specify the type of quantity of the response (displacement or force), at the "observation points" (node-component) of the result. In the presence of a response of displacement type, it will also be specified here whether the response corresponds to the absolute, relative or differential displacement.

REST_SPEC_PHYS supplies a table of cross-spectra which contains, according to the user's request, the cross-spectral matrix in displacement ${S}_{\text{XX}}$ , in velocity ${S}_{\dot{X}\dot{X}}$ , **or** in acceleration ${S}_{\ddot{X}\ddot{X}}$ for an expression in the absolute frame (index $a$ ), the relative frame (index $r$ ) or the entrainment frame (index $e$ ).

Each previous "combination" requires a specific call to the command REST_SPEC_PHYS.

## 4 Definition of the exciting power cross-spectral matrix

The seismic excitation is, as has been said, random by nature. It may therefore be known not by its time expression but in frequency form by a power spectral density, also called a cross-spectrum.

When there are several supports, they may be excited by identical or different excitations; the latter case is that of multi-support excitation.

For $m$ supports, the power cross-spectral density matrix of order $m$ is defined, or by abuse of language the cross-spectrum of order $m$ , which is a matrix ($m\times m$ ) of complex functions depending on the frequency.

The diagonal terms represent the "auto-" spectral power densities — or autospectra — at the excitation points; the off-diagonal terms correspond to the cross-spectral densities between the excitations at two distinct support points (each line or column of the matrix in fact represents a support point in the physical mesh or a mode in a modal analysis). By definition of these terms, it follows that the power cross-spectral density matrices handled are Hermitian. (See [bib2] or the Reference documentation associated with the command POST_DYNA_ALEA [R7.10.01](doc:r7.10.01))

The various *Code_Aster* commands which make it possible to obtain a power cross-spectral density matrix are presented below.

### 4.1 Reading from a file

The most elementary way of defining a power cross-spectral density matrix is to give, "by hand", the values at the various frequency steps.

The operator LIRE_INTE_SPEC [U4.36.01](doc:u4.36.01) is then used.

LIRE_INTE_SPEC reads from an "excitation cross-spectrum" file. The format of the file in which the cross-spectral matrix is recorded is simple: the function of each term of the cross-spectral matrix is described successively; for each function, one line per frequency is given, indicating the frequency, the real and imaginary parts of the complex number; or the frequency, the modulus and the phase of the complex number (keyword FORMAT).

*Example of an excitation cross-spectrum file (for a matrix reduced to one term):*

```text

DIM = 1

J = 1
NB_POIN = 4

```

### 4.2 Obtaining a cross-spectrum from functions of time

The power cross-spectral density matrix can be deduced from functions of time. The operator CALC_INTE_SPEC [U4.36.03](doc:u4.36.03) in *Code_Aster*  [bib3] is then used.

From a list of $N$ functions of time, this operator makes it possible to compute the power cross-spectrum $N\times N$ which corresponds to them.

For each term of the cross-spectral matrix ($N\times N$ ) the following approach is used [bib3].

To compute the cross-spectrum of two signals, the Wiener–Khintchine relation [bib7] is used, which makes it possible to establish a formula for computing the power spectral density by the Fourier transform of finite samples of the signals $x(t)$ and $y(t)$ .

It then follows:

${S}_{xy}(f)=\underset{T\to \infty }{\lim}\frac{1}{T}\mathrm{E}[{\mathrm{X}}_{k}(f,T).{\mathrm{Y}}_{k}\times (f,T)]$

where $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{X}_{k}(f,T)=\text{TF}[{x}_{k}](f)=\underset{0}{\overset{T}{\int}}{x}_{k}(t){e}^{-\mathrm{i2}\pi f}\text{dt}\\ {Y}_{k}(f,T)=\text{TF}[{y}_{k}](f)=\underset{0}{\overset{T}{\int}}{y}_{k}(t){e}^{-\mathrm{i2}\pi f}\text{dt}\end{array}$

are the discrete Fourier transforms of $«x»$ and $«y»$ .

When one is interested in signals coming from measurements, one most often has only signals known in a discrete manner; likewise, a result of a transient analysis is a discrete signal.

An approximation of the cross-spectrum of the discrete signals $x[n]$ and $y[n]$ defined on $L$ points spaced by $\Delta t$ , split into $p$ blocks of $q$ points, is obtained by the relation:

$\begin{array}{}{\stackrel{ˆ}{S}}_{xy}\left[k\right]=\frac{1}{\mathrm{pq}\Delta t}\sum_{i=1}^{p}{X}^{(i)}[k]{Y}^{(i)}\ast [k]\\ {X}^{(i)}[k]=\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{q}{x}^{(i)}[n]{e}^{-\mathrm{2i}\pi \text{kn}/q}\\ {Y}^{(i)}[k]=\Delta t\sum_{n=0}^{q}{y}^{(i)}[n]{e}^{-\mathrm{2i}\pi \text{kn}/q}\end{array}$

The various blocks may or may not overlap. The values $p$ and $q$ are at the user's choice.

This method is that of the WELCH periodogram [bib8].

The computation is carried out on a window which moves over the domain of definition of the functions. The user specifies in the command the length of the analysis window, the shift between two successive computation windows and the number of points per window.

### 4.3 Predefined excitations or excitations reconstructed from existing complex functions

One may wish to define a power cross-spectral density matrix in various ways:

- by a white noise: the values are constant
- according to the analytical formula of KANAI-TAJIMI, useful in seismic analysis (filtered white noise),
- or by taking up existing complex functions.

The operator DEFI_INTE_SPEC [U4.36.02](doc:u4.36.02) is then used.

#### 4.3.1 Existing complex functions

It is sufficient, under the factor keyword PAR_FONCTION, to give the name of the function for each pair of indices NUME_ORDRE_I, NUME_ORDRE_J, corresponding to the upper triangular matrix (because of its Hermitian character).

#### 4.3.2 White noise

A white noise is characterised by a constant value over the whole domain of definition considered. Under the factor keyword CONSTANT, this value (VALE_R or VALE_C) is given on the frequency band [FREQ_MIN, FREQ_MAX] for each pair of indices INDI_I, INDI_J, corresponding to the upper triangular matrix (because of its Hermitian character). In order to define the function completely, the interpolation and the extensions are specified.

#### 4.3.3 White noise filtered by KANAI-TAJIMI [bib9]

For a structure supported on the ground, it is common to take as excitation the power spectral density of Kanai–Tajimi. This spectral density represents the filtering of a white noise by the ground, considered as a single-degree-of-freedom system. The parameters of the formula make it possible to act on the central frequency and the bandwidth of the spectrum.

Le spectre $G(\omega )$ s’exprime par la relation suivante:

$\begin{array}{}G(\omega )=\frac{{\omega}_{g}^{4}+4{\xi}_{g}^{2}{\omega}_{g}^{2}{\omega}^{2}}{{({\omega}_{g}^{2}-{\omega}^{2})}^{2}+4{\xi}_{g}^{2}{\omega}_{g}^{2}{\omega}^{2}}{G}_{0}\\ \begin{array}{cc}{\omega}_{g}=2\pi f& \text{pulsation propre}\\ {\xi}_{g}& \mathrm{amortissement}\mathrm{total}\\ {G}_{0}& \mathrm{niveau}\mathrm{du}\mathrm{bruit}\mathrm{blanc}\mathrm{avant}\mathrm{filtrage}\end{array}\end{array}$

The user must specify the natural frequency ${f}_{g}$ of the filter, the modal damping ${\xi}_{g}$ and the level of the white noise ${G}_{0}$ (= VALE_R) before filtering; as well as, as for any function: the interpolation, the exterior profiles and the domain of definition (frequency band).

By default a common ground is well represented by the values ${f}_{g}=2.5\mathrm{Hz}$ and ${\xi}_{g}=0.6$ .

*Example of use for a white noise filtered by KANAI_TAJIMI:*

```text
Interex =        DEFI_INTE_SPEC (
DIMENSION : 1
KANAI_TAJIMI : (
NUME_ORDRE_I : 1                indices du terme de la matrice de densité
NUME_ORDRE_J : 1                    interspectrale de puissance
FREQ_MOY : 2.5                fréquence propre
AMOR : 0.6                    amortissement modal
VALE_R : 1                    niveau du bruit blanc
INTERPOL : ‘LIN’                interpolation linéaire
PROL_GAUCHE : ‘CONSTANT’    prolongation
PROL_DROIT : ‘ CONSTANT’
FREQ_MIN : 0.                domaine de définition
FREQ_MAX : 200.
PAS : 1.
) ) ;
```

### 4.4 Other types of excitation

The analyses of the previous paragraphs were carried out under the hypothesis of an excitation in **imposed motion** on a degree of freedom. With a few modifications it is possible to use the same approach for an excitation **in force** [§4.4.1] or **by fluid sources** [§ 4.4.2], the latter being expressed in a finite element [§4.4.3] or on a shape function of the structure [§4.4.4].

In the remainder of this paragraph, the random excitation is assumed known and supplied by the user in the form of a DSP, a power spectral density.

#### 4.4.1 Case of the excitation in imposed forces

Under the keyword EXCIT one has GRANDEUR = EFFO.

When the excitation at the supports is of imposed-force type, the general equation of motion is:

$M\ddot{X}(t)+C\dot{X}(t)+\text{KX}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}{F}_{j}$

The response of the structure is then computed on a **basis of dynamic modes** $\Phi =\left\lbrace {\phi }_{i,i=1,n}\right\rbrace$ , these modes being computed assuming the **exciting supports free.** No distinction is made, in this case, between absolute, relative and differential motion, and static modes are not used.

The modal participation factor is defined in the form: ${P}_{ij}=\frac{{}^{T}\text{}{\phi }_{i}{F}_{j}}{{\mu}_{i}}$

The transient, harmonic and random responses have the **same expressions** as the responses of the **relative motion** of the multi-support excitation in the general case [§3]. (Which corresponds to the absence of static modes). The exciting force is represented at each support degree of freedom by its DSP in the form of a term equivalent to ${S}_{\ddot{E}\ddot{E}}(\omega )$ .

#### 4.4.2 Excitation by fluid sources

Fluid sources appear, for example, in the study of a piping network. They correspond to active components or to connections of secondary piping. They are generally pressure sources or flow-rate sources. These various types of source are presented below according to their mathematical formulation and to what *Code_Aster* does in each configuration.

These fluid sources are not directly seismic excitations but may be induced by an earthquake. The solution of the mechanical problem calls on the same methods, because of their random character, which justifies their presentation here.

The modelling of the piping network is assumed to be carried out using the vibro-acoustic beams of *Code_Aster*.

The response to fluid sources is computed within the framework of the response to imposed forces (cf. [§4.4.1]); in this framework one is interested in responses of quantity type "displacement" (GRANDEUR = DEPL_R under the keyword REPONSE).

The **pressure and force sources**, for reasons of modelling of the fluid sources, are represented by dipoles [bib5]; it is therefore necessary to give **two points of application**.

**Volume flow-rate source:** **GRANDEUR** **= SOUR_DEBI_VOLU under the keyword** **EXCIT**

A volume flow rate is expressed in ${m}^{3}/s$ , its power spectral density in ${({m}^{3}/s)}^{2}/\mathrm{Hz}$ .

A volume flow-rate source is considered, in the $P-\phi$ formulation of the pipe elements with fluid, as an imposed force on the degree of freedom $\phi$ of the node of application of the source [R4.02.02](doc:r4.02.02).

The user supplies the DSP of the volume flow rate ${S}_{\text{vv}}(\omega )$ ; the DSP ${S}_{\text{vv}}^{'}(\omega )$ applied as a force on the degree of freedom $\phi$ is: ${S}_{\text{vv}}^{'}(\omega )={(\rho \omega )}^{2}{S}_{\text{vv}}(\omega )$

where $\rho$ is the density of the fluid.

**Mass flow-rate source:** **GRANDEUR** **= SOUR_DEBI_MASS under the keyword** **EXCIT**

A mass flow rate is expressed in $\mathit{kg}/s$ , its power spectral density in ${(\mathrm{kg}/s)}^{2}/\mathrm{Hz}$ . The mass flow rate is the product of the volume flow rate by the density of the fluid.

The user supplies the DSP of the mass flow rate ${S}_{\text{mm}}(\omega )$ ; the DSP ${S'}_{\text{mm}}(\omega )$ applied as a force on the degree of freedom $\phi$ is: ${S'}_{\text{mm}}\omega ={\omega}^{2}{S}_{\text{mm}}\omega$

**Pressure source:** **GRANDEUR** **= SOUR_PRESS under the keyword** **EXCIT**

A pressure source is applied in *Aster* at a **dipole** ${P}_{1}{P}_{2}$ .

For a pressure source whose DSP is ${S}_{\text{PP}}(\omega )$ , expressed in ${\mathrm{Pa}}^{2}/\mathrm{Hz}$ , *Aster* builds a power cross-spectral density matrix ${S'}_{\text{pp}}(\omega )$ which is applied as an imposed force on the degree of freedom $\phi$ of the points ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ .

${S'}_{\text{PP}}(\omega )={S}_{\text{PP}}(\omega )(\begin{array}{cc}{(\frac{S}{\text{dx}})}^{2}& -{(\frac{S}{\text{dx}})}^{2}\\ -{(\frac{S}{\text{dx}})}^{2}& {(\frac{S}{\text{dx}})}^{2}\end{array})$

where $S$ is the fluid cross-section, $\text{dx}$ the distance between the two points ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ .

**Force source:** **GRANDEUR** **= SOUR_FORCE under the keyword** **EXCIT**

The force simply corresponds to the product of the pressure by the fluid cross-section of the tube: $F=\mathrm{PS}$ . It is therefore also applied on a **dipole** ${P}_{1}{P}_{2}$ .

For a force source whose DSP is ${S}_{\text{FF}}(\omega )$ , expressed in ${N}^{2}/\mathrm{Hz}$ , *Aster* applies, as an imposed force on the degree of freedom $f$ of the points ${P}_{1}$ and ${P}_{2}$ , (a distance $\mathrm{dx}$ apart), the power cross-spectral density matrix ${S'}_{\text{FF}}(\omega )$ such that:

${S'}_{\text{FF}}(\omega )={S}_{\text{FF}}(\omega )(\begin{array}{cc}{(\frac{1}{\text{dx}})}^{2}& -{(\frac{1}{\text{dx}})}^{2}\\ -{(\frac{1}{\text{dx}})}^{2}& {(\frac{1}{\text{dx}})}^{2}\end{array})$

#### 4.4.3 Excitation distributed on a shape function

If the power spectral density of the excitation $E(\omega )$ corresponds to a force imposed on a shape function ${f}_{i}$ , $E(\omega )$ gives the frequency dependence of the level of the excitation.

The spatial weighting of the force is represented in *Code_Aster* by a field at the nodes which does not depend on the frequency: keyword CHAM_NO under the factor keyword EXCIT. This field at the nodes is an "assembled vector". From the theoretical point of view the computation formalism is the same as previously (excitation in imposed force [§4.4.1]), for a force vector on the right-hand side equal to ${f}_{i}$ .
