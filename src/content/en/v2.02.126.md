# v2.02.126 SDLL126 – Transient dynamic response of a beam with 3 discs, subject to gyroscopic effect.

**Summary:**

In the case of a transient response, the objective is to validate the effect of the gyroscopic matrix on a beam supported at each end, on linear supports. The beam is full, circular cross section and has three discs. All calculations are performed at constant rotor rotation speed.

There is no analytical reference for the transient calculation, so the comparison will be based on amplitudes and phases in continuous operation, the displacements of the node load obtained using three different methods.

Five calculations are therefore made:

- Calculation A: transient calculation in physical coordinates;
- Calculation B: transitional calculation in generalised data;
- Calculation C: harmonic calculation in physical coordinates, tree according to $X$;
- Calculation D: Harmonic calculation in physical coordinates, tree according to $Z$;
- Calculation E: Harmonic calculation in generalised cords, tree according to $Z$ .

This problem therefore allows testing the effect of the gyroscopic matrix that was developed for an straight beam and for discrete elements, in the case of a transient response.

The gyroscopic effect can introduce system instability. It is necessary to ensure that all modal damping is positive.

The results obtained by the different methods of calculation are consistent with each other. The harmonic calculation was also validated using Bibliographical references. The references are based on Timoshenko beam theory.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is the one obtained using Code_Aster, with a harmonic calculation. The harmonic calculation was itself validated via the results provided in the work of Michel LALANNE and Guy FERRARIS.

The comparison will therefore cover the results obtained through calculations A and B (transitional calculation, operation of the results in continuous operation), and the reference defined by calculation C (harmonic calculation). The load is of the bald type, the rotor rotation speed being constant.

The nodes moves are written in a permanent mode, depending on the $t$ time, in the form of:

$Y(t)={Y}_{\max}.\cos(\omega t+{\theta}_{y})$

$Z(t)={Z}_{\max}.\cos(\omega t+{\theta}_{z})$

With:

> ${Y}_{\max}$ and ${Z}_{\max}$ half amplitudes (in $m$)
> $\omega$: rotor rotation speed (${\mathrm{rd.s}}^{-1}$)
> ${\theta}_{y}$ and ${\theta}_{z}$ , the phases of both signals.

It is fairly easy, by viewing the curves or editing the result file, to raise the half amplitudes of the displacements following $Y$ and $Z$. For the calculation of the phases, it is first necessary to determine the temporal abscissus of an extreme of the sinusoid, and then to deduce only the phase. The phase corresponds to the shift from that of the bald, which is defined at the moment $t=0$, as being of zero value (force from the collinear bald to the $Y$ direction at the moment $t=0$).

So we find ${t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}$ (abscesses in time of an extreme of the next $Y$ displacement)

We have: $Y(t={t}_{\mathrm{ymax}})={Y}_{\max}.\cos(\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}+{\theta}_{y})={Y}_{\max}$

> $\cos(\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}+{\theta}_{y})=1$

$\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}+{\theta}_{y}=2k\pi$

> d’où:     ${\theta}_{y}=2k\pi -\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}=\mathrm{Ent}(\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}})-\omega .{t}_{\mathrm{ymax}}$

The same processing is performed for the following $Z$ displacement.

Code_Aster provides direct, harmonic calculation of phases and amplitudes of displacements to nodes.
