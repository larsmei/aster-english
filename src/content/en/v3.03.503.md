# v3.03.503 SSLS503 - Laminate sheet in flexion antisymmetric stacking simply supported

**Summary:**

This test represents a quasi-static calculation of a laminated plate, in antisymmetric bending stack, simply supported, subjected to uniformly distributed pressure.

The 4 modellings can be validated:

- modellings elements finished DKT (QUAD4, TRIA3) and DST (QUAD4, TRIA3) in the case of composite material (3 different orientation layers),
- transverse shear stresses.

The displacements and stresses obtained are compared with an analytical reference solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Displacement: analytical solution obtained by mass decomposition of the shape:

$w=\underset{i}{\Sigma}\underset{j}{\Sigma}{w}_{ij}\sin(\frac{i\pi x}{a})\sin(\frac{j\pi y}{b})$

Constraints: digital solution [bib1], [bib2]

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

- The reference solution is given for a number of terms in the series equal to 25.
- The transverse shear correction factor used is 5/6.
- With a large slenderness ($a/h=100$ ) , the transverse shear level is low and therefore difficult to obtain with precision. There is then uncertainty about the ${\sigma}_{ij}$ stress values calculated during validation of the $\mathit{VPCS}$ test , the differences obtained by the software on the shear components are in the order of $\text{10\%}$ .

### 3.4 Remarks

The stresses are expressed in the orthotropy benchmark defined by ANGL_REP (AFFE_CARA_ELEM), and in the normal element.

$\mathrm{SIXX}$ , $\mathrm{SIYY}$ and $\mathrm{SIYZ}$ components are the average values of the two mesh cellss at $A$ and $C$ points.

The difference obtained on $\mathrm{SIXZ}$ is due to the difference in transverse shear modelling: in the reference, a transverse shear correction coefficient of 5/6 is used. In *Code_Aster* , the shear distribution in thickness, assumed parabolic in each layer, is calculated.

The $\mathrm{SIXZ}$ sign is opposed to the reference solution sign.

## 7 Summary of the results

- *Movements*: Whatever the model used (DKT or DST) the results are satisfactory, the maximum error is less than $\text{0.7\%}$.
- *Planar constraints*: results are more accurate with DKT Modelling, error is less than $\text{1\%}$ except for $\mathrm{SIXY}$ (QUAD4) where error is $\text{5\%}$ . For DST Modelling the error is higher ($\text{<8\%}$) with a difference large on $\mathrm{SIXX}$ ($\text{28\%}$) for mesh cell TRIA3.
- *Transverse shearing*: whatever Modelling is used (DKT or DST) the results obtained with quadrangular meshes are closer to reference solution than those obtained with triangular meshes. In the first case the error on $\mathrm{SIXZ}$ is less than $\text{15\%}$, and the error on $\mathrm{SIYZ}$ is less than $\text{3\%}$, while in the second case the error on $\mathrm{SIXZ}$ is $\text{35\%}$ and the error on $\mathrm{SIYZ}$ is between $\text{2\%}$ and $\text{24\%}$. Except for the least accurate triangular meshes due to their anisotropy, the difference which remains with quadrangular meshes is due to the difference in transverse shear modelling: in the reference, a transverse shear correction coefficient of 5/6 is used. In Code_Aster, the shear distribution in thickness, assumed parabolic in each layer, is calculated.
