# v6.04.232 SSNV232 – Triaxial drained test with MOHR_COULOMB and MohrCoulombAS laws

*Summary*

A *triaxial calculation in pure mechanics* (equivalent to drained hydraulic conditions) is performed with the MOHR_COULOMB (modellings A and B) and MohrCoulombAS (modellings C and D) laws. The calculated solutions are compared to an analytical solution. This test consists of four modellings:

- A: Modelling on point hardware (SIMU_POINT_MAT) with MOHR_COULOMB law;
- B: 3D Modelling (STAT_NON_LINE) with MOHR_COULOMB law;
- C: Modelling on point hardware (SIMU_POINT_MAT) with MohrCoulombAS law;
- D: 3D Modelling (STAT_NON_LINE) with MohrCoulombAS law.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.232/10000000000005B30000047DFCD6776F6FFADCA9.jpg)

The triaxial test is performed on a single finite element isoparametric cubic $\mathit{CUB}8$. The length of each edge is $1$. The different facets of this cube are mesh cells groups named $\mathit{HAUT}$, $\mathit{BAS}$, $\mathit{DEVANT}$, $\mathit{DERRIERE}$, $\mathit{DROIT}$ and $\mathit{GAUCHE}$. The mesh-cell group *SYM* also contains the mesh cells $\mathit{BAS}$, $\mathit{DEVANT}$ and $\mathit{GAUCHE}$ groups; the mesh-cell group $\mathit{COTE}$ groups of mesh cells $\mathit{DERRIERE}$ and $\mathit{DROIT}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The elastic properties are:

- isotropic compressibility module: $K=516,2\mathit{MPa}$
- shear module: $\mu =238,2\mathit{MPa}$

The common parameters of the MOHR_COULOMB and MohrCoulombAS laws are:

- Friction angle (Friction Angle): $\mathrm{\phi }=33°$
- Dilatancy Angle: $\psi =27°$
- Cohesion (Cohesion): ${c}_{0}=1\mathit{kPa}$

The parameters specific to MohrCoulombAS are:

- Transition angle (Transition Angle): ${\theta}_{T}=29.9°$
- TensionCutOff: $a=0.25C/\tan(\varphi )$
- Hardening Hardening Parameter (HardeningCoef): ${h}_{C}=0$

The ${\theta}_{T}=29.9°$ parameter was selected to approximate the original (single) criterion of Mohr-Coulomb (${\theta}_{T}=30°$ ); and by ensuring that convergence was not too degraded.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A triaxial test consists of imposing a vertical load variation on the test piece while maintaining the constant lateral pressure. It can be drained (the fluid pore pressure does not vary during the test) or undrained (the valve is closed: the fluid pore pressure evolves in the sample). Here, we are interested in the drained case, simpler, because it does not involve the influence of the fluid pore pressure and *modelisable by this pure mechanical calculation*.

In the model considered (cases of models B and D), cubic l-element represents one eighth of the sample. The limit conditions are therefore as follows:

- The symmetry conditions:

  - ${u}_{z}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathit{BAS}$ group
  - ${u}_{x}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathit{GAUCHE}$ group
  - ${u}_{y}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathit{DEVANT}$ group
- Side pressure conditions:

  - ${P}_{n}=1$ on mesh cell $\mathit{COTE}$ group
- The loading conditions:

  - ${P}_{n}=1$ on mesh cell $\mathit{HAUT}$ Group (phase 1)
  - ${u}_{z}=-1$ on mesh cell $\mathit{HAUT}$ Group (Phase 2)

The load is carried out in two phases:

- *Initiation* . Isotropic loading between $t\in \left[-2;0\right]s$: the pressure $p$ on the mesh cells $\mathit{COTE}$ and $\mathit{HAUT}$ groups varies from $0$ to $p={P}_{0}=50\mathit{kPa}$ , the isotropic preconsolidation pressure in the intial state;
- *triaxial test*: displacement imposed on mesh-cell group $\mathrm{HAUT}$ with $t$ varying between $t\in \left[0-30\right]s$ and ${u}_{z}$ varying between ${u}_{z}\in \left[0;-0,3\right]\mathit{mm}$. The total vertical ${\epsilon}_{zz}$ deformation is $0,03\text{\%}$;

### 1.4 analytical solution

The loads applied to the cubic sample are shown below:

- ${\epsilon}_{1}=F\left(t\right)$
- ${\sigma}_{2}={\sigma}_{3}={\sigma}_{0}$

The behaviour of the sample is governed by Mohr-Coulomb law, which states:

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}f\left({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{3}\right)=\mid {\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}\mid +\left({\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}\right)\sin\varphi -{\mathrm{2c}}_{0}\cos\varphi \le 0\\ {\sigma}_{1}\ge {\sigma}_{2}={\sigma}_{3}\end{array}\right.
$$

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.232/Shape1.gif)

It is associated with the plastic flow potential:

$$
g\left({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{3}\right)=\mid {\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}\mid +\left({\sigma}_{1}+{\sigma}_{3}\right)\sin\psi -{\mathrm{2c}}_{0}\cos\psi
$$

so that, by noting $t=\sin\psi$ and $\xi =\mathit{signe}\left({\sigma}_{1}-{\sigma}_{3}\right)$, the flow law is written:

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}f({\sigma}_{1},{\sigma}_{3})\ge 0\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\sigma}_{1}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}_{1}^{p}}=\dot{\lambda}(t+\xi )\\ \frac{\partial g}{\partial {\sigma}_{2}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}_{2}^{p}}=\dot{{\varepsilon}_{3}^{p}}=\dot{\lambda}(t-\xi )\end{array}\right.
$$

where $\dot{\lambda}$ represents the plastic multiplier. So the two unknowns of the problem are ${\sigma}_{1}$ (or ${\varepsilon}_{3}$) and $\dot{\lambda}$.

#### 1.4.1 Elastically adjusted resolution

The equation is satisfied.

$$
\left\{   \begin{array}{l}   \sigma_1   = \underbrace{(K + \tfrac{4}{3}G)}_{E}\varepsilon_1^{e}     + 2\Bigl(K - \tfrac{2}{3}G\Bigr)\varepsilon_3^{e}
\\
\sigma_3   = \Bigl(K - \tfrac{2}{3}G\Bigr)\varepsilon_1^{e}     + \Bigl(K - \tfrac{2}{3}G\Bigr)\varepsilon_3^{e}     + \Bigl(K + \tfrac{4}{3}G\Bigr)\varepsilon_3^{e}
\\
\phantom{\sigma_3 =}\    = \underbrace{\Bigl(K - \tfrac{2}{3}G\Bigr)}_{C}\,\varepsilon_1^{e}     + \underbrace{2\Bigl(K + \tfrac{1}{3}G\Bigr)}_{D}\varepsilon_3^{e}   \end{array}   \right.
$$

Knowing that ${\sigma}_{3}={\sigma}_{0}$ , it is directly deduced from that:

$$
\dot{{\varepsilon}_{3}^{e}}=-\frac{C}{D}\dot{{\varepsilon}_{1}^{e}}
$$

#### 1.4.2 Resolution in plastic mode

The equation is satisfied. Either ${\sigma}^{p}={\sigma}^{e}$ the elastic prediction given by the equations , the final stress ${\sigma}^{+}$ is written:

$$
{\sigma}^{+}=\mathrm{ℂ}.{\varepsilon}^{+}={\sigma}^{p}-\dot{\lambda}\mathrm{ℂ}.\overrightarrow{{n}_{g}}`
$$

where $\mathrm{ℂ}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}E& \mathrm{2C}\\ C& D\end{array}\right]$ represents the linear elasticity and $\overrightarrow{{n}_{g}}=(t+\xi ,t-\xi )$ tensor.

The plastic multiplier is calculated by writing $f({\sigma}^{+})=0$ , i.e.:

$$
\begin{array}{cc}(s+\xi )(K+\frac{4}{3}G){\varepsilon}_{1}^{+}+2(s+\xi )(K-\frac{2}{3}G){\varepsilon}_{3}^{+}& +\\ (s-\xi )(K-\frac{2}{3}G){\varepsilon}_{1}^{+}+2(s-\xi )(K+\frac{G}{3}){\varepsilon}_{3}^{+}& =2{c}_{0}\cos\phi \end{array}

\mathrm{\iff}\ \underbrace{2\bigl(Ks + G(\xi + \tfrac{s}{3})\bigr)}_{A}\,\varepsilon_1^{+} + \underbrace{2\bigl(2Ks - G(\xi + \tfrac{s}{3})\bigr)}_{B}\,\varepsilon_3^{+} = 2c_0\cos\phi
$$

which gives, by replacing:

$$
\left\{ \begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{1}^{+}={\varepsilon}_{1}^{e}-\dot{\lambda}(t+\xi )\\ {\varepsilon}_{3}^{+}={\varepsilon}_{3}^{e}-\dot{\lambda}(t-\xi )\end{array}\right.
$$

in , we get: $\dot{\lambda}\,\underbrace{ \left[A(t+\xi) + B(t-\xi)\right] }_{\mathit{BB}}= -2c_0\cos\phi + A\,\varepsilon_1^{e} + B\,\varepsilon_3^{e}= f(\sigma^{e})$ ,

either:

$$
\dot{\lambda}=\frac{f({\sigma}^{e})}{\mathit{BB}}
$$

#### 1.4.3 Correction of imbalance

${\sigma}_{3}^{+}={\sigma}_{0}$ should be checked. A small $\delta$ virtual variation of the solution is assumed. $\delta {\sigma}_{3}^{+}$ is sought, i.e.:

$$
\delta {\sigma}_{3}^{+}=C\delta {\epsilon}_{1}^{e}+E\delta {\epsilon}_{3}^{e}-\delta \dot{\lambda}C\left(t+\xi \right)-\delta \dot{\lambda}E\left(t-\xi \right)
$$

Knowing that $\dot{\lambda}$ is given by , we have:

$$
\delta \dot{\lambda}=\frac{A}{\mathit{BB}}\delta {\epsilon}_{1}^{e}+\frac{B}{\mathit{BB}}\delta {\epsilon}_{3}^{e}
$$

that we report in . This then gives:

$$
\delta {\sigma}_{3}^{+}=\left[C-A\frac{C\left(t+\xi \right)+E\left(t-\xi \right)}{\mathit{BB}}\right]\delta {\epsilon}_{1}^{e}+\left[D-B\frac{C\left(t+\xi \right)+E\left(t-\xi \right)}{\mathit{BB}}\right]\delta {\epsilon}_{3}^{e}
$$

During the Newton process, a new $\delta {\epsilon}_{3}$ value is searched for knowing that $\delta {\epsilon}_{1}=0$ (i.e. the imposed load, which cannot be false) is so that the ${\sigma}_{0}-{\sigma}_{3}^{+}$ imbalance is corrected, either: $\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{3}^{+}}{\partial {\epsilon}_{3}}\delta {\epsilon}_{3}={\sigma}_{0}-{\sigma}_{3}^{+}$ , or using:

$$
\delta {\epsilon}_{3}=\frac{{\sigma}_{0}-{\sigma}_{3}^{+}}{D-B\frac{C\left(t+\xi \right)+E\left(t-\xi \right)}{\mathit{BB}}}
$$

#### 1.4.5 Coherent tangential matrix

For verification purposes, it is possible to display the coherent tangential matrix of the problem.

In addition to the equation, the following expression is also obtained for $\delta {\sigma}_{1}^{+}$:

$$
\delta {\sigma}_{1}^{+}=\left[E-A\frac{E\left(t+\xi \right)+C\left(t-\xi \right)}{\mathit{BB}}\right]\delta {\epsilon}_{1}^{e}+\left[C-B\frac{E\left(t+\xi \right)+C\left(t-\xi \right)}{\mathit{BB}}\right]\delta {\epsilon}_{3}^{e}
$$

The expression of the coherent tangent matrix $\mathit{DSDE}$ is therefore written:

$$
\mathit{DSDE}=\left[\begin{array}{cc}E-A\frac{E(t+\xi )+C(t-\xi )}{\mathit{BB}}& C-B\frac{E(t+\xi )+C(t-\xi )}{\mathit{BB}}\\ C-A\frac{C(t+\xi )+E(t-\xi )}{\mathit{BB}}& E-B\frac{C(t+\xi )+E(t-\xi )}{\mathit{BB}}\end{array}\right]
$$

### 1.5 Results

The solutions are post-treated with point $C$ for ${\sigma}_{zz}$ vertical and horizontal stresses, as well as ${\epsilon}_{v}^{p}$ plastic density deformation and $\mid {\epsilon}_{d}^{p}\mid =\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\left(\epsilon -\frac{{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}I\right):\left(\epsilon -\frac{{\epsilon}_{v}^{p}}{3}I\right)}$ plastic deviator deformation.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

The **A** Modelling is performed on a hardware point using SIMU_POINT_MAT.

The time step is $\Delta t=0,1\mathit{sec}$ , which is 300 time increments.

A slight ${\varepsilon}={10}^{-6}$ dissymmetry is introduced on the horizontal load to avoid a singularity too marked of the tangent matrix when entering plasticity:

$\left\{ \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{0}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{0}\left(1+{10}^{-6}\right)\end{array}\right.$

*This dissymmetry is not taken into account in the Modelling* **C** for which the criterion is regular on a triaxial compression path.

The solutions are calculated at point $C$ and compared to analytical solution at the final $t=30\mathit{sec}$ moment. ## 4 Modelling C

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

The **C** Modelling is performed on a hardware point using SIMU_POINT_MAT.

The axial deformation imposed during the isotropic compression phase is equal to $-{\sigma}_{0}/3K$ (axial deformation allowing to reach ${\sigma}_{zz}={\sigma}_{0}$ , applied in $t=1s$ ). During the same time interval, the lateral stresses are increased from ${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}=0$ to ${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{0}$ . The maximum axial deformation applied during the deviant phase is ${{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=-0.03\text{\%}$ (final moment $t=30s$ ). During the deviant phase, the lateral stresses are maintained constant, equal to ${\sigma}_{xx}={\sigma}_{yy}={\sigma}_{0}$ .
