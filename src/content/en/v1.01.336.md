# v1.01.336 ZZZZ336 – Validation of subpoint command variables

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the consideration of subpoint command variables by providing keyword AFFE_VARC of the AFFE_MATERIAU command with a field or result created by the SOUS_POINT method of the PROJ_CHAMP command.

The modellings considered are elements DKT, GRILLE_EXCENTREE, POU_D_EM and POU_D_TGM.

For elements DKT and GRILLE_EXCENTREE, the verification consists of projecting a temperature field from a 3D linear thermal calculation to a plate composed of multilayered elements DKT and an excreted grid. The deflection of the slab due to the thermal gradient is calculated in thermo-linear elasticity.

The reference solution comes from an identical calculation with the CREA_RESU option PREP_VARC method.

For modellings POU_D_EM and POU_D_TGM, validation is done in two stages. First, a single-material beam is submitted in support of a temperature field varying according to the Z axis. The results are compared to the same problem modelled by elements 3D. The two-material case, always on a support beam, is then treated by varying the temperature so that the thermal deformation is the same on each fibre, so that no bending is found.

This test also validates the EPVC_ELGA, EPME_ELGA and EPSP_ELGA options for the elements listed above.

## 1 Reference problem : Coques and grilles

### 1.1 Geometry and meshing

A concrete slab of $2m$ length, $1m$ width and $10\mathit{cm}$ thickness is considered. It has a steel sheet oriented on the $X$ axis, composed of $8\mathit{mm}$ diameter bars spaced from $20\mathit{cm}$ with the axis located at $2.5\mathit{cm}$ below the medium plane.

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.336/1000000000000284000000D464723370581BE50D.png)

### 1.2 Material properties

For 3D linear thermal calculation (on concrete only) the properties are:

$\rho {C}_{p}=0$

$\lambda =2W/{m}^{2}/K$

For mechanical calculation the materials are elastic linear:

Concrete: $E=30\mathit{GPa}$ steel: $E=200\mathit{GPa}$

> $\nu =0.2$         $\nu =0.3$
> $\alpha ={10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$         $\alpha =2.{10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$

In the third calculation in which you want to validate the EPSP_ELGA option, you go to the VMIS_ISOT_TRAC law, the parameters of which are as follows:

Concrete: D_SIGM_EPSI: $1E10$ steel: D_SIGM_EPSI: $5E10$

> SY: $5E6$         SY: $1E7$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

For thermal calculation, the temperature shall be imposed on the lower and upper surfaces:

${T}_{inf}=20°C$ et ${T}_{\sup}=50°C$

The initial temperature is ${T}_{\mathit{ini}}=20°C$

For mechanical calculation, the slab is simply pressed on its two supports parallel to $Y$:

- on the $X=0$ edge: $\mathit{DX}=\mathit{DZ}=0$
- on the $X=\mathrm{1m}$ edge: $\mathit{DZ}=0$
- for $Y=-\mathrm{0.5m}$ corner: $\mathit{DY}=0$

The load is to impose the temperature resulting from the 3D thermal calculation.

## 2 Reference solution : Coques and grilles

### 2.1 Method of calculation

In the reference solution, the thermal field is imposed using CREA_RESU option PREP_VARC for multilayer hulls and excreted grids. The temperature field, linear in thickness, varies from $20°C$ in front to $50°C$ in the upper face.

The EVOL_THER field (to subpoints) is transmitted to the mechanical calculation by keyword AFFE_VARC from AFFE_MATERIAU.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The thermal gradient in thickness imposes a mechanical deformation of bending in the slab. The expansion coefficients of the steel material and concrete material were deliberately chosen very different to generate stresses due to differential expansion and thus test the correct functioning of the elements grid at the same time as that of the multilayer hulls.

Constraints generated are uniform in planes parallel to the $\mathit{XY}$ plane

The quantities tested are:

- the vertical displacement of the node $\mathit{C5}$ in the middle of the $Y=-0.5m$ edge
- ${\sigma}_{xx}$ stress in the lower face of the concrete
- ${\sigma}_{xx}$ stress in the top face of the concrete
- ${\sigma}_{xx}$ stress in the steel layer

For thermal deformations of the EPVC_ELGA field, the analytical references are given by the formula ${\mathit{EPTHER}}_{L}=\alpha (\mathit{TEMP}-{\mathit{TEMP}}_{\mathit{REF}})$ . The mechanical deformation field EPME_ELGA is then validated by difference between total deformations and thermal deformations.

For EPSP_ELGA anelastic deformations, the reference is given by the relationship: anelastic deformation = mechanical deformation – elastic deformation.

Elastic deformations are found from the stress field and the E and N coefficients.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

None.

## 3 Reference problem : Multifibre beams

### 3.1 Geometry and meshing

A $2m$ length and square cross section beam is considered on the $0.2m$ side.

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.336/10000000000001C70000007540AD4BFBD08C4EF1.png)

For modellings multifibre beams, the section of the beam has 16 fibres arranged as shown in the following figure:

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.336/1000000000000212000001ECAC6083E05EA76341.png)

The same beam is also represented by a 3D geometry.

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.336/100000000000025E0000007C29CFFBB9A982D053.png)

### 3.2 Material properties

The materials are linear elastic:

Concrete: $E=30\mathit{GPa}$ steel: $E=200\mathit{GPa}$

> $\nu =0.2$         $\nu =0.3$
> $\alpha ={10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$         $\alpha =2.{10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$

In the first calculation (mono-material), the beam is entirely concrete.

In the second and third calculations (bi-material), the fibres under the $Y$ axis are concrete whereas the fibres above are steel.

### 3.3 Boundary conditions and loading

#### 3.3.1 boundary conditions

**Calculated beams 1 and 2:**

The node P1 is blocked in DX, DY, DZ, DRX and DRZ.

The node P2 is blocked in DY and DZ.

**Calculation beam 3:**

The nodes P1 and P2 are clamped.

**3D:**

Nodes on the ENC_0 segment are blocked in DX and DZ.

Nodes on the ENC_1 segment is blocked in DZ.

The nodes P0 and P1 are blocked in DY.

#### 3.3.2 Loading

**Single material scale:**

The temperature is defined as $T=(-200Z+20)t$ . The reference temperature is $0$ .

**Bi-materials:**

A thermal load dependent on $Z$ and time $t$ is imposed. The temperature is defined as follows:

- $T=20t$  si $Z>0$
- $T=40t$  si $Z<0$

The reference temperature is $0$ .

## 4 Reference solution : Multifibre beams

### 4.1 Single-material calculation

#### 4.1.1 Quantities and reference results

The reference solution is the one given by the 3D calculation results. The DZ component of the node N_MIL displacement for the 3D and P3 beams is tested.

#### 4.1.2 Uncertainties about the solution

Uncertainties related to 3D modelling.

### 4.2 First bi-material calculation

#### 4.2.1 Quantities and reference results

The $\alpha$ coefficient values of the two materials and temperature fields assigned to the different fibres result in the same thermal deformation value on each fibre.

If the temperature at the subpoints is correctly taken into account, this load does not induce bending of the beam.

Therefore, the DZ component of the displacement of the node P3 is checked to be zero.

#### 4.2.2 Uncertainties about the solution

None.

### 4.3 Second bimaterial calculation

#### 4.3.1 Quantities and reference results

The thermal load is the same as in the previous calculation, but this time the nodes end is clamped. The total deformation is zero and the thermal deformation is the same as in the previous calculation. Moreover, the mechanical deformation is equal to the thermal deformation.

On a: ${\varepsilon}_{\mathit{th}}=8.E-4$

In the general case, each $i$ fiber is:

${\sigma}_{xx}={E}_{i}({\varepsilon}_{xx}-{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{th}})$

Therefore in the present case:

${\sigma}_{xx}=-{E}_{i}{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{th}}$

Where for concrete fibres:

${\sigma}_{xx}=-2.4E7$

and for steel fibres:

${\sigma}_{xx}=-1.6E8$

#### 4.3.2 Uncertainties about the solution

None.

## 5 Modelling A

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The 3D thermal calculation uses luxuriant Modelling (3D_DIAG). However, since temperature is imposed on both sides of the element, it is determined everywhere. The thermal solver is used here only to generate the EVOL_THER field to project. The mechanical calculation uses multilayer elements DKT for concrete and GRILLE_EXCENTRE for steel. The number of layers in the DKT is 10. The temperature resulting from the 3D thermal calculation is projected on the layers of the elements DKT and on the elements GRILLE using the SOUS_POINT method of the PROJ_CHAMP command.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

For thermal calculation the mesh 3D is composed of one mesh cell HEXA8.

For mechanical calculation the mesh of the average plane is made with 30 elements QUAD4. The elements for grids are generated in the command file with CREA_MAILLAGE.

## 8 Summary of the results

The sub-point control variables are taken into account by providing AFFE_VARC with a field or result created by the SOUS_POINT method of the PROJ_CHAMP command, and validated for elements DKT multi-layer, elements GRILLE, elements POU_D_EM and POU_D_TGM. Similarly, this test validates the EPVC_ELGA, ELME_ELGA and EPSP_ELGA options for elements present in the modellings of this test.
