# r3.07.02 Numerical modelling of thin structures: axisymmetric thermo-elasto-plastic shells

**Summary:**

A numerical formulation is presented for the modelling of structures whose middle surface has a particular geometry: shells with symmetry of revolution about the axis $\mathit{Oy}$ (modelling COQUE_AXIS).

The isotropic thermo-elasto-plastic case is described completely, within the framework of the theories of LOVE‑KIRCHHOFF and of HENCKY-MINDLIN-REISSNER, as well as the various loadings studied, for the isoparametric finite element chosen.

## 1 Introduction

One is concerned in what follows with the mechanical modelling of thin structures whose middle surface has a particular geometry: shells with symmetry of revolution about the axis $\mathit{Oy}$

More particularly, one restricts oneself to the case in which the mechanical parameters (materials, loadings) are independent of one spatial direction (the circumference for the shells of revolution).

For the solution of chained thermomechanical problems, one must beforehand use the thermal shell finite element described in [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01) in its axisymmetric version, according to the case.

One first reviews below the description of the mechanical model: kinematics, thermo-elasto-plastic constitutive law. The finite element chosen, the interpolation and the integration method are then presented.

Finally, a few numerical application results are given, by comparison with analytical solutions.

## 2 Continuous problem

The geometry is defined in a one-dimensional manner by the meridian in the plane $(Oxy)$ for a shell of revolution.

### 2.1 Description of the geometry, of the kinematics

One considers a shell of revolution of axis $\mathit{Oy}$ . For this shell, the middle surface is defined by the curve $\omega =\text{AB}$ in the plane $Oxz$ : $\omega$ is a meridian for the shell of revolution.

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.02/10000200000000AF000000E95CFAB64A3DC7E4AE.png)

Figure 2.1-1: Shell of revolution

The curve $\omega =\text{AB}$ is parametrised by the curvilinear abscissa $s$ . The partial derivatives $\frac{\partial A}{\partial s}$ of a quantity $A$ will be denoted by ${A}_{,s}$ . At a point $m$ of $\omega$ one defines the local frame $(n,t,{e}_{z})$ by:

$$
t=\frac{{\text{Om}}_{,s}}{\mathrm{\parallel }{\text{Om}}_{,s}\mathrm{\parallel }}
$$

One also denotes the angle $\alpha$ such that:

$$
n=\cos\alpha {e}_{x}+\sin\alpha {e}_{y}
$$

The curvature of $\omega$ is defined by:

$$
\frac{1}{R}=-n\cdot {t}_{,s}={\alpha}_{,s}
$$

The position on the parallel passing through $m$ is denoted $\theta$ . The tangent vector on this parallel is ${e}_{\theta}$ . For the meridian lying in the plane $\mathit{Oxz}$ , $\theta =0$ and ${e}_{\theta}=-{e}_{z}$ . The radius of curvature of the parallel at $m$ is:

$$
{R}_{\theta}=\frac{r}{\cos\alpha }
$$

where $r$ is the abscissa $x$ of the point $m$ of $\omega$ . The kinematic transformations of the shell are defined by the displacement $U$ of the point $m$ of the middle surface, as well as by the rotation ${\beta}_{s}$ of the normal $n$ at the point $m$ . The vector $U$ may be expressed in the local basis:

$$
{U}_{(s)}={U}_{(s)}\cdot {t}_{(s)}+{W}_{(s)}\cdot {n}_{(s)}
$$

or in the Cartesian basis:

$$
{U}_{(s)}={u}_{x}(s)\cdot {e}_{x}+{u}_{y}(s)\cdot {e}_{y}
$$

The shell strains associated with this transformation $(U,{\beta}_{s})$ are determined by:

- a membrane strain tensor $E$ ,
- a curvature-change tensor $K$ ,
- a transverse distortion vector $\gamma$ .

The latter appears in the shell theory of HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI and not in that of LOVE. As a function of the displacement $U$ and of the rotation ${\beta}_{s}$ , these quantities are expressed (cf. [bib1]) in the case where $U$ is expressed in the local basis $(n,t,{e}_{z})$ :

$$
{E}_{\mathit{ss}}={U}_{,s}+\frac{W}{R}
{E}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{r}(-U\sin\alpha +W\cos\alpha )
{K}_{\mathit{ss}}={\beta}_{s,s}
{K}_{\theta \theta }=-\frac{\sin\alpha }{r}{\beta}_{s}
{\gamma}_{s}={\beta}_{s}+{W}_{,s}-\frac{U}{R}
$$

And for the case where $U$ is expressed in the global basis $({e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z})$ :

$$
{E}_{\mathit{ss}}={u}_{y,s}\cos\alpha -{u}_{x,s}\sin\alpha
{E}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{u}_{x}}{r}
{K}_{\mathit{ss}}={\beta}_{s,s}
{K}_{\theta \theta }=-\frac{\sin\alpha }{r}{\beta}_{s}
{\gamma}_{s}={\beta}_{s}+{u}_{x,s}\cos\alpha +{u}_{y,s}\sin\alpha
$$

**Note:**

*The change of sense of the curvilinear abscissa* $s$ *does not modify the values of:* ${\beta}_{s}$ ${E}_{\mathit{ss}}$ and ${E}_{\theta \theta }$ *but changes the sign of* $\alpha$ *,* $U$ *,* $W$ *,* $R$ *,* ${K}_{\mathit{ss}}$ *and* ${K}_{\theta \theta }$ *.*

Within the framework of LOVE theory, the condition ${\gamma}_{s}=0$ (the normals to the shell remain so after deformation) is expressed by a direct relation between the rotations ${\beta}_{s}$ and the slope ${W}_{,s}$ . The components of the curvature-change tensor are, as a function of the displacement in the local basis:

$$
{K}_{\mathit{ss}}=-{W}_{,\mathit{ss}}+\frac{{U}_{,s}}{R}-U\frac{{R}_{,s}}{{R}^{2}}
$$

If the displacement is expressed in the global basis:

| ${K}_{\text{ss}}=\frac{1}{R}\left({u}_{x,s}\sin\alpha -{u}_{y,s}\cos\alpha \right)-{u}_{x,\text{ss}}\cos\alpha -{u}_{y,\text{ss}}\sin\alpha$ and ${K}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{\sin\alpha }{r}({u}_{x,s}\cos\alpha +{u}_{y,s}\sin\alpha )$ |  |
| --- | --- |

One notes that the expression of the curvature changes as a function of the displacement in LOVE theory is rather complicated and that it involves second derivatives. If one requires a conforming interpolation, that is to say here ${C}^{1}$ , this necessitates the use of finite elements of high degree.

The tensors $E$ , $K$ and $\gamma$ make it possible to express the three-dimensional strain $\varepsilon$ through the thickness. One denotes by ${x}_{3}$ the position through the thickness $\left]-\frac{h}{2},\frac{h}{2}\right[$ with respect to the middle fibre, at the point $m$ , of curvilinear abscissa $s$ on $\omega$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.02/10000200000000AB000000B1D0A1129EAFA01FCA.png)

At a point through the thickness, the displacement is expressed in the global frame:

$$
U(s,{x}_{3})=({u}_{x}(s)-{\beta}_{s}(s).{x}_{3}\sin\alpha (s)).{e}_{x}+({u}_{y}(s)+{\beta}_{s}(s).{x}_{3}\cos\alpha (s)).{e}_{y}
$$

In order to take into account the variation of the metric through the thickness (due to the curvature of the middle surface), one defines the functions:

$$
{\rho}_{s}\left({x}_{3}\right)=1+\frac{{x}_{3}}{R}
$$

For a sufficiently thin shell, this correction is negligible:

$$
{\rho}_{s}\approx 1
$$

In practice this correction, carried out if MODI_METRIQUE=’OUI’ in AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01), is useless if the ratios $\frac{h}{R}$ and $\frac{h}{{R}_{\theta}}$ , when they exist, are less than $\frac{1}{15}$ .

In HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI theory, the components of the strain tensor $\varepsilon$ are:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{\text{ss}}\left(s,{x}_{3}\right)=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{s}}\left({E}_{\text{ss}}+{x}_{3}{K}_{\text{ss}}\right)\\ {\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }\left(s,{x}_{3}\right)=\frac{1}{{\rho}_{\theta}}\left({E}_{\theta \theta }+{x}_{3}{K}_{\theta \theta }\right)\\ {\epsilon}_{{\text{sx}}_{3}}\left(s,{x}_{3}\right)=\frac{1}{2{\rho}_{s}}{\gamma}_{s}\end{array}
$$

### 2.2 Thermo-elasto-plastic equilibrium

One considers that the material constituting the shell is isotropic thermo-elasto-plastic. One makes the commonly accepted assumption that the transverse normal stress is zero ${\sigma}_{{x}_{3}{x}_{3}}\mathrm{\equiv }0$ . The most general constitutive law is then written:

$$
(\begin{array}{c}{s}_{11}\\ {s}_{22}\\ {s}_{{\mathrm{1x}}_{3}}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{ccc}{C}_{1111}& {C}_{1122}& 0\\ {C}_{2211}& {C}_{2222}& 0\\ 0& 0& {C}_{11{x}_{3}{x}_{3}}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{11}-{\varepsilon}_{11}^{\text{th}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{22}-{\varepsilon}_{22}^{\text{th}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{{\mathrm{1x}}_{3}}\end{array})
$$

where $C(\varepsilon ,\mu )$ of components ${C}_{ijkl}$ is the local constitutive matrix in plane stress and $\mu$ represents the set of internal variables when the behaviour is non-linear. In what follows the index $1$ refers to the curvilinear abscissa $s$ and $2$ to $\theta$ . With the three-dimensional strains defined above, one then associates the components of the stress tensor $\sigma$ :

$$
\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{ss}}={C}_{\text{ssss}}({\varepsilon}_{\text{ss}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{ss}}^{\text{th}})+{C}_{\text{ss}\theta \theta }({\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }-{\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }^{\text{th}})\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }={C}_{\theta \theta \text{ss}}({\varepsilon}_{\text{ss}}-{\varepsilon}_{\text{ss}}^{\text{th}})+{C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }({\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }-{\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }^{\text{th}})\\ {\sigma}_{{\text{sx}}_{3}}=\text{}{C}_{{\text{ssx}}_{3}{x}_{3}}{\varepsilon}_{{\text{sx}}_{3}}\end{array}
$$

From this one draws the expression of the elastic strain energy, from which one will deduce the stiffness matrix as a function of the shell kinematics seen in paragraph [§ 2.1 ]:

$$
{W}^{\text{él}}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\left[\begin{array}{c}{C}_{\mathit{ssss}}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{ss}}^{2}+\\ {C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }{\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }^{2}+\\ ({C}_{\theta \theta }+{C}_{\theta \theta \mathit{ss}}){\epsilon}_{\mathit{ss}}{\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }+\\ {\mathrm{2C}}_{{\mathit{ssx}}_{3}{x}_{3}}{\epsilon}_{{\mathit{sx}}_{3}^{2}}\end{array}\right]\left({\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{\theta}-1\right)r\mathit{ds}d\theta {\mathit{dx}}_{3}
$$

**Note:**

*In thermoelasticity, if one denotes by* $E$ *YOUNG's modulus and by* $\nu$ *POISSON's ratio, one has* ${C}_{\text{iiii}}=\frac{E}{1-{\nu}^{2}}$ *,* ${C}_{\text{iijj}}=\frac{E\nu }{1-{\nu}^{2}}\forall (i,j)\in \left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace$ *and* ${C}_{11{x}_{3}{x}_{3}}=\frac{E}{1+\nu }$

One defines the following quantities:

- the membrane stiffness:

$$
\left[{C}_{ij}\right]={\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\frac{{\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{\theta}-1}{{\rho}_{i}{\rho}_{j}}.\text{}\left[\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{\text{ssss}}& {C}_{\text{ss}\theta \theta }\\ {C}_{\theta \theta \text{ss}}& {C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }\end{array}\right]{\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

which is equal to $\frac{\text{Eh}}{1-{\nu}^{2}}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& \nu \\ \nu & 1\end{array}\right]$ in elasticity and in the absence of a metric correction through the thickness;

- the membrane–bending coupling stiffness:

$$
\left[{B}_{ij}\right]={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}{x}_{3}.\frac{{\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{q}-1}{{\rho}_{i}{\rho}_{j}}.\text{}\left[\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{\text{ssss}}& {C}_{\text{ss}\theta \theta }\\ {C}_{\theta \theta \text{ss}}& {C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }\end{array}\right]{\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

which is zero in elasticity and in the absence of a metric correction through the thickness;

- the bending stiffness:

$$
\left[{D}_{ij}\right]={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}{x}_{3}^{2}.\frac{{\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{\theta}-1}{{\rho}_{i}{\rho}_{j}}.\left[\begin{array}{cc}{C}_{\text{ssss}}& {C}_{\text{ss}\theta \theta }\\ {C}_{\theta \theta \text{ss}}& {C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }\end{array}\right]{\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

which is equal to $\frac{{\text{Eh}}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}\left[\begin{array}{cc}1& \nu \\ \nu & 1\end{array}\right]$ in elasticity and in the absence of a metric correction through the thickness;

- the transverse-distortion stiffness:

$$
{G}_{{\text{sx}}_{3}}={\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}\text{}\frac{{\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{\theta}-1}{{\rho}_{s}^{2}}.{C}_{{\text{ssx}}_{3}{x}_{3}}{\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

which is equal to $\frac{\text{Eh}}{1+\nu }$ in elasticity and in the absence of a metric correction through the thickness.

In elasticity, the coefficients ${C}_{\mathit{ss}}^{C}$ , ${B}_{\mathit{ss}}^{C}$ and ${D}_{\mathit{ss}}^{C}$ are the products of the coefficients ${C}_{\mathit{ss}}^{D}$ , ${B}_{\mathit{ss}}^{D}$ and ${D}_{\mathit{ss}}^{D}$ by $1-{\nu}^{2}$ . One can thus express the elastic energy as a function of the shell strain tensors $E$ , $K$ and $\gamma$ by:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{W}^{\text{él}}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{\omega}^{}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }[{C}_{\text{ss}}{E}_{\text{ss}}^{2}+{\mathrm{2B}}_{\text{ss}}{E}_{\text{ss}}{K}_{\text{ss}}+{D}_{\text{ss}}{K}_{\text{ss}}^{2}+{C}_{\theta \theta }{E}_{\theta \theta }^{2}+{\mathrm{2B}}_{\theta \theta }{E}_{\theta \theta }{K}_{\theta \theta }+{D}_{\text{qq}}{K}_{\theta \theta }^{2}\\ \text{}+2\left({C}_{s\theta }{E}_{\text{ss}}{E}_{\theta \theta }+{B}_{s\theta }({E}_{\text{ss}}{K}_{\theta \theta }+{E}_{\theta \theta }{K}_{\text{ss}})+{D}_{s\theta }{K}_{\text{ss}}.{K}_{\theta \theta }\right)\\ \text{}\text{}+\frac{{G}_{{\text{sx}}_{3}}}{2}{\gamma}_{s}^{2}]r.\text{ds}.d\theta \end{array}
$$

To these expressions, one must add the potential associated with the thermal stresses, which will be a contribution to the right-hand side (which one will express below in the global frame):

$$
{L}_{(V)}^{\text{th}}={\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\left[\alpha (T-{T}^{\text{réf}})(({C}_{\text{ssss}}+{C}_{\text{ss}\theta \theta }){\varepsilon}_{\text{ss}}+({C}_{\theta \theta \text{ss}}+{C}_{\theta \theta \theta \theta }){\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta })\right]rd\theta {\text{dx}}_{3}\text{ds}
$$

This expression, for an isotropic elastic behaviour, becomes:

$$
{L}_{(V)}^{\text{th}}={\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\left[\frac{E\alpha }{1-\nu }(T-{T}^{\text{réf}})(\frac{{v}_{x}}{r}-{v}_{x,s}\sin\alpha +{v}_{y,s}\cos\alpha +{x}_{3}({\beta}_{s,s}-\frac{\sin\alpha }{r}{\beta}_{s}))\right]rd\theta {\text{dx}}_{3}\text{ds}
$$

In this expression, one has deliberately neglected the metric correction through the thickness (terms in ${\rho}_{s}$ and ${\rho}_{\theta}$ seen for the stiffness). Moreover the temperature $T$ which appears is defined by the three-field thermal shell model (cf. [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01)):

$$
T(s,{x}_{3})={T}^{m}(s).(1-{(\frac{{x}_{3}}{h})}^{2})+{T}^{s}(s)\frac{{x}_{3}}{\mathrm{2h}}(1+\frac{{x}_{3}}{h})+{T}^{i}(s)\frac{{x}_{3}}{\mathrm{2h}}(-1+\frac{{x}_{3}}{h})
$$

From this expression, one deduces the generalised-force tensors $N$ and $M$ (normal forces and bending moments) associated with the generalised strains $E$ and $K$ by the principle of virtual work. They are linked to the three-dimensional stress tensor ${\tau}_{\alpha \beta }$ by:

$$
{N}_{\alpha \beta }={\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{\tau}_{\alpha \beta }{\text{dx}}_{3}
$$

where one has neglected the variations of the metric through the thickness.

**Note:**

*Transverse-shear energy*

*The shell model presented above, known as that of HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI, rests on a kinematic assumption: the parameters* $W$ *and* ${\beta}_{s}$ *denote the normal displacement of the point* $m$ *of the middle surface* $\omega$ *and the rotation of the normal vector* $n$ *.*

*One also frequently finds the model known as that of REISSNER, which rests on a static assumption for the distribution of the transverse-shear stresses. The deduced kinematic parameters* $W$ *and* ${\beta}_{S}$ *in this model are weighted averages through the thickness of the normal displacement and of the local rotations. If one wishes to place oneself in this framework, it suffices to assign the coefficient* $\kappa =5/6$ *to the transverse-shear energy term (in* ${\gamma}_{s}^{2}$ ). (cf. [bib7], [bib9]).

*Finally, if one wishes, for a thin shell, to place oneself within the framework of the LOVE‑KIRCHHOFF model, one can neutralise the shear energy with a large value of* $\kappa$ *(which penalises the condition* ${\gamma}_{s}=0$ *), for example* ${10}^{6}h/R$ *, where* $h$ *is the thickness and* $R$ a characteristic radius of curvature or a characteristic distance of the loadings: (cf. [bib 2]). In practice the user can supply the value of $\kappa$ *under the keyword* *A_CIS of the command* *AFFE_CARA_ELEM* [U4.42.01](doc:u4.42.01).

## 3 Formulation of the finite element and discretisation

### 3.1 Description of the finite element chosen

#### 3.1.1 Motivations

The choice of the HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI framework to describe the shell kinematics, presented in paragraph [§ 2 ], leads to expressions of the strains in which the derivatives are limited to order 1, unlike the LOVE-KIRCHHOFF model. This offers the advantage of being able to use a finite element of limited order while ensuring conformity. The natural choice is the LAGRANGE element $\mathit{P2}$ , isoparametric, which makes it possible to have a fine representation of a curved geometry and good estimates of the stresses.

The degrees of freedom are of course the displacements and the rotations.

As stated previously, the LOVE-KIRCHHOFF model can be recovered by penalisation for a very large parameter $\kappa$ affecting the transverse-shear energy.

This formulation joins the category of shell finite elements known as “degenerated”, that is to say built by injecting the shell kinematics into elements of three-dimensional continuous media: cf. [bib10].

As for all shell finite elements, particular aspects must be analysed: the taking into account of the rigid-body modes and the risks of membrane or shear locking.

In the case of the axisymmetric shell of revolution, there is only one rigid-body mode: the translation along the axis of symmetry $\mathit{Oy}$ .

For the finite element to be efficient, it is necessary that the approximations retained for the description of the displacement ensure an exact representation of the state of zero strains (rigid-body mode). In practice, as the notion of rigid-body mode is defined with respect to the global frame, one therefore decides to describe the displacements in the global basis $({e}_{x},{e}_{y})$ , in which the rigid-body modes (affine functions) are represented by the interpolation chosen.

As regards the risks of locking in membrane and in transverse shear, the usual treatment consists in a selective numerical integration (cf. [bib2]), but practice reveals that these phenomena rarely appear for shells of revolution.

#### 3.1.2 General presentation of the element

The reference element chosen is quadratic, isoparametric, with three nodes and three degrees of freedom per node. These degrees of freedom are (see figure ):

| ${u}_{x},{u}_{y}$ : | components of the displacement $U$ in the global frame, |
| --- | --- |
| ${\beta}_{s}$ : | the rotation about ${e}_{z}$ of the normal $n$ . |

This element is a generalisation of the curved plane-beam element. It is well suited to the discretisation of shells with variable meridional curvature $R$ , cf. [bib2].

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.02/10000200000000FF000000BD024AA02D236809EF.png)

Figure 3.1.2-1: Reference element

The shape functions (basis functions) are the LAGRANGE polynomials:

$$
{\widehat{N}}_{1}\left(\xi \right)=\xi \frac{-1+\xi }{2}
{\widehat{N}}_{2}\left(\xi \right)=\xi \frac{1+\xi }{2}
$$

#### 3.1.3 Transformations from the finite element to the reference finite element

![Figure](/figures/r3.07.02/10000200000001C300000089BBEE10482AD0379A.png)

The geometry is interpolated by means of the coordinates $({x}_{i},{y}_{i})$ of the three nodes $\mathit{N1}$ , $\mathit{N3}$ and $\mathit{N2}$ :

$$
x\left(\xi \right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{x}_{i}{\widehat{N}}_{i}\left(\xi \right)
$$

Likewise, by means of the degrees of freedom $({u}_{{x}_{i}},{u}_{{y}_{i}},{\beta}_{{s}_{i}})$ at the nodes, one has:

$$
{u}_{x}\left(\xi \right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{u}_{{x}_{i}}{\widehat{N}}_{i}\left(\xi \right)
{u}_{y}\left(\xi \right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}{u}_{{y}_{i}}{\widehat{N}}_{i}\left(\xi \right)
$$

One also needs the Jacobian of the transformation:

$$
m(\xi )=\frac{\text{ds}}{d\xi }(\xi )=\sqrt{{({x}_{,\xi })}^{2}+{({y}_{,\xi })}^{2}}
$$

and the vectors of the local basis:

$$
t\left(\xi \right)=\frac{1}{m\left(\xi \right)}\left({x}_{,\xi }{e}_{x}+{y}_{,\xi }{e}_{z}\right)
$$

Finally:

$$
\cos\alpha =\frac{{y}_{,\xi }}{m\left(\xi \right)}
$$

The meridional curvature is obtained by:

$$
\frac{1}{R}=-(n.{t}_{,\xi }).\frac{d\xi }{\text{ds}}=\frac{1}{{m}^{3}(\xi )}({x}_{,\xi }.{y}_{,\xi \xi }-{y}_{,\xi }.{x}_{,\xi \xi })
$$

Because of the interpolation $\mathit{P2}$ , the second derivatives which appear below are expressed by means of the coordinates of the three nodes by:

$$
{x}_{,\xi \xi }={x}_{1}+{x}_{2}-2{x}_{3}
$$

#### 3.1.4 Surface numerical integration

For the numerical integrations along the element one uses a numerical integration formula with four GAUSS points, unique for all the terms to be integrated. This formula brings out the lockings mentioned in paragraph [§ 3.1.1 ] in the case of extremely localised plastification. One therefore advises avoiding the use of these elements in plasticity for the moment. The numerical integration formula with four Gauss points will subsequently be replaced by a formula with two Gauss points, which is supposed to avoid these inconveniences.

#### 3.1.5 Numerical integration through the thickness

For an elastic behaviour, inasmuch as one accepts that one restricts oneself to elastic characteristics uniform through the thickness, the stiffnesses $\left[{C}_{ij}\right]$ , $\left[{B}_{ij}\right]$ , $\left[{D}_{ij}\right]$ and ${G}_{{\mathit{sx}}_{3}}$ defined in paragraph [§ 2.2 ] are computed exactly.

**For a non-linear behaviour**, one subdivides the initial thickness into $N$ layers of identical thicknesses numbered in the direction of the normal to the middle surface of the element. For each layer one uses three integration points. The integration points are located on the upper skin of the layer, at the middle of the layer and on the lower skin of the layer. For $N$ layers, the number of integration points is $\mathrm{2N}+1$ . One advises using from 3 to 5 layers through the thickness for a number of integration points equal to 7, 9 and 11 respectively.

For each layer, one computes the stress state $({\sigma}_{11,}{\sigma}_{22,}{\sigma}_{12})$ and the set of internal variables, at the middle of the layer and on the upper and lower skins of the layer, from the local plastic behaviour and the local strain field $({\varepsilon}_{11,}{\varepsilon}_{22,}{\varepsilon}_{12})$ . The positioning of the integration points allows us to have the most accurate estimates, because they are not extrapolated, on the lower and upper skins of the layer, where one knows that the stresses are liable to be maximal. The plastic behaviour does not for the moment include the transverse-shear terms, which are treated in an elastic manner, because the transverse shear is uncoupled from the membrane behaviour in plane stress.

**For a thermoelastic behaviour**, one uses the integration, by layer through the thickness $\left]-\frac{h}{2},+\frac{h}{2}\right[$ described previously in the non-linear domain, of the thermomechanical terms seen in paragraph [§ 2.2 ]. It is then necessary to use STAT_NON_LINE with an elastic behaviour.

One has already mentioned in [§ 2.2 ]. and in [R3.07.04](doc:r3.07.04) that the value of the transverse-shear correction coefficient for the shell elements was obtained by identification of the *complementary elastic energies after solution of the 3D equilibrium. This method is no longer usable in elasto-plasticity and the choice of the transverse-shear correction coefficient then arises. The transverse-shear terms are therefore not affected by the plasticity and are treated elastically, for want of a better approach. In the case where one places oneself in Love–Kirchhoff theory for a value of this coefficient of* ${10}^{6}h/R$ *(* $h$ *being the thickness of the shell and* $R$ *its mean radius of curvature) the transverse-shear terms become negligible and the approach is more rigorous.*

### 3.2 Formulation of the elementary terms

#### 3.2.1 Mass, centre of gravity, inertia matrix

In the case of shells of revolution, the mass is:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\rho ({\rho}_{s}+{\rho}_{\theta}-1){\text{dx}}_{3}rd\theta \text{ds}={\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }\rho hrd\theta \text{ds}=2\pi \rho h{\int}_{\omega}r\text{ds}
$$

where $\rho$ is the density, assumed constant, of the element. The position of the centre of inertia is given in the frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$ of [§ 2.1 ] by:

$$
\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{x}_{G}=0\\ {y}_{G}=\frac{{\int}_{\omega}\text{yr}\text{ds}+\frac{{h}^{2}}{12}{\int}_{\omega}\sin\alpha \left(\frac{1}{R}+\frac{\cos\alpha }{r}\right)\text{rds}}{{\int}_{\omega}r\text{ds}}\\ {z}_{G}=0\end{array}
$$

The terms of the inertia matrix with respect to $O$ in the frame $\mathit{Oxyz}$ of [§ 2.1 ] then have as their expression:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{I}_{xx/O}=2\pi \rho {\int}_{\omega}\left[h(\frac{{x}^{2}}{2}+{y}^{2})+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}({\sin}^{2}\alpha +\frac{{\cos}^{2}\alpha }{2}+\delta x\cos\alpha +2\delta y\sin\alpha )\right]\text{rds}\\ {I}_{yy/O}=2\pi \rho {\int}_{\omega}\left[{\text{hx}}^{2}+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}({\cos}^{2}\alpha +2\delta x\cos\alpha )\right]\text{rds}\\ {I}_{zz/O}=2\pi \rho {\int}_{\omega}\left[h(\frac{{x}^{2}}{2}+{y}^{2})+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}({\sin}^{2}\alpha +\frac{{\cos}^{2}\alpha }{2}+\delta x\cos\alpha +2\delta y\sin\alpha )\right]\text{rds}\end{array}
$$

where $\delta =\frac{1}{R}+\frac{\cos\alpha }{r}$ .

#### 3.2.2 Mass matrix

The kinetic-energy term is treated by considering the density $\rho$ constant through the thickness and the metric correction due to the curvature negligible:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\rho v(s,{x}_{3}).\bar{v}(s,{x}_{3})r{\mathit{dx}}_{3}d\theta \text{ds}
$$

The integrand is split into three terms. The translational kinetic energy:

$$
\rho h({u}_{x}.{\bar{u}}_{x}+{u}_{y}.{\bar{u}}_{y})
$$

The rotational kinetic energy:

$$
\rho \frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\beta}_{s.}{\bar{\beta}}_{s}
$$

And the coupling kinetic energy:

$$
\rho \frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\delta (-\sin\alpha ({u}_{x}{\bar{\beta}}_{s}+{\bar{u}}_{x}{\beta}_{s})+\cos\alpha ({u}_{y}{\bar{\beta}}_{s}+{\bar{u}}_{y}{\beta}_{s}))
$$

With

$\delta =\frac{1}{R}+\frac{\cos\alpha }{r}$ .

#### 3.2.3 Right-hand side of centrifugal force

In the case of shells of revolution, one considers a rotation vector $\Omega ={\omega}_{2}.{e}_{y}$ , carried by the axis of revolution. The corresponding right-hand-side term is:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}\rho {\omega}_{2}^{2}.r({\bar{u}}_{x}-{\bar{\beta}}_{s}.{x}_{3}\sin\alpha ){\text{dx}}_{3}rd\theta \text{ds}\\ ={\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }h\rho {\omega}_{2}^{2}{r}^{2}.{\bar{u}}_{x}d\theta \text{ds}\end{array}
$$

Neglecting the metric correction through the thickness). The right-hand side is then:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}h\rho {\omega}_{3}^{2}(x.{\bar{u}}_{x}+y.{\bar{u}}_{y})\text{ds}
$$

#### 3.2.4 Right-hand side of gravity

Gravity is directed along ${e}_{y}$ . The right-hand side is:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }{\rho gh\bar{u}}_{y}rd\theta \text{ds}
$$

And therefore:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}\rho ({g}_{x}.{e}_{x}+{g}_{y}.{e}_{y})\text{ds}
$$

#### 3.2.5 Right-hand side of distributed loads

These distributed loads may be two forces in the plane $(\mathit{xOy})$ and the torque ${M}_{z}$ carried by the axis $\mathit{Oz}$ . The two forces, which one considers to be applied on the middle surface $\omega$ , may be supplied in the global frame $({e}_{x},{e}_{y})$ or the local frame $(t,n)$ . The right-hand side is:

$$
{\int}_{\omega}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }({F}_{x}{\bar{u}}_{x}+{F}_{y}{\bar{u}}_{y}+{M}_{z}{\bar{\beta}}_{s})rd\theta \text{ds}
$$

**Note:**

*The point actions are treated as nodal forces where they are applied, since they work in the degrees of freedom of the finite element.*

### 3.3 Computation of the strains and of the stresses

After solution, one has the possibility with the operator CALC_CHAMP [U4.81.04](doc:u4.81.04) of computing at the nodes the following elementary fields:

- the generalised strains ${E}_{\alpha \beta }$ and ${K}_{\alpha \beta }$ : option DEGE_ELNO,
- the three-dimensional strains ${\epsilon}_{\alpha \beta }$ on the middle fibre and on the inner and outer skins (with or without curvature correction): option EPSI_ELNO,
- the three-dimensional stresses ${\sigma}_{\alpha \beta }$ on the middle fibre and on the inner and outer skins (with or without curvature correction): option SIGM_ELNO,
- the generalised forces ${N}_{\alpha \beta }$ and ${M}_{\alpha \beta }$ (with or without curvature correction): option EFGE_ELNO.

These values at the nodes are obtained by extrapolation from the values at the GAUSS points of the element, according to the method set out in [bib4] [R3.06.03](doc:r3.06.03). Finally, one can also have the values ${N}_{\alpha \beta }$ and ${M}_{\alpha \beta }$ at the GAUSS points of the element: option SIEF_ELGA. No post-processing of stresses or of generalised forces is for the moment available for non-linear material behaviours.

## 4 Conclusion

The finite elements which we propose were chosen with a very particular aim: analysis of thin axisymmetric structures with the concern of obtaining a good accuracy on the membrane and flexural solution while having an element that is simple to implement and not too costly.

The choice of the degrees of freedom allows a good representation of the boundary conditions. Moreover, this displacement and rotation formulation leads to elements of lower degree: the elements are $\mathit{P2}$ in membrane and $\mathit{P2}$ in bending. It appears that they are easy to handle and that their formulation makes it possible to use a simple pre- and post-processor structure, a non-negligible advantage for producing fairly fine meshes (one-dimensional) and for visualising the results easily (on a simple curve). The kinematics chosen: formulation of HENCKY-MINDLIN-NAGHDI, in displacements and rotations of the middle surface, makes it possible to introduce the transverse-shear energy (of interest for shells of moderate thickness).

This energy may be affected by a correction factor $k$ : if one wishes to place oneself in REISSNER theory, it suffices to choose $k=5/6$ instead of 1 (but of course, the deflection $W$ and the rotations $\beta$ are in this theory only weighted averages through the thickness). Moreover, the LOVE-KIRCHHOFF shell formulation (for very thin structures) can be simulated by penalisation of the condition of vanishing of the transverse distortion, by choosing a factor $k={10}^{6}\times ` , :math:`h$ being the thickness and $L$ a characteristic distance (radius of curvature, zone of application of the loads…).

The non-linear behaviours in plane stress are available for these elements. One points out, however, that the stresses generated by the transverse distortion are treated elastically, for want of a better approach. Indeed, the taking into account of a non-zero constant transverse shear through the thickness and the determination of the associated correction on the shear stiffness relative to a model satisfying the boundary conditions are not possible and therefore make the use of these elements, when the transverse shear is non-zero, rigorously impossible in plasticity. Rigorously, for non-linear behaviours, one should therefore use these elements within the framework of Love–Kirchhoff theory.

Elements corresponding to the mechanical elements exist in thermal analysis; the thermomechanical chainings are therefore available with three-node thermal shell finite elements described in [R3.11.01](doc:r3.11.01) in its axisymmetric version.

In the test cases treated, the locking phenomena did not appear. The decomposition of the strain energy will make it possible, if need be, to integrate selectively the terms responsible for the locking, such a modification not being expected to pose any particular difficulty. A more detailed study must of course be carried out on this subject, as regards the numerical methods to be used to avoid this locking when the thickness becomes small.

The developments that can be envisaged are:

- anisotropy, in order to be able to treat multilayer shells,
- buckling problems,
- the decomposition into FOURIER series in order to study non-axisymmetric problems of shells of revolution,
- the taking into account of a variable thickness …
