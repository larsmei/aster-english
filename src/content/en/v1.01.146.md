# v1.01.146 ZZZZ146 - Computer validation of LEM methods in CALC_STAB_PENTE macro-command

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the computer functions of stability analysis of the
gradients by methods based on the equilibrium limit theory (LEM) of the macro-command
CALC_STAB_PENTE.

In this test case the stability of a homogeneous slope is analysed considering
Water infiltration into the soil.

**This test case has no physical sense or solution
Scientific reference.**

- **Model A**: Calculation of the safety factor according to the Drucker-Prager criterion
using Bishop method. This Modelling validates the following features:

  - Calculation of the safety factor by the simplified Bishop method.
  - Recovery of shear strength properties from the constitutive law Drucker-Prager.
  - Adaptation of the mesh with circular fracture surfaces.
  - Consideration of water infiltration into the structure.
  - Optimization algorithm for circular surfaces with limited radius terminals.
- **Model B**: Calculation of the safety factor according to Mohr-Coulomb criterion by method
Morgenstern-Price. This Modelling validates the following features:

  - Calculation of safety factor by Morgenstern-Price method.
  - Recovery of shear strength properties from the constitutive law Mohr-Coulomb.
  - Adaptation of mesh with non-circular fracture surfaces.
  - Consideration of water infiltration into the structure.
  - Optimization algorithm EFWA.
  - Resumption of the EFWA calculation by defining the initial state.
- **Model C**: Calculation of the safety factor according to the Druck-Prager criterion by
the Fellenius method.

This Modelling is identical with Modelling A apart from the method of calculating the FS.

- **Modelling D**: Calculation of safety factor according to Mohr-Coulomb
by the Spencer method.

This Modelling is identical with Modelling B apart from the method of calculating the MSDS.

- **Modelling E**: Definition of the pore pressure field by `COEF_RU`.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and mesh

![Figure](/figures/v1.01.146/10000000000004F3000001F6F90A46D4FEA014CE.png)

***Geometry and mesh of the homogeneous slope***

A slope (slope 1:2) consisting of the homogeneous material of height 4.5m is considered.
The model is 18m wide and 6.5m high.

The right side is defined as the upstream surface in contact with water
(river or water reservoir), and slope as the surface swallows.

The mesh is shown in fig1-geom-mail.
This is the same model used in the test case ssnp006 ([V6.03.006](doc:v6.03.006))
but with a rough mesh to speed up the calculation.

### 1.2 Material properties

The same material properties are adopted as the test case ssnp006, i.e.:

- Unit mass of soil: ${\gamma}_{\mathit{sol}}=16\mathit{kN}$
- Ground friction angle: $\varphi =20deg$
- Soil Cohesion: $c=5\mathit{kN}/{m}^{2}$
- Soil permeability: $k=1e-7m/s$
- Biot coefficient: $b=1.0$

For Mohr_Coulomb law, the associated plastic flow law is assumed.

The parameters of the Drucker-Prager Act were obtained by the following formulae:

$A=\dfrac{2\sin(\varphi )}{3-\sin(\varphi )},{\sigma}_{y}=\dfrac{6c\cos(\varphi )}{3-\sin(\varphi )}$

- The dilatance angle is equal to the friction angle in the Drucker-Prager law.
In order to simplify the calculation, the cumulative plastic deformation
ultimate ${p}_{\mathit{ultm}}$ is considered to be null (= 0).
- The porosity is uniform in the model and equals 0.62.

### 1.3 boundary conditions

The hydraulic boundary conditions applied to the model are as follows:

- The bottom of the model is waterproof.
- Hydraulic pressure from the upstream side of 4m (peak elevation = 6.5m).
- Hydraulic pressure defined from the level of the downstream table, which is 2m.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

The slope stability factor is calculated according to the Druck-Prager criterion.
taking into account water infiltration into the soil.
Before analysing the stability, the calculation of the pore pressure field (PRE1) is therefore performed.
using D_PLAN_HS model. The boundary conditions hydraulics are used in PRE1
This will result in a reasonable line of water in spite of the
positive pore pressure above the water line. Indeed,
it generates underpressure below the slices of earth that reduces the force
This makes the slope less stable than the state without infiltration.
The pore pressure field is introduced via keyword `CHAM_PRES` of the CALC_STAB_PENTE macro-command.

In this model, the fracture surface is assumed to be circular and the
analyses stability by the Simplified Bishop method (`METHODE_LEM ='BISHOP'`).

The preliminary study showed that the area of search for the fracture surface was reduced to
setting `X1_MINI = X1_MAXI = 3,0` and `X2_MINI = X2_MAXI = 13,2`.

In order to speed up the calculation,
manually control the ray search strip by putting `Y_MINI = 1,2 et Y_MAXI = 1,6`.

The boundary order of the tangential line being less than the order of the slope foot,
ensure that the supposed fracture surfaces are kinematically permissible.

## 3 Modelling B

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Model B performs the same hydraulic calculation as model A,
but analyses the slope stability according to Mohr-Coulomb by
Morgenstern-Price method assuming that the fracture surface is **noncircular**.

To quickly test the improved fireworks algorithm (EFWA),
the result of the preliminary calculation is used to configure the initial state already optimal.

Thus, by setting the authorized number of iteration (`ITER_MAXI = 1`) and the number of the
fireworks (`N = 2`), ordinary sparks (`M = 2`) and Gaussian sparks (`MG = 1`):
it becomes almost impossible for the result of optimisation to become different
the initial state.

The mesh being coarse, we configure `MARGE_PENTE = 0,8` which
is relatively large in order to avoid failure to create groups of mesh cells of
slices when the generated fracture surface is close to the slope profile.
