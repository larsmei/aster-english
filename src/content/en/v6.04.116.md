# v6.04.116 SSNV116 – POLYCRISTAL steel UMAT law – benchmark with Abaqus

**Summary:**

Within SINDRI research project [4] a benchmark between Abaqus and code_aster has been developed based on a Crystal Plasticity Finish Element (CPFE) before law using the formalism of UMAT [1,2].

The geometry is a cube 1x1x1 originated from DREAM3D software which produces synthetic EBSD grain orientations. The sample is submitted to a fixed displacement to one end and clamped on another end.

With this test it is shown that code_aster results compares well to Abaqus.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The test cases simulate a polycrystal law on a representative elementary volume (VER) which is a cube sample of 10x10x10. The cube (Figure 1) sample has been generated thanks to **Dream3D** software. ### 1.4 Description of the tests

In this testcase the displacement is a ramp that reach Y μm in a given time. The Von Mises stress field will be compiled.

pre-process

The simulation runs for 1 second. The command file descriptions the different steps followed by code-Aster. The UMAT law is executed with the native code_aster function **make_shared** . UMAT file requirements 8 files:

- param_array.inc: fortran parameters instructions (could be defined directly in the fortran hand routine);
- orien.inc: orientation of grains;
- xvalues.bin, yvalues.bin, zvalues.bin, el_centroid.bin: location of each element and calculation of its length scale;
- boundfeat.bin: read bordery feature.

UMAT routine

The UMAT routine accounts for various physical phenomena that are tested in elementary simulations (Models A to D). The time-step has a significant influence on the mechanical response and the convergence of the model due to the relationship between the loading velvetity and the time step. In case of large time steps the simulation can end up with singular matrix errorers generated by the UMAT routine itself (LUDCMP subroutine) as shown in Figure 2.

## 7 Concluded marks

The results obtained during this benchmark demonstration:

- The capacity in code_aster to read the initial UMAT routine developed by University of Bristol for ABAQUS with a good confidence on the results calculated;
- The UMAT routine read in code_aster apps to be robust with good computing performance;
- Further tests have been performed in code_aster, studying effect of the number of grains and cyclic behavior. The results obtained meet expectations, both in terms of physical lenghtscale behavior;
- The generation of the models have been made using a recently developed automated process generating Crystal Plasticity Finish Element (CPFE) models from experimental Electron Backscatter Diffraction (EBSD), also developed during the SINDRI project [2]. This demonstrates the effectiveness of this standardized procedure.
