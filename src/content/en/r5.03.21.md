# r5.03.21 Elasto(visco)plastic modelling with isotropic hardening in large strains

**Summary**

One describes here a thermoelastoplastic constitutive relation with isotropic hardening written in large strains and proposed by Simo and Miehe. This model is available in the command STAT_NON_LINE through the keyword RELATION:’VMIS_ISOT_TRAC’, ‘VMIS_ISOT_PUIS’ or ’VMIS_ISOT_LINE’ under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT and with the keyword DEFORMATION:’SIMO_MIEHE’. A viscous version of this model is also proposed: ’VISC_ISOT_TRAC’ and ’VISC_ISOT_LINE’.

This model is implemented for the three-dimensional (3D), axisymmetric (AXIS) and plane-strain (D_PLAN) modellings.

One presents the writing and the numerical treatment of this law, as well as the associated variational formulation. It is an Eulerian variational formulation, with updating of the geometry, which takes into account the constitutive stiffness and the geometric stiffness.

## 1 Introduction

We present here a thermoelastoplastic constitutive law written in large strains proposed by SIMO J.C. and MIEHE C. [bib1] which tends, in small strains, towards the elastoplastic constitutive model with isotropic hardening and von Mises criterion, described in [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02). The kinematic choices make it possible, as with the simple updating available via the keyword PETIT_REAC, to treat large displacements and large strains but also large rotations in an exact manner.

The specific features of this model are the following:

- just as in small strains, one assumes the existence of a relaxed configuration, that is locally stress-free, which makes it possible to decompose the total strain into a thermoelastic part and a plastic part,
- the decomposition of this strain into thermoelastic and plastic parts is no longer additive as in small strains (or for the large-strain models written in strain rates with for example a Jaumann derivative) but multiplicative,
- the elastic strains are measured in the current (deformed) configuration whereas the plastic strains are measured in the initial configuration,
- as in small strains, the stresses depend only on the thermoelastic strains,
- the plastic strains take place at constant volume. The variation of volume is then due only to the thermoelastic strains,
- this model leads, during its numerical integration, to an incrementally objective model (cf. [§3.3]) which makes it possible to obtain the exact solution in the presence of large rotations.

A viscous version of this model is also available (hyperbolic-sine law as in the case of the Rousselier model ROUSS_VISC, cf. [R5.03.07](doc:r5.03.07)).

In what follows, one briefly recalls a few notions of mechanics in large strains, then one presents the constitutive relations of the model and its numerical integration in order to treat the equilibrium equations.

One proposes an Eulerian variational formulation, with updating of the geometry. In this respect, one expresses the work of the internal forces and its variation (with a view to a solution by the Newton method) for the continuous problem, which supply respectively, after discretisation by finite elements, the internal-force vector and the tangent matrix.

## 3 Reminders on large strains

### 3.1 Kinematics

Consider a solid subjected to large strains. Let ${\Omega}_{0}$ be the domain occupied by the solid before deformation and $\Omega (t)$ the domain occupied at the instant $t$ by the deformed solid.

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.21/10000790000020C3000007DB47E7BB97C64D7DAF.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Representation of the initial and deformed configurations**

In the initial configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ , the position of any particle of the solid is designated by $X$ (Lagrangian description). After deformation, the position at the instant $t$ of the particle which occupied the position $X$ before deformation is given by the variable $x$ (Eulerian description).

The global motion of the solid is defined, with $u$ the displacement, by:

$x=\hat{x}(X,t)=X+u$

In order to define the change of metric in the neighbourhood of a point, one introduces the transformation-gradient tensor $F$ :

$F=\frac{\partial \hat{x}}{\partial X}=\text{Id}+{\nabla}_{X}u$

The transformations of the volume element and of the density are:

$d\Omega =\text{Jd}{\Omega}_{o}$ with $J=\detF=\frac{{\rho}_{o}}{\rho}$

where ${\rho}_{o}$ and $\rho$ are respectively the density in the initial and current configurations.

Various strain tensors can be obtained by eliminating the rotation in the local transformation. For example, by computing directly the variations of length and of angle (variation of the scalar product), one obtains:

$E=\frac{1}{2}(C-\text{Id})$ with $C={F}^{T}F$

$A=\frac{1}{2}(\text{Id}-{b}^{-1})$ with $b={\text{FF}}^{T}$

$E$ and $A$ are respectively the Green-Lagrange and Euler-Almansi strain tensors and $C$ and $b$ the right and left Cauchy-Green tensors respectively.

In the Lagrangian description, one will describe the strain by the tensors $C$ or $E$ because these are quantities defined on ${\Omega}_{0}$ , and in the Eulerian description by the tensors $b$ or $A$ (defined on $\Omega$ ).

**Note**:

*Let a solid undergo two successive transformations, for example the first transformation takes the solid from the initial configuration* ${O}_{0}$ *to a configuration* ${O}_{1}$ *(gradient tensor* ${F}_{1/0}$ *and displacement vector* ${u}_{1/0}$ *), then the second transformation from the configuration* ${O}_{1}$ *to* ${O}_{2}$ *(gradient tensor* ${F}_{2/1}$ *and displacement vector* ${u}_{2/1}$ *).*

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.21/10000CFC000069D500001D25499C62D7EAC33D0D.svg)

*The passage from the configuration* ${O}_{0}$ *to* ${O}_{2}$ *is given by the gradient tensor* ${F}_{2/0}$ *(displacement* ${u}_{2/0}={u}_{2/1}+{u}_{1/0}$ *) such that:*

${F}_{2/0}={F}_{2/1}{F}_{1/0}$

*One then obtains, for example, for the Green-Lagrange strain tensor* $E$

${E}_{2/0}={F}_{1/0}^{T}{E}_{2/1}{F}_{1/0}+{E}_{1/0}$

*where* ${E}_{2/0}$ *,* ${E}_{1/0}$ *and* ${E}_{2/1}$ *are the Green-Lagrange strains of the configurations* ${\Omega}_{2}$ *with respect to* ${O}_{0}$ *associated with* ${F}_{2/0}$ *,* ${O}_{1}$ *with respect to* ${O}_{0}$ *associated with* ${F}_{1/0}$ *and* ${O}_{2}$ *with respect to* ${\Omega}_{1}$ *associated with* ${F}_{2/1}$ *, respectively.*

*This constitutes one of the difficulties encountered when writing a constitutive law in large strains because one can no longer write a formula analogous to that written in small strains, namely* ${\varepsilon}_{2/0}={\varepsilon}_{2/1}+{\varepsilon}_{1/0}$ *where* $\varepsilon$ *is the linearised total strain tensor.*

*In order to recover* ${\varepsilon}_{2/0}={\varepsilon}_{2/1}+{\varepsilon}_{1/0}$ *in small strains from the expression of* ${E}_{2/0}$ *, one must neglect all the second-order terms of* $\nabla {u}_{2/0}$ *,* $\nabla {u}_{1/0}$ *and* $\nabla {u}_{2/1}$ *. In this case, one has* ${E}_{2/0}\simeq {\varepsilon}_{2/0}$ *,* ${\mathrm{E}}_{1/0}\mathrm{\simeq }{\varepsilon}_{1/0}$ *and* ${F}_{1/0}^{T}{E}_{2/1}{F}_{1/0}\simeq {\varepsilon}_{2/1}$ *.*

### 3.2 Stresses

For the model described here, the stress tensor used is the Eulerian Kirchhoff tensor $\tau$ defined by:

$J\sigma =\tau$

where $\sigma$ is the Eulerian Cauchy tensor. The tensor $\tau$ therefore results from a "scaling" by the variation of volume of the Cauchy tensor $\sigma$ ; this is not the case of other stress tensors used (first and second Piola-Kirchhoff tensors).

In the Eulerian description, the equilibrium equations are given by:

$\begin{array}{}{div}_{x}\sigma +\rho f=0\text{sur}\Omega \\ \sigma .n={t}^{d}\text{sur}\partial {\Omega}^{f}\end{array}$

where $f$ is the body force applied on the domain $\Omega$ , $n$ the outward normal to the boundary $\partial {\Omega}^{f}$ and $\partial {\Omega}^{f}$ the part of the boundary of the domain $\Omega$ where the surface forces ${t}^{d}$ are applied.

### 3.3 Objectivity

When one writes a constitutive law in large strains, one must check that this law is objective, that is invariant under any change of spatial frame of the form:

${x}^{\text{*}}=c(t)+Q(t)x$

where $Q$ is an orthogonal tensor which expresses the rotation of the frame and $c$ a vector which expresses the translation.

More concretely, if one carries out a tensile test in the direction ${e}_{1}$ , for example, followed by a rotation of 90° about ${e}_{3}$ , which amounts to carrying out a tensile test along ${e}_{2}$ , then the danger with a non-objective constitutive law is not to recover a uniaxial stress tensor in the direction ${e}_{2}$ (which is notably the case with the PETIT_REAC kinematics).

## 4 Presentation of the constitutive model

### 4.1 Kinematic aspect

This model assumes, just as in small strains, the existence of a relaxed configuration ${\Omega}^{r}$ , that is locally stress-free, which then makes it possible to decompose the total strain into elastic and plastic parts, this decomposition being multiplicative.

In what follows, one will denote by $F$ the gradient tensor which takes one from the initial configuration ${O}_{0}$ to the current configuration $\Omega (t)$ , by ${F}^{p}$ the gradient tensor which takes one from the configuration ${O}_{0}$ to the relaxed configuration ${\Omega}^{r}$ , and ${F}^{e}$ from the configuration ${O}^{r}$ to $\Omega (t)$ . The index $p$ refers to the plastic part, the index $e$ to the elastic part.

![Figure](/figures/r5.03.21/10000FCA00001CD6000013A398ECE64086423371.svg)

**Figure 4.1-a: Decomposition of the gradient tensor** $F$ **into an elastic part** ${F}^{e}$ **and a plastic part** ${F}^{p}$

By composition of the motions, one obtains the following multiplicative decomposition:

$F={F}^{e}{F}^{p}$

The elastic strains are measured in the current configuration with the left Eulerian Cauchy-Green tensor ${b}^{e}$ and the plastic strains in the initial configuration by the tensor ${G}^{p}$ (Lagrangian description). These two tensors are defined by:

${b}^{e}={F}^{e}{F}^{\text{eT}}$ , ${G}^{p}=({F}^{\text{pT}}{F}^{p}{)}^{-1}$ whence ${b}^{e}={\text{FG}}^{p}{F}^{T}$

The model presented is written in such a way as to distinguish the isochoric terms from the terms of change of volume. One introduces for this purpose the following two tensors:

$\bar{F}={J}^{-1/3}F$ and ${\bar{b}}^{e}={J}^{-2/3}{b}^{e}$ with $J=\detF$

By definition, one has: $\det\bar{F}=1$ and $\det\bar{{b}^{e}}=1$ .

### 4.2 Constitutive relations

The law presented is a thermoelasto(visco)plastic model with isotropic hardening which tends, under the small-strain assumption, towards the model [R5.03.02](doc:r5.03.02) with von Mises criterion (it is the plastic model). The plastic strains take place at constant volume so that:

${J}^{p}=\det{F}^{p}=1$ whence $J={J}^{e}=\det{F}^{e}$

The constitutive relations are given by:

- the definition of the hyperelastic free energy:

$\psi ={\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,J)+{\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})+K(\alpha )$ ,

with in particular ${\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})=\frac{1}{2}\frac{E}{3(1-2\nu )}\left[\frac{1}{2}({J}^{2}-1)-\lnJ\right]+\frac{\mu}{2}(\mathrm{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}-3)$

and ${\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,J)=-3\alpha \Delta T(J-\frac{1}{J})$

- thermoelastic stress–strain relation:

$\tilde{\tau}=\mu {\bar{\tilde{b}}}^{e}$

$\text{tr}\tau =\frac{\mathrm{3K}}{2}({J}^{2}-1)-\frac{\mathrm{9K}}{2}\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{ref}})(J+\frac{1}{J})$

- plasticity threshold (one admits that it is expressed with the Kirchhoff stresses):

$f={\tau}_{\text{eq}}-R(p)-{\sigma}_{y}$

where $R$ is the isotropic hardening variable, a function of the cumulative plastic strain *p* .

- flow laws:

$\bar{F}{\dot{G}}^{p}{\bar{F}}^{T}=-\dot{\lambda}\frac{3}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\tilde{\tau}{\bar{b}}^{e}=-3\dot{\lambda}(\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\bar{{b}^{e}}+\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{\mu})\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$

$\dot{p}=\dot{\lambda}$

For the plasticity model, the plastic multiplier is obtained by writing the consistency condition $\dot{f}=0$ and one has:

$\dot{p}\ge 0,f\le 0\text{et}\dot{p}f=0$

In the viscous case, one takes $\dot{p}$  equal to:

$\dot{p}={\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}{\left[\text{sh}(\frac{\langle f\rangle }{{\sigma}_{0}})\right]}^{m}$

where ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}$ , ${\sigma}_{0}$ and $m$ are the viscosity coefficients. Let us point out that this law reduces to a law of Norton type when the 2 material parameters ${\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}$ and ${\sigma}_{0}$ are very large.

One recalls that:

${\bar{b}}^{e}={J}^{-2/3}{b}^{e}$

$\bar{F}={J}^{-1/3}F$

and that the partition of the strains is written:

${\bar{b}}^{e}=\bar{F}{G}^{p}{\bar{F}}^{T}$

For metallic materials where the ratio ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}/\mu$ is small compared with 1, the expression of the flow law can be approximated by:

$\bar{F}{\dot{G}}^{p}{\bar{F}}^{T}=-\dot{\lambda}\text{tr}\bar{{b}^{e}}\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}+O(\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}{\mu})$                                 eq.4.2-1

where $O(\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}{\mu})$ is negligible compared with the first term.

It is this last expression which is implemented in *Code_Aster*.

**Note**:

*If the strains are small, one has:*

$\begin{array}{}J\simeq 1+\text{tr}\varepsilon \\ {b}^{e}\simeq \text{Id}+2{\varepsilon}^{e}\\ {G}^{p}\simeq \text{Id}-2{\varepsilon}^{p}\end{array}$

*where* $\varepsilon$ *is the total strain,* ${\varepsilon}^{e}$ *the elastic strain and* ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ *the plastic strain in small strains.*

By replacing these three expressions in the equations of the constitutive law presented here, one indeed recovers the classical thermo-elasto-plastic model with isotropic hardening and von Mises criterion.

### 4.3 Correction of the elastic energy in the presence of thermal effects

The expression of the hyperelastic energy ${\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}$ poses a few difficulties. Indeed, it depends on $J$ ; in the presence of thermal strain, $J$ includes a thermal component which perturbs the expression and which it is appropriate to correct.

The approach of the correction of the Simo-Miehe energy in the presence of thermal effects is the following:

1. cancellation of the energy in pure thermal conditions when the stress is zero;
2. solution of the equation in $J$ obtained, of which one calls ${J}_{0}$ the solution.
3. final computation of the elastic energy of Simo-Miehe in the presence of thermal effects:

${\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}^{\mathrm{corrigee}}={\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})+{\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,J)+{\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})-{\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}({J}_{0,}{\bar{b}}^{e}={I}_{d})-{\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,{J}_{0})$

Cancellation of the stress in pure thermal conditions leads to the equation:

$\text{tr}\tau =0\iff ({J}^{2}-1)-3\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{ref}})(J+\frac{1}{J})=0\Leftarrow \lbrace \begin{array}{}{J}^{3}-J-3\alpha \Delta T({J}^{2}-1)\\ J\ne 0\end{array}$

This equation, under the assumption $3\alpha \Delta T\ll 1$ , has solutions close to -1, 0 and 1. Only the largest is physically acceptable. One therefore sets:

${J}_{0}=\mathrm{MAX}\lbrace J\mathrm{tq}{J}^{3}-J-3\alpha \Delta T({J}^{2}-1)=0\rbrace$

The corrected energy in the presence of thermal effects is finally written:

${\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}^{\mathrm{corrigee}}={\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})+{\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,J)+{\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}(J,{\bar{b}}^{e})-{\psi}_{\mathrm{elas}}({J}_{0,}{\bar{b}}^{e}={I}_{d})-{\psi}_{\mathrm{ther}}(T,{J}_{0})$

**Note**:

*The user must make sure that the “experimental” tensile curve used, either directly or in order to deduce from it the hardening slope, is indeed given in the plane rational stress* $\sigma =F/S$ *- logarithmic strain* $\ln(1+\Delta l/{l}_{0})$ where ${l}_{0}$ *is the initial length of the gauge length of the specimen,* $\Delta l$ *the change in length after deformation,* $F$ *the applied force and* $S$ *the current area* . *One will note that* $\sigma =F/S=\frac{F}{{S}_{0}}\frac{{l}_{0}}{l}\frac{1}{J}$ *whence* $\tau =J\sigma =\frac{F}{{S}_{0}}\frac{{l}_{0}}{l}$ . *In general, it is indeed the quantity* $\frac{F}{{S}_{0}}\frac{{l}_{0}}{l}$ *which is measured by experimentalists and this gives directly the Kirchhoff stress used in the model of Simo and Miehe.*

### 4.5 Stresses and internal variables

The stresses are the Cauchy stresses $$ , computed therefore on the current configuration (six components in 3D, four in 2D).

The internal variables produced in *Code_Aster*  are:

- $\mathit{V1}$ , the cumulated plastic strain $p$ ,
- $\mathit{V2}$ the plasticity indicator (0 if the last computed increment is elastic, 1 otherwise).
- $\mathit{V3}$ to $\mathit{V8}$ : the opposite of the elastic strain ${\bar{b}}^{e}$

**Note**:

*If the user wishes possibly to recover strains in the post-processing of the analysis, the Green-Lagrange strains* $E$ *, which represent a measure of the strains in large strains (options* EPSG_ELGA *or* EPSG_ELNO *), must be plotted. The classical linearised strains* $\varepsilon$ *measure strains under the small-strain assumption and have no meaning in large strains.*

### 4.6 Range of use

The choice of a kinematics DEFORMATION: ‘PETIT_REAC’ also makes it possible to treat a thermo-elastoplastic constitutive law with isotropic hardening and von Mises criterion in large strains. The law is written in small strains and large strains are taken into account by updating the geometry.

Between the law presented here (SIMO_MIEHE ) and PETIT_REAC,

- there is no difference if the strains are small
- there is no difference if the strains are large but the rotations small
- there are differences if the rotations are large.

In particular, the solution obtained with the kinematics PETIT_REAC may depart noticeably from the exact solution in the presence of large rotations, and this whatever the size of the time steps chosen by the user, unlike the kinematics SIMO_MIEHE.

### 4.7 Integration of the constitutive law

In the case of an incremental behaviour, factor keyword COMPORTEMENT, knowing the stress ${\sigma}^{-}$ , the cumulated plastic strain ${p}^{-}$ , the trace divided by three of the elastic strain tensor $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e-}$ , the displacements ${u}^{-}$ and $\Delta u$ and the temperatures ${T}^{-}$ and $T$ , one seeks to determine $(\sigma ,p,\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e})$ .

The displacements being known, the gradients of the transformation from ${\Omega}_{0}$ to ${\Omega}^{-}$ , denoted ${F}^{-}$ , and from ${\Omega}^{-}$ to $\Omega$ , denoted $\Delta F$ , are known.

The *implicit*  discretisation of the law gives:

$F=\Delta F{F}^{-}$

$J=\detF$

$\bar{F}={J}^{-1/3}F$

${\bar{b}}^{e}=\bar{F}{G}^{p}{\bar{F}}^{T}$

$J\sigma =\tau$

$\tilde{\tau}=\mu {\bar{\tilde{b}}}^{e}$

$\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}\tau =\frac{1}{2}K({J}^{2}-1)-\frac{3}{2}K\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{ref}})(J+\frac{1}{J})$

$f={\tau}_{\text{eq}}-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)-{\sigma}_{y}$

$\bar{F}({G}^{p}-{G}^{p-}){\bar{F}}^{T}=-\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\Delta p$ whence ${\bar{b}}^{e}=\bar{F}{G}^{p-}{\bar{F}}^{T}-\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\Delta p$

In the plastic case: $\Delta p\ge 0,f\le 0\text{et}f\Delta p=0$

In the viscous case: $\langle {\tau}_{\text{eq}}-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)-{\sigma}_{y}\rangle -{\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\Delta p}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]=0$

**Note**:

*This formulation is incrementally objective because the only incremental tensor quantity which enters the discretisation is* ${\dot{G}}^{p}$ . *As* ${G}^{p}$ *and* ${G}^{p-}$ *are measured on the same configuration, that is the initial configuration, the discretisation of* ${\dot{G}}^{p}$ *, namely* $\Delta {G}^{p}={G}^{p}-{G}^{p-}$ *, is incrementally objective.*

One introduces ${\tau}^{\text{Tr}}$ , the Kirchhoff tensor which results from an elastic prediction (Tr: trial, in English trial):

${\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}=\mu {\tilde{\bar{b}}}^{\text{eTr}}$

where

${\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}=\bar{F}{G}^{p-}{\bar{F}}^{T}=\Delta \bar{F}{\bar{b}}^{e-}\Delta {\bar{F}}^{T}$ , $\Delta \bar{F}=(\Delta J{)}^{-1/3}\Delta F$ and $\Delta J=\det(\Delta F)$

One obtains ${\bar{b}}^{e-}$ from the stresses ${\tau}^{-}$ by the thermoelastic stress-strain relation and from the trace of the elastic strain tensor.

${\bar{b}}^{e-}=\frac{{\tilde{\tau}}^{-}}{{\mu}^{-}}+\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e-}$

**Note**:

*The advantage of this formulation is that it is not necessary to compute the plastic strain* ${G}^{p-}$ *which would oblige us to invert the transformation gradient* $\bar{F}$ *. One needs only to know* $\bar{F}{G}^{p-}{\bar{F}}^{T}$ *.*

If ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}<R({p}^{-})+{\sigma}_{y}$ , one remains elastic. In this case, one has:

$p={p}^{-}$ , $\tau ={\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}+\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\tau}^{\text{Tr}}\text{Id}$ and $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}$

otherwise, one obtains:

$\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}=\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}$ , thanks to the simplification of the flow rule: ${\bar{b}}^{e}={\bar{b}}^{{e}^{\text{Tr}}}-\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}\Delta p$

By taking the deviatoric parts of this equation, and by multiplying them by $\mu$ one arrives at: ${\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}=\tilde{\tau}(1+\frac{\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}\Delta p}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}})$

By computing the equivalent stress, one reduces the problem to a non-linear scalar equation in $\Delta p$ :

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}-{\tau}_{\text{eq}}-\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}\Delta p=0$

**In the plastic case**: ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}+R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)$ , which leads to $\Delta p$  the solution of the equation:

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}-{\sigma}_{y}-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)-\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}\Delta p=0$

**In the viscoplastic case:** ${\tau}_{\text{eq}}={\sigma}_{y}+R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)+{\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\Delta p}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]$ , which leads to $\Delta p$ the solution of the equation:

${\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}-{\sigma}_{y}-R({p}^{-}+\Delta p)-{\sigma}_{0}{\text{sh}}^{-1}\left[{(\frac{\Delta p}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t})}^{\frac{1}{m}}\right]-\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}\Delta p=0$

In the case where the hardening is linear, or else given by a tensile curve defined point by point, hence piecewise affine, the equation to be solved is linear. One obtains the solution directly. $\Delta p$ In the other cases, the solution is carried out in *Code_Aster* by a secant method with a search interval (cf. [R5.03.05](doc:r5.03.05)). The integration may be controlled by the parameters RESI_INTE_RELA and ITER_INTE_MAXI.

Once $\Delta p$ is known, one can then deduce the Kirchhoff tensor, namely:

$\tau =\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}}{\tilde{\tau}}^{\text{Tr}}+\left[\frac{K}{2}({J}^{2}-1)-\frac{\mathrm{3K}}{2}(T-{T}_{\text{ref}})(J+\frac{1}{J})\right]\text{Id}$

Once the cumulated plastic strain, the stress tensor and the tangent matrix have been computed, one carries out a correction on the trace of the elastic strain tensor ${\bar{b}}^{e}$ in order to take into account plastic incompressibility, which is not preserved with the simplification made to the flow rule [eq 4.2.1]. This correction is carried out by using a relation between the invariants of ${\bar{b}}^{e}$ and ${\tilde{\bar{b}}}^{e}$ and by exploiting the plastic incompressibility condition ${J}^{p}=1$ (or equivalently $\det{\bar{b}}^{e}=1$ ). This relation is written:

${x}^{3}-{\bar{J}}_{2}^{e}x-(1-{\bar{J}}_{3}^{e})=0$

with ${\bar{J}}_{2}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}{\bar{b}}_{\text{eq}}^{{e}^{2}}=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{2}}{2{\mu}^{2}}$ , ${\bar{J}}_{3}^{e}=\det{\tilde{\bar{b}}}^{e}=\det\frac{\tilde{\tau}}{\mu}$ and $x=\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}$

The solution of this cubic equation makes it possible to obtain $\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}$ and consequently the thermoelastic strain ${\bar{b}}^{e-}$ at the next time step. In the case where this equation admits several solutions, one takes the solution closest to the solution of the previous time step. This is moreover why one stores in an internal variable $\frac{1}{3}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{e}$ .

## 5 Variational formulation

Insofar as the stresses supplied by the constitutive law are Eulerian, one chooses a variational formulation written on the current (Eulerian) configuration and not on the initial configuration, namely:

$\underset{{F}_{int}.\delta v}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\underset{\Omega}{\int}\sigma {\nabla}_{x}\delta vd\Omega }}=\underset{{F}_{\text{ext}}.\delta v}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\underset{\Omega}{\int}\rho f\delta vd\Omega +\underset{\partial {\Omega}^{f}}{\int}{t}^{d}\delta v\text{dS}}}$ $\delta v$ Kinematically admissible

We are interested here only in the work of the internal forces and in its variation with a view to a solution by the Newton method. The proof of the expressions presented will be found in [bib 4].

### 5.1 Case of the continuous medium

One rewrites here the work of the internal forces in indicial form, namely:

${F}_{int}.\delta v=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\sigma}_{ij}\frac{\partial \delta {v}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}d\Omega$

We also need to express the variation of the internal forces in the current configuration $\Omega$ namely:

$\begin{array}{}\begin{array}{}\delta {F}_{int}.\delta u.\delta v=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[{\sigma}_{ij}\frac{\partial \delta {u}_{p}}{\partial {x}_{p}}-{\sigma}_{\text{ik}}\frac{\partial \delta {u}_{j}}{\partial {x}_{k}}\right]\frac{\partial \delta {v}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}d\Omega \text{}\text{rigidité géométrique}\text{}\\ +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial \Delta {F}_{\text{pq}}}\frac{\partial \delta {u}_{p}}{\partial {x}_{q}^{-}}\right]\frac{\partial \delta {v}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}d\Omega \text{}\text{rigidité de comportement}\text{}\end{array}\\ \end{array}$

where ${x}^{-}$ are the coordinates of a point on the configuration ${\Omega}^{-}$ .

### 5.2 Discretisation by finite elements

One discretises the displacements $u$ and the virtual displacements $v$ by finite elements. The notations are the following, adopting the summation convention on repeated indices:

${u}_{i}(x)={N}^{n}(x){U}_{i}^{n}\frac{\partial {u}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}}={D}_{j}^{n}(x){U}_{i}^{n}\frac{\partial {u}_{i}}{\partial {x}_{j}^{-}}={D}_{j}^{-n}(x){U}_{i}^{n}$

where:

${N}^{n}(x)$ is the shape function associated with the node $n$

${U}_{i}^{n}$ , the component *i*  of the nodal displacement of the node $n$

${D}_{j}^{n}(x)$ , the components of the gradient of the shape functions on the configuration $\Omega$

${D}_{j}^{-n}(x)$ , the components of the gradient of the shape functions on the configuration ${O}^{-}$

One obtains for the internal-force vector:

$({F}_{int}{)}_{i}^{n}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\sigma}_{ij}{D}_{j}^{n}d\Omega$

and for the tangent matrix, which is not symmetric a priori:

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{i\text{}p}^{nm}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[{D}_{p}^{m}{\sigma}_{ij}{D}_{j}^{n}-{D}_{k}^{m}{\sigma}_{\text{ik}}{D}_{p}^{n}\right]d\Omega \\ +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[{D}_{q}^{-n}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{ij}}{\partial \Delta {F}_{\text{pq}}}{D}_{j}^{n}\right]d\Omega \end{array}$

In the case of a two-dimensional modelling (plane strain), the expressions of the internal-force vector and of the tangent matrix are identical except that the indices corresponding to the components vary from 1 to 2 only.

In the case of an axisymmetric modelling, numbering the axes in the order $(r,z,\theta ),$  the internal-force vector is written:

$({F}_{int}^{\text{axi}}{)}_{\alpha}^{n}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\left[{\sigma}_{\alpha \beta }{D}_{\beta}^{n}+{\sigma}_{33}\frac{{N}^{n}}{r}{\delta}_{\alpha 1}\right]d\Omega$ , $\alpha \in \left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace$ , $\beta \in \left\lbrace 1,2\right\rbrace$

and the tangent matrix:

$\left[{K}^{\text{axi}}\right]=\left[K\right]+\left[{K}^{\text{corr}}\right]$

with:

${\left[{K}_{{}^{(1)}}^{\text{corr}}\right]}_{1\beta }^{\mathrm{nm}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\frac{{N}^{n}}{r}{\sigma}_{\beta \gamma }{D}_{\gamma}^{m}d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}\frac{{N}^{n}}{{r}^{-}}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{\beta \gamma }}{\partial \Delta {F}_{33}}{D}_{\gamma}^{m}d\Omega$

${\left[{K}_{{}^{(2)}}^{\text{corr}}\right]}_{\alpha 1}^{\mathrm{nm}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}{D}_{\alpha}^{n}{\sigma}_{33}\frac{{N}^{m}}{r}d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{{D}_{\gamma}^{-}}^{n}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{33}}{\partial \Delta {F}_{\alpha \gamma }}\frac{{N}^{m}}{r}d\Omega$

${\left[{K}_{{}^{(3)}}^{\text{corr}}\right]}_{11}^{\mathrm{nm}}=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\frac{{N}^{n}}{{r}^{-}}\frac{\partial {\sigma}_{33}}{\partial \Delta {F}_{33}}\frac{{N}^{m}}{r}$

From an algorithmic point of view, the elementary tangent matrix $K$ is not symmetric a priori. The global solution will therefore be carried out by default with a non-symmetric solver. It is however possible to symmetrise the global tangent matrix before solution (keyword SOLVEUR), which makes it possible to save computing time but may degrade convergence.

### 5.3 Expression of the constitutive tangent matrix

One gives here the expression of the tangent matrix (option FULL_MECA during the Newton iterations, option RIGI_MECA_TANG for the first iteration). It is obtained by linearising the system of equations which governs the constitutive law. We give here the final result of this linearisation. The detail of this calculation will be found in [bib4].

One sets:

$J=\detF$ , ${J}^{-}=\det{F}^{-}$ and $\Delta J=\det\Delta F$

- For the option FULL_MECA, one has:

$\begin{array}{}A=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \Delta F}=\frac{(\Delta J{)}^{-1/3}}{J}H-\frac{1}{3J\Delta J}(H\Delta \bar{F})\otimes B-\frac{{J}^{-}}{{J}^{2}}\tau \otimes B\\ +\frac{{J}^{-}}{J}\left[\text{KJ}-\frac{3}{2}K\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{ref}})(1-{J}^{-2})\right]\text{Id}\otimes B\end{array}$

where **B**  is:

$\begin{array}{}{B}_{11}=\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{33}-\Delta {F}_{23}\Delta {F}_{32}\\ {B}_{22}=\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{33}-\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{31}\\ {B}_{33}=\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{22}-\Delta {F}_{12}\Delta {F}_{21}\\ {B}_{12}=\Delta {F}_{31}\Delta {F}_{23}-\Delta {F}_{33}\Delta {F}_{21}\\ {B}_{21}=\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{32}-\Delta {F}_{33}\Delta {F}_{12}\\ {B}_{13}=\Delta {F}_{21}\Delta {F}_{32}-\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{31}\\ {B}_{31}=\Delta {F}_{12}\Delta {F}_{23}-\Delta {F}_{22}\Delta {F}_{13}\\ {B}_{23}=\Delta {F}_{31}\Delta {F}_{12}-\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{32}\\ {B}_{32}=\Delta {F}_{13}\Delta {F}_{21}-\Delta {F}_{11}\Delta {F}_{23}\end{array}$

$H$ and $H\Delta \bar{F}$ are given by:

In the elastic case ($f<0$ ):

${H}_{ijkl}=\mu ({\delta}_{\text{ik}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{jp}}+\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{ip}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{pl}}^{e-}{\delta}_{\text{jk}})-\frac{2\mu }{3}{\delta}_{ij}\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{kp}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}$

and

$H\Delta \bar{F}=2\mu {\tilde{\bar{b}}}^{\text{eTr}}$

In the plastic case ($f=0$ ) or viscoplastic:

$\begin{array}{}{H}_{ijkl}=\frac{\mu}{a}({\delta}_{\text{ik}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{jp}}+\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{ip}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{pl}}^{e-}{\delta}_{\text{jk}})\\ \text{}-2\mu \left[\frac{{\delta}_{ij}}{\mathrm{3a}}+\frac{\bar{R}\Delta p{\tilde{\tau}}_{ij}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}(\bar{R}+\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}})}\right]\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{kp}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\\ \text{}+\frac{3{\mu}^{2}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}(\bar{R}\Delta p-{\tau}_{\text{eq}})}{a{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{3}(\bar{R}+\text{}mu\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}})}{\tilde{\tau}}_{ij}{\tilde{\tau}}_{\text{kq}}\Delta {\bar{F}}_{\text{qp}}{\bar{b}}_{\text{lp}}^{e-}\end{array}$

and

$H\Delta \bar{F}=\frac{2\mu }{a}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}-2\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}\left[\frac{\text{Id}}{\mathrm{3a}}+\frac{\bar{R}\Delta p\tilde{\tau}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}(\bar{R}+\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}})}\right]+\frac{3{\mu}^{2}\text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}}(\bar{R}\Delta p-{\tau}_{\text{eq}})}{a{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{3}(\bar{R}+\mu \text{tr}{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}})}(\tilde{\tau}:{\bar{b}}^{\text{eTr}})\tilde{\tau}$

where $a=\frac{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}^{\text{Tr}}}{{\tau}_{\text{eq}}}$

and $\bar{R}=\text{R'}(p)+\underset{\text{uniquement cas visqueux}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{\sigma}_{0}\times {(1+{(\frac{\Delta p}{{\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t})}^{2})}^{\frac{-1}{2}}\times \frac{1}{m{({\dot{\varepsilon}}_{0}\Delta t)}^{\frac{1}{m}}}\times {(\Delta p)}^{-1}}}$ ,

${R}^{'}(p)$ being the derivative of the isotropic hardening with respect to the cumulated plastic strain $p$ .

- For the option RIGI_MECA_TANG, these are the same expressions as those given for FULL_MECA but with $\Delta p=0$ and with all the variables and material coefficients taken at the time instant ${t}^{-}$ (in principle, in the viscous case, one should take the expressions of FULL_MECA in the elastic case, all the variables being taken at the time instant ${t}^{-}$ ). In particular, one will have $\Delta \bar{F}=\text{Id}$ .
