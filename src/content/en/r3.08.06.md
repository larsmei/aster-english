# r3.08.06 Straight and curved pipe finite elements with ovalisation, swelling and warping in elastoplasticity

**Summary:**

This document presents the modelling of a pipe finite element usable in piping analyses in elasticity or in plasticity. The pipes, curved or straight, may be relatively thick (ratio of thickness to radius of the cross-section up to $0.2$ ) and are subjected to various combined loadings — internal pressure, in-plane and out-of-plane bending, torsion, extension — and may have a non-linear behaviour.

This line element combines both shell and beam properties. The mean fibre of the pipe behaves as a beam and the surface of the pipe as a shell. The element produced is a straight or curved pipe element in small rotations and strains, with an elastoplastic behaviour in plane stress.

Three modellings, corresponding to three different types of element, are available:

- TUYAU_3M, which takes into account 3 Fourier modes at most, and which may be supported on mesh cells with 3 nodes or with 4 nodes.
- TUYAU_6M, which takes into account up to 6 Fourier modes, and is supported on mesh cells with 3 nodes.

## 1 Introduction

There exists an extensive bibliography on the modelling of piping, and numerous finite elements for straight and elbow pipes are available in the major finite-element codes. Reviews have already been carried out [bib1], [bib5], [bib6] in the past, which have been completed by incorporating the latest developments known in the field [bib11]. The important effects to be taken into account are the swelling due to the internal pressure and the ovalisation of the cross-sections by combined in-plane and out-of-plane bending. One places oneself in the assumption of small rotations and strains within the framework of this document.

It is a line element with 3 or 4 nodes, of curved or straight beam type with local plasticity taking ovalisation, warping and swelling into account. The beam kinematics is enriched by a shell kinematics for the description of the behaviour of the cross-sections. This kinematics is discretised in $M$ Fourier modes, the number $M$ of which must at once be sufficient to obtain good results in plasticity and not too large in order to limit the computation time. The literature encourages us to use $M=6$ [bib9], [bib13] in plasticity. In elasticity, for thick pipes, one may be content with $M=2$ or $M=3$ .

## 2 The various shell and beam theories for straight or elbow pipe finite elements

This chapter presents the elements of kinematics in three-dimensional curvilinear geometry, as well as their restrictions within the framework of beam and shell models. Indeed, in order to build the enriched piping finite element which meets the specification defined in the introduction, one exploits a technique of decomposition of the three-dimensional kinematics. The shell kinematics provides the description of ovalisation, swelling and warping, while the beam kinematics describes the general motion of the piping line.

The various shell and beam theories used for each of the elements reflect the assumptions chosen a priori on the type of strains and of behaviours.

### 2.1 The pipe in beam theory

#### 2.1.1 Case of an elbow pipe

A first, relatively simple, approach amounts to considering the elbow represented below as a hollow beam of circular section. The beam is obtained by rotation through an angle $\Theta$ of the circular section about $\mathit{OZ}$ . A point of the beam is located by its distance $r$ from the axis of the beam and by the two angles $\theta$ , $\phi$ where $\theta$ is the longitudinal angle with $\mathit{OY}$ indicated above and $\phi$ the trigonometric angle with $\mathit{OZ}$ measured on the circular section.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/1000000000000381000001659267A73EE06F8913.png)

**Figure 2.1.1-a: Geometry and kinematics of the elbow in beam theory**

In the curvilinear coordinate system ($r$ , $\theta$ , $\phi$ ), the relations between the displacements $u$ of the points of the elbow of position ${r}_{0}=\text{OM}=-\text{R}{e}_{y}(\theta )+r{e}_{r}(\theta ,\phi )$ and the Green–Lagrange strains are given by the following tensor in the natural basis ($r$ , $\theta$ , $\phi$ ):

$2{f}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{\partial ({r}_{0}+u)}{\partial \alpha }.\frac{\partial ({r}_{0}+u)}{\partial \beta }-\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \alpha }.\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \beta }$ , $(\alpha ,\beta )\in \left\lbrace r,\theta ,\phi \right\rbrace$ .

The unit vectors in the directions ($r$ , $\theta$ , $\phi$ ) are:

${e}_{r}=\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial r},{e}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \theta },{e}_{\phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \phi }$ where $A=\sqrt{\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \theta }.\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \theta }}\text{et}B=\sqrt{\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \phi }.\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial \phi }}$ .

If one expresses the position of a point of the elbow in the local orthonormal toroidal basis (${e}_{r},{e}_{\theta},{e}_{\phi }$ ) by (${y}_{r},{y}_{\theta},{y}_{\phi }$ ) one has the following relations:

${e}_{r}=\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial {y}_{r}},{e}_{\theta}=\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial {y}_{\theta}},{e}_{\phi }=\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial {y}_{\phi }}$ .

The expression of the Green-Lagrange strain tensor in this basis is then:

$2{\varepsilon}_{\alpha \beta }=\frac{\partial ({r}_{0}+u)}{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}.\frac{\partial ({r}_{0}+u)}{\partial {y}_{\beta}}-\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial {y}_{\alpha}}.\frac{\partial {r}_{0}}{\partial {y}_{\beta}}$ .

The transformation relations between the expression of the Green-Lagrange strains in the curvilinear coordinate system and in the local toroidal basis defined previously are:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\text{rr}}={f}_{\text{rr}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{f}_{\theta \theta }}{{A}^{2}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{{f}_{\phi \phi }}{{B}^{2}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{{f}_{\theta \phi }}{\text{AB}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta r}=\frac{{f}_{\theta r}}{A}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi r}=\frac{{f}_{\phi r}}{B}\end{array}$

The use of this basis is particularly interesting because the constitutive relations in the orthonormal toroidal basis are simple to use. For the elbow above, if one considers that the strains remain small, one then obtains [bib4] after linearisation of the Green–Lagrange strains:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{rr}}=\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }+\frac{{u}_{\phi }}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }+\frac{{u}_{r}}{{R}_{\theta}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }+\frac{{u}_{r}}{{R}_{\phi }}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }={\gamma}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \phi }+\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial \theta }-\frac{{u}_{\theta}}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta r}={\gamma}_{\theta r}=\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \theta }-\frac{{u}_{\theta}}{{R}_{\theta}}+\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\phi r}={\gamma}_{\phi r}=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \phi }-\frac{{u}_{\phi }}{{R}_{\phi }}+\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial r}\end{array}$

with:

$A=R+r\sin\phi ,B=r,{R}_{\theta}=\frac{R+r\sin\phi }{\sin\phi },{R}_{\phi }=r$

The expressions of the strains established above are then written:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{rr}}=\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }+{u}_{\phi }\cos\phi +{u}_{r}\sin\phi )\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }+{u}_{r})\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }={\gamma}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial \theta }-{u}_{\theta}\cos\phi )+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\theta }={\gamma}_{r\theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \theta }-{u}_{\theta}\sin\phi )+\frac{\partial {u}_{\theta}}{\partial r}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\phi }={\gamma}_{r\phi }=\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \phi }-{u}_{\phi })+\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial r}\end{array}$

The displacement ${u}_{r},{u}_{\theta},{u}_{\phi }$ of a point of the elbow in the toroidal basis associated with the cross-section under observation can easily be expressed as a function of the displacements and of the rotations associated with the centre of the cross-section. Indeed, if one denotes by ${u}_{1},{u}_{2},{u}_{3}$ the displacement in the local curvilinear basis $(o(\theta ),x(\theta ),y(\theta ),z(\theta ))$ associated with the cross-section as indicated in [Figure 2.1.1-a] one has the following relations, valid within the framework of the kinematics of Timoshenko beams [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01):

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{x}(\theta )+{\theta}_{z}(\theta )r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y}(\theta )r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{2}(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{y}(\theta )+{\theta}_{x}(\theta )r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{3}(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{z}(\theta )-{\theta}_{x}(\theta )r\sin\phi \end{array}$

where ${u}_{x},{u}_{y},{u}_{z}$ is the translational displacement of the section and ${\theta}_{x}$ , ${\theta}_{y}$ , ${\theta}_{z}$ the rotation of its centre $o$ . The expression of the components of the displacement in the local orthonormal toroidal basis (${e}_{r},{e}_{\theta},{e}_{\phi }$ ) is obtained by change of frame:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{\theta}(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{1}(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{x}(\theta )+{\theta}_{z}(\theta )r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y}(\theta )r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{\phi }(r,\theta ,\phi )={u}_{3}(r,\theta ,\phi )\sin\phi -{u}_{2}(r,\theta ,\phi )\cos\phi ={u}_{z}(\theta )\sin\phi -{u}_{y}(\theta )\cos\phi -{\theta}_{x}(\theta )r\\ {u}_{r}(r,\theta ,\phi )=-[{u}_{3}(r,\theta ,\phi )\cos\phi +{u}_{2}(r,\theta ,\phi )\sin\phi ]=-[{u}_{z}(\theta )\cos\phi +{u}_{y}(\theta )\sin\phi ]\end{array}$

The introduction of this displacement field into the expression of the linearised strains allows us to obtain the expression of the three-dimensional strains associated with the beam kinematics:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\text{rr}}=0\\ {\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }({u}_{x,\theta }-{u}_{y}-r{\theta}_{x}\cos\phi +{\theta}_{z,\theta }r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y,\theta }r\cos\phi )\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=0\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(-{u}_{x}\cos\phi -{u}_{y,\theta }\cos\phi +{u}_{z,\theta }\sin\phi -r{\theta}_{x,\theta }+{\theta}_{y}r{\cos}^{2}\phi -{\theta}_{z}r\sin\phi \cos\phi )\\ +({\theta}_{z}\cos\phi +{\theta}_{y}\sin\phi )\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(-{u}_{x}\sin\phi -{u}_{y,\theta }\sin\phi -{u}_{z,\theta }\cos\phi +{\theta}_{y}r\sin\phi \cos\phi -{\theta}_{z}r{\sin}^{2}\phi )\\ +({\theta}_{z}\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y}\cos\phi )\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\phi }=0\end{array}$

#### 2.1.2 Case of the straight pipe

The expressions of the strains established above also apply to the case of the straight pipe, where one replaces $\theta$ by $s$ where $s$ is the curvilinear abscissa along the mean fibre of the pipe, with:

$A=1,B=r,1/{R}_{\theta}=0,{R}_{\phi }=r.$

The expressions given for the elbow are then written for the straight pipe:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\text{rr}}=\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial r}\\ {\varepsilon}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {u}_{x}}{\partial x}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }+{u}_{r})\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{x\phi }={\gamma}_{x\phi }=\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {u}_{x}}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\text{rx}}={\gamma}_{\text{rx}}=\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial x}+\frac{\partial {u}_{x}}{\partial r}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\phi }={\gamma}_{r\phi }=\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial {u}_{r}}{\partial \phi }-{u}_{\phi })+\frac{\partial {u}_{\phi }}{\partial r}\end{array}$

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/100050FE000069D50000374BC191A845D347021B.svg)

**Figure 2.1.2-a: Geometry and kinematics of a straight pipe in beam theory**

As previously, the displacement , ${u}_{r},{u}_{x},{u}_{\phi }$  of a point of the pipe in the toroidal basis associated with the cross-section under observation can easily be expressed as a function of the displacements and of the rotations associated with the centre of the cross-section. Indeed, if one denotes by ${u}_{1},{u}_{2},{u}_{3}$ the displacement in the local curvilinear basis $(o,x,y,z)$ associated with the cross-section as indicated in the figure below one has the following relations:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{x}(x)+{\theta}_{z}(x)r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y}(x)r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{2}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{y}(x)+{\theta}_{x}(x)r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{3}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{z}(x)-{\theta}_{x}(x)r\sin\phi \end{array}$

> and:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{x}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{1}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{x}(x)+{\theta}_{z}(x)r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y}(x)r\cos\phi \\ {u}_{\phi }(r,x,\phi )={u}_{3}(r,x,\phi )\sin\phi -{u}_{2}(r,x,\phi )\cos\phi ={u}_{z}(x)\sin\phi -{u}_{y}(x)\cos\phi -{\theta}_{x}(x)r\\ {u}_{r}(r,x,\phi )=-[{u}_{3}(r,x,\phi )\cos\phi +{u}_{2}(r,x,\phi )\sin\phi ]=-[{u}_{z}(x)\cos\phi +{u}_{y}(x)\sin\phi ]\end{array}$

The introduction of this displacement field into the expression of the strains given below allows us to obtain the expression of the strains associated with the beam kinematics:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{rr}}=0\\ {\varepsilon}_{xx}={u}_{x,x}+{\theta}_{z,x}r\sin\phi -{\theta}_{y,x}r\cos\phi \\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=0\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{x\phi }=-r{\theta}_{x,x}+({\theta}_{y}+{u}_{z,x})\sin\phi +({\theta}_{z}-{u}_{y,x})\cos\phi \\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\text{rx}}=({\theta}_{z}-{u}_{y,x})\sin\phi -({\theta}_{y}+{u}_{z,x})\cos\phi \\ 2{\varepsilon}_{r\phi }=0\end{array}$

#### 2.1.3 Notes

The fact that ${\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{rr}},{\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }\text{et}{\varepsilon}_{r\phi }$ are simultaneously zero shows that the beam kinematics cannot represent the strains of the sections transverse to the mean fibre of the pipe. Indeed, the cross-sections undergo a rigid-body motion, which precludes modelling warping, swelling and ovalisation.

### 2.2 The pipe in linearised shell theory

#### 2.2.1 General case

The elbow pipe is considered as a thin shell of revolution (portion of a torus). The mean surface is obtained by rotation through an angle $\Theta$ of a circle of radius $a$ whose centre is at a distance $R$ from the axis of revolution $\mathit{Oz}$ . One denotes by h the thickness of the elbow. One imposes that this thickness remain constant and that the section of the elbow be perfectly circular. A point on the mean surface is characterised by the two angles $\theta ,\phi$ and its position $-h/2\le \zeta \le +h/2$ with respect to the mean surface, where $\theta$ is the longitudinal angle, varying between $0$ and $\Theta$ , and $\phi$ the angle measured on the cross-section.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/10000000000002680000010F20BFA22E493797BF.png)

**Figure 2.2.1-a: Geometry and kinematics of the elbow in shell theory**

One first places oneself within the framework of the linearised theory of shells with transverse shear as it has been described for example in Washizu [bib14]. It limits our study to the framework of small strains. Moreover, the large rotations of the mean surface are not taken into account. The displacements and rotations are thus defined with respect to the initial geometry of the elbow. **If the displacements of the points of the mean surface in the three directions** $\theta$ **axial,** $\phi$ **circumferential and** $\zeta$ **radial are denoted** $u,v$ **and** $w$ **those of any point of the elbow are written in the following way** :

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{\theta}=u(\theta ,\phi )+\zeta {\beta}_{\phi }(\theta ,\phi )\\ {u}_{\phi }=v(\theta ,\phi )-\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}(\theta ,\phi )\\ {u}_{\zeta}=w(\theta ,\phi )\end{array}$

where ${\beta}_{\theta}$ and ${\beta}_{\phi }$ are the rotations with respect to the vectors ${e}_{\theta}$ and ${e}_{\phi }$ respectively.

The strains at every point are thus given by [bib14]:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{E}_{\theta \theta }+\zeta {\kappa}_{\theta \theta }}{1+\zeta /{R}_{\theta}}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{{E}_{\phi \phi }+\zeta {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }}{1+\zeta /{R}_{\phi }}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }={\gamma}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{2{E}_{\theta \phi }+2\zeta {\kappa}_{\theta \phi }}{(1+\zeta /{R}_{\theta})(1+\zeta /{R}_{\phi })}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \zeta }={\gamma}_{\theta \zeta }=\frac{2{E}_{\theta \zeta }}{(1+\zeta /{R}_{\theta})}\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\phi \zeta }={\gamma}_{\phi \zeta }=\frac{2{E}_{\phi \zeta }}{(1+\zeta /{R}_{\phi })}\end{array}$

with:

$A=R+a\sin\phi ,B=a,{R}_{\theta}=\frac{R+a\sin\phi }{\sin\phi },{R}_{\phi }=a.$

where ${E}_{\theta \theta },{E}_{\phi \phi }$ and ${E}_{\theta \phi }$ are the membrane strains of the mean surface, ${\kappa}_{\theta \theta },{\kappa}_{\phi \phi },{\kappa}_{\theta \phi }$ the bending strains of the mean surface and ${E}_{\theta \zeta },{E}_{\phi \zeta }$ the transverse distortions. The strains of the mean surface are related to the displacements of the mean surface by replacing the displacement field of the preceding paragraph by that given above. One then finds:

$\begin{array}{}{E}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta }+\frac{v}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }+\frac{w}{{R}_{\theta}}\\ {E}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial v}{\partial \phi }+\frac{w}{{R}_{\phi }}\\ 2{E}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }+\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }-\frac{u}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }\\ {\kappa}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \theta }-\frac{{\beta}_{\theta}}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }\\ {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }=-\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\kappa}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }-\frac{{\beta}_{\phi }}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi }-\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }+[\frac{1}{{R}_{\theta}}\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }+\frac{1}{{R}_{\phi }}(\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }-\frac{u}{\text{AB}}\frac{\partial A}{\partial \phi })]\\ 2{E}_{\theta \zeta }={\beta}_{\phi }+\frac{1}{A}\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-\frac{u}{{R}_{\theta}}\\ 2{E}_{\phi \zeta }=-{\beta}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{B}\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-\frac{v}{{R}_{\phi }}\end{array}$

That is also:

$\begin{array}{}{E}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta }+v\cos\phi +w\sin\phi )\\ {E}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \phi }+w)\\ {\gamma}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }-u\cos\phi )+\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }\\ {\kappa}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \theta }-{\beta}_{\theta}\cos\phi )\\ {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }=-\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \phi }\\ {\kappa}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }-\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }+{\beta}_{\phi }\cos\phi )+[\frac{\sin\phi }{R+a\sin\phi }\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }+\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }-u\cos\phi )]\\ {\gamma}_{\theta \zeta }={\beta}_{\phi }+\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-u\sin\phi )\\ {\gamma}_{\phi \zeta }=-{\beta}_{\theta}+\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

In this theory there are therefore five unknowns; 3 displacements $u,v$ and $w$ as well as two rotations ${\beta}_{\theta},{\beta}_{\phi }$ . If the Love-Kirchhoff assumption is applied (thin tube) the transverse shears are zero and there remain only the 3 displacements $u,v$ and $w$ since:

$\begin{array}{}{\beta}_{\phi }=-\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-u\sin\phi )\\ {\beta}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

#### 2.2.2 Case of the straight pipe

If one applies these equations to the case of the straight pipe with:

$A=1,B=a,1/{R}_{\theta}=0,{R}_{\phi }=a.$

One recovers the more usual expression for this kind of geometry:

$\begin{array}{}{E}_{xx}=\frac{\partial u}{\partial x}\\ {E}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \phi }+w)\\ 2{E}_{x\phi }=\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}+\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }\\ {\kappa}_{xx}=\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial x}\\ {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }=-\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\kappa}_{x\phi }=\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }-\frac{\partial {\beta}_{x}}{\partial x}+[\frac{1}{a}\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}]\\ 2{E}_{x\zeta }={\beta}_{\phi }+\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ 2{E}_{\phi \zeta }=-{\beta}_{x}+\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

In this theory there are therefore five unknowns; 3 displacements $u,v$ and $w$ as well as two rotations ${\beta}_{x},{\beta}_{\phi }$ . If the Love-Kirchhoff assumption is applied (thin tube) the transverse shears are zero and there remain only the 3 displacements $u,v$ and $w$ since:

$\begin{array}{}{\beta}_{\phi }=-\frac{\partial w}{\partial x}\\ {\beta}_{x}=\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

#### 2.2.3 Note

One can introduce the shell kinematics directly into the 3D strain field. In that case one has:

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\theta \theta }={E}_{\theta \theta }+\zeta {\kappa}_{\theta \theta }\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }={E}_{\phi \phi }+\zeta {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \phi }={\gamma}_{\theta \phi }=2{E}_{\theta \phi }+2\zeta {\kappa}_{\theta \phi }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\theta \zeta }={\gamma}_{\theta \zeta }=2{E}_{\theta \zeta }\\ 2{\varepsilon}_{\phi \zeta }={\gamma}_{\phi \zeta }=2{E}_{\phi \zeta }\end{array}$

where the expressions ${E}_{\theta \theta },{E}_{\phi \phi }$ and ${E}_{\theta \phi }$ for the membrane strains, ${\kappa}_{\theta \theta },{\kappa}_{\phi \phi },{\kappa}_{\theta \phi }$ for the bending strains and ${E}_{\theta \zeta },{E}_{\phi \zeta }$ for the transverse distortions are given by the following expression in the general case:

$\begin{array}{}{E}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta }+v\cos\phi +w\sin\phi )\\ {E}_{\phi \phi }=\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \phi }+w)\\ 2{E}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }+\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }-u\cos\phi )\\ {\kappa}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \theta }-{\beta}_{\theta}\cos\phi )\\ {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }=-\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \phi }\\ 2{\kappa}_{\theta \phi }=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\phi }}{\partial \phi }-\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial {\beta}_{\theta}}{\partial \theta }+{\beta}_{\phi }\cos\phi )\\ 2{E}_{\theta \zeta }={\beta}_{\phi }\frac{R+a\sin\phi }{R+r\sin\phi }+\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-u\sin\phi )\\ 2{E}_{\phi \zeta }=-{\beta}_{\theta}\frac{a}{r}+\frac{1}{r}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

One notes that to order 1 in $\zeta$ the two ways of proceeding give identical results. It is the definition of the membrane or bending strain which changes. In the first case it is independent of the position through the thickness and is computed for the mean radius of the cross-section of the pipe, whereas it depends on it in the case of the 3D approach. The term in brackets in the expression of [§2.2.1] represents a coupling between bending and membrane which appears when one expresses $R+r\sin\phi$ and $r$ as a function of $R+a\sin\phi$ and $a$ . In the remainder of our analysis we shall use this degenerated 3D expression of the shell kinematics.

If moreover we use the Love-Kirchhoff assumption for the transverse shears, ${E}_{\theta \zeta }={E}_{\phi \zeta }=0$ one indeed recovers the following expressions of the rotations:

$\begin{array}{}{\beta}_{\phi }=-\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-u\sin\phi )\\ {\beta}_{\theta}=\frac{1}{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)\end{array}$

and:

$\begin{array}{}{\kappa}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{1}{R+r\sin\phi }[-\frac{1}{R+a\sin\phi }(\frac{{\partial}^{2}w}{\partial {\theta}^{2}}-\frac{\partial u}{\partial \theta }\sin\phi )-\frac{\cos\phi }{a}(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \phi }-v)]\\ {\kappa}_{\phi \phi }=-\frac{1}{\text{ar}}(\frac{{\partial}^{2}w}{\partial {\phi }^{2}}-\frac{\partial v}{\partial \phi })\\ 2{\kappa}_{\theta \phi }=(\frac{\partial w}{\partial \theta }-u\sin\phi )[\frac{\cos\phi }{(R+r\sin\phi )(R+a\sin\phi )}+\frac{a\cos\phi }{r(R+a\sin\phi {)}^{2}}]\\ -\frac{{\partial}^{2}w}{\partial \theta \partial \phi }[\frac{1}{a(R+r\sin\phi )}+\frac{1}{r(R+a\sin\phi )}]\\ +\frac{\partial v}{\partial \theta }\frac{1}{a(R+r\sin\phi )}+(\frac{\partial u}{\partial \phi }\sin\phi +u\cos\phi )\frac{1}{r(R+a\sin\phi )}\end{array}$

the expressions for ${E}_{\theta \theta },{E}_{\phi \phi }$ and ${E}_{\theta \phi }$ remaining unchanged.

One can easily extend this note to the case of the straight pipe.

### 2.3 Analysis of straight and elbow pipes

In conclusion of the two preceding analyses one can model the pipe as a beam element whose section is a thin shell. This interpretation is made in most codes ([bib2], [bib8], [bib9], [bib10], [bib12], etc...). In the absence of warping of the cross-sections (i.e. the cross-sections remain plane) the axial beam displacement gives the new position of the cross-section and the ovalisation displacements (it then suffices to take u=0 in the thin-shell equations) make it possible to know how the latter deforms. The total strain is obtained as the superposition of the beam strains and of the ovalisation strains. The displacement field which one represents in the figure below is written:

$U={U}^{p}+{U}^{s}$ .

In the first displacement field the image of the cross-section is an identical cross-section obtained by translation and rotation of the first. In the second displacement field, the cross-section is deformed.

The finite-element modelling must therefore account for two different mechanical responses: that of the beam and that of the shell for ovalisation, swelling and warping. These last three modellings involve degrees of freedom which are not nodal (Fourier-series decomposition for example).

## 3 Mixed shell-beam elements for straight and curved pipes

### 3.1 Kinematics

One decomposes the displacement field into a macroscopic “beam” part and a supplementary local “shell” part. $V$ is the useful space of the three-dimensional displacement fields defined on any section of pipe.

For the beam part, as in [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03), one introduces the space $T$ of the fields associated with a wrench (defined by two vectors):

$T=\left\lbrace v\in V/\exists (T,\Omega )\text{tel que}v(M)=T+\Omega \wedge \text{GM}\right\rbrace$

For the displacement fields of $T$ , $T$ is the translation of the section (or of the point $G$ ), $\Omega$ the infinitesimal rotation and the fields $v$ are the displacements preserving the section $S$ plane and undeformed (one again uses the assumptions of NAVIER-BERNOULLI).

$T$ is a vector subspace of finite dimension equal to 6. It possesses an orthogonal complement for the scalar product on $V$ :

. ${T}^{\perp }=\left\lbrace v\in V/\underset{S}{\int}v.w=0\forall w\in T\right\rbrace$

Any field $u$ of $V$ is then decomposed in a unique way as the sum of an element of $T$ and of an element of ${T}^{\perp }$ :

$u={u}^{p}+{u}^{s}{u}^{p}\in T,{u}^{s}\in {T}^{\perp }$ .

One then postulates for the surface displacements of the pipe defined in [§2.2] the following Fourier-series decomposition which satisfies the preceding orthogonality principle with the beam displacements up to order 3 in the thickness of the pipe:

$u(x,\phi )=\sum_{m=2}^{M}{u}_{m}^{i}(x)\cosm\phi$

$+\sum_{m=2}^{M}{u}_{m}^{o}(x)\sin m\phi$

$v(x,\phi )={w}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{i}(x)\sin\phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{v}_{\text{nm}}^{i}(x)\sin m\phi$

$\cdots -{w}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{o}(x)\cos\phi +\sum_{m=2}^{M}{v}_{\text{nm}}^{o}(x)\cosm\phi$

$w(x,\phi )={w}_{n}^{o}(x)$ (uniform radial expansion)

$\cdots +\sum_{m=1}^{M}{w}_{\text{nm}}^{i}(x)\cosm\phi$

$\cdots +\sum_{m=1}^{M}{w}_{\text{nm}}^{o}(x)\sin m\phi$

where $x$ is the curvilinear abscissa along the elbow or the straight pipe, indifferently, and $M$ the number of Fourier modes. The rotations ${\beta}_{x}(x,\phi )$ and ${\beta}_{\phi }(x,\phi )$ are deduced from $u(x,\phi ),v(x,\phi )\text{et}w(x,\phi )$ by the Love-Kirchhoff relations of [§2.2.1].

**Note:**

*One may note that in the decomposition of* $v(x,\phi )\text{et}w(x,\phi )$ *the terms in* $\cos\phi \text{et}\sin\phi$ *are not completely independent because of the orthogonality with the beam displacements. This moreover makes it possible to avoid rigid-body motions, for if* ${v}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{i},{v}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{o}\text{et}{w}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{i},{w}_{\mathrm{n1}}^{o}$ *are independent, one can find a non-zero solution giving zero strains. Moreover in the expression of* $u(x,\phi )$ *one notes the absence of the terms in* $\cos\phi$ and $\sin\phi$ *already present in the beam part.*

*If one neglects the variation of the metric with the thickness of the pipe the conditions of rigorous orthogonality between the beam displacements and those of the surface of the pipe are satisfied. In the contrary case, in order to satisfy this condition rigorously one would need a Fourier-series expansion of the rotations* ${\beta}_{x}(x,\phi )$ *and* ${\beta}_{\phi }(x,\phi )$ *starting at order 2. This is incompatible with the Love_Kirchhoff assumptions for these rotations.*

### 3.2 Constitutive law

The behaviour of the new element is a 3D behaviour in plane stress, because the global behaviour of the structure is that of a thin shell. It follows that ${\sigma}_{\zeta \zeta }=0$ and the constitutive law is written in general in the following way:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{\phi \phi }\\ {\sigma}_{x\phi }\\ {\sigma}_{\zeta \phi }\\ {\sigma}_{x\zeta }\end{array})=C(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p}+{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{s}\\ {\epsilon}_{\phi \phi }^{p}+{\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }^{s}\\ {\gamma}_{x\phi }^{p}+{\gamma}_{x\phi }^{s}\\ {\gamma}_{r\phi }^{p}+{\gamma}_{\zeta \phi }^{s}\\ {\gamma}_{\mathrm{xr}}^{p}+{\gamma}_{x\zeta }^{s}\end{array})=C(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{xx}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }\\ {\gamma}_{x\phi }\\ {\gamma}_{\zeta \phi }\\ {\gamma}_{x\zeta }\end{array})$

In our case one will neglect the transverse shears for the shell part of our displacement field. It therefore follows that ${\gamma}_{\zeta x}^{s}={\gamma}_{\zeta \phi }^{s}=0$ . As moreover [§2.1.2] one has shown that ${\gamma}_{r\phi }^{p}=0$ it follows that ${\sigma}_{\zeta \phi }=0$ . For an elastic behaviour one thus has:

$(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{\phi \phi }\\ {\sigma}_{x\phi }\\ {\sigma}_{x\zeta }\end{array})=\frac{E}{1-{\nu}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccc}1& \nu & 0& 0\\ \nu & 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\varepsilon}_{xx}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }\\ {\gamma}_{x\phi }\\ {\gamma}_{x\zeta }\end{array})$ and $C=\frac{E}{1-{\nu}^{2}}(\begin{array}{cccc}1& \nu & 0& 0\\ \nu & 1& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}& 0\\ 0& 0& 0& \frac{1-\nu }{2}\end{array})$ .

### 3.3 Strain work

The general expression of the 3D strain work for the elbow element with the type of behaviour stated above is:

${W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}({\varepsilon}_{xx}{\sigma}_{xx}+{\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }{\sigma}_{\phi \phi }+{\gamma}_{x\phi }{\sigma}_{x\phi }+{\gamma}_{x\zeta }{\sigma}_{x\zeta })\text{dV}$

where $l$ is the curvilinear abscissa which is equal to $l=(R+r\sin\phi )\theta$ for an elbow where $\theta$ is the angle travelled in order to describe the elbow. In the case of an elbow, one thus has $\text{dV}=(R+r\sin\phi )d\theta rd\phi d\zeta$ and for a straight pipe $\text{dV}=\mathrm{dxrd}\phi d\zeta$ where $\zeta$ . is the position through the thickness of the elbow which varies between -h/2 and +h/2. In what follows, in order to lighten the notation, one will use the second expression.

### 3.4 Elastic internal energy of the elbow

In the case of an elastic behaviour, the elastic internal energy of the elbow is expressed in the following way:

${\Phi}_{int}=\frac{1}{2}\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}(\frac{E}{1-{\nu}^{2}}({\varepsilon}_{xx}^{2}+{\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }^{2}+2\nu {\varepsilon}_{xx}{\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi })+G({\gamma}_{x\phi }^{2}+{\gamma}_{x\zeta }^{2}))\text{dV}$

This energy can be decomposed into a beam-energy part, an energy part for the surface of the pipe and coupling terms of the type $\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{p}.{\varepsilon}_{xx}^{s}\text{dV}$ .

### 3.5 Work of the external forces and couples

With the decomposition of the displacements stated at the head of the paragraph, the work of the forces acting on the pipe is expressed in the following way:

$\begin{array}{}{W}_{\text{ext}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.({U}^{P}+{U}^{S})\text{dV}+\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.({U}^{P}+{U}^{S})(a\pm h/2)d\phi \text{dx}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.({U}^{P}+{U}^{S})rd\phi d\zeta =\\ \underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.{U}^{p}\text{dV}+\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.{U}^{p}(a\pm h/2)d\phi \text{dx}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.{U}^{p}rd\phi d\zeta +\\ \underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.{U}^{s}\text{dV}+\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.{U}^{s}(a\pm h/2)d\phi \mathrm{dx}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.{U}^{s}rd\phi d\zeta ={W}_{\text{ext}}^{p}+{W}_{\text{ext}}^{s}\end{array}$

by simple linear decomposition, where ${F}_{v},{F}_{s},{F}_{c}$ are the body, surface and contour forces acting on the pipe, respectively.

One thus determines:

${W}_{\text{ext}}^{p}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.{U}^{p}\text{dV}+\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.{U}^{p}(a\pm h/2)d\phi \text{dx}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.{U}^{p}rd\phi d\zeta$

and:

${W}_{\text{ext}}^{s}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.{U}^{s}\text{dV}+\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.{U}^{s}(a\pm h/2)d\phi \text{dx}+\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.{U}^{s}rd\phi d\zeta$

The work of the external forces can therefore be separated into two distinct contributions of the same forces, on the beam kinematics and on its complement.

### 3.6 Principle of virtual work

It is written in the following way: $\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}=\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{p}+\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{s}=\delta {W}_{int}$ with:

$\delta {W}_{int}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}({\sigma}_{xx}\delta {\varepsilon}_{xx}+{\sigma}_{\phi \phi }\delta {\varepsilon}_{\phi \phi }+{\sigma}_{x\phi }\delta {\gamma}_{x\phi }+{\sigma}_{x\zeta }\delta {\gamma}_{x\zeta })\text{dV}$

#### 3.6.1 External forces and couples part for the beam part

The discretisation of the principle of virtual work for the external forces gives:

$\begin{array}{}\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{p}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}({f}_{x}\delta {u}_{x}+{f}_{y}=\delta {u}_{y}+{f}_{z}\delta {u}_{z}+{m}_{x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{m}_{y}\delta {\theta}_{y}+{m}_{z}\delta {\theta}_{z})\text{dx}+\\ [{\phi }_{x}\delta {u}_{x}+{\phi }_{y}\delta {u}_{y}+{\phi }_{z}\delta {u}_{z}+{\mu}_{x}\delta {\theta}_{x}+{\mu}_{y}\delta {\theta}_{y}+{\mu}_{z}\delta {\theta}_{z}{]}_{0,l}\end{array}$

${f}_{x},{f}_{y},{f}_{z}$ : line forces acting along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ passing through the centre of gravity of the cross-sections:

${f}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{v}.{e}_{i}rd\phi d\zeta +\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{s}.{e}_{i}(a\pm h/2)d\phi$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}$ are the vectors of the local curvilinear basis.

${m}_{x},{m}_{y},{m}_{z}$ : line couples acting about the axes $x$ , $y$ and $z$ :

${m}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}(r\times {F}_{v}).{e}_{i}rd\phi d\zeta +\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}(r\times {F}_{v}).{e}_{i}(a\pm h/2)d\phi$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}$ are the vectors of the local curvilinear basis.

${\phi }_{x},{\phi }_{y},{\phi }_{z}$ : concentrated forces acting along $x$ , $y$ and $z$ passing through the centre of gravity of the cross-sections:

${\phi }_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}{F}_{c}.{e}_{i}rd\phi d\zeta$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}$ are the vectors of the local curvilinear basis.

${\mu}_{x},{\mu}_{y},{\mu}_{z}$ : concentrated moments about the axes $x$ , $y$ and $z$ :

${\mu}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}(r\times {F}_{c}).{e}_{i}rd\phi d\zeta$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{y},{e}_{z}$ are the vectors of the local curvilinear basis.

#### 3.6.2 External forces and couples part for the shell part

One assumes that the external forces applied on the elbow are independent of the thickness of the elbow

$\begin{array}{}\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{s}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}({F}_{x}\delta u+{F}_{\phi }\delta v+{F}_{r}\delta w+{M}_{x}\delta {\beta}_{x}+{M}_{\phi }\delta {\beta}_{\phi })d\theta d\phi \\ +\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}[{\Phi}_{x}\delta u+{\Phi}_{\phi }\delta v+{\Phi}_{r}\delta w+{{\rm M}}_{\theta}\delta {\beta}_{\theta}+{{\rm M}}_{\phi }\delta {\beta}_{\phi }{]}_{0,l}d\phi \end{array}$

where:

${F}_{x},{F}_{\phi },{F}_{r}$ : surface forces acting along $x$ , $\phi$ and $r$ :

${F}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{v}.{e}_{i}rd\zeta +{F}_{s}.{e}_{i}(a\pm h/2)$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{\phi },{e}_{r}$ are the vectors of the local toroidal basis.

${M}_{x},{M}_{\phi }$ : surface couples acting about $x$ and $\phi$ :

${M}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}({\mathrm{\varsigma e}}_{r}x{F}_{v}).{e}_{i}r\mathrm{d\zeta }+(\pm h/2{e}_{r}x{F}_{s}).{e}_{i}(a\pm h/2)$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{\phi },{e}_{r}$ are the vectors of the local toroidal basis.

${\Phi}_{x},{\Phi}_{\phi },{\Phi}_{r}$ : line forces acting along $x$ , $\phi$ and $r$ :

${\Phi}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{F}_{c}.{e}_{i}rd\zeta$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{\phi },{e}_{r}$ are the vectors of the local toroidal basis.

${{\rm M}}_{x},{{\rm M}}_{\phi }$ : line couples acting about $x$ and $\phi$ :

${M}_{i}=\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}(\zeta {e}_{r}\times {F}_{c}).{e}_{i}rd\zeta$ where ${e}_{x},{e}_{\phi },{e}_{r}$ are the vectors of the local toroidal basis.

**Note:**

*When the external forces applied are independent of* $\phi$ *the external work on the shell kinematics is zero except that of the pressure forces corresponding to the forces along* ${e}_{r}$ *. One also notes that the expressions of the line and concentrated moments with respect to* $r$ *are zero. One indeed recovers that there is no moment exerted perpendicularly to the plane of the shell.*

### 3.7 Generalised forces

If $S$ is the area of the cross-section $S$ of the pipe, one sets:

$N=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}$ : normal force at the centre of gravity of the cross-section.

${T}_{y}=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xy}\text{dS}=-\underset{S}{\int}(\sin\phi {\sigma}_{x\zeta }+\cos\phi {\sigma}_{x\phi })\text{dS}$ and

${T}_{z}=\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{xz}\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}(\sin\phi {\sigma}_{x\phi }-\cos\phi {\sigma}_{x\zeta })\text{dS}$ the shear forces along $y$ and $z$ .

${M}_{x}=\underset{S}{\int}(y{\sigma}_{xz}-z{\sigma}_{xy})\text{dS}=-\underset{S}{\int}{\sigma}_{x\phi }\text{dS}$ : torsional moment about $x$ .

${M}_{y}=\underset{S}{\int}z{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}=-\underset{S}{\int}r\cos\phi {\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}$ : bending moment about $y$ .

${M}_{z}=-\underset{S}{\int}y{\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}r\sin\phi {\sigma}_{xx}\text{dS}$ : bending moment about $z$ .

### 4.4 Stiffness matrix

The variational formulation of the strain work is:

$\delta {W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace (\begin{array}{cccc}{\text{δε}}_{xx}& {\text{δε}}_{\text{φφ}}& {\text{δγ}}_{\mathrm{x\phi }}& {\text{δγ}}_{\mathrm{x\zeta }}\end{array})(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{\text{φφ}}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathrm{x\phi }}\\ {\sigma}_{\mathrm{x\zeta }}\end{array})\right\rbrace \text{rd}\phi \text{dxd}\zeta$

that is also:

$\delta {W}_{\text{def}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace (\begin{array}{cccc}{\epsilon}_{xx}& {\epsilon}_{\text{φφ}}& {\gamma}_{\mathrm{x\phi }}& {\gamma}_{\mathrm{x\zeta }}\end{array})C(\begin{array}{c}{\text{δε}}_{xx}\\ {\text{δε}}_{\text{φφ}}\\ {\text{δγ}}_{\mathrm{x\phi }}\\ {\text{δγ}}_{\mathrm{x\zeta }}\end{array})\right\rbrace \text{rd}\phi \text{dxd}\zeta$

$\begin{array}{}{\mathrm{dW}}_{\text{def}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace {(\sum_{k=1}^{N}{B}_{k}{U}_{k})}^{T}C(\sum_{k=1}^{N}{B}_{k}\delta {U}_{k})\right\rbrace rd\phi \mathrm{dx}d\zeta \\ =\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace (\sum_{k=1}^{N}{U}_{{k}^{T}}{B}_{{k}^{T}})C(\sum_{k=1}^{N}{B}_{k}\delta {U}_{k})\right\rbrace rd\phi \mathrm{dx}d\zeta \\ =\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace (\begin{array}{ccc}{U}_{{1}^{T}}& \mathrm{...}& {U}_{{N}^{T}}\end{array}){B}^{T}\mathrm{CB}(\begin{array}{c}\delta {U}_{1}\\ \mathrm{...}\\ \delta {U}_{N}\end{array})\right\rbrace rd\phi \mathrm{dx}d\zeta \\ =(\begin{array}{ccc}{U}_{{1}^{T}}& \mathrm{...}& {U}_{{N}^{T}}\end{array})K(\begin{array}{c}\delta {U}_{1}\\ \mathrm{...}\\ \delta {U}_{N}\end{array})\end{array}$

The principle of virtual work is then written ${\mathrm{\delta U}}^{T}KU=F\mathrm{\delta U}$ where $K$ is the stiffness matrix which is equal to:

$K=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}\left\lbrace {B}^{T}\text{C B}\right\rbrace \text{r d}\phi \text{dxd}\zeta$

**Note:**

*One makes no assumption on the constitutive law. This expression is therefore in particular valid in the case of non-linear behaviours (plasticity).*

### 4.5 Mass matrix

The terms of the mass matrix are obtained after discretisation of the following variational formulation of the non-centrifugal inertia terms:

${\mathrm{dW}}_{\text{mass}}^{\text{ac}}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2p}}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho \ddot{u}.\mathrm{dv}\text{rdxd}\mathrm{fdz}$ with $u=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}(x,\phi ,r)\\ {u}_{2}(x,\phi ,r)\\ {u}_{3}(x,\phi ,r)\\ u(x,\phi ,r)\\ v(x,\phi ,r)\\ w(x,\phi ,r)\end{array})$ .

The notation used is that of [§2.1]: ${u}_{1},{u}_{2}$ and ${u}_{3}$ are the beam displacements at a point of the section and $u,v$ and $w$ are the displacements of the mean fibre of this section at this same point.

The discretisation then gives:

$u=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{H}_{k}{N}_{k}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}^{k}\\ {u}_{y}^{k}\\ {u}_{z}^{k}\\ {\theta}_{x}^{k}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{k}\\ {\theta}_{z}^{k}\\ {u}_{\text{km}}^{i}\\ {v}_{\text{km}}^{i}\\ {w}_{\text{km}}^{i}\\ {u}_{\text{km}}^{o}\\ {v}_{\text{km}}^{o}\\ {w}_{\text{km}}^{o}\\ {w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{i}\\ {w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{o}\\ {w}_{k}^{o}\end{array})$ $\begin{array}{}\\ \\ \\ \\ \\ \\ \end{array}\rbrace m=2,M$

where the matrices ${N}_{k}$ have the expression:

${N}_{k}=\left[\begin{array}{ccccccccccccccc}{x}_{k}\cdot x& {y}_{k}\cdot x& 0& -r\cos\phi ({x}_{k}\cdot y)& -r\cos\phi ({y}_{k}\cdot y)& r\sin\phi & 0& & & \text{...}& & & 0& 0& 0\\ {x}_{k}\cdot y& {y}_{k}\cdot y& 0& r\cos\phi ({x}_{k}\cdot x)& r\cos\phi ({y}_{k}\cdot x)& 0& 0& & & \text{...}& & & 0& 0& 0\\ 0& 0& 1& -r\sin\phi ({x}_{k}\cdot x)& -r\sin\phi ({y}_{k}\cdot y)& 0& 0& & & \text{...}& & & 0& 0& 0\\ 0& & & \text{...}& & & \cos(m\phi )& 0& 0& \sin(m\phi )& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0\\ 0& & & \text{...}& & & 0& \sin(m\phi )& 0& 0& \cos(m\phi )& 0& \sin(\phi )& \cos(\phi )& 0\\ 0& & & \text{...}& & & 0& 0& \cos(m\phi )& 0& 0& \sin(m\phi )& \cos(\phi )& \sin(\phi )& 1\end{array}\right]$

The mass matrix then has the expression:

$M=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}\rho {N}^{T}N\text{rdxd}\mathrm{\phi d\zeta }$ .

with

$N={({H}_{k}{N}_{k})}_{k=1,N}$ .

**Note:**

*In the case of the straight pipe, one has* ${x}_{k}.x={y}_{k}.y=1$ *and* ${x}_{k}.y={y}_{k}.x=0$ *.*

### 4.6 Shape functions

One chooses shape functions at least quadratic for the beam part (displacements and rotations) in order to avoid the phenomena of numerical locking [bib3]. This choice implies the use of a finite element with three or four nodes. In the case of an element with 3 nodes, the shape functions are quadratic, and for an element with 4 nodes, the shape functions will be cubic. For the supplementary part, one chooses to take the same shape functions as for the beam part.

The quadratic shape functions (element with 3 nodes) are the following:

$\begin{array}{}{H}_{1}(x)=(\frac{\mathrm{2x}}{l}-1)(\frac{x}{l}-1)\\ {H}_{2}(x)=\frac{x}{l}(\frac{\mathrm{2x}}{l}-1)\\ {H}_{3}(x)=-4\frac{x}{l}(\frac{x}{l}-1)\end{array}$

The cubic shape functions (element with 4 nodes) are the Lagrange functions of order 3:

$\begin{array}{c}{H}_{1}(x)=\frac{(\xi -{\xi}_{2})(\xi -{\xi}_{3})(\xi -{\xi}_{4})}{({\xi}_{1}-{\xi}_{2})({\xi}_{1}-{\xi}_{3})({\xi}_{1}-{\xi}_{4})}\\ {H}_{2}(x)=\frac{(\xi -{\xi}_{1})(\xi -{\xi}_{3})(\xi -{\xi}_{4})}{({\xi}_{2}-{\xi}_{1})({\xi}_{2}-{\xi}_{3})({\xi}_{2}-{\xi}_{4})}\\ {H}_{3}(x)=\frac{(\xi -{\xi}_{1})(\xi -{\xi}_{2})(\xi -{\xi}_{4})}{({\xi}_{3}-{\xi}_{1})({\xi}_{3}-{\xi}_{2})({\xi}_{3}-{\xi}_{4})}\\ {H}_{4}(x)=\frac{(\xi -{\xi}_{1})(\xi -{\xi}_{2})(\xi -{\xi}_{3})}{({\xi}_{4}-{\xi}_{1})({\xi}_{4}-{\xi}_{2})({\xi}_{4}-{\xi}_{3})}\\ -1\le \xi \le 1\end{array}$

### 4.7 Numerical integration

The integration is carried out by the Gauss method along the mean fibre, and by Simpson's method through the thickness and on the circumference.

For the Gauss integration:

| Matrix | TUYAU_3M - SEG3 | TUYAU_6M - SEG3 | TUYAU_3M - SEG4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stiffness | 2 | 2 | 3 |
| Mass | 3 | 3 | 3 |

All the stiffness matrices are under-integrated; the mass matrix of the TUYAU_3M elements on SEG4 is under-integrated.

The integration through the thickness is an integration by layers whose number may be fixed later by the user. For each layer one takes 3 Simpson points, the 2 end points being common with the neighbouring layers. Thus for $n$ layers one uses $\mathrm{2n}+1$ points. The number of sectors for the integration on the circumference may also be fixed later by the user. At present the numbers of layers and of sectors are fixed at their maximum value: 3 layers (7 points) and 16 sectors (33 points), which gives in total 693 integration points. ### 4.8 Discretisation of the external work

The variational formulation of the external energy for the beam part is written: $\begin{array}{}{\mathrm{\delta W}}_{\text{ext}}^{p}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}({f}_{x}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{x}+{f}_{y}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{y}+{f}_{z}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{z}+{m}_{x}{\text{δθ}}_{x}+{m}_{y}{\text{δθ}}_{y}+{m}_{z}{\text{δθ}}_{z})\text{dx}+\\ [{\phi }_{x}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{x}+{\phi }_{y}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{y}+{\phi }_{z}{\mathrm{\delta u}}_{z}+{\mu}_{x}{\text{δθ}}_{x}+{\mu}_{y}{\text{δθ}}_{y}+{\mu}_{z}{\text{δθ}}_{z}{]}_{0,l}\end{array}$

and for the supplementary part it is written taking into account only the pressure loading: ${\mathrm{\delta W}}_{\text{ext}}^{s}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}{F}_{r}\mathrm{\delta w}\text{dx}+[{\Phi}_{r}\mathrm{\delta w}{]}_{0,l}$

Taking the discretisation of the displacements into account, one can write:

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/10000000000003620000011FEEC2B04AFB50DAC8.png)

and

$\begin{array}{}\delta {W}_{\text{ext}}^{s}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}{F}_{r}{H}_{k}(x){\mathrm{dw}}_{k}^{o}\text{dx}+[{F}_{r}{H}_{k}(x){\mathrm{dw}}_{k}^{o}{]}_{0,l})\\ =\sum_{k=1}^{N}(\begin{array}{ccccccc}0& 0& 0& 0& 0& 0& \underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}{F}_{r}{H}_{k}(x)\text{dx}+[{F}_{r}{H}_{k}(x){]}_{0,l}\end{array})\delta {U}_{k}^{s}\\ =\sum_{k=1}^{N}{F}_{k}^{s}\delta {U}_{k}^{s}={F}^{s}\delta {U}^{s}\end{array}$

**Note:**

*For the end nodes of the elbow one has* ${x}_{k}.x={y}_{k}.y=1$ *and* ${x}_{k}.y={y}_{k}.x=0$ *. In the case of the straight pipe, one has* ${x}_{k}.x={y}_{k}.y=1$ *and* ${x}_{k}.y={y}_{k}.x=0$ *for the whole element.*

## 5 Geometric characteristics of the pipe element

This chapter presents a few results useful for characterising the pipe element and which are computed by the computation option MASS_INER of *Code_Aster* . In what follows the index $d$ denotes the results for the straight pipe and the index $c$ for the curved pipe.

- **Volume:** $\begin{array}{}{V}_{d}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}r\text{dx}\mathrm{d\phi }\mathrm{d\zeta }=\mathrm{2\pi }\text{lah}\\ {V}_{c}=\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}r\text{dx}\mathrm{d\phi }\mathrm{d\zeta }=\underset{0}{\overset{\Theta}{\int}}\underset{0}{\overset{2\pi }{\int}}\underset{-h/2}{\overset{h/2}{\int}}[R+(a+\zeta )\sin\phi ](a+\zeta )\mathrm{d\theta }\mathrm{d\phi }\mathrm{d\zeta }=2\pi R\Theta \text{ah}\end{array}$ .
- **Centre of gravity:** The position of the latter is computed from the mid-point of the two end nodes of the pipe element, in the frame associated with the internal node of the element (cf [§2.1.1]). In this frame, the coordinates of the centre of gravity are:

$(\begin{array}{c}{x}_{\text{Gd}}\\ {y}_{\text{Gd}}\\ {z}_{\text{Gd}}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{c}0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})$ and $(\begin{array}{c}{x}_{\text{Gc}}\\ {y}_{\text{Gc}}\\ {z}_{\text{Gc}}\end{array})=-R(\begin{array}{c}0\\ \frac{2}{\Theta}\sin\frac{\Theta}{2}(1+\frac{1}{{\mathrm{2R}}^{2}}[{a}^{2}+\frac{{h}^{2}}{4}])-\cos\frac{\Theta}{2}\\ 0\end{array})$

- **Inertia matrix:**  It is relatively easy to compute the inertia matrix at the centre of curvature of the elbow $O$ in the frame defined above. In order to have its expression one then uses the fact that:

$\begin{array}{}{I}_{xx}(G)={I}_{xx}(O)-{\text{mb}}^{2}\\ {I}_{yy}(G)={I}_{yy}(O)\\ {I}_{zz}(G)={I}_{zz}(O)-{\text{mb}}^{2}\end{array}$

where b is the distance between the centre of gravity and the centre of curvature, which is equal to:

$b=R\frac{2}{\Theta}\sin\frac{\Theta}{2}(1+\frac{1}{2{R}^{2}}[{a}^{2}+\frac{{h}^{2}}{4}])$ .

In the case of a straight pipe, the notion of centre of curvature has no meaning. $O$ and $G$ coincide with the internal node of the element and the middle of the segment joining the two vertex nodes.

If one denotes by $A$ the area of the cross-section and by $I$ its inertia with respect to the centre of the section one can write:

$\begin{array}{}{I}_{xx}^{d}(O)=\rho \text{lI}\\ {I}_{yy}^{d}(O)=\rho l(I/2+{\text{Al}}^{2}/12)\\ {I}_{zz}^{d}(O)=\rho l(I/2+{\text{Al}}^{2}/12)\end{array}$ and $\begin{array}{}{I}_{xx}^{c}(O)=\rho R\Theta (\frac{I}{2}+[{\mathrm{AR}}^{2}+3\frac{I}{2}][\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\sin\Theta }{4\Theta }])\\ {I}_{yy}^{c}(O)=\rho R\Theta (\frac{I}{2}+[{\mathrm{AR}}^{2}+3\frac{I}{2}][\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\sin\Theta }{4\Theta }])\\ {I}_{zz}^{c}(O)=\rho R\Theta ({\text{AR}}^{2}+3\frac{I}{2})\end{array}$ .

## 6 Pipe-pipe connection

In order to be able to represent correctly a piping line in which the elbows are not coplanar, one must choose a common origin of the $\phi$ . Thus for two elbows belonging to two planes perpendicular to one another, one must be able to take into account the fact that the displacements in the plane of the first elbow are equal to the out-of-plane displacements of the second in the straight connection section.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/10003076000069BB000034EBF54E2BCEC9412791.svg)

**Figure 6-a: Representation of two non-coplanar elbows connected by a straight pipe**

In [bib12], this common origin is defined by a continuous generator line along the piping as indicated above. This generator intersects each cross-section at a point. The angle between $Z$ defined in [Figure 2.1.1-a] and the straight line passing through the centre of the cross-section and this point is equal to $\Omega$ .

### 6.1 Construction of a particular generator

For a cross-section at the end of the piping line, one defines a unit origin vector ${z}_{1}$ in the plane of this section. The intersection between the direction of this vector and the mean surface of the elbow determines the trace of the generator on this section. One calls ${x}_{1},{y}_{1},{z}_{1}$ the direct triad associated with this section where ${x}_{1}$ is the unit vector perpendicular to the cross-section constructed in [Figure 2.1.1-a]. For all the other cross-sections, the triad ${x}_{k},{y}_{k},{z}_{k}$ is obtained either by rotation of the triad ${x}_{k-1},{y}_{k-1},{z}_{k-1}$ in the case of the elbow parts, or by translation of the triad ${x}_{k-1},{y}_{k-1},{z}_{k-1}$ for the straight parts of the piping. The intersection between the cross-section and the straight line issuing from the centre of this section directed by ${z}_{k}$ is the trace of a generator represented below.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/10002B48000069BB00005632384D4BAAD36FE3EE.svg)

**Figure 6.1-a: Representation of the reference generator**

The origin of the $\phi$ common to all the elements is defined with respect to the trace of this generator on the cross-section. The angle between the trace of the generator and the current position on the cross-section is then called $\psi$ .

### 6.2 Connection of one element to the next

The kinematics of [§3.1] is given in the plane of the elbow. The latter is determined by the arc of a circle generated by the axis of the elbow. The origin of the angles is the normal to the plane chosen as in [§2.1]. Defining the origin from a generator makes it possible to remove the problems of continuity of displacements from one element to another. Indeed if one postulates that the relative displacements of the cross-sections are of the type $\sum_{p=1}^{M}{u}_{p}^{i}\cos\mathrm{p\psi }+{u}_{p}^{o}\sin\mathrm{p\psi }$ where $\psi$ is the angle with the trace of the generator on the cross-section, the continuity of the displacements is automatically ensured from one element to the next.

One denotes by $Z$ the vector perpendicular to the plane of the elbow corresponding to the origin of the angles chosen up to now. One notes that the vectors $Z$ and ${z}_{k}$ are in the plane of the section $k$ . $\phi$ is the angle defined with respect to $Z$ . If one introduces $\psi$ the angle counted from the trace of the generator on the cross-section (therefore with respect to ${z}_{k}$  ) one has the following relation: $\psi =\phi -{\Omega}_{k}$ where ${\Omega}_{k}=({Z,z}_{k})$ is the angle between $Z$ and ${z}_{k}$ in the plane of the cross-section. Thus the displacements are henceforth of the type $\sum_{p=1}^{M}{u}_{p}^{i}\cos p(\phi -{\Omega}_{k})+{u}_{p}^{o}\sin p(\phi -{\Omega}_{k})$ . It is to be noted that for a given elbow the angle ${\Omega}_{k}$ is identical whatever the cross-section chosen. It is on passing from one elbow to the next that the value of ${\Omega}_{k}$ changes.

**Note** :

*When the piping is made up of collinear straight elements, one arbitrarily chooses* $\Omega =0$ *.*

### 6.3 Numerical implementation

The piping line is meshed by straight or curved elements to be ordered. The first element indicates the beginning of the piping line. One determines for this element the associated triad ${x}_{1},{y}_{1},{z}_{1}$ . If this element is straight, one chooses ${\Omega}_{1}=0$ , otherwise one computes ${\Omega}_{1}$ as indicated in the preceding paragraph. If the first element is straight the triad associated with the first cross-section of the second element ${x}_{2},{y}_{2},{z}_{2}$ is obtained by translation of ${x}_{1},{y}_{1},{z}_{1}$ . If the first element is curved, the associated triad ${x}_{2},{y}_{2},{z}_{2}$ is obtained by rotation of ${x}_{1},{y}_{1},{z}_{1}$ in the plane of the elbow. In that case ${\Omega}_{2}=0$ if the second element is straight and ${\Omega}_{2}=({z}^{2},{z}_{2})$ if the second element is curved where ${z}^{2}$ is constructed like the $z$ of [Figure 2.1.1-a]. The remainder of the construction is easily deduced by recurrence from the preceding scheme.

## 7 Shell-pipe and 3D-pipe connections

### 7.1 Approach followed

One adopts here an approach similar to the cases 3D-beam [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03), and shell-beam [R3.03.06](doc:r3.03.06): it consists in characterising the connection between an end node of a pipe element and a group of edge mesh cells of shell or 3D elements. This makes it possible to mesh part of the piping (for example an elbow) in shells or 3D elements, and the rest in straight pipes.

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/1000000000000212000001F83245C0FF8FD65E73.png)

**Figure 7.1-a: Connection between a** COQUE_3D mesh and straight pipes [HI75-98/001]

Thanks to the kinematics introduced in the pipe element, the shell–pipe and 3D–pipe connections must make it possible to mesh in shell elements or in 3D only the elbow, without straight parts, since the damping of the ovalisation (and of the warping) is taken into account in the pipe element.

The connection is expressed by kinematic relations between the degrees of freedom of the nodes of $S$ (which represents the connection section, modelled by shell or 3D edge elements), and the pipe node $N$ .

For the connection to be effective, it is necessary [R3.03.03](doc:r3.03.03) that it satisfy the following properties:

1. be able to transmit beam forces to the shell or 3D mesh, and be able to transmit all the degrees of freedom of the pipe element (or the forces dual to them),
2. not generate parasitic stresses in the shell or 3D elements,
3. not favour the kinematic relations or the static conditions with respect to one another,
4. admit arbitrary behaviours and work in dynamics.

The linear relations will have the same form as in the shell-beam case, with additional equations specific to the degrees of freedom of the pipe.

One has already introduced in [§3.1] the space $T$ of the fields associated with a wrench (defined by two vectors):

$T=\left\lbrace v\in V/\exists (T,\Omega )\text{tel que}v(M)=T+\Omega \wedge \text{GM}\right\rbrace$

where for the displacement fields of $T$ , $T$ is the translation of the section (or of the point $G$ ), $\Omega$ the infinitesimal rotation and the fields

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/Object_387.svg)

are the displacements preserving the section $S$ plane and undeformed (one again uses the assumptions of NAVIER-BERNOULLI).

The displacement of the pipe is then equal to:

$\begin{array}{ccc}{u}^{t}={u}^{p}+{u}^{s}& {u}^{p}\in T& ,{u}^{s}\in {T}^{\perp }\end{array}$

where:

${T}^{\perp }=\left\lbrace v\in V/\underset{S}{\int}v.w=0\forall w\in T\right\rbrace$

The approach consists in decomposing the shell displacement field ${u}^{c}$ or the three-dimensional displacement field ${u}^{\mathrm{3d}}$ into three fields:

${u}^{c}={u}^{p}+{u}^{s}+{u}^{\epsilon}$

- a displacement field following a beam kinematics ${u}^{p}$ (wrench),
- a local displacement field of the section following the pipe kinematics ${u}^{s}$ (Fourier series) defined in [§3.1],
- a supplementary field ${u}^{\epsilon}$ orthogonal to the first two in the sense of the scalar product.

**Note:**

When the Fourier-series decomposition of [§3.1] is infinite one has ${u}^{\epsilon}=0$ *.*

In order to translate the equation above into linear relations, one shows that one must compute the following integrals, for the shell (or the 3D) and the pipe:

- mean displacement: ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\text{dS}$
- mean rotation: ${\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge {u}^{c}\text{dS}$
- mean swelling: ${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}\text{dS}$
- Fourier modes: ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS},{\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ . For the Fourier modes one will choose the relations simplest to exploit since some are redundant (see the note of [§6.7]).

**Note:**

*One easily passes from the analytical expressions of the 3D–pipe connection to those of the shell–pipe connection. It suffices to substitute* ${u}^{\mathrm{3d}}$ *for* ${u}^{c}$ in the set of connection integrals proposed above. One will therefore speak of this connection again only in [§7.8] dealing with the numerical implementation.

### 7.2 Kinematics of the pipe.

In the curvilinear basis $(o,x,y,z)$ associated with the cross-section of [§2.1] one denotes the displacements ${u}_{1},{u}_{2}\text{et}{u}_{3}$ where:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{x}(x)-{\theta}_{y}(x)r\cos\phi +{\theta}_{z}(x)r\sin\phi +u(x,\phi )+\zeta {\beta}_{\phi }(x,\phi )\\ {u}_{2}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{y}(x)+{\theta}_{x}(x)r\cos\phi -v(x,\phi )\cos\phi -w(x,\phi )\sin\phi +\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )\cos\phi \\ {u}_{3}(r,x,\phi )={u}_{z}(x)-{\theta}_{x}(x)r\sin\phi +v(x,\phi )\sin\phi -w(x,\phi )\cos\phi -\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )\sin\phi \end{array}$ .

Once discretised this expression becomes:

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{1}(r,x,\phi )={\sum}_{k=1}^{N}{H}_{k}(x)({x}_{k}\cdot x){u}_{x}^{k}+{H}_{k}(x)({y}_{k}\cdot x){u}_{y}^{k}-\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({x}_{k}\cdot y){\theta}_{y}^{k}r\cos\phi -\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({y}_{k}\cdot y){\theta}_{y}^{k}r\cos\phi +\bar{{H}_{k}}(x){\theta}_{z}^{k}r\sin\phi +u(x,\phi )+\zeta {\beta}_{\phi }(x,\phi )\\ {u}_{2}(r,x,\phi )={\sum}_{k=1}^{N}\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({x}_{k}\cdot y){u}_{x}^{k}+{H}_{k}(x)({y}_{k}\cdot y){u}_{y}^{k}+\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({x}_{k}\cdot x){\theta}_{x}^{k}r\cos\phi +\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({y}_{k}\cdot x){\theta}_{y}^{k}r\cos\phi -v(x,\phi )\cos\phi -w(x,\phi )\sin\phi +\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )\cos\phi \\ {u}_{3}(r,x,\phi )={\sum}_{k=1}^{N}{H}_{k}(x){u}_{z}^{k}-\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({x}_{k}\cdot x){\theta}_{x}^{k}r\sin\phi -\bar{{H}_{k}}(x)({y}_{k}\cdot x){\theta}_{y}^{k}r\sin\phi +v(x,\phi )\sin\phi -w(x,\phi )\cos\phi -\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )\sin\phi \end{array}$

where $u(x,\phi ),v(x,\phi ),w(x,\phi ),{\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )\text{et}{\beta}_{\theta}(x,\phi )$ are discretised as in [§3.1].

The displacement at a node $k$ of abscissa ${x}_{k}$ at the pipe end is then written:

$\begin{array}{cc}{u}_{1}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )=& {u}_{x}^{k}-{\theta}_{y}^{k}r\cos(\phi )+{\theta}_{z}^{k}r\sin(\phi )+{\sum}_{m=2}^{M}({u}_{\mathrm{km}}^{i}\cosm\phi +{u}_{\mathrm{km}}^{0}\sinm\phi )+\zeta {\beta}_{\phi }({x}_{k},\phi )\\ {u}_{2}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )=& {u}_{y}^{k}+{\theta}_{x}^{k}r\cos(\phi )-\cos\phi {\sum}_{m=2}^{M}({v}_{\mathrm{km}}^{i}\sinm\phi +{v}_{\mathrm{km}}^{0}\cosm\phi )-\sin\phi {\sum}_{m=2}^{M}({w}_{\mathrm{km}}^{i}\cosm\phi +{w}_{\mathrm{km}}^{0}\sinm\phi )\\ & -{w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{i}\sin2\phi +{w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{0}\cos2\phi -{w}_{k}^{0}\sin\phi +\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}({x}_{k},\phi )\cos\phi \\ {u}_{3}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )=& {u}_{z}^{k}-{\theta}_{x}^{k}r\sin(\phi )+\sin\phi {\sum}_{m=2}^{M}({v}_{\mathrm{km}}^{i}\sinm\phi +{v}_{\mathrm{km}}^{0}\cosm\phi )-\cos\phi {\sum}_{m=2}^{M}({w}_{\mathrm{km}}^{i}\cosm\phi +{w}_{\mathrm{km}}^{0}\sinm\phi )\\ & -{w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{i}\cos2\phi -{w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{0}\sin2\phi -{w}_{k}^{0}\cos\phi -\zeta {\beta}_{\theta}({x}_{k},\phi )\sin\phi \end{array}$

For the pipe the angular-momentum vector $\text{GM}\wedge u(M)$ and the swelling $\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.u(M)$ have the respective expressions:

$\text{GM}\wedge u(M)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-{u}_{z}(x)r\sin\phi +{u}_{y}(x)r\cos\phi +{r}^{2}{\theta}_{x}(x)-\text{rv}(x,\phi )\\ -{\text{ru}}_{1}(r,x,\phi )\cos\phi \\ {\text{ru}}_{1}(r,x,\phi )\sin\phi \end{array}$ ,

and:

$\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.u(M)=-{u}_{z}(x)\cos\phi -{u}_{y}(x)\sin\phi +w(x,\phi )$ .

**Note:**

*The first component of the displacement field involves* $u(x,\phi )$ *in isolation. The same is true of the first component of the rotation vector with respect to* $v(x,\phi )$ *and of the swelling with respect to* $w(x,\phi )$ . This note will be used in [§5.6] to link the Fourier modes to the degrees of freedom of the shell edges.

### 7.3 Shell kinematics

The Love-Kirchhoff or Naghdi-Mindlin shell kinematics is written through the thickness:

${u}^{c}(M)={u}^{c}(Q)+({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n).{y}_{3}$

- ${u}^{c}(Q)$ constitutes the displacement vector of the mean surface at $Q$ ,
- ${\theta}^{c}(Q)$ constitutes the rotation vector at $Q$ of the normal along the directions ${t}_{1}$ and ${t}_{2}$ of the plane tangent at $Q$ .

![Figure](/figures/r3.08.06/Object_428.svg)

This displacement and this rotation are computed in the global frame. It is possible by change of frame to have their expressions in the curvilinear basis $(o,x,y,z)$ of [§2.1] associated with the cross-section of the junction between the shell and the pipe.

For each node, the program computes the coefficients of the $9+6(M-1)$ linear relations which relate:

- the 6 degrees of freedom of the beam node $P$ of the pipe,
- the $2+3\times 2(M-1)$ Fourier degrees of freedom of the pipe,
- the swelling degree of freedom of the pipe,
- with the degrees of freedom of all the nodes of the list of mesh cells of the shell edge.

These linear relations will be dualised, like all the linear relations issuing for example from the keyword LIAISON_DDL of AFFE_CHAR_MECA. They are constructed as for the 3D-beam connection from the assembly of elementary terms.

### 7.4 Computation of the mean displacement on the section S

It consists in computing the integral ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\text{dS}$ , where ${u}^{c}$ is the shell displacement (comprising 6 degrees of freedom per node), $S$ is the shell edge.

The mean displacement on the section $S$ is written:

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n)({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\text{dl}$

that is ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\text{dl}$ .

Moreover one also has for the pipe part:

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\text{dS}={\int}_{S}[{u}^{p}(M)+{u}^{s}(M)]\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}^{k}\\ {u}_{y}^{k}\\ {u}_{z}^{k}\end{array})\text{dS}=S(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}^{k}\\ {u}_{y}^{k}\\ {u}_{z}^{k}\end{array})$ .

One thus establishes that the mean displacement of the pipe section at node $k$ is equal to the beam displacement of node $k$ . One can thus link linearly the translational beam degrees of freedom at node $k$ with the mean of the displacement degrees of freedom of the shell edge.

One neglects in this expression the variation of the metric through the thickness of the shell.

### 7.5 Computation of the mean rotation of the section S

$\begin{array}{}{\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge {u}^{c}(M)\text{dS}={\int}_{l}{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}(\text{GQ}+{y}_{3}n(Q))\wedge ({u}^{c}(Q)+{\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n(Q){y}_{3})\text{dl}{\text{dy}}_{3}\\ =h{\int}_{l}\text{GQ}\wedge {u}^{c}(Q)\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}\text{GQ}\wedge ({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n(Q))\text{dl}{\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3}\\ +{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge {u}^{c}(Q)({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}n(Q)\wedge ({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n(Q)){\int}_{-\frac{h}{2}}^{\frac{h}{2}}{y}_{3}^{2}{\text{dy}}_{3}.\text{dl}\end{array}$

that is ${\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge {u}^{c}(M).\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}\text{GQ}\wedge {u}^{c}(Q)\text{dl}+\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}{\int}_{l}{\theta}^{c}(Q)\text{dl}$ .

Moreover one also has for the pipe part:

${\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge {u}^{c}(M).\text{dS}={\int}_{S}\text{GM}\wedge \left[{u}^{p}(M)+{u}^{s}(M)\right].\text{dS}={\int}_{S}(\begin{array}{c}{r}^{2}.{\theta}_{x}^{k}\\ {r}^{2}.{\cos}^{2}\phi {\theta}_{y}^{k}\\ {r}^{2}.{\sin}^{2}\phi {\theta}_{z}^{k}\end{array})=I(\begin{array}{c}{\theta}_{x}^{k}\\ {\theta}_{y}^{k}\\ {\theta}_{z}^{k}\end{array})$

where *I*  is the inertia tensor of the beam. One thus establishes that the mean rotation of the pipe section at node $k$ is equal to the beam rotation at node $k$ . One can thus link linearly the rotational beam degrees of freedom at node $k$ with the rotation degrees of freedom of the shell edge.

One neglects in this expression the variation of the metric through the thickness of the shell.

### 7.6 Computation of the mean swelling of the section S

It consists in computing the integral ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}.n\text{dS}={\int}_{S}{u}^{c}.\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\text{dS}$ , where $n=\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }=\frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }$ is the normal to the shell mean surface.

The mean displacement on the section $S$ is written:

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M).n\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q).n\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}({\theta}^{c}(Q)\mathrm{\wedge }n).n({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\text{dl}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q).n\text{dl}$ .

Moreover one also has for the pipe part:

${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\mathrm{\parallel }\text{GM}\mathrm{\parallel }}.{u}^{c}(M)\text{dS}={\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\mathrm{\parallel }\text{GM}\mathrm{\parallel }}.[{u}^{p}(M)+{u}^{s}(M)]\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}{w}_{k}^{o}\text{dS}$

One thus establishes that the mean swelling of the pipe section at node $k$ is equal to the swelling degree of freedom of the pipe at node $k$ . One can thus link linearly the pipe swelling degree of freedom at node $k$ with the displacement degrees of freedom of the shell edge.

One neglects in this expression the variation of the metric through the thickness of the shell.

### 7.7 Computation of the Fourier coefficients on the section S

It consists in computing the six integrals ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , ${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$  and ${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ , where ${u}^{c}$ is the shell displacement (comprising 6 degrees of freedom per node), $S$ is the shell edge.

One has the following relation for the displacements on the section $S$ :

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n)({\int}_{-h/2}^{h/2}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}$

that is ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}$ and ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}$ .

Moreover one also has for the pipe part:

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\\ {u}_{2}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\\ {u}_{3}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\end{array})\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ .

The first component of this relation then allows us to link linearly the Fourier coefficient ${u}_{\text{kp}}^{i}$ to the components of the displacements of the shell edge in the following way:

$h{\int}_{l}{u}_{1}^{c}(Q)\cos\mathit{p\phi }\text{dl}={\int}_{S}{u}_{1}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\cos\mathit{p\phi }\text{dS}=\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}-\underset{S}{\int}{\mathit{r\theta }}_{y}^{k}{\cos}^{2}\phi \text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{u}_{\text{kp}}^{i}{\cos}^{2}\mathit{p\phi }\text{dS}& \text{si}p\mathrm{\ne }1\end{array}$

Likewise ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=\underset{S}{\int}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{1}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\\ {u}_{2}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\\ {u}_{3}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\end{array})\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}$ whence one deduces that:

$h{\int}_{l}{u}_{1}^{c}(Q)\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}={\int}_{S}{u}_{1}(r,{x}_{k},\phi )\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}{\mathrm{r\theta }}_{z}^{k}{\sin}^{2}\phi \text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{u}_{\text{kp}}^{o}{\sin}^{2}\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}& \text{si}p\ne 1\end{array}\begin{array}{c}\\ \end{array}$

One has the following relation for the rotations on the section $S$ :

${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}(M)\wedge .\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}{u}^{c}(Q)\wedge \frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}+{\int}_{l}({\theta}^{c}(Q)\wedge n)\wedge n(\underset{-h/2}{\overset{+h/2}{\int}}{y}_{3}{\text{dy}}_{3})\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}$ .

The first component of this relation then allows us to link linearly the Fourier coefficient ${v}_{\text{kp}}^{o}$ to the components of the displacements and of the rotations of the shell edge in the following way:

$h{\int}_{l}[{u}^{c}(Q)\wedge \frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }{]}_{1}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}-\underset{S}{\int}[{\text{ru}}_{y}^{k}{\cos}^{2}\phi +{\text{rw}}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{o}{\cos}^{2}\phi ]\text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{\text{rv}}_{\text{kp}}^{o}{\cos}^{2}(\mathrm{p\phi })\text{dS}& \text{si}p\ne 1\end{array}$

Likewise one has:

$h{\int}_{l}[{u}^{c}(Q)\wedge \frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }{]}_{1}\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}[{\text{ru}}_{z}^{k}{\sin}^{2}\phi +{\text{rw}}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{i}{\sin}^{2}\phi ]\text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{\text{rv}}_{\text{kp}}^{i}{\sin}^{2}(\mathrm{p\phi })\text{dS}& \text{si}p\ne 1\end{array}$

One has the following relation for the swelling on the section $S$ :

${\int}_{S}\frac{\text{GM}}{\parallel \text{GM}\parallel }.{u}^{c}(M).\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dS}=h{\int}_{l}\frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }.{u}^{c}(Q)\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}$ .

This relation allows us to link linearly the Fourier coefficient ${w}_{\text{kp}}^{i}$ to the components of the displacements of the shell edge in the following way:

$h{\int}_{l}\frac{\text{GQ}}{\mathrm{\parallel }\text{GQ}\mathrm{\parallel }}.{u}^{c}(Q)\cos\mathit{p\phi }\text{dl}=\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}[-{u}_{z}^{k}{\cos}^{2}\phi +{w}_{\mathit{k1}}^{i}{\cos}^{2}\phi ]\text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{w}_{\text{kp}}^{i}{\cos}^{2}(\mathit{p\phi })\text{dS}& \text{si}p\mathrm{\ne }1\end{array}$ .

Likewise, one has:

$h{\int}_{l}\frac{\text{GQ}}{\parallel \text{GQ}\parallel }.{u}^{c}(Q)\sin\mathrm{p\phi }\text{dl}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\underset{S}{\int}-{u}_{y}^{k}{\sin}^{2}\phi +{w}_{\mathrm{k1}}^{o}{\sin}^{2}\phi \text{dS}& \text{si}p=1\\ \underset{S}{\int}{w}_{\text{kp}}^{o}{\sin}^{2}(\mathrm{p\phi })\text{dS}& \text{si}p\ne 1\end{array}$

One uses for all these relations the fact that $\underset{S}{\int}\cos\mathrm{p\phi }\cos\mathrm{q\phi }\text{dS}=0\text{si}p\ne q.$

One neglects in this expression the variation of the metric through the thickness of the shell.

**Note:**

One may note that some of the relations established in this paragraph for p=1 are redundant with those established in paragraphs [§7.4] and [§7.5]. Of the six relations established from the computation of the integral forms ${\int}_{S}{u}^{\mathrm{c.}}\cos\phi \mathrm{dS}$ *,* ${\int}_{S}{u}^{\mathrm{c.}}\sin\phi \mathrm{dS}$ *,* ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\mathrm{GM}}{∥\mathrm{GM}∥}.\cos\phi \mathrm{dS}$ *,* ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\mathrm{GM}}{∥\mathrm{GM}∥}.\sin\phi \mathrm{dS}$ *,* ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\mathrm{GM}}{∥\mathrm{GM}∥}.{u}^{c}\cos\phi \mathrm{dS}$ *and* ${\int}_{S}{u}^{c}\wedge \frac{\mathrm{GM}}{∥\mathrm{GM}∥}.{u}^{c}\sin\phi \mathrm{dS}$ , only two among the last four are linearly independent of the others. Thus the first two have already been established in [§7.4] and combinations of the last four give back those of [§7.5].

### 7.8 Implementation of the method

The computation of the coefficients of the linear relations is carried out in three stages:

- computation of elementary quantities on the elements of the list of mesh cells of shell edges (mesh cell of type SEG2):
- area = ${\int}_{\text{elt}}1;{\int}_{\text{elt}}x;{\int}_{\text{elt}}y;{\int}_{\text{elt}}z$ .
- summation of these quantities on ($S$ ) whence the computation of:
- $A=\mid S\mid$
- position of $G$ .
- knowing $G$ , elementary computation on the elements of the list of mesh cells of shell edges of:

${\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{Ni};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{xNi};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{yNi};{\int}_{\text{elt}}\text{zNi}\text{où :}\begin{array}{c}\text{GM}=\left\lbrace x,y,z\right\rbrace \\ \text{Ni}=\text{fonctions de forme de l'élément}\end{array}$

It must be noted that in the case of the shell–pipe connection, the integrals on the edge elements are to be multiplied by the thickness of the shell: $\underset{\text{elt}}{\int}{N}_{i}=h\underset{l}{\int}{N}_{i}$ where $l$ represents the mean fibre of the shell edge element. Moreover, one adds the supplementary term: $\frac{{h}^{3}}{12}\underset{l}{\int}{N}_{i}$ .

- “assembly” of the terms computed above in order to obtain, at each of the nodes of the connection section, the coefficients of the terms of the linear relations,
- connection between the Fourier modes and the shell displacements as shown at the beginning of [§7].

More precisely:

- for the shell–pipe connection, one carries out elementary computations on all the edge elements of the connection section $S$ of the type:

${u}_{\text{cm}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}{u}^{c}\cos(m\varphi )d\varphi =\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{\varphi}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{r}^{c}}\end{array})\cos(m\varphi )d\varphi =\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\cos(m\varphi )P(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{y}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{z}^{c}}\end{array})d\varphi$

and

${u}_{\text{sm}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}{u}^{c}\sin(m\varphi )d\varphi =\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{0}{\overset{\mathrm{2\pi }}{\int}}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{\varphi}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{r}^{c}}\end{array})\sin(m\varphi )d\varphi =\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\sin(m\varphi )P(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{y}^{c}}\\ {u}_{{z}^{c}}\end{array})d\varphi$

if $m>1$ where $P$ is the transformation matrix from the local frame of the element to the global frame and ${\varphi}_{1}$ the circumferential position of the element. Expressing the displacement as a function of the nodal degrees of freedom:

${u}_{\text{cm}}=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{m}^{i}\\ {v}_{m}^{o}\\ {w}_{m}^{i}\end{array})=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\cos(m\varphi )P\sum_{n=1}^{{\text{Nb}}_{\text{noeuds}}}{N}_{n}(\varphi )(\begin{array}{c}{U}_{{x}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{y}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{z}^{n}}\end{array})d\varphi$

and

${u}_{\text{sm}}=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{m}^{o}\\ {v}_{m}^{i}\\ {w}_{m}^{o}\end{array})=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\sin(m\varphi )P\sum_{n=1}^{{\text{Nb}}_{\text{noeuds}}}{N}_{n}(\varphi )(\begin{array}{c}{U}_{{x}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{y}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{z}^{n}}\end{array})d\varphi$

where the $N$ are the shape functions of the element, one obtains for each computation twice 9 coefficients at the nodes of the current element of $S$ :

$\begin{array}{}{u}_{\text{cm}}=(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{m}^{i}\\ {v}_{m}^{o}\\ {w}_{m}^{i}\end{array})=\left\lbrace \sum_{\text{éléments}\in S}^{}\sum_{n=1}^{{\text{Nb}}_{\text{noeuds}}}(\begin{array}{ccc}{a}_{11}& {a}_{12}& {a}_{13}\\ {a}_{21}& {a}_{22}& {a}_{23}\\ {a}_{31}& {a}_{32}& {a}_{33}\end{array})\right\rbrace (\begin{array}{c}{U}_{{x}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{y}^{n}}\\ {U}_{{z}^{n}}\end{array}).\\ {a}_{ij}^{n}=\frac{1}{\pi}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}}{\int}}\cos(m\varphi ){P}_{ij}(\varphi ){N}_{n}(\varphi )d\varphi =\frac{1}{\mathrm{\pi R}}\underset{0}{\overset{l}{\int}}\cos(\text{mx}){P}_{ij}(x){N}_{n}(x)\text{dx}\end{array}$

and an equivalent expression for ${u}_{\mathrm{sm}}$ where $I$ is the length of the shell edge element.

- for the 3D–pipe connection, one carries out elementary computations on all the edge elements of the connection section $S$ of the type:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{\text{cm}}=\frac{2}{S}\underset{S}{\overset{}{\int}}{u}^{\text{3d}}\cos(m\varphi )\text{dS}=\frac{2}{S}\underset{S}{\overset{}{\int}}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{\text{3d}}}\\ {u}_{{\varphi}^{\text{3d}}}\\ {u}_{{r}^{\text{3d}}}\end{array})\cos(m\varphi )r.d\varphi .\mathit{d\zeta }\\ =\frac{2}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\underset{{h}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{h}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\cos(m\varphi )P\sum_{n=1}^{{\text{Nb}}_{\text{noeuds}}}{N}_{n}(\varphi ,\zeta ){(\begin{array}{c}{U}_{x}^{\mathrm{3d}}\\ {U}_{y}^{\mathrm{3d}}\\ {U}_{z}^{\mathrm{3d}}\end{array})}^{n}r.d\varphi .\mathit{d\zeta }\end{array}$

and

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{\text{sm}}=\frac{2}{S}\underset{S}{\overset{}{\int}}{u}^{\text{3d}}c\sin(m\varphi )\text{dS}=\frac{2}{S}\underset{S}{\overset{}{\int}}(\begin{array}{c}{u}_{{x}^{\text{3d}}}\\ {u}_{{\varphi}^{\text{3d}}}\\ {u}_{{r}^{\text{3d}}}\end{array})\sin(m\varphi )r.d\varphi .\mathit{d\zeta }\\ =\frac{2}{S}\sum_{n=1}^{\text{Nb}\varphi \text{éléments}\in S}\underset{{\varphi}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{\varphi}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\underset{{h}_{1}^{n}}{\overset{{h}_{2}^{n}}{\int}}\sin(m\varphi )P\sum_{n=1}^{{\text{Nb}}_{\text{noeuds}}}{N}_{n}(\varphi ,\zeta ){(\begin{array}{c}{U}_{x}^{\mathrm{3d}}\\ {U}_{y}^{\mathrm{3d}}\\ {U}_{z}^{\mathrm{3d}}\end{array})}^{n}\mathit{r.}d\varphi .\mathit{d\zeta }\end{array}$

## 8 Implementation of the TUYAU element in Code_Aster

### 8.1 Description

This new element (of name METUSEG3) is supported on a curvilinear SEG3 or SEG4 mesh cell. It assumes that the section of the pipe is circular. Unlike the elements POU_D_E, POU_D_T, [R3.08.01](doc:r3.08.01) this element is not “exact” at the nodes for loadings or wrenches concentrated at the ends. One must therefore mesh with several elements in order to obtain correct results.

### 8.2 Modelling data

The element is used in the following way:

AFFE_MODELE ( MODELISATION = 'TUYAU_3M' ...)

The mesh cells with 4 nodes are generated from the mesh cells with 3 nodes by means of:

MAIL=CREA_MAILLAGE(MAILLAGE=MAIL,MODI_MAILLE=_F(OPTION='SEG3_4',TOUT='OUI'))

One calls the routine INI090 for the shape functions, their derivatives and their second derivatives (for the shell part) at the Gauss points, as well as the corresponding weights.

The characteristics of the section are defined in AFFE_CARA_ELEM

AFFE_CARA_ELEM (

> POUTRE = _F(SECTION = 'CERCLE' ,CARA = ( 'R' 'EP' ),VALE = (.....) , ) ,

> ORIENTATION =_F(GROUP_NO=D,CARA=’GENE_TUYAU’,VALE=(X Y Z),),

> TUYAU_NCOU=’NOMBRE DE COUCHES’, TUYAU_NSEC=’NOMBRE DE SECTEURS’,),

)

R and EP represent, as for the classical beam elements, respectively the external radius and the thickness of the section. One also defines on one of the end nodes of the piping line the vector whose projection on the cross-section is the origin of the angles for the Fourier-series decomposition. This vector must not be collinear with the mean line of the elbow at the end node considered. One also defines at this level the number of layers and of angular sectors to be used for the numerical integration.

```text
AFFE_CHAR_MECA ( DDL_IMPO = _F (

DX=..,DY=..,DZ=..,DRX=..,DRY=..,DRZ=..,DDL de poutre

UI2=..,VI2=..,WI2=..,UO2=..,VO2=..,WO2=..,DDL liés au mode 2

UI3=..,VI3=..,WI3=..,UO3=..,VO3=..,WO3=..,DDL liés au mode 3

WO=..,WI1=..,WO1=..,                             ,DDL de gonflement et mode 1 sur :math:`W`
```

The loadings supported in AFFE_CHAR_MECA are:

- the nodal forces (FORCE_NODALE), which work only on the beam displacements.
- the internal pressure (FORCE_TUYAU= _F (PRES =.. ))
- gravity, (PESANTEUR)
- the line forces ( FORCE_POUTRE)

The internal pressure working on the swelling degree of freedom $\mathit{WO}$ , one then computes:

${W}_{\text{pres}}={\int}_{0}^{l}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }p{w}^{o}{r}_{{}_{int}}d\phi \text{dx}={\int}_{0}^{l}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }p\sum_{k=1}^{N}{H}_{k}{w}_{k}^{o}{r}_{{}_{int}}d\phi \text{dx}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}{H}_{k}\left[{\int}_{0}^{l}{\int}_{0}^{2\pi }p{r}_{{}_{int}}d\phi \text{dx}\right]{w}_{k}^{o}$

### 8.3 Analysis in linear elasticity

The stiffness matrix and the mass matrix (respectively the options RIGI_MECA and MASS_MECA) are integrated numerically in TE0582. The computation takes into account the fact that the terms corresponding to the beam degrees of freedom are expressed classically in the global frame, and that the Fourier degrees of freedom are in the local frame of the element. In the case where the element does not belong to any elbow, this local frame is defined by the generator and the director vector carried by the mean fibre of the element as indicated in [Figure 8.3-a]. In the case where the element belongs to an elbow, the local frame is defined from the plane of the elbow as mentioned in [§2.1].

### 8.4 Non-linear analyses

The tangent stiffness matrix (options RIGI_MECA_TANG and FULL_MECA) as well as the plastic projection (options FULL_MECA and RAPH_MECA) are integrated numerically in TE0586. All the plane-stress laws available in *Code_Aster*  can be used: if they are not integrated directly, it is always possible to use a constitutive law formulated in plane strain, and to treat the plane-stress assumption by means of the method of De Borst.

The pipe elements must be used only in small strains and small displacements.

### 8.5 Post-processing

The elementary computations currently available correspond to the options:

- SIEF_ELGA which provide the stresses at the integration points in the user frame. One stores these values in the following way:
- for each Gauss point along the length, ($n=1,2$ or $n=1,3$)
- for each integration point through the thickness, ($n=1$ *,* $2{N}_{\mathit{COU}}+1=7$ )
- for each integration point on the circumference, ($n=1$ *,* $2{N}_{\mathit{SECT}}+1=33$ )
- 6 stress components: SIXX SIYY SIZZ SIXY SIXZ SIYZ

where $X$ denotes the direction given by the two vertex nodes of the element, $Y$ represents the angle $\phi$ describing the circumference and $Z$ represents the radius. SIZZ and SIYZ corresponding to ${\sigma}_{\text{rr}},{\sigma}_{\mathrm{r\phi }}$ are taken equal to zero.

- EFGE_ELNO which makes it possible to obtain the generalised forces per element at the nodes in the frame of the beam.
- VARI_ELNO which computes the field of internal variables per element at the nodes for all the layers and all the sectors, in the local frame of the element.
- EPSI_ELGA which provides the total strains at the integration points in the local frame of the element. The computation is carried out in TE0584, and currently gives the values at the 693 integration points (for an element with 3 Fourier modes). These fields are called fields at integration “sub-points”. One stores these values in the following way:
- for each Gauss point along the length, ($n=1,2$ or $n=1,3$)
- for each integration point through the thickness, ($n=1$ *,* $2{N}_{\mathit{COU}}+1=7$ )
- for each integration point on the circumference, ($n=1$ *,* $2{N}_{\mathit{SECT}}+1=33$ )
- 6 strain components: EPXX EPYY EPZZ EPXY EPXZ EPYZ

where $X$ denotes the direction given by the two vertex nodes of the element, $Y$ represents the angle $\phi$ describing the circumference and $Z$ represents the radius. EPZZ and EPYZ corresponding to ${\epsilon}_{\text{rr}},{\epsilon}_{\mathrm{r\phi }}$ are taken equal to zero.

- The options SIEQ_ELGA and EPEQ_ELGA allow the computation of the invariants (von Mises, signed von Mises, trace) at each integration point (fields at “sub-points”).
- EFGE_ELNO provides the classical generalised beam forces: N, VY, VZ, MT, MFY, MFZ. These forces are given in the local curvilinear frame of the element.
- The command POST_CHAMP / MIN_MAX_SP makes it possible to extract, at each of the line Gauss points of an element, the maximum and minimum values of a component of a field. The min / max is taken over the set of sub-points of a point.

## 9 Conclusion

The elbow finite elements which we describe here are usable for piping analyses in elasticity or in plasticity. The piping may be subjected to various combined loadings — internal pressure, in-plane and out-of-plane bending, torsion, extension.

For the moment, the element produced is a line element of beam type, straight or curved, with three nodes, in small rotations and strains, with a local elastoplastic behaviour in plane stress. It makes it possible to take ovalisation, warping and swelling into account. It combines the properties of shells and of beams. The beam kinematics for the axis of the elbow is augmented by a shell kinematics, of Love-Kirchhoff type without transverse shear, for the description of the behaviour of the cross-sections. This latter kinematics is discretised in $M$ Fourier modes, the number $M$ of which, which the literature encourages us to choose equal to 6 [bib8], [bib13], must at once be sufficient to obtain good results in plasticity and not too large in order to limit the computation time. In elasticity, for relatively thick piping (the ratio of thickness to radius of the cross-section greater than 0.1), one may be content with $M=2$ or $M=3$ .
