# r4.05.04 Soil-structure interaction with spatial variability (operator DYNA_ISS_VARI)

**Summary:**

This document is a theoretical note describing the methods developed in the operator DYNA_ISS_VARI. This operator makes it possible to treat soil-structure interaction (SSI) problems in seismic analysis in which one wishes to take account of the spatial variability of the incident seismic field. Within the framework of a standard seismic study with soil-structure interaction by means of the *Code_Aster*/MISS3D chaining, one assumes that the seismic excitation does not vary spatially. However, the spatial variability may have non-negligible effects on the response of structures subjected to an earthquake. One observes in particular a reduction of the response in translation, which may make it possible to release margins in the SSI analyses.

DYNA_ISS_VARI makes it possible to compute the response of a structure subjected to a seismic motion variable in space from a coherence function, from the impedance matrix and from the seismic force. The latter may be computed by the software MISS3D. More precisely, one constructs the spectral vectors of modal response (coming from a spectral decomposition of the coherence matrix) by way of a harmonic analysis in generalised components. On output, one obtains the spectral density of the modal response (for a unit excitation) or the time response (in acceleration).

## 1 Introduction

In seismic analyses with soil-structure interaction, the current practice consists in considering a free-field motion that is uniform at every point of the surface of the soil. Recent observations of strong motions on seismographic arrays have nevertheless revealed spatial variations on fairly small scales. Taking the spatial variability of the incident field into account leads to a filtering of the translational motions for the high frequencies. The introduction of the spatial variability may thus lead to reduced floor spectra.

The computation of the response is carried out in three steps:

- Computation of the generalised matrices of the structure fixed on the interface.
- Determination of the interface impedance matrices and of the seismic force by MISS3D: The software MISS3D rests on a substructuring method. The domain of the study is decomposed into subdomains — in our case the soil and the structure — coupled to one another by interfaces. One applies a multi-domain solution method and only the interfaces between domains need to be meshed by boundary finite elements. This makes it possible to model the structure (building) as well as the loadings applied to it entirely by *Code_Aster* . The code MISS3D, for its part, makes it possible to determine the interface impedances between soil and structure as well as the seismic force exerted by the incident field at the level of the interface. The solution of the dynamic problem and the post-processing are carried out again with *Code_Aster* .
- Solution of the dynamic problem on a reduced basis (coming from the substructuring) in the frequency domain (DYNA_ISS_VARI) and post-processing.

## 2 Description of the command DYNA_ISS_VARI

The operator DYNA_ISS_VARI [U4.53.31](doc:u4.53.31) makes it possible to compute the response of a structure subjected to a seismic motion variable in space from a coherence function, from the interface impedance matrix and the seismic force. The latter may be computed with the aid of the *Code_Aster*/MISS3D chaining, cf. [U2.06.07](doc:u2.06.07). On output from DYNA_ISS_VARI, one obtains, in generalised coordinates, the response spectral density or a time response to a time excitation.

More precisely, one constructs the spectral vectors of modal response (coming from a spectral decomposition of the coherence matrix) by way of a harmonic analysis in generalised components. Then, one determines the power spectral density (PSD) of the modal response or else the time response in generalised components.

The results that one can obtain by using the command DYNA_ISS_VARI (for the cases with or without spatial variability) are the following:

- Computation of transfer functions between the seismic excitation and the response of the structure (the transfer functions are obtained by choosing an excitation by white noise).
- Computation of the response spectral density for the case in which the seismic excitation is given by a spectral density (the Kanai–Tajimi spectrum is most often used to describe the seismic excitation, see also [R4.05.02](doc:r4.05.02)).
- Computation of the time response in generalised components. The simulation of a realisation of the time response then makes it possible to determine floor spectra.

### 2.1 Seismic analysis with SSI in the frequency domain

The software MISS3D is based on a frequency solution on the soil side; it makes it possible to determine the impedance matrices as well as the seismic force at the interface. The soil-structure interaction problem amounts to solving on the interface the harmonic dynamic equation:

$$
\left[\underset{\text{Equation d'équilibre du bâtiment}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{K}_{b}+i\omega {C}_{b}-{\omega}^{2}{M}_{b}}}+\underset{\mathit{Impédance}d'\mathit{interface}\mathit{sol}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{K}_{s}(\omega )}}\right]q(\omega )-\underset{\text{Force sismique}}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{{f}_{s}(\omega )}}=0
$$

In equation (), $q(\omega )\in {ℂ}^{m}$ is the vector of the generalised unknowns describing the displacement.

In SSI analyses with MISS3D, one must supply a free-field accelerogram. The Fourier transform of this signal and the impedance matrix computed by MISS3D make it possible to determine the seismic force in the frequency domain, ${f}_{s}(\omega )$ .

For what follows, one defines the complex displacement transfer function $H(\omega )\in {\text{Mat}}_{\mathrm{ℂ}}(m,m)$ as:

$$
H(\omega ){f}_{s}(\omega )=q(\omega )
$$

If the excitation is assumed to be a stationary Gaussian stochastic process, then the response is likewise a stationary Gaussian stochastic process. This is true because the transfer function is a linear filter. Thus, one may write the relation between the spectral densities of the seismic force and of the response:

$$
H(\omega ){S}_{f}(\omega ){H}^{\text{*}}(\omega )={S}_{q}(\omega )
$$

Where ${H}^{\text{*}}$ denotes the conjugate transpose of $H$ and ${S}_{f}$ is the spectral-density matrix of the seismic force, which may be evaluated from $L$ realisations of the time seismic force ${f}_{s}^{l}(t)$ :

$$
{S}_{f}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}{f}_{s}^{l}(\omega ){f}_{s}^{l}\text{*}(\omega )
$$

In this expression, $T$ denotes the time interval and $W(t)=1/\sqrt{(T)}$ is the natural window on $[0,T]$ (cf. [bib10]). It is an unbiased estimator of the spectral density [bib10].

**Note**:

*One may also write:*

${S}_{f}(\omega )=\underset{T\to \infty }{\lim}\frac{1}{2\pi T}E({f}_{s}(\omega ){f}_{s}\text{*}(\omega ))$

*where* $E(\cdot )$ *denotes the mathematical-expectation operator.*

### 2.2 Taking spatial variability into account

DYNA_ISS_VARI is based on a probabilistic description of the incident seismic field by its power spectral density (PSD). The latter is generally constructed with the aid of a point spectrum and of a spatial coherence function. Thus, the cross spectral density of the free-field ground motion is written:

$$
{S}_{u}(x,x\text{',}\omega )=\gamma (x,x\text{',}\omega ){S}_{0}(\omega )
$$

Where $\gamma (x,x\text{',}\omega )$ is the coherence function of the seismic signal between two points $x$ and $x'$ and ${S}_{0}\in ℝ$ is the point spectral density of the free-field seismic motion. One assumes here that the spectral density is defined on the interval $O=\left[-{\omega}_{s},+{\omega}_{s}\right]$ and that it is zero outside this frequency range. If one discretises with respect to the spatial variable, $x$ and $x'$ , one obtains the following spectral-density matrix:

$$
{S}_{u}(\omega )=\gamma (\omega ){S}_{0}(\omega )\in {\text{Mat}}_{ℂ}(m,m)
$$

In this expression, $\gamma$ (of dimension $m\times m$ ) denotes the coherence matrix and one denotes its components ${\gamma}_{ij}(\omega )=\gamma ({x}_{i},{x}_{j},\omega )$ . The elements of ${S}_{u}(\omega )=\left[{S}_{ij}(\omega )\right]\in \text{Mat}(m,m)$ are the cross spectral densities, ${S}_{ij}(\omega )=S({x}_{i},{x}_{j},\omega )$ , $m$ being the number of spatial discretisation points. In general, one assumes that the incident seismic field is homogeneous, that is that the stochastic description of the field depends only on the distance, $d=\mid x-x'\mid$ , but that it is independent of the spatial position.

The computation of the seismic response relies on the spectral decomposition of the coherence matrix $\gamma (\omega )$ . Let us specify that this is a spectral decomposition with respect to the spatial variable and not with respect to the time variable. Thus, one has:

$$
{S}_{u}(\omega )=\Phi (\omega )\Lambda (\omega )\Phi \text{*}(\omega ){S}_{0}(\omega )
$$

Where $\Phi$ is a matrix containing the eigenvectors ${\varphi}_{k}$ of the coherence matrix $\gamma$ and $\Lambda$ is the diagonal matrix containing the eigenvalues, $\Lambda =\text{diag}({\lambda}_{k}),k=1,\dots m$ . In what follows, one will speak of POD modes (Proper Orthogonal Decomposition) to designate the ${\varphi}_{k}$ . This makes it possible to distinguish them from the mechanical modes. From the expression of equation (), one defines:

$$
{s}_{u}^{k}(\omega )={\varphi}_{k}(\omega )\sqrt{{\lambda}_{k}(\omega )}\sqrt{{S}_{0}(\omega )},\forall \omega \in O
$$

Knowing that ${S}_{u}(\omega )=\sum_{k=1}^{m}{s}_{u}^{k}(\omega ){s}_{u}^{k}\text{*}(\omega )$ . The spectral density of the seismic force is obtained from the motion at the soil interface by the matrix transfer function $G(\omega )$ :

$$
{S}_{f}(\omega )=G(\omega ){S}_{u}(\omega )G\text{*}(\omega )
$$

And thus:

$$
{s}_{f}^{k}(\omega )=G(\omega ){\varphi}_{k}(\omega )\sqrt{{\lambda}_{k}(\omega )}\sqrt{{S}_{0}(\omega )},\forall \omega \in \Omega
$$

In SSI studies with *Code_Aster* , the transfer function $G(\omega )$ is computed by MISS3D; this is described in more detail in section § 2.2.1 .

Expression () is the input for the traditional seismic analysis, see equation (). The model is “reduced” if one can truncate expression () to $K\le m$ POD modes.

As has just been seen, the computation of the seismic forces with spatial variability of the incident field implies a spectral decomposition of the coherence matrix $\gamma$ . For the remainder of the analyses, one retains only a reduced number of POD modes, namely $K\le m$ modes. The parameter precision gives the share of the “energy” of the matrix that one retains by keeping only a reduced number of eigenvectors and eigenvalues of $\gamma$ . If one designates by $K$ the number of POD modes retained (one retains the $K$ largest eigenvalues), one has:

$$
\text{precision}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{K}{\lambda}_{i}^{2}}{\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\lambda}_{i}^{2}}
$$

The value $0,999$ is taken by default for the precision.

#### 2.2.1 Coherence functions

The coherence function depends on the separation distance $d$ between two points $x$ and $x'$ and on the frequency. In general, one expresses it by a modulus term and a phase term:

$$
\gamma (d,\omega )=∣\gamma (d,\omega )∣\exp(-i\theta (\omega ,d))
$$

The term $\exp(-i\theta (\omega ,d))$ represents the phase shift due to the different arrival times of the waves. The amplitude term corresponds to “pure incoherence”. It may be evaluated from the PSDs (auto-spectra and cross-spectra) at the points $x$ and $x'$ :

$$
{∣\gamma (d,\omega )∣}^{2}=\frac{S{(\omega ,x,x')}^{2}}{S(\omega ,x)S(\omega ,x')}
$$

The coherence functions currently available in DYNA_ISS_VARI are the coherence function of Mita & Luco and the coherence function of Abrahamson (for rock). One does not introduce a phase term.

The coherence function of Mita & Luco [bib5, bib6] is written:

$$
\gamma (d,f)=\exp\left[-{\left(\frac{\alpha \omega d}{{v}_{s}}\right)}^{2}\right]
$$

In this expression, ${v}_{s}$ is the propagation velocity of the SH wave (typically $200-\mathrm{1000 }m/s$ ) and the parameter $\alpha$ may vary from $0,1$ to $0,5$ depending on the cases but is generally taken equal to $0,5$ . If one chooses $\alpha =0.0$ , then one carries out an analysis without spatial variability.

One sees that the correlation length for the coherence function of Mita and Luco [bib5, bib6] is characterised by the expression $(\omega \alpha /{v}_{s}{)}^{-1}$ .

 Avec les valeurs suivantes des paramètres pour le mouvement horizontal:

$$

#### 2.2.2 Modelling of the seismic forces with Code_Aster

  - **Case of a rigid foundation**

The interface modes are the six rigid-body modes. The modal seismic force computed by MISS3D is then written, for a unit excitation (white noise):

$$
{f}_{s}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){x}_{0}
$$

Where ${K}_{s}(\omega )$ is the modal impedance matrix and ${x}_{0}=(1.,0.,0.,0.,0.,0.)$ for a seismic excitation in the direction $x$ , ${x}_{0}=(0.,1.,0.,0.,0.,0.)$ for a seismic excitation in $y$ and ${x}_{0}=(0.,0.,1.0.,0.,0.,0.)$ for a vertical earthquake. The seismic force is non-zero only for the modal component in the direction of the earthquake $(x,y,z)$ . Likewise, the coherence function is constructed only for the translational degrees of freedom in the direction of the earthquake. The other degrees of freedom are not affected.

In order to take the spatial variability into account, one determines the modal participation for each POD mode characterising the spatial variability:

$$
{f}_{s}^{k}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){\Theta}^{T}{s}_{u}^{k}
$$

Where $\Theta$ is the matrix containing the (mechanical) interface modes.

  - **Case of a flexible foundation**

The interface modes are the $d\times 6$ unit modes relating to the $d$ nodes of the interface. The modal seismic force computed by MISS3D is then written:

$$
{f}_{s}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){x}_{0}
$$

${x}_{0}$ is the modal participation vector comprising $1$ for the degrees of freedom relating to the direction of the earthquake and zeros for the other directions. Likewise, the seismic force and the coherence function are non-zero only for the degrees of freedom in the direction of the earthquake. One constructs the coherence matrix and then the vectors ${s}_{u}^{k}$ for the degrees of freedom in the direction of the earthquake:

$$
{f}_{s}^{k}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){s}_{u}^{k}
$$

  - **Case of an “arbitrary” foundation**

In these cases, corresponding either to an embedded foundation, or to a case of soil-fluid-structure interaction, or to a case in which the interface modes are arbitrary modes different from unit modes relating to the nodes of the interface, the modal participation vector ${x}_{0}$ is no longer itself unitary, nor independent of the frequency, and one no longer has identity between the physical and modal coordinates of the interface.

The modal seismic force computed by MISS3D is then written:

$$
{f}_{s}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){x}_{0}(\omega )
$$

${x}_{0}(\omega )$ the modal participation vector is therefore obtained by inversion of the seismic force ${f}_{s}(\omega )$ with respect to the soil impedance ${K}_{s}(\omega )$ ; the corresponding physical vector on the interface ${X}_{0}(\omega )$ is then written: ${X}_{0}(\omega )=\Theta {x}_{0}(\omega )$ where $\Theta$ is the matrix containing the (mechanical) interface modes.

In order to take the spatial variability into account, one determines the physical contribution on the interface for each POD mode characterising the spatial variability:

$$
{X}_{u}^{k}(\omega )={X}_{0}(\omega ){s}_{u}^{k}
$$

The physical vector on the interface ${X}_{u}^{k}(\omega )$ corresponds to a modal participation vector ${S}_{u}^{k}(\omega )$ such that: ${X}_{u}^{k}(\omega )=\Theta {S}_{u}^{k}(\omega )$ where $\Theta$ is the matrix containing the (mechanical) interface modes. The corresponding seismic force will then be expressed as follows:

$$
{f}_{s}^{k}(\omega )={K}_{s}(\omega ){S}_{u}^{k}(\omega )
$$

#### 2.2.3 Computation of transfer functions and of the response PSD

By linear filtering, one obtains:

$$
{s}_{q}^{k}(\omega )=H(\omega ){s}_{f}^{k}(\omega )
$$

Which makes it possible to reconstruct the response spectral-density matrix as:

$$
{S}_{q}(\omega )=\sum_{k=1}^{K\le m}{s}_{q}^{k}({s}_{q}^{k})\text{*}
$$

The model is reduced if one can truncate expression () to $K\le m$ POD modes.

For an excitation by white noise, one obtains directly the PSD of the transfer function on output.

**Note**:

*Without spatial variability the seismic field and thus the seismic force are the same for the whole set of nodes of the interface (the foundation):*

${S}_{u}(x,x\text{',}\omega )={S}_{0}(\omega )$

**Warning:**  In the present state, DYNA_ISS_VARI can treat only the case of a shallow foundation. As for the interface modes, one may choose between a modelling by the 6 rigid-body modes (rigid foundation), arbitrary interface modes (flexible foundation) and a modelling by all the FE modes (flexible foundation).

#### 2.2.4 Computation of the time response to an earthquake

In general, it is possible to simulate trajectories of a stationary Gaussian process whose PSD one knows by using the spectral representation theorem.

One could therefore obtain a realisation of the structural time response (stochastic process characterised by its spectral density ${S}_{q}(\omega )$ ) by the formula (cf. [bib4]):

$$
q(t)=\sum_{k}\sum_{n=0}^{{N}_{T}}H({\omega}_{n})G({\omega}_{n}){e}^{i{\omega}_{n}t}{\varphi}_{k}({\omega}_{n})\sqrt{{\lambda}_{k}({\omega}_{n})}\sqrt{{S}_{0}({\omega}_{n})}{\xi}_{n}^{k}\sqrt{\Delta \omega }
$$

where the ${\xi}_{n}^{k}$ are independent complex random variables of reduced centred normal law and where $\Delta \omega$ denotes the (constant) frequency step, ${\omega}_{n},n=1,{N}_{T}$ are the frequencies coming from the discretisation. Under the hypothesis that one can approximate the point spectral density with the aid of a number of accelerograms, one has:

$$
{S}_{0}^{L}(\omega )=\frac{1}{2\pi }\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}{u}_{0}^{l}(\omega ).{u}_{0}^{l}\ast (\omega )
$$

Where ${u}_{0}(\omega )$ is obtained from the free-field accelerogram $u(t)$ :

$$
{u}_{0}(\omega )=\frac{1}{\sqrt{T}}\underset{0}{\overset{T}{\int}}{u}_{0}(t){e}^{-i\omega t}\text{dt}
$$

Here, one does not use this method of generating signals from their PSD, because one is working with non-stationary processes of which one knows a realisation, namely the accelerogram at the input of the mechanical analysis. Given this context, one places oneself rather in a deterministic framework of signal filtering. Thus, one introduces a deterministic filter modelling the effect of the spatial incoherence. This filter is given by the matrix $\Phi (\omega )\Lambda (\omega {)}^{1/2}$ . One can thus compute the frequency response and obtain the time response by inverse FFT. The frequency response is written:

$$
q(\omega )=\sum_{k}H(\omega )G(\omega ){\phi }_{k}(\omega \sqrt{{\lambda}_{k}(\omega )}{u}_{0}(\omega )
$$

Considering equation (), one can check that the spectral density of the response $q$ is that given by equation (). The model is “reduced” if one can truncate expression () to $K\le m$ POD modes.

### 2.3 Results computed by DYNA_ISS_VARI

In summary, the operator DYNA_ISS_VARI makes it possible to obtain the following results:

- Computation of the spectral density of the modal response for a unit seismic excitation (in displacement). In order to obtain the response to a seismic excitation, one multiplies the PSD obtained for a unit excitation by the spectrum modelling the excitation (Kanai–Tajimi or other).
- Computation of the transfer functions for a unit seismic excitation (in displacement). The transfer functions are given by the square root of the modulus of the response auto-spectra, $\sqrt{\mid {\left[{S}_{v}(\omega )\right]}_{ii}\mid }$ (square root of the absolute value of the auto-spectrum [bib9] in physical coordinates). By comparing the transfer functions with and without spatial variability, one can determine margins linked to the incoherence of the seismic signal (cf. also [bib8]: “incoherence” margin factor in the seismic PSAs).
- Computation of the floor spectra by way of the transient response (FONC_SIGNAL must be supplied). DYNA_ISS_VARI makes it possible to compute the transient response in acceleration. The oscillator response spectra (SRO) for a floor may be obtained in post-processing.

## 3 Other software: SASSI and CLASSI

The two tools most used to carry out SSI analyses with spatial variability for the nuclear field are the software packages CLASSI and SASSI. The method implemented in *Code_Aster*  is close to that of SASSI [bib8, bib9].

Just as DYNA_ISS_VARI in *Code_Aster* , SASSI makes it possible on the one hand to determine transfer functions (unit excitation) and on the other hand to compute time responses taking the spatial variability into account.

The computation of the transfer functions is carried out according to the same principle in the two codes (*Code_Aster*  and SASSI): one carries out a spectral decomposition (POD) of the coherence matrix, one determines the response POD mode by POD mode in order to recompose from it the response PSD as described in this note.

For the computation of time responses, there is a difference between the SASSI methodology and the implementation in Code_Aster. More precisely, SASSI introduces a random phase for each POD mode [bib9]:

$$
q(\omega )=\sum_{k}H(\omega )G(\omega ){\phi }_{k}(\omega )\sqrt{{\lambda}_{k}(\omega )}{e}^{i{\eta}^{k}(\omega )}{u}_{0}(\omega )
$$

Where the ${\eta}^{k}(\omega )$ are uniform random variables on the interval $[-\pi ,\pi ]$ . One then determines $m$ responses and $m$ SRO in order to retain the mean. However, the introduction of the random phase is not useful if one considers the problem as a (deterministic) filtering problem of a deterministic signal (the accelerogram) as described above. One can furthermore check that the expectation of expression () is equal to expression ().

The method used by CLASSI is different, inasmuch as one remains within the framework of classical stochastic dynamics: one solves a linear filtering problem (as is the case for DYNA_ISS_VARI when no seismic signal is given). The PSD of the seismic excitation is approximated directly with the aid of an algorithm making the equivalence between the PSD and the SRO. The response SRO is obtained in the same manner from the response PSD, namely by determining the SRO corresponding to the computed response PSD [bib9].
