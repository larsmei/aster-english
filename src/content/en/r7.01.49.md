# r7.01.49 Finite element for soil–pile interaction (3D_INTERF_POU)

**Summary:**

This document presents a finite element for interaction between a matrix and a reinforcement. The corresponding keyword for the modelling is `3D_INTERF_POU`.

## 1 Introduction

This model describes the interaction between a reinforcement and its surrounding matrix. Their kinematics are represented respectively by those of a two-node Timoshenko beam and of an eight-node hexahedral 3D element. The interaction domain considered (the interface) is a line coincident with the domain of the beam. The element `3D_INTERF_POU` integrates there, at its Gauss points, a phenomenological law [R7.01.50](doc:r7.01.50) returning the interaction forces between the matrix and the reinforcement as a function of their relative displacement, carried by three components.

First, a general formulation is presented for the description of the interaction between a reinforcement and its matrix. Second, the formulation of the element `3D_INTERF_POU` is detailed.

## 2 General formulation of a mixed matrix–reinforcement finite element

### 2.1 Formulation of the continuous problem

Under certain conditions, a reinforced soil may be represented by a homogenised medium. The classical technique consists in defining a medium with anisotropic properties. The multiphase approach emerges as an alternative. The anisotropic medium is replaced by two superimposed phases in interaction. At each point of the medium, a matrix particle and a reinforcement particle are superimposed. Each particle has its own kinematics. The method extends to an arbitrary number of reinforcement phases, each describing a reinforcement direction.

Let $\dispVirt$ be an arbitrary virtual displacement field. It is composed of the kinematics of the matrix $\dispVirt_s$ and of those of the reinforcement $\dispVirt_p$. The internal work $\work{int}$ of the two-phase system comprises three contributions: those of the matrix $\work{s}$, of the reinforcement $\work{p}$ and of the interaction forces $\work{f}$:

$$
\forall \dispVirt \, , \quad \work{int} = \work{s} + \work{p} + \work{f}
$$

From the point of view of a single phase, the interaction forces may be regarded as external.

## 3 Formulation of the element 3D_INTERF_POU

In the case of the interaction element `3D_INTERF_POU`, the discretisation adopted (and the associated kinematics) for the reinforcement is a two-node Timoshenko beam, whereas the discretisation adopted for the matrix is an eight-node hexahedral 3D element (hence no additional kinematic assumption relative to the usual 3D case). The corresponding modellings for the "independent" elements are respectively the modelling `POU_D_T` and the modelling `3D`.

## 4 Conclusion

A finite-element model of the interaction between matrix and reinforcement is proposed. The kinematics of the elements used together with the interaction element to represent the behaviours specific to the reinforcement and to the matrix must correspond to the kinematics adopted in the interaction element. The element carries unknowns corresponding to the degrees of freedom of the reinforcement (including rotations) and of the matrix.
