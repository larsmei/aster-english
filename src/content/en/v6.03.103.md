# v6.03.103 SSNP103 - Calculation of the nonlinear energy yield rate

**Summary:**

This test is a test case in nonlinear fracture mechanics.

A finished rectangular plate is considered to be cracked and subjected to traction loading. The constitutive law used is a nonlinear elastic law of the Henky von Mises type.

This test case includes four modellings in $\mathrm{2D}$:

- Model A, in flat deformations, deals with a crack pressed in pure mode 1. Only half of the plate is modelled by symmetry.
- Model B, in flat deformations, deals with a crack pressed in mixed modes. The complete plate is modelled.
- Modelling C takes over the geometry of Modelling A but solves the associated axisymmetric problem. It is therefore not a cracked plate here but a cracked cylinder.
- Modelling D reproduces the calculation of Modelling B by adding a thermal load.
- Model E reproduces the calculation of model B with linear HHO.
- Modelling F, reproduces the calculation of Modelling B with quadratic HHO.

## 2 reference solution

In this test we manufacture the reference solution by making an estimate of the energy return rate in two ways.

The first solution is obtained by the classical G-theta method (operator CALC_G), i.e. by estimating the superfacial integral on a crown around the crack.

In order to find this same value by another means, we note first that in the case of l-linear elasticity, $G$ can be estimated using the d-Irwin formula linking the global quantities to the local quantities of stress factors ${K}_{I};{K}_{\mathit{II}}$:

$$
G=\frac{1-{\nu}^{2}}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})
$$

Et:

$$
G=\frac{({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})}{E}
$$

The stress intensity factors can be estimated by the analysis of the singularity of the stress fields or of the displacement close to the peak. In the case of hyper-elasticity, the energy restitution rate is still an invariant of the integration contour, which allows us to imagine a similar procedure.

First we can theoretically contract the outline of integration to the crack tip indefinitely while keeping the same $G$ value. So even for a hyper-elastic law we can establish a link between the overall $G$ and local $K$ quantities. However, the generalization of the concept of stress factor is not always easy for all types of hyper-elastic laws. On the other hand, for a bilinear hyper-elastic law this link can be established without too much difficulty. For this law, near the tip, the behavior will be linear and this regardless of the external loading applied. So we will find the singularity of stress and classical deformation in the form of $\sqrt{r}$.

In this test case we therefore use the Hencky type elastic nonlinear law, which has the advantage of replicating the plasticity law of von Mises for any monotonous radial loading:

$$
\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})=K\mathit{Tr}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})\underline{\underline{I}}+2\mu ({\epsilon}^{\mathit{eq}})\underline{\underline{{\epsilon}^{D}}}
$$

where $3K=E/(1-2\nu )$ is the compressibility module and $\mu ({\epsilon}^{\mathit{eq}})$ is a Lamé coefficient, which is assumed to depend exclusively on the equivalent deformation of von Mises: ${\epsilon}^{\mathit{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\cdot {\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{D}:{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{D}}$.

In the general form of Hencky's nonlinear law, the dependence of the coefficient of Lamé $\mu ({\epsilon}^{\mathit{eq}})$ on deformation is identified from the uniaxial tensile curve. For the bilinear case the law ss written in the initial d

$$
\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})=K\mathit{Tr}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})\underline{\underline{I}}+2\mu \underline{\underline{{\epsilon}^{D}}}
$$

And in the field of nonlinear elasticity:

$$
\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})=K\mathit{Tr}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})\underline{\underline{I}}+{\sigma}_{c}(1-{\mu}_{\infty}/\mu )\underline{\underline{{\epsilon}^{D}}}/\sqrt{{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{D}:{\underline{\underline{\epsilon}}}^{D}}+2{\mu}_{\infty}\underline{\underline{{\epsilon}^{D}}}
$$

with $3K=E/(1-2\nu )$ and $2\mu =E/(1+\nu )$ .

The coefficient of Lamé $2{\mu}_{\infty}$ for loads exceeding the threshold d的linear elasticity is related to the second slope of the law, which is noted ${E}_{\infty}$ (keyword D_SIGM_EPSI):

$$
2{\mu}_{\infty}=6{E}_{\infty}K/(9K+{E}_{\infty})
$$

Finally, for ${\epsilon}^{\mathit{eq}}\gg \frac{{\sigma}_{c}}{2\mu }$ important loads, this law becomes asymptotically linear with the following linear characteristics:

$$
\underline{\underline{\sigma}}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})=\frac{{E}_{\infty}}{3(1-2{\nu}_{\infty})}\mathit{Tr}(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})\underline{\underline{I}}+\frac{{E}_{\infty}}{1+{\nu}_{\infty}}\underline{\underline{{\epsilon}^{D}}}+o(\underline{\underline{\epsilon}})
$$

With $E\to {E}_{\infty}$ and $2{\nu}_{\infty}=1-(1-2\nu )\cdot {E}_{\infty}/E$ .

Analysis of the singularity of the shift jump with these new values of coefficients of elasticity with "infinite" loads allows us to identify the values of stress intensity factors thanks to the operator already available in code_aster: POST_K1_K2_K3, then use the classic Irwin formula:

$$
G=\frac{1-{\nu}_{\infty}^{2}}{{E}_{\infty}}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathit{II}}^{2})
$$

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.103/10000200000002BD00000144FB1373D443B21CE3.png)

Two tests are carried out on calculated energy return rates.

First test:

The ratio between the G from CALC_G and the G from POST_K1_K2_K3 is tested directly. This ratio must be equal to 1.

This is a reference to the ANALYTIC type.

Second test:

The test case was executed, for each model, on a mesh ten times finer at the bottom of the crack and then on a mesh one hundred times finer (size of mesh cells connected to the bottom of the cracks of ${10}^{-4}\mathit{mm}$ and ${10}^{-5}\mathit{mm}$ respectively), with a load covering a greater range than that of the test case.

At each moment of calculation, $G$ values from method 1 and 3 of POST_K1_K2_K3 calculation (see U4.82.05 documentation) were extracted.

Comparison of the quantities calculated for each mesh shows the convergence (as defined by mesh) of these.

The reference solution selected is the $G$ value obtained with the 3 method with the finest mesh (size of mesh cells connected to the ${10}^{-5}\mathit{mm}$ crack bottom). the difference relative between the $G$ value from methods 1 and 3 gives an estimate of the accuracy of this reference value.

This is a reference of type AUTRE_ASTER.

## 9 Summary of the results

This test case is designed to validate the calculation of the nonlinear energy yield rate:

    - Modelings A and B validate the G calculation in flat deformations, pure mode 1 and mixed mode
    - Modelling C validates the calculation of G in axisymmetric, in pure mode 1.
    - Modelling D validates the calculation of G following a thermo-mechanical calculation.
