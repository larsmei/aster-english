# v6.02.114 SSNL114 - Weighing cable with thermal expansion

**Summary:**

This test validates the calculation of the gravity cables, with or without thermal expansion.

- Static analysis
- Elastic behaviour
- Large movements
- 2 modellings: CABLE et POU_D_T_GD

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A $2{l}_{0}$ cable in the $x$ direction is subjected to its own weight (gravity in the $-Z$ direction). It is clamped at the $O$ and $B$ ends, which are themselves distant from $\mathrm{2L}$.

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.114/1000000000000192000000759F942EEEF5F0D230.png)

Initially, $2{l}_{0}=2L=\mathrm{325m}$

The area of the cable section is: $2.2783E-04{m}^{2}$

### 1.2 Material properties

$E=5.70E+10\mathrm{Pa}$

$\nu =0.3$ (modelization B only)

$\mathrm{ALPHA}:2.3E-5{K}^{-1}$

$\mathrm{RHO}:2.844230E+03\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

clamped support en $O$ et $B$

gravity: $(9.81,0.0,0.0,-1.0)$

The temperature in the cable varies with time, the reference temperature is $0.°C$ .

- Instant: 0. Temperature $T=0.°C$
- Instant: 1.Temperature $T=39.26°C$

The following are therefore dealt with:

- at instant 0, a cable subject to its own weight only
- at moment 1, a heavy cable subjected to thermal expansion.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

analytical solution :

For an extendable (elastic) cable, subject to its own weight, the displacement is equal to:

$x(s)=a\mathit{Argsh}\left(\frac{s}{a}\right)+\frac{\rho g}{E}as$

$z(x)=a\sqrt{1+\frac{{s}^{2}}{{a}^{2}}}+\frac{\rho g}{E}\frac{{s}^{2}}{2}-a\sqrt{1+\frac{{l}_{0}^{2}}{{a}^{2}}}-\frac{\rho g}{E}\frac{{l}_{0}^{2}}{2}$

$a$ solution of equation $L=a\mathrm{Argsh}(\frac{{l}_{0}}{a})+\frac{\rho g}{E}a{l}_{0}=f(a)$

With $s$ abscisse curvilinear, $s\in [-{l}_{o},{l}_{o}]$ . Here we are interested in the deflection in the center (point $C$ ) :

$z(C)=a-a\sqrt{1+\frac{{l}_{0}^{2}}{{a}^{2}}}-\frac{\rho g}{E}\frac{{l}_{0}^{2}}{2}$

$a$ solution of equation $L=a\mathrm{Argsh}(\frac{{l}_{0}}{a})+\frac{\rho g}{E}a{l}_{0}=f(a)$

The only difficulty in calculating this solution is the resolution of the $L=f(a)$ equation. This resolution was numerically made (fortran program using the routine search for zero d-Aster ZEROFO).

**Note:**

*In the case of thermal expansion, the solution is the same as before, considering that the initial length* $2{l}_{0}$ * is equal to its initial length* $\mathrm{2L}$ *increased linear expansion:* ${l}_{0}=L(1+\alpha T)$

### 2.2 Baseline

Moving $Z$ to point $C$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

Semi-analytical solution: The numerical resolution of the $L=f(a)$ equation gives a value to the ${10}^{-3}$.

## 5 Summary of the results

The results show that the solution of the problem of the heavy cable with good precision for the elements cable ($\text{0.02\%}$ ) and acceptable accuracy for the elements POU_D_T_GD ($\text{0.4\%}$ ) can be obtained.

This mechanical problem is difficult for the resolution algorithm, because the solution can only be obtained with the hypothesis of large displacements. Convergence can only be achieved with the geometric rigidity matrix.
