# v3.03.118 SSLS118 - Square plate placed under sinusoidal pressure

**Summary:**

The case of a square monolayer plate and then multilayer, simply supported and subjected to sinusoidal pressure, is treated.

The displacement at the centre, $\sigma_{xx}, \sigma_{yy}, \sigma_{xy}, \sigma_{xz}, \sigma_{yz}$ stresses at the lower middle and upper interfaces, $N_{xx}, N_{yy}, N_{xy}$ membrane forces, shear forces $T_{x}, T_{y}$ and $M_{xx}, M_{yy}, M_{xy}$ moments are calculated.

The test consists of 14 modellings: for modellings A to F, the results obtained are compared for modellings DKQ, DSQ, DKT, DST, COQUE_3D with triangular mesh cells and COQUE_3D with rectangular mesh cells.

The modellings G and H test results in a different user benchmark from the global benchmark.

Modellings I and J measure the sensitivity of the results to the fineness of the mesh, for the DSQ configuration.

The modellings K to N are for the multilayer plate, for the modellings DST and DSQ, as a global and user benchmark, and allow the distribution of flat stresses and transverse shear within the plate to be estimated.

Modellings O to R validates the element Q4G.

Modellings S to T validates Q4GG Modelling.

The W Modelling validates the element T3G.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The analytical solution reference is based on Love-Kirchhoff theory, commonly used for so-called "thin" bib1 plates.

Given the problem and in all point plate, we have for the calculation of the deflection:

$w=\frac{{f}_{0}{a}^{4}}{4{\pi}^{4}D}\sin\pi \frac{x}{a}\sin\pi \frac{y}{a}$

with:

$D=\frac{E{h}^{3}}{12(1-{\nu}^{2})}$ , ${f}_{0}=1$ , $a=1$ et $\nu =0.25$

For the calculation of moments, the theory leads to the following expressions:

$\begin{array}{ccc}{M}_{xx}& =& \alpha (1+\nu )\sin\pi \frac{x}{a}\sin\pi \frac{y}{a}\\ {M}_{yy}& =& {M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{xy}& =& -\alpha (1-\nu )\cos\pi \frac{x}{a}\cos\pi \frac{y}{a}\end{array}$

For the calculation of curvature, the theory leads to the following expressions:

${\kappa}_{xx}=-\frac{{f}_{0}{a}^{2}}{4{\pi}^{2}D}\sin(\pi \frac{x}{a})\sin(\pi \frac{y}{a})$

${\kappa}_{yy}=-\frac{{f}_{0}{a}^{2}}{4{\pi}^{2}D}\sin(\pi \frac{x}{a})\sin(\pi \frac{y}{a})$

${\kappa}_{xy}=\frac{{f}_{0}{a}^{2}}{4{\pi}^{2}D}\cos(\pi \frac{x}{a})\cos(\pi \frac{y}{a})$

with $\alpha =\frac{{f}_{0}{a}^{2}}{4{\pi}^{2}}$

For shear forces, we get:

$\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}=\frac{{f}_{0}a}{2\pi }\cos\pi \frac{x}{a}\sin\pi \frac{y}{a}\\ {T}_{y}=\frac{{f}_{0}a}{2\pi }\sin\pi \frac{x}{a}\cos\pi \frac{y}{a}\end{array}$

For a uniform plate, the plane stresses are given by:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{xx}\\ {\sigma}_{yy}\\ {\sigma}_{xy}\end{array}\right)=z[A]\left(\begin{array}{c}{M}_{xx}\\ {M}_{yy}\\ {M}_{xy}\end{array}\right)$

with $[A]=\frac{12}{{h}^{3}}[I]$ and $z$ position in plate thickness

and transverse shear stresses by:

$\left(\begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{x}\\ {\sigma}_{y}\end{array}\right)=[{D}_{1}(z)]\left(\begin{array}{c}{T}_{x}\\ {T}_{y}\end{array}\right)$ ,

with $[{D}_{1}(z)]=\frac{6}{{h}^{3}}\left({\left(\frac{h}{2}\right)}^{2}-{z}^{2}\right)$

For the strain energy in bending, we obtain:

${E}_{\mathit{flexion}}=\frac{1}{2}{\int}_{S}[({M}_{xx}.{\kappa}_{xx}+{M}_{yy}.{\kappa}_{yy}+{M}_{xy}.{\kappa}_{xy})]\mathit{dS}$

## 26 Summary of the results

The results of the survey show that:

- DSQ Modelling provides a more accurate estimate of stresses and shear forces (<5%) than DKQ Modelling (=20%). The moment estimate is equivalent for both modellings,
- mesh Modelling is more accurate than Q4G Modelling (compared to modellings B and O). Both models converge to the same results (compared to modellings J and P). This is explained by the order of rotation approximations that is quadratic for DSQ and bilinear for Q4G,
- The stress estimate is more accurate with DKT modelling (<2%) compared to DST modelling (<3.5%). However, L-shear force is better estimated by DST (<10% for DST and <20% for DKT),
- for COQUE_3D triangle or quadrangle configurations, the estimate of the transverse shear stress is constant in plate thickness, according to the Modelling assumptions,
- the results expressed in the user mark for the DST and COQUE_3D configurations are identical to those expressed in the global mark,
- mesh refinement for DSQ configuration improves the estimation of stresses, shear forces and moments; the reverse trend in displacement,
- The multilayer configuration allows the visualization of the distribution of flat and transverse shear stresses in the plate thickness, and confirms the theory, namely a linear distribution for flat and parabolic stresses for shear stresses; on the other hand, the rotation of the reference does not affect the values of the stresses,
- In general, at nodes where the expected stresses or analytically zero forces are expected, the numerical results obtained are not correct because the estimates are made mesh cell by mesh cell and then extrapolated to nodes. Even the averaged values for the nodes in question are not necessarily zero.
