# v9.01.106 SZLZ106 - Fatigue under random solicitation

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to calculate damage from random solicitation, which is characterised by its spectral moments.

From the spectral moments of random loading the average damage to the [R7.04.02](doc:r7.04.02) structure is determined.

Two methods of counting stress cycles are available for this purpose:

- method of counting peaks of stress,
- method of exceedances of a given level.

Various possibilities of introducing the Wöhler curve and taking into account the $\mathrm{Ke}$ elasto-plastic concentration coefficient are also tested.

The reference results of this test are the values provided by POSTDAM software developed by the REME Department (EDF-DER-EP).

The results provided by the POST_FATI_ALEA operator are quite identical to those provided by POSTDAM software.

## 1 Reference problem

The analysis is to determine the average damage to a piece subjected to random loading.

Random loading is fully characterized by the spectral moments of 0, 2, and 4: ${\lambda}_{0}$ , ${\lambda}_{2}$ and ${\lambda}_{4}$ that are introduced under the keywords MOMENT_SPEC_0, MOMENT_SPEC_2 and MOMENT_SPEC_4.

For the calculation of damage, a counting method must be chosen from the two available in *Code_Aster*:

- method of counting peaks of stress,
- method of counting the exceedance of a given level.

The Wöhler curve of the material must also be introduced, which can be defined in three distinct mathematical forms:

- point function by point, which gives the value of the number of cycles at break, depending on the ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}$ alternating stress,
- Basquin analytical form: $D=A{S}_{\mathrm{alt}}^{\beta}$
- analytical form

${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}$ = alternating stress = $1/2({E}_{C}/E)\Delta \sigma$

$X={\log}_{10}({S}_{\mathrm{alt}})$

$N={10}^{\mathrm{a0}+\mathrm{a1}X+\mathrm{a2}{X}^{2}+\mathrm{a3}{X}^{3}}$

| $D=$ | $1./N$ | si ${S}_{\mathrm{alt}}\ge {S}_{l}$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | $0.$ | if not |

where ${E}_{C}$ = Young's modulus associated with the material fatigue curve,

$E$ = Young's modulus used to determine stresses,

$\mathrm{a0}$ material constants , $\mathrm{a1}$ , $\mathrm{a2}$ and $\mathrm{a3}$ ,

and ${S}_{l}$ the endurance limit of the material.

In addition, a $\mathrm{Ke}$ elasto-plastic concentration coefficient may be used , defined by:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{K}_{e}=1& \mathrm{si}& \Delta \sigma <3{S}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1+(1-n)/(\Delta \sigma /3{S}_{m}-1)/(n(m-1))& \mathrm{si}& 3{S}_{m}<\Delta \sigma <3m{S}_{m}\\ {K}_{e}=1/n& \mathrm{si}& 3m{S}_{m}<\Delta \sigma \end{array}$

where ${S}_{m}$ is the maximum permissible stress,

and $n$ and $m$ two material-dependent constants.

In this test, for a single random load, the average damage is determined in ten separate configurations, depending on the shape of the Wöhler curve and the cycle counting method.
