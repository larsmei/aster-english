# v2.02.137 SDLL137 – Structural modification of a beam

**Summary**

This test case is intended to validate the procedure and the structural modification calculation from the measured information.

The structural modification method used is based on the joint operation of measured data and the numerical model of the modification to the initial structure, followed by the substructure technique for coupling of the two models.

For this test case, the measurement was numerically simulated and the reference results are obtained by direct calculation on the complete structure.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v2.02.137/10003F6A000069F000003B0403148B5657D952DA.svg)

In the above diagram, the measuring points on the initial structure are materialized by deflections, the tip of which indicates the sensitive direction of the sensor. These measuring points are located at $0.2m$, $0.4m$ , $0.6m$ and $0.8m$ apses according to $y$ and $z$ directions. The measured information is available only at these measuring points.

The initial structure is a rectangular cross section beam ($9\mathrm{mm}\times 38\mathrm{mm}$ ) of $0.9m$ length.

The modification is an additional beam, with rectangular cross section ($9\mathrm{mm}\times 38\mathrm{mm}$ ), which is applied between the coordinates $0.6m$ and $0.8m$ .

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus : $E=2.1{10}^{11}N/{m}^{2}$

Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.3$

Density: $\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The beam is clamped at one of its ends (the origin of the axes) and free at the other end.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Not applicable.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This involves linking an experimental model representing the actual structure with a numerical model of the modification that is desired to be made to the initial structure according to the technique proposed by Mr. Corus. [1] This coupling is done via a "support" model allowing the measurement to condense to the interfaces between the actual structure and the modification. The two models are then assembled using the substructure technique. A more detailed description of the modification procedure used is presented in [U2.07.03](doc:u2.07.03). The extension of this technique to dampening structures was studied by B. Groult [2].

The input data of the calculation are: the identified eigenmodes of the initial structure, the numerical model of the "support" and the numerical model of the "modification".

It is proposed that the changes in the first two eigenfrequencies of the clamped-free beam be calculated following the modification of a portion of the beam.

The chosen "support" model for this test case is a digital elements finite model of the clamped-free beam described in the previous paragraph. The first two eigenfrequencies of this beam are: $9.31\mathrm{Hz}$ and $39.32\mathrm{Hz}$.

Condensation of the information measured at the interfaces is obtained by expanding the measurement via a previously chosen projection base, defined on this "support" model. The quality of the result depends on the choice of this projection base.

The measurement was numerically simulated from a calculation from the support model.

The modification was numerically modelled by elements finished.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The first two frequencies of the modified structure are compared to the eigenfrequencies obtained by direct calculation on the complete model. The first two frequencies of the modified structure are: $7.78\mathrm{Hz}$ and $32.85\mathrm{Hz}$ .

The structural modification procedure is also checked for smoothness by comparing the field obtained with the interfaces of the modified structure in two different ways.

The first calculation corresponds to the field calculation at interfaces on the coupled model.

The second calculation corresponds to the field calculation at the interfaces by static field expansion obtained at the measuring points of the modified structure.

The difference between these two fields can be estimated by calculating the sum of the terms of the MAC matrix (Modal Assurance Criterion) between these two fields. A MAC matrix close to the identity matrix indicates that the two vectors are almost parallel. The IERI criterion (Energy Indicator of Regularity d-Interface) is also evaluated. This energy criterion tends to 0 if the two fields are very close.

### 2.3 Uncertainties

The reference solution on the eigenfrequencies of the modified structure is obtained by direct calculation on the modified structure.

We consider that the chosen discretization leads to very similar results to analytical solution.

## 3 modellings a à d

The first 4 modellings use strictly the structural modification method as proposed by Mr. Corus, i.e. the basic structure is described in modal form (a base of eigenmodes extended on a simplified numerical model) and the modification is described as a physical model. The elements calculations used here allow for the pairing of the two aspects.

For more details, please refer to U2.07.03 documentation (Section 3).

## 4 Modelling e

The Modelling e implements the same calculation in the form of a classical calculation by substructure. The main structure and the modification are described on a modal basis. The assembly of the two substructures is done classically with the DEFI_MODELE_GENE command.

For more details, please refer to documentation U2.07.03 (section 4).

### 4.1 Bibliographic reference

[1] Mr. Corus, Thesis ECP No. 2003-23, Improvement of methods of structural modification by using expansion and model reduction techniques.

[2] B. Groult, Thesis ECP No. 2008-14, Extension of a structural modification method for the design of dissipative devices incorporating viscoelastic materials.

## 5 Modelling A

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The "support" model and the modification were modelled by elements POU_D_E. The projection base chosen for the expansion of the measured information corresponds to the static deformities obtained by applying a load in each point of measurement according to the sensitive direction of the sensor. The "measurement" model was constructed from the first five modes identified in the initial structure.

## 9 Summary of the results

The results obtained depend on the measured information and the projection base chosen for the expansion of the measurement at the degrees of freedom interfaces. Here, the first five identified modes and the chosen expansion base were used as static responses. The results obtained are correct.

The uncertainty of the first two eigenfrequencies obtained by the structural modification technique used is less than $\text{1 \%}$ of the solution obtained by direct calculation.

The fields of displacement at the interfaces of the coupled model and the field at the interfaces obtained by static expansion of the fields at the measured points on the modified structure are very close. The associated MAC matrix is very close to the identity matrix. The IERI criterion is also close to 0 (modellings C and D).
