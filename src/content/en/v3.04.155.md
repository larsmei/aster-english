# v3.04.155 SSLV155 – Tensile lens crack

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the calculation of stress intensity factors (SIF s) along the bottom of a non-plane 3D crack in the linear elasticity.

This test involves a cube with a lens-shaped crack, subjected to hydrostatic tension.

This test contains 3 modellings:

- Modelling A: the crack is meshed to 2D-axis (FEM);
- Modelling B: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level sets (X-FEM) in 2D-axis;
- Modelization C: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level sets (X-FEM) in 3D;
- Modelling D: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level sets (X-FEM) in 2D-axi and a structured mesh is used;
- Modelling E: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level sets (X-FEM) in 2D-axis with quadratic elements;
- Modelling F: the crack is not meshed, it is represented by level sets (X-FEM) in 2D-axis with quadratic elements and a structured mesh is used;

There is no FEM 3D Modelling because creating the mesh is extremely difficult.

For each model, the SIF s are evaluated by POST_K1_K2_K3 and CALC_G (FEM)/CALC_G_XFEM (X-FEM) commands.

The numerical values are compared with the analytical values.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A $\mathrm{2L}$ side cube and a lens-shaped crack (Lens shaped crack) of $R$ radius such as $\frac{L}{R}=5$ and $\alpha =\frac{\pi}{4}$ centre angle (see ) are considered.

The characteristic equation of the crack surface shape is:

${x}^{2}+{y}^{2}+{(z-R)}^{2}={R}^{2}$ with $0\le z\le (1-\cos\alpha )R$ .

We're putting $a=R\sin\alpha$ on.

### 1.2 The characteristic equation of the crack bottom is:

${x}^{2}+{y}^{2}={a}^{2}$ with $z=(1-\cos\alpha )R$

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.155/1000000000000445000002EBAB849099C5E163DF.png)

Figure 1: géométrie du cube fissuré

### 1.3 Material properties

The material is elastic isotropic properties:

$E=210000\mathit{MPa}$

$\nu =0,22$

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

The cube is subjected to $\sigma$ hydrostatic tension.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

(1)
The reference solution for a lens-shaped crack of $R`dans un milieu infini, soumise à une pression uniforme :math:$sigma` radius, which is quite far from the crack, shows that stress intensity factors are constant along the crack bottom and are:
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

${K}_{I}=0,877(\frac{2}{\pi})\sigma \sqrt{\pi a}$

${K}_{\mathit{II}}=0,235(\frac{2}{\pi})\sigma \sqrt{\pi a}$

${K}_{\mathit{III}}=0$

with $a=R\sin\alpha$ .

### 2.2 Baseline

For the load considered $\sigma =1\mathit{MPa}$ and the following geometrical characteristics:

$L=10m$ , $R=2m$ , $a=\sqrt{2}$ , you will find:

${K}_{I}=1,177\mathit{MPa}.\sqrt{m}$

${K}_{\mathit{II}}=0,3153\mathit{MPa}.\sqrt{m}$

${K}_{\mathit{III}}=0$

### 2.3 Bibliographical references

1. J.P. Pereira, C.A. Duarte, D. Guoy and X. Jiao: hp-Generalized FEM and crack surface representation for non-planar 3-D cracks, Int. J. Numer. Engng, 77:601-633, 2009.

## 4 Modelling B: Modelling X-FEM 2D-axis

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the crack is not meshed (case X-FEM) and the structure is modeled in 2D-axisymetry.

The crack is represented by level sets:

$\mathit{lsn}=\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{(y-R)}^{2}}-R$

$\mathit{lst}=\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{(y-{y}_{h})}^{2}}-R'$

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.155/10000000000001FD000002410CB9473C7B5842B1.png)

Figure 4.1-1: level sets
with ${y}_{h}=R-\frac{R}{\cos(\alpha )}$ and $R'=R\tan(\alpha )$

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The original healthy mesh is relatively coarse: 252 nodes and 442 TRIA3. The size of the mesh cells is ${h}_{0}=1m$. A successive refinement procedure is used to achieve a target size corresponding to half the size of the mesh cells Modelling A, i.e. ${h}_{c}=0,0125m$. Indeed, the A Modelling uses quadratic elements, so finer linear elements is required to obtain equivalent accuracy. To achieve this, we call Homard iteratively. After refinement, the size of the mesh cells near the crack bottom is $h=0,0078125m$. All mesh cells are refined in a $5h$ ray disc around the crack bottom.

## 5 Modelling C: Modelling X-FEM 3D

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the crack is not meshed (case X-FEM) and the structure is modeled in 3D. Only a quarter of the structure is modeled, for symmetry reasons.

The crack is represented by level sets:

$\mathit{lsn}=\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{(y-R)}^{2}+{z}^{2}}-R$

$\mathit{lst}=\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{(y-{y}_{h})}^{2}+{z}^{2}}-R'$

with ${y}_{h}=R-\frac{R}{\cos(\alpha )}$ and $R'=R\tan(\alpha )$

**Note:**

*In 3*D *, there is an arbitrary choice of orientation of the local base in the crack bottom. Depending on the orientation of this base, the sign of*${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ *and*${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ *will be different (as linked to the base). But this does not influence the physical result (the possible bifurcation angle of the crack expressed in the global marker). Here, to find*${K}_{\mathit{II}}>0$ *as in the reference solution, you have to define the normal level set as the opposite of the formula above. In this Modelling, you will finally remember*$\mathit{lsn}=-\sqrt{{x}^{2}+{(y-R)}^{2}+{z}^{2}}+R$ *. This remains a convention and does not change the physical result!*

*In 2*D *, the sign of the normal level set does not affect the sign of*${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ *because there is no choice*x *to define the local base.*

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

The original healthy mesh is relatively coarse: 2508 nodes and 11945 TETRA4. The size of the mesh cells is ${h}_{0}=1m$. A successive refinement procedure is used to achieve a target size that is substantially the same as that of Modelling A, i.e. ${h}_{c}=0,025m$. For this, we call Homard iteratively. After refinement, the size of mesh cells near the crack bottom is $h=0,015625m$. All mesh cells are refined in a $5h$ ray disc around the crack bottom.

Number of nodes: 18166

Number of mesh cells and type: 103079 TETRA4

The characteristic length of a element near the crack bottom is 0.0156 m.

## 9 Summary of the results

This test case validates the calculation of stress intensity factors of a non-plane crack in 2D and 3D.

${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ results by POST_K1_K2_K3 are not good for E and F models and differ by 10 and 15% from the desired value for modellings A, C and D.
