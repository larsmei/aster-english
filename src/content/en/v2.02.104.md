# v2.02.104 SDLL104 - Primary and secondary structures subjected to random excitation

**Summary:**

A main beam of high rigidity and large mass (primary structure), clamped at its base, supports a light and flexible beam (secondary structure) at three points of effort transmission.

The primary structure is excited at its base by an acceleration given by its DSP.

The test compares a direct calculation of the two structures and a lineed calculation where the main beam response at restraint points is used as the secondary beam excitation.

The following features are tested:

- Analytical interspectral matrix predefined by the function of KANAI-TAJIMI,
- random dynamic response in absolute motion, in imposed acceleration,
- modal interspectral response,
- modal interspectral excitement.

This approach is representative of what is required for industrial studies: to determine the responses of different secondary structures knowing the response of the primary structure.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Movement is allowed in the $(\mathrm{DX},\mathrm{DY})$ plane.

The $\mathrm{PA}$ beam is clamped in the support.

The $\mathrm{PB}$ beam is connected to the $\mathrm{PA}$ beam by three points. In each, the $\mathrm{DX}$ and $\mathrm{DY}$ displacements of the node and $\mathrm{PB}$ node are identical. Rotations are not linked.

The interspectral matrix that transmits the displacements of the $\mathrm{PA}$ structure to the $\mathrm{PB}$ structure in the chained calculation is 6 dimension (6 degrees of freedom transmission).

## 4 Summary of the results

The results obtained confirm the hypothesis of equivalence between direct and chained calculations, on the one hand, and the correct consistency of the algorithm for calculating dynamic random response.

An difference is inevitable between the calculations of the two methods, but the one obtained at $25\mathrm{Hz}$ is nevertheless very high (11%).
