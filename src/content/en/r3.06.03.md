# r3.06.03 Computation of stresses at the nodes by local smoothing

**Summary:**

A local method is presented for computing stresses at the nodes from the stresses at the GAUSS points. It is used in the options SIGM_ELNO and SIEF_ELNO.

This method amounts to computing the stresses at the vertices of an element by multiplying the stresses at the GAUSS points by a smoothing matrix, constant for each element type.

For isoparametric elements of degree 2, the stresses at the mid-side nodes are obtained by averaging the values of the stresses at the 2 vertices of the edge.

This smoothing method has two advantages:

- the nodal stresses obtained have one order of accuracy more than by direct computation at the nodes,
- the method is inexpensive in CPU time.

This method has been generalised:

- to the computation of the strains (option EPSI_ELNO) and of the internal variables (option VARI_ELNO) at the nodes in mechanics,
- to the computation of the fluxes (option FLUX_ELNO) at the nodes in thermal analysis.

## 1 Preliminaries

This method is based on the observation that there exist points at which the computation of the stresses, from the displacements in a primal displacement formulation, is more accurate.

In the case of isoparametric finite elements of order 2 (SEG3 in 1D, QUAD8 and QUAD9 in 2D, HEXA20 in 3D), it is shown that the GAUSS points of the quadrature formula with ${2}^{n}$ points ($n$: dimension of the space) are such that one may hope, without this being formally demonstrated, for the computation of $\sigma$ the same order of accuracy as for the computation of the displacement field $u$.

The idea of the method is to compute, for each element, the stresses $\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$ at the nodes from ${\sigma}^{k}$ at the GAUSS points, the latter being computed on each element by the formula:

${\sigma}^{k}=D{B}^{k}u=D\sum_{i=1}^{\mathrm{NNO}}{B}_{i}^{k}{U}_{i}$

where:

$D$ is the elasticity matrix,

${B}^{k}$ is the matrix relating the strains to the displacements at the GAUSS point $k$,

${U}_{i}$ are the nodal displacements ($\mathrm{NNO}$ nodes)

## 2 Local least-squares minimisation method

In general, one wishes to approximate, in the least-squares sense, the spatial distribution of the stresses by $\sigma (x)$, a polynomial function:

$\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{\begin{array}{}i=0,\mathrm{...},p\end{array}}{a}_{i}{P}^{i}(x)$

The problem amounts to finding the coefficients ${a}_{i}$ which minimise the functional:

$\chi =\int\int{(\sigma -\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma})}^{2}\mathrm{dx}\mathrm{dy}$

The values of the function $\sigma$ are known here only at the Gauss points: ${\sigma}^{k}=\sigma ({x}_{k})$

The minimum will be reached if and only if:

$\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial {a}_{i}}=0\begin{array}{}\forall i=0,\mathrm{...},p\end{array}$

Within the framework of the displacement finite-element method, the following smoothing function is chosen:

$\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}(x)\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}$

where:

${N}_{i}$ is the shape function associated with node $i$ on the finite element considered,

$\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}$ is the value of the stress at node $i$ that is sought,

$n$ the number of nodes retained for the smoothing.

The following system must therefore be solved:

$$
\frac{\partial \chi }{\partial \stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}}=0\forall i=1,\mathrm{...},n                                      éq 2-1
$$

One may choose between two local smoothing methods: continuous smoothing or discrete smoothing.

## 3 Local smoothing methods (ref. [bib2]_ and [bib3]_)

### 3.1 Continuous local smoothing

This type of smoothing leads to solving the system [] with the functional defined on the current finite element:

$\chi =\underset{e}{\int}{(\sigma -\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma})}^{2}=\underset{e}{\int}{(\sigma -\sum_{i=1}^{n}{N}_{i}\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}})}^{2}$

The minimisation leads to ${M}^{e}\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}={F}^{e}$

with:

$\begin{array}{}{M}_{ij}^{e}=\underset{e}{\int}{N}_{i}{N}_{j}\mathrm{dx}\mathrm{dy}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}\stackrel{ˉ}{{N}_{i}}({\xi}_{k}){N}_{j}({\xi}_{k}){(\detJ)}_{k}{\omega}_{k}\\ {F}_{i}^{e}=\underset{e}{\int}{N}_{i}\sigma \mathrm{dx}\mathrm{dy}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}\stackrel{ˉ}{{N}_{i}}({\xi}_{k}){\sigma}_{k}{(\detJ)}_{k}{\omega}_{k}\end{array}$

| where | ${\xi}_{k}$ are the GAUSS points in the reference element |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${(\detJ)}_{k}$ | the Jacobian of the geometric transformation between the reference element and the current element at the point ${\xi}_{k}$. |

${\omega}_{k}$: the weight associated with the point ${\xi}_{k}$

${\xi}_{k}$: the stress at the point ${\xi}_{k}$

$\stackrel{ˉ}{{N}_{i}}({\xi}_{k})$: the value of the shape function in the reference element at the point ${\xi}_{k}$

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.03/10002A0C0000220100001442A9EBADD2D49A3A00.svg)

**Notes:**

- *If the interpolation spaces of* $\sigma$ *and of* $\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$ *are the same, one has* $\sigma =\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$ *. In practice, one retains for the space of* $\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$ *a space smaller than that in which* $\sigma$ *is defined by the finite element.*
- *One sees the link between the approximation at the GAUSS points of* $\sigma$ *, where* $\sigma$ *therefore converges better, and this smoothing procedure, whose justification is on the contrary continuous.*
- *The way in which* $\sigma$ *is computed at the GAUSS points does not come into play. The generalisation to non-linear problems is therefore obvious, although it cannot rest on the same justification.*

This method is nevertheless not retained, because it requires the solution of a linear system for each computation of $\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$.

### 3.2 Discrete local smoothing

In this case, the functional $\chi$ is replaced by the summation:

$\tilde{\chi }=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}{(\sigma ({\xi}_{k})-\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}({\xi}_{k}))}^{2}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}{(\sigma ({\xi}_{k})-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}{N}_{i}({\xi}_{k}))}^{2}$

![Figure](/figures/r3.06.03/10001432000027CA0000124B8440E2EE23094EA7.svg)

The system to be solved is again written: $\frac{\partial \tilde{\chi }}{\partial \stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}}=0$, that is:

$\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}\sum_{j=1}^{n}{N}_{i}({\xi}_{k}){N}_{j}({\xi}_{k})\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{j}}=\sum_{k=1}^{\mathrm{npg}}{N}_{i}({\xi}_{k})\sigma ({\xi}_{k})\forall i=1,\mathrm{...},n$

that is, in matrix form: $M\lbrace {\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}}_{\text{noeud}}\rbrace =P\lbrace {\sigma}_{\text{GAUSS}}\rbrace$

The matrices $M$ (square $n\times n$) and $P$ (rectangular $n\times \mathrm{npg}$) are then independent of the current element $e$.

They may therefore be computed once and for all on the reference element.

**Notes:**

- This method is more economical than the previous one and gives comparable results [bib2],
- *Here again, the way in which* ${\sigma}^{k}$ *is computed at each GAUSS point is immaterial (provided that the number of GAUSS points used for the computation of* $\sigma$ *and that of* $\stackrel{ˆ}{\sigma}$ *is the same). This method may therefore be used in the non-linear range*.

## 4 Application of the method to the computation of the stresses at the nodes for various elements

The local smoothing adopted in *code_aster* is the discrete local smoothing [r3.06.03-lissage-local-discret], which makes it possible to avoid the computation of integrals over the element.

On all the 2D and 3D continuum elements, a smoothing space based on the shape functions relating to the vertices of the element has been chosen.

The method therefore makes it possible to obtain the stresses at the vertices. In the case of elements of order 2, the stresses at the mid-side nodes are computed by taking the arithmetic mean of the two vertices "bracketing" the mid-side node considered.

The mapping matrices making it possible to compute the stresses at the vertex nodes from the stresses at the GAUSS points are given below. These matrices may be square or rectangular. Indeed, the mapping matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$ are computed once and for all at the initialisation of each finite-element type (in AFFE_MODELE). Two types of matrix exist:

- square matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$, which are to be used when the number of GAUSS points used for the computation of the stresses at the GAUSS points ${\sigma}^{k}$ is identical to the number of vertex nodes,
- rectangular matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$, which are to be used when the number of GAUSS points of ${\sigma}^{k}$ is different from (in general greater than) the number of vertex nodes.

### 4.1 Square mapping matrices

These matrices are used in the elements for which the number of GAUSS points of the computation of SIEF_ELGA/SIGM_ELGA is equal to the number of vertices. The option first computes the stresses at a number of GAUSS points equal to the number of vertices. The matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$ (given below) are then used to compute the stresses at the nodes. The elements concerned are:

- in 2D: QUAD4, TRIA6, under-integrated QUAD8,
- in 3D: TETRA4, PENTA6, HEXA8, PYRAM5 and under-integrated HEXA20.

#### 4.1.1 Square mapping matrices for the 2D elements

##### 4.1.1.1 Triangles

${M}^{\text{-1}}P=\frac{1}{3}\left[\begin{array}{ccc}5& -1& -1\\ -1& 5& -1\\ -1& -1& 5\end{array}\right]$

##### 4.1.1.2 Quadrilaterals

${M}^{\text{-1}}P=\left[\begin{array}{cccc}1+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}& 1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}\\ -\frac{1}{2}& 1+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}& 1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\\ 1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}& 1+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}\\ -\frac{1}{2}& 1-\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}& -\frac{1}{2}& 1+\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}\end{array}\right]$

#### 4.1.2 Square mapping matrices for the 3D elements

##### 4.1.2.1 Tetrahedra

$\begin{array}{}{M}^{\text{-1}}P=\frac{1}{a-b}\left[\begin{array}{cccc}a& a& a-1& a\\ a& a-1& a& a\\ a-1& a& a& a\\ a& a& a& a-1\end{array}\right]\\ \\ \text{avec}a=\frac{5-\sqrt{5}}{20}b=\frac{5+3\sqrt{5}}{20}\end{array}$

##### 4.1.2.2 Pentahedra

$\begin{array}{}{M}^{\text{-1}}P=\left[\begin{array}{cccccccc}\alpha & -\alpha & \alpha & & & 1-\alpha & \alpha -1& 1-\alpha \\ \alpha & \alpha & -\alpha & & & 1-\alpha & 1-\alpha & \alpha -1\\ -\alpha & \alpha & \alpha & & & \alpha -1& 1-\alpha & 1-\alpha \\ & & & & & & & \\ 1-\alpha & \alpha -1& 1-\alpha & & & \alpha & -\alpha & \alpha \\ 1-\alpha & 1-\alpha & \alpha -1& & & \alpha & \alpha & -\alpha \\ \alpha -1& 1-\alpha & 1-\alpha & & & -\alpha & \alpha & \alpha \end{array}\right]\\ \alpha =\frac{\sqrt{3}+1}{2}\end{array}$

##### 4.1.2.3 Hexahedra

${M}^{\text{-1}}P=\left[\begin{array}{cccccccc}a& b& b& c& b& c& c& d\\ b& c& c& d& a& b& b& c\\ c& d& b& c& b& c& a& b\\ b& c& a& b& c& d& b& c\\ b& a& c& b& c& b& d& c\\ c& b& d& c& b& a& c& b\\ d& c& c& b& c& b& b& a\\ c& b& b& a& d& c& c& b\end{array}\right]$ $\begin{array}{}a=\frac{5+3\sqrt{3}}{4}\\ b=\frac{1+\sqrt{3}}{4}\\ c=\frac{\sqrt{3}-1}{4}\\ d=\frac{5-3\sqrt{3}}{4}\end{array}$

### 4.2 Rectangular mapping matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$

In the non-linear range, for certain types of element (TRIA3, QUAD8 and QUAD9 in 2D, TETRA10, PENTA15 and HEXA20 in 3D), the stresses and the internal variables at the GAUSS points are computed on a richer family of GAUSS points (9 points for the quadrilaterals, 15 points for the tetrahedra, 21 points for the pentahedra, 27 points for the hexahedra).

The discrete local smoothing is then performed from these fields, and the transfer to the nodes involves matrices different from the previous ones. They are no longer square, being of dimension (number of vertices, number of GAUSS points). The mapping matrices ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$ are not computed explicitly; in particular $M$ is inverted by *Code_Aster*.

In the particular case of the 3-node triangle, the fields are assumed constant per element (a single GAUSS point) and:

${M}^{\text{-1}}P=\left[\begin{array}{}1\\ 1\\ 1\end{array}\right]$

For example, the computation performed by the option SIGM_ELNO is then the following:

If the stresses have been computed on a family possessing a number of GAUSS points greater than the number of vertices (for the elements indicated above), ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$ is then rectangular, and $\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\text{nb sommets}}\sum_{k=1}^{\text{nb pts Gauss}}{({M}^{\text{-1}}P)}_{\mathrm{ik}}{\sigma}^{k}$

Otherwise, if the number of GAUSS points is equal to the number of vertices, ${M}^{\text{-1}}P$ is then square. One computes $\stackrel{ˆ}{{\sigma}_{i}}={({M}^{\text{-1}}P)}_{\mathrm{ik}}{\sigma}^{k}$ [r3.06.03-matrices-passage-carrees].

## 5 Other computation options using the same method

The method described previously is used in *code_aster* to compute the strains, the internal variables and the fluxes at the nodes.

The fields produced are cham_elem at the nodes.

## 6 Other stress-smoothing methods

There exist two other smoothing methods, bearing only on the stresses, used by the Zhu–Zienkiewicz estimators, versions 1 and 2 [R4.10.01 §3].

The nodal stress fields produced are then cham_no.

The corresponding computation options are accessible through the command CALC_ERREUR [U4.81.06](doc:u4.81.06).
