# v6.04.247 SSNV247 - Application of a pressure distributed over the lips of a curved XFEM interface through a spherical cap

**Summary:**

This is a test to validate the contact facets of XFEM integration elements.

The contact facets used by default are not from the integration elements subs and do not give the possibility to have quadratic 3D contact facets. It is possible to enable the recovery of contact facets from the integration elements subs into the MODI_MODELE_XFEM operator with the DECOUPE_FACETTE='SOUS_ELEMENTS' key word. Linear and quadratic facets in 2D as well as in 3D have the advantage of being in line with the integration elements subs since they are chosen from the sides of the elements subs of the 2D integration and the faces of the elements subs of the 3D integration. This recovery mode also has the following advantage: the nodes coordinates of these facets are not recalculated as is the case by default, since these nodes are recovered during the cutting that is performed in TOPOSE.

In this test, the correct operation of the recovery of these facets is ensured when mechanical pressure is applied to the lips of an interface for a three-dimensional geometry. The results obtained are compared to an analytical solution. A iso-zero "lsn" curve representing the interface is chosen voluntarily to appreciate the performance gain brought by quadratic "facetization".

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is an analytical solution. Taking into account the boundary conditions, the displacements can be obtained from the analytical resolution of the motion quantity conservation equation.

By neglecting the gravity, the equation is written (in total constraints):

$\text{Div}(\sigma )=0$

The Poisson $\nu$ module being zero, and being elastic in the case, we have $\sigma =Eϵ$ .

The volume studied is spherical symmetry, made up of homogeneous and isotropic material; the boundary conditions also possess spherical symmetry. Therefore, a solution of the problem is sought in a system of $(r,\theta ,\varphi )$ spherical coordinates such that the fields of displacement, stress and deformation are respectively of the form:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{\text{r}}=h(r)\\ {u}_{\theta}={u}_{\varphi}=0\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{rr}}={f}_{1}(r)\\ \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\theta \theta }={\sigma}_{\varphi \varphi }={g}_{1}(r)\\ {\sigma}_{r\theta }={\sigma}_{r\varphi }={\sigma}_{\theta \varphi }=0\end{array}\end{array}$ $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\text{rr}}={f}_{2}(r)\\ \begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\theta \theta }={ϵ}_{\varphi \varphi }={g}_{2}(r)\\ {ϵ}_{r\theta }={ϵ}_{r\varphi }={ϵ}_{\theta \varphi }=0\end{array}\end{array}$

The $\text{Div}(\sigma )=0$ equilibrium equation is then reduced to: $\frac{d{\sigma}_{\text{rr}}}{\mathit{dr}}+\frac{2}{r}\ast ({\sigma}_{\text{rr}}-{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })=0$

The static boundary conditions are of the shape: ${\sigma}_{\text{rr}}(R)=-p$

The kinematic equations have the form: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{ϵ}_{\text{rr}}=\frac{{\mathit{du}}_{\text{r}}}{\mathit{dr}}\\ {ϵ}_{\theta \theta }=\frac{{u}_{\text{r}}}{r}\end{array}$ is finally: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\sigma}_{\text{rr}}=E\ast \frac{{\mathit{du}}_{\text{r}}}{\mathit{dr}}\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }=E\ast \frac{{u}_{\text{r}}}{r}\end{array}$

By replacing these two relationships in the equilibrium equation we obtain:

$\frac{\mathit{d²}{u}_{\text{r}}}{\mathit{dr²}}+\frac{2}{r}\ast (\frac{{\mathit{du}}_{\text{r}}}{\mathit{dr}}-\frac{{u}_{\text{r}}}{r})=0$ i.e. $\frac{d}{\mathit{dr}}(\frac{1}{\mathit{r²}}\frac{d({\mathit{r²u}}_{r})}{\mathit{dr}})=0$

The solution of this differential equation is: ${u}_{r}(r)={C}_{1}r+\frac{{C}_{2}}{\mathit{r²}}$ . The solution sought is discontinuous in $R$ , and this equation is solved separately on both $[{R}_{i},R]$ and $[R,{R}_{e}]$ domains .

Finally: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{u}_{\text{r}}(r)={C}_{1}r+\frac{{C}_{2}}{\mathit{r²}}\text{}\mathit{sur}[{R}_{i},R]\\ {u}_{r}(r)={C}_{3}r+\frac{{C}_{4}}{\mathit{r²}}\text{}\mathit{sur}[R,{R}_{e}]\end{array}$

Based on the kinematic boundary conditions, ${u}_{r}({R}_{i})={u}_{r}({R}_{e})=0$ so $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{C}_{1}{R}_{i}+\frac{{C}_{2}}{{R}_{i}\mathrm{²}}=0\\ {C}_{3}{R}_{e}+\frac{{C}_{4}}{{R}_{e}\mathrm{²}}=0\end{array}$

In addition ${\sigma}_{\text{rr}}=E\ast \frac{{\mathit{du}}_{\text{r}}}{\mathit{dr}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}E\ast {C}_{1}-2E\ast \frac{{C}_{2}}{\mathit{r³}}\text{}\mathit{sur}[{R}_{i},R]\\ E\ast {C}_{3}-2E\ast \frac{{C}_{4}}{\mathit{r³}}\text{}\mathit{sur}[R,{R}_{e}]\end{array}$ therefore according to the static boundary conditions: $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{C}_{1}-2\ast \frac{{C}_{2}}{\mathit{R³}}=-\frac{p}{E}\\ {C}_{3}-2\ast \frac{{C}_{4}}{\mathit{R³}}=-\frac{p}{E}\end{array}$ .

System resolution gives $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\begin{array}{c}{C}_{1}=\frac{-p}{E\ast (2\ast \frac{{R}_{i}\mathrm{³}}{R\mathrm{³}}+1)}\\ {C}_{2}=\frac{p}{E\ast (\frac{2}{\mathit{R³}}+\frac{1}{{R}_{i}\mathrm{³}})}\end{array}\\ \begin{array}{c}{C}_{3}=\frac{-p}{E\ast (2\ast \frac{{R}_{e}\mathrm{³}}{R\mathrm{³}}+1)}\\ {C}_{4}=\frac{p}{E\ast (\frac{2}{\mathit{R³}}+\frac{1}{{R}_{e}\mathrm{³}})}\end{array}\end{array}$

## 5 Conclusion

The 2 modellings validate the use of contact facets from elements integration XFEM for applying mechanical effort to the lips of a non-meshed 3D interface. They also illustrate the gain in precision achieved with quadratic elements compared to linear elements.
