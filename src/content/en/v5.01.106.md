# v5.01.106 SDND106 – frictional slider with static and dynamic friction coefficients

**Summary:**

This test case is designed to validate the functionality of non-linear friction by penalising, using two friction coefficients, a static (for adhesion phases) and a dynamic (for sliding phases).

The system studied contains two degrees of freedom. Its evolution contains a transition of adhesion – slip and a transition of slip – grip.

The reference solution is an analytical.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.106/Cadre1.gif)

**Figure** 1.1-a **: Schematic of test case.**

### 1.2 Material properties

The two masses are identical and are worth ${m}_{1}={m}_{2}=m=5\mathrm{kg}$ . The stiffness placed between these masses is $k={10}^{4}N/m$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

${m}_{1}$ and ${m}_{2}$ masses move according to $\overrightarrow{x}$ only.

The ${F}_{N}$ normal force module is constant equal to ${F}_{N}={10}^{4}N$.

The ${m}_{1}$ mass is affected by a contact condition – friction. The friction coefficients 5 are:

- in the static case: ${\mu}_{S}=0,3$ ,
- in the dynamic case: ${\mu}_{D}=0,2$ .

In this Modelling, the system is divided into three phases:

- A adhesion phase, during which the force exerted on the ${m}_{2}$ mass is constant: $F=3\cdot {10}^{3}N$ .
- A sliding phase, starting at ${t}_{1}$ , when the tangential force can no longer compensate for the pull force exerted by the spring on ${m}_{1}$ . After arbitrary time (${t}_{2}=0,2s$ ), the imposed force is set to 0 and the kinetics of the system then tend to 0.
- A second adhesion phase, starting at ${t}_{3}$ , when the speed of the ${m}_{1}$ mass is canceled.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

At the moment $t=0$ , both masses are at rest (moving and zero speed).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The system pulse is noted ${\omega}_{0}$ and is such that ${\omega}_{0}^{2}=\frac{k}{m}$ .

**Adhesion phase**

At the moment $t=0$ , the ${m}_{1}$ mass checks

${x}_{1}(t)=0$

$\dot{{x}_{1}}(t)=0$

and mass ${m}_{2}$ checks

${x}_{2}(t)=0$

$\dot{{x}_{2}}(t)=0$ .

The fundamental principle of dynamics applied to the ${m}_{2}$ mass allows to write the following equation

$\ddot{{x}_{2}}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}=\frac{F}{m}$ ,

and on the mass ${m}_{1}$ the following equation

${F}_{T}=-k{x}_{2}$

The general solution of ${x}_{2}$ is the form:

${x}_{2}=\tilde{A}\cos({\omega}_{0}t)+\tilde{B}\sin({\omega}_{0}t)+\frac{F}{(m{\omega}_{0}^{2})}$

where $\tilde{A}$ and $\tilde{B}$ are constants.

Taking into account the initial conditions, the displacement of the ${m}_{2}$ mass is therefore written

${x}_{2}=\frac{F}{k}[1-\cos({\omega}_{0}t)]$ .

This expression is valid until

$∥{F}_{T}∥=\mu {F}_{N}$

In other words, until the moment ${t}_{1}$ checking the following expression

$F[1-\cos({\omega}_{0}{t}_{1})]={\mu}_{S}{F}_{N}$

The sliding phase starts at ${t}_{1}$ defined by

${t}_{1}=\frac{1}{{\omega}_{0}}\arccos(1-{\mu}_{S}\frac{{F}_{N}}{F})$

**Slide phase**

At the moment $t={t}_{1}$ , the ${m}_{1}$ mass checks

${x}_{1}({t}_{1})=0$

$\dot{x}{}_{1}({t}_{1})=0$

The fundamental principle of the dynamics applied to the mass ${m}_{1}$ allows to write the equation

$\ddot{x}{}_{1}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}=\frac{-{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{m}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}$ ,

and on the mass ${m}_{2}$ the equation

$\ddot{x}{}_{2}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}=\frac{F}{m}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}$ .

By changing variables

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}X={x}_{1}+{x}_{2}\\ Y={x}_{1}-{x}_{2}\\ {\Omega}_{0}=\sqrt{2}{\omega}_{0}\end{array}$

the previous system is written

$\begin{array}{}\ddot{X}=\frac{F-{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{m}\\ \ddot{Y}+{\Omega}_{0}^{2}Y=-\frac{F+{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{m}\end{array}$

For $t\ge {t}_{1}$, the general solution of $X$ is the form

$X=\frac{(F-{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N})}{\mathrm{2m}}{(t-{t}_{1})}^{2}+\tilde{C}(t-{t}_{1})+\tilde{D}$ .

and that of $Y$ is of the form

$Y=\tilde{E}\cos({\Omega}_{0}(t-{t}_{1}))+\tilde{F}\sin({\Omega}_{0}(t-{t}_{1}))-\frac{F+{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{\mathrm{2k}}$ .

Taking into account the initial conditions, the expressions of the constants $\tilde{C}$ , $\tilde{D}$ , $\tilde{E}$ and $\tilde{F}$ are:

$\begin{array}{}\tilde{C}=\dot{x}{}_{2}({t}_{1})\\ \tilde{D}={x}_{2}({t}_{1})\\ \tilde{E}=\frac{F+{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{\mathrm{2k}}-{x}_{2}({t}_{1})\\ \tilde{F}=-\frac{\dot{x}{}_{2}({t}_{1})}{{\Omega}_{0}}\end{array}$

${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ moves are deduced from previous expressions.

**Slide phase with loading** $F=0$ **from** ${t}_{2}$ **(arbitrary but greater than** ${t}_{1}$ **) to the moment** ${t}_{3}$ **(re-entry phase)**

The fundamental principle of the dynamics presented above is still valid. For the ${m}_{1}$ mass, the verified equation is the same:

$\ddot{x}{}_{1}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}=\frac{-({\mu}_{D}{F}_{N})}{m}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}$ ,

and for mass ${m}_{2}$

$\ddot{x}{}_{2}+{\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{2}={\omega}_{0}^{2}{x}_{1}$

With the same variable change as before, the system is written

$\begin{array}{}\ddot{X}=-\frac{{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{m}\\ \ddot{Y}+{\Omega}_{0}^{2}Y=-\frac{{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{m}\end{array}$

For $t>{t}_{2}$, the general solution of $X$ is the form

$X=-\frac{{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{\mathrm{2m}}{(t-{t}_{2})}^{2}+\tilde{G}(t-{t}_{2})+\tilde{H}$

The general solution of $Y$ is of the shape

$Y=\tilde{I}\cos({\Omega}_{0}(t-{t}_{2}))+\tilde{J}\sin({\Omega}_{0}(t-{t}_{2}))-\frac{{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{\mathrm{2k}}$

Taking into account the initial conditions, the expressions of the constants $\tilde{G}$ , $\tilde{H}$ , $\tilde{I}$ and $\tilde{J}$ are:

$\begin{array}{}\tilde{G}=\dot{x}{}_{1}({t}_{2})+\dot{x}{}_{2}({t}_{2})\\ \tilde{H}={x}_{1}({t}_{2})+{x}_{2}({t}_{2})\\ \tilde{J}=\frac{{\mu}_{D}{F}_{N}}{\mathrm{2k}}+{x}_{1}({t}_{2})-{x}_{2}({t}_{2})\\ \tilde{J}=\frac{\dot{x}{}_{1}({t}_{2})-\dot{x}{}_{2}({t}_{2})}{{\Omega}_{0}}\end{array}$

${x}_{1}$ and ${x}_{2}$ moves are deduced from previous expressions.

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The quantities tested are the kinematics of the ${m}_{1}$ and ${m}_{2}$ masses at different times in the different phases of the Modelling. The following transition moments are also tested:

- transition from adhesion to sliding phase ( moment ${t}_{1}$ ) ;
- transition from the sliding phase to the adhesion phase ( moment ${t}_{3}$ ).

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Analytical solution exact.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

In this Modelling, the DYNA_VIBRA operator (see [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03)) is used with the DIS_CHOC relationship. The integration scheme is ADAPT_ORDRE2.

In this Modelling, the force exerted on the ${m}_{2}$ mass is canceled in ${t}_{2}=0,2s$ .

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh contains 2 mesh cells discreet at one node (one for each mass) and 1 mesh cell discreet for the spring.

## 4 Summary of the results

This test case shows that the adhesion – slip and slip – adhesion transitions are well captured.

It also validates the DYNA_VIBRA operator's ability to integrate problems in large translation movements.
