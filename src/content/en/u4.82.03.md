# u4.82.03 Operator CALC_G

## 1 Purpose

This operator computes the following fracture-mechanics quantities, in 2D and in 3D:

- the energy release rate by the theta method in the case of a linear or non-linear thermoelastic problem [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) and [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03), in statics or in dynamics [R7.02.02](doc:r7.02.02);
- the stress intensity factors $K1$, $\mathrm{K2}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ by the singular-displacement method in the case of a linear thermoelastic problem [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05);
- the maximisation of $G$ and of $\mathrm{K1}$ under bound constraints.
- the computation of the stress intensity factor $\mathrm{K1}$ from the energy release rate and Irwin's formula in mode I.

Before a first use, it is advisable to refer to the corresponding reference documents and user's guides, in particular the document [U2.05.01](doc:u2.05.01).

This operator generates a concept of type table_sdaster.

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operand RESULTAT

```text
♦    RESULTAT    =    resu
```

Name of a result concept of type evol_elas, evol_noli, dyna_trans or mode_meca. This operand makes it possible to recover the displacement field (and the velocity and acceleration fields for a dynamic analysis).

The model and the material field, which are required for the analysis, are also extracted from the result data structure. The analysis options available for each type of modelling are recalled in the table below.

|  | **Computation of** $G$ | **Computation of** $K$ | **Computation of** $\mathit{KJ}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **D_PLAN / C_PLAN,** **D_PLAN_INCO_UPG** | CALC_G (OPTION ‘G’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘K’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘KJ’) |
| **AXIS, AXIS_INCO_UPG** | CALC_G (OPTION ‘G’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘K’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘KJ’) |
| **3D, 3D_INCO_UPG** | CALC_G (OPTION ‘G’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘K’) | CALC_G (OPTION ‘KJ’) |

**Table 3.1: Availability, by modelling, of the analysis options.**

**Notes on the material properties:**

The characteristics of the material, recovered in the data structure resu, are the following:

- Young's modulus E,
- Poisson's ratio NU,
- thermal expansion coefficient ALPHA (for a thermomechanical problem),
- yield stress SY (for a non-linear elastic problem),
- slope of the tension curve D_SIGM_EPSI (for a non-linear elastic problem with linear isotropic hardening).

For the computation of the energy release rate, these characteristics may depend on the geometry (options ‘G’ and ‘KJ’) and on the temperature (option ‘G’ and ‘KJ’). They must be independent of the temperature for the computation of the stress intensity factors (option ‘K’).

The characteristics SY and D_SIGM_EPSI are treated only for a non-linear elastic problem with Von Mises hardening. The computation of the stress intensity factors is treated only in linear elasticity.

**Note:**

*For the computation of the stress intensity factors (option* *'K'*), the characteristics must be defined on all the materials, including on the edge elements, because of the computation method [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05). To make sure of this, it is advisable to do an *AFFE = _F (TOUT = 'OUI') in the command* *AFFE_MATERIAU* [U4.43.03](doc:u4.43.03), even if the overloading rule is used afterwards.

*For the incompressible elements (_INCO_UPG), it is advisable to use* *STAT_NON_LINE in order to obtain the results.*

The stress intensity factors obtained with the option ‘K’ are computed by evaluating the bilinear form of G with a purely mechanical singular solution (Westergaard asymptotic solution). If a thermomechanical problem is solved, the singularity due to the thermal field is then not taken into account.

An indicator of the error due to this approximation can be obtained by evaluating the difference between G and G_IRWIN. In practice, the quantity $\frac{∣G-{G}_{\mathit{irwin}}∣}{∣G∣}$ is evaluated at every point of the crack front, and the arithmetic mean of it is then taken. If this mean exceeds 50%, it is considered that one is outside the domain of validity of the approach. This check is the responsibility of the user.

**Bimaterial problem:**

**1st case**: one has a bimaterial but the crack tip is in a single material, cf. Figure 3.1-a. If one is assured that the crown, defined between the lower radius R_INF and the upper radius R_SUP, is supported by elements of the same material, the analysis is possible whichever option is chosen. Otherwise only the option 'G' is possible.

### 3.3 Operand OPTION

```text
♦    OPTION    =    |    'G'    ,
                    |    'K',
                    |    'G_EPSI',
                    |    'KJ',
                    |    'KJ_EPSI',
```

#### 3.3.1 OPTION = 'G' [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) and [R7.02.03](doc:r7.02.03)

It makes it possible to compute the energy release rate $G$ by the theta method in 2D or in local 3D for a linear or non-linear thermoelastic problem.

For this computation, the stress and displacement fields of the result supplied as input to the operator are used directly.

#### 3.3.2 OPTION = 'K' [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05)

This option computes **in 2D and in 3D** the stress intensity factors ${K}_{1}$, ${K}_{2}$ and ${K}_{3}$ in plane linear thermoelasticity by the singular-field method (use of the bilinear form of $G$, [R7.02.05](doc:r7.02.05)), as well as the quantity ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}$ (see section 3.9).

**Notes:**

- *For this option, only linear elastic analyses without an initial state, or linear elastic analyses with an initial stress, are available.*
- *The computation of this option is possible only if the lips are initially stuck together (* CONFIG_INIT='COLLEE' *in* DEFI_FOND_FISS [U4.82.01] *).*

For this option, the computation of the SIFs can be improved in the case of a curved or elliptical crack with the keyword `FORM_FISS`.
If this keyword is supplied, the singular fields take into account additional terms linked to the curvature of the crack.

#### 3.3.3 OPTION = 'G_EPSI'

It makes it possible to compute the energy release rate $G$ by the theta method in 2D or in local 3D for a linear or non-linear thermoelastic problem when the stresses are recomputed from the displacement field and from the constitutive law.

This operand is available only in elasticity (linear or not) and without an initial state coming from the keyword ETAT_INIT (see section 3.7).

**Note:**

*If the constitutive laws used for the mechanical analysis and for the post-processing are the same — which is normal practice — then the results with or without recomputation of the stresses are identical.*

*A usual practice for taking plasticity into account consists, however, in carrying out an elastoplastic mechanical analysis, followed by a non-linear elastic post-processing for the computation of* $G$ *. If one remains well within the domain of validity of the computation of* $G$ *(radial and monotonic loading), then the results with or without recomputation of the stresses,* $G$ and $G\text{\_}\mathit{EPSI}$ *, are identical. As soon as one leaves this domain of validity, the discrepancy grows.*

This option, which should be reserved for experienced users, therefore makes it possible to check a posteriori that one does indeed remain within the assumptions of the computation of $G$.

#### 3.3.4 OPTION = 'KJ'

It makes it possible to compute a pseudo stress intensity factor $K_J$ as defined in the RSE-M from the energy release rate $G$ and from Irwin's formula:

> $K_J=\sqrt{EG}$ in plane stress
> $K_J=\sqrt{\frac{EG}{1 - \nu^2}}$ in plane strain, in axisymmetric conditions and in 3D

If the loading is mode I, then this quantity is equal to $K_1$ (see section 3.9).

The method used for the computation of the energy release rate $G$ is identical to that used in the case OPTION = 'G'.

**Note:**

*If G is negative at a point, then the command will by convention return KJ = 0 for this point.*

#### 3.3.5 OPTION = 'KJ_EPSI'

It makes it possible to compute a pseudo stress intensity factor $K_J$ as defined in the RSE-M from the energy release rate $G$ and from Irwin's formula:

> $K_J=\sqrt{EG}$ in plane stress
> $K_J=\sqrt{\frac{EG}{1 - \nu^2}}$ in plane strain, in axisymmetric conditions and in 3D

If the loading is mode I, then this quantity is equal to $K_1$ (see section 3.9).

The method used for the computation of the energy release rate $G$ is identical to that used in the case OPTION = 'G_EPSI'.

**Note:**

*If G is negative at a point, then the command will by convention return KJ = 0 for this point.*

### 3.5 Keyword THETA

The theta field is computed in CALC_G from the keyword FISSURE.

Advice on the choice of the crowns (in CALC_G):

- *Avoid using a theta field defined with a lower radius* R_INF *equal to zero. The displacement fields are singular at the crack front and introduce inaccurate results in fracture-mechanics post-processing.*
- *It is advisable to use the command* CALC_G *successively with at least three theta fields of different crowns in order to make sure of the stability of the results. In the event of a significant variation (greater than 5–10%) one must question whether the whole modelling has been properly taken into account.*
- *For the option* *K in 2D-axisymmetric conditions, the radius of the crowns must be small compared with the radius of the crack front in order to have the best possible accuracy. It is forbidden to have crowns of radius greater than the radius of the crack front.*

#### 3.5.1 Operands FISSURE

```text
♦    /    FISSURE    =    ff,
```

This operand makes it possible to define the theta field (or fields).

ff is the crack front defined by the command DEFI_FOND_FISS [U4.82.01] for an open or closed crack front (double fronts are forbidden in CALC_G).

#### 3.5.2 Operand CHAM_THETA

```text
◊    CHAM_THETA    =         /CO('cham_theta') [CO, cham_no_sdaster]
```

A concept of type CO makes it possible to output the theta field computed in the operator (see the reference documentation [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) to find out how the field is computed), whose name is the character string given. This concept contains a cham_no (field at the nodes) in 2D and in 3D.

A concept of type cham_no_sdaster makes it possible to supply a theta field, instead of computing one in the operator. In that case, the keywords R_INF, R_INF_FO, NB_COUCHE_INF, R_SUP, R_SUP_FP, NB_COUCHE_SUP are no longer supported, because the necessary information is already supplied in the theta field.

#### 3.5.3 Operands R_INF, R_INF_FO, NB_COUCHE_INF, R_SUP, R_SUP_FO, NB_COUCHE_SUP

These operands make it possible to compute the theta field when it has not been determined beforehand. They correspond respectively to the lower radii (R_INF and R_INF_FO) and to the upper radii of the crowns (R_SUP and R_SUP_FO) (a scalar or, in 3D, a function of the curvilinear abscissa).

The two radii may be introduced either by constant real values, which are arguments of the simple keywords R_INF and R_SUP, or by functions of the curvilinear abscissa on the oriented crack front, which are arguments of the simple keywords R_INF_FO and R_SUP_FO.

Some advice is given below. When the radii are not a function of the curvilinear abscissa, the operands R_INF and R_SUP are optional. If they are not indicated, they are computed automatically from the maximum h of the sizes of the mesh cells connected to the nodes of the crack front. These mesh-cell sizes at each node of the front are computed in the command DEFI_FOND_FISS. It has been chosen to set R_SUP = 4h and R_INF = 2h. If the automatically computed value is chosen for R_SUP and R_INF, one should nevertheless make sure that these values (displayed in the .mess file) are consistent with the dimensions of the structure.

Alternatively, it is possible to define the integration crowns of the theta field in terms of a number of layers (NB_COUCHE_INF and NB_COUCHE_SUP). The notion of layers is here based on the connectivity of the mesh (see the reference documentation [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01) for further details).

In the case of a defect that is initially open and whose front is not plane, it is not at present possible to compute the energy release rate.

#### 3.5.4 Operand DISCRETISATION

The domain of application of this keyword is limited to the 3D case.

##### 3.5.4.1 Operand DISCRETISATION = LINEAIRE

The trace of the theta field on the crack front is discretised according to the shape functions associated with the discretisation of the crack front ('LAGRANGE') [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01).

It is possible to reduce the number of nodes of the front used for a ‘LINEAIRE’ discretisation with the following keyword:

```text
◊    NB_POINT_FOND    =    nbnofo,
```

By default, the analysis is carried out on all the nodes of the crack front for a meshed crack. If the mesh is free, the number of nodes on the crack front may be large, which leads to very long run times.

The operand NB_POINT_FOND makes it possible to fix a priori the number of post-processing points, in order to improve the regularity of the results. The $\mathit{nbnofo}$ points are evenly distributed along the crack front.

In the case where the operand NB_POINT_FOND is not supplied for a quadratic mesh, a treatment, by smoothing via a “hat” function, is added in order to remove the strong oscillations in the post-processing of $G(s)$ or $K(s)$.

##### 3.5.4.2 Operand DISCRETISATION = LEGENDRE

The trace of the theta field on the crack front is discretised according to the basis of the $N$ first Legendre polynomials ('LEGENDRE'), [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01).

The user may choose the number of polynomials desired for the analysis with the following keyword:

```text
◊    DEGRE    =    n
```

$n$ is the maximum degree of the Legendre polynomials used for the decomposition of the field $\theta$ on the crack front.

By default $n$ is set to 5. The value of $n$ must lie between 0 and 7.

##### 3.5.4.3 Advice on the discretisation

- *it is difficult to give a preference to one or the other smoothing method. In principle the two give equivalent numerical results. Nevertheless smoothing of type* 'LINEAIRE' *is a little more costly in CPU time than smoothing of type* 'LEGENDRE' *;*
- *smoothing of type* *'LEGENDRE' is sensitive to the maximum degree of the polynomials chosen. The maximum degree must be defined as a function of the number of nodes on the crack front* *NNO. If* $n$ *is too large with respect to* $\mathit{NNO}$ the results are poor [U2.05.01 §2.4];
- *oscillations may appear with smoothing of type* *'LINEAIRE', in particular if the mesh comprises quadratic elements. If the mesh is radiating at the crack front (meshed crack), it is then recommended to define crowns* *R_INF and* *R_SUP coinciding with the boundaries of the elements.*
- *for meshed cracks, when smoothing of type* 'LINEAIRE' *is used it is recommended to use the operand* NB_POINT_FOND *in order to guarantee an even distribution of the computation points on the crack front. The choice of a ratio of the order of 5 between the total number of points on the crack front (to be looked for in the information printed in the message file by the command* DEFI_FOND_FISS *) and the number of computation points seems appropriate in order to limit the oscillations;*
- *the use of at least two types of smoothing with several integration crowns and the comparison of the results is* **indispensable** *in order to check the validity of the model.*

### 3.6 Keyword EXCIT and operands CHARGE/FONC_MULT

```text
◊    EXCIT = _F(    ♦    CHARGE    =    charge
◊    FONC_MULT = fmult )
```

The keyword EXCIT makes it possible to recover a list of loadings charge, coming from the commands AFFE_CHAR_MECA or AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01), and the possible associated multiplicative coefficients fmult.

If the keyword EXCIT is absent from the command, the loading taken into account is that extracted from resu. If the loading is supplied via EXCIT, then it is this loading that will be used in CALC_G. If the loading supplied in EXCIT is different from that present in resu (consistency of the name and of the number of loads, of the load–function pairs), an alarm is issued and the analysis continues with the loadings indicated by the user.

However, if the result is of type dyna_trans or mode_meca, it is possible to recover a list of loadings from the resu. Taking these loadings into account in the computation of G therefore requires the keyword EXCIT.

The loadings currently supported by the various modellings and that may have a meaning in fracture mechanics are the following:

- Body force: ROTATION, FORCE_INTERNE, PESANTEUR.
- Surface force on the lips of the crack: FORCE_CONTOUR (2D), FORCE_FACE (3D), PRES_REP.
- Thermal expansion: the temperature is transmitted via AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC
- Initial strain: this strain is transmitted via the command variable EPSA (anelastic strain) in AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC
- Pre-strain: PRE_EPSI (only in the case of a meshed crack, for the option G_EPSI. Outside this particular configuration, applying a PRE_EPSI loading leads to incorrect results)
- In the case of a thermomechanical problem, the temperature is transmitted via the material properties (AFFE_MATERIAU / AFFE_VARC / EVOL). The thermal expansion is therefore automatically taken into account in the analysis with CALC_G. The same is true if an initial strain is transmitted via the command variable EPSA.

**Note:**

The loadings not supported by an option are ignored. To date, the following loadings, which may have a meaning in fracture mechanics, are not treated:

- *FORCE_NODALE*
- *FORCE_ARETE*
- *DDL_IMPO* *on the lips of the crack*
- *FACE_IMPO*
- *PRE_EPSI* *outside the only case treated, “meshed crack and option* *G_EPSI”*

It is important to note that the only loadings taken into account in a fracture-mechanics analysis with the method $\theta$ are those supported by the elements inside the crown, where the theta vector field is non-zero (between R_INF and R_SUP [R7.02.01 §3.3]). **The only types of load liable to influence the computation of** $G$ **are therefore the body loadings (gravity, rotation), a non-uniform temperature field or forces applied on the lips of the crack.**

**Warning:**

- *If several loadings of the same kind (for example a body force) appear in the analysis, they are combined with one another for the post-processing. It is not, however, possible to date to carry out this combination if loadings of type* *FORMULE* are present*: the analysis then ends in error.*
- *An exclusion rule is also applied when a pre-strain field (via* 'PRE_EPSI' *) and an initial-stress field are present simultaneously.*
- *An exclusion rule is also applied when an initial-strain field* *(via* 'EPSA' *) and an initial-stress field are present simultaneously.*
- *An exclusion rule is also applied when a pre-strain field (via* 'PRE_EPSI' *) and an initial-strain field* *(via* *'EPSA') are present simultaneously.*
- *It is not possible to date to associate a load defined from a* *FORMULE and a multiplicative coefficient (* FONC_MULT *). In this case, the analysis ends in error.*
- *The kinematic loads (* AFFE_CHAR_CINE *and* AFFE_CHAR_CINE_F *) cannot be taken into account in the analysis.*
- *For the option* K *, if a loading is imposed on the lips of the crack (* PRES_REP *or* FORCE_CONTOUR *), then the mesh cells of the lips must* **necessarily** *be oriented correctly (using* ORIE_PEAU_2D *or* ORIE_PEAU_3D *) prior to the computation of K.*
- *It is noted that the computation of* CALC_G *is available* *only for the results of thermomechanical analyses in* *small-perturbation conditions (keyword* *DEFORMATION=* *'PETIT').*

### 3.7 Operand ETAT_INIT

```text
◊    ETAT_INIT=_F(SIGM = siefelga)
```

In the case of an incremental elastic constitutive relation, it is possible to define a non-zero initial stress state.

The use of this keyword requires beforehand the definition of an initial state in the operator STAT_NON_LINE when solving the analysis to be post-processed by CALC_G.

STAT_NON_LINE allows this definition in two ways (see u4.51.03):

- with an initial state of result type, supplied under the keyword EVOL_NOLI of the factor keyword ETAT_INIT
- with an initial state of field type, supplied under the keywords SIGM/DEPL/VARI/STRX/COHE of the factor keyword ETAT_INIT

To date, post-processing with CALC_G of analyses using the keywords EVOL_NOLI/DEPL/VARI/STRX/COHE is not possible. Only post-processing with CALC_G of analyses using the keyword SIGM is possible.

The initial stress field supplied may be of type SIEF_ELGA, SIEF_ELNO or SIEF_NOEU in an FEM modelling.

In every case, this initial stress field must be **self-equilibrated**, in the absence of a crack, with the boundary conditions alone. The user must check that the initial stress field is admissible by applying it in the keyword ETAT_INIT of the operator STAT_NON_LINE, with a linear elastic constitutive behaviour (RELATION = 'ELAS'), with the boundary conditions alone; the mechanical result must be the same stress field without additional strains (see ).

The initial state of the structure may also be represented through a pre-strain field (*via* the keyword PRE_EPSI of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA). In this case, it is a load included in the result data structure (see section 3.6) and the keyword ETAT_INIT must not be filled in.

Given the difficulty of validating the formulation that has been implemented, it is not at present admissible to combine a pre-strain (*via* the keyword PRE_EPSI of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA) and an initial stress, or an initial strain (via the command variable EPSA of AFFE_MATERIAU/AFFE_VARC) and an initial stress.

${G}_{\mathit{IRWIN}}$ is the energy release rate obtained from the stress intensity factors ${K}_{1}$ and ${K}_{2}$ (and ${K}_{3}$) with the following formulae:

> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{1}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$ in plane stress
> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})$ in plane strain and axisymmetric conditions
> ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}({K}_{I}^{2}+{K}_{\mathrm{II}}^{2})+\frac{{K}_{\mathrm{III}}^{2}}{2\mu }$ in 3D

with $E$ Young's modulus and $\nu$ Poisson's ratio and $\mu =\frac{E}{2(1+\nu )}$. The comparison between $G$ and ${G}_{\mathrm{IRWIN}}$ makes it possible to make sure of the consistency of the results: too large a discrepancy must lead one to check the parameters of the analysis (refinement of the mesh, choice of the crowns for theta, smoothing in 3D...).

The command IMPR_TABLE [U4.91.03](doc:u4.91.03) makes it possible to print the results in the desired format.

## 4 Normalisation of the energy release rate G

### 4.1 2D plane stress and plane strain

In dimension 2 (plane stress and plane strain), the crack front is reduced to a point and the value $G(\theta )$ coming from the command CALC_G is independent of the choice of the field $\theta$:

> $G=G(\theta )\forall \theta \in \Theta$

### 4.2 Symmetry of the model

If only half of the solid is modelled with respect to the crack:

- either specify the keyword SYME='OUI' in the commands concerned;
- or do not forget to multiply by 2 the values of the energy release rate $G$ or $G(s)$, and by 4 those of ${G}_{\mathrm{Irwin}}$*.* In addition the values of the stress intensity factors corresponding to the symmetry mode must also be multiplied by 2.

## 5 Examples

Let us take the case of a meshed crack:

The crack front is defined in DEFI_FOND_FISS:

```text
ff=DEFI_FOND_FISS(  MAILLAGE=MA,
                    FOND_FISS=_F(GROUP_MA = 'LFF'),

)
```

Examples of computation of the energy release rate in 3D (local):

```text
G1LOC    =    CALC_G (    OPTION            =    ‘G’,
                         RESULTAT        =    resu,
                         THETA = _F(    FISSURE        =    ff,
                         R_INF            =    1.,
                         R_SUP            =    2.,
                         DISCRETISATION = ‘LINEAIRE’),
)
```

Example of computation of the stress intensity factors in 3D:

```text
KLOC    =    CALC_G (    OPTION            =    ‘K’,
                         RESULTAT        =    resu,
                         THETA = _F(    FISSURE    =    ff,
                         R_INF            =    1.,
                         R_SUP            =    2.,
                         DISCRETISATION = ‘LINEAIRE’),
)
```
