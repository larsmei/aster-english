# v1.01.107 RCCM01 - Operator POST_RCCM

**Summary:**

This test is a basic POST_RCCM check test.

The analytical solution is simple, and allows testing the post-processing in the sense of the RCC-M. The constraints are not calculated but extracted from tables.

More specifically, Modelling A allows testing the PM_PB option for EVOLUTION and B3200 results with and without earthquake.

The B Modelling allows testing the SN option for EVOLUTION and B3200 results with and without earthquake.

The C-modelling allows testing the FATIGUE option for EVOLUTION and B3200 results with and without earthquake. The KE_MIXTE keyword for the fatigue calculation is tested in this Modelling. The EFAT option with B3200 is also tested.

The D Modelling allows you to test the EFAT option again for B3200 results.

The E Modelling allows you to test the FATIGUE option for B3200 results with different input situations.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Material properties

The material properties are as follows:

1. Young's modulus: $E=2.E+05\mathit{MPa}$ ;
2. Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.3$ ;
3. coefficient of thermal expansion: $\alpha =1.E–05\mathrm{m.}°{C}^{-1}$ .

The characteristics of the RCC-M calculation are:

1. material constants for $\mathrm{Ke}$ calculation: $n=0.2$ , $m=\mathrm{2 }$ ;
2. Young's modulus reference: ${E}_{\mathit{REFE}}=2.E+05\mathit{MPa}$;
3. permissible constraint : $\mathrm{Sm}=200\mathrm{MPa}$ .

The Wöhler curve is defined analytically: ${N}_{\mathrm{adm}}=\frac{{5.10}^{5}}{{S}_{\mathrm{alt}}}$

**Note**

For the validation of the elastoplastic concentration factor* $\mathrm{Ke}$ , some calculations are performed with a lower or higher permissible stress: $\mathit{Sm}=50\mathit{MPa}$ (modelling C) and $\mathit{Sm}=2000\mathit{MPa}$ (modelling D and E).

#### 2.1.7 Calculation of environmental fatigue for situations 3 and 4 in the same group

The calculation is detailed for the combination of situations 3 and 4 only and ** at the end** with ${\mathit{Nocc}}_{3}=1$ and ${\mathit{Nocc}}_{4}=1$ .

Environmental fatigue is applied only after the normal fatigue, so the combinations that have occurred in the classical fatigue are used to calculate the total use factor.

In classical fatigue, ${\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{TOTAL}}^{\mathit{EXT}}={\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ and involves the combination of situations 3 and 4. Therefore, the FEN of this combination must be calculated.

The $\Delta \sigma$ stress increment is easy to calculate because the tensioner is uniaxial, so there is no need to diagonalize. Only the thermal intervention in this increment is possible because the other quantities are not time dependent.

When the stress increment is negative, the environment is considered to have no effect, so the other quantities are not calculated.

Otherwise $\Delta {\varepsilon}=\frac{\mathit{Ke}\ast \Delta \sigma }{E(T)}$ . In this example, we have returned a constant Young's modulus based on temperature and order of 200,000 MPa. And the $\mathit{Ke}$ is the one that served for the combination of situations 3 and 4, i.e. $\mathit{Ke}=1$ .

Then we calculate $\dot{{\varepsilon}}=\frac{\Delta {\varepsilon}}{{t}_{i}-{t}_{i-1}}$ . In this case it is below the threshold ${{\varepsilon}}_{\mathit{seul},inf}$ so

$\dot{{\varepsilon}}\text{*}=\ln(\frac{{{\varepsilon}}_{\mathit{seuilinf}}}{{{\varepsilon}}_{\mathit{seuilsup}}})=\ln(\frac{1}{2})$

$F=\exp[(A+B\dot{{\varepsilon}}\text{*})S\text{*}O\text{*}T\text{*}+C]=\exp[\dot{{\varepsilon}}\text{*}T\text{*}]$ knowing that T* is a T function, with

$T=\frac{T({t}_{i})+T({t}_{i-1})}{2}$ . The user obviously turned the temperature in each situation under the keyword TABL_TEMP.

| Time increment treated | $\Delta \sigma$ | $\Delta {\varepsilon}$ | $\dot{{\varepsilon}}$ (${s}^{-1}$ ) | $\dot{{\varepsilon}}\text{*}$ | T | T* | F |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1-2 | -250 | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ |
| 2-3 | +150 | ${\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ | ${\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ | $\ln(\frac{1}{2})$ | 150 | 2,2 | $\exp(2,2\ast \ln(\frac{1}{2}))$ =0,218 |
| 3-4 | -50 | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ |
| 1,5-2,5 | -250 | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ | _ |
| 2,5-3,5 | +150 | ${\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ | ${\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ | $\ln(\frac{1}{2})$ | 150 | 2,2 | $\exp(2,2\ast \ln(\frac{1}{2}))$ =0,218 |

Finally, ${\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{EXTREMITE}}=\frac{{F}_{2-3}\Delta {{\varepsilon}}_{2-3}+{F}_{2,5-3,5}\ast \Delta {{\varepsilon}}_{2,5-3,5}}{\Delta {{\varepsilon}}_{2-3}+\Delta {{\varepsilon}}_{2,5-3,5}}=0,218$.

The partial use factor and the total use factor with environmental effect ${\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{partiel},\mathit{env}}^{\mathit{EXT}}={\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{EXTREMITE}}\ast {\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{partiel}}=0,218\ast 7,5\ast {10}^{-4}={\mathrm{1,6323.10}}^{-4}$ can be calculated.

The ${\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{EXTREMITE}}$ is then compared to the FEN_INTEGRE parameter.

If ${\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{EXTREMITE}}\le {\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{INTEGRE}}$ (case or FEN_INTEGRE = 50) then:

${\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{TOTAL},\mathit{env}}^{\mathit{EXT}}={\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{TOTAL}}^{\mathit{EXT}}={\mathrm{7,5.10}}^{-4}$ .

If ${\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{EXTREMITE}}>{\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{INTEGRE}}$ (in case FEN_INTEGRE = 0.1) then:

${\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{TOTAL},\mathit{env}}^{\mathit{EXT}}={N}_{\mathit{occ}}\ast {\mathit{FU}}_{\mathit{partiel},\mathit{env}}^{\mathit{EXT}}/{\mathit{FEN}}_{\mathit{INTEGRE}}=1\ast {\mathrm{1,6323.10}}^{-4}/0,1$ .
