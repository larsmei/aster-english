# r5.05.01 Computation of an oscillator response spectrum

**Summary:**

We present in this document the computation of an oscillator response spectrum (SRO).

## 1 Introduction

When computing an oscillator response spectrum (SRO), one is led to compute the response of a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator to a seismic signal.

This consists in solving a second-order differential equation whose time solution is given
by a convolution product known as the Duhamel integral.
The NIGAM method is an efficient method for solving this problem.

In the frequency domain, the convolution product becomes a simple product and can be solved efficiently by means of the analytical transfer function.

This document presents these two methods, which are available.

The computation of an SRO can be carried out via the operator [CALC_FONCTION](doc:u4.32.04), factor keyword SPEC_OSCI.

## 2 Mathematical definition of the response spectrum

### 2.1 Acceleration response spectrum

The spectral acceleration is the name given to the maximum absolute response of a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator of natural frequency $f$ excited at its base by an accelerogram.
The oscillator response spectrum (SRO) is a function of frequency whose ordinates are the spectral accelerations.

The equation of motion of a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator excited at its base by an imposed acceleration, $\alpha(t)$, is written:

$$
m \ddot{q}(t) + c \dot{q}(t) + k q(t) = -m \alpha(t)
$$

where $q(t)$ is the relative displacement of the oscillator.
Thus one has

$$
\ddot{q}(t) + 2  \xi \, \omega_0 \dot{q}(t) + \omega_0^2 q(t)=-\alpha(t)
$$

where $\omega_0=\sqrt{ \frac{k}{m}}$ and $\xi=\frac{c}{2\sqrt {k m}}$.

In the frequency domain this equation is written:

$$
-\omega^2 \hat{q}(\omega) + 2 \xi \, i\omega \,  \omega_0 \hat{q}(\omega)+\omega_0^2 \hat{q}(\omega)=-\hat{\alpha}(\omega)
$$

Response spectra are generally expressed in spectral pseudo-acceleration ${PS_{a}}$.
It corresponds to the product of the maximum of the absolute relative displacement $|q(t)|$ of the simple oscillator
by the square of its natural frequency $f=\frac{\omega}{2 \pi}$:

$$
PS_{a}(f)=(2\pi f)^2 \max_{T}|q(t)|
$$

where the spectral pseudo-acceleration is denoted ${PS_{a}}$ and $T$ is the duration of the observation. The response $q(t)$ depends on the frequency of the oscillator $f$. This dependence is not mentioned explicitly in the expressions for the SRO, in order to lighten the notation.

It is customary to work with the response spectra in pseudo-acceleration rather than to consider the absolute spectral acceleration.
For a given frequency, the absolute spectral accelerations and the pseudo-acceleration coincide over a wide range of dampings.
For higher dampings, beyond 20%, differences can be observed between the pseudo-acceleration and the absolute acceleration.
The absolute spectral acceleration $S_{a}(f)$ is readily obtained as the maximum value of the absolute acceleration response $y(t) =\alpha(t) + \ddot{q}(t)$:

$$
S_{a}(f) = \max_{T}|y(t)|
$$

The notable values of the acceleration response spectrum are:

- the maximum frequency $f_{max}$, beyond which the spectrum reaches a constant value; one reaches the asymptote of the spectrum. This is the frequency beyond which the seismic signal no longer contains any frequency content. An oscillator whose natural frequency exceeds $f_{max}$ follows the motion of its support. The acceleration associated with the asymptote is called the ZPA (Zero Period Acceleration), which corresponds to the maximum value of the entrainment acceleration. The ZPA corresponds to the value of the maximum absolute acceleration of the time signal (PGA) and does not depend on the damping of the oscillator.
- the pseudo-acceleration at the peak (there may be several peaks) or of the plateau in the case of a smoothed and broadened response spectrum;
- the pseudo-acceleration at low frequency is related to the maximum entrainment displacement.

### 2.2 Velocity and displacement response spectra

The displacement response spectrum ${S_{d}}(f)$ is defined as:

$$
S_{d}(f)= \max_{T}|q(t)|
$$

The pseudo-velocity response spectra $PS_{v}(f)$ correspond to the product of the maximum of the absolute relative displacement $|q(t)|$ of the simple oscillator by its natural frequency $f= \frac{\omega}{2 \pi}$:

$$
PS_{v}(f) = (2\pi f) \max_{T}|q(t)| = (2\pi f) S_{d}(f)
$$

The relative-velocity response spectrum is computed directly from the response of the oscillator:

$$
S_{v}(f)= \max_{T}|\dot{q}(t)|
$$

### 2.3 Damping of the response spectra

The response spectra also depend on the reduced damping of the oscillator
(here this dependence has been omitted from the equations in order to simplify the notation).

## 3 Nigam time-domain method

### 3.1 Analytical solution of the response of a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator

When computing the oscillator spectrum of an accelerogram [R4.05.03](doc:r4.05.03), one is led to solve the following linear second-order differential equation:

$$
\ddot{q} + 2\xi \omega \dot{q} + {\omega}^{2}q
=
\alpha \left(t\right)
$$

where $q(t)$ is the relative displacement, $\alpha (t)$ is the acceleration of the motion imposed at the base, $\omega$ is the angular frequency of the oscillator and $\xi$ is the reduced damping of the oscillator.

With initial conditions on $q$ and $\dot{q}$ . The solution of this equation is written in the form:

$$
q\left(t\right)
=
{\int}_{0}^{t} {
    h \left(t-\tau \right) \, \alpha \left(\tau \right) d\tau }
    +
    q\left(0\right)g\left(t\right)
    +
    \dot{q}\left(0\right)h\left(t\right)
$$

where $q(0)$ and $\dot{q}(0)$ are the displacement and the velocity at the initial time instant.

We are interested in the expressions for $h(t)$ and for $g(t)$ according to the value of the reduced damping $\xi$ .

If $\xi <1$ (subcritical damping), then:

$$
h \left(t\right)
=
\frac{{e}^{-\xi \omega t}}{\omega \sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}}
\,
\sin\left(\omega t\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}\right)
$$

and

$$
g\left(t\right)
=
{e}^{-\xi \omega t}
\left[
    \cos \left(\omega t\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}} \right)
    +
    \frac{\xi}{\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}}
    \,
    \sin \left(\omega t\sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}} \right)
\right]
$$

If $\xi =1$ (critical damping), then:

$$
h\left(t\right)
=
t \, {e}^{-\omega t}
$$

and

$$
g\left(t\right)
=
\left(1-\omega \right){e}^{\omega t}
$$

If $\xi >1$ (supercritical damping):

$$
h\left(t\right)
=
\frac{{e}^{-\xi \omega t}}{\omega \sqrt{{\xi}^{2}-1}}
\,
\sinh \left(\omega t\sqrt{{\xi}^{2}-1}\right)
$$

and:

$$
g\left(t\right)
=
{e}^{-\xi \omega t}
\left[
    \cosh \left( \omega t\sqrt{{\xi}^{2}-1}\right)
    +
    \frac{\xi}{\sqrt{{\xi}^{2}-1}} \, \sinh \left(\omega t\sqrt{{\xi}^{2}-1}\right)
\right]
$$

### 3.2 Numerical solution

The time-domain numerical method implemented was proposed by Nigam and Jennings [bib2], and is more efficient than the Newmark method (see [Bib3]) in the case of **subcritical damping**, which corresponds to our initial seismic problem [R4.05.03](doc:r4.05.03).

One is therefore led to solve the differential equation:

$$
\ddot{q}\left(t\right)
+
2 \xi \omega \dot{q}\left(t\right)
+
{\omega}^{2}q\left(t\right)
=
\alpha \left(t\right)
$$

with zero initial conditions and a subcritical damping, whose solution is written:

$$
q\left(t\right)
=
- \frac{1}{{\omega}_{d}}
{\int}_{0}^{t} {
    {e}^{-\xi \omega \left(t-\tau \right)} \, \sin\left[{\omega}_{d} \left(t-\tau \right)\right]\alpha \left(\tau \right)
d\tau }
$$

With ${\omega}_{d}=\omega \sqrt{1-{\xi}^{2}}$. Assuming that $\alpha (t)$ varies linearly inside each interval $\Delta (t)$ as in figure r5.05.01-fig-approximationLineaire, one can write:

![Figure](/figures/r5.05.01/100009B0000010F300000B7993BB5AED2AA0213E.svg)

$$
\alpha \left(\tau \right)
=
\alpha \left(t-\Delta t\right)
+
\frac{\tau}{\Delta t}
  \left[
    \alpha \left(t\right)
    -
    \alpha \left(t-\Delta t\right)
  \right] \text{ pour } \tau \in \left[0,\Delta t\right]
$$

*Linear approximation of the function*

**Note**

The time step $\Delta t$ is not necessarily constant.

Whence the equation to be solved (expressed in the new variable $\tau$ ):

$$
\ddot{q}\left(\tau \right)
+
2\xi \omega \dot{q}\left(\tau \right)
+
{\omega}^{2}q\left(\tau \right)
=
a+b\tau \text{ pour } \tau \in \left[0,\Delta t\right]
$$

Where $a=\alpha (t-\Delta t )$ and $b=\left[\alpha (t)-\alpha (t-\Delta t )\right]/\Delta t$ . With the initial conditions:

$$
\begin{split}\left \lbrace \begin{array}{c}
    q\left(0\right)=q\left(t-\Delta t\right)\\ \dot{q}\left(0\right)=\dot{q}\left(t-\Delta t\right)
\end{array} \right .\end{split}
$$

The solution of this equation is the superposition of a particular solution:

$$
{q}_{p}(t)
=
-
\frac{a}{{\omega}^{2}}
+
\frac{2 \xi b }{{\omega}^{3}}
-
\frac{b}{{\omega}^{2}}\tau
$$

and of the solutions of the homogeneous problem:

$$
{q}_{h}(t)
=
{e}^{-\xi \omega \tau } \left[
    {C}_{1} \, \sin({\omega}_{d}\tau )
    +
    {C}_{2} \, \cos({\omega}_{d}\tau )
\right]
$$

Hence:

$$
q(\tau)
=
{e}^{-\xi \omega \tau } \left[
    {C}_{1} \, \sin({\omega}_{d}\tau )
    +
    {C}_{2} \, \cos({\omega}_{d}\tau )
\right]
-
\frac{a}{{\omega}^{2}}
+
\frac{2 \xi b}{{\omega}^{3}}
-
\frac{b}{{w}^{2}} \tau
$$

Differentiating $q$ (with respect to $\tau$) one has:

$$
\begin{array}{c}
    \dot{q}\left(\tau \right) =
        -\xi \omega {e}^{-\xi \omega \tau }
        \left({C}_{1} \, \sin{\omega}_{d}\tau + {C}_{2} \, \cos{\omega}_{d}\tau \right) +\\
    {e}^{-\xi \omega \tau } \left(
         {C}_{1} \, {\omega}_{d}\cos{\omega}_{d}\tau
        - {C}_{2} \, {\omega}_{d}\sin{\omega}_{d}\tau
    \right) - \frac{b}{{\omega}^{2}}
\end{array}
$$

The coefficients ${C}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}$ are then determined by the initial conditions at the beginning of the interval (that is, for $\tau =0$):

$$
{C}_{2}
=
q\left( t-\Delta t\right)
+
\frac{a}{{\omega}^{2}}
-
\frac{2\xi b}{{\omega}^{3}}
$$

and:

$$
{C}_{1}
=
\frac{1}{{\omega}_{d}}\left[
    \dot{q} \left(t-\Delta t\right)
    +
    \xi \omega q \left(t-\Delta t\right)
    +
    \frac{\xi a}{\omega}
    -
    \frac{2{\xi}^{2}-1}{{\omega}^{2}}b
\right]
$$

Substituting ${C}_{1}$ and ${C}_{2}$ into the expressions for $q$ and $\dot{q}$ one obtains the matrix equality for $\tau =\Delta t$ :

$$
\begin{split}\left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}
    q(t) \\ \dot{q}(t)
\end{array} \right\rbrace
=
A(\xi ,\omega ,\Delta t) \, \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}
    q(t-\Delta t)\\ \dot{q}(t-\Delta t)
\end{array} \right\rbrace
+
B(\xi ,\omega ,\Delta t) \, \left\lbrace \begin{array}{c}
    \alpha (t-\Delta t)\\ \alpha (t)
\end{array}\right\rbrace\end{split}
$$

## 4 Harmonic method

### 4.1 Computation of the displacement response

One applies the Fourier transform to the seismic signal and to the equation of motion.
The transfer function thus obtained is written

$$
h(\omega)
=
\frac{1}{(\omega_0^2 - \omega^2 + 2 \xi \, \omega_0 \, i \omega)}
$$

Equation expresses the transfer function between an accelerogram and the relative displacement response of the oscillator forced at its base.
The response in the frequency domain is thus written:

$$
\hat{q}(\omega)
=
h(\omega) \hat{\alpha}(\omega)
$$

The time response $q(t)$ is obtained by the inverse Fourier transform of $\hat{q}(\omega)$.

### 4.2 Computation of the absolute-acceleration response

The transfer function of equation must be replaced by the transfer function between an accelerogram and the absolute-acceleration response of the oscillator:

$$
h(\omega)
=
\frac{\omega_0^2+2 \xi \,i\omega \,\omega_0}{\omega_0^2-\omega^2+2 \xi \,\omega_0 \, i \omega}
$$

The response in the frequency domain is thus written:

$$
\hat{y}(\omega)
=
h(\omega) \hat{\alpha}(\omega)
$$

The time response in absolute acceleration $y(t) = \alpha(t) + \ddot{q}(t)$ is obtained by the inverse Fourier transform of $\hat{y}(\omega)$.

This is the HARMO method in [CALC_FONCTION](doc:u4.32.04), factor keyword SPEC_OSCI.
