# r3.03.02 Linkage conditions of a solid body

**Summary**

This documentation presents a way of modelling non-deformable parts of a structure, by means of the keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE of AFFE_CHAR_MECA.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE of the commands AFFE_CHAR_MECA makes it possible to model a non-deformable part of a structure. The principle adopted is to write relations between the degrees of freedom of the “solid” part; these relations express the fact that the distances between the nodes are invariable. The relations expressing the non-deformability of a solid **are not linear**.

To be convinced of this, consider the example of a segment $\mathit{AB}$ in 2D:

![Figure](/figures/r3.03.02/Object_1.svg)

The non-deformability of $\mathit{AB}$ is written:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\left[\left({x}_{A}+{\text{dx}}_{A}\right)-\left({x}_{B}+{\text{dx}}_{B}\right)\right]}^{2}+{\left[\left({y}_{A}+{\text{dy}}_{A}\right)-\left({y}_{B}+{\text{dy}}_{B}\right)\right]}^{2}={\left({x}_{A}-{x}_{B}\right)}^{2}+{\left({y}_{A}-{y}_{B}\right)}^{2}\\ \iff {\left({\text{dx}}_{B}-{\text{dx}}_{A}\right)}^{2}+2\left({x}_{B}-{x}_{A}\right)\left({\text{dx}}_{B}-{\text{dx}}_{A}\right)+{\left({\text{dy}}_{B}-{\text{dy}}_{A}\right)}^{2}+2\left({y}_{B}-{y}_{A}\right)\left({\text{dy}}_{B}-{\text{dy}}_{A}\right)=0\end{array}
$$

Denoting by $\left\lbrace {x}_{A},{y}_{A},{x}_{B},{y}_{B}\right\rbrace$ the coordinates and by $\left\lbrace {\mathit{dx}}_{A},{\mathit{dy}}_{A},{\mathit{dx}}_{B},{\mathit{dy}}_{B}\right\rbrace$ the displacements of $A$ and $B$ . One sees that the expression is quadratic in ${\text{dx}}_{A},{\text{dx}}_{B},{\text{dy}}_{A}$ and ${\text{dy}}_{B}$ *.*

When the displacements are small, the quadratic terms are negligible and the linearisation of these relations can be justified. They no longer depend on the displacement and they can be computed (on the initial geometry) as soon as the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA is executed.

On the other hand, when the rotations are finite, the linearisation of these relations depends on the displacement and must therefore be recomputed at each iteration of the Newton algorithm. If the user thinks that the rotations will not remain infinitesimal, this must be indicated in the command STAT_NON_LINE (keyword EXCIT / TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV'). The code will then recompute the linearisation of the relations in the course of the analysis.

The taking into account of the “follower” type of these relations is subject to certain restrictions which are detailed in the user documentation (U4.44.01). In order to “stiffen” a solid part, if the program does not allow the use of LIAISON_SOLIDE, one is obliged to use a “hard” material (relative to the rest of the structure).

## 2 Principle of the use of the keyword

The keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE is a repeatable factor keyword. At each occurrence of the keyword, the user defines a “piece of model” that is to be stiffened.

From this “piece of model”, defined by the keywords GROUP_MA, GROUP_NO, MAILLE and NOEUD, the **list of nodes** to be stiffened is deduced.

Once this list has been established, the relations required to express that the “rigid piece” has only the degrees of freedom of a solid are written (in general: three in 2D and six in 3D).

Note:

If all the nodes of a finite element are subjected to a condition of type LIAISON_SOLIDE, this element does not deform. Its stress state will then always be zero. If one wishes to access the stress state of a “rigid” zone, the technique of the “hard” material must be used.

## 3 Which cases are treated?

Depending on the degrees of freedom carried by the nodes of the list of nodes to be stiffened, one places oneself in one of the following four cases. If one does not fall into one of these cases, the code stops with a fatal error:

- The cases $\mathrm{2DA}$ and $\mathrm{2DB}$ correspond to “plane” or axisymmetric problems;
- The cases $\mathrm{3DA}$ and $\mathrm{3DB}$ correspond to 3D problems.

**Case** $\mathrm{2DA}$ **:**

**All** the nodes of the list of nodes to be stiffened carry the degrees of freedom $\mathit{DX}$ and $\mathit{DY}$ and at least one of the nodes carries $\mathit{DRZ}$ .

**Case** $\mathrm{2DB}$ **:**

All the nodes of the list of nodes to be stiffened carry $\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ but they do not carry $\mathit{DRX}$ , $\mathit{DRY}$ and $\mathit{DZ}$ .

**Case** $\mathrm{3DA}$ **:**

**All** the nodes of the list of nodes to be stiffened carry the degrees of freedom $\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ and $\mathit{DZ}$ and at least one of the nodes carries $\mathit{DRX}$ , $\mathit{DRY}$ or $\mathit{DRZ}$ .

**Case** $\mathrm{3DB}$ **:**

All the nodes of the list of nodes to be stiffened carry $\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ , $\mathit{DZ}$ and there exists no node of the list of nodes to be stiffened carrying rotational degrees of freedom $\mathit{DRX}$ , $\mathit{DRY}$ , $\mathit{DRZ}$ .

Only the cases $\mathrm{2DB}$ and $\mathrm{3DB}$ can currently be treated in non-linear analysis (TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV').

## 4 Treatment of cases 2DA and 3DA

In these two cases, it has been possible to find a node of the list of nodes to be stiffened which carried **all** the degrees of freedom of the solid. Let $A$ be this node, then:

- In 2D: $\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ , $\mathit{DRZ}$ ;
- In 3D: $\mathit{DX}$ , $\mathit{DY}$ , $\mathit{DZ}$ , $\mathit{DRX}$ , $\mathit{DRY}$ , $\mathit{DRZ}$

Let $M$ be an arbitrary node of the list of nodes to be stiffened. In the theory of small displacements, the motion of a solid body is expressed by:

$$
{U}_{M}={U}_{A}+\theta \Lambda \text{AM}
$$

### 4.1 Case 2DA

The linear relations are written:

$$
\begin{array}{}\forall M\ne A:\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\text{DX}(M)-\text{DX}(A)+y\text{DRZ}(A)=0\\ \text{DY}(M)-\text{DY}(A)-x\text{DRZ}(A)=0\end{array}\right\rbrace \mathrm{avec}\mathrm{AM}=(\begin{array}{}x\\ y\end{array})\\ +\text{si}M\text{porte}\text{DRZ}:\text{DRZ}(M)-\text{DRZ}(A)=0\end{array}
$$

### 4.2 Case 3DA

$\begin{array}{}\forall M\ne A:\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\text{DX}(M)-\text{DX}(A)-\text{DRY}(A).z+\text{DRZ}(A).y=0\\ \text{DY}(M)-\text{DY}(A)-\text{DRZ}(A).x+\text{DRX}(A).z=0\\ \text{DZ}(M)-\text{DZ}(A)-\text{DRX}(A).y+\text{DRY}(A).x=0\end{array}\right\rbrace \\ \\ +\text{si}M\text{porte}\text{DRX},\text{DRY},\text{DRZ}:\left\lbrace \begin{array}{}\text{DRX}(M)-\text{DRX}(A)=0\\ \text{DRY}(M)-\text{DRY}(A)=0\\ \text{DRZ}(M)-\text{DRZ}(A)=0\end{array}\right\rbrace \end{array}$

## 5 Treatment of cases 2DB and 3DB

Four cases are distinguished for the cloud of “solidified” nodes:

- Volumetric: there exist at least 4 non-coplanar nodes (to within an epsilon);
- Plane: there exist at least 3 non-aligned nodes (to within an epsilon);
- Segment: there exist at least 2 non-coincident nodes (to within an epsilon);
- Point: all the nodes are geometrically coincident (to within an epsilon)

The routine that determines the case also returns the 1, 2, 3 or 4 nodes that make it possible to “define” the solid. The kinematic relations that are written depend (rather slightly) on the case.

Consider the example of the “Plane” case in 3D.

One has three non-aligned nodes $A$ , $B$ , $C$ . One writes that the square of the three distances $\mathit{AB}$ , $\mathit{AC}$ and $\mathit{BC}$ remains constant during the motion. These three relations are non-linear. They are quadratic and they can easily be differentiated in order to obtain the linearised tangent problem.

For each node ($M$ ) different from $A$ , $B$ , $C$ , the barycentric coordinates $\alpha$ , $\beta$ and $\gamma$ are computed such that:

$$
M=\alpha A+\beta B+\gamma C
$$

Then the three linear relations are written:

$$
U(M)=\alpha U(A)+\beta U(B)+\gamma U(C)
$$

In total, if the cloud comprises $n\ge 3$ nodes, one writes:

- Three quadratic relations (easily linearisable);
- $3(n-3)$ linear relations.

The cloud had $\mathrm{3n}$ degrees of freedom. $3n-6$ independent relations have been written. It has six degrees of freedom left, which corresponds to the number of possible motions for a 3D solid.

Notes:

- For the “segment” case in 3D, for example, one writes $\mathrm{3n}-5$ relations, which means that the solid has only five possible motions, which is normal because the rotation of the solid about the straight line is indeterminate;
- Each solid generates only a few non-linear relations (six at most).
