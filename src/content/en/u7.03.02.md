# u7.03.02 Macro-command MACR_INFO_MAIL

## 1 Purpose

Provide information on the quality of a mesh.

This macro-command makes it possible to obtain information on a mesh, of degree 1 or 2, containing point mesh cells, segments, triangles, quadrangles, tetrahedra, hexahedra or pentahedra. The printout is directed to the message file.

One first finds an overall summary of the mesh: dimension, degree, number of nodes, number of mesh cells, extreme dimensions.

Then six items of information are available:

- a summary of the quality of the mesh cells,
- a summary of the diameter of the mesh cells,
- a check of the non-interpenetration of the mesh cells,
- a summary of the connectivity of the mesh
- a summary of the size of the various subdomains
- the number of over-constrained elements

This analysis is performed by the mesh-adaptation software HOMARD. For further details, see: [https://www.code-aster.org/outils/homard](https://www.code-aster.org/outils/homard) or refer to the documents cited in the bibliography.

    ◇ QUALITE = / "OUI" (par défaut),

                  / "NON",
    ◇ TAILLE = / "OUI" (par défaut),
               / "NON",

                         / "NON" (par défaut),
    ◇ PROP_CALCUL = / "OUI" (par défaut),
                    / "NON",
    ◇ DIAMETRE = / "OUI" (par défaut),
                 / "NON",
    ◇ LANGUE = / "FRANCAIS" (par défaut),
               / "FRENCH",
               / "ANGLAIS",

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operand MAILLAGE

```text
♦    MAILLAGE = ma
```

The mesh of type [maillage] to be analysed. Caution: the analysis will cover only the nodes, the point mesh cells, the segments, the triangles, the quadrangles, the tetrahedra, the hexahedra or the pentahedra. If one supplies a mesh containing other mesh cells, for example pyramids, two cases are possible: either an error stop, or information on the authorised zone, the rest of the mesh being ignored. The choice between these two modes of operation is made by the keyword ELEMENTS_NON_HOMARD.

The mesh is of degree 1 or 2, but it is not possible to mix the two.

In all cases, the presence of enriched HEXA27 mesh cells is forbidden.

### 3.2 Operand NOMBRE

```text
◊    NOMBRE=    /'OUI'                                                    [DEFAUT]
                /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a summary of the numbers of nodes and of mesh cells is printed to the message file.

### 3.3 Operand QUALITE

```text
◊    QUALITE=    /'OUI'                                                    [DEFAUT]
                 /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a summary of the quality of the mesh cells is printed to the message file.

The quality of a triangle is defined as the ratio between the length of the longest side and the radius of the inscribed circle.

The quality of a quadrangle is defined as the quotient of the product of the greatest length and of the averages over the sides and the diagonals by the smallest of the areas of the triangles internal to the quadrangles.

Likewise, the quality of a tetrahedron is defined as the ratio between the length of the longest side and the radius of the inscribed sphere.

The quality of a hexahedron is defined as the worst of the qualities of the inscribed tetrahedra.

All these quality measures are normalised so as to equal 1 in the case of an equilateral triangle, a square, an equilateral tetrahedron or a cube. For any non-equilateral mesh cell, the quality is greater than 1. See reference [bib1]_ for detailed explanations.

The result is presented in the form of tables, with the extreme values.

The interpretation of the values produced depends on the numerical method employed for the analysis. Depending on whether the problem is isotropic or not, on the rapidity of the spatial variation of the data, and on the analysis technique, one and the same mesh cell may or may not lead to a good Jacobian. The essential point, in a first stage, is to identify the frankly bad mesh cells. If one observes that the maximum quality exceeds 100, or even 1,000 or 100,000, one must be concerned: one or more mesh cells are very distorted and the mesh certainly needs to be redone. In a second stage, this quality information should make it possible to compare two a priori correct meshes, without a large value. If the problem is isotropic, it will be advantageous to use the mesh with the quality distribution closest to 1.

Illustrations of quality values for various mesh cells will be found in [bib2]_.

### 3.4 Operand DIAMETRE

```text
◊    DIAMETRE=    /'OUI'                                                [DEFAUT]
                  /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a summary of the diameters of the mesh cells is printed to the message file.

The diameter of a mesh cell is defined as the length of the longest segment that it is possible to insert into the mesh cell.

For a triangle or a tetrahedron, the diameter corresponds to the length of the longest side.

For a quadrangle, a hexahedron, a pentahedron or a pyramid, the diameter is the maximum between the length of the longest side and the length of the longest diagonal.

The result is presented in the form of tables, with the extreme values.

### 3.5 Operand CONNEXITE

```text
◊    CONNEXITE=    /'OUI'                                                [DEFAUT]
                   /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a summary of the connectivities is printed to the message file. One will then know whether the segments, the 2D mesh cells (triangles and quadrangles taken together) or the 3D mesh cells (tetrahedra, hexahedra, pentahedra and pyramids taken together) are in a single piece or distributed over several blocks. One will also know the number of holes in the structure: the through-holes or the internal holes.

### 3.6 Operand TAILLE

```text
◊    TAILLE=    /'OUI'                                                    [DEFAUT]
                /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a summary of the sizes of the subdomains is printed to the message file. A subdomain is defined as a set of mesh cells of the same dimension and belonging to the same groups.

### 3.7 Operand PROP_CALCUL

```text
◊    PROP_CALCUL=    /'OUI'                                                [DEFAUT]
                     /'NON'
```

If the choice is 'OUI', a diagnostic on the properties of the mesh cells as elements for the analysis is printed to the message file. One counts the number of over-constrained elements: the elements all of whose vertices lie on the boundary. One counts the volume mesh cells (respectively surface mesh cells) which touch the boundary of the domain but which are not bordered by surface mesh cells (respectively line mesh cells).

### 3.8 Operand INTERPENETRATION

```text
◊    INTERPENETRATION=        /'OUI'
                              /'NON'                                    [DEFAUT]
```

If the choice is 'OUI', one checks that the mesh is correct from the point of view of overlap: no mesh cell has one of its vertices inside another mesh cell.

Caution: this operation may prove costly for large meshes, hence the default choice ‘NON’.

### 3.9 Operand MAILLAGE_FRONTIERE

```text
◊    MAILLAGE_FRONTIERE=maf
```

The choice of this option means that the segments forming the boundary of the analysis mesh or an internal boundary are attached to a fine description of this boundary. This fine mesh is transmitted here. The link is made by the segments belonging to the same groups.

### 3.10 Operand GROUP_MA_FRONT

```text
◊    GROUP_MA_FRONT=l_grma
```

If this option is absent, the link between the segments is made for all the groups present in the boundary mesh. If the link is made only for a few groups, they are designated here.

### 3.11 Operand ELEMENTS_ACCEPTES

```text
◊    ELEMENTS_ACCEPTES=    /'HOMARD'                                    [DEFAUT]
                           /'IGNORE_PYRA'
```

In its current version, HOMARD only applies the information to certain mesh cells: point mesh cells, segments, triangles, quadrangles, tetrahedra, hexahedra, pentahedra of degree 1 or 2.

By retaining the option 'HOMARD', the transmission of a mesh containing something other than these types of mesh cells will lead to an error stop. This is the default option.

By choosing the option 'IGNORE_PYRA', one will be able to analyse a mesh containing pyramids. The information will cover only the zones authorised by HOMARD; the rest of the mesh will be ignored.

In all cases, the presence of enriched HEXA27 mesh cells is forbidden.

### 3.12 Operand VERSION_HOMARD

```text
◊    VERSION_HOMARD=    /'V11_10'                        [DEFAUT]
                        /'V11_N'
                        /'V11_N_PERSO'
```

This operand makes it possible to select the version of HOMARD which is used for the adaptation. By default, HOMARD 11.10 is launched. This is the reference version. The choice 'V11_N' activates version 11.n of HOMARD, which is the development version. The choice 'V11_N_PERSO' activates a development version specific to the user. This option allows the HOMARD development team to develop new functionalities. It also allows the user to benefit from an innovation in HOMARD before it is put into service in *Code_Aster*.

### 3.13 Operand LANGUE

```text
◊    LANGUE=        /'FRANCAIS'                    [DEFAUT]
                    /'FRENCH'
                    /'ANGLAIS'
                    /'ENGLISH'
```

This operand specifies the language in which the messages issued by HOMARD are printed.

### 3.14 Operand LOGICIEL

```text
◊    LOGICIEL=    logiciel                [K]
```

This option proposes using a coupling interface between *Code_Aster* and HOMARD other than the one supplied by default in the directory of the tools associated with *Code_Aster*. This option is in fact reserved for the HOMARD development team in order to develop new functionalities. It makes it possible to test new features before having modified the driving macro-command.

### 3.15 Operand UNITE

```text
◊    UNITE=    unite                [I]
```

This option is possible only if one has activated the development version of HOMARD, 11.n. The data file transmitted by the user under this logical unit number will be transmitted directly as a complement to the HOMARD configuration file. This option is in fact reserved for the HOMARD development team in order to develop new functionalities. It makes it possible to test new features before having modified the driving macro-command.

### 3.16 Operand INFO

```text
◊    INFO=    /1
              /2
              /3
              /4
```

If INFO is equal to 1, the printouts are minimal; one obtains only those which have explicitly been requested, the quality of the mesh cells for example, and any error messages.

If INFO is equal to 2, one will obtain the messages issued by the commands underlying the macro-command: IMPR_RESU, LIRE_MAILLAGE, LIRE_RESU.

If INFO is equal to 3, one will have the standard messages of HOMARD, summarising the execution.

If INFO is equal to 4, one will have all the messages issued by HOMARD, for debugging purposes.

## 4 Example

The connectivity check makes it possible to ensure that the analysis domain is in a single piece. It may happen that this domain is in several disjoint parts, for example for contact problems. But the diagnostic of several pieces often reveals a problem during the creation of the mesh: sub-parts have been poorly rejoined. And since visual checking does not always make it possible to notice this, the analysis is wrong … while appearing to be right. The worst situation that can arise. It is therefore strongly recommended to use this option.

```text
CONNEXITE DES ENTITES DU CALCUL
===============================

**********************************************************
* Les triangles sont en un seul bloc.                    *
**********************************************************
**********************************************************
* Les segments sont en un seul bloc.                     *
* Cette ligne est fermee.                                *
**********************************************************
```

The display of the sizes of the sub-parts of the mesh is done by dimensions. The sorting takes place roughly according to the declared mesh-cell groups; there may be a finer subdivision when the groups overlap. This presentation makes it possible to check that one has indeed meshed what one wanted. In particular, it makes it possible to uncover errors of rejoining or of creation of groups during the creation of the mesh. Here again, it is strongly recommended to use this option.
