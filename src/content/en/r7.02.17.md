# r7.02.17 Determination of an equivalent crack from a damage field

**Summary:**

The command POST_ENDO_FISS carries out a post-processing on a result coming from continuum mechanics. The aim is to find the curve which represents the crack from a representative field, and the crack opening. The field may be the damage or another scalar field. On input, the command uses a concept evol_noli or cham_gd, containing the field to be post-processed. The latter must be a field at the nodes. On output, the command returns the mesh of the crack, together with a table containing the coordinates of the nodes of the crack and the value of the opening on each node. The procedure applies to 2D studies.

**Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The estimation of crack opening is important in many industrial applications, for example the evaluation of the permeability in structures whose function is to guarantee leaktightness.

The aim of POST_ENDO_FISS [U4.86.01](doc:u4.86.01) is to extract the crack opening from an analysis coming from continuum mechanics. The command is used as a post-processing and therefore does not burden the analysis itself. The method used is capable of finding the cracking path from the map of a (scalar) field $X$ which represents the cracking, and then of determining the opening on the crack.

The evaluation of the crack opening is therefore decomposed into two steps: first the automatic search for the crack path, then the extraction of the opening. For the moment, the command applies to 2D studies.

If damage models or other softening constitutive laws are used for the mechanical analysis, it is necessary, for a correct application of the command, that regularisation methods be employed: this will make it possible to obtain fields well distributed over several finite elements. Also, the regularisation methods will avoid any dependence of the results on the mesh.

## 2 Search for the cracking path

### 2.1 General points

One assumes that the crack belongs to a certain mesh-cell group (GROUP_MA), on which the field $X$ (damage, plastic strain) is defined. The crack is identified by a zone in which the values of this field are especially high, and normally greater than a certain value: damage greater than $0$, for example.

In what follows, the procedure for searching for the cracking path is described. We exploit the fact that, in the plane orthogonal to the crack, the field $X$ has a maximum on the crack itself. The procedure is step by step; a new point of the crack is found at each step. It can be described by the *typical step*, by its *initiation* and by the *stopping criteria*.

### 2.2 Typical step

At each step, the search requires two points. The last point found ${P}_{i}$ is the *starting point* and the vector $\overrightarrow{{P}_{i-1}{P}_{i}}$ (${P}_{i-1}$ being the last point but one found) gives the *search direction*.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.17/10000000000002EA000001A34D6CDC1A76A17F35.png)

Figure 1: diagram of the typical step

At each typical step, the following actions are carried out (see also the diagram of ) :

1. the position of the following point is first estimated (*prediction point* ${P}_{i}^{\mathit{pr}}$) at the distance $a$ from the starting point in the search direction, $a$ being the *advance step;*
2. the field $X$ is projected onto a line orthogonal to the search direction and passing through the prediction point;

1. the projected field is smoothed by convolution (**eq**) in order to free oneself partly from the mesh (for example, if one relies on finite elements with linear interpolation, the projected field is piecewise linear);
2. the new point of the crack ${P}_{i+1}$ is the one at which the smoothed field reaches its maximum value.

The smoothing mentioned at point 3 is necessary: for example, if the field projected onto the orthogonal profile is piecewise linear, its maximum is necessarily located on the edge of an element. The smoothing is carried out by convolution:

$\overline{X}(s)=\frac{{}_{{l}_{\mathit{orth}}}\intX(\zeta )\Psi (\mid \zeta -s\mid )d\zeta }{{}_{{l}_{\mathit{orth}}}\int\Psi (\mid \zeta -s\mid )d\zeta }$ **eq** 2.2-1

where $\overline{X}$ is the smoothed field, ${l}_{\mathit{orth}}$ is the length of the line orthogonal to the search direction, $s$ is the curvilinear abscissa on this line and $\Psi$ is a Gaussian function:

$\Psi (\mid \zeta -s\mid )=\exp(-{(\frac{2(\zeta -s)}{{l}_{\mathit{reg}}})}^{2})$ **eq** 2.2-2

An example of the fields $X$ and $\overline{X}$ as a function of the parameter ${l}_{\mathit{reg}}$ is given in the .

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.17/1000000000000242000001BB53FDCD79BE30BF60.tif)

Figure 2: Smoothing of the field $X$ projected onto the orthogonal line.

An additional parameter is given by the number of points on the orthogonal line, ${N}_{\mathit{orth}}$ . This determines the accuracy of the discrete convolution product, as well as the accuracy ${\delta}_{\mathit{orth}}={l}_{\mathit{orth}}/{N}_{\mathit{orth}}$ (distance between the points).

The discrete convolution is carried out by the function *convolve* of the Python library *numpy*. The functions $X(s)$ and $\Psi (s)$ are sampled on ${l}_{\mathit{orth}}$ at the constant distance ${\delta}_{\mathit{orth}}$ . Denoting by $\mathrm{X}$ , $\Psi$ the two sampled vectors, the point $\overline{{X}_{j}}$ of the vector $\overline{\mathrm{X}}$ which approximates by points the smoothed function $\overline{X}(s)$ will be given by:

$\overline{{X}_{j}}=\frac{\sum_{i}{\Psi}_{i}{X}_{i+j}}{\sum_{i}{\Psi}_{i}}$ **eq** 2.2-3

### 2.3 Initialisation

The aim of the initialisation is to obtain the first two points, in order to continue afterwards as described in the typical step. A diagram of the initialisation procedure is given in the :

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.17/1000000000000245000001CFE77C4B917749C01A.png)

Figure 3: diagram of the initialisation procedure.

In detail:

1. the field $X$ is projected onto a circle of radius $a$ and centred on the node $1$ where the field at the nodes has its maximum on the chosen GROUP_MA;
2. the field $X$ is smoothed on the circle via a discrete convolution product (**eq.**), the second point of the crack ${P}_{2}$ is the point of the circle where $\overline{X}$ is maximum;
3. one corrects the position of the point $1$ by projection and smoothing of the field $\overline{X}$ onto a line orthogonal to $\overrightarrow{{P}_{2}1}$ and passing through ${P}_{2}$: the new point $3={P}_{1}$ is found;
4. two possible search directions are thus determined: $\overrightarrow{{P}_{1}{P}_{2}}$ and $\overrightarrow{{P}_{2}{P}_{1}}$.

### 2.4 Stopping criteria

The search for the cracking path is carried out in the two directions $\overrightarrow{{P}_{1}{P}_{2}}$ and $\overrightarrow{{P}_{2}{P}_{1}}$ until stopping, in one of the following cases:

- the value of the field corresponding to the new point is lower than a threshold value, defined a priori. This value must be supplied among the parameters of the command under the operand BORNE_MIN of the factor keyword RECHERCHE.
- the prediction point is outside the material.

### 2.5 Fields for the search for the cracking path

The method described can be applied to any scalar fields which represent the cracking of the material. Typically, it is the damage $d$ which can be used.

It sometimes happens that the damage field is too “flat”, and that at failure it presents a plateau with $d=1$ . As there would not then be a well-defined maximum, the procedure described in paragraphs 2.2–2.3 might not give the desired results. One can then choose other relevant fields: a principal-strain component, an equivalent strain...

An example of the choice of the relevant field is given by the test cases zzzz264b,c [V1.01.264](doc:v1.01.264).

## 3 Crack opening

The crack opening is found by the displacement difference $\mathit{DN}$ in the direction normal to the cracking path, between two points ${P}_{g},{P}_{d}$ on this normal.

It is then necessary to determine:

- the line normal to the cracking path (taking into account the fact that the crack is known in a discrete way by points),
- the appearance of the displacements on this line, in particular the displacement component in the same direction,
- the coordinates of the two points ${P}_{g},{P}_{d}$ in order to carry out the displacement difference $\mathit{DN}$.

The **normal** is determined on each node of the crack as the bisector of the angle formed by the two normals to the segments which have the node in common (see ).

Once the normal $n$ has been determined on a node of the cracking path, one carries out a change of frame on the displacement field, from the global frame to the frame $(n,t)$ ($t$ is the tangent vector); in particular the component $\mathit{DN}$ is computed.

The points ${P}_{g},{P}_{d}$ are those for which the following relation is satisfied: $X\ge$ BORNE_MAX (BORNE_MAX is a parameter to be supplied as an operand under the keyword RECHERCHE). The projection of the field $X\ge$ onto the line normal to the path is therefore necessary for the determination of ${P}_{g},{P}_{d}$ (see ).

Normally, for the damage laws, the value $X=d=0.8$ is sufficient to capture the displacement jump (see [V1.01.264](doc:v1.01.264)).

## 4 Features and validation

### 4.1 Advice on the use of the parameters

In summary, for the search for the **cracking path** the necessary parameters are:

- ${l}_{\mathrm{orth}}$ (LONG_ORTH)
- ${l}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ (LONG_REG)
- $a$ (PAS)
- ${N}_{\mathit{orth}}$ (NB_POINT)
- BORNE_MIN

to be supplied under the factor keyword RECHERCHE. The recommended range of use is the following:

- ${l}_{\mathit{orth}}$ must be greater than or equal to the width of the damaged band; good results are already obtained by adopting twice this width;
- ${l}_{\mathrm{reg}}$ may lie in the interval $0.5\Delta \le {l}_{\mathit{reg}}\le 2\Delta$ , $\Delta$ being the size of the finite elements in the damaged zones (a compromise between sufficient smoothing and the risk of losing too much information from the unsmoothed damage profile);
- $a$ depends on the curvature of the path and on how many points the user wishes in order to describe the crack; as an indication one may also take it in an interval $0.5\Delta \le a\le 2\Delta$ ;
- the choice of ${N}_{\mathrm{orth}}$ must guarantee at least 10 points per finite element;
- BORNE_MIN is the value of the field for which the search is stopped in one direction; in the case of damage one takes values $>0.5$ in order to be sure that the crack is well formed.

As regards the computation of the crack opening, the parameter BORNE_MAX must be lower than 0.8 if the field $X$ is the damage.

### 4.2 Validation

The command is validated by the test case zzzz264 [V1.01.264](doc:v1.01.264).

In modelling A one searches for a cracking path and one compares it with an analytical path.

In modellings B and C, one searches for the cracking path and the opening on a mechanical result, obtained by using damage laws of *Code_Aster*.
