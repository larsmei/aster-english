# v8.21.102 ADLS102 - Meridian fluid-elastic oscillator

**Summary:**

The objective is to calculate the displacement of the piston of a "merid fluid-elastic oscillator".

It is a spring plunger coupled with a fluid contained in a rigid and fixed wall channel; the channel is crossed by a depressurization wave.

The plan problem of this meridian model is considered. This two-dimensional problem is reduced to a one-dimensional problem by considering by approximation that the transverse flow speeds induced by the piston movement are instantly transmitted in axial speeds.

Only one model is used. The mode calculation is in $u,p,\phi$ formulation.

Elements 2D is therefore used; these elements are based on mesh cells QUAD4 for fluid and piston, on mesh cells SEG2 for fluid and piston interface to account for fluid interaction structure (PHENOMENE=‘MECANIQUE

The non-wave return boundary conditions are made by Modelling a shock absorber piston at each end; the excitation is achieved by applying a depression to the inlet piston.

The fluid considered is water (chaud), the model schematizing fluid-structure interaction in the ring space between the core vessel and the core envelope during rapid depressurization.

An exact analytical solution exists. Its comparison with the results produced allows to validate the consideration of the fluid coupling structure in 2D.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The meridian fluid-elastic d-oscillator model schematizing fluid-structure interaction in the ring space of the core-bulletin is described below.

The meridian fluid elastic oscillator is a model of the reactor core ring-shaped space, consisting of an oscillator (lateral-spring track with a movable wall) coupled with a compressible fluid contained in a rigid and fixed wall channel.

The channel is crossed by a depressurization wave.

The [Figure] illustrates the model described.

![Figure](/figures/v8.21.102/Modele3D.png)

*Maridian fluid-elastic oscillator global scheme*

The channel is rectangular cross section $e \times H$ in size and the wall piston is rigid and is only moving on $z$ perpendicular to a wall.

A wave of depressurization arrives from the left; by going to the right (without possibility of return) this wave sucks the piston which, by its resulting displacement, generates waves propagating towards the ends of the conduit, supposedly infinitely long so that there is no reflection.

A two-dimensional model of this system, as shown in [Figure], is being designed.

![Figure](/figures/v8.21.102/Modele2D.svg)

*Theoretical two-dimensional mechanical representation*

Its geometrical characteristics are as follows:

- length of the wall piston: ${2L}=5,0 \, m$
- fluid height: $e=0,5 \,  m$
- fluid width: $H=1,0 \,  m$
- fluid section: $S=e \times H$

### 1.2 Material properties

The physical characteristics of the fluid material (hot water) in the tube are as follows:

- density: ${\rho}_{f}=0,75 \times {10}^{3} \, {kg}/{m}^{3}$ ,
- speed of sound: ${c}_{f}=1,0 \times {10}^{3} \, m/s$ .

The physical characteristics of the materials constituting the wall piston and the end pistons are only a formal role in the calculation, as the structure (the channel) is considered to be perfectly rigid.

These physical characteristics of material are as follows:

- Young's modulus $E=2,0 \times {10}^{12} \,{Pa}$ ,
- Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0,3$ ,
- density ${\rho}_{s}=0\, {kg}/{m}^{3}$ .

### 1.3 Characteristics of springs, masses and shock absorbers

The characteristics of the wall plunger as an oscillator are as follows:

- stiffness: $K=5,0 \times {10}^{10} \, N/m$
- Mass: $M=200,0 \times {10}^{3} \, {kg}$
- Depreciation: $A = 0 \ {Ns}/m$

The characteristics of the end pistons as oscillators are as follows:

- stiffness: $k=0,0 \, N/m$
- Mass: $m=0,0 \, {kg}$
- Depreciation: $a={\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f} \, S = 37,5 \times {10}^{4} \, {Ns}/m$

### 1.4 Boundary conditions and loads

The wall piston is infinitely rigid and its displacement is only on the vertical axis.

The fluid length is infinite and there is no reflection of the end of the waves: this boundary condition is simulated in the model by a piston at each end, of zero mass, moving only on the axis of the $x$ and equipped with an adequate shock absorber with $a$ damping; these pistons are also infinitely rigid.

A total reflection of the waves is made on infinitely rigid walls of the fluid tube. This condition is realized simply by omitting to model the wall by elements structure.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The purpose is to determine the $z(t)$ time shift of the wall piston.

The plane problem of this meridian model is considered to be the geometric, mechanical and fluid characteristics of which are described in [Figure]; the lateral piston is ${2L}$ long.

The two-dimensional problem is reduced to a one-dimensional problem by considering by approximation that $\dot{z}$ transverse flow speeds induced by piston motion are instantly transmitted in axial speeds in the channel.

Under the wall plunger, in a volume of $dx$ extended control, one considers $d\Omega = e \, dx$. One can then write:

$$
d(\delta V)
=
\frac{1}{2e} \, d\dot{z} \, {dx}
=
\frac{1}{2e} \, \ddot{z} \, {dx} \, {dt}
$$

In the fluid, the velocity variation and the pressure variation in adiabatic evolution are connected by:

$$
d(\delta P)
=
{\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f} \, d(\delta V)
$$

The pressure at the instant $t$ in a point d-abscisse $x$ results from the superposition of the propagation of all the elementary sources distributed on the piston.

The coupling therefore consists in this: the movement of the $\ddot{z}(t)$ acceleration piston induces a pressure field $P(x,t)$ in the channel whose resulting stress on the extent of the piston itself acts in return on the dynamic of the oscillator.

The geometric, mechanical and fluid characteristics of the model are presented in [Figure].

The piston and fluid are first considered to be at rest and a release of the oscillator is made at the moment $t=0$ by imposing an initial speed.

The expression of the pressure in a channel point develops:

$$
P(x,t)
=
\frac{\rho_f \, c_f}{2 \, e}
\,
\int_{0}^{t}
{
\left[
    \int_{-L}^{x}
    {
        \ddot{z}
        \left(
            \tau
            -
            \frac{\vert x - u \vert}{c_f}
        \right)
        du
    }
    +
    \int_{x}^{L}
    {
        \ddot{z}
        \left(
            \tau
            -
            \frac{\vert u - x \vert}{c_f}
        \right)
        du
    }
    \,
    d\tau
\right]
}
$$

$z(0)=0$ and $z(t)=0$ are available for negative $t$ and $z\left(-\frac{L-x}{{c}_{f}}\right)=0$ since upstream of the piston (i.e. $x$ between $-L$ and $L$) the amount in brackets is still negative. Similarly, $z\left(-\frac{L+x}{{c}_{f}}\right)=0$ is available.

Finally, he comes:

$$
P(x,t)
=
\frac{\rho_f \, c_f^2}{2 \, e}
\left[
    2z(t)
    -
    z \left( t-\frac{L-x}{{c}_{f}} \right)
    -
    z \left( t-\frac{L+x}{{c}_{f}} \right)
\right]
$$

This expression is incorporated on $x$ to obtain the result of the pressure forces on the piston:

$$
R(t)
=
-H \, {\int}_{-L}^{+L} {P(x,t)\, {dx} }
=
-2H \, {\int}_{0}^{+L} {P(x,t)\, {dx} }
$$

$P(x,t)$ is paired in $x$, so just integrate on half the piston.

Hence the expression of the resultant pressure forces on the piston in the hypothesis of small movements:

$$
R(t)
=
- \frac{H \, \rho_f \, c_f^2}{e}
\,
\left[
    2 \, L \, z(t)
    -
    c_f
    \,
    \int_{t-\frac{2\, L}{c_f}}^{t}
    {
        z(u)
        \,
        du
    }
\right]
$$

The movement of the oscillator therefore follows the equation:

$$
M\ddot{z}
+
Kz
+
\frac{2 \, H \, L \, {\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f}^{2}}{e} z
-
\frac{H \, {\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f}^{3}}{e}
\, \int_{t-\frac{2\, L}{c_f}}^{t} { z(u) \, du }
=
0
$$

or:

$$
M\ddot{z}
-
{F}_{int}
-
{F}_{cpl}
=
0
$$

if we put it down

$$
{F}_{int}
=
-
Kz
-
\frac{2 \, H \, L \, {\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f}^{2}}{e} z
$$

et

$$
{F}_{cpl}
=
\frac{H \, {\rho}_{f} \, {c}_{f}^{3}}{e}
\, \int_{t-\frac{2\, L}{c_f}}^{t} { z(u) \, du }
$$

The case of the propagation of a steep front decompression wave $\Delta {P}_{0}$ along the duct is now considered. At the moment $t=0$, this wave attacks the wall plunger still at rest, creating an excitation force such as:

$$
{F}_{exc}
=
\left \lbrace
\begin{array}{ccc}
    H \, {c}_{f} \, t \, \Delta {P}_{0} & \text{si}& t < \frac{2L}{{c}_{f}}\\
    2 \, H \, L \, \Delta {P}_{0} & \text{si}& t \ge \frac{2L}{{c}_{f}}
\end{array}
\right .
$$

The equation of motion then reads:

$$
M\ddot{z}
=
{F}_{int}
+
{F}_{cpl}
+
{F}_{exc}
$$

This equation is solved numerically for the presented characteristics of the meridian l-oscillator.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

#### 3.1.1 Vibro-acoustic system equivalent to model

In order to avoid the return waves from the ends of a necessarily finite dimension Modelling these ends are equipped with "piston-absorbent" systems as on the [Figure]. The exciter piston is of zero mass and is added to an $a$ shock absorber. The anechoic piston is of zero mass, with a damping of $a$ value.
The wall piston is modeled with $M$ mass and $K$ rigidity, without damping.

The channel is modelled over a total length of $28 \, m$ sufficient to obtain with certainty, at least the first two *extrema* of the piston displacement curve without disturbance of a reflection wave at the ends.

![Figure](/figures/v8.21.102/systemeEquivalent.png)

*Equivalent Vibro-acoustic System*

We chose to model in 2D.

The mesh of [Figure] consists of 2870 QUAD4 cells and 3164 nodes cells. The three pistons are represented by a single layer of cells.

### 3.3 Calculation

The elements of fluid-structure interaction in transient state is validated by excitation loading. The displacement of the wall piston is calculated with the [DYNA_VIBRA](doc:u4.53.03) operator. The $17 \, MPa$ pressure is applied to the piston.

**Note:**

To validate the loads on the fluid-structure part, in this Modelling the pressure is applied to the interface and not to the structure. The structure being assumed infinitely rigid, it is strictly equivalent.
