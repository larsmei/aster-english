# v8.21.101 ADLV101 - Balking and acoustic modes of a water-filled parallelepipedic tank

**Summary:**

This test, from the acoustic and fluid domain, is concerned with the search for the eigenfrequencies of a compressible fluid domain, including a free surface, contained in a rigid parallelepipedic tank. The modal analysis thus determines the first modes of stowage (in the presence of gravity) of the free surface of the fluid filling the tank, then the first acoustic modes.

The first eight eigenfrequencies of stowage are found at less than 1% of the analytically calculated frequencies. The first two sound eigenfrequencies are found at less than 1% of the analytically calculated frequencies.

The free surface subject to the action of the gravity is affected by linear modelling of (serious) sluggishing waves to the first order of AIRY (or STOKES) via elements finished $(P,\Phi ,z)$. ## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference [bib3] provides the general formula for the stowing modes of a compressible fluid domain comprising a free surface, contained in a rigid walled parallelepipedic tank:

${f}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2\pi }\sqrt{\pi g\sqrt{\frac{{i}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}+\frac{{j}^{2}}{{l}^{2}}}\tanh\left[\pi H\sqrt{\frac{{i}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}+\frac{{j}^{2}}{{l}^{2}}}\right]}$

where $i$ and $j$ are the whole orders of the longitudinal and transverse modes (number of nodal lines in each horizontal direction). Fluctuation pressure modes have an expression of the shape:

${p}_{ij}(x,y,z)=\cos\left(i\pi \frac{2x+l}{2l}\right)\cos\left(j\pi \frac{2y+L}{2L}\right)\left(\cosh\left({\lambda}_{ij}(H-z)\right)+{A}_{ij}\sinh\left({\lambda}_{ij}(H-z)\right)\right)$

where ${\lambda}_{ij}$ is the vertical wave number checking: ${\lambda}_{ij}H\tanh\left({\lambda}_{ij}H\right)=4{\pi}^{2}{f}_{ij}H/g$ and with a checking coefficient: ${A}_{ij}=-\tanh\left({\lambda}_{ij}H\right)$ . Flushing pressures of slurry are maximum in free surface. The height of the free surface level is expressed by: ${z}_{l}(x,y)={p}_{ij}(x,y,0)/(\rho .g)$ .

In the particular case where $\frac{L}{l}$ is large, the formula simplifies for longitudinal modes [bib1], [bib2].

The reference [bib3] also provides the general formula for acoustic modes of a compressible fluid domain comprising a free surface contained in a rigid walled parallelepipedic tank:

${f}_{\mathit{ijk}}=\frac{c}{2}\sqrt{\frac{{i}^{2}}{{L}^{2}}+\frac{{j}^{2}}{{l}^{2}}+\frac{{(2k+1)}^{2}}{4{h}^{2}}}$

where $i$ , $j$ and $k$ are the entire orders of longitudinal, transverse and vertical modes (number of nodal lines in each horizontal direction and vertical direction).

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v8.21.101/100011F200002A6000001565D60579CF322E003C.svg)

Two Modelling options are performed:

- $(p,\Phi ,z)$ free surface Modelling with57 elements MEFP_FACE4(modelling 2D_FLUI_PESA on the mesh-cell group MASURF) quadrangles at 4 nodes while the fluid volume is modeled with 513 elements of $(p,\Phi )$ fluid (modelling 3D_FLUIDE on the mesh-cell group MAFLUIDE) cubic at 8 nodes. This Modelling gives access to the sluggish modes and acoustic modes;
- A fluid volume Modelling that is modelled by 513 elements of $(p,\Phi )$ fluid (3D_FLUIDE Modelling on mesh-cell group MAFLUIDE) cubic to 8 nodes, while the free surface (node group: NOSURF) is affected by a Dirichlet condition: PRES=0.0, PHI=0.0. This Dirichlet condition is performed successively in two ways: by dualization (AFFE_CHAR_MECA(DDL_IMPO)), then by elimination (AFFE_CHAR_CINE(MECA_IMPO)). This Modelling gives access to the only acoustic modes.

In both cases, no boundary condition is affected on the side walls and the bottom: it is a natural boundary condition: $\vec{\nabla p}.\vec{n}=0$ .

## 9 Summary of the results

Modelling A implements elements of $(p,\Phi )$ fluid and heavy free surface in $(p,\Phi ,z)$ formulation, mesh cells of Hexa8 type and elements of quad4 free surface. The result shows a maximum error of 1.51% compared to analytical solution of the eigenfrequencies of the slurry modes and acoustic modes.

Model B involves elements fluid in $(p,\Phi ,z)$ formulation, mesh cells type Hexa20 and elements free surface on squad8 meshes. The eigenfrequency results of the spooling modes are perfectly consistent with the analytical solution.

Fluid elements in $(p,\Phi ,z)$ formulation, mesh cells in Hexa27 and Quad9 heavy fluid are used for C Modelling. The eigenfrequency results of the stowage modes are perfectly consistent with the analytical solution.

The D Modelling allows the validation of fluid elements in $(p,\Phi ,z)$ formulation, on mesh cells of Penta6 type and elements of free surface on Tria3 meshes. The results of the eigenfrequencies of the sling modes show a relative error of 1.479% compared to the reference solution.

The E Modelling allows the validation of the free surface elements of the Tria6 type. They are connected to elements fluid on Penta15 meshes. The eigenfrequency results of the stowage modes match perfectly with the analytical solution.

The F Modelling allows the elements linear acoustic fluid (degrees of freedom pressure $p$) to be validated on Hexa8 meshes. There are perfect coincidences of the results with the analytical solution (maximum error less than 1.2% for this mesh).

Generally speaking, the results obtained by quadratic interpolation elements (HEXA20, HEXA27) are in excellent agreement with the analytical results. Nevertheless, linear interpolation elements have an acceptable accuracy on the results, which is consistent with the current mesh cells elements number recommendations.
