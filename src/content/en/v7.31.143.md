# v7.31.143 WTNV143 - Application of a distributed pressure on the lips of a XFEM crack in a hydro-mechanical model

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to ensure the proper functioning of the extended elements finite (XFEM) method in a hydromechanical model, in order to represent a fractured saturated medium.

In this test, it is assumed that pore pressure is taken uniformly zero throughout the field of study: the hydro-mechanical model then corresponds to a "classical" mechanical model without coupling. It is checked that there is a discontinuity of the displacement field on either side of the interface.The results are compared to an analytical solution and the results obtained with a mechanical XFEM test (similar to ssnv203, modellings F and H tests).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is an analytical solution. Taking into account the boundary conditions, the displacements can be obtained from the analytical resolution of the motion quantity conservation equation.

By neglecting the gravity, the equation is written (in total constraints):

$\text{Div}(\sigma )+{r}_{0}g=0$

In the case of coupled modelling, the total stress tensor is written:

$\sigma =\sigma '-{p}_{1}1$

$\sigma '$ is the stress tensor in the skeleton and ${p}_{1}$ and the pore pressure in the solid. The module of Poisson $\nu$ being zero, and being in the elastic case, we have $\sigma '=Eϵ$.

$\forall y{p}_{1}=0$ gold so finally $E\ast \text{Div}({\varepsilon})+{r}_{0}g=0$

In the 2D case, $\nu$ being zero, boundary conditions and loading render the problem one-dimensional according to $y$. Only ${ϵ}_{yy}$ is non-zero and:

$E\ast \frac{\partial {{\varepsilon}}_{yy}}{\partial y}-{r}_{0}g=0$ is $\frac{{\partial}^{2}{u}_{y}}{\partial {y}^{2}}=\frac{{r}_{0}}{E}g$ and therefore finally ${u}_{y}(y)=\frac{{r}_{0}g}{2E}{y}^{2}+\mathit{Ay}+B$

with $A$ and $B$ of the integration constants to be determined. The problem is solved separately in each of the two subblocks. To determine the integration constants, the boundary condition is used on the ${u}_{y}=0$ moves at the lower and upper ends of the column and the Neumann ${\sigma}_{yy}=-p$ condition at the interface level.

Finally, the $y$ direction is moved on either side of the interface:

- moving over the $y=\frac{{\mathit{LY}}^{+}}{2}$ interface is written ${u}_{y}\left(\frac{{\mathit{LY}}^{+}}{2}\right)=\frac{p}{E}\left(\frac{\mathit{LY}}{2}\right)-\frac{{r}_{0}g}{2E}{\left(\frac{\mathit{LY}}{2}\right)}^{2}$
- the movements below the $y=\frac{{\mathit{LY}}^{-}}{2}$ interface are written ${u}_{y}\left(\frac{{\mathit{LY}}^{-}}{2}\right)=-\frac{p}{E}\left(\frac{\mathit{LY}}{2}\right)-\frac{{r}_{0}g}{2E}{\left(\frac{\mathit{LY}}{2}\right)}^{2}$

In the 3D case, the reference solution is strictly the same:

- moving over the $z=\frac{{\mathit{LZ}}^{+}}{2}$ interface is written ${u}_{z}\left(\frac{{\mathit{LZ}}^{+}}{2}\right)=\frac{p}{E}\left(\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}\right)-\frac{{r}_{0}g}{2E}{\left(\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}\right)}^{2}$
- the movements below the $z=\frac{{\mathit{LZ}}^{-}}{2}$ interface are written ${u}_{z}\left(\frac{{\mathit{LZ}}^{-}}{2}\right)=-\frac{p}{E}\left(\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}\right)-\frac{{r}_{0}g}{2E}{\left(\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}\right)}^{2}$
- the following $x$ and $y$ moves are zero.

## 9 Summary of the results

For each of the finished elements HM-XFEM tested, the results are consistent with the analytical solution. For the elements HM-XFEM, the following features are now validated:

- MODI_MODELE_XFEM,
- application of distributed pressure,
- POST_CHAM_XFEM,
- the calculation of the elementary matrices and vectors in the case where the mechanical degrees of freedom Heaviside were introduced into the development of the HM coupling model.
