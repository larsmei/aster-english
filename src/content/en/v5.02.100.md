# v5.02.100 SDNL100 - Single pen in large oscillation

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to calculate the movement of a heavy bar articulated at a fixed point by one of its ends, free elsewhere and oscillating with great amplitude in a vertical plane.

Interest: Test the element two-way cable nodes - which is actually a element bar - dynamically and how it works in the DYNA_NON_LINE operator.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v5.02.100/100005F4000014770000130422CBDAA539531E59.svg)

A rigid $\mathit{OP}$ pendulum of length 1 and centre of gravity $G$ oscillates around the point $O$ .

The angular position of the pendulum is marked by: $\alpha =\theta -\pi$

### 1.2 Material properties

Linear mass of pendulum: $1.\mathrm{kg}/m$

Axial Rigidity (Product of Young's modulus by the area of the right section): ${1.10}^{8}N$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The pendulum is articulated to the point fixed $O$ . Under the action of the gravity, its $P$ end oscillates on the $(\Gamma )$ semi-circle of the $O$ centre and $1$ radius . There is no friction.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The pendulum is released without speed from the horizontal position $\mathrm{OP}$ .

$\theta =+\frac{\pi}{2}$ , $\dot{\theta}=0$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The $T$ period of a mobile pendulum without friction around the fixed point $O$ , whose mass is concentrated at the centre of gravity $G$ ($\mathrm{OG}=l$ ) and whose maximum angular amplitude is ${\theta}_{0}$ is given by the [bib1] series:

$T=2\pi \sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}\left[1+\underset{n=1}{\overset{\infty}{\Sigma}}{a}_{n}^{2}{(\sin\frac{{\theta}_{0}}{2})}^{\mathrm{2n}}\right]$

with

${a}_{n}=\frac{\mathrm{2n}-1}{\mathrm{2n}}$

### 2.2 Baseline

For $l=0.5m$ , $g=9.81m/{s}^{2}$ and ${\theta}_{0}=\pi /2$ , you can find : $T=1.6744s$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

The terms of the series were ordered up to $n=12$ inclusive, the last term taken into account being less than ${10}^{-5}$ times the calculated sum.

### 2.4 Bibliographical references

  1. HAAG, "Vibrating Movements", P.U.F. (1952).

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

The pendulum is modeled by a element cable with 2 nodes, identical to a element constant section bar.

Discretization:

- space: element cable MECABL2
- Time: analysis of the motion over a complete $T$ by time step period equal to $T/40$.

### 4.2 Remarks

- The time integration is done by the NEWMARK method (trapezian rule),
- At each time step, convergence is achieved in less than 9 iterations.

## 5 Summary of the results

It is apparent from this test case that the time integration by the "trapezeze rule" of Newmark only slightly modifies the frequency and does not provide parasitic damping, since after a period we return to the initial position.
