# v7.30.100 WTNL100 - Consolidation of a saturated poro-elastic soil column (Terzaghi)

**Summary:**

This test case is about consolidating a column of saturated poro-elastic soil, watertight laterally and at the base, subjected to a force step in the head. The objective is to test the displacements (scratch of the ground), the pressures and compare the total results with an analytical solution, the outline of which is presented.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This one-dimensional test case is a product of the literature and has a analytical solution [1]. It is inspired by the problem of consolidation of a saturated porous soil of water at room temperature (Terzaghi problem). Water is assumed to be incompressible ($1/{K}_{\mathit{lq}}=0,0$). Temperature is uniform. In this case, the action of the gravity is neglected. The coupled problem of saturated poro-mechanical is described by the variables of ${u}_{y}$ displacement (soil stowage) and ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}$ fluid pressure (the hydraulic load being ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}/{\rho}_{\mathrm{eau}}$). In order to obtain a purely one-dimensional solution, the Poisson's ratio is chosen equal to ${\nu}_{0}=0,0$. The dimensions are as follows: $L=1,00m$ , $H=10,00m$ .

### 1.2 Boundary conditions and loads

#### 1.2.1 boundary conditions

The interstitial fluid pressure remains zero on the entire top surface $\mathit{CD}$: ${P}_{\mathit{lq}}=0$. The side faces have blocked movements in $x$. The bottom surface $\mathit{AB}$ has blocked movements in $x$ and $y$, and it is waterproof: ${P}_{\mathrm{lq},y}=0$

#### 1.2.2 Initial conditions

The column is initially resting in a virgin state: ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}=0$ , ${\sigma}_{yy}=0$ .

#### 1.2.3 Loading

It is exercised a ${F}_{0}=-1,0\mathrm{Pa}$ pressure step on the top side CD to $t=\mathrm{0s}$ .

The gravity is neglected here.

## 2 reference solution

The total poro-elastic stresses are: $\sigma ={E}_{0}\varepsilon (u)-{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq}}$ . Only the vertical component is present: ${\sigma}_{yy}(y,t)={E}_{0}{u}_{y,y}(y,t)-{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,t)$ .

The poro-elastic hydromechanical equilibrium $\mathrm{1D}$ is therefore written, in the absence of gravity force, for $t\ge 0$:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{E}_{0}{u}_{y,yy}(y,t)-{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq},y}(y,t)=0,\mathrm{équilibre }\mathrm{mécanique}\\ {\lambda}_{\mathrm{lq}}^{H}{P}_{\mathrm{lq},yy}(y,t)-b{\dot{u}}_{y,y}(y,t)=0,\mathrm{équilibre }\mathrm{hydraulique}\end{array}$

with the initial conditions: ${u}_{y}(y,0)=0$ , ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,0)=0$ and boundary conditions for $t>0$ : ${u}_{y}(0,t)=0$ , ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(0,t)=0$ , ${\sigma}_{yy}(H,t)={F}_{0}\eta (t)={E}_{0}{u}_{y,y}(H,t)-{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq}}(H,t)$ where $\eta (t)$ is the step function in $t=0$ (Heaviside).

The mechanical balance gives directly the total stress uniformity for $t>0$ on $∣0,H∣$ , where: ${\sigma}_{yy}(y,t)={F}_{0}={E}_{0}{u}_{y,y}(y,t)-{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,t)$ , i.e. ${u}_{y,y}(y,t)=\frac{1}{{E}_{0}}({F}_{0}+{\mathrm{bP}}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,t))$ , for $t>0$ on $∣0,H∣$ .

The hydraulic balance then leads to:

$\frac{{b}^{2}}{{E}_{0}}{\dot{P}}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,t)-{\lambda}_{\mathrm{lq}}^{H}{P}_{\mathrm{lq},yy}(y,t)=0$ , for $t>0$ on $∣0,H∣$

with as initial ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,0)=-\frac{{F}_{0}}{b}$ on $∣0,H∣$, and two boundary conditions ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(H,t)=0$ and ${P}_{\mathrm{lq},y}(0,t)=0$ for $t>0$ , i.e. a thermal shock problem on $∣0,H∣$ .

The ${c}_{\nu}={\lambda}_{\mathrm{lq}}^{H}{E}_{0}/{b}^{2}$ consolidation coefficient is valid here: ${c}_{\nu}=0,1{m}^{2}/s$ . It controls the length of the consolidation process.

This results in a characteristic time ${\tau}_{c}={H}^{2}/{c}_{\nu}$ ($=\mathrm{1000s}$ here) used to identify the step of temporal discretization for the numerical method of integration. At this value ${\tau}_{c}$, it was reached a little over 90% of the consolidation.

The solution is, cf. [2]:

${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(y,t)=\frac{-4{F}_{0}}{\pi b}\sum_{m=1,2,3\mathrm{..}}^{+\infty }\frac{{(-1)}^{m-1}}{\mathrm{2m}-1}{e}^{-{\lambda}_{\mathrm{lq}}^{H}{E}_{0}{\pi}^{2}{(\mathrm{2m}-1)}^{2}\frac{t}{({\mathrm{4b}}^{2}{H}^{2})}}.\cos(\frac{\pi y(\mathrm{2m}-1)}{\mathrm{2H}})$

et : ${u}_{y}(y,t)=\frac{{F}_{0}y}{{E}_{0}}+\frac{b}{{E}_{0}}\underset{0}{\overset{y}{\int}}{P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(\xi ,t)d\xi$

either: ${u}_{y}(y,t)=\frac{{F}_{0}y}{{E}_{0}}-\frac{8H{F}_{0}}{{\pi}^{2}{E}_{0}}\sum_{m=1,2,3\mathrm{..}}^{+\infty }\frac{{(-1)}^{m-1}}{{(\mathrm{2m}-1)}^{2}}{e}^{-{\lambda}_{\mathrm{lq}}^{H}{E}_{0}{\pi}^{2}{(\mathrm{2m}-1)}^{2}\frac{t}{({\mathrm{4b}}^{2}{H}^{2})}}.\sin(\frac{\pi y(\mathrm{2m}-1)}{\mathrm{2H}})$

The actual stresses (acting on the skeleton) are: ${\sigma}_{yy}^{\mathrm{eff}}(y,t)={E}_{0}{u}_{y,y}(y,t)$ .For $t\to \infty$ moments we get: ${P}_{\mathrm{lq}}(0,\infty )=0$ and ${u}_{y}(H,\infty )=\frac{{F}_{0}H}{{{\rm E}}_{0}}$ (here $-{10}^{-6}m$ ).

The reference solution is an analytical.

### 2.2 Influence of modelling choice

In this paragraph, attention is drawn to a difficulty in numerical modelling related to this type of test (Terzaghi column). The following discussion is thus made of the influence of the Modelling chosen and is based in particular on calculations made with the Lagamine code (University of Liège) which has different elements from those of Code_Aster. For this reason, the test presented in this paragraph is derived from an external study and is not quantitatively the same as that studied in the rest of the document. It therefore does not give the same reference solutions. This paragraph therefore constitutes an informational supplement.

At the moment $t=0$ , the *solution is discontinuous* on the free surface $y=H$ :

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\frac{{P}_{\mathit{lq}}(y,0)}{{F}_{0}}=1& \forall y<H\\ {P}_{\mathit{lq}}(H,0)=0& \end{array}$

Because of this discontinuity, this test is a school case showing the phenomena of numerical oscillations of hydraulic pressure associated with the use of a mixed formulation by the elements method. These oscillations usually occur in the vicinity of a draining wall, and are due to:

- the violation of the inf-sup condition;
- Choosing a time step too small, violating the principle of maximum [:ref:` **R3.06.07**  < **R3.06.07** >`];

The choice of the finished elements type has a considerable influence on the solution obtained in the vicinity of the free surface around $t=0$. In Code_Aster, the selective Modelling* (HMS) eliminates these oscillations ( **Figure 2.2-a**), unlike conventional Modelling (HM).

However, obtaining the exact *solution for hydraulic pressure* ${P}_{\mathit{lq}}(y,0)$ by HMS modelling results in a greater approximation (compared to HM Modelling, see the case of mesh end of **Figure 2.2-b** ) for the *solution on vertical displacement* ${u}_{y}(y,0)$ . This approximation is all the more important as the mesh is more coarse around the free surface.

Finally, we note that the displacement calculated by the Lagamine code (University of Liège) is perfectly accurate and independent of the finesse of the mesh, but with in return a solution in hydraulic pressure very oscillating [:ref:` **HT66-05-012-A**  < **HT66-05-012-A** >`]. This is due to the type of finished elements used by Lagamine which are different from ours since it is a P2P2 with a 9PG/9PG quadrature. *This element does not check the inf-sup condition and therefore cannot give a correct solution in hydraulic pressure* .
