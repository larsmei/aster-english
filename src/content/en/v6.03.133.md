# v6.03.133 SSNP133 - Frustration of a hole plate with cohesive models

**Summary:**

This test allows to model the propagation of two cracks in an elastic hole plate.

$A$ Modelling: elements joint, CZM_LIN_REG cohesive law

$C$ Modelling: elements interface, CZM_OUV_MIX cohesive law

$D$ Modelling: elements joint, CZM_EXP_REG cohesive law

$E$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_EXP_REG cohesive law. mesh conforming to cracks.

$F$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_EXP_REG cohesive law. mesh triangles not compliant.

$G$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_EXP_REG cohesive law. mesh unstructured.

$H$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_LIN_REG cohesive law. mesh conforming to cracks.

$I$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_OUV_MIX cohesive law. mesh conforming to cracks.

$J$ Modelling: XFEM Modelling, CZM_OUV_MIX cohesive law. mesh not compliant.

The local numbering *ad hoc* of the cohesive elements (except $X-\mathrm{FEM}$) is ensured by the MODI_MAILLAGE command and the ORIE_FISSURE keyword. The sudden opening of cracks is ensured by the control of the load available for each of these laws (see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11)).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and loading

A square plate on the $2000\mathit{mm}$ side with a $R=500\mathit{mm}$ radius hole centred in the vertical direction and decentralized in the horizontal direction (see Figure 1.1-a). The plate allows a horizontal symmetry plane ($\mathit{AB}'$ ) passing through the centre of the hole. This symmetry can allow us to reduce our study to the upper half of the plate where possible.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.133/1000020000000161000001573796DB89694923A1.png)

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.133/Shape1.gif)

**Figure1.1-a: Diagram of the hole plate, boundary conditions and loading.**

The elements is a single ${F}^{i}$ load density, which is directed in the $Y$ direction on the upper part of the domain. The strength of this force will be given by the load control (see [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11)). In addition, symmetry conditions (zero displacements following $Y$) are imposed on the lower side of the elements cohesives. Finally, rigid body movements are blocked by imposing a zero displacement following $X$ on the point $P$ , the top of the hole.

For modellings E to J, we have an X-FEM formulation: it is then necessary to model the entire structure for the moment. An interface is introduced on the right $(AB')$ . It is recalled the approximation of the displacement field for nodes whose support is intersected by an XFEM interface :

${u}_{h}(x)=\sum_{i}{\phi }_{i}(x){a}_{i}+\sum_{i}{\phi }_{i}(x){b}_{i}H(\mathit{lsn}(x))$

In order to block rigid body movements, we want the interface to be a symmetry axis for the problem. If we consider ${M}^{+}$ and ${M}^{-}$ points immediately above and below the interface, we want ${u}_{y}({M}^{+})+{u}_{y}({M}^{-})=0$ .

For a compliant interface, this implies ${a}_{y}=0$ on the nodes interface (Figure ).

For a non-conform interface that intersects the edges in their middle, noting ${N}_{\text{SUP}}$ and ${N}_{\mathit{INF}}$ the nodes screws on either side of the crack (Figure ), this implies:

${a}_{y}({N}_{\text{SUP}})+{a}_{y}({N}_{\text{INF}})=0$

### 1.2 Material properties

The values of Young's modulus, Poisson's ratio, critical stress and tenacity of the material are selected as follows:

$\begin{array}{ccc}E=30000\mathit{MPa}& \nu =0.2& {\sigma}_{c}=0.2\mathit{MPa}\\ {G}_{c}=100\mathit{Pa.mm}& \text{}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\text{}& \text{problème symétrisé}\\ {G}_{c}=200\mathit{Pa.mm}& \text{}\mathrm{\Rightarrow }\text{}& \text{problème non symétrisé}\end{array}$

These are "tests" values that do not correspond to any particular material. The purpose of the choice of cohesive parameters is to obtain a solution with a relatively coarse mesh. It is recalled that to ensure a correct solution (precision and good convergence) with these models, it is necessary that there are several elements in the cohesive zone (see [U2.05.07](doc:u2.05.07) doc).

## 6 Modelling E

### 6.1 Modelling characteristics E

A $\left[Y=0\right]$ equation interface is introduced into the model using an XFEM formulation, via a normal level set. The simulation of crack propagation by brittle fracture is performed by a CZM_EXP_REG relationship between stress and displacement jump between the interface lips. Syntaxically, this relationship is included in the definition of a contact area between the two crack lips, with FPG3 or FPG2 integration and CZM formulation. This means that the contact is managed by cohesive law and there is no friction. Physically, we are then rigorously in the same situation as $D$ modelling. The elements density, in D_PLAN flat deformations, are elastic.

### 6.2 Mesh characteristics

A mesh of the crack-compliant hole plate is made.

Elements density ($\mathrm{DCB}$ ): 804 TRI3

## 12 Summary of the results

The cohesive models allow the qualitative simulation of the sudden spread of two cracks in fragile fracture through a hole plate. The overall responses of the seven models (in terms of force-moving) are substantially the same. The load control allows to follow the sudden rupture of the two ligaments on either side of the hole, leading to two "reverse returns" in the overall response.
