# v3.01.404 SSLL404 - Flaming of an arch

**Summary**

The field of application of this test is structural stability analysis. The structure studied is a arc bent by moments applied to both ends; it is modelled by elements straight beams. The purpose is to calculate the critical values of the moments.

The value of this test lies in the following aspects:

- calculation of a geometric rigidity matrix for elements POU_D_E.
- CALC_MODES modal methods test in stability
- presence of neighbouring own values

The calculated own loads are compared with analytically obtained values for a beam model d-Euler-Bernoulli.

In this test, the OPTION_INV='RAYLEIGH' option (under SOLVEUR_MODAL) of the CALC_MODES command is also valid.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

The beam is bi-supported. The section is prevented from twisting at the ends $A$ and $B$. To meet the assumptions of the theoretical model taken as reference, it is important that the moment is constant and that the normal effort is zero along the beam. Therefore, the $u$ displacement is left free on $X$ to point $B$. The boundary conditions are:

- Au point $A$ : $u=v=w=0$ ; ${\Phi}_{Y}=0$
- Au point $B$ : $v=w=0$ ; ${\Phi}_{X}=0$

The initial stress state that allows the stability analysis to be performed is obtained by imposing an bending moment around the $Z$ axis at the $A$ and $B$ points: $M=1\mathit{Nm}$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

Not applicable in static stability analysis.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is obtained analytically for a beam d-Euler-Bernoulli. The theoretical aspects are developed in the reference [bib1].

Using the notations in paragraph [§1], critical values are given by the expression:

${M}_{\mathrm{CR}}=-\frac{{\mathrm{EI}}_{x}+\mathrm{GJ}}{\mathrm{2R}}\pm \sqrt{{(\frac{{\mathrm{EI}}_{x}-\mathrm{GJ}}{\mathrm{2R}})}^{2}+{\mathrm{4n}}^{2}\frac{{\mathrm{EI}}_{x}\mathrm{GJ}}{{R}^{2}}}n=1,2,3,\mathrm{...}$

The plus sign corresponds to positive moments as shown in the figure in [§1.1].

## 4 Summary of the results

The Sorensen and reverse iteration methods (OPTION='PROCHE' or 'SEPARE' or 'AJUSTE') give identical and satisfactory results since the difference maximum with analytical solution is less than $\text{4.5\%}$. It is recalled that the analytical solution takes into account the curvature of the structure.
