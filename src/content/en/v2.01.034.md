# v2.01.034 SDLD34 – Dropping a single mass/spring

**Summary:**

A simple oscillator, consisting of a mass connected to a support by a spring, is subjected to a release from the strained spring. It is checked that *Code_Aster* calculates the oscillation response to the initial conditions without external forces.

The linear transient calculation features on physical and modal basis of the DYNA_VIBRA operator are tested.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.034/1000055800000DF400000757BCE9867C7144ED7A.svg)

We're interested in the $m$ mass movement.

### 1.2 Material properties

| Point mass: | $m=1\mathrm{kg}$ |
| --- | --- |
| Elastic spring: | $k={\pi}^{2}N/m$ |

*Case 1*: system of conservative (without depreciation)

*Cas 2*: $c=0,2\pi \mathrm{N.s}/m$ dissipative system

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The problem is one-dimensional in the $x$ direction, and at a degree of freedom: the $m$ mass displacement.

The mass is left free, without external excitement.

Initially it is out of balance: the spring is tense with a 1 metre elongation.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The reference solution is analytical. In the absence of damping, it is a simple sinusoid whose period is equal to the proper pulse of the oscillator, ${\omega}_{0}=\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}$ , and whose amplitude is the initial $({x}_{0})$ extension of the spring. The $x(t)$ position of the mass is given by the equation:

> $x(t)={x}_{0}\cos({\omega}_{0}t)$             (1)

The speed of the mass is therefore:

> $v(t)=-{\omega}_{0}{x}_{0}\sin({\omega}_{0}t)$             (2)

In the presence of $({c}_{[\mathrm{N.s}/m]})$ viscous damping, the oscillations become damped and the $x(t)$ position is written:

> $x(t)={x}_{0}{e}^{-\zeta {\omega}_{0}t}[\cos(\omega t)+(\frac{\zeta}{\sqrt{1-{\zeta}^{2}}})\sin(\omega t)]$      (3)

where $\zeta$ is the reduced damping given by $\zeta =\frac{c}{2{\omega}_{0}m}$. $\zeta$ is considered to be less than 1 to keep the oscillations. Pulsation is given by the formula $\omega ={\omega}_{0}\sqrt{(1-{\zeta}^{2})}$. It is therefore different from the system's own $({\omega}_{0})$ pulse.

### 2.2 Results

*Case 1*: system of conservative (without depreciation)

For this system, the clean pulse ${\omega}_{0}=\pi \mathrm{rad}/s$ . The eigenfrequency is therefore ${f}_{0}={\omega}_{0}/2\pi =0,5\mathrm{Hz}$ .

The displacement (m) and speed (m/s) of the mass given by Eqs.1 and 2 respectively are:

> $x(t)=\cos(\pi t)$ et $v(t)=-\pi \sin(\pi t)$

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.034/100000000000034A00000253D0D0B4AE766DB174.jpg)

*Cas 2*: viscous dissipative system

The reduced damping is $\zeta =0,1$ . The pulse is $\omega =0,995\pi \mathrm{rad}/s$ and the frequency is $f=\omega /2\pi =0,4975\mathrm{Hz}$ .

The displacement (m) can then be calculated according to Eq.3.

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

analytical solution.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

> Model A is repeated, but with a damping to the mass/spring system.

** Discrete element in translation** **DIS_T**

![Figure](/figures/v2.01.034/10000262000010F30000048C5BF6BDB2083723BD.svg)

Features of elements:

Nodes $\mathrm{P1}$ and $\mathrm{P2}$: M_T_D_N mass matrices with $m=100\mathrm{kg}$ .

Between $\mathrm{P1}$ and $\mathrm{P2}$: a stiffness matrix of type K_T_D_L with ${K}_{x}={10}^{6}N/m$

boundary conditions :

All degrees of freedom are blocked except degree of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$ of node $\mathrm{P2}$ .

Depreciation: the system is added to a reduced depreciation of $0,1\text{}$.

It is introduced into the test case, either as usual by the key word AMOR_REDUIT, or, to validate the RELA_EFFO_VITE functionality, by a linear relationship between the mass/spring speed and a force applied to the node $\mathrm{P2}$.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes: 2

Number of mesh cells and types: 1 SEG2, 2 POI1

### 4.3 Tested Features

In particular, the RELA_EFFO_VITE functionality of the DYNA_VIBRA(BASE_CALCUL='GENE') operator is tested in this Modelling. By its use, nonlinear behavior dependent on the speed of an point can be introduced. Here, this relationship is validated in the linear case by comparing it with a modal damping behavior (which, in the case of a single mode, is a viscous damping).

### 4.4 Tested quantities and results

| **Identification** | **Reference** | **Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DYNA_VIBRA(BASE_CALCUL='GENE') AMOR_REDUIT | $0,53\text{m}$ | 1 % |
| DYNA_VIBRA(BASE_CALCUL='GENE') RELA_EFFO_VITE | $0,53\text{m}$ | 1 % |
| DYNA_VIBRA(BASE_CALCUL='GENE') AMOR_REDUIT | $0.531338$ (non-regression) | 1.E-4% |
| DYNA_VIBRA(BASE_CALCUL='GENE') RELA_EFFO_VITE | $0.531338$ (non-regression) | 1.E-4% |

## 5 Summary of the results

The results are satisfactory. The relative error corresponds to the numerical error associated with time integration. The initial conditions are well taken into account. It is concluded that *Code_Aster* correctly simulates a linear dynamic release.

This test is also a functional validation of recovery as a function of the evolution in time of a mode's participation, as well as validation, on the case of a linear relationship, of the RELA_EFFO_VITE functionality.
