# v2.02.006 SDLL06 - Transient Response of a clamped-Free Post

**Summary**

In this test case, the transient response of an unabated clamped-free beam, modelled by a spring mass system and subjected to any dynamic loading, is analyzed.

The discrete flexion element, the calculation of the eigenmodes by the Lanczos method, and the calculation of the transient response by modal recombination of the structure subjected either to an accelerogram (modelization A) or to an equivalent imposed force (modelization B) are tested.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The problem is treated by a model with an degree of freedom. The pole is considered an unabated and non-heavy beam slender with $k=3E{I}_{Z}/{l}^{\mathrm{3 }}=3,942{.10}^{\mathrm{7 }}N/m$ rigidity. The superstructure at the top of the pole is modelled by a point mass $m=43,8{10}^{\mathrm{3 }}\mathrm{kg}$.

Both load cases lead to the calculation of the system response to an degree of freedom subject to $\gamma (t)$ acceleration of any shape:

$\ddot{{x}_{r}}+{\omega}^{2}{x}_{r}=-\gamma (t)$ with $\omega =\sqrt{\frac{k}{m}}=\sqrt{\frac{3E{I}_{z}}{m{l}^{3}}}$ the system's eigenfrequency and ${x}_{r}$ the relative displacement of point $B$ from point $A$ . The solution is obtained by integration of Duhamel's integral [bib3]:

${x}_{r}(t)=-\frac{m}{\omega}\underset{0}{\overset{t}{\int}}\gamma (t)\sin\omega (t-\tau )d\tau$

### 2.2 Baseline

Movement related to point $B$ .

For an imposed triangular acceleration, Duhamel's integral can be calculated analytically [bib3]:

$t<{t}_{0}$     :    ${x}_{r}=-\frac{{P}_{0}}{{\omega}^{2}{t}_{0}}(t-\frac{\sin\omega t}{\omega})$

${t}_{0}<t<2{t}_{0}$     : ${x}_{r}=-\frac{{P}_{0}}{{\omega}^{2}{t}_{0}}(2{t}_{0}-t-\frac{2\sin\omega (t-{t}_{0})}{\omega}-\frac{\sin\omega t}{\omega})$

${t}_{0}<t<2{t}_{0}$     : ${x}_{r}=-\frac{{P}_{0}}{{\omega}^{3}{t}_{0}}(2\sin\omega (t-{t}_{0})-\sin\omega (t-2{t}_{0})-\sin\omega t)$

### 2.3 Uncertainty about the solution

None if Duhamel's integral is calculated analytically [bib3]. In the order of the accuracy of the numerical integration method used to calculate Duhamel's integral ([bib1], [bib2]): Simpson's method with 40 points per period.

## 5 Summary of the results and general remarks

The simplified model presented in this test case validates the numerical resolution method. To deal with the real physical problem, we should take into account the inertia effects (post mass, inertia effect of rotation around B of the superstructure) and the compression of the pole (clean weight).

For Modelling A, the error with time step of $5.{10}^{-4}s$ is of the order of $\text{0,01\%}$; for Modelling B (time step of ${10}^{-3}s$) it is of the order of $\text{0,6\%}$.

This test case can be completed by checking the convergence of the results for other time step values and comparing the results with other integration patterns.
