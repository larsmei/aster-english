# v7.31.102 WTNV102 - Undrained triaxial test with VISC_MAXWELL and VISC_MAXWELL_MT models

**Summary**

This test validates the VISC_MAXWELL model with hydraulic coupling and its version with parameters modified by Mori-Tanaka, VISC_MAXWELL_MT. It is a triaxial test in undrained condition. The hydraulic coupling is taken into account, the sample is fully saturated, the skeleton is assumed to be incompressible. Due to symmetry, one only cares about the eighth of a sample subjected to a triaxial test.

The containment level is 5 bar.

### 1.2 Initial conditions, boundary conditions, and loading

**Phase 1:**

The sample is taken to a homogeneous state of effective stresses: ${\sigma}_{xx}^{0}={\sigma}_{yy}^{0}={\sigma}_{zz}^{0}=0.5\mathit{MPa}$ in 7000 seconds, by imposing the corresponding total pressure on the front, right and upper sides and by imposing zero water pressures everywhere. The displacements are blocked on the rear (${u}_{x}=0$), left (${u}_{y}=0$) and lower (${u}_{z}=0$) sides.

**Phase 2:**

The movements are held on the rear (${u}_{x}=0$), left (${u}_{y}=0$) and lower (${u}_{z}=0$) sides. On all sides, the hydraulic flows are zero.

A displacement is applied to the upper face to obtain ${\varepsilon}_{zz}=-0,06$ deformation (counted from the beginning of phase 2) in 13000 s. On the front and right side faces, boundary conditions are maintained as total stress:

$\sigma .n={\sigma}_{0}(=0.5\mathit{MPa})$

## 4 Modelling C

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

This case is the same as case A (VISC_MAXWELL) but with infinite viscosity parameters so as to be equivalent to an elastic case. Furthermore, a temperature loading (10°C compared to the initial state) is applied to verify that the latter is taken into account and that the result is the same as in elasticity. This test case is performed on 7000 s only by saving time.

## 5 Summary of the results

This test case is a non-regression test developed to validate VISC_MAXWELL and VISC_MAXWELL_MT models in hydromechanical conditions in undrained conditions. It gives results consistent with physics. In addition, modelling with infinite viscosity coefficients (equivalent to elasticity) and thermal loading gives the same results as equivalent elastic modelling showing the correct consideration of thermal.
