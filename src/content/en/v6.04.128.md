# v6.04.128 SSNV128 - Plate with contact and friction on a rigid plane

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a quasi-static analysis of a mechanical problem with contact and friction. A square plate is compressed on an unformable plane where it undergoes contact and friction forces. This test is based on 2D results from a code average.

This test includes modellingss to test:

- modellings 2D (QUAD4, QUAD8, TRIA3 and TRIA6) and modellings 3D (HEXA8, HEXA20, HEXA27, PENTA6, PENTA15, TETRA4 and TETRA10),
- friction contact processing algorithms ‘PENALISATION' and ‘CONTINUE';
- geometric updating of contact
- different matching algorithm options
- different options specific to the case of friction

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.128/100013D0000069D500004B73FCB24D9EA571DA94.svg)

Side of plate (square) $a=\mathrm{0,04 }m$ .

Position of reference points under the contact surface ($m$ )

|  | $x$ | $y$ | $z$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $A$ | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $B$ | 0,00125 | 0 | 0 |
| $C$ | 0,005 | 0 | 0 |
| $D$ | 0,0075 | 0 | 0 |
| $E$ | 0,01125 | 0 | 0 |
| $R$ | 0,03875 | 0 | 0 |

### 1.2 Material properties

**Place**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,2$

Young's modulus: $1,3\ast {10}^{11}{\mathit{N.m}}^{-2}$

**Build** (only in case of elements modeled with the same size as the plate):

Poisson's ratio: $0.2$

Young's modulus: ${10}^{16}{\mathit{N.m}}^{-2}$

The friction coefficient under the rigid plane is $\mu =1$ **.**

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The frame, when $N-1$ in relation to the plate size, is blocked:

- by a complete clamped support.

The frame, when it is of the same size as the plate, is blocked:

- in the $x=\mathrm{40 }\mathrm{mm}$ plane for $x$ displacement (problem symmetry);
- by an clamped support of its lower face.

The plate is blocked:

- in the $x=\mathrm{40 }\mathrm{mm}$ plane for $x$ displacement (problem symmetry);
- $Y$ at node at the intersection of the symmetry plane, the contact face and the $Z=0$ plane to prevent rigid body movements.

In 3D, to reduce to a 2D problem:

- the next $Z$ move is blocked for all nodes.

The plate is subjected to two distributed pressures:

- a vertical acting on the top face: $f=–5\ast {10}^{7}{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$;
- a horizontal acting on the face initially in $x=0$ , $F=15\ast {10}^{7}{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$ .

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The reference solution is derived from results obtained by an average on other calculation codes [bib1].

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

Tangential displacements (according to $x$) at the $ABCDE$ points of the contact surface (external reference).

Contact status, game and contact reaction to point $R$ for some modellings (non-regression).

Contact pressures at $ABCDE$ points of the contact surface for modellings with wear (non-regression).

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

Important (average code).

### 2.4 Bibliographic reference

[1] R.A. FEIJOO H.J.C. BARBOSA and N. ZOUAIN "Numeric formulations for contact problems with friction" Journal of Theorical and Applied Mechanics GAUTHIER-VILLARS.

## 19 Summary of the results

The results obtained on all modellings of this test case are satisfactory, both in 2D and 3D. On $A$ , $B$ , $C$ and $D$ , a relative error is less than 1% compared to the GRECO results. On the other hand, for point $E$ , the relative error of about 2.5% remains acceptable.

The method of penalisation is rather rapid, as the use of point is not obvious, since the convergence and quality of the results obtained are conditioned by the coefficient of penalisation.

Qualitatively visible point:

- in 2D, there was a positive effect on the results of the quadratic mesh compared to the linear mesh (see modellings A and B),
- In 3D, we find that calculations with mesh quadratic do not improve the results. This is explained by the fact that we have reduced the number of mesh cells from the linear mesh by 50% to have an equivalent number of nodes. In all cases only the use of mesh cells TETRA10 or HEXA27 is recommended in discrete formulation, the mesh cells HEXA20 need to be linearized (they lose their interest).

In the case of L-modelling, inclined plate, there is good convergence and satisfactory results.

The ADAPTATION='CYCLAGE' or ADAPTATION='TOUT' method allows for a better convergence of the cases of razing pressures.

Finally, for the continuous method (modellings M, N, P, Q, R, V, W and X), results are obtained in 3D with relative errors slightly greater than those obtained by the penalized methods. These errors are derived from the blocking in the direction DZ which is not complete in continuous formulation in modellings which do not use the SANS_GROUP_NO_FR feature of excluding the friction nodes.
