# v6.01.104 SSNA104 - Pressured hollow cylinder, linear viscoelasticity

**Summary:**

This test case validates the laws of LEMAITRE and LEMA_SEUIL implanted in *Code_Aster* in the case of linear viscoelastic behavior. The results found are compared to an analytical solution.

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus: $E=1\mathit{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0.3$

Law of LEMAITRE:

$g(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)={(\frac{1}{K}\frac{\sigma}{{\lambda}^{\frac{1}{m}}})}^{n}$ with $\frac{1}{K}=1$ , $\frac{1}{m}=0$ , $n=1$

Law LEMA_SEUIL:

$g(\sigma ,\lambda ,T)=A(\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\sigma )\Phi$ with $A=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2}$ , $\Phi =1$ on all mesh

$S={10}^{-10}$

Given the value of the different material parameters, the two laws are absolutely identical and can therefore be compared to the same analytical solution.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loading

**boundary conditions :**

The cylinder is blocked in $\mathit{DY}$ on the sides $[\mathrm{AB}]$ and $[\mathrm{CD}]$ .

**Charge:**

The cylinder is subjected to internal pressure on $[\mathit{DA}]\mathit{P0}=1.E-3\mathit{MPa}$

## 2 Reference solutions

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solutions

All of this demonstration can be read with more details in the [bib1] document.

In the case of linear viscoelastic isotropic material, the behaviour over time can be described using two $I(t)$ and $K(t)$ functions so that deformations and stresses can be written:

$\varepsilon (t)=(I+K)\mathrm{\ast }\frac{d\sigma (t)}{d\tau }-K\mathrm{\ast }\frac{d(\mathit{Tr}(\sigma (t)))}{d\tau }{I}_{3}$

where ${I}_{3}$ is the row 3 identity matrix

and $\mathrm{\ast }$ the convolution product: $(f\mathrm{\ast }g)(t)={\int}_{0}^{t}f(t-\tau )g(\tau )d\tau$

We find $I(t)=\frac{1}{E}+\mathit{kt}$ , $K(t)=\frac{\nu}{E}+\frac{1}{2}\mathit{kt}$

The ${P}_{0}$ pressure is imposed at the moment $t=0$, the internal pressure is $p(t)=H(t){P}_{0}$ where $H(t)=\lbrace  \begin{array}{c}0\mathit{si}t-\tau <0\\ 1\mathit{si}t-\tau \ge 0\end{array}$ with in this case $\tau =0$

Laplace Carson ${f}^{+}(n)=L(f(t))=n{\int}_{0}^{\infty}f(t){e}^{-\mathit{nt}}\mathit{dt}$ transform

D’où ${p}^{+}={P}_{0}$

The equivalent elastic problem solution is:

${\sigma}^{+}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\gamma (1-\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0& 0\\ 0& \gamma (1+\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0\\ 0& 0& {\sigma}_{Z}^{+}\end{array})$ où $\gamma =\frac{{P}_{0}{r}_{0}^{2}}{{r}_{1}^{2}-{r}_{0}^{2}}$

${\sigma}_{Z}^{+}$ is determined by the ${\varepsilon}_{Z}^{+}$ condition given by the boundary conditions:

${\varepsilon}_{Z}^{+}=0=({I}^{+}+{K}^{+}){\sigma}_{Z}^{+}-{K}^{+}(2\gamma +{\sigma}_{Z}^{+})={I}^{+}{\sigma}_{Z}^{+}-2{K}^{+}\gamma$

D’où ${\sigma}_{Z}^{+}=\gamma (1+\frac{(2\nu -1)p}{p+\mathit{Ek}})$ .

The Laplace ${\sigma}_{z}(t)=\gamma (1-(1-2\nu ){e}^{-\mathit{Eht}})$ reverse transform, as well as the Laplace ${\sigma}_{r}$ reverse transform, can be found

${\sigma}^{+}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\gamma (1-\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0& 0\\ 0& \gamma (1+\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0\\ 0& 0& \gamma (1-(1-2\nu ){e}^{-\mathit{Eht}})\end{array})$

From this we derive:

$\dot{{\varepsilon}_{V}}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{3}{2}k\gamma (\frac{1-2\nu }{3}{e}^{-\mathit{Ekt}}-\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{3}{2}k\gamma (\frac{1-2\nu }{3}{e}^{-\mathit{Ekt}}-\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}})& 0\\ 0& 0& -k\gamma ((1-2\nu ){e}^{-\mathit{Eht}})\end{array})$

and integrating with ${\varepsilon}_{V}(0)=0$;

$\dot{{\varepsilon}_{V}}=(\begin{array}{ccc}\frac{3}{2}\gamma (\frac{1-2\nu }{3}{e}^{-\mathit{Ekt}}-k\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}t)& 0& 0\\ 0& \frac{3}{2}\gamma (\frac{1-2\nu }{3}{e}^{-\mathit{Ekt}}-k\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}t)& 0\\ 0& 0& -\gamma \frac{(1-2\nu )}{E}(1-{e}^{-\mathit{Eht}})\end{array})$ .

The radial displacement is deduced from this

$w(r,t)=r\gamma \left[\frac{1}{E}\left[(1+\nu )\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}+\frac{1-2\nu }{2}(3-(1-2\nu ){e}^{-\mathit{Ekt}})\right]+\frac{3}{2}k\frac{{r}_{1}^{2}}{{r}^{2}}t\right]$
