# v6.01.121 SSNA121 - Concrete tube subjected to internal pressure with model BETON_UMLV

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the correct treatment of tensile states to treat the creep of concrete, model BETON_UMLV, under these tensile stress states. The test consists of applying internal pressure to a concrete tube modelled in axisymmetric conditions.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

An infinite tube of $\mathrm{20m}$ internal radius and $\mathrm{21m}$ external radius is considered. The length of the tube is not involved in the evaluation of the reference solution, but this length is fixed to $\mathrm{10m}$ for the physical modelling of the problem (illustration).

![Figure](/figures/v6.01.121/10000000000001D30000027336934B21081ADB55.png)

Figure 1.1.1: Geometry of 1m thick concrete tube with internal radius 20m

### 1.2 Material properties

The concrete material is elastic isotropic with properties of:

- $E=31000\mathrm{MPa}$
- $\nu =0.2$

The properties of clean-flow concrete (model ULMV_FP) are given below:

- K_RS = $2.0e11\mathrm{Pa}$ ,
- ETA_RS = $4.0e16\mathrm{Pa.s}$ ,
- K_IS = $5.0e10\mathrm{Pa}$ ,
- ETA_IS = $1.0e17\mathrm{Pa.s}$ ,
- K_RD = $5.0e11\mathrm{Pa}$ ,
- ETA_RD = $1.0e16$ ,
- ETA_ID = $1.0e17$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

On the $\mathrm{AB}$ edge, vertical displacements are blocked along the $Y$ axis.

On the $\mathrm{AD}$ edge, a containment pressure of $\mathrm{1MPa}$ is imposed.

On the $\mathrm{BC}$ edge, a uniform restraint is imposed for all nodes in the $X$ direction.

On the $\mathrm{CD}$ edge, a uniform restraint is imposed for all nodes in the $Y$ direction.

The boundary conditions type restraint uniform ensures that an infinite cylinder is modeled, not a finished cylinder.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

None

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

#### 2.1.1 Elastic solution

The analytical solution is established on an infinite cylinder in the direction $Z$ , with an internal ${R}_{int}$ radius , with an external ${R}_{\text{ext}}$ radius , subjected to internal pressure. In cylindrical coordinates and with the following boundary conditions:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}(r={R}_{int})& =& -P\\ {\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}(r={R}_{\text{ext}})& =& 0\end{array}$

In flat stress, the elastic solution is written:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccccccc}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}& =& \frac{-{R}_{int}^{2}}{{R}_{int}^{2}-{R}_{\text{ext}}^{2}}P(1-\frac{{R}_{\text{ext}}}{{r}^{2}})& & {\epsilon}_{\mathrm{rr}}& =& ({\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}-\nu {\sigma}_{\theta \theta })/E\\ {\sigma}_{\theta \theta }& =& \frac{-{R}_{int}^{2}}{{R}_{int}^{2}-{R}_{\text{ext}}^{2}}P(1+\frac{{R}_{\text{ext}}}{{r}^{2}})& & {\epsilon}_{\theta \theta }& =& ({\sigma}_{\theta \theta }-\nu {\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}})/E\\ {\sigma}_{zz}& =& 0& & {\epsilon}_{zz}& =& -\nu ({\sigma}_{\mathrm{rr}}+{\sigma}_{\theta \theta })/E\end{array}$

This is the reference solution of the elastic calculation which will serve as initial conditions for the own creep calculation. This solution is applied with the following physical data: ${R}_{int}=20\text{m}$ and ${R}_{\text{ext}}=21\text{m}$ .

#### 2.1.2 Solution with clean creep

The concrete's own creep model, BETON_UMLV, is presented in detail in [R7.01.06](doc:r7.01.06). It is briefly recalled the spherical and deviatoric decomposition of the own creep deformations. Each of these parts is then separated into reversible or irreversible components.

A particular solution of this model of delayed deformations for constant loading and relative humidity is examined here. The focus is on the spherical part of deformations.

The model distinguishes loadings leading to a positive deformation rate (trenching state) or short-term behaviour and the reverse, the long-term behaviour for negative deformation speeds.

The interest of this test case is to ensure only the good behaviour of the model for traction loading. The following equation specifies the changes to be observed with respect to the model:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}(t)=\frac{h}{{k}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}}\left[1-\exp(\frac{-t{k}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}}{{\eta}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}})\right]{\sigma}^{\mathrm{sph}}\\ {\epsilon}_{i}^{\mathrm{sph}}(t)=0\end{array}$

with:

${\epsilon}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}$: short-term spherical density deformation

$h$: the relative humidity of the continuous medium

$t$: time expressed in seconds

${k}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}$: apparent reversible spherical rigidity

${\eta}_{r}^{\mathrm{sph}}$: apparent viscosity

${\sigma}^{\mathrm{sph}}$: spherical part of the load imposed

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The only reference quantity tested in this example is irreversible creep density deformation. The value of this quantity remains zero for any tensile stress state.

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The uncertainties are zero, because it is an analytical solution.
