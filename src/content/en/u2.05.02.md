# u2.05.02 User's guide for the X-FEM method

**Summary**

The aim of this note is to give the information necessary for a user to be able to carry out analyses with *Code_Aster* using the extended finite element method (X-FEM, for eXtended Finite Element Method) [1].

The first part introduces the aims of the extended finite element method and recalls the basic principles of X-FEM and of level sets.

The second part presents the possibilities and the limits of the use of X-FEM in *Code_Aster*.

The next three parts detail the implementation of X-FEM in *Code_Aster*, for an analysis without or with contact on the crack lips, then for an under-thickness analysis.

Finally, the last part gives a great deal of practical advice.

## 1 Introduction

### 1.1 Why use X-FEM?

The X-FEM method [1] is a simple extension of the finite element method. It allows a mesh which is independent of the geometry of the problem. Boundaries, holes and cracks become entities which one can insert, move and propagate, without having to modify the mesh. A single, simple mesh thus replaces several complex meshes. In order to represent a discontinuity or a singularity within the finite elements, the basis of the shape functions is enriched, using the properties of the partition of unity. In cracking, the displacement (or temperature) discontinuity due to the crack is introduced by a generalised Heaviside function, and the addition of the asymptotic fields at the crack tip improves the accuracy in elastic fracture mechanics. In addition, the level-set method proves particularly convenient for representing 3D cracks and efficient for the propagation phase, the main idea being to consider the interface as the zero iso-value of a distance function.

X-FEM can be used to:

- represent a crack (discontinuity of the displacements or of the temperature),
- represent an interface between two disjoint solids (discontinuity of the displacements or of the temperature),
- represent a hole or a void (an under-thickness, for example),
- represent the interface between two materials (stress discontinuities).

*Representation of a crack*

In fracture mechanics, a crack comprises two lips and a crack front. The lips are initially coincident; otherwise, one speaks of a notch. The displacement (or temperature) field is discontinuous across the crack lips, and the stress field is singular at the crack front.

In *Code_Aster*, it is possible to define a crack (see the implementation in § 18) by means of two level-set functions and to propagate it.

*Representation of an interface between two disjoint solids*

The two solids are separated by an interface (examples: a geological fault between two layers, the crushing of a billet on a fixed frame, two thermally insulated solids). The displacement or temperature field is discontinuous across the interface. The materials on either side of the interface may be different, but they have the same constitutive law. Contact is possibly defined on the interface.

This feature is available in *Code_Aster*, by defining the interface by a level-set function (see the implementation in § 4).

*Representation of a hole or of a void*

This is a particular case of the previous case, in which one of the solids is void. For this, no contact condition is defined on the interface. If no loading is applied on the solid corresponding to the void (apart from the blocking of the rigid-body modes), it does not take part in the analysis, and plays the role of a 'void'.

This feature is available in *Code_Aster*, by defining the interface between the material and the void by a level-set function (see the implementation in § 5).

*Representation of an interface in a bi-material*

In a bi-material, the two materials are 'bonded': the displacement across the interface between the two materials is continuous, but the stresses are discontinuous.

This feature is not available in *Code_Aster*.

In the remainder of this document, the general term 'crack' will be used; it will designate either a crack or an interface.

### 1.2 Specific features of an analysis with X-FEM

Compared with a classical analysis, certain stages are particular:

- creation of the mesh: the mesh does not contain a crack,
- definition of the crack: since the crack is not contained in the mesh, it must be defined in another way,
- modification of the model: certain elements must be enriched in order to represent a discontinuity of displacement or of temperature across the crack and the stress singularity at the crack front,
- visualisation post-processing: in order to visualise the opening of the crack or the discontinuity of the temperature field, it is necessary to create a visualisation mesh and the associated result fields.

## 2 Possibilities and limits of X-FEM in Code_Aster

This part summarises the possibilities offered by *Code_Aster* to date, as regards the use of X-FEM.

Note:

*The very first building blocks of X-FEM are available from version 7, and are of little interest.* Version 8 corresponds to the outcome of the first three years of development, and makes it possible to carry out a mechanical analysis in 2D or 3D linear elasticity, with boundary conditions far from the crack, the crack itself being represented by level sets. Account can be taken of contact on the crack lips. Version 9 brings many advanced features (multi-cracking, plasticity...). Version 10 provides robust automatic propagation algorithms. In version 11 the X-FEM method is extended to the linear thermal operator, so as to take into account, in the thermal problem, the crack initially defined for the mechanical problem.

### 2.1 Phenomenon, modelling and enriched finite elements

X-FEM finite elements are available only in **mechanics** and in **thermal analysis**. It is therefore not possible to model a crack with X-FEM for an acoustic analysis.

*For the phenomenon* 'MECANIQUE' *:*

X-FEM finite elements may come from the modellings 3D, C_PLAN, D_PLAN or AXIS. Conversely, under-integrated X-FEM elements (modellings *_SI) are not available.

All geometric types of mesh cell are available:

In 3D:

- main mesh cells: TETRA4, PYRAM5, PENTA6, HEXA8, TETRA10, PYRAM13, PENTA15, HEXA20;
- boundary mesh cells: TRIA3, QUAD4, TRIA6, QUAD8.

In plane stress / plane strain / axisymmetric:

- main mesh cells: TRIA3, QUAD4, TRIA6, QUAD8;
- boundary mesh cells: SEG2, SEG3.

**Warning: quadratic X-FEM elements lack robustness (especially in 3D) and the use of linear X-FEM elements is advised.**

*For the phenomenon* 'THERMIQUE' *:*

X-FEM finite elements may come from the modellings 3D, PLAN and AXIS.

It is, on the other hand, not possible to enrich the other modellings of this phenomenon: 3D_DIAG, PLAN_DIAG, AXIS_DIAG, AXIS_FOURIER, COQUE, COQUE_PLAN, COQUE_AXIS.

The following geometric types of mesh cell are available:

In 3D:

- main mesh cells: TETRA4, PYRAM5, PENTA6, HEXA8;
- boundary mesh cells: TRIA3, QUAD4

In plane or axisymmetric modelling:

- main mesh cells: TRIA3, QUAD4;
- boundary mesh cells: SEG2

*Note on the 1D elements of a 3D modelling:*

*The 1D boundary elements contained in a 3D modelling cannot be enriched. If these 1D elements are not used in the analysis, the best course is not to put them in the model. If they are genuinely used, they must not be close to the crack.*

### 2.2 Constitutive law

In mechanics, all the constitutive laws are available (COMPORTEMENT) in **small strains**:

- in HPP (small displacements and small rotations): DEFORMATION='PETIT'
- in large displacements and rotations: DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP'.

The other strain models (such as, for example, DEFORMATION='PETIT_REAC') are not available.

Note on the axisymmetric modelling:

*It is noted that the axisymmetric modelling is available only in small strain under the HPP assumption.*

In thermal analysis, the X-FEM method is available only for the operator THER_LINEAIRE: only the case of linear thermal analysis is treated (material parameters independent of the temperature). In addition, the thermal conductivity must be isotropic.

### 2.3 Definition of the cracks

The number of cracks is unlimited. It is also possible to bring them close enough for them to cut the same element. One can also branch one onto the other. The crack fronts must nevertheless respect a minimum separation from the neighbouring crack: at least two uncut mesh cells must separate them. This restriction can be circumvented by refining the mesh locally.

Four different ways of defining a crack are possible:

- either by predefined geometric shapes (ellipse…) for which one supplies the geometric parameters (semi-major axis…). The predefined shapes available are the most usual cracks: in 2D, a crack on a segment or a half-line; in 3D, a plane crack with a straight or elliptical front, a cylindrical crack. **This is the recommended method**.
- or by supplying two mesh-cell groups (one for a lip, the other for the crack front). This method is convenient when one already has a mesh of the crack;
- or by supplying the analytical formulae of the level sets. This method is not suited to cracks of complex shape;
- or by rereading 2 nodal fields which already exist.

Thanks to these four methods of defining the level sets, every situation encountered so far has been treatable.

The definition of an interface is done in the same way. In fact, only the normal level set has to be defined.

Notes:

The X-FEM method theoretically makes it possible to represent a strong discontinuity (cracks or an interface) or a weak discontinuity (interface between bonded bi-materials). Everything depends on the enrichment function introduced into the approximation of the displacement or of the temperature. In Code_Aster, only a strong discontinuity (discontinuous displacement, stress or temperature field) is possible [§ 1.1].

### 2.4 Frictional contact

It is possible to take into account contact (possibly sliding contact) on the lips of one and the same crack. The only authorised method is the continuous method, without wear. It is impossible to define other contact zones. The taking into account of friction is not correct in the general case (only very specific configurations work correctly) and it is strongly advised against using it. The post-processing of the contact degrees of freedom is not easy, because the contact table is not completely created if the integration of the contact terms is done by the Gauss method.

With the restrictions above, the taking into account of contact and of friction is also possible under large-sliding assumptions, in 2D as in 3D. This feature is activated as soon as one supplies the keyword REAC_GEOM='AUTOMATIQUE' or REAC_GEOM='CONTROLE' (keyword of the operator DEFI_CONTACT).

The integration scheme used for the contact terms is by default a Gauss scheme. The other possible schemes are nodal integration, the Simpson schemes (3 and 5 points), and the Newton–Cotes schemes (4, 5 and 10 points). The user is nevertheless advised to choose integration at the nodes.

At the present time, contact on elements containing the crack tip is treated in small sliding and in large sliding.

In summary, the aspects related to frictional contact are present on an experimental basis and must not be used for an industrial study.

### 2.5 Cohesive zones

It is also possible to take into account the presence of cohesive forces during the opening of an X-FEM interface. Cohesion is modelled by the cohesive law CZM_EXP_REG, which already exists in the classical finite element method (see [U2.05.07](doc:u2.05.07)).

The cohesive law must not be used on elements containing the crack tip in 2D and in 3D, but on an interface only.

Just as for frictional contact, the aspects related to cohesive laws are still present on an experimental basis and must not be used for an industrial study.

### 2.6 Boundary conditions and loadings

*Mechanical case*

Only certain boundary conditions can be imposed on X-FEM nodes or elements:

- DDL_IMPO (DX, DY or DZ) on an X-FEM node (but not on an intersection point),
- FACE_IMPO (DNOR or DTAN) on an X-FEM node (but not on an intersection point),
- pressure, distributed force on X-FEM boundary elements,
- body force (of internal-force, gravity or rotation type) on the X-FEM elements,
- pressure on the lips.

In particular, it is not possible to impose a relation between enriched degrees of freedom, nor to use AFFE_CHAR_CINE on enriched nodes.

In order to impose a pressure on the crack lips, one cannot use the classical keyword GROUP_MA, because no mesh-cell group corresponds to the lips. For this, one must use AFFE_CHAR_MECA/PRES_REP/FISSURE.

*Particular case of Dirichlet conditions*

There are two means of imposing Dirichlet conditions, the concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_R and the concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F. In the first, the values of the imposed displacements are specified by real values at the nodes of the mesh, whereas in the second they are specified over the whole of space. This second concept makes it possible, in particular, to impose discontinuous kinematic conditions across the discontinuity easily.

The active XFEM nodes of the mesh possess more degrees of freedom than the classical nodes, because certain fields are enriched.

Concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_R

When one uses the concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_R, by subjecting, for example, a node group whose status is Heaviside to the following condition _F(GROUP_NO=C, DY=α), the code imposes the following linear relation on each of the nodes of the group 'A':

$\mathit{DY}+\mathit{signe}({\mathit{lsn}}_{\mathit{noeud}})\ast \mathit{H1Y}=\alpha$

In the case of multiple cracks, one will even have the following linear relation:

$\mathit{DY}+\Sigma \mathit{signe}({\mathit{lsn}}_{\mathit{noeud},i})\ast \mathit{HiY}=\alpha$

Thus, for two or more degrees of freedom associated with the field DY, one has a single imposed linear relation. The boundary conditions are therefore not completely imposed. The field DY will have the desired value at the nodes, but nothing is imposed on the field DY at the crack lips. If one is content to impose this single linear relation at the XFEM nodes, unwanted situations are liable to occur, as in the example below:

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.02/10000000000003EC0000023DF048A3E8282BD41F.jpg)

Figure 2.6-a: example of partially imposed Dirichlet conditions

In the example above, the upper figure represents the boundary conditions which one wishes to impose. They are imposed by means of the concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_R, respectively on the upper nodes (DY=0) and the lower nodes (DY=-0.05). One sees in the figure that the kinematic condition is correctly imposed at the XFEM nodes (in orange in the figure) but not at the crack lips. In order to obtain the expected result, namely a uniform stretching of the bar downwards for the lower part of the figure, it is necessary to impose an additional relation for each XFEM node. The degrees of freedom associated with the displacement field DX will all be fixed in this way. One can, for example, fix H1Y=0 on the upper nodes and the lower nodes.

One must be very careful when the crack lips coincide with a node of the mesh. In this situation, the sign of the normal level set is indeterminate at the node, since it is zero. There are then two possibilities when one imposes, for example, DY=α by means of the concept AFFE_CHAR_MECA_R.

- First possibility

The kinematic relation is imposed on both sides of the crack (master side and slave side), above and below the interface. The relation $\mathit{DY}=\alpha$ written in the command file then amounts to imposing $\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\mathit{DY}+\mathit{H1Y}=\alpha \\ \mathit{DY}-\mathit{H1Y}=\alpha \end{array}$ for a node on the interface, and therefore to blocking any discontinuity of the field DY at that node. WARNING: in this case, therefore, exceptionally, two relations instead of one are imposed on one and the same node. One must not over-constrain this node by imposing in addition H1Y=0, for example, because the matrix will then not be factorisable. An alarm message making it possible to identify the nodes concerned by this configuration is issued. It is important to note that, because of the *fit to vertex* (readjustment of the normal level set at the node), this situation occurs frequently.

- Second possibility

The kinematic relation is imposed only on the master side or only on the slave side, that is either above or below the interface. The relation $\mathit{DY}=\alpha$ written in the command file then amounts to imposing $\mathit{DY}-\mathit{H1Y}=\alpha$ if it is the slave side and $\mathit{DY}+\mathit{H1Y}=\alpha$ if it is the master side.

The situation in which the crack is conforming at the node is summarised below:

To do this, when one treats a node belonging to an edge intersected by the discontinuity, one uses fictitious nodes located on either side of the crack (in orange in the figure below) in order to impose the correct values of the classical and enriched degrees of freedom of the node. One thus ensures that the displacements are correct both at the nodes and at the crack lips, by simply imposing _F(GROUP_NO=C, DX=f(x,y))

*Thermal case:*

In order to impose an exchange condition between the crack lips, or in order to impose the continuity of the temperature field across a crack, one must use AFFE_CHAR_THER (or AFFE_CHAR_THER_F)/ECHANGE_PAROI/FISSURE. In the first case one will supply the keyword COEF_H, in the second the keyword TEMP_CONTINUE.

No other boundary condition can be imposed on X-FEM elements or on nodes of X-FEM elements. Likewise, no source term can be imposed on the X-FEM elements.

The user must therefore make sure to apply the loadings sufficiently 'far' from the crack, that is to say on unenriched elements (or on nodes belonging to unenriched elements). It is important to emphasise that, since no loading is available on the X-FEM boundary elements of the thermal modellings, a zero-flux condition is imposed by default on the intersection between the boundary of the domain under consideration and the edges of the main X-FEM elements.

### 2.7 Thermo-mechanical analysis

There are two possibilities for carrying out a thermo-mechanical analysis with X-FEM. In both cases, the temperature field is transferred in the classical way to the mechanical operators (MECA_STATIQUE, STAT_NON_LINE, CALC_G and POST_K1_K2_K3) as a command variable (operator AFFE_MATERIAU, factor keyword AFFE_VARC). These two possibilities differ in the way in which the result of the thermal analysis is produced.

#### 2.7.1 Thermo-mechanical analysis with a sound thermal model

The thermal analysis is carried out on a 'sound' thermal model with the operators THER_LINEAIRE or THER_NON_LINE. One does not model the discontinuity of the temperature field across the crack lips. It is therefore a temperature field continuous at the nodes which is transferred to the mechanical operators. This assumption constitutes an approximation of which one must be aware, but which remains valid for short cracks and/or if one considers an exchange condition between the crack lips which involves a high value of the exchange coefficient.

Note:

*A temperature field continuous at the nodes can also be used as a command variable when a* cham_no *of temperature supplied by the user is given under the simple keyword* CHAM_GD *of the factor keyword* AFFE_VARC *of the operator* AFFE_MATERIAU *, or when an* evol_ther *created on the sound mesh by the operator* CREA_RESU *is given under the simple keyword* EVOL *of the factor keyword* AFFE_VARC.

#### 2.7.2 Thermo-mechanical analysis with an X-FEM thermal model

The thermal analysis is carried out on an X-FEM thermal model (a feature available only with the operator THER_LINEAIRE), and one then takes into account the potential discontinuity of the temperature field across the crack lips.

The thermo-mechanical chaining with an X-FEM thermal solution and an X-FEM mechanical solution relies on a single mesh, so as to preserve an identical subdivision between the thermal and mechanical models. The positions of the integration points are then identical from one model to the other, which makes it possible on the one hand to avoid having to project the temperature field, and on the other hand to avoid having to reconstruct the temperature gradient, in particular for the computation of $G$ in the operator CALC_G. In return, this choice imposes that the displacement field be interpolated by linear shape functions, as is the temperature field.

For this type of chaining, a syntax different from that which is usually used must be adopted in the operator MODI_MODELE_XFEM (see [U4.41.11](doc:u4.41.11)) when defining the enriched mechanical model (simple keyword MODELE_THER), so as to duplicate the subdivision of the enriched thermal model. The main commands to be used in this case are summarised below.

# definition of the crack

FISS = DEFI_FISS_XFEM(MAILLAGE = MA,

DEFI_FISS = _F(...))

# definition of the cracked thermal model

MOTH = AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE = MA,

AFFE = _F(PHENOMENE = 'THERMIQUE',...))

MOTHX = MODI_MODELE_XFEM(MODELE_IN = MOTH,

FISSURE = FISS)

# solution on the cracked thermal model

THLIX = THER_LINEAIRE(MODELE = MOTHX, ...)

# definition of the cracked mechanical model

MODELE = AFFE_MODELE(MAILLAGE = MA,

AFFE = _F(PHENOMENE = 'MECANIQUE',...))

MODELX = MODI_MODELE_XFEM(MODELE_IN = MODELE,

MODELE_THER = MOTHX)

# assignment of the material / command variable

CHAMPMAT = AFFE_MATERIAU(MODELE = MODELX,

AFFE = _F(...),

AFFE_VARC = _F(NOM_VARC = 'TEMP',

EVOL = THLIX, ...))

# solution on the cracked mechanical model

RESUX = MECA_STATIQUE(MODELE = MODELX,

CHAM_MATER=CHAMPMAT,...)

# mechanical post-processing of fracture

TABG = CALC_G(RESULTAT = RESUX,

THETA = _F(FISSURE = FISS, ...))

### 2.8 Hydro-mechanical analysis with an HM-XFEM model

There is also the possibility of carrying out a coupled hydro-mechanical analysis with X-FEM. One can then introduce into a hydro-mechanical model hydraulic interfaces, possibly cohesive, in the same way as with the hydraulic joint elements (see document [R7.02.15](doc:r7.02.15)). The advantage of the XFEM discretisation is that the cohesive interfaces do not need to be meshed. For more details on the operation of the HM-XFEM elements, refer to the documentation [R7.02.18](doc:r7.02.18).

#### 2.8.1 Mesh

HM-XFEM elements D_PLAN and 3D are available in *Code_Aster*. As for the classical HM modellings, the interpolation of the displacement field is quadratic; these modellings therefore require exclusively quadratic meshes.

#### 2.8.2 Cohesive interfaces

In an HM-XFEM model, one can introduce cohesive interfaces or cracks, in the same way as in a classical mechanical model. The types of cohesive-zone model available in *Code_Aster* for the HM-XFEM elements are STANDARD (the associated equations are detailed in *2D coupled HM-XFEM modeling with cohesive zone model and applications to fluid-driven fracture network*, M. Faivre, B. PAUL, F. Golfier, R. Giot, P. Massin and D. Colombo, Engineering Fracture Mechanics 2016) and MORTAR (the associated equations are detailed in the documentation [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12)). With a contact of type MORTAR, one will use exclusively the cohesive law CZM_LIN_MIX, whereas with a contact of type STANDARD, one will have the choice between the law CZM_OUV_MIX and the law CZM_TAC_MIX.

#### 2.8.3 Material

Only the 'fluid-saturated' constitutive behaviour is available for the HM-XFEM modellings. One therefore defines the material in the same way as for the classical saturated HM modellings, without forgetting to add the paragraph RUPT_FRAG if cohesive interfaces have been introduced (in this paragraph one supplies the parameters of the cohesive-zone model). In the command STAT_NON_LINE, the constitutive behaviour will also be defined in the same way as for a classical saturated HM modelling: COMPORTEMENT=_F(RELATION= 'KIT_HM',RELATION_KIT=('ELAS','LIQU_SATU','HYDR_UTIL',).

#### 2.8.4 Loadings

With an HM-XFEM model, one can impose the same loadings as for a classical saturated HM model, proceeding in the same way. But there exists an additional loading specific to HM-XFEM models coupled with a cohesive-zone model: the imposition of fluid fluxes directly in the cohesive interfaces. This loading is introduced exclusively with the command AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F/FLUX_THM_REP and the keyword FLUN_FISS. One therefore imposes it by means of a function of space, on the main elements and not on the boundary elements (indeed, only the main elements are cohesive). In order to impose such a flux at the mouth of a hydraulic cohesive interface (which is the most common case), one defines the function of space 'flux' as non-zero at the boundary of the domain around the mouth and zero elsewhere. In the Figure on the left, the blue band corresponds to the zone where the function of space 'flux' is defined as non-zero. One thus imposes the flux in the mouth (red point). For the 2D modellings, the mouth of the cohesive interfaces is a point, so the imposed flux is a point flux and is expressed in kg/s. On the other hand, for the 3D modellings, the mouths are curves (see the Figure on the right), so the flux defined is a line flux which is expressed in kg/m/s.

Figure 2.8.4-a: imposition of a flux at the mouth of a cohesive interface for a 2D model (left) and for a 3D model (right).

#### 2.8.5 Multi-cracking

There is the possibility of introducing several XFEM interfaces per element in the HM-XFEM modellings, and in particular the possibility of introducing interface junctions, in the same way as in classical mechanics (see documentation [R7.02.12](doc:r7.02.12): eXtended Finite Element Method)

The test cases of the database which concern HM-XFEM modellings are the following:

- WTNV144: Consolidation of a column of poro-elastic and fractured soil, use of the XFEM method.

### 2.9 Visualisation post-processing

The visualisation of the results on a sound mesh is not very meaningful. A specific post-processing makes it possible to generate a cracked 'pseudo' mesh. This mesh is intended only for post-processing and must not be used for an analysis (it is not a true mesh, because it does not satisfy certain conformity properties, in particular).

Then, another post-processing makes it possible to generate, from the result of the X-FEM analysis and from this cracked 'pseudo' mesh:

- fields of displacements, of stresses and of internal variables in mechanics
- a temperature field in thermal analysis

These fields can then be used for other post-processings (CALC_CHAMP…).

Note:

*The passage from a* *CHAM_ELNO to a* *CHAM_NO is not possible because of the 'pseudo' mesh.*

### 2.10 Fracture mechanics

The computation of the energy release rate $G$ and of the stress intensity factors ${K}_{I}$, ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ is possible either by extrapolation of the displacement jumps (POST_K1_K2_K3) or by the $G-\theta$ method (CALC_G). There are certain particular restrictions for X-FEM and CALC_G (see [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03) for more details).

Note on the computation of the dynamic stress intensity factor:

It is possible to compute, for X-FEM, the dynamic stress intensity factors (for a transient analysis) and the modal stress intensity factors (associated with the mode shapes); see [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03) for more information.

### 2.11 Fatigue propagation

The operator PROPA_FISS for the automatic propagation of cracks in fatigue is available in 2D and in 3D.

The operator PROPA_FISS offers 4 methods (METHODE_PROPA = 'MAILAGE', 'SIMPLEXE', 'UPWIND' and 'GEOMETRIQUE'), available in 2D and in 3D.

As input, it is necessary to supply it with a table containing the stress intensity factors along the crack front(s) for each crack. These tables must come from CALC_G or from POST_K1_K2_K2. Note that all the cracks of the structure must be propagated in a single call to the operator PROPA_FISS.

The propagation law is a Paris law (at each point of the crack front). The maximum propagation increment at each iteration is imposed by the user.

The bifurcation angle $\beta$ and, where relevant, the twist angle $\gamma$ — if the twist is taken into account — are computed in PROPA_FISS (the choice of the criterion for the computation of the bifurcation angles is left to the user). The default criterion is that of the maximum circumferential stress ('maximum hoop stress criterion'). It is also possible to choose bifurcation angles computed beforehand. In that case, these angles must have been added in the columns BETA and GAMMA of the table supplied as input to PROPA_FISS. It should be noted that, at the present time, only the method GEOMETRIQUE makes it possible to take the twist into account.

*Method* *MAILLAGE (projection):* definition of the 2 level sets from a mesh of the lips and of the front. In order to propagate the level sets, one modifies this mesh at each propagation iteration (METHODE_PROPA='MAILLAGE') [11][12]. One therefore uses two distinct meshes (crack and structure) and only the 'mesh' of the crack is modified. Once this 'mesh' has been updated, one recomputes the new level sets by direct computation of the distance to the crack (orthogonal projection onto the 'mesh' of the crack).

*Method* SIMPLEXE *:* Reinitialisation of the level sets by a fast-marching method based on scalar distance computations. This method is developed for simplex elements (triangles and tetrahedra), but a cutting of the other mesh cells into simplices makes it possible to handle all types of mesh cell.

Method UPWIND: Reinitialisation of the level sets by a fast-marching method with an upwind finite-difference discretisation of the reinitialisation equation on a regular grid disjoint from the structure. It is presented as an acceleration of the UPWIND method.

*Method* GEOMETRIQUE: the level sets are recomputed at each propagation step using geometric relations from the crack front. This method has no limitation.

Advice on the choice of the propagation method is given in § 7.5.

### 2.12 Miscellaneous restrictions

Compatibility with the advanced methods of *Code_Aster* is not ensured. In particular, it is impossible to use X-FEM with:

- dynamics,
- substructuring,
- a cohesive-zone model except those mentioned in § 9.

## 3 Implementation: without contact on the crack lips

This part explains step by step the various stages of an analysis with X-FEM. One may refer, for example, to the test case SSNV192a, which implements an analysis of a 3D specimen with a straight central crack.

### 3.1 Creation of the mesh and of the sound model

The specific feature, compared with a classical analysis, comes from the fact that, with X-FEM, the mesh must not contain the crack. One therefore starts from a mesh of the sound structure. This mesh is thus simpler to produce than a mesh in which the crack is meshed: there is no need to define two lips, no need to mesh a torus at the crack front.

If necessary, do not forget to orient the boundary mesh cells which will carry a loading of pressure type, as for a usual mesh.

A model is then assigned to this mesh by the command AFFE_MODELE. Only the phenomena 'MECANIQUE' and 'THERMIQUE' are authorised. This model is described as 'sound' because it contains only classical finite elements and no X-FEM element. It is used for the definition of the crack.

*The modellings supported in mechanics are:*

3D, C_PLAN, D_PLAN and AXIS.

*The modellings supported in thermal analysis are:*

3D, PLAN and AXIS.

### 3.2 Definition of the crack

Each crack is defined by a call to the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM [2]. As recalled in § 2.3, three different ways of defining a crack are possible. This paragraph details in which cases it is preferable to use one way or another.

*By supplying two mesh-cell groups*

This method is convenient in the case where one has a mesh of the crack (coming from a previous study, for example); it is then sufficient to supply the mesh-cell group corresponding either to the upper lip or to the lower lip, and also to supply the mesh-cell group of the crack front.

This method can also be used in the case where the shape of the crack is too complex to be defined by means of analytical formulae. One then produces a mesh of the crack surface, disjoint from the mesh of the structure. One could envisage, in the future, the following sequence, already used during the thesis of Jean-Baptiste Esnault [20]:

- 3D image by tomography of the crack in the real structure,
- digitisation of the image and creation of the surface CAD associated with the crack,
- meshing of the crack surface,
- definition of the crack by the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM.

Note that if the mesh of the crack is disjoint from the mesh of the sound structure, they must be concatenated prior to the definition of the model (ASSE_MAILLAGE/OPERATION='SUPERPOSE').

*By supplying the analytical formulae of the level sets*

The formulae characterising the level sets must represent the true distance functions (to the surface and to the crack front). Otherwise, the computation of the distance to the crack front by:

$r=\sqrt{({\mathit{lsn}}^{2}+{\mathit{lst}}^{2})}$

will be erroneous.

This technique is well suited to cracks of simple shape: circular plane crack, straight plane crack…

*By predefined geometric shapes*

A catalogue of crack shapes is available. Such a catalogue is indispensable, because certain distance functions do not possess an explicit mathematical expression and require a numerical computation. Moreover, since the use of a catalogue is the fastest, other simple usual shapes will be included, which will also limit the risk of error in the definition of the level sets.

The second stage of the definition of the crack is the orientation of the crack front (in 3D). This stage is somewhat delicate, and proceeds by the definition of two orientation vectors and of an anchor point. It is advisable to refer to the examples detailed in [2] and to the command files of the test cases. Moreover, it is essential always to check the list of the points of the crack front which have been found (and their coordinates), printed by the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM in the message file, the ideal being to plot the curve of the crack front thus discretised. It is also important to check that the number of crack fronts found does indeed correspond to the number expected.

Note:

*In the case where the crack is defined from the catalogue of predefined geometric shapes, the orientation of the front is done automatically.*

Two other checks are possible: visualising the level-set fields, and visualising the 'pseudo' mesh intended for visualisation. The level-set fields can be extracted from the concept created by the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM, each level-set field being extracted by a call to the command CREA_CHAMP/OPERATION=’EXTR’ [3]. Each level set can then be visualised (for example with gmsh or Salomé) following an IMPR_RESU in the appropriate format. Version 5.1.4 of Salomé is not ideal for visualising the level sets, because it does not make it possible to display filled iso-values in a Scalar map (*filled iso-values* in the *gmsh* language) without colour smoothing. The use of gmsh is therefore recommended, with the following settings of the *Range* tab (example given for version 1.65):

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.02/100000000000052B0000028AB69A216C79C5CE30.png)

Figure 3.2-a: settings of *gmsh* (v1.65) for the visualisation of level sets

Two colours are displayed: blue for the zones where the level set is negative, and red for the zones where the level set is positive. The choice of the bounds $\mathit{Min}$ and $\mathit{Max}$ may be different; it is sufficient that the interval be centred on 0.

One may nevertheless use Salomé and display the iso-surfaces of the level sets as 'Iso-surfaces'.

The second check is that of the 'pseudo' mesh, but the latter is created only after the enriched model. This last check will be explained in § 3.5.

### 3.3 Creation of the enriched model

The enriched model is created by the command MODI_MODELE_XFEM [4] from the sound model [§ 3.1] and from the crack defined by DEFI_FISS_XFEM [§ 3.2]. If several cracks have been defined by several prior calls to DEFI_FISS_XFEM, then the input of MODI_MODELE_XFEM will be the list of the names of these cracks. There is no limitation on the number of cracks, in mechanics as in thermal analysis.

In mechanics, it is moreover possible to cut an element with several cracks. Certain nodes of the element will then be enriched with several Heaviside functions. The support of an element may be cut by as many cracks as one wishes, provided that the support of each of its nodes is not cut more than 4 times. Warning: this does not work for crack-front enrichments. A crack front must be separated by at least 2 uncut mesh cells from another crack. One may also have certain conditioning problems which produce a zero pivot during the factorisation of the matrix: this happens in 3D and, pending further development, one can get by using the MUMPS solver. If the cracks cross or branch onto one another, it is imperative to use beforehand the keyword JONCTION in DEFI_FISS_XFEM, otherwise wrong results will be produced. Figure 3.3-a presents an example of a plate meshed with quadrangles, containing three X-FEM cracks (two in red and one in blue). The grey elements are those crossed by the red cracks. The green elements are the crack-front elements. The violet elements are those concerned by the third crack, which branches onto the first two. These elements may be in common with the grey elements of the first two cracks, but the elements cannot be both green and violet.

In thermal analysis, this feature is not available. If the sound model is a thermal model and the keyword JONCTION is used, the code stops in fatal error with a user message.

![Figure](/figures/u2.05.02/Object_40.png)

**Figure** 3.3-a **: example of a plate containing 3 cracks**

This enriched model must be used for the whole of the rest of the analysis. In particular, one must assign the materials and the loadings on this model, and use it in the solution operations MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE.

Note:

*The operator* *MODI_MODELE_XFEM automatically carries out the cutting into sub-elements* [1] *prior to the numerical integration of the elementary matrices and vectors carried out by the analysis operators. Since this subdivision is based on the sound mesh, it is strongly advised against making modifications to the latter (for example, a reorientation of the skin mesh cells by means of the operator* *MODI_MAILLAGE) once the command* *MODI_MODELE_XFEM has been called.*

### 3.4 Solution

The solution of the mechanical equilibrium with X-FEM is carried out in the classical way, but only with the solution operators MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE.

The solution of the heat equation with X-FEM is carried out in the classical way, but only with the solution operator THER_LINEAIRE.

### 3.5 Visualisation post-processing

The creation of the visualisation mesh is done by the command POST_MAIL_XFEM [5], which generates a non-conforming 'pseudo' mesh. This mesh is based on the sound mesh, whose X-FEM elements have been subdivided. Checking the visualisation mesh makes it possible to check the position of the crack, and therefore to check that the level sets have been correctly computed. Note that the creation of the visualisation mesh uses only the enriched model and the sound mesh: this verification stage can therefore be carried out just after the creation of the enriched model, before the solution.

In order to create result fields corresponding to this visualisation mesh, it is necessary first to create a visualisation model. For this, one must use the command AFFE_MODELE, in exactly the same way as for the sound model, changing only the name of the mesh and putting the name of the visualisation mesh.

The creation of the result concept associated with the visualisation model is done by the command POST_CHAM_XFEM[6]. This command generates, for mechanics, the result fields DEPL, SIEF_ELGA, VARI_ELGA, and for thermal analysis the field TEMP. This result can then be used like a result coming from a classical analysis; in particular, the fields can be printed with the command IMPR_RESU (in MED format, for example) in order to be visualised with a post-processing software.

This result can be used for other post-processings (equivalent stresses, strains, nodal forces...) in particular with the command CALC_CHAMP. For certain post-processing options (EPSI_ELGA, for example) it is necessary to recreate a material field on the cracked 'pseudo' mesh by the command AFFE_MATERIAU. This material field will then be given as input to CALC_CHAMP under the keyword CHAM_MATER. This is also the case if structural elements are contained in the mesh. It will then be necessary to recreate the cara_elem and to supply it under the keyword CARA_ELEM of CALC_CHAMP.

An example of complete post-processing (POST_MAIL_XFEM, AFFE_MODELE, POST_CHAM_XFEM, AFFE_MATERIAU, CALC_CHAMP, IMPR_RESU) is given in the test case ssnv185b.

### 3.6 Post-processing in fracture mechanics

As for a meshed crack, the computation of the energy release rate $G$ and of the stress intensity factors ${K}_{I}$, ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}$ is possible either by extrapolation of the displacement jumps (POST_K1_K2_K3) or by the G-theta method (CALC_G). The crack is supplied directly in these operators by the keyword FISSURE.

Advice on the choice of the parameters for the computation of $G$ and of $K$ is given in paragraphs § 7.2 and § 7.3.

### 3.7 Miscellaneous post-processings

The other post-processings (for example, Von Mises stresses…) are done from the result associated with the visualisation model. Thus, one must not directly follow the solution (MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE) by the post-processing of the elementary fields (CALC_CHAMP). One must imperatively post-process the fields from the result coming from POST_CHAM_XFEM.

## 4 Implementation: with contact on an interface

This part explains step by step the various stages of an analysis with X-FEM in which contact is defined on the crack lips (here, an interface). It is advisable to have read the previous chapter. One may refer to the test cases SSNV182, SSNV186, SSNV201, SSNV209, SSNP503 or SSNP504.

For this formulation, the creation of the sound model is done exactly as in the case without contact.

### 4.1 Definition of the interface

An interface is defined by a single level set: the normal level set. One must specify in DEFI_FISS_XFEM the type of discontinuity: TYPE_DISCONTINUITE='INTERFACE'.

Unlike the case of a true crack, it is not necessary to define the true distance function. Any level set is sufficient, provided that the zero iso-value of the level set coincides with the interface. For example, the true signed distance function to the circle of centre $C$ and of radius $R$ is:

$\mathrm{LSN}=\sqrt{({(X-{X}_{C})}^{2}+{(Y-{Y}_{C})}^{2})}-R$

but a level-set function whose zero iso-value coincides with the circle is, for example:

$\mathrm{LSN}={(X-{X}_{C})}^{2}+{(Y-{Y}_{C})}^{2}-{R}^{2}$

### 4.2 Creation of the enriched model

The particular feature of the case in which contact is defined on the X-FEM interface or crack is that one must specify it when creating the enriched model, by the keyword CONTACT='STANDARD' of the command MODI_MODELE_XFEM. Within the framework of the cohesive approaches, one will on the other hand use the keyword CONTACT='MORTAR'. The restriction on the positioning of several cracks is the same as that explained in § 3.3.

### 4.3 Bi-material

The definition of a different material on either side of the interface is not straightforward, because normally a material is defined by mesh-cell groups; thus, on a mesh cell, one necessarily has the same material. Now, in the case of an interface crossing a mesh cell, one might wish to have different materials on either side of the interface. This is nevertheless possible if:

- the normal level set is an explicit function of space,
- the constitutive law is the same over the whole mesh cell.

In this case, one can define the material characteristics (such as $E$ and $\nu$) as a function of a command variable $\mathit{VC}$, which is in fact the normal level set $\mathit{LSN}$. One therefore proceeds as follows:

$(X,Y)\to \mathrm{LSN}(X,Y)\to \mathrm{VC}=\mathrm{LSN}(X,Y)\to (E,\nu )=f(\mathrm{VC})$

One goes through a command variable because the material cannot depend directly on the coordinates of space. The function $f$ is a function of the type:

$\mathrm{if}\mathit{VC}>0\mathrm{then}(E,\nu )=({E}^{1,}{\nu}^{1})\mathrm{else}(E,\nu )=({E}^{2,}{\nu}^{2})$

where $({E}^{1,}{\nu}^{1})$ and $({E}^{2,}{\nu}^{2})$ are the properties of the two materials in question.

### 4.4 Contact load

When creating the contact load, one must specify METHODE = 'XFEM', and give the name of the crack under the keyword FISS_MAIT. The contact method activated is the continuous method.

It may be useful for the convergence of the algorithm to specify the integration scheme by INTEGRATION='NOEUD' or INTEGRATION='GAUSS' or INTEGRATION='SIMPSON'.

### 4.5 Visualisation post-processing

Everything explained in § 3.5 remains valid, but the sound mesh to be supplied is the initial linear mesh (in POST_MAIL_XFEM and POST_CHAM_XFEM). In addition, the visualisation model is the copy of the sound model, but with the classical modellings '3D', 'CPLAN', 'DPLAN' and 'AXIS'.

### 4.6 Post-processing in fracture mechanics

It is entirely possible to compute $G$ and $K$ on a fully or partially closed crack. The computation is done with the operators POST_K1_K2_K3 or CALC_G. For the operator POST_K1_K2_K3, the mesh to be supplied is the initial linear mesh.

If the crack front is closed, the theoretical value of $G$ or $K$ is 0, but one approaches it with the command CALC_G only with very small mesh cells.

As for an unmeshed crack, it is possible to carry out computations of G in the presence of an initial stress field.

### 4.7 Post-processing of the contact

As for a classical contact analysis, the field CONT_NOEU, which gives information on the values of certain quantities related to contact, is computed and can be post-processed. However, if the contact terms are integrated numerically by a Gauss method, the quantities defined at the Gauss points of the contact facets will not be able to be displayed.

## 6 Advice and recommendations on the mesh

This part deals with the match between the mesh used for the analysis and the quality of the solution obtained. First, the problem of mesh refinement within the framework of the X-FEM method is recalled. After a reminder of the definition of the mesh-cell size, a criterion to be respected on the mesh-cell sizes is given. Then, different refinement strategies are presented. The one based on the software Homard coupled to Code_Aster is then detailed. Finally, the recommended methodology for mesh refinement within the X-FEM framework is summarised.

### 6.1 The problem of mesh refinement within the X-FEM framework

The use of the X-FEM method makes it possible to remove certain difficulties related to the mesh. In particular, a free mesh of the sound structure is sufficient. In what follows, it will be assumed that the initial sound mesh is fine enough to obtain a sufficient accuracy on the displacements and stresses for an analysis of a structure without a crack.

The introduction of the crack leads one to ask 2 questions:

- Is the initial mesh fine enough to represent the geometric shape of the crack?
- Is the initial mesh fine enough for the post-processing of the quantities characteristic of fracture mechanics (energy release rate and stress intensity factors)?

The first question is legitimate, because the geometry of the crack is characterised by the level sets, whose values are carried at the nodes of the mesh. Thus the fineness of the mesh influences the quality of the representation of the crack, and therefore the shape of the crack. In the great majority of cases, the crack surface is plane. This type of surface can be represented faithfully even with a relatively coarse mesh. In the contrary case (curved crack), one must make sure that the curvature of the crack can be well represented.

The quality of the representation of the crack front is also important. But the requirements in terms of a correct geometric representation are negligible compared with those necessary for the post-processing (cf. 2nd question).

The 2nd question is the most worrying. Indeed, the stresses are singular at the crack tip. A suitable mesh is therefore needed in order to capture this singularity well. This problem is treated with the finite element method (FEM) by the use, on the one hand, of quadratic elements whose mid-side nodes are moved to the quarter of the edges (elements known as 'Barsoum' elements) and, on the other hand, of a radiating mesh which allows a strong refinement in the post-processing zone.

With X-FEM, the addition of asymptotic enrichment functions is the counterpart of the Barsoum elements. But this has an influence only very locally. A relatively refined mesh is therefore needed in the post-processing zone. Note also that only linear X-FEM elements are considered in this note (the quadratic X-FEM elements currently being in the course of optimisation).

Obtaining a sufficiently fine mesh is the heart of this chapter. The objective is to specify, first, what the fineness of the mesh in the post-processing zone must be. Then, the procedure for obtaining such a refinement is explained.

*Note:*

The case of interfaces is more problematic, because on the one hand the interfaces are generally curved and on the other hand unilateral contact conditions are often defined on the interfaces. Now, in order to solve correctly the problem of contact between 2 curved surfaces, it is imperative that the surfaces be represented by finite elements of order 2. A thesis on curved interfaces represented by level sets of order 2 is in progress. However, the case of mesh refinement for interfaces will not be treated in this chapter.

### 6.2 Definition of the size of a mesh cell

One defines the size of a mesh cell (or else the diameter of a mesh cell) as the value of the length of the longest edge of the mesh cell. This size can easily be computed in the SMESH module of Salomé (Control → Element Diameter 2D or 3D).

### 6.3 Criteria on the mesh-cell sizes

The accuracy of the computation of the energy release rate ($G$) and of the stress intensity factors ($K$) is directly linked to the size of the elements close to the crack front. Two lengths should be distinguished:

- the length of the elements in the plane orthogonal to the crack front in 3D, that is to say the complete plane in 2D;
- the length of the elements along the crack front (in 3D only).

In the plane, one considers that the zone of interest is a disc around the crack front of radius $R<a/5$, $a$ being the crack depth. In this zone, one observes that about 4 to 6 elements are necessary in order to obtain an error on the energy release rate of between 1% and 2%. Consequently, the maximum size of an element in this zone, denoted ${h}_{1}$, must be of the order of $a/20$.

Along the crack front, the discretisation is linked to the curvature of the crack front. For a straight front, a few elements are sufficient. For a curved front, for example a quarter-circle, the number of elements along the front, denoted ${h}_{2}$, must be at least equal to 20.

In certain cases (in particular with a mesh of hexahedra), one can set independently the mesh-cell sizes ${h}_{1}$ and ${h}_{2}$. But in most cases (meshes of tetrahedra), the mesh-cell size is linked to a single user parameter $h$. Thus h must satisfy the 2 conditions above, that is $h=min({h}_{1,}{h}_{2})$. The 2nd condition is not easy to write, because it depends on the curvature of the crack front. Now, it is very often the 1st condition which is the most constraining on the mesh-cell size $({h}_{1}<{h}_{2})$. One can thus conclude, in the general case, the condition to be respected on the mesh-cell size in the zone of interest: $h$ must not exceed $a/20$.

**Bottom line:**

**In the general case, the size of the mesh cells in a zone close to the crack front must be of the order of** $a/20$ **,** $a$ **being the crack depth. Sizes of the order of** $a/10$ **already give a good idea of the result.**

### 6.4 Mesh-refinement techniques within the X-FEM framework

Obtaining an X-FEM mesh on an industrial structure is not so easy. Two approaches can be envisaged: the introduction of a crack block, or mesh adaptation.

The introduction of a crack block consists in defining, when creating the mesh, a box enclosing the crack. The mesh in this box will be mapped, and the fineness of the mesh must be a parameter of the meshing procedure. The box must be wide enough if the study of crack propagation is envisaged. It is also necessary to create a transition zone between the mapped box and the rest of the structure, which is free-meshed. This approach has recently been used in several studies (see the study notes [7] and [8]). This refined block can also be obtained by exploiting the local size maps of the meshing algorithms (Netgen, Blsurf).

The second approach (recommended) consists in producing a suitable mesh by successive refinements of an initial free mesh judged to be coarse. The refinement criterion (or error criterion) is based on the distance to the crack front. An example of the use of this approach by mesh adaptation with the software Homard is presented in [9]. Its implementation is now very easy, thanks to the macro-command RAFF_XFEM, which creates the error field directly from the list of cracks. This error field must then be given as input to the macro-command MACR_ADAP_MAIL (see an example of mesh adaptation for X-FEM with the test case sslp317a,b,c).

The following paragraph details this 2nd approach.

### 6.5 Mesh-refinement methodology with Homard and level sets

This technique does not require one to have the geometry (CAD). It calls only on the initial sound mesh. It uses the mesh-refinement software Homard, which can be called from *Code_Aster* by the command MACR_ADAP_MAIL.

#### 6.5.1 Determination of the number of calls to Homard

At each call to Homard, the mesh cells which will be cut will have their size divided by a factor of 2. It is therefore easy to know the number of times that Homard must be called successively. Knowing the initial size of the mesh cells in the zone of interest ${h}_{0}$ and the size of the mesh cells which one wishes to obtain in the end ${h}_{c}$ (target size), one must call Homard at least $\text{nb\_raff}$ times, with $\text{nb\_raff}\ge \frac{\ln({h}_{0})-\ln({h}_{c})}{\ln(2)}$. It is recommended to take $\text{nb\_raff}=E(n)+1$, with $n=\frac{\ln({h}_{0})-\ln({h}_{c})}{\ln(2)}$. The size of the mesh cells after refinement will therefore be $h=\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{\text{nb\_raff}}}$. Note that, very often, because of the integer division, the final size is not exactly the desired target size, but one knows that $\frac{{h}_{c}}{2}\le h\le {h}_{c}$.

Now that the number of calls to Homard has been determined, it is necessary to know, for each call to Homard, how many mesh cells, and which ones, must be cut (driving of Homard).

#### 6.5.2 Driving of Homard (error indicator and refinement criterion)

Homard must refine only the mesh cells closest to the crack front. For this, it is sufficient to supply it with a field representing the distance to the crack front at each node of the mesh. The nodes for which this distance is the smallest make it possible to determine the mesh cells to be refined as a priority. The question is then the following: how many mesh cells must be cut at each call to Homard? One can either impose:

- a percentage of mesh cells to be cut,
- or else choose to cut all the mesh cells whose distance to the front is less than a limit value (threshold). This limit value can be the value of the radius of the post-processing zone (denoted $R$ in § 6.3).

Criterion with a percentage of elements: the difficulty is to find the right percentages, which may depend on the position of the front (length of the front). This poses a problem in practice for propagation. Although simple, this criterion is not recommended, because it leads to meshes which are not very optimal in terms of the number of nodes (see the illustration on the test case sslp317a).

Criterion with a threshold: very large initial mesh cells can pose a problem. Thus no mesh cell is cut initially, because no mesh cell lies completely at a distance less than the threshold $R$. One must then force the algorithm to cut, and use a variable threshold. The threshold at refinement level $k$ is then written $max(R,\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{k}})$.

*Note:*

Homard makes it possible to cut only the mesh cells whose value of the 'error' field is greater than the threshold. It was therefore necessary to create an error field such that the mesh cells closest to the crack front have the greatest error. For this, the error is not the distance $d$ to the crack front, but the opposite of the distance to the front, that is $e=-d$. The condition $d<max(R,\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{k}})$ therefore becomes $e=-d>-max(R,\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{k}})$. Thus the threshold to be defined in Homard (CRIT_RAFF_ABS) is in fact equal to $-max(R,\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{k}})$. One must also use the keyword USAGE_CMP = 'RELATIF' (see the illustration on the test case sslp317b).

On certain meshes where the mesh cells are somewhat elongated, the use of a criterion with a variable threshold such as that mentioned previously does not work, and no mesh cell is cut initially. A new error indicator by zone (binary indicator) has recently been introduced (RAFF_XFEM/TYPE='ZONE'). The principle of this indicator is to be equal to 1 in the zone to be refined (zone of interest) and 0 everywhere else. The zone of interest is, in 2D, a disc around the crack front and, in 3D, a torus around the crack front, characterised by the supply of a radius $R$. This indicator makes it possible always to refine at least one mesh cell, and is simpler to use than the previous indicator (see [U7.03.51](doc:u7.03.51)). **The use of this new indicator, as well as the associated methodology, is advised**. It is used exclusively with a criterion in absolute value in MAC_ADAP_MAIL (CRIT_RAFF_ABS set to 0.5). With this indicator, it is preferable to make one more call to Homard than $\text{nb\_raff}$, with DIAM_MIN set to ${h}_{c}$.

For an illustration of this methodology, one may refer to the test cases sslv155[b,c], sslv110f, sslp317c. A complete summary of this methodology is given in the following paragraph.

### 6.6 Summary of the recommended mesh-refinement methodology

The recommended methodology can be summarised by the following algorithm:

- estimation of the size of the initial mesh cells in the zone of interest (it is preferable that the sizes in this zone be homogeneous) → ${h}_{0}$
- supply of the target mesh-cell size after refinement of the zone of interest: ${h}_{c}$ (between $a/10$ and $a/40$)
- computation of the minimum number of refinements: $\text{nb\_raff}=E(n)+1$, with $n=\frac{\ln({h}_{0})-\ln({h}_{c})}{\ln(2)}$
- computation of the mesh-cell size after refinement: $h=\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{\text{nb\_raff}}}$
- computation of the radius of the zone of interest: $R=5h$
- true number of iterations of the adaptation loop: $\text{nb\_iter}=\text{nb\_raff}+1$.
- adaptation loop

- definition of the crack/interface on the current mesh
- computation of the indicator by zone (RAFF_XFEM/TYPE='ZONE', RAYON= $R$).
- call to Homard with CRIT_RAFF_ABS = 0.5 and DIAM_MIN = ${h}_{c}$.

*Note:*

In the case of meshes whose mesh-cell size is not uniform in the zone of interest, one can use the same algorithm, denoting by ${h}_{0}$ the greatest mesh-cell size in this zone. But the formula $h=\frac{{h}_{0}}{{2}^{\text{nb\_raff}}}$ no longer applies. Thus the mesh-cell size after refinement is unknown. One therefore cannot choose $R=5h$, because $h$ is unknown. One then chooses $R=5{h}_{c}$, although this choice is not optimal. One then advises, after the refinement phase, determining $h$ 'by hand', then unrefining outside the zone of interest defined by $R=5h$. This being somewhat delicate to implement, a macro-command is under consideration.

## 7 Advice and recommendations on the use of X-FEM

### 7.1 Enrichment

The influence of the asymptotic enrichment zone is noticeable. The more extended the enrichment zone, the better the accuracy (in linear elastic fracture mechanics). However, one cannot extend this enrichment zone to the whole structure, because that adds many additional degrees of freedom and, above all, it strongly degrades the conditioning of the stiffness matrix, to such an extent that the analysis may stop abruptly because of a zero pivot. A detailed study of the influence of the size of the enrichment zone on the accuracy, the rate of convergence of the error and the conditioning of the system is presented in [10]. It emerges from it that one must limit the number of layers of enriched elements to seven layers, a good compromise being four layers.

The choice of the enrichment zone is made at the present time in the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM by the keyword TYPE_ENRICHISSEMENT = 'TOPOLOGIQUE' or 'GEOMETRIQUE'. In the case of geometric enrichment, one supplies either the value of the enrichment radius with RAYON_ENRI, or the number of layers with NB_COUCHES; if neither of these 2 keywords is present, an enrichment over 4 layers is carried out by default.

### 7.2 Parameters of CALC_G

The use of the G-theta method with X-FEM raises the same questions as regards the choice of the numerical parameters as for a meshed crack: values of the radii of the crown ${R}_{inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$, type of smoothing in 3D (and degree if the smoothing is of Legendre type).

The fact that the crack is meshed (classical method) or not (X-FEM method) does not change the way of proceeding. Complementary advice will therefore be found in the user's guide of the fracture-mechanics operators [13].

*Choice of the crown*

In general, the crown must not be too close to the crack front (independently of the mesh-cell size), but sufficiently far from the edges of the structure (beware of structures with holes) for the assumption of a crack in an infinite medium to remain valid. To give an order of magnitude, certain studies [14-15] have shown that the choice ${R}_{inf}=2h$ and ${R}_{\sup}=5h$, where $h$ is the size of an element at the crack front, is a satisfactory choice.

An analysis is judged to be good when the results are independent of the mesh refinement and independent of the choice of the radii ${R}_{inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$.

Once the crown has been validated, if one refines the mesh it is better to keep the same crown than to choose a crown which depends on the mesh-cell size.

Note that, for X-FEM and a geometric enrichment, it is preferable that the radius ${R}_{inf}$ be greater than the enrichment radius.

An automatic mode of computation of the radii ${R}_{inf}$ and ${R}_{\sup}$ is also proposed by CALC_G. But one is advised to check carefully that these automatically computed values lead to results independent of the choice of the radii.

*Choice of the type of smoothing*

Two families of smoothing are available:

- smoothing by Legendre polynomials: $G$ (or $K$) is then a linear combination of Legendre polynomials. This solution gives regular results and is less costly in computation time than smoothing of Lagrange type. Legendre polynomials of high degree nevertheless amplify the numerical errors at the ends of the crack front [13];
- smoothing of Lagrange type is based on the shape functions of the line elements of the crack front. Thus, if there are many points along the crack front, the computation of $G$ will be costly (because it is proportional to the number of points along the crack front). In addition, the result will often be very irregular along the crack front, because of the variable distance between two successive points of the front.

The solution for improving the results of the Lagrange smoothing and for reducing the computation times is to reduce the number of points on the crack front and to distribute them uniformly. This is possible by means of the keyword NB_POINT_FOND of CALC_G. A ratio of the order of 4 between the total number of initial points and the number of equally spaced points seems a good compromise.

*Conclusion*

It is strongly recommended to validate one's model by comparing the results obtained for different mesh refinements, for different integration crowns and for two types of smoothing (for example Legendre of degree 5 and Lagrange with equal spacing of the points of the front).

### 7.3 Parameters of POST_K1_K2_K3

The only parameter to be chosen is the maximum curvilinear abscissa $s$. One advises taking $s$ greater than the enrichment radius. One also advises linking the choice of $s$ to the mesh-cell size, for example $s=5h$. An automatic mode of computation of $s$ is also proposed. But one is advised to check carefully that the automatically computed value leads to results independent of the choice of $s$.

In order to reduce the computation times with POST_K1_K2_K3, which can rapidly become prohibitive, one advises limiting the number of post-processing points by means of the keyword NB_POINT_FOND.

### 7.4 Post-processing on several crack fronts

In X-FEM analyses, several crack fronts can be defined in the same data structure FISS_XFEM (command DEFI_FISS_XFEM). Each call to a post-processing operator (CALC_G or POST_K1_K2_K3) makes it possible to compute $G$ and $K$ for a single front at a time (operand NUME_FOND). Now, in the case of a study of crack propagation with PROPA_FISS (see § 33), one must have a single table containing the results on all the crack fronts. This is possible by calling the operators CALC_G or POST_K1_K2_K3 as many times as there are crack fronts present, then by concatenation of the tables into a single table (see test case sslp318a).

*Note:*

If one wishes to remain in PAR_LOT='OUI', this requires knowing in advance the number of crack fronts. In cases of complex propagation where the front subdivides into several fronts, or if the fronts recombine with one another, one cannot know in advance the number of fronts, and one must then recover this number in the SD_FISS_XFEM (in PAR_LOT='NON' with the command RECU_TABLE, cf. ssnv192a).

### 7.5 Propagation

A propagation analysis is based on three ingredients:

- a propagation criterion (giving the increment of advance of the crack),
- a bifurcation criterion (giving the bifurcation angle),
- an algorithm for updating the crack.

For a propagation analysis to be correct, these three ingredients must be correct as well. The first two ingredients are common to meshed and unmeshed cracks. Only the third presents specific features peculiar to the X-FEM framework.

*Propagation criterion*

Usually, one uses a Paris law whose inputs are material parameters, the stress intensity factors and the number of cycles to be simulated. It is therefore necessary to know, on the one hand, these material parameters. Moreover, the accuracy and the regularity of the stress intensity factors is a key element of the propagation. A small error (of the order of 1 or 2% on $\Delta K$) can lead to significant differences in the lifetime (figure 48 of [16]). The number of cycles to be simulated is normally a free parameter. However, too small a choice of this number of cycles being liable to imply a propagation of a length very much smaller than an element, it is therefore more convenient to drive the advance not by the number of cycles but by the maximum increment of advance ('DA_MAX'). It is recommended to choose a maximum advance of 1 or 2 elements.

*Bifurcation criterion*

A comparison of the different bifurcation criteria has not yet been made. But it would seem that, in 2D, all the bifurcation criteria are equivalent, which is not the case in 3D. A thesis is in progress on a model of fatigue crack propagation in mixed mode with LMT Cachan and AREVA [17].

*Algorithm for updating the crack*

Within the X-FEM framework, the cracks being represented by level sets, updating the crack amounts to updating (or re-updating) the level sets. The operator PROPA_FISS offers 4 methods for this (cf. § 16). These four methods have no theoretical limit of use, but in practice they are not all equivalent, and each of them has its own specific features.

In general, the UPWIND method requires the mesh to be regular. If this is not the case, it is imperative to create an auxiliary (regular) grid and to define it in PROPA_FISS.

The GEOMETRIQUE method is a method which does not require an auxiliary grid, as in the case of the UPWIND method, and uses only the mesh of the structure, unlike the MAILLAGE method. Its use is therefore identical to that of the methods SIMPLEXE and UPWIND without an auxiliary grid.

The SIMPLEXE method is a new method which has been formulated and introduced into *Code_Aster* recently. It does not require an auxiliary grid, as in the case of the UPWIND method. This method proves robust, and the first results show good performance on complex tests which show up the other methods.

Note on the choice of the update zone (methods UPWIND, SIMPLEXE and GEOMETRIQUE):

The keywords ZONE_MAJ='TORE' and RAYON_TORE make it possible to limit the zone of update of the level sets. Warning: in the cases of cracks with several crack fronts (for example in the case of a crack of segment type, in 2D, which possesses 2 crack fronts), the torus must not enclose the other fronts of the crack (even if these other fronts lie 'outside the mesh'). One must therefore limit the value of RAYON_TORE to a zone 'close' to the crack front. One advises using the torus with the upwind method in the case of use of the auxiliary grid.

In 2D:

For a sufficiently fine mesh, the 4 methods give very similar results. One may refer to [11] for examples of 2D propagation with the method 'MAILLAGE' and to [15] for comparisons between the three methods in 2D.

In 3D:

In in-plane propagation, a comparison between the four methods for 3D propagation [18] has shown similar results on an industrial structure. Note, however, that the method 'UPWIND' is more delicate to set up on free meshes, because of the addition of an auxiliary grid. One advises using the methods SIMPLEXE and GEOMETRIQUE.

In non-plane propagation, a study [19] has shown that the method 'MAILLAGE' is no longer completely robust when the propagation becomes truly non-plane. In this case, one advises the methods 'UPWIND' or 'SIMPLEXE' and 'GEOMETRIQUE'.

One may also refer to [12] for examples of plane 3D propagation with the method 'MAILLAGE'.

The test case sslv317a presents a case of out-of-plane propagation with adaptive refinement following the evolution of the crack front with the method 'MAILLAGE'.

Note:

It is also possible to update the level sets without going through PROPA_FISS: by modifying, at each propagation step, the characteristics of the crack of imposed shape, in DEFI_FISS_XFEM (similar to the PROFAT methodology). This technique gives satisfactory results [16], but remains limited to cases of plane propagation, with a crack which propagates while keeping the same shape (semi-elliptical, for example).

**In summary:**

- **For 2D propagation,** **it is better to use the method** MAILLAGE **;**
- **For 3D propagation,** **it is better to use the method** GEOMETRIQUE or SIMPLEXE

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

### 7.6 Frictional contact

The use of frictional contact with the X-FEM method is present in the code on an experimental basis, and is the subject of research. The taking into account of the friction terms is not correct in the general case. In particular, only cracks of small curvature will give correct results.

Moreover, the convergence of the continuous method is far from being systematic. In the present state, the choice of the integration scheme for the frictional-contact terms is a crucial point for the convergence of the X-FEM simulations. The schemes advised are the schemes which contain the nodes of the element as integration points (INTEGRATION='NOEUD', 'SIMPSON', 'NCOTES' under the keyword CONTACT).
