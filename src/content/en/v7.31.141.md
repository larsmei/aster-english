# v7.31.141 WTNV141 – Validation of an evolutionary load for a saturated hydromechanical problem

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to validate the EVOL_CHAR keyword in THM in 2D and 3D. An analytical solution is available.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

In 2D, one considers a square on the $\mathrm{1m}$ side. In 3D, one considers a cube on the $\mathrm{1m}$ side.

### 1.2 Material properties

Intrinsic permeability: $\kappa =0.05{m}^{2}.{\mathit{Pa}}^{-1}.{s}^{-1}$

Biot coefficient : $b=1.$

Young's modulus : $E=2.5\mathit{Pa}$

Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0.25$

So we have the following Lamé coefficients: $\lambda =\mu =1.0\text{Pa}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The boundary conditions are the Dirichlet conditions corresponding to the analytical solution available.

In 2D, $A=2\times {\pi}^{2}\times \kappa$ is defined and mechanical volume loading is defined:

$f(t,x,y)=\left[\begin{array}{c}\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\\ \sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)\end{array}\right]\pi {e}^{-\mathit{At}}\left[-(\lambda +2\mu )+b\right]$

In 3D, $A=3\times {\pi}^{2}\times \kappa$ is defined and mechanical volume loading is defined:

$f(t,x,y,z)=\left[\begin{array}{c}\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\sin(\pi z)\\ \sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)\sin(\pi z)\\ \sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\cos(\pi z)\end{array}\right]\pi {e}^{-\mathit{At}}\left[-(\lambda +2\mu )+b\right]$

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The initial conditions correspond to the analytical solution available.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

We recall the system of equations that we solve:

$\lbrace  \begin{array}{cc}-\nabla \cdot \sigma (u)+b\nabla p& =f\\ {\partial}_{t}(\nabla \cdot u)-\kappa \Delta p& =0\end{array}$

where $\sigma (u)=\lambda \nabla \cdot u{I}_{d}+2\mu \varepsilon (u)$ and ${I}_{d}$ denote the $d$ dimension identity matrix.

In 2D, we have the following analytical solution:

$p(t,x,y)={e}^{-\mathit{At}}\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)$

$u(t,x,y)=-\left[\begin{array}{c}\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\\ \sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)\end{array}\right]\frac{{e}^{-\mathit{At}}}{2\pi }$

In 3D, we have the following analytical solution:

$p(t,x,y)={e}^{-\mathit{At}}\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\sin(\pi z)$

$u(t,x,y)=-\left[\begin{array}{c}\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\sin(\pi z)\\ \sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)\sin(\pi z)\\ \sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y)\cos(\pi z)\end{array}\right]\frac{{e}^{-\mathit{At}}}{3\pi }$

### 2.2 Quantities and reference results

The digital solution is compared to the analytical solution in 3 separate points of the mesh (moving and pressurizing).
