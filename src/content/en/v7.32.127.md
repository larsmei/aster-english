# v7.32.127 WTNP127 – Modelling a water flow in a saturated bar, establishing a permanent regime

**Summary:**

This test case represents the modelling of a water flow in a bar subjected to a pressure gradient. Here we model a diphasic flow that is degenerated into a monophase problem. In this test case we are interested in the establishment of the permanent regime which allows to calculate the flow of water at the outlet analytically. This test case has the main objective of validating the calculation of the integral flow of hydraulics on a surface.

## 1 Reference problem

The objective of this test case is to test the calculation of the integral fluxes on a surface.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This bar is $L$ long and $h$ high and has an initial pressure of ${P}_{l}(x,y,t=0)={P}_{i}$ and is subjected to a pressure gradient such as ${P}_{l}(0,y,t)={P}_{G}$ and ${P}_{l}(L,y,t)={P}_{i}$ .

This evolution problem leads to a linear permanent state such as $P(x,y,t>{t}_{p})=\frac{{P}_{\mathrm{ini}}-{P}_{G}}{L}x+{P}_{G}$ after a time ${t}_{p}$

The ${M}_{11}$ water flow (pressure gradient factor) is then constant along the bar. If integrated into a vertical $\Gamma$ cut of the bar, and normal $\nu$ output, we obtain:

${\int}_{\Gamma}{M}_{11}.\nu =\mathit{h.}{\rho}_{l}\frac{{K}_{int}}{{\mu}_{l}}.\frac{{P}_{G}-{P}_{\mathit{ini}}}{L}.x.\nu$

The calculation of this integral will be done in this test case on 3 surfaces (or side in $\mathrm{2D}$).

### 2.2 Simplification assumptions

In order to test the flow calculation on the most complete hydraulic model possible, a diphasic model is used which is degenerated into monophase modelling. To this end, the medium is considered to be completely saturated with water and zero gas pressure is imposed on all nodes. The biphasic system then resolves the following problem:

$\frac{\partial (\varphi {\rho}_{l})}{\partial t}-div({K}_{int}\frac{{\rho}_{l}{k}_{\mathit{rl}}}{{\mu}_{l}}\nabla {P}_{l})=0$

- Liquid is incompressible: ${\rho}_{l}=c$
- The matrix is compressible and the porosity evolves proportionally to the liquid pressure: $\frac{\partial \varphi }{\partial {P}_{l}}={E}_{m}$
- Relative permeability is taken equal to 1: ${k}_{\mathrm{rl}}=1$

The mass retention equation for the liquid is thus written:

${\rho}_{l}{E}_{m}\frac{\partial {P}_{l}}{\partial t}-d({K}_{i}\frac{{\rho}_{l}}{{\mu}_{l}}\nabla {P}_{l})=0$

A truly saturated model will also be tested ($D$ Modelling).

### 2.3 Uncertainties about the solution

The uncertainties are zero, because reference solution is analytical.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Here we model a case $\mathrm{2D}$ . The flows are calculated on 3 sides :

- left vertical side, noted 'MGAUCHE'
- the vertical right side, noted 'MDROIT'
- a vertical side in the substance, noted 'MMIL'.

The mesh cells will be re-directed to have a normal exit. The flow being directed from right to left in this example will be positive on MGAUCHE and negative on MDROIT.

MMIL will be reoriented so that normal is output to the right-hand part of the bar relying on MMIL. This way, the flow will be positive on MMIL.

To redirect mesh cells to $\mathrm{2D}$ , the factor keyword ORIE_PEAU_2D command is used. If the side is internal, specify a superfacial mesh-cell group on which it is based to determine the normal output to this group (keyword GROUP_MA_SURF).

To redirect mesh cells to $\mathrm{3D}$ , the factor keyword ORIE_PEAU_3D of the MODI_MAILLAGE command is used.If the surface is internal, a mesh-cell group density must be specified to determine the normal output to this group (keyword GROUP_MA_VOLU).

The amount of water on the $\Gamma$ surfaces thus defined will therefore be equal to

${\int}_{\Gamma}{M}_{11}.\nu =\mathit{h.}\rho \frac{{K}_{int}}{\mu}.\frac{{P}_{\mathit{ini}}-{P}_{G}}{L}=2{10}^{\text{-10}}{\mathit{kg.s}}^{-1}$ .

The ${M}_{12}$ steam, ${M}_{21}$ dry air and ${M}_{22}$ dissolved air flows are virtually zero (to the extent that they are simplified).

The Modelling tested here is D_PLAN_HH2S
