# v2.02.158 SDLL158 – Validation of beam modal parameters

**Summary:**

The problem is to validate the modal parameters calculated on a mode_meca modal basis from a mode_gene concept, in comparison with a direct calculation. For this, the eigenmodes of a beam are calculated by direct calculation, and by an orthogonized Ritz base using a mode_gene concept.

The Ritz base is calculated using Craig-Bampton's method: The eigenmodes are calculated with additional blockages, then the modal base is enriched with the static lifts to release these blockages. This modal base is then orthogonized by a calculation of the generalised modes to make appear the calculated modes without these blockages. Two problems are present, with and without rigid body modes.

This test case allows the validation of frequencies, actual masses, and participation factors from an orthogonized Ritz base via the generalized modes.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

For both problems, the reference solution is the one obtained by direct calculation of the eigenmodes. Thus, the eigenmodes calculated by Code_Aster with direct calculation are considered to be validated by test case SDLL01 and correspond to analytical solution. The actual masses and participation factors from this modal base are also used as a reference for validation.

Both problems are modelled with Euler straight beam elements: POU_D_E

### 2.2 Baseline

Frequency, actual masses and participation factors of the first 5 modes obtained with direct calculation.

For both problems, it is checked that the reference quantities are exactly the same as the one obtained on a mode_meca modal basis from a mode_gene concept.

For problem 2, it is also checked that for the three rigid body modes:

- the sum of the actual masses corresponds to the mass of the beam,
- the sum of the unit effective mass is equal to 1.

The modal base is filtered to keep the first 5 modes.

### 4.2 Remarks

The eigenmodes are calculated with a lock in the X and Y directions for the point $A$ and in the Y direction for the $B$ and $C$ points. The four static modes corresponding to these locks are added, with imposed displacements. The Ritz base is composed of dynamic modes and static modes. It is orthogonized with a calculation of generalized modes and then returned to the mode_meca concept.

The modal base is filtered to maintain the first 5 modes, then to maintain the 3 rigid body modes.
