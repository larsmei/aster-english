# r7.02.23 Interface constitutive law for dams CZM_FROT_MIX

**Summary:**

This document describes the cohesive constitutive law CZM_FROT_MIX, modelling the opening and the slip of an interface according to Coulomb's friction law. CZM_FROT_MIX aims to cover the same phenomenology as JOINT_MECA_FROT for the modelling of the mechanical behaviour of dams, but relying on the interface finite element and a mixed augmented-Lagrangian formulation (see [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13)).

## 1 Introduction

The modelling of the mechanical integrity of dams has relied for nearly twenty years on the development of cohesive constitutive laws. These laws relate the displacement jump across a potential discontinuity surface, defined beforehand, to the associated stress vector (otherwise called cohesive forces). The laws currently available — JOINT_MECA_RUPT, JOINT_MECA_FROT and JOINT_MECA_ENDO — thus aim to represent different mechanisms of failure and/or of slip at the principal joints of the dam: concrete–rock interface, concreting joints, or monolith joints [R7.01.25](doc:r7.01.25).

Historically, these laws rest on the joint finite elements (modellings * _JOINT), which rely on a *direct formulation* in displacement, simple to implement [R3.06.09](doc:r3.06.09). Nevertheless, it has been observed on several occasions that this direct formulation may lack robustness, leading to convergence difficulties, in particular when very stiff penalisation parameters are used or in the presence of geometric singularities in the vicinity of the joints.

In parallel, the interface finite elements have also been developed (modellings * _INTERFACE). These rest on a *mixed formulation*, whose degrees of freedom are both the displacements and the cohesive forces (at spatial convergence) [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13). This mixed formulation, on which a wide set of models dedicated to the modelling of failure has been built, proves particularly well suited to modelling with exactness the adherence conditions prior to cracking [R7.02.11](doc:r7.02.11).

The following table presents, in addition, a brief comparison of the two types of finite element according to several criteria.

| Comparison criterion | Joint element | Interface element |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Formulation** | Primal (penalised) | Mixed |
| **Degrees of freedom** | Displacements | Displacements and cohesive forces |
| **Modelling of the adherence** | Approximate | Exact |
| **Hydromechanical extension** | Yes (existing) | No (to be developed) |
| **Robustness / Performance** | Poor conditioning, difficulty of convergence, oscillations of the stresses | LBB condition satisfied (with quadratic elements), stabilisation possible in the case of softening |
| **Sensitivity** | Solutions dependent on the penalisation parameters | Solutions convergent with mesh refinement |

In order to increase the robustness (and the performance) of the cohesive laws used for the modelling of the mechanical behaviour of dams, the aim is now to make available the phenomenological elements currently covered by JOINT_MECA_RUPT, JOINT_MECA_FROT and JOINT_MECA_ENDO by means of equivalent laws relying on the mixed formulation of the interface elements. In this direction, this document presents in a first stage an equivalent of JOINT_MECA_FROT, named CZM_FROT_MIX.

## 2 Phenomenology and use

### 2.1 Expected behaviours of CZM_FROT_MIX

The cohesive law CZM_FROT_MIX, as indicated in the introduction, aims to cover the same phenomenology as JOINT_MECA_FROT:

- In mode I (tension), the evolution of the normal component $⟦u_n⟧$ of the displacement jump at the interface is always reversible. The normal component $T_n$ of the stress vector evolves in proportion to $⟦u_n⟧$, with a normal stiffness $K_n$, until the maximum tensile strength threshold is reached, equal to $c/\mu$, where $c$ denotes the cohesion and $\mu$ the friction coefficient.
- In mode II (shear), the evolution of the tangential component $⟦u_t⟧$ of the displacement jump at the interface is reversible so long as the tangential component $T_t$ of the stress vector remains within the open interval $(-c+\mu T_n; c-\mu T_n)$. In this domain, $T_t$ evolves linearly with $⟦u_t⟧$, according to a tangential stiffness $K_t$. For $T_t = \pm (c-\mu T_n)$, the evolution becomes irreversible when the loading continues, which leads to a plastic slip $\dot{\delta}^p$.

The following figure presents these two loading modes. In the plane $(T_n, T_t)$, they lie in the reversibility domain defined by the Coulomb cone $f(\vector{T})=\normEucl{\vector{T}_t}+\mu T_n-c\leq 0$.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.23/pheno.png)

*Behaviour of CZM_FROT_MIX: (a) response in mode I, (b) response in mode II, (c) loading paths and reversibility domain in 2D.*

**Note**

Unlike JOINT_MECA_FROT, treating the case of infinitely large stiffnesses $K_n$ and $K_t$ (perfect adherence) is made possible with CZM_FROT_MIX because of the mixed formulation on which the law relies. The associated responses in modes I and II are represented by dashed lines on the preceding figure, respectively in (a) and (b).

### 2.2 Parameters of the model

The parameters of the model are six in number:

| Name | Symbol | Interval | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RIGI_NOR | $K_n$ | $\tensFour{R}_+^*$ | Optional |
| RIGI_TAN | $K_t$ | $\tensFour{R}_+^*$ | Optional |
| COHESION | $c$ | $\tensFour{R}_+$ | Mandatory |
| COEF_FROT | $\mu$ | $\tensFour{R}_+$ | Mandatory |
| PENA_LAGR_ABSO | $r$ | $\tensFour{R}_+^*$ | Mandatory |
| ADHE | -- | {ELAS, PARFAITE} | Mandatory |
| RIGI_REGU_VISC | $k$ | $\tensFour{R}_+$ | Optional |
| TAU_REGU_VISC | $\tau$ | $\tensFour{R}_+$ | Optional |

The values of $K_n$ and $K_t$ must be supplied when the adherence is linear elastic (ADHE = ELAS). On the other hand, when the adherence is perfect (ADHE = PARFAITE), neither $K_n$ nor $K_t$ enters into the solution of the equations of CZM_FROT_MIX.

$r$ denotes the augmentation coefficient of the augmented Lagrangian of the mixed formulation [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13). Its value is strictly positive, and its effect disappears with mesh refinement. Although a precise estimate of $r$ for all analysis configurations is delicate, it may be proposed to take $r\sim 10-1000\times(K_n+K_t)$ in the case of elastic adherence, and $r\sim K_{mesh}$ in the case of perfect adherence, where $K_{mesh}$ denotes an “average” stiffness of a mesh cell adjacent to the interface.

**Note**

The role of the parameters RIGI_REGU_VISC TAU_REGU_VISC is set out in section r7.02.23-regularisation_visqueuse.

**Note**

Only V1 and V2 (and V8, V9 and V10 if viscous regularisation) are “true” internal variables, in the sense that they are necessary for the solution of the discretised constitutive equations of CZM_FROT_MIX. The other variables are intended solely for the post-processing of the results.

## 3 Equations of the model

This paragraph presents the constitutive equations of CZM_FROT_MIX. In order to make them easier to read, they are first expressed in their direct formulation, before being introduced in their mixed formulation.

To lighten the presentation further, the equations are given only in the case of a linear elastic adherence, for which the stiffnesses $K_n$ and $K_t$ appear explicitly. The case of a perfect adherence is then obtained naturally by considering the limit $K_n, K_t \rightarrow +\infty$ in the mixed formulation (which the reader is invited to check).

### 3.1 Primal formulation

Let $⟦\vector{u}⟧$ be the displacement jump *seen by the volume*, and $\vector{\delta}^p$ the plastic slip. The relation between the stress vector $\vector{T}$ and the displacement jump is written

$$
\vector{T} = \min\left\{K_n ⟦u_n⟧, \frac{c}{\mu}\right\}\vector{n} + K_t\left(⟦\vector{u}_t⟧-\vector{\delta}^p\right).
$$

The evolution of the plastic slip is obtained by means of the following threshold law:

$$
\dot{\vector{\delta}}^p = \dot{\kappa}\frac{\vector{T}_t}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t}}, \quad \dot\kappa \geq 0,
 \quad f(\vector{T}) \leq 0, \quad \dot\kappa f(\vector{T}) = 0,
$$

with $f(\vector{T})=\normEucl{\vector{T}_t}+\mu T_n-c$ the criterion of the Coulomb cone. The evolution of the plastic slip and the tangential component of the stress vector are then collinear and in the same direction.

### 3.2 Mixed formulation

Let $\vector{\delta}$ be the displacement jump *seen by the interface*, and $\vector{\lambda}$ the Lagrange multipliers ensuring the equality between $\vector{\delta}$ and $⟦\vector{u}⟧$ in a weak manner by the mixed formulation of the interface elements [R3.06.13](doc:r3.06.13). In an entirely general manner, that is to say independently of the cohesive law concerned, the stress vector is then expressed as follows:

$$
\vector{T} = \vector{\lambda} + r\left(⟦\vector{u}⟧ - \vector{\delta}\right) = \vector{\varphi}-r\vector{\delta},
$$

where $\vector{\varphi} = \vector{\lambda} + r⟦\vector{u}⟧$ is a quantity given during the integration of the
interface behaviour (because it depends only on the nodal unknowns of the problem).

Then taking equations  and  into account, the stress vector and the displacement jump seen by the interface are expressed by the following two relations:

$$
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
 \vector{T} &= \min\left\{\cfrac{\varphi_n}{1+r/K_n}, \cfrac{c}{\mu}\right\}\vector{n} + \cfrac{\vector{\varphi}_t-r\vector{\delta}^p}{1+r/K_t}, \\
 \vector{\delta} &= \max\left\{\cfrac{\varphi_n/K_n}{1+r/K_n}, \cfrac{\varphi_n-c/\mu}{r}\right\}\vector{n} + \cfrac{\vector{\varphi}_t/K_t+\vector{\delta}^p}{1+r/K_t}.
 \end{array}
 \right.
$$

$$
\delta_n \geq 0, \quad T_n - c/\mu \leq 0, \quad \delta_n\left(T_n-c/\mu\right) = 0.
$$

**Note**

When the normal stiffness $K_n$ is infinitely large, $T_n$ and $\delta_n$ in  satisfy the contact condition

## 4 Numerical implementation

### 4.1 Principle

Given the displacement jump $⟦\vector{u}⟧$ and the Lagrange multipliers $\vector{\lambda}$  at the current time instant $t_{k+1} = t_k + \Delta t$, and knowing the plastic slip $\vector{\delta}^p_k$ at the previous step $t_k$, the integration of the cohesive law takes care of computing:

- The plastic slip at the current time instant $\vector{\delta}^p$ ;
- The displacement jump seen by the interface at the current time instant $\vector{\delta}$ ;
- The tangent matrix at the current time instant $d\vector{\delta} / d\vector{\varphi}$, arranged as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d\vector{\delta}}{d\vector{\varphi}} =
 \begin{pmatrix}
    \delta_{n,n} & \delta_{n,t1} & \delta_{n,t2} \\
    \delta_{t1,n} & \delta_{t1,t1} & \delta_{t1,t2} \\
    \delta_{t2,n} & \delta_{t2,t1} & \delta_{t2,t2}
 \end{pmatrix},
\end{aligned}
$$

where $(\vector{n}, \vector{t}_1, \vector{t}_2)$ defines the local frame of the interface.

### 4.2 Solution

Following the classical solution method of elastic prediction – plastic correction type, $\Delta\vector{\delta}^p = \vector{\delta}^p-\vector{\delta}^p_k$, $\vector{\delta}$ and $d\vector{\delta} / d\vector{\varphi}$
are obtained by means of the analysis of the function

$$
Q(x) = \normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}} + \mu T_n^{el} - c - \frac{r}{1+r/K_t}x,
$$

with

$$
\vector{T}^{el} = \min\left\{\cfrac{\varphi_n}{1+r/K_n}, \cfrac{c}{\mu}\right\}\vector{n} + \cfrac{\vector{\varphi}_t-r\vector{\delta}^p_k}{1+r/K_t},
$$

which represents the value of the stress vector during a reversible evolution at the current time instant.

Two cases are then to be considered:

- *Reversible solution*: If $Q(0) \leq 0$, the response is reversible, and therefore $\Delta\vector{\delta}^p=\vector{0}$. The value of $\vector{\delta}$ is obtained via equation .2 (with $\vector{\delta}^p=\vector{\delta}^p_k$), and the tangent matrix is written

$$
\frac{d\vector{\delta}}{d\vector{\varphi}} =
\begin{pmatrix}
\delta_{n,n} & 0 & 0 \\
0 & \frac{1}{K_t+r} & 0 \\
0 & 0 & \frac{1}{K_t+r}
\end{pmatrix},
\quad\text{avec}\quad\delta_{n,n} =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
\frac{1}{K_n+r} & \text{si } T_n^{el} < c/\mu, \\
\frac{1}{r} & \text{si } T_n^{el} = c/\mu.
\end{array}
\right.
$$

- *Irreversible solution*: If $Q(0) > 0$, the response exhibits a plastic slip:

$$
\Delta\vector{\delta}^p = \Delta\kappa\vector{N}_t,
\quad\text{avec}\quad\Delta\kappa =
\frac{Q(0)}{r/(1+r/K_t)}, \quad \vector{N}_t = \frac{\vector{T}_t^{el}}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}}}.
$$

The value of $\vector{\delta}$ is still obtained via equation .2 (with $\vector{\delta}^p=\vector{\delta}^p_k+\Delta\vector{\delta}^p$), and the tangent matrix is

$$
\frac{d\vector{\delta}}{d\vector{\varphi}} =
\begin{pmatrix}
\delta_{n,n} & 0 & 0 \\
\delta_{t1,n} & \frac{1}{K_t+r}+\frac{1}{r(1+r/K_t)}[Q(0)\frac{1-N_{t1}^2}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}}} + N_{t1}^2] & \frac{1}{r(1+r/K_t)}[-Q(0)\frac{N_{t1}N_{t2}}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}}} + N_{t1}N_{t2}] \\
\delta_{t2,n} & \frac{1}{r(1+r/K_t)}[-Q(0)\frac{N_{t1}N_{t2}}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}}} + N_{t1}N_{t2}] &  \frac{1}{K_t+r}+\frac{1}{r(1+r/K_t)}[Q(0)\frac{1-N_{t2}^2}{\normEucl{\vector{T}_t^{el}}} + N_{t2}^2]
\end{pmatrix},
\quad\text{avec}\quad\delta_{n,n} =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
\frac{1}{K_n+r} & \text{si } T_n^{el} < c/\mu \\
\frac{1}{r} & \text{si } T_n^{el} = c/\mu
\end{array}
\right.
,\quad  \delta_{ti,n} =
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
\frac{\mu N_{ti}}{r(1+r/K_n)} & \text{si } T_n^{el} < c/\mu \\
0 & \text{si } T_n^{el} = c/\mu
\end{array}
\right.
$$

## 5 Viscous regularisation

The “perfectly plastic” type behaviour of the cohesive constitutive law CZM_FROT_MIX leads to the existence of limit loads. The introduction of a viscous regularisation then aims to allow the stress vector to exceed *temporarily* the slip and opening thresholds of the Coulomb cone, in order in particular to facilitate analyses on force-loaded structures (impounding of a dam, for example).

### 5.1 Principle

The principle of this regularisation is similar to that presented in [R5.03.34](doc:r5.03.34). A Maxwell chain, composed of a linear spring of stiffness $k$ and a linear dashpot of viscosity $k\tau$ ($\tau$: characteristic time) is added in parallel with the two non-linear components of CZM_FROT_MIX (the non-linear contact spring and the slip slider). The following figure describes the rheological diagram.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.23/schema_rheo.png)

*Rheological diagram of the viscous regularisation on CZM_FROT_MIX.*

The evolution laws will then be written

$$
\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
 &\hat T_n - c/\mu \leq 0, \quad &\delta^p_n \geq 0, \quad &\delta^p_n\left(\hat T_n - c/\mu\right) = 0, \\
 &\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t} + \mu \hat T_n - c \leq 0, \quad &\Delta\vector{\delta}_t^p = \Delta\kappa\cfrac{\vector{\hat T}_t}{\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t}}, \quad &\Delta\kappa\left(\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t} + \mu \hat T_n - c\right) = 0,
 \end{array}
 \right.
$$

$$
\begin{split}\left\lbrace
\begin{array}{lll}
 &\hat T_n - c/\mu \leq 0, \quad &\delta^p_n \geq 0, \quad &\delta^p_n\left(\hat T_n - c/\mu\right) = 0, \\
 &\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t} + \mu \hat T_n - c \leq 0, \quad &\Delta\vector{\delta}_t^p = \Delta\kappa\cfrac{\vector{\hat T}_t}{\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t}}, \quad &\Delta\kappa\left(\normEucl{\vector{\hat T}_t} + \mu \hat T_n - c\right) = 0,
 \end{array}
 \right.\end{split}
$$

with $\vector{\hat T}=\vector{T} - k(\vector{\delta}^p-\vector{\delta}^v)=\vector{T} - k\tau\Delta\vector{\delta}^v/\Delta t$. They are therefore nothing other than the same evolution laws as in the absence of viscosity, having subtracted from the stress vector the quantity $k(\vector{\delta}^p-\vector{\delta}^v)= k\tau\Delta\vector{\delta}^v/\Delta t$ (stress of the Maxwell chain). Consequently, the numerical implementation is only slightly modified as a result.

### 5.2 Illustration

In order to illustrate the effect of the viscosity, let us consider the following opening loading (the case of slip displaying identical results), in the situation of an elastic adherence. The normal component of the displacement jump is prescribed as $\delta_n(t) = \min\{\dot{\delta}_n t, \delta_{\max}\}$ with $\dot{\delta}_n =$ 1 mm/s and $\delta_{\max} =$ 5cm, and the parameters:

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| $K_n$ | 10 GPa/m |
| $c/\mu$ | 1 MPa |
| $\tau$ | 10 s |
| $k$ | 1 GPa/m |

The response of the normal component of the stress vector is shown in the following figure.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.23/visco.png)

*Loading and response of a relaxation test in opening mode.*

One notes that:

- For $0\leq \dot{\delta}_n t \leq c/(\mu K_n)$, the response of $T_n$ with $\delta_n$ is linear elastic of slope $K_n$, up to $c/\mu$.
- For $c/(\mu K_n)/\dot{\delta}_n < \dot{\delta}_n t \leq \delta_{\max}$, the response is increasing and non-linear, of initial slope $K_nk/(K_n+k)$, reaching asymptotically $c/\mu + k\tau\dot{\delta}_n$ according to a characteristic time of a decreasing exponential equal to $(1+k/K_n)\tau$.
- For $\delta_{\max} \leq \dot{\delta}_n t$, the response is decreasing and non-linear, returning asymptotically to $c/\mu$ with the same characteristic time $(1+k/K_n)\tau$.

**Note**

In the case of a perfect adherence ($K_n=+\infty$), the characteristic time of the exponentials becomes equal to the characteristic time of the Maxwell branch $\tau$.

### 5.3 Measurement of the viscous effect

In order to measure the effect of the viscosity on the responses of CZM_FROT_MIX, one introduces the internal variable (for post-processing) SIGMVISC. It computes at each time step the stress in the Maxwell chain as

$$
\text{SIGMVISC} = k\tau\normEucl{\Delta\vector{\delta}^v}/\Delta t.
$$

The following figure illustrates the response of SIGMVISC on the preceding relaxation test.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.23/sigmvisc.png)

*Response of SIGMVISC during the relaxation test.*

When $\dot{\delta}_n t\leq c/(\mu K_n)$, the variable SIGMVISC remains equal to zero. It increases only when $c/(\mu K_n)\leq \dot{\delta}_n t$ towards $k\tau\dot{\delta_n}$ when $\delta$ approaches $\delta_{\max}$. Finally, for $\delta_{\max} < \dot{\delta}_n t$, SIGMVISC decreases towards zero.
