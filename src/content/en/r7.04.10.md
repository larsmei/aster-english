# r7.04.10 Operator for the computation of wear

**Summary:**

This note presents three wear laws which make it possible to evaluate the worn volume from the quantities coming from a dynamic analysis carried out with the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.54.03](doc:u4.54.03) and the keyword CHOC.

- Archard's law,
- The KWU_EPRI law,
- The EDF_MZ law.

The wear coefficients required for these computations are supplied by the user or else specified in a database.

From the worn volume and the geometry of the contact, it is possible to compute the wear depth for the moving body or its obstacle.

An angular subdivision of the clearance pattern allows the operator to compute the quantities relating to wear by sectors.

> **Contents**

## 1 Introduction

The assessment of damage by wear requires a thorough knowledge of the bodies present during contact, of the loadings and of the kinematics. The investigations carried out at the Component Mechanics and Technology Department make it possible to supply coefficients for wear laws relating to wear configurations affecting the components of nuclear power plants. A transient analysis by modal recombination, by means of the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.54.03](doc:u4.54.03), makes it possible to know the kinematics and the dynamics of the contact for line structures such as the control-rod clusters and the steam-generator tubes which impact and slide against their guides.

In order to compute the wear power, the wear post-processing module of *Code_Aster* , (POST_USURE [U4.67.03](doc:u4.67.03)), uses, at an impact node, the result in generalised coordinates (tran_gene) coming from DYNA_VIBRA. It combines the normal forces and the sliding velocities according to the method defined in the following paragraph. From knowledge of the wear power, it is possible to recover the worn volumes by using one of the wear laws proposed in POST_USURE. The coefficients to be used are to be defined by the user or to be looked up in a database integrated into the operator.

In a second stage, knowledge of the geometry of the internal structures of nuclear power plants makes it possible to compute the wear depths from the worn volumes.

The operator POST_USURE makes it possible to subdivide the clearance pattern into sectors in order to assign several wear coefficients to one and the same impact zone, so as to take complex geometries into account. For example, edge contact leads to greater losses of material than conforming contact in the case of control-rod clusters.

The table generated by POST_USURE gives the value of the worn volumes for several values of time.

## 2 Wear laws

In its initial form, Archard's law [bib1] expresses, for an adhesive wear configuration, in sliding, a relation between the worn volume and quantities characteristic of the contact:

$V=\frac{k.\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel .L}{H}$

| where | $V$ | : | worn volume, |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | $k$ | : | dimensionless wear coefficient, |
|  | $\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel$ | : | modulus of the normal contact force, assumed constant, |
|  | $L$ | : | sliding length, |
|  | $H$ | : | hardness. |

The coefficient $k$ is different for each of the bodies present. It depends on the geometric and thermodynamic conditions during contact.

It has been shown that Archard's law can be extended to other mechanisms, with sliding dominant. Subject to a redefinition of certain parameters, the previous equation can be written:

$V=K.W$

| where | $K$ | : | is equal to $\frac{k}{H}$ , |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | $W$ | : | is equal to $\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel .L$ . |

$W$ has the dimension of a work. By convention, it is called the "wear work".

In the case where the normal contact force varies in the course of time (for example, in an impact-sliding situation, $\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel$ exhibits very strong variations of short duration during the impacts), the definition of $W$ becomes:

$W=\underset{{t}_{0}}{\overset{{t}_{1}}{\int}}\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel \cdot \parallel {V}_{t}\parallel \cdot \mathrm{dt}$

| where | $W$ | : | wear work, |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | $\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel$ | : | modulus of the normal force in the course of the contact, |
|  | $\parallel {V}_{t}\parallel$ | : | modulus of the sliding velocity in the course of the contact, |
|  | ${t}_{0}$ | : | time instant of the start of the analysis, |
|  | ${t}_{1}$ | : | time instant of the end of the analysis. |

Henceforth, by analogy with the usual laws of mechanics, it is possible to define a "wear power" by setting:

$P=\parallel {F}_{n}\parallel \cdot \parallel {V}_{t}\parallel$

where $P$ : wear power.

In the case where a stationary regime is reached, the wear power is assumed constant in the course of time. In order to make sure of this stationarity, the interval $[{t}_{0,}{t}_{1}]$ can be subdivided into several blocks in the operator POST_USURE [U4.67.03](doc:u4.67.03). For each of these blocks, one should verify that the wear power evolves little (strictly speaking, the use of the wear laws below assumes that the wear power is constant).

### 2.1 Wear law 'ARCHARD'

The law is of linear type [bib1]: $V=K\cdot P\cdot t$

| where | $V$ | : worn volume, |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | $K$ | : wear coefficient, |
|  | $P$ | : wear power, |
|  | $t$ | : time interval. |

The coefficient $K$ is supplied by the user or is taken from a database (see [§3]). It is different for the two bodies present and depends on the geometric and thermodynamic conditions in the contact. The time interval $t$ used for the computation of the wear does not correspond to the effective simulation time but to the time interval over which the user wishes to evaluate the wear.

### 2.2 Wear law 'KWU_EPRI'

The approach of the model consists in determining a wear coefficient $K$ , in the sense of Archard's law, by taking into account the particular conditions of the contact studied [bib2].

The normal forces ${F}_{i}(N)$ are distributed into 5 classes, as are the sliding velocities ${V}_{j}(m/s)$ .

One obtains 25 classes whose identification is indicated as follows:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.10/Object_36.svg)

For a given analysis, one determines the percentages obtained for each of the 25 classes.

The processing is done by applying appropriate weighting factors for each class, which account for its particular contribution in the overall wear process.

In the case of pure impacts (classes 1.1 to 1.5), the contribution of these classes is modelled by calling upon a weighting factor ${m}_{{h}_{ij}}$ defined by:

${m}_{{h}_{ij}}={k}_{1}\cdot k\cdot {(\frac{{F}_{i}}{c})}^{3}$

| where | ${m}_{{h}_{ij}}$ | : | dimensionless factor of impact-hardening intensity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${k}_{1}$ | : | dimensional correction coefficient |
|  | $k$ | : | experimental dimensionless constant |
|  | $c$ | : | experimental dimensionless constant |
|  | ${F}_{i}$ | : | mean value of the normal force for class ij |

In the case of sliding (class 1.1 and classes 2.1 to 5.5), the contribution of these classes is modelled by calling upon a weighting factor ${m}_{{w}_{ij}}$ defined by:

${m}_{{w}_{ij}}={k}_{2}\cdot {F}_{i}\cdot {({V}_{j})}^{2}$

| where | ${m}_{{w}_{ij}}$ | : | dimensionless factor of sliding-wear intensity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${k}_{2}$ | : | dimensional correction coefficient |
|  | ${F}_{i}$ | : | mean value of the normal force for class ij |
|  | ${V}_{j}$ | : | mean value of the sliding velocity for class ij |

One must then compute the weighted percentages for each class of the two categories impact-hardening and sliding wear:

${P}_{{h}_{ij}}={m}_{{h}_{ij}}\cdot {p}_{ij}$

${P}_{{w}_{ij}}={m}_{{w}_{ij}}\cdot {p}_{ij}$

where ${p}_{ij}$ is the percentage of elements of the class $ij$ .

Which leads to an overall wear-intensity factor

$w=\frac{{(\sum{P}_{{w}_{ij}})}^{2}}{\sum{P}_{{h}_{ij}}+\sum{P}_{{w}_{ij}}}$

The overall intensity factor $w$ is used as a correction factor of the wear coefficient in the sense of ARCHARD's law according to the expression:

${K}_{\mathrm{KWU}}={k}_{r}\cdot w/{w}_{r}$

$V={K}_{\mathrm{KWU}}\cdot P\cdot t$

| where ${k}_{r}$ : | is the reference wear coefficient obtained experimentally for conventional test conditions in oscillating sliding, |
| --- | --- |
| and ${w}_{r}$ | is the overall intensity factor evaluated for this same test. |

### 2.3 Wear law 'EDF_MZ'

It is at present developed for the sole case of the control-rod clusters.

Feedback from experience shows that the wear kinetics slows down with time $t$ ; one way of taking the observations into account is to express the worn volume in the form:

$V=(\frac{{S}_{0}-S}{n})\cdot (1-{e}^{-\mathrm{nt}})+S\cdot t$

where ${S}_{0}$ is the initial rate and $S$ the asymptotic wear rate (see below),

$n$ is a parameter of the model.

The values of $n$ and of $S$ are deduced from feedback from experience.

Tests on simulators, of short duration compared with that of an operating cycle of a reactor, show that the initial wear rate ${S}_{0}$ follows a law of the type:

${S}_{0}=A\cdot {({P}_{0})}^{b}$

where ${P}_{0}$ is the initial wear power

$A$ and $b$ are coefficients determined by tests on simulators [bib4]

Feedback from experience shows that the wear rate reaches an asymptotic value $S$ . The previous relation, observed on a simulator, is assumed valid for all the instants of the wear phenomenon. This assumes a wear power $P$ which makes it possible to reach $S=A\cdot {(P)}^{b}$ , for the high values of the time $t$ (typically, one or several operating cycles).

The corresponding evolution of the worn volume as a function of time is of the form:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.10/Object_74.svg)

The worn volume $V$ computed by means of the operator POST_USURE is written:

$V=(\frac{A\cdot {({P}_{0})}^{b}-S}{n})\cdot (1-{e}^{\mathrm{nt}})+S\cdot t$

| where | $V$ | : worn volume, |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${P}_{0}$ | : wear power computed by *Code_Aster* *®* , |
|  | $A,b,S,n$ | : coefficients of the model defined above. |

This model is described in detail by reference [bib4].

## 4 Relation between the worn volume and the wear depth

From the wear power, the operator POST_USURE computes the worn volumes and then the wear depths. The geometric relations between the worn volumes and the worn depths depend on the type of contact.

| Let: | ${d}_{m}$ | : | worn depth of the moving tube, |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
|  | ${d}_{o}$ | : | worn depth of the obstacle, |
|  | ${R}_{m}$ | : | outer radius of the moving tube, |
|  | ${R}_{o}$ | : | inner radius of the obstacle, |
|  | $l$ | : | width of the obstacle, |
|  | $\theta$ | : | angle between moving body and obstacle, |
|  | ${V}_{m}$ | : | worn volume of the moving tube, |
|  | ${V}_{o}$ | : | worn volume of the obstacle. |

### 4.1 Situation ‘CLUSTER - BORE’

The keyword used is "GRAPPE_ALESAGE". The cluster is centred in a bore. The wear trace has a lune-shaped cross-section [bib6]. The worn volume is reduced to a worn area in a cross-section, multiplied by the worn height $l$

The worn volumes are written [bib3]:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{{V}_{m}}{l}={r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))-{R}_{m}^{2}(\alpha -\sin(2\alpha ))\\ \frac{{V}_{o}}{l}={R}_{o}^{2}(\gamma -\sin(2\gamma ))-{r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))\\ {R}_{m}\sin(\alpha )=r\sin(\alpha )\\ r\sin(\beta )={R}_{o}\sin(\gamma )\end{array}$

The variables $r,\alpha ,\beta$ and $\gamma$ are intermediate computation variables defined in the figure below:

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.10/Object_92.svg)

A numerical solver integrated into *Code_Aster®*  makes it possible to go from solving this system of coupled equations with 4 unknowns, $r,\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma$ . The wear depths are then given by the following relations:

$\begin{array}{}{d}_{o}=r-{R}_{t}-(r\cos(\beta )-{R}_{t}\cos(\alpha ))\\ {d}_{m}={R}_{o}-r-({R}_{o}\cos(\gamma )-r\cos(\beta ))\end{array}$

### 4.2 Situation ‘CLUSTER - SINGLE NOTCH’

The keyword used is "GRAPPE_1_ENCO".

The guide card comprises a single notch. The worn volume is reduced to a worn area in a cross-section, multiplied by the worn height

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.10/Object_95.svg)

.

The worn volumes are written [bib7]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}\frac{{V}_{m}}{l}={A}_{m}{d}_{m}^{3}+{B}_{m}{d}_{m}^{2}+{C}_{m}{d}_{m}+{D}_{m}\\ {V}_{o}=0,47\cdot h\cdot {R}_{o}\cdot {d}_{o}\cdot \pi \end{array}$

with [bib7]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{A}_{m}=-2,76\\ {B}_{m}=10,30\\ {C}_{m}=0,83\\ {D}_{m}=0\end{array}$

These coefficients are based on feedback from experience. They apply only to control-rod clusters whose characteristics are:

- outer diameter of the cluster rodlet: 9.7 mm
- inner diameter of the guide card: 10.5 mm

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine ${d}_{m}$ as a function of ${V}_{m}$

### 4.3 Situation ‘CLUSTER - DOUBLE NOTCH’

The guide card is formed of 2 diametrically opposite notches. The worn volume is reduced to a worn area in a cross-section, multiplied by a worn height $l$ .

The worn volumes are written [bib7]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}\frac{{V}_{m}}{l}={A}_{m}{d}_{{m}^{3}}+{B}_{m}{d}_{{m}^{2}}+{C}_{m}{d}_{m}+{D}_{m}\\ {V}_{o}=0,94\cdot h\cdot {R}_{o}\cdot {d}_{o}\cdot \pi \end{array}$

with [bib7]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{A}_{m}=-5,52\\ {B}_{m}=20,60\\ {C}_{m}=1,66\\ {D}_{m}=0\end{array}$

- diameter of the rodlet: 9.7 mm
- diameter of the card: 10.5 mm

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine ${d}_{m}$ as a function of ${V}_{m}$

### 4.4 Situation ‘Steam-generator tube - Anti-vibration bar’

The keyword used is "TUBE_BAV".

**Case 1:**

The tube is presented vertically, the bar impacts perpendicularly to the tube, and one assumes that the bar does not wear.

The wear depths are written [bib3]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{d}_{m}={(\frac{1}{2{R}_{m}})}^{1/3}\cdot {(\frac{3{V}_{m}}{41})}^{2/3}\\ {d}_{o}=0\end{array}$

**Case 2:**

The bar is presented inclined (angle $\theta$ ) with respect to the tube, the bar impacts perpendicularly to the tube, and one assumes that the bar does not wear.

- if ${d}_{m}<l\theta$

The wear depths are written [bib3]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{d}_{m}={(\frac{1}{2{R}_{m}})}^{1/5}\cdot {(\frac{15\cdot \theta \cdot {V}_{m}}{8})}^{2/5}\\ {d}_{o}=0\end{array}$

- if ${d}_{m}\ge l\theta$

The relations between worn volume and wear depths are written [bib3]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{V}_{m}=\frac{8\sqrt{2{R}_{m}}}{15\theta }\cdot \left[{d}_{m}^{5/2}-{({d}_{m}-\theta l)}^{5/2}\right]\\ {d}_{o}=0\end{array}$

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine ${d}_{m}$ as a function of ${V}_{m}$

**Case 3:**

The tube is presented vertically, the bar impacts perpendicularly to the tube, and one takes into account the wear of the bar. $\alpha$ is an unknown to be determined.

The relations between worn volume and wear depths are written [bib3]:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{d}_{m}=(\frac{{V}_{m}}{{V}_{m}+{V}_{o}}){(\frac{1}{2{R}_{m}})}^{1/3}{(\frac{3\cdot ({V}_{m}+{V}_{o})}{4\cdot l})}^{2/3}\\ \frac{{V}_{m}+{V}_{o}}{l}=\alpha \cdot {R}_{m}^{2}-{R}_{m}^{2}\sin(\alpha )\cos(\alpha )\\ {d}_{o}={R}_{t}(1-\cos(\alpha ))-{d}_{m}\end{array}$

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine $\alpha$

**Case 4:**

The bar is presented inclined (angle $\theta$ ) with respect to the tube, the bar impacts perpendicularly to the tube, and one takes into account the wear of the bar. $\alpha$ is an unknown to be determined.

- if $({d}_{m}+{d}_{o})<l\theta$

The relations between worn volume and wear depths are written [bib3]:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{d}_{m}=(\frac{{V}_{m}}{{V}_{m}+{V}_{o}}){(\frac{1}{2{R}_{m}})}^{1/5}{(\frac{15\cdot \theta \cdot ({V}_{m}+{V}_{o})}{8})}^{2/5}\\ \frac{{V}_{m}+{V}_{o}}{l}=\alpha \cdot {R}_{m}^{2}-{R}_{m}^{2}\sin(\alpha )\cos(\alpha )\\ {d}_{o}={R}_{m}(1-\cos(\alpha ))-{d}_{m}+\frac{l}{2}\sin(\theta )\end{array}$

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine $\alpha$

- if $({d}_{m}+{d}_{o})\ge l\theta$

The relations between worn volume and wear depths are written [bib3]:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{}{V}_{m}=\frac{8\cdot \sqrt{2{R}_{m}}}{15\cdot \theta \cdot (1+k)}\cdot \left[{(({d}_{m}+{d}_{o})\cdot (1+k))}^{5/2}-{(({d}_{m}+{d}_{o})\cdot (1+k)-l\theta )}^{5/2}\right]\\ \frac{{V}_{m}+{V}_{o}}{l}=\alpha \cdot {R}_{m}^{2}-{R}_{m}^{2}\sin(\alpha )\cos(\alpha )\\ {d}_{o}={R}_{m}\cdot (1-\cos(\alpha ))-{d}_{m}+\frac{l}{2}\sin(\theta )\end{array}$

where $k$ is the ratio between the worn volumes of the bar and of the tube ($k=\frac{{V}_{o}}{{V}_{m}}$ )

A solver integrated into POST_USURE makes it possible to determine ${d}_{m}$ as a function of ${V}_{m}$ . Likewise, a solver makes it possible to determine $\alpha$ .

### 4.5 Situation ‘Steam-generator tube - Bore’

The keyword used is "TUBE_ALESAGE".

**Case 1:**

The tube is perfectly centred in a bore animated by a pure orbital motion which wears uniformly over the whole of the periphery in contact with the obstacle.

The worn depths are written [bib3]: $\lbrace \begin{array}{}{d}_{m}=\frac{{V}_{m}}{2\cdot \pi \cdot l\cdot {R}_{m}}\\ {d}_{o}=\frac{{V}_{o}}{2\cdot \pi \cdot l\cdot {R}_{o}}\end{array}$

**Case 2:**

The tube is centred in a bore animated by an impact-sliding motion of elliptical type which leads to the formation of wear traces of cylindrical type, diametrically opposite on the tube and having a lune-shaped cross-section.

The worn volumes are written [bib3]:

$\begin{array}{}\frac{{V}_{m}}{l}={r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))-{R}_{m}^{2}(\alpha -\sin(2\alpha ))\\ \frac{{V}_{o}}{l}={R}_{o}^{2}(\gamma -\sin(2\gamma ))-{r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))\\ {R}_{m}\sin(\alpha )=r\sin(\beta )\\ r\sin(\beta )={R}_{o}\sin(\gamma )\end{array}$

system of coupled equations with four unknowns to be determined: $r,\alpha ,\beta ,\gamma$

$\begin{array}{}{d}_{o}=r-{R}_{m}-(r\cos(\beta )-{R}_{m}\cos(\alpha ))\\ {d}_{m}={R}_{o}-r-({R}_{o}\cos(\gamma )-r\cos(\beta ))\end{array}$

These formulae have the same origin as those of paragraph [§4.1].

**Case 3:**

The tube, animated by an impact-sliding motion, this time presents an inclination with respect to the support. One obtains two symmetric wear traces on the tube.

$\begin{array}{}\frac{{V}_{m}}{l}={r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))-{R}_{m}^{2}(\alpha -\sin(2\alpha ))\\ \frac{{V}_{o}}{l}={R}_{o}^{2}(\gamma -\sin(2\gamma ))-{r}^{2}(\beta -\sin(2\beta ))\\ {R}_{m}\sin(\alpha )=r\sin(\beta )\\ r\sin(\beta )={R}_{o}\sin(\gamma )\end{array}$

These formulae have the same origin as those of paragraph [§4.1].

## 5 Subdivision of the clearance pattern into sectors

The user has the possibility of defining a subdivision of the clearance pattern into angular sectors for which he or she gives a type of contact (GRAPPE_1_ENCO...), a wear coefficient and the start and end angles of the subdivision (these angles must be increasing between -180° and +180°). The wear power for each sector is then computed as the arithmetic mean, over the instants previously subdivided into blocks, of the product of the norms of the normal impact force and of the sliding velocity, taking into account only the contacts which take place in the angular sector concerned. From this power, it is possible to define a worn volume by multiplying the wear power of the sector by the wear coefficient of the sector and by an operating time given by the user. It is also possible to compute the wear depth for this sector, assuming that the angular extent of the defect does not exceed that of the sector in which it is detected.

It is the keyword SECTEUR which makes it possible to define all of these modifications.

It is not planned to verify the overall consistency of the computations carried out. In particular, a wear may be distributed over several sectors and in this case the computation of the wear depth no longer has any meaning. It is for the operator to make sure a posteriori of the validity of the results. A new analysis with another subdivision must possibly be carried out in order to obtain the value of the wear depth. This choice is not constraining because of the speed of the post-processing considered. The interest of carrying out these analyses in POURSUITE is obvious, taking into account what precedes.

## 6 Updating of the table

The operator POST_USURE extrapolates the worn volume obtained in a few seconds of simulation to durations defined by the user (typically a few months, or even a few years).

It returns a table which contains the worn volumes and the wear depths for all the sectors and all the instants defined by the user, accumulating them from the initial instant of the simulation.

It is possible to give a table to be updated by using the keyword ETAT_INIT. This makes it possible to take into account the evolution of the geometries linked to wear:

- From a clearance pattern, the user carries out a dynamic analysis.
- He or she obtains worn volumes and wear depths at the output of POST_USURE.
- He or she carries out a new dynamic analysis with the modified clearance pattern.
- He or she deduces from it new quantities linked to wear and accumulates them in the result table of POST_USURE.

By iterating the process a certain number of times [bib9], it is possible to take into account the evolution of the geometries as a function of wear and to deduce from it the impact of this phenomenon on the dynamics of the system studied.
