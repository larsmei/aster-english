# v6.04.182 SSNV182 – Block with frictional contact interface with X-FEM

**Summary**

The purpose of this test is to validate the consideration of contact on the lips of the crack, limiting itself to the case where the crack completely crosses the structure. The contact is taken into account by the continuous method [bib1] adapted to the framework of the X-FEM [bib2] method. Two contact algorithms are tested: the Lagrangian augmented method and the penalised method [bib2].

This test involves a parallelepipedic block in compression modelled in $\mathrm{2D}$ and $\mathrm{3D}$ . The interface through it is represented by a level-set as part of the X-FEM method. Several angular positions of the interface are taken into account: the interface follows the faces of the elements ($\theta =0°$ ) and the interface cuts elements ($\theta =26.56°$ in $\mathrm{3D}$ and $\theta =30°$ in $\mathrm{2D}$ ). By taking a friction coefficient of Coulomb high enough to be gripped, we find the solution of the same problem without interface. The modellings d, e and f enable the method to be validated also in $\mathrm{2D}$ .

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is a straight parallelepipeda with a square and healthy base. The dimensions of the block (see[ Figure -1.1-a ]) are: $\mathrm{LX}=5m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=20m$ and $\mathrm{LZ}=20m$ . It does not have cracks.

The interface is introduced by level set functions directly in the command file using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08) operator. The interface is present in the middle of the structure through its representation by the level set. The normal level set ($\mathrm{LSN}$ ) allows to define a flat interface making an $\theta$ angle with the $\mathrm{Oxy}$ plane by the following equation:

$\mathrm{LSN}=Z-(\mathrm{aY}+b)$                     éq 1.1-1

where $a$ is the slope of the interface, i.e. $a=-\tan(\theta )$ and $b=\frac{\mathrm{LZ}}{2}-a\frac{\mathrm{LY}}{2}$.

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The nodes of the lower side of the bar is clamped and a $\mathrm{UZ}=-{10}^{-6}m$ displacement is imposed on those of the upper side which corresponds to a pressure load following the $z$ axis. The displacements along the $x$ and $y$ axes are blocked for the nodes of the upper surface. In $\mathrm{2D}$ , the displacement is carried out on the $y$ axis.

### 1.4 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled in $\mathrm{3D}$ by a regular mesh composed of $5\times 20\times 20$ HEXA8 [Figure 1.4-a].

These elements have linear shape functions and a quadratic mesh cell support. On these elements, the top nodes carry the unknowns of the displacement, and the middle nodes carry the unknowns related to the contact. ## 2 Modelization A: straight interface, Lagrangian method increased

In this Modelling, one represents a straight interface, the $\theta$ angle is then 0. The interface coincides with the faces of some finished elements. The increased Lagrangian method is used for the treatment of contact / friction.

### 2.1 Analytical resolution

When the adhering contact is taken into account on the interface, the problem is equivalent to that of a healthy bar in pure compression. The solution to the problem is that of the same problem without interface.

The constraint in the structure is:

${\sigma}_{zz}=E\frac{\mathit{UZ}}{\mathit{LZ}}$                             éq 2.1-1

and the value of the contact pressure on the interface is:

$\lambda ={\sigma}_{zz}$                                     éq 2.1-2

With the numerical values previously introduced, $\lambda =-5.0\mathit{Pa}$ .

### 2.2 Tested Features

As indicated in the previous paragraph, friction must be activated. In the DEFI_CONTACT operator, FROTTEMENT=的COULOMB is specified, and the coefficient of friction is chosen equal to 1.

In addition, from the first iteration of active stresses, it is assumed that contact points have contact status. This is possible by specifying CONTACT_INIT=

Otherwise, at the end of the first iteration, the contact being not activated, the upper block enters the lower block but the two blocks have not deformed. Their stress state is therefore zero, and a global criterion (RESI_GLOB_MAXI) for the convergence of the Newton-Raphson algorithm [bib3] must be chosen, which may be inappropriate in the further calculations when the contact is activated.

To avoid this, and to have a relative criterion, it takes a non-zero state of stress from the first iteration, and therefore activate contact from the beginning.

The algorithm of the active stresses thus converges into an iteration.

### 2.3 Tested quantities and results

The values of the normal contact pressure after convergence of the iterations of the STAT_NON_LINE operator and the loop on the active stresses are tested. All contact points, corresponding to the nodes of the mesh on the interface, are tested. The values determined in [§2.1] are checked.

| **Identification** | **Reference** | **% Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LAGS_C for all nodes interface | -5.00 | 1.00e-10 |

To test all nodes at one time, you test the minimum and maximum of the column.

### 2.4 Remarks

This Modelling shows the possibilities of the continuous contact method applied to the X-FEM frame. The advantage is that the matching procedure is intrinsic to the X-FEM method since here there is no really master and slave surface since it is kept in small movements.

## 3 Modelling B: lean interface, Lagrangian augmented method

In this Modelling, one represents a lean interface. L-angle $\theta$ is $\arctan(1/2)$ , i.e. a $\alpha$ slope of $-1/2$ . The interface no longer coincides with the faces of the finished elements, and now cuts elements . Normal at the interface is noted $n$ and the tangential vector is noted $\tau$ :

$n=(\begin{array}{}0\\ 1/\sqrt{5}\\ 2/\sqrt{5}\end{array})$ ,     $\tau =(\begin{array}{}0\\ 2/\sqrt{5}\\ -1/\sqrt{5}\end{array})$         éq 4-1

The increased Lagrangian method is used for contact/friction treatment.

### 3.1 Analytical resolution

The interface is tilted and may be slipped. To avoid this, we force adhesion by choosing a sufficiently high coefficient of friction of Coulomb. Theoretically, just take:

$\mu >\tan(\theta )$                 éq 4.1-1

Thus, the solution to the problem remains the same as the one of the same problem without an interface.

The stress in the structure is always that of [eq 2.1-1-o], and the value of the contact pressure on the interface is a function of the normal $n$ at the interface:

$\lambda =n.\sigma .n={n}_{z}{\sigma}_{zz}{n}_{z}$             q 4.1-2

where ${n}_{z}$ is the following $z$ component of $n$.

The $\Lambda$ friction semi-multiplier is defined by:

${r}_{\tau}=\lambda \mu \Lambda$                 éq 4.1-3

With the tangential force density, which reads as follows:

${r}_{\tau}=(\tau .\sigma .n)\tau$             éq 4.1-4

D’où:

$\Lambda =(\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{\tau .\sigma .n}{n.\sigma .n})\tau =(\frac{1}{\mu}\frac{{\tau}_{z}}{{n}_{z}})\tau$         éq 4.1-5

We're taking $\mu =1$.

With the numerical values previously introduced, $\lambda =-4.0\mathrm{MPa}$ and $\Lambda .\tau =-0.5$ .

### 3.2 Tested quantities and results

The normal contact pressure and the friction semi-multiplier are tested after the iterations of the STAT_NON_LINE operator converge, the loop on the active stresses and the loop on the friction thresholds. The value of the contact and friction multipliers at the mesh points of contact of the visualization is tested: the GROUP_NO of the contact points is extracted by the keyword PREF_GROUP_CO of the POST_CHAM_XFEM command.

It is checked that the values determined in [§3.1] are correctly reflected. LAGS_F1 corresponds to the friction semi-multiplier in the direction $\mathit{Ox}$ (it is therefore zero), while LAGS_F2 corresponds to the friction semi-multiplier following $\tau$.

| **Identification** | **Reference** | **% Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LAGS_C for all contact points | -4.00 | 1.00e-8 |
| LAGS_F1 for all contact points | 0.00 | 1.00e-8 |
| LAGS_F2 for all contact points | -0.50 | 1.00e-8 |

To test all contact points at a single time, you test the $\text{MIN}$ value and the $\text{MAX}$ value of the column.

### 3.3 Remarks

In this study, the key word CONTACT_INIT = ‘OUI' allows the loop to begin on the active stresses with a contact status hypothesis for all contact points. This allows a relative criterion (‘RESI_RELA_MAX') for the convergence of Newton's iterations. Indeed, if you choose CONTACT_INIT = ‘NON', during the prediction phase of Newton, the contact is not activated, the upper structure moves without deforming, and the lower structure remains still. The constraints are then zero and a relative criterion is not usable, only a global criterion is l'est, the value of which is left to the user's choice. The problem is that this value can be revealed by the calculation (active contact....) inadequate to the loads and constraints then at play. Thus, it is better to be able to take a single relative criterion from the beginning.

In addition, the initial value of the friction threshold was taken at $-{10}^{9}$ to ensure that the friction thresholds were gripped from the first iteration.

### 14.2 Tested Features

This test is used to test the XFEM contact on quadratic meshes (CONTACT='P2P1' attribute in the MODI_MODELE_XFEM command) in the three-dimensional case. The friction is activated, so it is also tested (FROTTEMENT='COULOMB').

### 14.3 Tested quantities and results

The values of the normal contact pressure and the semi-multiplier friction after convergence are tested. All contact points are tested. In this 'P2P1' discretization, the contact degrees of freedom are carried by the nodes peaks of the elements cut off by the interface: it is therefore at these points that the values are tested.

| **Identification** | **Reference** | **% Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LAGS_C for all contact points | -3.75 | 0.01 |
| LAGS_F1 for all contact points | 5.77350E-01 | 0.01 |

To test all contact points at a single time, you test the $\text{MIN}$ value and the $\text{MAX}$ value of the column.

### 14.4 Remarks

This test validates the P2P1 3D Modelling, namely:

- Calculation of the stiffness matrix (the correct offset when filling the matrix because nodes do not carry the same number of degrees of freedom),
- the calculation of contact matrices.

At present, 3D quadratic elementss are sub-cut with linear elementss, with quadratic sub-cuts, possibly with curved faces, not yet available. Similarly, the contact facets are TRI3s. This test is not therefore a validation of sub-elementss or particular contact facets.

## 15 Summary of results

The objectives of this test are achieved:

- The aim is to show the feasibility of taking frictional contact into account on the lips of the crack with the continuous method adapted to the X-FEM frame. Only the case of a crack completely crossing the structure was considered (interface).
- The cases where the interface follows the elements ($\theta =0°$) border and the interface cuts elements ($\theta =26.56°$ to $\mathrm{3D}$ and $\theta =30°$ to $\mathrm{2D}$) have been validated.
- The method was validated in $\mathrm{2D}$ for $\text{P1P1A}$ and $\text{P2P1}$ and in $\mathrm{3D}$ for $\text{P1P1A}$.
- The method was validated with both the increased Lagrangian method and the penalized method for the treatment of contact / friction.
