# r7.01.42 Homogeneous damage law ENDO_LOCA_EXP

**Summary:**

This document describes the elastic brittle constitutive model ENDO_LOCA_EXP, available in local modelling only (as opposed to a non-local formulation of GRAD_VARI type). This implies restricting the use of the model to situations in which the damage is diffuse and not localised. The corresponding non-local version is supplied by the model ENDO_FISS_EXP [R5.03.28](doc:r5.03.28). Although the damage is modelled there in an isotropic manner, the damage criterion is for its part well suited to the modelling of concrete, distinguishing in particular the states of tension and of compression, unlike the model ENDO_ISOT_BETON [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04) for example. Moreover, a recovery of stiffness in compression is introduced in order to reflect the closure of the cracks. On the other hand, the model is a priori not suited to modelling the damage of concrete in compression.

## 1 Scope

### 1.1 Purposes

The constitutive law ENDO_LOCA_EXP, available in the command STAT_NON_LINE, has the objective of describing the diffuse quasi-brittle damage of concrete under tensile mechanical loadings. The damage is described there in an isotropic manner. On the phenomenological level, the model aims more particularly to reflect:

- the contrast between the elastic limits in tension and in compression, thus avoiding premature damage in compression under prestress, for example;
- the specific shape of the damage surface in multiaxial tension, close to a Rankine criterion;
- the impact on the stiffness of the closure of the cracks in the compression directions, which also reinforces the robustness of the simulations in the presence of surface-breaking cracks.

In the presence of localised damage, one will prefer to it the non-local model ENDO_FISS_EXP, whose parameter definition is close to that of ENDO_LOCA_EXP.

The interested reader will be able to find more detailed elements on the formulation of the law and its validation in the reference [Lorentz, 2020]. Given the proximity to the constitutive laws ENDO_ISOT_BETON and ENDO_FISS_EXP, the reader may also refer to the references [Badel et al., 2007], [Lorentz, 2017], [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04) and [R5.03.28](doc:r5.03.28).

### 1.2 Material parameters

The constitutive law is based on six internal parameters (including the elastic constants), as will be seen in the presentation of the equations in the following chapter. However, in a diffuse-cracking regime in which a mean inter-crack distance emerges, one will be able to reformulate the parameters in terms of quantities more accessible to the engineer. Besides Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio, one will supply the tensile and compressive strengths (the latter being less significant for the problems targeted), the fracture energy and a parameter to describe the shape of the softening response. Finally, the mean inter-crack distance will be fixed on the basis of the characteristics of the reinforcement, for example. The command DEFI_MATER_GC takes charge of the transformation of these engineering parameters into internal characteristics of the law and can therefore be substituted for the command DEFI_MATERIAU, but it requires in that case that the mean inter-crack distance also be supplied.

### 1.3 Internal variables

The constitutive law ENDO_LOCA_EXP gives access to five internal variables. The degree of damage $b$ , lying between 0 for a sound material and 1 for a fully damaged material, constitutes a priori the only true internal variable of the model. For reasons of numerical efficiency, one also keeps the state of the current time step, with the values 0=elastic, 1=damaging, 2=saturated. Finally, one also groups there three variables in order to facilitate the post-processing.

The internal variables are grouped in the following table:

| ENDO | V1 | Damage $b$ |
| --- | --- | --- |
| INDIENDO | V2 | State of the current time step (0, 1 or 2) |
| ENDORIGI | V3 | Loss of stiffness $1-B(b)$ |
| ENERELAS | V4 | Volumetric elastic energy |
| ENERDISS | V5 | Volumetric energy consumed |

## 2 Continuous model

### 2.1 Constitutive equations

The stress–strain relation takes the following form, in which ${⟨x⟩}_{-}$ and ${⟨x⟩}_{+}$ denote respectively the negative and positive parts of a scalar or of a symmetric tensor:

$$
\sigma (\epsilon ,b)\text{}=\text{}B(b)\left[\lambda {⟨\mathit{tr}(\epsilon )⟩}_{+}+2\mu {⟨\epsilon ⟩}_{+}\right]\text{}+\text{}\left[\lambda {⟨\mathit{tr}(\epsilon )⟩}_{-}+2\mu {⟨\epsilon ⟩}_{-}\right]
$$

As for the stiffness function $B(b)$ , it is equal to:

$$
B(b)\text{}=\text{}\frac{1-b}{\left(1-b\right)+\frac{\kappa}{2}b\left({m}_{0}+({D}_{1}-{m}_{0}){b}^{r-1}\right)}
$$

The evolution of the damage is governed by the following Kuhn–Tucker conditions:

$$
\dot{b}⩾0;Q(\epsilon )-\varphi (b)⩽0;\dot{b}\left[Q(\epsilon )-\varphi (b)\right]=0
$$

The threshold function $\varphi (b)$ has the expression:

$$
\varphi (b)={\left[(1-b)+\frac{\kappa}{2}b\left({m}_{0}+({D}_{1}-{m}_{0}){b}^{r-1}\right)\right]}^{2}
$$

As for the function of the strain $Q(\epsilon )$ , it is written:

$$
Q(\epsilon )={\epsilon}_{\text{*}}^{2}
$$

In this expression, the pseudo-norm ${\epsilon}_{\text{*}}$ is the solution of the following scalar equation:

$$
{f}_{\sigma}\left(\frac{\lambda \mathit{tr}(\epsilon )\text{Id}+2\mu \epsilon }{{\epsilon}_{\text{*}}}\right)=0
$$

Finally, the initiation threshold has the expression:

$$
{f}_{\sigma}(\sigma )=\Vert \frac{\sigma}{{\sigma}_{0}}+\left({\beta}_{0}-\frac{1}{3}\right)\mathrm{tr}\left(\frac{\sigma}{{\sigma}_{0}}\right)\text{Id}\Vert +\Vert \exp\left(\frac{\sigma}{{\sigma}_{0}}\right)\Vert -{\gamma}_{0}
$$

The stiffness function $B(b)$ which appears in the stress–strain relation measures the impact of the damage level on the residual stiffness (in tension). Its particular form, as well as that of the threshold $\varphi (b)$, is guided by consistency with the response of the law ENDO_FISS_EXP in a confined uniaxial tension test. It plays the same role as the more classical form $(1-b)$ used in a number of models in the literature.

### 2.2 Elastic energy and tension–compression transition

In order to take into account the recovery of stiffness in compression, the elasticity law is no longer linear, even though it remains hyperelastic (*i.e.* it derives from an energy) in order to avoid any spurious production or consumption of energy. It is similar to the formulation adopted for the law ENDO_ISOT_BETON [R7.01.04](doc:r7.01.04); it takes into account not only the sign of the principal strains but also that of the trace of the strains. On the practical level, it therefore requires the principal strains to be determined. Its value is supplied among the internal variables (ENERELAS).

Its properties are studied in detail in [Badel et al., 2007]. One will retain that it is continuously differentiable, which ensures the continuity of the stress–strain relation. On the other hand, it is not twice continuously differentiable, which means that the stiffness presents a jump when the tension–compression transition is crossed. This may harm the convergence of the analysis; this is why one proposes to regularise this transition.

The idea, set out in [Lorentz, 2017] and [Lorentz, 2020], consists in regularising the elastic energy. This has as a consequence on the stress–strain relation () that the positive and negative parts in it are approximated by infinitely differentiable functions:

$$
{⟨x⟩}_{-}\simeq \text{}{N}_{\gamma}(x);{⟨x⟩}_{+}\simeq \text{}x-{N}_{\gamma}(x)
$$

The approximation ${N}_{\gamma}$ depends on a parameter $\gamma >0$ which tends to infinity in the absence of regularisation. It has the expression:

$$
{N}_{\gamma}(x)=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}\left(x+\frac{1}{2\gamma }\right)\exp\left(-\text{}\frac{1}{\gamma x}\right)& \text{si}x<0\\ 0& \text{si}x⩾0\end{array}
$$

### 2.3 Parameters of the model

One can list the parameters used in the constitutive equations. These are $\lambda$, $\mu$, ${\sigma}_{0}$, ${\gamma}_{0}$, ${\beta}_{0}$, $\kappa$, ${m}_{0}$, ${D}_{1}$ and $r$. One proposes to give their meaning and their expression, in order finally to list those which must be supplied by the user.

**Elasticity** – The elastic characteristics, which appear in the expression of the strain energy, reduce classically to the Lamé coefficients $\lambda$ and $\mu$ or, equivalently, to Young's modulus $E$ and Poisson's ratio $\nu$. One deduces from them in particular the confined stiffness ${E}_{c}=\lambda +2\mu$.

**Damage initiation threshold** – The initiation threshold is defined via the function ${f}_{\sigma}$ which depends on three parameters: ${\sigma}_{0}$, ${\gamma}_{0}$ and ${\beta}_{0}$. In practice, one fixes ${\beta}_{0}$ at 0.1 and one determines ${\sigma}_{0}$ and ${\tau}_{0}$ as a function of the tensile strength ${f}_{t}$ and the compressive strength ${f}_{c}$ of the concrete, according to the methodology presented in [Lorentz, 2017]. These data then make it possible to compute $Q\left(\epsilon \right)$ for any strain. In particular, one can determine the initiation stress in confined uniaxial tension ${\sigma}_{c}$ by the solution of the following equation, in which $n$ denotes the (arbitrary) direction of tension:

$$
{f}_{\sigma}\left(\left(\frac{\lambda}{{E}_{c}}\mathit{Id}+\frac{2\mu }{{E}_{c}}n\otimes n\right){\sigma}_{c}\right)=0
$$

One then has access to ${w}_{c}$, the strain energy when the initiation threshold is reached in confined uniaxial tension, via ${w}_{c}={\sigma}_{c}^{2}/2{E}_{c}$.

- **Damage energy** – The fracture energy ${G}_{F}$ measures the energy consumed per unit cracked surface. It is to be related to the mean distance $L$ which separates two cracks in order to construct the volumetric energy $k={G}_{F}/L$ consumed by the damage model; one chooses to normalise the latter via $\kappa ={G}_{F}/\left(L{w}_{c}\right)$. The two parameters ${G}_{F}$ and $L$ therefore appear in the damage model only through their ratio, even though both have their own physical meaning.
- **Shape factor** – A last parameter, $p⩾1$, influences the shape of the response in the damage phase (a response which is all the more curved in a diagram $\sigma -\epsilon$ as $p$ is large). From the value of $p$, one deduces the parameters ${m}_{0}$, ${D}_{1}$ and $r$ according to the following expressions:

$$
{m}_{0}=\frac{3\pi }{2}{\left(p+2\right)}^{-\text{}\frac{3}{2}}
$$

$$
{D}_{1}=\frac{3\pi }{4}\sqrt{p+1}
$$

$$
r=\frac{2({D}_{1}-1)-{m}_{0}}{2-{m}_{0}}
$$

And from there, also knowing the normalised volumetric energy at failure $\kappa$, one can express for any damage value the stiffness function $B(b)$ and the threshold $\varphi \left(b\right)$. The datum of $p$ also limits the possible values for $\kappa$ for questions of stability of the material point, which, indirectly, fixes an upper bound ${L}_{c}$ on the inter-crack distance $L$ via the following inequality:

$$
L\text{}⩽\text{}{L}_{c}=\frac{{G}_{F}}{{w}_{c}}\frac{3\pi }{4}{(p+2)}^{-\text{}\frac{3}{2}}
$$

- **Regularisation of the tension–compression transition** – The last parameter, $\gamma$, conditions the regularisation of the positive and negative part functions appearing in the stress–strain relation, as explained in § 2.2. Alternatively to supplying a value for $\gamma$, one may prefer to fix the proportion of the stiffness (without regularisation) which is recovered for a (compressive) strain of the order of ${f}_{c}/E$, a proportion which one denotes ${\rho}_{\gamma}$.

Finally, relying on the command DEFI_MATERIAU, one is led to supply the following nine parameters: $E$, $\nu$, ${\sigma}_{\mathrm{c}}$, ${\sigma}_{0}$, ${\gamma}_{0}$, ${\beta}_{0}$, $\kappa$, $p$ and $\gamma$. Default values are proposed: ${\beta}_{0}=0.1$ and $\gamma =0$ (no regularisation). Alternatively, the command DEFI_MATER_GC makes it possible to supply these parameters in a more usual manner. One indeed supplies there the following ten parameters: $E$, $\nu$, ${f}_{t}$, ${f}_{c}$, ${\gamma}_{0}$, ${\beta}_{0}$, ${G}_{F}$, $L$, $p$ and ${\rho}_{\gamma}$. Here too, two default values are proposed: ${\beta}_{0}=0.1$ and ${\rho}_{\gamma}=0.95$ (a regularisation judged reasonable).

## 3 Numerical integration

### 3.1 Discretised equations

The discretisation in time of the constitutive equations is based on an implicit Euler scheme, that is to say that the various variables of the problem are expressed at the final time instant of the time step. The expression of the stress at the end of the time step thus obeys (), the strain and the damage also being expressed there at the end of the time step. There remains the delicate part, which consists in computing the evolution of the damage.

Integrating the evolution equation of the damage is a non-linear scalar problem. It results from the time discretisation of the Kuhn–Tucker conditions (), indexing by $n$ the quantities at the beginning of the time step and omitting any index for those at the end of the time step:

$$
b-{b}_{n}⩾0;Q(\epsilon )-\varphi (b)⩽0;(b-{b}_{n})\left[Q(\epsilon )-\varphi (b)\right]=0
$$

It is then a matter of determining the value of $b$, $\epsilon$ being known; this is what one calls the integration of the constitutive law.

It must also be noted that the strain $\epsilon$ corresponds to the mechanical part of the strains, that is to say the total strain from which one has removed the shrinkage strains, in particular the thermal strain (expansion). In the terminology specific to code Aster (Code_Aster), this is a law expressed in mechanical strain.

### 3.2 Solution of the algorithmic evolution equation

In order to treat the alternative (), one takes up the method described in [Lorentz, 2020], where the existence and the uniqueness of the solution $b$ have also been demonstrated. One begins by evaluating the function $Q(\epsilon )$; this involves the solution of the scalar equation (). The reader is referred to document [R5.03.28](doc:r5.03.28) for the solution method, the function $Q$ being equal, up to a factor, to the function $\Gamma$ defined in that booklet.

One then proceeds to an elastic trial by supposing that $b={b}_{n}$. If $Q(\epsilon )-\varphi ({b}_{n})⩽0$, then this is the solution; this is the elastic regime. Otherwise, one examines the ultimate value $b=1$. If $Q(\epsilon )-\varphi (1)⩾0$, then this is the solution; this is the saturated regime. Finally, the last case, the damaging regime, the solution $b$ is the root of the following equation, lying between ${b}_{n}$ and 1:

$$
\varphi (b)-Q(\epsilon )=0
$$

The left-hand side is increasing with $b$ and of opposite sign at the two bounds. The solution is carried out by a Newton method with controlled bounds.

The accuracy with which the two scalar equations are solved, that for evaluating $Q(\epsilon )$ and (), is fixed indirectly by the user, so that the error committed on the stress is less than the tensile limit ${\sigma}_{c}$ multiplied by the parameter RESI_INTE_RELA of STAT_NON_LINE. One may refer to [Lorentz, 2020] for the computation of the accuracies expected for these two scalar equations.

### 3.3 Tangent matrix

The tangent matrix is obtained by variation of the stress in () with respect to the strain. For that, one begins by denoting ${E}_{+}(\epsilon )$ and ${E}_{-}(\epsilon )$ the positive and negative parts of the elasticity operator:

$$
{E}_{+}(\epsilon )=\lambda {⟨\mathit{tr}(\epsilon )⟩}_{+}+2\mu {⟨\epsilon ⟩}_{+};{E}_{-}(\epsilon )=\lambda {⟨\mathit{tr}(\epsilon )⟩}_{-}+2\mu {⟨\epsilon ⟩}_{-}
$$

By differentiating (), one then has:

$$
\delta \sigma =\left[B(b)\frac{{\mathit{dE}}_{+}}{d\epsilon }(\epsilon )+\frac{{\mathit{dE}}_{-}}{d\epsilon }(\epsilon )\right]:\delta \epsilon +{E}_{+}(\epsilon )\frac{\mathit{dB}}{\mathit{db}}\delta b
$$

In the elastic or saturated regime, the last term is zero (no variation of $b$). In the damaging regime, the variation of $b$ is obtained by differentiating equation (). This leads to:

$$
\delta b={\left(\frac{d\varphi }{\mathit{db}}(b)\right)}^{-1}\frac{\mathit{dQ}}{d\epsilon }:d\epsilon
$$

The delicate part then consists in differentiating $\Gamma$, ${E}_{+}$ and ${E}_{-}$ with respect to $\epsilon$. One refers for that to document [R5.03.28](doc:r5.03.28), in which these operations are detailed. Although these quantities depend on the principal strains, one may note that the differentiation methodology which is implemented does without the derivative of the associated eigenvectors which, one recalls, are not defined when the eigenvalues are not distinct.

## 4 Piloting by elastic prediction

This being a damage law, material instabilities may appear. In order to make it possible to follow them, one recommends the use of a method of piloting by elastic prediction (PRED_ELAS) in the absence of command variables (such as a thermal transient) and of loadings depending on the physical time; see document [R5.03.80](doc:r5.03.80).

The piloting increment, denoted $\Delta \tau$, is interpreted here as a target maximum damage increment. At an integration point, it is then a matter of determining the value or values of the piloting parameter $\eta$ which place the strain on the damage threshold. At this stage, the strain is expressed as an affine function of $\eta$, that is to say $\epsilon ={\epsilon}_{0}+\eta {\epsilon}_{1}$. Given the expression of the consistency condition (), it follows that $\eta$ is the solution of the following scalar equation:

$$
Q({\epsilon}_{0}+\eta {\epsilon}_{1})=\varphi ({b}_{n}+\tau )
$$

Given the convexity of $Q$, this equation admits at most two solutions. One details in [Lorentz, 2020] how to establish a bounded interval in which to look for these solutions and the application of a Newton method in order to determine them.
