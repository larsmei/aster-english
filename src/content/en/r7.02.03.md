# r7.02.03 Energy release rate in non-linear thermoelasticity

**Summary:**

The calculation of the energy release rate by the theta method in 2D or in 3D is presented for a non-linear thermoelastic problem. The non-linear elastic constitutive relation is described in [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20).

## 1 Calculation of the energy release rate by the theta method in non-linear thermoelasticity

### 1.1 Constitutive relation

A cracked solid is considered, occupying the domain $\Omega$ of the space ${R}^{2}$ or ${R}^{3}$. Let:

- $u$ be the displacement field,
- $T$ the temperature field,
- $f$ the field of body forces applied on $\Omega$,
- $g$ the field of surface forces applied on a part $S$ of $\partial \Omega$,
- $U$ the field of displacements imposed on a part ${S}_{d}$ of $\partial \Omega$.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.03/100007A0000011FC00000C9CC2DE366FF99B1574.svg)

The behaviour of the solid is assumed non-linear elastic, such that the constitutive relation coincides with the elastoplastic Hencky–von Mises law (isotropic hardening) in the case of a loading which induces a radial and monotonic evolution at every point. This model is chosen in the commands CALC_G by means of the keyword RELATION='ELAS_VMIS_LINE' or 'ELAS_VMIS_TRAC' or 'ELAS_VMIS_PUIS' under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT [R5.03.20](doc:r5.03.20).

One denotes by:

- $\varepsilon$ the strain tensor,
- $\varepsilon °$ the tensor of the initial strains,
- $\sigma$ the stress tensor,
- $\sigma °$ the tensor of the initial stresses,
- $\Psi (\varepsilon ,\varepsilon °,\sigma °,T)$ the free-energy density.

$\varepsilon$ is related to the displacement field $u$ by:

$\varepsilon (u)=\frac{1}{2}({u}_{i,j}+{u}_{j,i})$

The free-energy density $\Psi (\varepsilon ,\varepsilon °,\sigma °,T)$ is a convex and differentiable function, known for a given state [R5.03.20éq3]. The constitutive relation of the material is written in the form:

${\sigma}_{ij}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{ij}}(\varepsilon ,\varepsilon °,\sigma °,T)$

It derives from the free-energy potential. For this hyperelastic constitutive relation, a meaning can be given to the energy release rate within the framework of the global approach in fracture mechanics. This is not the case for a plastic constitutive relation.

### 1.2 Potential energy and equilibrium relations

The spaces of kinematically admissible fields $V$ and ${V}_{0}$ are defined.

$\begin{array}{}V=\lbrace v\text{admissibles},v=U\text{sur}{S}_{d}\rbrace \\ {V}_{0}=\lbrace v\text{admissibles},v=O\text{sur}{S}_{d}\rbrace \end{array}$

With the assumptions of paragraph [§1.1] (with $\varepsilon °=\sigma °=0$), the equilibrium relations in weak form are:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}u\in V& \\ \underset{\Omega}{\int}{\sigma}_{ij}{v}_{i,j}d\Omega =\underset{\Omega}{\int}{f}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{g}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Gamma ,& \forall v\in {V}_{0}\end{array}$

They are obtained by minimising the global potential energy of the system:

$W(v)=\underset{\Omega}{\int}\Psi (\varepsilon (v),T)d\Omega =\underset{\Omega}{\int}{f}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{g}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Gamma$

The demonstration is identical to that in linear elasticity [R7.02.01§1.2].

### 1.3 Lagrangian expression of the energy release rate

Let $m$ be the unit normal to ${\Gamma}_{0}$ lying in the plane tangent to $\partial \Omega$ in $\Omega$.

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.03/1000041C0000102000000BE38C0EA4EADD9D0DCF.svg)

Let the field $\theta$ be such that:

$\theta \in \Theta =\lbrace \mu \text{tels que}\mu .n=0\text{sur}\Omega \rbrace$

denoting by $n$ the normal to $\partial \Omega$.

The energy release rate $G$ is the solution of the variational equation:

${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{o}}G\theta \cdot m\text{dS}=G(\theta ),\forall \theta \in \Theta$

where $G(\theta )$ is defined by:

$\begin{array}{cc}G(\theta )& ={\int}_{\Omega}{\sigma}_{ij}{u}_{i,p}{\theta}_{p,j}-\Psi {\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T}{T}_{\text{,k}}{\theta}_{k}d\Omega \\ & +{\int}_{\Omega}({\sigma}_{ij}-\frac{1}{2}{\sigma}_{ij}^{°}){\varepsilon}_{ij,k}^{°}{\theta}_{k}-({\varepsilon}_{ij}-{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{\text{th}}-\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{°}){\sigma}_{ij,k}{\theta}_{k}d\Omega \\ & +{\int}_{\Omega}{f}_{i}{u}_{i}{\theta}_{k,k}+{f}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{u}_{i}d\Omega \\ & +{\int}_{S}{g}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{u}_{i}+{g}_{i}{u}_{i}({\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {n}_{k}}{n}_{k})d\Gamma \\ & -{\int}_{{S}_{d}}{\sigma}_{ij}{n}_{j}{U}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}d\Gamma \end{array}$

The demonstration is identical to that of the calculation of $G$ in linear elasticity [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01). The expression is the same, so the post-processing is identical.

### 1.4 Implementation of G in non-linear thermoelasticity

The types of elements and of loadings, and the necessary environment, are the same as for the implementation of $G$ in linear thermoelasticity [R7.02.01 §2.4].

For the calculation of the various terms of $G(\theta )$, at a given state, the free-energy density $\Psi (\varepsilon ,T)$, the strains $\varepsilon$ and the stresses $\sigma$ are recovered, computed for the non-linear constitutive relation.

It is assumed that $\varepsilon °=\sigma °=0$ (a term identical in linear or non-linear thermoelasticity). The free-energy density is then written [R5.03.20 §1.5]:

- in linear thermoelasticity:

$\Psi (\varepsilon ,T)=\frac{1}{2}K{({\varepsilon}_{kk}-3\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{réf}}))}^{2}+\frac{2\mu }{3}{\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}^{2}$

with

$\begin{array}{}{\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}^{2}=\frac{3}{2}{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{D}{\varepsilon}_{ij}^{D}=\frac{3}{2}({\varepsilon}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\varepsilon}_{kk}{\delta}_{ij})({\varepsilon}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\varepsilon}_{kk}{\delta}_{ij})\\ {\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}^{2}=\frac{3}{2}({\varepsilon}_{ij}{\varepsilon}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\varepsilon}_{kk}^{2})\end{array}$

- in non-linear thermoelasticity $(2\mu {\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}\ge {\sigma}_{y})$:

$\Psi (\varepsilon ,T)=\frac{1}{2}K{({\varepsilon}_{kk}-3\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{réf}}))}^{2}+\frac{1}{6\mu }R{(p({\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}))}^{2}+{\int}_{0}^{p({\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}})}R(s)\text{ds}$

with $R(p({\varepsilon}_{\text{eq}}))$: hardening function.

For a linear isotropic hardening (RELATION= ‘ELAS_VMIS_LINE’) one has:

$\begin{array}{}R(p)={\sigma}_{y}+p\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}={\sigma}_{y}+ap\\ p=\frac{{\sigma}_{\text{eq}}-{\sigma}_{y}}{3\mu +a}\text{avec}a=\frac{E{E}_{T}}{E-{E}_{T}}\\ A(p)={\int}_{0}^{p}R(s)\text{ds}={\sigma}_{\text{yp}}+\frac{1}{2}a{p}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}{\sigma}_{y}p+\frac{p}{2}({\sigma}_{y}+ap)\\ A(p)=\frac{p}{2}({\sigma}_{y}+R(p))\end{array}$

The post-processing is then identical to the problem in linear elasticity, except for the thermal term:

$\text{THER}=-\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T}{T}_{,k}{\theta}_{k}$

If the Lamé coefficients $\lambda (T)$ and $\mu (T)$ are independent of temperature, this term is zero. Otherwise, $\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T}(\varepsilon ,T)$ must be computed at a given time instant.

For a linear isotropic hardening, one has:

$\begin{array}{cc}\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T}& (\varepsilon ,T)=\left[\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{dK}(T)}{\text{dT}}({\varepsilon}_{kk}-3\alpha (T-{T}_{\text{réf}}))-\mathrm{3K}(\alpha +\frac{d\alpha (T)}{\text{dT}}(T-{T}_{\text{réf}}))\right]({\varepsilon}_{kk}-3\alpha T-{T}_{\text{réf}})\\ & +\frac{R(p)}{6{\mu}^{2}}\left[2\mu \frac{\text{dR}(P)}{\text{dT}}-\frac{d\mu (T)}{\text{dT}}R(p)\right]+\frac{\text{dA}(p)}{\text{dT}}\end{array}$

with

$\begin{array}{c}\frac{\text{dR}(p)}{\text{dT}}=\frac{d{\sigma}_{y}(T)}{\text{dT}}+\frac{da(T)}{\text{dT}}p+a\frac{dp(T)}{\text{dT}}\\ \frac{da(T)}{\text{dT}}=\frac{1}{{(E-{E}_{T})}^{2}}(\frac{{\text{d E}}_{T}(T)}{\text{dT}}{E}^{2}-\frac{dE(T)}{\text{dT}}E{}_{T}{}^{2}\text{})\\ \frac{\mathrm{dp}(T)}{\text{dT}}=\frac{1}{{(3\mu +a)}^{2}}\left[({\sigma}_{y}-{\sigma}_{\text{eq}})(3\frac{d\mu (T)}{\text{dT}}+\frac{\text{dA}(T)}{\text{dT}})-(3\mu +a)\frac{d{\sigma}_{y}(T)}{\text{dT}}\right]\\ \frac{\text{dA}(p)}{\text{dT}}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\text{dp}(T)}{\text{dT}}({\sigma}_{y}+{R}_{p})+\frac{1}{2}p(\frac{d{\sigma}_{y}(T)}{\text{dT}}+\frac{\text{d Rp}(T)}{\text{dT}})\end{array}$

### 1.5 Warning

**Warning!** By definition, in the general case: $\Psi (\varepsilon ,T)\ne \sigma :\varepsilon$

Although it is possible to carry out an elastoplastic analysis followed by the calculation of

## 2 Calculation of the energy release rate by the theta method in large transformations

The constitutive relation of [§1] is extended to large displacements and large rotations, in so far as it derives from a potential (hyperelastic law). This functionality is triggered by the keyword DEFORMATION='GROT_GDEP' in the command CALC_G.

### 2.1 Constitutive relation

One denotes by:

- $\mathrm{E}$ the Green–Lagrange strain tensor,
- $\mathrm{S}$ the second Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor,
- $\Psi (E)$ the internal-energy density.

The behaviour of the solid is assumed hyperelastic, namely:

- $E$ is related to the displacement field $u$ measured with respect to the reference configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ by:

${E}_{ij}(u)=\frac{1}{2}({u}_{i,j}+{u}_{j,i}+{u}_{k,i}{u}_{k,j})$

- $S$ is related to the Cauchy stress tensor

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.03/Object_68.svg)

by:

${S}_{ij}=\det(F){F}_{\mathrm{ik}}^{\text{-1}}{T}_{kl}{F}_{\mathrm{jl}}^{\text{-1}}$

$F$ being the gradient of the transformation which takes one from the reference configuration ${\Omega}_{0}$ to the current configuration $\Omega$, related to the displacement by:

${F}_{ij}=({\delta}_{ij}+{u}_{i,j})$

The constitutive relation of a hyperelastic material is written in the form:

${S}_{ij}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{ij}}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{jj}}={S}_{ji}$

This relation describes a non-linear elastic behaviour, analogous to that of [§1.1]. It offers the possibility of treating fracture-mechanics problems without incorporating plasticity into them. And in the case of a monotonic radial loading, it makes it possible to obtain strains and stresses of the structure similar to those which would be obtained if the material presented an isotropic hardening. The hyperelastic material has a reversible mechanical behaviour, that is any loading cycle generates no dissipation.

This model is chosen in the command CALC_G [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03) by means of the keyword:

RELATION: 'ELAS'

for a “linear” elastic relation, that is the relation between the strains and the stresses considered is linear,

RELATION: 'ELAS_VMIS_LINE' or 'ELAS_VMIS_TRAC' or 'ELAS_VMIS_PUIS'

for a “non-linear” elastic constitutive relation (Hencky–von Mises law with isotropic hardening).

Such a constitutive relation makes it possible, strictly speaking, to take large strains and large rotations into account. However, one confines oneself to small strains in order to ensure the existence of a solution and in order to be identical to an elastoplastic behaviour under a monotonic radial loading [R5.03.20§2.1].

### 2.2 Potential energy and equilibrium relations

The loading considered reduces to a non-follower surface density $R$ applied on a part ${\Gamma}_{0}$ of the boundary of ${\Omega}_{0}$ (dead-load assumption [R5.03.20 §2.2]).

A space of kinematically admissible fields $V$ is defined:

$V=\lbrace v\text{admissibles,}v=0\text{sur}{\Gamma}_{0}\rbrace$

The equilibrium relations in weak form are:

$\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{F}_{\mathrm{ik}}{S}_{\mathrm{kj}}{v}_{i,j}d{\Omega}_{0}=\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Gamma$

They may be obtained by minimising the global potential energy of the system:

$W(v)=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\Psi (E(v))d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}{v}_{i}d\Gamma$

Indeed, if this functional is minimal for the displacement field $u$, then:

$\begin{array}{cc}\delta W& =\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{ij}}\delta {E}_{ij}d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}\delta {v}_{i}d\Gamma =0,\forall \delta v\in V\\ & =\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{S}_{ij}\frac{1}{2}(\delta {v}_{i,j}+\delta {v}_{j,i}+\delta {v}_{p,i}{u}_{p,j}+{u}_{p,i}\delta {v}_{p,j})d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}\delta {v}_{i}d\Gamma \\ & =\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{S}_{ij}({\delta}_{\mathrm{ip}}+{u}_{p,i})\delta {v}_{p,i}d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}\delta {v}_{i}d\Gamma \\ & =\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{F}_{pi}{S}_{ij}\delta {v}_{p,j}d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}\delta {v}_{i}d\Gamma \\ & =\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{F}_{\mathrm{ik}}{S}_{\mathrm{kj}}\delta {v}_{i,j}d\Omega -\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{R}_{i}\delta {v}_{i}d\Gamma =0\end{array}$

The equilibrium equations and the constitutive relation are thus recovered, having set:

${S}_{ij}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{ij}}$

### 2.3 Lagrangian expression of the energy release rate in non-linear thermoelasticity and in large transformations

By definition, the energy release rate $G$ is defined by the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy at equilibrium with respect to the domain $\Omega$ [bib1]. It is computed by the theta method, which is a Lagrangian method of derivation of the potential energy [bib4] and [bib2]. Transformations ${F}^{\eta}:M\to M+\eta \theta (M)$ of the domain are considered, taking ${\Omega}_{0}$ to a domain ${\Omega}_{0}$ which correspond to propagations of the crack. To these families of reference configurations thus defined ${\Omega}_{\eta}$ there correspond families of deformed configurations in which the crack has propagated. The energy release rate $G$ is then the opposite of the derivative of the potential energy $W(u(\eta ))$ at equilibrium with respect to the initial evolution of the crack front: $\eta$

$G=-{(\frac{\text{d}W(u(\eta ))}{\text{d}\eta })}_{\eta =0}$

As in [bib4], . denotes the Lagrangian derivative in a virtual crack propagation of velocity $\theta$. Let $\varphi (\eta ,M)$ be an arbitrary field ($\eta$ a positive real number and $M$ belonging to the domain ${\Omega}_{0}$); one will denote:

$\bar{\varphi}(\eta ,M)=\bar{\varphi}(\eta ,{F}^{\eta}M)\text{et}\dot{\varphi}={(\frac{\partial \bar{\varphi}}{\partial \eta })}_{\eta =0}$

The potential energy defined on ${\Omega}_{\eta}$ is brought back onto ${\Omega}_{0}$,

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.03/Object_102.svg)

is assumed independent of $\eta$; derivation with respect to the propagation parameter $\eta$ is then straightforward, and the energy release rate in this propagation is the solution of the variational equation:

${\int}_{{\Gamma}_{o}}G\theta \cdot m\text{dS}=G(\theta ),\forall \theta \in \Theta$

with:

$-G(\theta )={\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}\stackrel{.}{\stackrel{}{(\Psi (E,T))}}+\Psi (E,T){\theta}_{k,k}\text{d}\Omega -{\int}_{\Gamma}{R}_{i}\dot{{u}_{i}}+{R}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{u}_{i}+{R}_{i}{u}_{i}({\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {n}_{k}}{n}_{k})\text{d}\Gamma$

Now:

$\stackrel{.}{\stackrel{}{(\Psi (E,T))}}=\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{ij}}{\dot{E}}_{ij}+\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial T}\dot{T}$

In what follows, only the term $\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {E}_{ij}}{\dot{E}}_{ij}$ will be considered, the thermal term being treated in the same way as in small displacements [R7.02.01](doc:r7.02.01).

And according to proposition 2 of [bib4]:

$\begin{array}{c}{\dot{\mathrm{E}}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}({\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{i,j}+{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{j,i}+{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{k,i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{k,j})\\ \phantom{{\dot{\mathrm{E}}}_{ij}}-\frac{1}{2}({\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{j,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},i}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},j})\end{array}$

One can eliminate $\dot{u}$ from the expression of

![Figure](/figures/r7.02.03/Object_111.svg)

as in small strains, by noting that $\dot{u}$ is kinematically admissible (cf. [bib3] for the regularity problems) and by using the equilibrium equation:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}\stackrel{.}{\stackrel{}{(\Psi (E))}}\text{d}\Omega -{\int}_{\Gamma}{R}_{i}\dot{{u}_{i}}\text{d}\Gamma =$

$\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\mathrm{E}}_{ij}}\frac{1}{2}({\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{i,j}+{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{j,i}+{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{k,i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{k,j})d\Omega \\ -{\int}_{\Gamma}{\mathrm{R}}_{i}{\dot{\mathrm{u}}}_{i}d\Gamma -{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial {\mathrm{E}}_{ij}}\frac{1}{2}({\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{j,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},i}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j})d\Omega \\ =-{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}{\mathrm{S}}_{ij}({\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j})d\Omega \\ =-{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}{\mathrm{S}}_{ij}({\mathrm{d}}_{\text{ki}}+{\mathrm{u}}_{k,i}){\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j}d\Omega \\ =-{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}{\mathrm{S}}_{ij}{\mathrm{F}}_{\text{ki}}{\mathrm{u}}_{k,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},j}d\Omega \\ =-{\int}_{{\Omega}_{o}}{\mathrm{F}}_{\text{ik}}{\mathrm{S}}_{\text{kj}}{\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\mathrm{q}}_{\mathrm{p},j}d\Omega \end{array}$

Finally, one obtains:

$G(\theta )=\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{ik}}{\mathrm{S}}_{\mathrm{kj}}({\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j})-\Psi (\mathrm{E}){\theta}_{k,k}\text{d}\Omega +\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{\mathrm{R}}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}+{\mathrm{R}}_{i}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}({\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {\mathrm{n}}_{k}}{\mathrm{n}}_{k})\text{d}\Gamma$

The complete expression for the following loadings:

- non-follower surface density $R$ applied on a part $\Gamma$ of the boundary of ${\Omega}_{0}$,
- non-follower body density $\mathrm{f}$ applied on the domain $\Omega$,

and taking the thermal part into account:

$\begin{array}{cc}G(\theta )=& \underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{\mathrm{F}}_{\mathrm{ik}}{\mathrm{S}}_{\mathrm{kj}}({\mathrm{u}}_{i,\mathrm{p}}{\theta}_{\mathrm{p},j})-\Psi (\mathrm{E}){\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \Psi }{\partial \mathrm{T}}{\mathrm{T}}_{,k}{\theta}_{k}\text{d}\Omega \\ & +\underset{{\Omega}_{0}}{\int}{\mathrm{f}}_{i}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}{\theta}_{k,k}+{\mathrm{f}}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}\text{d}\Omega \\ & +\underset{\Gamma}{\int}{\mathrm{R}}_{i,k}{\theta}_{k}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}+{\mathrm{R}}_{i}{\mathrm{u}}_{i}({\theta}_{k,k}-\frac{\partial \theta }{\partial {\mathrm{n}}_{k}}{\mathrm{n}}_{k})\text{d}\Gamma \end{array}$

### 2.4 Implementation

Comparison of the formulae for $G(\theta )$ of [§1.3] and of [§1.4] shows that the terms of $G(\theta )$ are very close. The introduction of large transformations requires little modification in post-processing.

Use of the command CALC_G in large transformations does not require any particular syntax. The presence of the keyword DEFORMATION='GREEN_LAGRANGE' under the factor keyword COMPORTEMENT of the command STAT_NON_LINE then indicates to the command CALC_G that it is necessary to recover the Piola–Kirchhoff stress tensor $S$ and the gradient of the transformation $F$ (routines NMGEOM and NMELNL).

The types of finite elements are the same as in linear elasticity [R7.02.01 §2.4]. They are the 2D and 3D isoparametric elements.

The loadings supported are those supported in linear elasticity, provided that they are dead loads: typically an imposed force is a dead load, whereas pressure is a follower loading since it depends on the orientation of the surface, and therefore on the transformation. This restriction also applies to the boundary conditions in displacement.

### 2.5 Restriction

With the constitutive relation specified in §2, one has a formulation of $G$ valid for large strains for hyperelastic materials, but... if one wishes consistency with the real material which, it is recalled, is elastoplastic, it is imperative to confine oneself to small strains, the displacements and the rotations being allowed to be large.

The conditions of proportional and monotonic loadings, indispensable for ensuring the consistency of the model with the real material, lead to important restrictions on the field of problems capable of being treated by this method (the thermal part, in particular, may lead to local unloadings). It can therefore only be a palliative solution, until one is in a position to give a meaning to the energy release rate within the framework of plastic behaviours.
