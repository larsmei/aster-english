# r7.01.21 Constitutive law (in 2D) for the steel–concrete bond: JOINT_BA

**Summary:**

The constitutive law JOINT_BA describes the phenomenon of degradation and of failure of the bond between the steel bars and the concrete, in reinforced-concrete structures. This documentation presents the theoretical writing within the thermodynamic framework and the numerical integration of the law, as well as the parameters which govern the model.

For its use, one will rely on the joint-type finite elements (see the document[R3.06.09]) already existing in the code.

## 1 Introduction

The constitutive law JOINT_BA describes the phenomenon of degradation and of failure of the existing bond between the steel bars (smooth or ribbed) and the concrete surrounding them. A key point for the design of reinforced-concrete structures, the modelling of the steel–concrete bond has as its aim the representation as well as the simplification of this complex phenomenon of interaction between the two materials, which develops in the interface and which undergoes an increasing degradation when one exceeds certain strength thresholds, specific to each material. The structural models which do not take the effects of the bond into account are generally incapable of predicting the localisation of the cracks as well as the networks created. Moreover, the degradation of the stiffness of the bond increases the vibration period, reduces the energy-dissipation capacity and leads to a significant redistribution of the internal forces (according to *Bertero* , 1979, cf. ).

The constitutive law JOINT_BA is described within the framework of the thermodynamics of irreversible processes: the writing and the use of a “ classical ” material model coupling cracking and friction makes it possible to integrate in a robust manner fine non-linear mechanisms concomitant with the particular description of the slip kinematics. This last point allows us not to resort to the classical modellings of ‘contact’ type very often used in this context despite the numerous sources of numerical instabilities. Thus, under monotonic loading the taking into account of the normal-force–shear coupling makes it possible to treat cases of strong multiaxial confinement; under cyclic loading, the hysteretic behaviour and the corresponding dissipations are expressed thanks to the coupling between the damage state and the kinematic hardening. The use of an implicit scheme makes it possible to obtain a robust implementation.

The paragraph r7.01.21-breve-description describes in brief form the phenomenon of the steel–concrete bond. The paragraph r7.01.21-ecriture-theorique presents the thermodynamic writing of the constitutive law, whereas the paragraph r7.01.21-integration-numerique specifies the numerical-integration stage of the law. The parameters which govern the model and which can be obtained from the properties of the materials involved are described in the paragraph r7.01.21-parametres-loi.

## 2 Brief description of the steel–concrete bond

Conceptually, the bond phenomenon corresponds to the physical interaction of two different materials, which occurs over an interface zone, allowing the transfer and the continuity of the forces and of the stresses between the two bodies in contact. In the case of reinforced-concrete structures, this phenomenon is also known as the «tension stiffness» which develops around a reinforcing element, partially or totally embedded in a volume of concrete. The tensile forces which appear inside the reinforcement are transformed into shear stresses on the surface, and are transmitted directly to the concrete in contact, which will finally balance them, and vice versa. The response of the assembly will depend on the capacity of the concrete to deform as much as the steel, since the steel will tend to slip inside the concrete surrounding it. The bond phenomenon corresponds to this capacity of the concrete to deform and to degrade locally by creating a kind of layer, or envelope, around the reinforcement, whose kinematic and material properties differ from those of the rest of the concrete or of the reinforcement used.

The phenomenon can be decomposed into three well-defined mechanisms:

- a bond of chemical origin,
- a friction mechanism between two rough surfaces (steel–concrete or concrete–concrete),
- a mechanical action created by the presence of the ribs of the steel bar on the neighbouring concrete.

From this decomposition, one can deduce clearly that for a smooth bar, the predominant mechanism is the friction between the two materials, whereas for a ribbed bar (commonly called in French «armature HA: Haute Adhérence»), the dominant mechanism is the mechanical interaction between surfaces. When the reinforcement is constituted by strands with steel cables, it is possible to control or to combine the various mechanisms since they are a direct function of the surface of the cables.

The bond will undergo a different degradation according to the type of loading applied, whether monotonic or cyclic. Moreover, among the most important parameters which influence the behaviour of the bond, one may cite:

1. the characteristics of the loading,
2. the geometric characteristics of the steel bar,
3. the spacing between active bars,
4. the characteristics of the concrete,
5. the confinement by passive reinforcement,
6. the lateral pressure.

When studying a cylindrical bar embedded in an infinite medium, one can identify the surface of discontinuity where one will place the effects of the bond, which develops in a certain zone of cracked and crushed concrete around the steel bar. At a given moment, this surface will correspond to the cylindrical crack created during the coalescence of the shear cracks. Looking at the crack network, one may suppose that, under ideal conditions, the cracking plane is always perpendicular (normal direction) to the surface of the bar and parallel (tangential direction) to its longitudinal axis (see r7.01.21-fig-liaison-elements-finis). This allows us to project the components of the displacement onto the normal and tangential direction of the cracking plane, and consequently to obtain the corresponding strains and stresses.

## 3 Theoretical formulation

The formulation presented here has been developed within the framework of the thermodynamics of irreversible processes; it gives the constitutive relation between the normal force, the shear stress and the slip, taking into account the influence of the cracking of the concrete, the friction and the various couplings in the phenomenon. For this, the constitutive relations which link the stress tensor and the strain tensor must include:

- the cracking of the interface material by shear
- the inelastic strains due to the slip
- the hysteretic behaviour due to the friction
- the coupling between the tangential response and the normal stresses

### 3.1 Presentation of the model

One places oneself within the framework of a plane formulation in 2D, in the local frame defined in r7.01.21-fig-liaison-elements-finis. The stress tensors $\sigma$ and the strain tensors $\varepsilon$ are written:

$$
\varepsilon =\begin{pmatrix}{\varepsilon}_{N}& {\varepsilon}_{\tau}\\ {\varepsilon}_{\tau}& 0\end{pmatrix}` et :math:`\sigma =\begin{pmatrix}{\sigma}_{N}& {\sigma}_{\tau}\\ {\sigma}_{\tau}& 0\end{pmatrix}
$$

where ${\sigma}_{N}$ is the normal stress and ${\sigma}_{\tau}$ is the tangential stress of the interface element; ${\varepsilon}_{N}$ corresponds to the normal strain and ${\varepsilon}_{\tau}$ to the tangential strain. The normal strain in the direction tangential to the interface is considered to be zero. This mode of strain for a bond element is one of zero strain energy.

The normal and tangential behaviours being considered as decoupled at the level of the state, the thermodynamic potential obtained from the Helmholtz free energy is expressed in the following way:

$$
\begin{array}{c}\rho \cdot \psi =\frac{1}{2}[{\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }_{-}E{\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }_{-}+{\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }_{+}E\cdot (1-{D}_{N}){\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }_{+}\\ +{\varepsilon}_{T}G(1-{D}_{T}){\varepsilon}_{T}+({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})G\cdot {D}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})+\gamma {\alpha}^{2}]+H(z)\end{array}
$$

where $\rho$ is the density, $E$ is Young's modulus, ${D}_{N}$ is the normal-damage internal variable and ${D}_{T}$ the tangential-damage internal variable, the two being linked to cracking and lying between 0 and 1. $G$ is the stiffness or shear modulus, ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{f}$ is the irreversible strain induced by slip with friction of the cracks, $\alpha$ is the kinematic-hardening internal variable, $\gamma$ is a material parameter and $z$ , the pseudo «isotropic hardening» variable by damage, with its consolidation function $H(z)$ . ${\langle \rangle }_{-}$ and ${\langle \rangle }_{+}$ define respectively the positive and negative parts of the tensor considered.

One may remark in the equation  that in the normal direction, the damage will be activated upon the appearance of the positive strains produced by tensile forces, whereas if the strains are negative because of the effects of compression, the behaviour will remain elastic. As regards the tangential part of the behaviour, one can recognise a classical elasticity–damage coupling as well as a new term making it possible to associate, with the damageable-elasticity state, a state of slip with friction. The coupling between slip and cracking is possible thanks to the presence of the damage variable as a multiplier in the second term of the right-hand side of the equation .

The state laws are obtained classically by derivation of the thermodynamic potential, and thus make it possible to define the associated thermodynamic variables. The normal stress is expressed as:

$$
{\sigma}_{N}=\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{N}}= \left\{ \begin{array}{ccc}E\cdot {\varepsilon}_{N}& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}\le 0\\ & & \\ (1-{D}_{N})\cdot E\cdot {\varepsilon}_{N}& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}>0\end{array}\right.
$$

and the total tangential stress as:

$$
{\sigma}_{T}=\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{T}}=G(1-{D}_{T}){\varepsilon}_{T}+G\cdot {D}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})
$$

One can also define the tangential stress due to slip with friction (strain ${\varepsilon}_{\tau}^{s}$ ):

$$
{\sigma}_{T}^{f}=-\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {\varepsilon}_{T}^{f}}=G\cdot {D}_{T}({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})
$$

**Note:**

Such a formulation departs considerably from a classical formulation of plasticity–damage coupling. The assumption leading to the introduction of the damage into the stress by slip is based on an experimental observation, which is that all the inelastic phenomena in a brittle material come from the growth of the cracks.

The rate of energy restored by damage–friction can be written as:

$$
-\mathrm{\Upsilon }=-\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial {D}_{T}}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{T}\cdot G\cdot {\varepsilon}_{T}-\frac{1}{2}({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})\cdot G\cdot ({\varepsilon}_{T}-{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})=-({\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{DT}}+{\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\text{fT}})
$$

In this last expression, ${\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{DT}}$ corresponds to the rate of energy restored by damage and ${\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\text{fT}}$ to the rate of energy restored by friction of the cracks.

The state law of the kinematic hardening leads to the definition of the back stress:

$$
X=\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial \alpha }=\gamma \alpha
$$

Concerning the hardening law of the isotropic damage, it is expressed by:

$$
Z=\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial z}=H'(z)
$$

We must now set out in a more detailed way the evolution of the damage mechanism in the bond, in other words specify the expression of $H(z)$ . For a small value of damage, the mechanism which predominates is the interaction of the concrete with the ribs of the steel bar, whereas for a much larger value, it is the friction between the concrete and the steel which prevails. During the evolution of the damage, 2 principal phases can be identified:

1. the first phase corresponds to a stable growth of transverse cracks linked to the presence of ribs on the steel (apparent positive hardening of the evolution law),
2. the second brings into play only the coalescence of these transverse cracks, leading one to consider only the friction mechanisms (negative hardening towards a residual friction stress).

### 3.2 Analysis of the damage in the tangential direction

The evolution law of the damage is divided into three stages:

- region of perfect bond,
- region of transition from small strains to large slips,
- region of maximum bond strength and degradation down to the ultimate residual strength.

In order to identify these regions, one establishes two thresholds:

1. the perfect-bond threshold ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}$ ,
2. the continuity threshold before coalescence of the cracks ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}$ .

Thus, taking up again the expressions linked to the damage, namely that of the energy-restitution rate  and that of the internal variable associated with the isotropic hardening , one may note:

1. a true separation between the damage and the friction of the cracks (which makes it possible to modify only the evolution law of the damage without affecting the «friction» part),
2. the partition into two parts of the isotropic hardening, since one has two different stages in the damage.

Henceforth we shall write, for the hardening linked to the damage variable:

$$
{Z}_{T}=\rho \frac{\partial \psi }{\partial z}=H(z)=\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{Z}_{\mathrm{T1}},& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}<{\varepsilon}_{T}\le {\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}\\ & & \\ {Z}_{\mathrm{T1}}\cdot {Z}_{\mathrm{T2}},& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}<{\varepsilon}_{T}\end{array}
$$

The components $Z_{\mathrm{T1}}$ and $Z_{\mathrm{T2}}$ are expressed in the following way:

$$
{Z}_{\mathit{T1}}={\left[\sqrt{{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}}+\frac{1}{{A}_{{1}_{\mathit{DT}}}}\cdot \sqrt{\frac{G}{2}}\cdot \ln((1+{z}_{T})\frac{{\varepsilon}_{T}}{{\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}})\right]}^{2}
$$

$$
{Z}_{\mathit{T2}}=\left[{y}_{\mathit{T2}}+\frac{1}{{A}_{{2}_{\mathit{DT}}}}(\frac{-{z}_{T}}{1+{z}_{T}})\right]
$$

One also defines the threshold function ${\varphi}_{\mathrm{DT}}$ which depends on ${\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{DT}}$ and which is written as:

$$
{\varphi}_{\mathrm{DT}}={\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{DT}}-({\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{T1}}+Z_{T})\le 0
$$

The thresholds which govern the evolution law of the damage are also expressed in terms of ${\mathrm{\Upsilon }}_{\mathrm{DT}}$ (see r7.01.21-fig-construction-seuils-energie). The first expression corresponds to the perfect-bond threshold and is written:

$$
{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}={Y}_{\text{elas}}{\mid}_{T}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}\cdot G\cdot {\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}
$$

Where ${Y}_{\mathit{T1}}$ is the initial damage threshold defined as a function of the limit strain of perfect bond ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}$ , which will correspond to the limit shear strain – or tensile strain – of the concrete before the initiation of the damage. Moreover, ${Y}_{\mathit{T2}}$ is the threshold of initiation of coalescence of microcracks, which is defined as a function of the initial tangential strain of the large slips ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}$ :

$$
{Y}_{\mathit{T2}}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}\cdot G\cdot {\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}
$$

*Construction of the threshold functions in terms of energy*

The evolution laws of the internal variables within the framework of the standard associated laws make it possible to obtain the derivative of the damage multiplier ${\lambda}_{D}$ :

$$
\stackrel{.}{D}={\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{D}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{D}}{\partial {Y}_{D}}={\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{D}\text{et}\stackrel{.}{z}={\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{D}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{D}}{\partial Z}=-{\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{D}
$$

By using moreover the consistency condition, one obtains the expression of the damage:

${D}_{T}=1-\sqrt{\frac{{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}}{{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}}\cdot \exp\left\lbrace {A}_{{1}_{\mathit{DT}}}\cdot {\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{G}}\cdot (\sqrt{{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}-\sqrt{{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}})\right]}^{{B}_{\mathrm{1DT}}}\right\rbrace \mathrm{\ast }$

$$
\text{*}\left\lbrace \frac{1}{1+{A}_{{2}_{\mathit{DT}}}\cdot {\langle {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}-{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}\rangle }_{+{{B}_{\mathrm{2DT}}}^{}}}\right\rbrace
$$

In this expression, one can identify the part which corresponds to the region of the passage from the ${A}_{{1}_{\mathrm{DT}}}$ small strains to large slips, with two parameters and ${B}_{{1}_{\mathrm{DT}}}$ , as well as the final damage part in mode 2, with the parameters ${A}_{{2}_{\mathrm{DT}}}$ and ${B}_{{2}_{\mathit{DT}}}$ . It must be noted that the relation $\langle {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}-{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}\rangle$ is governed by a *Macaulay* function, that is to say that this energy difference must always be positive or zero.

The functions which govern the isotropic hardening in the tangential direction are expressed as:

$$
{Z}_{\mathrm{T1}}={Y}_{\mathrm{DT}}-{Y}_{\mathrm{T1}};
$$

$$
{Z}_{\mathit{T2}}=\left\{  \begin{array}{cc}0,& \text{si}{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}<{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}\le {Y}_{\mathit{T2}}\\ {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}-{Y}_{\mathit{T2}},& \text{si}{Y}_{\mathit{T2}}<{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}\end{array}\right.
$$

From these expressions, one may remark that $Z_{\mathrm{T2}}$ is not taken into account in the region of transition from small strains to large slips.

### 3.3 Analysis of the damage in the normal direction

The two most important mechanisms which can appear in the normal direction are the detachment between the concrete and the steel bars, and the penetration of the reinforcement into the body of the concrete. These two conditions can be interpreted respectively as an opening or a closing of a crack, and can be described by a particular constitutive law in the normal direction decoupled from the tangential behaviour.

In order to simplify the solution for the compression between surfaces, one has decided to allow a small penetration between them, which implies that ${\varepsilon}_{N}\le 0$ , and by adopting an elastic constitutive law, one will have:

$$
{\sigma}_{N}=\begin{array}{ccc}E\cdot {\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }^{-}& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}\le 0\end{array}
$$

The case of the decohesion of the interface can be described by a damageable behaviour in the normal direction, namely:

$$
{\sigma}_{N}=\begin{array}{ccc}(1-{D}_{N})\cdot E\cdot {\langle {\varepsilon}_{N}\rangle }^{+}& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}>0\end{array}
$$

with ${D}_{N}$ the scalar damage variable in the normal direction, computed with the following expression:

$$
{D}_{N}=\left\{ \begin{array}{ccc}0& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}\le {\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}\\ & & \\ \frac{1}{1+{A}_{\mathit{DN}}\cdot {\langle {Y}_{\mathit{DN}}-{Y}_{\mathit{N1}}\rangle }_{+{{B}_{\mathit{DN}}}^{}}}& \text{si}& {\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}<{\varepsilon}_{N}\end{array}\right.
$$

In this expression, two material parameters, ${A}_{\mathit{DN}}$ and ${B}_{\mathit{DN}}$ , control the decohesion by the tensile damage of the concrete. Moreover, ${Y}_{\mathit{N1}}$ is the damage threshold defined in terms of energy, equivalent to the elastic threshold in the normal direction ${Y}_{\text{elas}}{\mid}_{N}$ and which is expressed as:

$$
{Y}_{\mathit{N1}}={Y}_{\text{elas}}{\mid}_{N}=\frac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}\cdot E\cdot {\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}
$$

${\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}$ being the limit strain of perfect bond, which corresponds to the limit tensile strain of the concrete before the initiation of the damage. It must be mentioned that when the detachment – or the crack opening – reaches the maximum value of the tensile strength, no shear force should be transmitted between the two materials: this is the only condition in which the scalar damage variable in the tangential direction becomes 1 because of the damage in the normal direction

### 3.4 Analysis of the contribution of crack friction by slip

As regards the “slip” part of the formulation, one assumes that it has a pseudo-plastic behaviour, with non-linear kinematic hardening. Initially introduced by *Armstrong & Frederick* , 1966, cf. , the non-linear kinematic hardening allows the formulation to overcome the principal disadvantage of the kinematic-hardening law of *Prager* , namely the linearity of the state law which links the forces associated with the kinematic hardening. Here, the non-linear terms are added in the dissipation potential. The slip criterion takes the classical form of the threshold function of *Drucker-Prager*, which takes into account the effect of the radial confinement on the slip:

$$
{\varphi}_{f}=\mid {\sigma}_{T}^{f}-X\mid +c\cdot {I}_{1}\le 0
$$

Here $X$ is the back stress, $c$ is a parameter linked to the material, expressing the influence of the confinement, whereas ${I}_{1}$ corresponds to the first invariant of the stress tensor, which for our case is expressed as:

$$
{I}_{1}=\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\left[\sigma \right]=\frac{1}{3}{\sigma}_{N}
$$

Moreover, the initial threshold for the slip is 0. Furthermore, by considering the principle of maximum plastic dissipation, the evolution laws can be derived from the expression of the plastic potential, which is:

$$
{\varphi}_{f}^{p}=\mid {\sigma}_{T}^{f}-\mathrm{\chi }\mid +c\cdot {I}_{1}+\frac{3}{4}\cdot a\cdot {\mathrm{\chi }}^{2}
$$

Where $a$ is a material parameter. It must be mentioned that the quadratic term in $\mathrm{\chi }$ makes it possible to introduce the non-linearity of the kinematic hardening. The evolution laws for the slip strain as well as for the kinematic hardening take the following forms:

$$
\stackrel{.}{{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f}}={\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{f}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}\text{et}\stackrel{.}{\alpha}={\stackrel{.}{\lambda}}_{f}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial X}
$$

The slip multiplier $\dot{{\lambda}_{f}}$ is computed numerically by imposition of the consistency condition.

### 3.6 Expression of the tangent matrix

In order to ensure the robustness and the efficiency of the model in the numerical implementation and for the global analysis of massive structures, one must compute the tangent matrix, which can be determined from the following expression:

$$
\stackrel{.}{{\sigma}_{T}}=H\cdot \stackrel{.}{{\varepsilon}_{T}}
$$

After a few analytical computations, one can deduce the expression of the tangent modulus by using the consistency condition and the respective evolution laws:

$$
H=\frac{G\cdot (1-(\partial g({\varepsilon}_{T})/\partial {\varepsilon}_{T})\cdot {\varepsilon}_{T}^{f})}{1+G\cdot {D}_{T}(\frac{(\partial {\varphi}_{f}/\partial {\sigma}_{T})\cdot (\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}/\partial {\sigma}_{T})}{(\partial {\varphi}_{f}/\partial \mathrm{\chi })({\partial}^{2}\rho \psi /\partial {\alpha}^{2})(\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}/\partial \mathrm{\chi })})}
$$

With

$$
\frac{\partial g({\varepsilon}_{T})}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{T}}=\frac{\partial {D}_{T}}{\partial {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}\cdot \frac{\partial {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}{\partial {\varepsilon}_{T}}=\left[\frac{f\cdot g\cdot h'-f'\cdot g\cdot h-f\cdot g'\cdot h}{{h}^{2}}\right]\cdot G\cdot {\varepsilon}_{T}
$$

Where $f,g$ and $h$ are the following functions, obtained by means of:

$$
f=\sqrt{\frac{{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}}{{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}}
$$

$$
g=\exp\left\lbrace {A}_{{1}_{\mathit{DT}}}\cdot {\left[\sqrt{\frac{2}{G}}\cdot (\sqrt{{Y}_{\mathit{DT}}}-\sqrt{{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}})\right]}^{{B}_{\mathrm{1DT}}}\right\rbrace
$$

$$
h=1+{A}_{{2}_{\mathit{DT}}}\cdot {\langle {Y}_{\mathit{DT}}-{Y}_{\mathit{T1}}\rangle }_{+{{B}_{\mathrm{2DT}}}^{}}
$$

**Note:**

*In practice in Aster, the tangent matrix has not been implemented; only the secant matrix is used, namely* $H=\begin{pmatrix}E(1-{D}_{N})& 0\\ 0& G(1-{D}_{T})\end{pmatrix}$ .

## 4 Numerical integration

The separation into two parts in the formulation: damage – slip, allows us to treat each of them separately. Thus, the integration of the damage part is carried out explicitly by the definition of the two threshold surfaces. On the other hand, the “slip” part is solved implicitly by a classical method, namely the “return-mapping” algorithm proposed by *Ortiz & Simo* , Cf , which will ensure convergence in an efficient way.

### 4.1 Computation of the « crack-friction » part with an implicit integration method

The effects on the bond associated with the phenomenon of crack friction can be computed within the framework of a pseudo-plastic behaviour with a non-linear kinematic hardening. For the implementation with the integration method proposed, we shall carry out a linearisation of the threshold function about the current values of the associated internal variables. At iteration $(i+1)$ , the threshold surface is written:

$$
{\varphi}_{f}={\varphi}_{f}^{(i)}+\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{(i)}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}:({\sigma}_{T}^{{f}^{(i+1)}}-{\sigma}_{T}^{{f}^{(i)}})+\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{(i)}}{\partial X}:({X}^{(i+1)}-{X}^{(i)})\approx 0
$$

From equations , , and , one has:

$$
\stackrel{.}{X}=\gamma \cdot \stackrel{.}{\alpha}=-\gamma \cdot \stackrel{.}{{\lambda}_{f}}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial X}
$$

$$
\stackrel{.}{{\sigma}_{T}^{f}}=-G\cdot {D}_{T}\cdot \stackrel{.}{{\varepsilon}_{T}^{f}}=-G\cdot {D}_{T}\cdot \stackrel{.}{{\lambda}_{f}}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}
$$

Which one can discretise in the following way:

$$
\Delta X={X}^{(i+1)}-{X}^{(i)}=\gamma \cdot \Delta \alpha =-\gamma \cdot \Delta {\lambda}_{f}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial X}
$$

$$
\Delta {\sigma}_{T}^{f}={\sigma}_{T}^{{f}^{(i+1)}}-{\sigma}_{T}^{{f}^{(i)}}=-G\cdot {D}_{T}\cdot \Delta {\varepsilon}_{T}^{f}=-G\cdot {D}_{T}\cdot \Delta {\lambda}_{f}\cdot \frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}
$$

By combining these expressions with the expression of the threshold surface and by writing that ${\varphi}_{f}$ is equal to zero, one can deduce the increment of the multiplier $\Delta {\lambda}_{f}$ at each iteration $i$ :

$$
\Delta {\lambda}_{f}=\frac{{\varphi}_{{f}^{(i)}}}{{\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}}^{(i)}\cdot G\cdot {D}_{T}\cdot {\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial {\sigma}_{T}^{f}}}^{(i)}+{\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}}{\partial X}}^{(i)}\cdot \gamma \cdot {\frac{\partial {\varphi}_{f}^{p}}{\partial X}}^{(i)}}
$$

After obtaining the value of ${\mathrm{\Delta \lambda }}_{f}$ , one can substitute it into equations  and  in order to update the thermodynamic forces ${\sigma}_{T}^{f}$ and $\mathrm{\chi }$ . The iterations must continue until the moment when the consistency condition is verified.

## 5 Parameters of the law

The constitutive law presented here is controlled by 14 parameters, of which 3 govern the response in the normal direction and the others affect the response in the tangential direction. Moreover, Young's modulus is recovered from the elastic data supplied by the operator ELAS, which must always appear in the command file.

These parameters, or the analytical expressions which make it possible to obtain them, have been obtained or determined from the numerical simulation of the experimental tests carried out by *Eligehausen et al.* , 1983, Cf . The carrying out of multiple simulations has made it possible to determine a relation between the geometric and material characteristics of the materials in question (steel and concrete) and the parameters which govern the interface model.

### 5.1 The initial parameters

#### 5.1.1 The parameter « hpen »

The joint element operating on the notion of a displacement jump, it is necessary to introduce a characteristic dimension of the degraded interface zone making it possible to define the notion of strain in the interface. To this end the principle of penetration between surfaces has been introduced: the parameter « $\mathit{hpen}$ » makes it possible to define this zone surrounding the steel bar. This parameter corresponds to the maximum possible penetration, which depends on the thickness of the compressed – crushed concrete. At the same time, « $\mathit{hpen}$ » governs the energy dissipation in the element as well as the kinematics of the slip.

In order to give the user a reference for the choice of this parameter, one proposes to compute it from the diameter of the bar ${d}_{b}$ and the relative area of the ribs ${\alpha}_{\text{sR}}$ defined by:

$$
{\alpha}_{\text{sR}}=\frac{k\cdot {F}_{R}\cdot \sin\mathrm{\beta  }}{\pi \cdot {d}_{b}\cdot c}
$$

where $k$  is the number of ribs on the perimeter; ${F}_{R}$ the transverse area of a rib; $\mathrm{\beta  }$  is the angle between the rib and the longitudinal axis of the steel bar; and $c$ is the distance between ribs measured centre to centre. Finally, « $\mathit{hpen}$ » will be computed with the expression:

$$
{h}_{\text{pen}}={d}_{b}\cdot {\alpha}_{\text{sR}}
$$

According to *Eligehausen et al.* , the reinforcement commonly used in the United States has values of ${\alpha}_{\text{sR}}$ between $0.05$ and $0.08$ . For the smooth bars, given that one needs a small value of « *hpen* », one proposes values of ${\alpha}_{\text{sR}}$ between $0.005$ and $0.02$ .

The following table gives the values of « $\mathit{hpen}$ » as a function of the diameter of the bar:

| **Diameter (** $\mathit{mm}$ **)** | **Relative area** | $\mathit{Hpen}$ **(** $\mathit{mm}$ **)** | **Description** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | 0.01 | (0.08)  0.1 | Smooth commercial bar |
| 8 | 0.08 | 0.64 | Ribbed commercial bar |
| 20 | 0.08 | 1.50 | Ribbed commercial bar |
| 25 | 0.08 | 2.00 | Ribbed commercial bar |
| 32 | 0.08 | 2.54 | Ribbed commercial bar |

The unit of «hpen» must of course correspond to the unit used for the mesh.

#### 5.1.2 The parameter $G$ or stiffness modulus of the bond

Generally, because of the difficulty of measuring the shear strains, the stiffness modulus of a material is computed from Young's modulus and Poisson's ratio, common parameters obtained experimentally. However, for our case, the interface is a pseudo-material whose characteristics must depend on the properties corresponding to the materials in contact, steel and concrete. Given that the material which one expects to damage is the concrete, one proposes to use initially for the bond the same value of $G$ as for the concrete under study, but it may be higher, up to a value similar to the value of Young's modulus $E$ , when one increases the value of «hpen». In the case of reinforcement with stiffnesses higher than those of the usual commercial bars (because of a special layout or geometry of the ribs), one can make a correction of the chosen value, by multiplying the stiffness modulus by a correction coefficient computed from the relative areas of the commercial bars, with the expression:

$$
{C}_{\text{arm}}=\frac{({\alpha}_{\text{SR}})\text{barre}}{({\alpha}_{\text{SR}}){\text{barre}}_{\text{comm}}}
$$

Then, the stiffness modulus of the bond $G$ will be:

$$
{G}_{\text{liai}}={C}_{\text{arm}}\cdot {G}_{\text{beton}}
$$

In the last expressions, ${G}_{\text{lia}}$ is the stiffness modulus of the bond; ${G}_{\text{beton}}$ is the stiffness modulus of the concrete; ${C}_{\text{arm}}$ is the correction coefficient by reinforcement; $({\alpha}_{\text{SR}})\text{barre}$ *,* relative area of the ribs of the bar concerned; and $({\alpha}_{\text{SR}}){\text{barre}}_{\text{com}}$ *,*  relative area of the ribs of the commercial bar of the same diameter (preferably, $0.08$ ).

### 5.2 The damage parameters

#### 5.2.1 The elastic strain limit ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{1}$ or perfect-bond threshold

In order to define the perfect-bond threshold, one considers that the shear damage must be initiated when a certain strain threshold is exceeded. Consequently, one proposes to adopt the limit tensile strains of the concrete, that is, between $1\times {10}^{-4}$ and $0.5\times {10}^{-3}$ , which correspond to the shear stresses between $0.5$ and $4\mathit{MPa}$ in perfect bond.

#### 5.2.2 The damage parameter ${A}_{\mathrm{1DT}}$ for the passage from small strains to large slips

In this region, the evolution law of the damage is expressed in terms of strains and its construction depends on the elastic slope defined for the linear behaviour (shear stress versus strain) in the region of perfect bond: this parameter controls the value of the stress with respect to the slip in the passage from small strains to large slips.

The determination of the value of this parameter is a key and delicate point of the model, since the evolution of the damage must take place with certain conditions noted by several researchers; for example:

- the strength of the bond is directly proportional to the compressive strength of the concrete. However, as one increases the strength of the concrete, the behaviour becomes stiffer, leading to the brittle failure of the bond,
- the particular stiffness of the reinforcement, which is linked to the diameter and to the quantity of ribs on the surface, must increase the strength of the bond,
- the relation between the elasticity moduli of the two materials concerned must directly govern the kinematics of the bond.

From the numerical simulations which one has carried out, one has observed that this value lies between a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 5, and that it will have to be adjusted as a function of the reference test chosen. Optionally, one proposes an expression which makes it possible to adopt an initial value and which depends on the characteristics specific to the materials:

$$
{A}_{{1}_{\mathit{DT}}}=\frac{1}{(1+{\alpha}_{\text{SR}})}\cdot \sqrt{\frac{f'c}{30}}\cdot \sqrt{\frac{{E}_{a}}{{E}_{b}}}
$$

In the last expression, ${E}_{b}$ will be computed with the expression supplied in section A.2.1,2 of BAEL'91:

$$
{E}_{b}=11000\times {(f'c)}^{1/3}
$$

In the last two expressions, one has:

1. $f'c$ , compressive strength of the concrete in $\mathit{MPa}$ ;
2. ${E}_{a}$ , elasticity modulus of the steel, in $\mathit{MPa}$ ;
3. ${E}_{b}$ , elasticity modulus of the concrete, in $\mathit{MPa}$ ;
4. ${\alpha}_{\text{sR}}$ , relative area of the ribs of the bar concerned.

The r7.01.21-fig-croissance-resistance-liaison gives a graphical comparison of it.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.21/croissance_resistance_liaison.png)

*Comparison of ${A}_{\text{1DT}}$ : growth of the strength of the bond*

#### 5.2.3 The damage parameter ${B}_{\mathrm{1DT}}$

This parameter has as its aim to soften the shape of the behaviour curve, as well as to facilitate the transition from the elastic slope towards the non-linear region. It may have a value lying between $0.1$ and $0.5$ (never greater than $0.5$ since that is the equivalent of the square root of the formula). One may advise adopting the value $0.3$ for ordinary computations. (See r7.01.21-fig-modification-courbure).

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.21/modification_courbure.png)

*Comparison of ${B}_{\text{1DT}}$ : modification of the curvature*

#### 5.2.4 The strain limit ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}$ or threshold of the large slips

According to several authors, the large slips are overall greater than $1\mathit{mm}$ of displacement, but this is an indicator which depends on the shape and the dimensions of the specimens tested; therefore, one proposes that this strain never exceed $1.00$ (dimensionless value). In a more precise way, one proposes to apply the following expression:

$$
{\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}=\frac{1}{{({h}_{\mathrm{pen}})}^{2}}\cdot (1-\frac{{({A}_{\mathrm{1DT}})}^{n}}{C+{({A}_{\mathrm{1DT}})}^{n}})=\frac{1}{{({h}_{\mathrm{pen}})}^{2}}\cdot (1-\frac{{({A}_{\mathrm{1DT}})}^{4}}{9+{({A}_{\mathrm{1DT}})}^{4}})\le 1.0
$$

In this expression, one has applied a sigmoid function whose coefficients $C$ and $n$ make it possible to adjust the kinematic effect of ${A}_{\mathrm{1DT}}$ on the slip, that is, when the bond becomes more resistant because of an increase in stiffness, the slip is reduced gradually. One has adopted the values $9.0$ and $4.0$ respectively, but they are always optional.

The choice of the value of the strain limit ${\varepsilon}_{T}^{2}$ is very important because it introduces a greater or lesser brittleness of the response by translation of the threshold of passage from small strains to large slips. This brittleness is linked to the stiffness of the concrete by way of the parameter ${A}_{\mathrm{1DT}}$ . It must be noted that the following parameters which govern the damage must also be adjusted at the local level in order to ensure the correct continuity of the shear behaviour of the bond and thus to be able to obtain the desired or expected response of a real steel – bond – concrete system.

#### 5.2.5 The damage parameter ${A}_{\mathrm{2DT}}$

The damage, such as it has been conceived in the model, obeys two evolution laws which are expressed with the aid of a single classical scalar variable which will ensure the consistency of the damage. The parameters of each of the 2 laws are independent and numerically stable, but they are liable to generate serious errors in the continuity of the behaviour if one does not pay attention to the shape of the local stress–strain curve: see the case of the curve shown in the graph of r7.01.21-fig-a-endommagement-rupture-liaison, with a value ${A}_{\text{2DT}}=1\times {10}^{-3}{\mathit{MPa}}^{-1}$ . We are not able to propose an analytical relation for the choice of this parameter, but the experience acquired allows us to state that the value of this parameter must lie between $1\times {10}^{-3}$ and $9\times {10}^{-2}{\mathit{MPa}}^{-1}$ approximately.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.21/10021DC6000069D500006E47B1F624AB919C1A1A.svg)

*Comparison of ${A}_{\text{2DT}}$ : damage and failure of the bond*

#### 5.2.6 The damage parameter ${B}_{\mathrm{2DT}}$

This parameter, which completes the damage-evolution law in large slips, controls not only the growth of the strength of the bond or the shape of the behaviour curve at the peak and in the post-peak region, but also the kinematics of the response, which implies the determination of the slip for the maximum shear stress as well as the amplitude of the curve at the peak of the behaviour. Thus, although the values of the damage parameters ${A}_{\text{2DT}}$ and ${B}_{\text{2DT}}$ will have to be adjusted at the same time when one constructs the behaviour curve of the bond in order to respect the continuity of the shape, one can say that the value of ${B}_{\text{2DT}}$ is inversely proportional to the slip amplitude at the peak, that is, a value of $0.8$ allows wider large slips at the peak than a value of $1.2$ , for example.

For practical cases, one recommends using a value lying between $0.8$ and $1.1$ in order to reproduce a consistent behaviour curve (See r7.01.21-fig-b-endommagement-rupture-liaison).

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.21/endo_rupt_liaison.png)

### 5.3 The damage parameters in the normal direction

#### 5.3.1 The strain limit ${\varepsilon}_{N}^{1}$ or threshold of the large displacements

In a way similar to the elastic behaviour in the tangential direction, one considers that the decohesion must be initiated when a certain strain threshold is exceeded. We propose to adopt a value between ${10}^{-4}$ and ${10}^{-3}$ .

#### 5.3.2 The damage parameter ${A}_{\mathit{DN}}$

This parameter essentially controls the slope of degradation of the normal stress with respect to the strain due to the opening of the interface. We propose to use a minimum value of $1\times {10}^{-1}{\mathit{MPa}}^{-1}$ , which corresponds to a degradation similar to that of the concrete. Nevertheless, if one wishes to have an even more brittle behaviour of the bond, it suffices to increase this value.

#### 5.3.3 The damage parameter ${B}_{\mathit{DN}}$

In combination with the previous parameter, this parameter controls the damage of the bond, in particular the shape of the behaviour curve in the post-peak phase.

We propose to use a value equal to $1,$ or $1,2$ for more pronounced curves.

### 5.4 The friction parameters

#### 5.4.1 The material parameter $\gamma$ of crack friction

One of the assets of the model proposed here is that it is capable of taking into account the effects of the friction of the cracks, which, in the case of monotonic loading, is manifested by a positive contribution to the shear strength of the bond; moreover, in the cases of cyclic loadings, it is obvious that the shape of the hysteresis loops depends directly on the choice of the value of this material parameter. However, the corresponding values have not been calibrated, since we have not yet simulated tests with cyclic loadings in order to validate them. Temporarily, one proposes to use values lower than $10\mathit{MPa}$ , with a maximum value of $\alpha$ equal to $1.0{\mathit{MPa}}^{-1}$ .

#### 5.4.2 The material parameter $\alpha$ of kinematic hardening

On [r7.01.21-fig-effets-boucles-hysteresis-cyclique], one can see that the decrease in the value of $\alpha$ increases the hysteretic dissipation, but also the shear strength and the residual pseudo-plastic strain. This is very important for the cyclic modelling of the bond since, in reality, when one exceeds the peak of maximum strength, one remarks that at the moment of unloading there is no longer any elastic contribution of the slip, that is to say that the residual pseudo-plastic strain corresponds exactly to the total slip reached. In other words, once all the cracks in the potential failure layer, longitudinal and tangential to the steel bar, have been connected, the only strength which will prevent the displacement of the reinforcement is the friction strength of the bond, produced by the contact and the interlocking of the asperities between the concrete–concrete surfaces.

As previously, our experience is limited: one proposes to use a maximum value of $0.1{\mathit{MPa}}^{-1}$ which gives correct results for applications under monotonic loading, and which seems suitable for cyclic loadings.

![Figure](/figures/r7.01.21/1003DCD8000069D5000066D6A84EA521F8FC5C52.svg)

*Comparison of $\alpha$ : effects on the hysteresis loops under cyclic loading*

#### 5.4.3 The confinement-influence parameter $c$

In our model, the influence of the confinement has been taken into account thanks to the application of this parameter, which controls these effects on the bond, and which is manifested by an increase in the maximum shear stress as well as by the increase in the maximum displacement at the peak when the confinement increases.

For the calibration, we have carried out simulations with confinements of $0$ , $5$ , $10$ and $15\mathit{MPa}$ , always using a value of $1.0$ for this parameter. One has remarked that if one wishes to produce a kinematic translation of the slip caused by the confinement, it suffices to adopt a value of $1.2$ or $1.5$ (dimensionless). Optionally, it is advised to keep the value of $1.0$ for ordinary computations.
