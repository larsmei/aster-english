# v6.04.402 SSNV402 – constitutive law BETON_RAG verification: phenomena coupling

**Summary:**

These tests check the constitutive law BETON_RAG on a cube that is required by a temperature and drying load. The model's response is compared to solutions obtained by analytically resolving the equations that govern the [R7.01.26](doc:r7.01.26) behavior.

Several modellings are proposed:

- Modelization a: the cube is free of deformation.
- Modelization b: the cube is blocked on movement.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Geometry is a cube of $1m$ on the side.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.402/10000000000002A9000001EAF7840BF799183D95.png)

*Geometry adopted*

### 1.2 Mesh

The mesh is made with 1 HEXA8.

### 1.3 Materials

The material is of BETON_RAG type. The necessary characteristics are those related to chemical advancement phenomena as well as those related to RAG.

**You must not use the material characteristics of** "BETON_RAG" **of this test case to study. They are not physical, they are used for mathematical verification.**

The material parameters used in this test case are given in v6.04.402-paramsBRAG.

### 1.4 boundary conditions

#### 1.4.1 For the free side deformation cube

These boundary conditions are for Modelling a.

The following movements are imposed on the faces:

- BAS    : DZ = 0
- GAUCHE    : DY = 0
- ARRIERE    : DX = 0

#### 1.4.2 For the cube blocked in lateral deformations

These boundary conditions are for Modelling a.

The following movements are imposed on the faces:

- BAS, HAUT    : DZ = 0
- GAUCHE, DROITE    : DY = 0
- ARRIERE, AVANT    : DX = 0

### 1.5 Modelling

All modellings are 3D.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

This Modelling is done on a cube free of deformation. Loading is a temperature and drying that varies over time.

The figure shows the evolution of the load over time.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.402/1000020100000849000005A96D8C02F88684F931.png)

* Evolution of temperature loading and drying.*

### 2.2 reference solution

The equations are from [R7.01.26](doc:r7.01.26).

#### 2.2.1 Chemical advance

Chemical advancement follows the following law:

$$
\frac{\partial A}{\partial t}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}=\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{a}_{0}.\exp\left[\frac{{E}_{a}}{R}\left(\frac{1}{{T}_{\mathit{ref}}}-\frac{1}{T}\right)\right]\frac{{⟨{S}_{r}-{S}_{r}^{0}⟩}_{+}}{\left(1-{S}_{r}^{0}\right)}{⟨{S}_{r}-A⟩}_{+}
$$

This equation depends on temperature and drying, so it can be checked without knowing the strain and strain state of the sample.

#### 2.2.2 Water pressure

Capillary pressure is related to saturation, by the relationship:

$$
P_c=-a{S}_{r}{({S_{r}}^{-b}-1)}^{1-\frac{1}{b}}
$$

This equation depends only on drying and can therefore be verified without knowing the deformation and strain state of the sample.

#### 2.2.3 Freeze pressure

The effective freezing pressure follows the following law

$$
{P}_{{gel}}={b}_{g}{M}_{g}{⟨A.{V}_{g}-{⟨{A}_{0}.{V}_{g}+{b}_{g}{Tr}({\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{total})⟩}_{+}⟩}_{+}
$$

This equation depends on chemical progress and the trace of deformation, so it can only be verified by knowing the deformation state of the sample.

#### 2.2.4 Mechanical condition of the sample

To solve the previous equations, we must determine the deformations. The following equations govern the problem:

$$
{\varepsilon}^{\text{e}}=\frac{1+{\nu}^{0}}{{E}^{0}}.{\sigma}^{\text{e}}-\frac{{\nu}^{0}}{{E}^{0}}\text{tr}({\sigma}^{\text{e}}){Id}
$$

with:

$$
{\varepsilon}^{\text{total}}={\varepsilon}^{\text{e}}+{\varepsilon}^{\text{fluage}}+{\varepsilon}^{\text{vdt}}+{\varepsilon}^{\text{ther}}
$$

$$
{\sigma}^{total}={\sigma}^{\text{e}}-{P}_{gel}{Id}-{P}_{c}{Id}
$$

We are in case ${\sigma}^{total}=0$ due to boundary conditions. Materials parameters are chosen so that ${\varepsilon}^{\text{fluage}}=0$, ${\varepsilon}^{\text{vdt}}=0$, ${\varepsilon}^{\text{ther}}=0$.

So we have

$$
Tr({\boldsymbol{\varepsilon}}^{\text{total}})=\frac{3.(1-2.{\nu}^{0})}{{E}^{0}}({P}_{\mathit{gel}}+{P}_{c})
$$

The test cases test the correct integration of chemical advance, gel pressure and capillary pressure. The numerical resolution of the differential equation system shows a very good coincidence with the results of code_aster.
