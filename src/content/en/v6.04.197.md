## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.197/10000000000005B30000047DFCD6776F6FFADCA9.jpg)

The triaxial test is performed on a single finite element isoparametric cubic $\mathrm{CUB8}$. The length of each edge is 1. The different facets of this cube are mesh cells groups named $\mathrm{HAUT}$, $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$, $\mathrm{DERRIERE}$, $\mathrm{DROIT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$. The mesh-cell group *SYM* also contains the mesh cells $\mathrm{BAS}$, $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ and $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ groups; the mesh-cell group $\mathrm{COTE}$ groups of mesh cells $\mathrm{DERRIERE}$ and $\mathrm{DROIT}$.

### 1.2 Material properties

The elastic properties are:

  - isotropic compressibility module: $K=516200\mathrm{kPa}$
  - shear module: $\mu =238200\mathrm{kPa}$

The anelastic properties (Hurch) are:

  - power of non-linear elastic law: ${n}_{e}=0.4$
  - $\beta =24$
  - $d=2.5$
  - $b=0.2$
  - friction angle: $\varphi =33°$
  - Dilatance angle: $\psi =33°$
  - critical pressure: ${P}_{\mathrm{c0}}=-1000\mathrm{kPa}$
  - Reference pressure: ${P}_{\mathrm{ref}}=-1000\mathrm{kPa}$
  - isotropic mechanism elastic radius: ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{s}=0.001$
  - elastic radius of the deviatory mechanism: ${r}_{\mathrm{éla}}^{d}=0.005$
  - ${a}_{\mathrm{mon}}=0.0001$
  - ${a}_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0.008$
  - ${c}_{\mathrm{mon}}=0.2$
  - ${c}_{\mathrm{cyc}}=0.1$
  - ${r}_{\mathrm{hys}}=0.05$
  - ${r}_{\mathrm{mob}}=0.9$
  - ${x}_{m}=1$
  - $\text{dila}=1$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

A triaxial test consists of imposing a vertical load variation on the test piece while maintaining the constant lateral pressure. It can be drained (the fluid pore pressure does not vary during the test) or undrained (the valve is closed: the fluid pore pressure evolves in the sample). Here, we are interested in the drained case, simpler, because it does not involve the influence of the fluid pore pressure and *modelisable by this pure mechanical calculation*.

In the model under consideration, the cubic element represents one eighth of the sample, so the limit conditions are as follows:

    - The symmetry conditions:

      - ${u}_{z}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{BAS}$ group
      - ${u}_{x}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{GAUCHE}$ group
      - ${u}_{y}=0$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{DEVANT}$ group
    - Side pressure conditions:

      - ${P}_{n}=1$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{COTE}$ group
    - The loading conditions:

      - ${P}_{n}=1$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{HAUT}$ Group (phase 1)
      - ${u}_{z}=-1$ on mesh cell $\mathrm{HAUT}$ Group (Phase 2)

The load is carried out in two phases:

  - isotropic loading between $t=-2$ and $t=0$ where the pressure on the mesh cells $\mathrm{COTE}$ and HAUT groups varies between $p=0$ and $p={p}_{c}$ (isotropic preconsolidation pressure). In modellings A, B and C, the value of ${p}_{c}$ is $50,100,200\mathrm{kPa}$, respectively;
  - mesh-cell group $\mathrm{HAUT}$ displacement varying between $t=0$ and $t=10$ from ${u}_{z}=0$ to ${u}_{z}=-0.2$ (total vertical deformation of $20\text{\%}$).

### 1.4 Results

The solutions are post-treated with point $C$ , in terms of $Q$ equivalent stress , ${\varepsilon}_{v}$ total volume deformation and $({r}_{\text{ela}}^{\text{s},m}+{r}_{\text{iso}}^{m})$ isotropic and $({r}_{\text{ela}}^{d,m}+{r}_{\text{dev}}^{m})$ deviatory nutring coefficients , depending on the ${\epsilon}_{zz}$ axial deformation evolution

The validation is performed by comparison with GEFDYN solutions provided by the École Centrale Paris.

For modellings with GDEF_LOG operator, $\underline{\underline{E}}=1/2\log(I+2\underline{\underline{\Lambda}})$ logarithmic deformation is considered, with $\underline{\underline{\Lambda}}$ the Green-Lagrange tensor. The density deformation in this case is obtained directly from the deformity gradient determinant, $J=det(F)$.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

Model A is *tridimensional* and *nonlinear* static.

First, a *elastic preconsolidation* ($\mathrm{ELAS}$) of the sample up to ${p}_{c}=\mathrm{50kPa}$ (first phase of calculation) is performed. This preconsolidation takes place in $1$ time step between $t=-2$ and $t=0$.

The vertical displacement imposed on the upper facet varies between $0.$ and $-0.2$ (second phase of calculation) in $100$ time step between $t=0$ and $t=10$. In this second phase, the automatic subdivision of the time step is activated to manage non-convergence situations of local integration.

In integrating equilibrium equations, a re-upgrading of the tangent matrix is required, which is provided by the Hujeux Law routines and significantly accelerates convergence. Also, the time step (DEFI_LIST_INST command) subdivision is required to treat local integration failure situations due to too large load increments. **This feature is highly recommended.**

## 8 Modelling G

### 8.1 Modelling characteristics

$C$ Modelling is *tridimensional* and *nonlinear* static.

First, a *elastic preconsolidation* ($\mathrm{ELAS}$) of the sample up to ${p}_{c}=200\mathrm{kPa}$ (first phase of calculation) is performed. This preconsolidation takes place in $1$ time step between $t=-2$ and $t=0$.

The vertical displacement imposed on the upper facet varies between $0$ and $-0.2$ (second phase of calculation) in $100$ time step between $t=0$ and $t=10$. The Modelling considers a large kinematic deformation with the operator GDEF_LOG (DEORMATION='GDEF_LOG').

In integrating equilibrium equations, a re-upgrading of the tangent matrix is required, which is provided by the Hujeux Law routines and significantly accelerates convergence. Also, the time step (DEFI_LIST_INST command) subdivision is required to treat local integration failure situations due to too large load increments. **This feature is highly recommended.**
