# v6.03.136 SSNP136 – Threading foundation test with Cam-Clay-type elastoplastic law

**Summary**

A spinning foundation test is performed using an elastoplastic Cam-Clay-type law. The solutions calculated in pure mechanical hypothesis, without hydromechanical coupling, under monotonous loading, are compared with results from elements finite FLAC code. In Modelling A, the soil behavior is modelled by the law of Cam-Clay [R7.01.14](doc:r7.01.14);

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Description of the model

The filant foundation model consists of a rigid foundation of $B$ width equal to $2m$ laid on a half-plane representing porous elastoplastic soil. The foundation is considered to be infinitely long, so that the problem can be reduced to a vertical plane ($\mathrm{2D}$ ) containing a section of the foundation (Figure 1). A vertical monotonous downward shift is imposed on the foundation, and the evolution of the soil behavior below it is observed. The problem also presents symmetry with respect to the vertical plane sharing the foundation length in two equal parts, only half of the problem is represented. Thus, the ground is represented by a square of $10m$ side, large enough to not disrupt the evolution of the soil behavior around the foundation (the hypothesis of the infinite half-plane for the soil).

The behaviour of the soil is modelled by an elastoplastic law of the Cam-Clay type. It is also assumed that a fluid (water) is present in saturated condition* (hydromechanical coupling).

### 1.2 Boundary conditions and initial conditions

The mechanical boundary conditions are:

- $\mathit{Ux}=0$ on the side edges (horizontal symmetry);
- $\mathit{Uy}=0$ on the lower edge (vertical symmetry sufficiently far from foundation);
- an imposed pressure ${P}_{A}={1.E}^{+5}\mathit{Pa}$ (air pressure) on the upper edge, assumed to be free air;

The hydraulic conditions are:

- Interstitial pressure $\mathit{PRE1}=98100\mathit{Pa}$ on the lower edge (down drain).

It is recalled that the absence of explicit boundary conditions is tantamount to imposing a zero flow on the edges (non-draining condition).

The initial conditions in the soil are:

- *in the case without water* *(no hydromechanical coupling):* ${\sigma}_{y}={\rho}_{S}.g.(h-y)+{P}_{A}$ ${\sigma}_{x}={K}_{0}.({\sigma}_{y}-{P}_{A})+{P}_{A}$
- *in the case with water* *(with hydromechanical coupling):* ${\sigma}_{y}={\rho}_{h}.g.(h-y)+{P}_{A}$ ${\sigma}_{x}={K}_{0}.({\sigma}_{y}-{P}_{A})+{P}_{A}$ $\mathrm{PRE1}={\rho}_{e}.g.(h-y)$

${\rho}_{S}$ , ${\rho}_{e}$ and ${\rho}_{h}={\rho}_{S}+\mathrm{n.}{\rho}_{e}$ represent the density of soil, fluid and homogenized, respectively; $n$ represents the porosity of the soil; $h$ represents the thickness of the soil domain (or the free surface side), with $h=10m$; ${P}_{A}$ represents the fixed part of the initial stress in the soil, equal to atmospheric pressure ${1.E}^{+5}\mathit{Pa}$; ${K}_{0}$ represents the earth thrust coefficient, here equal to 1.

### 1.3 Material properties

The anelastic parameters of the elastoplastic law of Cam-Clay, cf.[R7.01.14], are:

- porosity: $n=0.5$ (corresponding to an index of the initial ${e}_{0}=\frac{n}{1-n}=1$ voids);
- elastic compressibility coefficient: $\kappa =0.05$ (elastic slope in $[e,\ln(P)]$ plane);
- plastic compressibility coefficient: $\lambda =0.2$ (plastic slope in $[e,\ln(P)]$ plane);
- the slope of the critical state right: $M=1.02$ (corresponding to a friction angle of 25.85°);
- critical pressure: ${P}_{\mathit{CR}}={P}_{\mathit{CO}}/2=\mathit{trace}(\sigma )/6={\sigma}_{xx}/3+{\sigma}_{yy}/6$;

The elastic parameters of the soil are:

- grain density: ${\rho}_{S}=1900\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$;
- le Poisson's ratio: $\nu =0.3$ ;
- le Young's modulus 1 : $E=10\mathit{MPa}$ ;
- the parameters for controlling traction: ${K}^{\mathit{cam}}=\mathit{Ptrac}=0$ .

Finally, the hydraulic parameters are:

- the density of water: ${\rho}_{e}=1000\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$;
- viscosity: $\nu =0.001$;
- Intrinsic permeability 2: ${K}^{int}=1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{-12}{m}^{3}/\mathit{kg}/s$;
- water compressibility coefficient: ${K}_{e}={1.E}^{+10}$;

### 1.4 Loading

The loads are as follows:

- gravity $g=9.81m/{s}^{2}$ , downwards;
- a downward vertical displacement imposed on the foundation, varying linearly from $0$ to ${D}_{Y}=-0.05m$ between ${t}_{0}=0s$ and ${t}_{1}=1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{+7}s$.

If one considers a time step between $1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{+5}s$ and $1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{+6}s$ , one obtains a characteristic distance of fluid diffusion between each time step between $1m$ and $10m$ . Compared to the size characteristic of the foundation ($2m$ ), one can consider that the *permanent regime is reached between each time step* .

### 1.5 Results

The solutions are post-processed at $O$ , $G$ points directly below the foundation and $F$ , in terms of loading paths in the $(P’,Q)$ stress plane . We are also interested in the evolution of the resultant vertical force on the $B$ width of the foundation, depending on its sinking. The solutions obtained by *Code_Aster* are compared to those calculated by *FLAC* .

On doit choisir un jeu de données $(E,\nu )$ satisfaisant la relation : $E<\frac{3(1+\nu ){P}_{0}(1+{e}_{0})}{\kappa}$ à l’état initial. Dans notre cas, avec $E=10\mathit{MPa}`et :math:$nu =0.3`, on a :$E<\frac{3(1+\nu ){P}_{0}(1+{e}_{0})}{\kappa}={\mathrm{156.P}}_{\mathit{co}}$, où ${P}_{\mathit{co}}$ représente la pression de consolidation initiale du sol.Or ${P}_{\mathit{co}}=\mathit{trace}({\sigma}_{0})/3$ est strictement croissante en fonction de la profondeur. La valeur minimale de ${P}_{\mathit{co}}$ est donc atteinte à la surface libre, et vaut ${P}_{\mathit{co},min}={P}_{A}={10}^{+5}\mathit{Pa}$. Donc $E=10\mathit{MPa}<15\mathit{MPa}$.

Hydraulic conductivity is written: $\lambda =\frac{{\rho}_{e}g{K}^{int}}{\nu}\approx {1.E}^{-5}m/s$.

## 2 Modelling A

### 2.1 Modelling characteristics

Model A is *bidimensional* and *static nonlinear* . The calculation is made in pure mechanical *, without hydromechanical coupling (equivalent to a perfectly drained soil).

The initial state consistency (including boundary conditions with the soil pre-consolidation state) can be verified at first: mechanical equilibrium must be established when only gravity acts, so the system condition must not evolve.

The elastoplastic constitutive law used is Cam-Clay, cf.[R7.01.14].

Vertical displacement is imposed on the GROUP_MA = ‘APPUI' representing the foundation-ground interface, and varies between $0.$ and $–0.05m$ in 20 time step between $t=0.s$ and $t=1.0\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{10}^{+7}s$

## 3 Executive summary

### 3.1 Comments: Cam-Clay model comparison

Comparison of the solutions given by the two codes in the previous tables shows a relatively satisfactory convergence. Only the difference to point $F$ for $P’=\mathrm{101900 }\mathit{Pa}$ appears high relative ($\text{+199\%}$ ), but is actually an absolute order of quantity acceptable compared to the other points.

To better understand the comparison between the different solutions obtained by *Code_Aster* and *FLAC* , the comparison of the loading routes at $O$ , $G$ and $F$ in the $(P’,Q)$ plane is presented in Figure **3** and the comparison of the results of the forces on the foundation is shown in Figure **4**.

In terms of loading path in the $(P’,Q)$ plane (FIG. **3**), if the solutions coincide fairly well with the point $O$ , they have differences relative to the point $F$ , but more significant to the point $G$ . These differences can be explained by the combination of two factors:

- On the one hand, the two codes do not post-process the solutions in the same way: *Code_Aster* at nodes and *FLAC* at Gauss points;
- The $G$ and $F$ points are located around the end of the foundation, which is a rather critical place since it is the boundary between the compression (under the foundation) and the expansion of the soil (outside the foundation). The stress gradients are elevated from Gauss to Gauss point, and it can thus be understood that extrapolation to the nodes from the nearest Gauss points is only imperfectly representative of the actual values at these Gauss points.

In terms of force result (Figure **4**), the solutions given by *Code_Aster* and *FLAC* coincide relatively well, but tend to sever as the foundation sink increases.
