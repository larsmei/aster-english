# v3.04.163 RCCM15 - POST_RCCM B3600 validation

**Summary:**

This test validates the fatigue calculation option according to the RCC-M (B3600) code. The stresses are not calculated but extracted from tables.

This test consists of two modellings, which differ by taking into account thermal transients:

- the first one calculates the two thermal transients on two axisymmetric sections of the same diameter and thickness as the straight pipes and elbow,
- the second reads directly the values from a temperature reading on the thermal calculation of the elbow, previously modelled in $\mathrm{3D}$ by the MMC department.

The reference solution is digital (code SYSPIPE and note EDF/MMC).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

This test is extracted from a study by the MMC Department of industrial piping modelling, which resulted in a fatigue calculation following the RCC-M B3600.

The line has 10 elbows. It is oriented from the node N1 to the node N2.

**Characteristics of the sections:**

- Right parties:

  - $R=406.4\mathit{mm}$ , $\mathit{EP}=32.\mathit{mm}$
  - Tube 1, $R=410.\mathit{mm}$ , $\mathit{EP}=38.\mathit{mm}$ ;
  - Tube2 , $R=444.4\mathit{mm}$ , $\mathit{EP}=70.\mathit{mm}$ ;
- Shoulders:

  - mesh-cell group $\mathit{POUCT}$ : $R=406.4\mathit{mm}$ ; $\mathit{EP}=34.\mathit{mm}$ ;
  - Flexibility coefficient for all elbows, $\mathit{cflex}=6.032$;
  - Earring radius: $1220\mathit{mm}$

In addition, 6 discreet elements (DIS_T) are added in 3 points of the line (a vertical and a horizontal by point anchor).

For the transient thermal analysis calculation (which allows for the estimation of temperature gradients across the wall), a calculation is not performed on an $\mathrm{3D}$ elbow, but only on two representative sections:

- of the section of straight tubes (thickness $32\mathit{mm}$),
- of the elbow section (thickness $34\mathit{mm}$ ).

These calculations are axisymmetric. The solution is independent of the axis and the pipe portion is modelled by a rectangle, finely meshed according to the radius, with a single element in the axis.

Two transients are modelled.

Thermal calculations:

The results available in the note were performed on a 3D model of the elbow located after the component exit The results are provided for analysis segments located in the right and elbow:

$T_{\text{moy}}(i,j) =   \frac{1}{e} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} T(i,j)(y) \, dy =   \frac{1}{e} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} T(i)(y) \, dy -   \frac{1}{e} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} T(j)(y) \, dy =   T_{\text{moy}}(i) - T_{\text{moy}}(j)$: average value of $T(i)-T(j)$ on the ligament

$\Delta T_{1}(i,j) =   \frac{12}{e^2} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} y \, T(i,j)(y) \, dy =   \frac{12}{e^2} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} y \, T(i)(y) \, dy -   \frac{12}{e^2} \int_{-e/2}^{e/2} y \, T(j)(y) \, dy =   \Delta T_1(i) - \Delta T_1(j)$: variation of a linear distribution of T(i,j).

$\Delta T_{2}(i,j)=\max\!\left\lvert\begin{array}{l}\left|T_{\mathrm{max}}(i,j)-T_{\mathrm{moy}}(i,j)\right|-\left|\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Delta T_{1}(i,j)\right|\\\left|T_{\mathrm{min}}(i,j)-T_{\mathrm{moy}}(i,j)\right|-\left|\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Delta T_{1}(i,j)\right|\\0\end{array}\right.$
