# v5.01.120 SDND120 - Transitional response of an anti-seismic patch

**Summary**

A non-seismic patch has been tested on a vibrating table. This test case is designed to reproduce this test numerically. The device is modelled by two unabsorbed spring mass systems, separated by a non-linearity of the anti-seismic patch type.

The discrete element in traction-compression, the calculation of eigenmodes, static modes, and the calculation of the transient response by nonlinear modal recombination of the structure subject to an accelerogram are tested. The nonlinearity is of the ANTI_SISM type.

The reference result is a MATLAB program.

The results obtained are in very good agreement with the reference results.

The calculated results are also compared with the measured forces and displacements on an experimental device (qualitative comparison only).

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

An anti-seismic patch is placed between two jaws (rectangles cut off on the following figure) which are placed on a vibrating table subject to an acceleration imposed in the direction X. It is modelled by a non-linearity of the "anti-seismic device" type placed on both sides of a mass-spring system.

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.120/Object_1.svg)

### 1.2 Material properties

The jaws that insert the device are each modelled by a mass-spring system:

stiffness de restraint : $k={10}^{10}N/m$ ;

point mass: $m=25\mathrm{kg}$ .

The device tested is an anti-seismic patch of type JARRET. Its characteristics are as follows:

- $\mathrm{K1}=6.{10}^{6}N/m$ (RIGI_K1),
- $\mathrm{K2}=0,53{10}^{6}N/m$ (RIGI_K2),
- $\mathrm{Py}=1200$ (SEUIL_FX),
- $C=0,07{10}^{5}$ (C),
- $alpha=0,2$ (PUIS_ALPHA),
- $\mathrm{xmax}=0,03m$ (DX_MAX).

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

**boundary conditions**

The only travels allowed are $X$-axis translations. $C$ and $D$-points are clamped: $\mathrm{dx}=\mathrm{dy}=\mathrm{dz}=0$. The other points are free to translate according to $\mathrm{dx}$: $\mathrm{dy}=\mathrm{dz}=0$.

**Charge**

The point $D$ is subject to a transverse acceleration in the $x$ ${\gamma}_{1}(t)=0,66\sin(\omega t)m/{s}^{2}$ direction with $\omega =2\pi$ , the point $C$ is fixed.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

At the initial moment, the device is at rest: $t=0$ , $\mathrm{dx}(0)=0$ , $\mathrm{dx}/\mathrm{dt}(0)=0$ in all point.

Maximum and RMS values of relative and absolute $B$ displacements, and of the stress due to the anti-seismic patch.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

![Figure](/figures/v5.01.120/10001396000069D500002F2192DBE4272AE86F4A.svg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Modelling of the seismic anti-seismic device**

The jaws that insert the device are each modeled by a discrete element at 3 degrees of freedom DIS_T.

The anti-seismic patch is simulated via the ANTI_SISM key word factor of the DYNA_VIBRA operator.

The node $\mathrm{NO1}$ is subject to an accelerated ${\gamma}_{1}(t)$ , the node $\mathrm{NO11}$ to ${\gamma}_{2}(t)=0$ . The relative displacement of the node $\mathrm{NO2}$ and its absolute displacement are calculated.

The time integration is done with the algorithm d , Euler and a time step of $1,25.{10}^{-5}$ second. The calculations are archived every 80 time step.

A reduced ${\xi}_{i}$ depreciation is considered to be zero for all two modes calculated.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh consists of 4 nodes and 4 mesh cells of the DIS_T type.

## 4 Summary of the results

The results, in terms of effort and displacement, obtained with *Code_Aster* are comparable to those calculated by an MATLAB script. The differences between the calculated quantities and the experimental quantities are related to the approximation made on excitation.
