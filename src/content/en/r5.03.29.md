# r5.03.29 Plastic and viscoplastic ductile-damage constitutive laws GTN and VISC_GTN

**Summary:**

This document describes the ductile damage model of Gurson – Tvergaard – Needleman (GTN). It is available in a local version and with a gradient of internal variables (GRAD_VARI or GRAD_INCO). The modelling of large strains is accessible via the GDEF_LOG kinematics. The constitutive behaviour can be time-independent (GTN) or viscoplastic (VISC_GTN). In the latter case, plasticity evolves according to a Norton model in which the threshold function appears.

## 1 Scope of application

### 1.1 Purpose

Ductile damage of metallic materials results, broadly, from three mechanisms: the nucleation of cavities, their growth within an elastoplastic matrix and their coalescence. [Gurson, 1977] proposed a constitutive model of micromechanical inspiration able to account in a simple manner for the growth of cavities. It was completed in a phenomenological manner in [Tvergaard & Needleman, 1984] in order also to represent the stages of nucleation and coalescence: one then speaks of the GTN model.

The evolution of the porosity (volume of the cavities relative to the total volume) results in a softening of the material, that is to say a decrease in the capacity to resist stresses. This often results in a localisation of the strains and of the porosity in bands of small thickness. The strong gradients which result then require an extension of the constitutive law in order to take distant interactions into account: one speaks of non-local formulations of the constitutive behaviour. Such an extension has been proposed for the GTN model [Zhang et al., 2018], which relies on the gradient of the hardening internal variable (GRAD_VARI, GRAD_INCO) according to [Lorentz & Andrieux, 1999]. This is the formulation introduced in code_aster.

These phenomena generally take place at strain levels which require a non-linear kinematics. Here, it relies on the logarithmic strain (GDEF_LOG). Furthermore, at these strain levels, plasticity first induces a quasi-incompressible constitutive behaviour. In order to avoid the problems of numerical locking which may result, the use of a mixed formulation (GRAD_INCO) is recommended, possibly completed by a penalty term. The model is however also available in small strains (PETIT) as well as in the absence of a mixed formulation (GRAD_VARI), or even in a local formulation of the constitutive behaviour (standard finite elements).

The rate effects of the loading can be taken into account through a viscoplastic formulation based on a Norton law (VISC_GTN), or else ignored (GTN). Let us add that viscous regularisation [R5.03.34](doc:r5.03.34) is also recommended in order to stabilise the behaviour of fully broken points and therefore without resistance to stress.

Finally, let us state that the elastic part of the model is expressed explicitly, that is to say that the stress is expressed as a function of the strain and of the plastic strain in the current state. There is therefore no incremental evolution of the stress; it does not contribute to defining the mechanical state of the system, it is a consequence of it.

**Note:** for a point broken in the viscoplastic regime, the stress does not vanish instantaneously but it no longer depends on the strain, as will be seen later. In this very particular case, the evolution of the stress is purely incremental, which justifies the stress appearing (from the programming point of view) among the internal variables. On the other hand, it is not useful to supply it in the initial state given in STAT_NON_LINE/ETAT_INIT by SIGM; this value is ignored.

### 1.2 Material parameters

The modular expression of the constitutive relation makes it possible to group the various parameters of the model by categories:

- the elastic part is isotropic and depends on Young's modulus $E$ and on Poisson's ratio $\nu$ defined under the factor keyword ELAS (E, NU) of the command DEFI_MATERIAU;
- the parameters of the isotropic hardening are defined under the factor keyword ECRO_NL (R0, RH, R1, GAMMA_1, R2, GAMMA_2, RK, P0, GAMMA_K). Note that it is not possible to define a Lüders plateau, unlike the VMIS_ISOT_NL model;
- the damage parameters of the model are gathered under the factor keyword GTN

  - Cavity growth: PORO_INIT, Q1 and Q2
  - Nucleation with a Gaussian distribution: NUCL_GAUSS_PORO ${f}_{N}$ , NUCL_GAUSS_PLAS ${\kappa}_{N}$ , NUCL_GAUSS_DEV ${s}_{N}$
  - Nucleation with a uniform distribution over an interval: NUCL_CRAN_PORO ${c}_{0}$ , NUCL_CRAN_INIT ${\kappa}_{i}$ , NUCL_CRAN_FIN ${\kappa}_{f}$
  - Nucleation proportional to the cumulative plastic strain starting from a threshold: NUCL_EPSI_INIT ${E}_{c}^{p}$ , NUCL_EPSI_PENTE ${b}_{0}$
  - Coalescence: COAL_PORO ${f}_{c}$ , COAL_ACCE ${\delta}_{c}$ and porosity at failure PORO_RUPT ${f}_{F}$ . The user defines either ${\delta}_{c}$ , or ${f}_{F}$ , the two quantities being related by:

$$
{f}_{F}=\left(1-\frac{1}{\delta}\right){f}_{c}+\frac{1}{\delta}\frac{1}{{q}_{1}}
$$

  - Numerical regularisation of the model: ENDO_CRIT_VISC denotes the damage threshold ${d}_{v}$ beyond which a coupling between viscoplasticity and damage is activated; ENDO_CRIT_RUPT denotes the damage ${d}_{f}$ from which a point is considered to be broken.
- the parameters of the Norton viscosity law, when it is present, are defined under the factor keyword NORTON (coefficient K and exponent N)
- in a non-local formulation [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01), the corresponding parameters must also be defined under the factor keyword NON_LOCAL (C_GRAD_VARI, PENA_LAGR and PENA_LAGR_INCO).

The parameters may depend on the temperature (factor keywords *_FO). The constitutive law is expressed in mechanical strains, that is to say that it is compatible with the usual shrinkage terms, in particular the thermal strain.

## 2 Continuous model

### 2.1 Constitutive equations: local plastic model

We place ourselves in logarithmic large strains, denoting by $E$ the mechanical (logarithmic) strain and by $T$ the associated stress (by duality). The internal variables of the model consist of the plastic strain ${E}^{p}$ , the hardening variable $\kappa$ , the damage $d$ (which depends on the porosity $f$ ) and the cumulative plastic strain (which governs part of the nucleation porosity).

First, the stress–strain relation relies on an additive split into a plastic part and an elastic part:

$$
T=K\mathit{tr}(E-{E}^{p})\mathit{Id}+2\mu \mathit{dev}(E-{E}^{p})
\textrm{avec}
3K=\frac{E}{1-2\nu }` et :math:`2\mu =\frac{E}{1+\nu }
$$

where :math:`` and :math:`` denote respectively the trace and the deviator of a second-order tensor.

The plasticity threshold function denoted $F$ depends on the hardening level $\kappa$ through the hardening function :math:`` , on an (isotropic) measure of the intensity of the stresses denoted ${T}_{\text{*}}$ which also depends on the damage and on the strain through the Jacobian of the transformation $J=\exp(\mathrm{tr}E)$ . It is written as follows:

$$
F(T,\kappa ;d,E)=\frac{{T}_{\text{*}}(T,d)}{J(E)}-R(\kappa )
$$

The hardening function is identical to that deployed in the law VMIS_ISOT_NL [R5.03.33](doc:r5.03.33), namely:

$$
R(\kappa )={R}_{0}+{R}_{H}\kappa +{R}_{1}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{1}\kappa })+{R}_{2}(1-{e}^{-{\gamma}_{2}\kappa })+{R}_{K}{({p}_{0}+\kappa )}^{{\gamma}_{K}}
$$

It depends on at most nine parameters, some of which may remain zero if one does not wish to activate all the terms of the expression (default choice).

**Note:**  it is recalled that it is not possible to introduce a Lüders plateau, unlike the law VMIS_ISOT_NL.

The definition of the Gurson stress ${T}_{\text{*}}$ is implicit. For a given stress $T$ and damage $d$ , ${T}_{\text{*}}$ is the solution of the following equation:

$$
G(T,{T}_{\text{*}},d)=0;G(T,{T}_{\text{*}},f)\equiv {\left(\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}^{2}+2d\mathrm{chm}\left(\frac{3}{2}{q}_{2}\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)-{(1-d)}^{2};\mathrm{chm}(x)\equiv \mathrm{ch}(x)-1
$$

where ${q}_{1}$ and ${q}_{2}$ are two material parameters and where the stress invariants have been introduced:

$$
{T}_{H}=\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{tr}(T)
\textrm{et}
{T}_{\mathit{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{dev}(T):\mathrm{dev}(T)}
$$

The evolution of the plastic strain is governed by the flow equation. When the threshold function is differentiable with respect to $T$ , that is to say when $T\ne 0$ , the flow direction is normal to the threshold surface:

$$
\text{si}T\ne 0\text{}{\dot{E}}^{p}=\dot{\kappa}N\text{};\text{}N=\frac{\partial F}{\partial T}=-\frac{1}{J}{\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial {T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}^{-1}\left(\frac{\partial G}{\partial T}\right)
$$

In the singular case where $T=0$ , the derivative is generalised via notions of convex analysis (subgradient). The flow direction is then subject only to the following condition:

$$
\text{si}T=0\text{}{\Pi}_{N}(\dot{{E}^{p}},d)\le \frac{\dot{\kappa}}{J}\text{}
$$

The support function ${\Pi}_{N}$ has the following expression, with notation similar to () :

$$
{\Pi}_{N}(\dot{{E}^{p}},d)=\frac{2}{{q}_{2}}\mathrm{arcosh}(1+{C}^{\text{*}})\left|\dot{{E}_{H}^{p}}\right|+\frac{2}{3}\left(1-d\right)\sqrt{1-\gamma {C}^{\text{*}}}\dot{{E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}}
$$

$$
{C}^{\text{*}}=-\mathrm{\chi }+\sqrt{{\mathrm{\chi }}^{2}+\frac{2}{\gamma}(\mathrm{\chi }-1)};\mathrm{\chi }=\frac{{({q}_{2}\dot{{E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}})}^{2}+\frac{{(3\dot{{E}_{H}^{p}})}^{2}}{d}}{{({q}_{2}\dot{{E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}})}^{2}};\gamma =\frac{2d}{{\left(1-d\right)}^{2}}
$$

The evolution of the hardening variable is fixed by the consistency condition:

$$
\dot{\kappa}\ge 0;F(T,\kappa )\le 0;\dot{\kappa}F(T,\kappa )=0
$$

It remains finally to define the evolution of the damage. The model is specific to EDF; it coincides with the classical GTN model in loading but it also specifies what happens in unloading. More precisely, the damage is defined as follows:

$$
d={q}_{1}f+{d}^{\mathit{co}};f={f}^{nu}+{f}^{\mathit{gr}}
$$

Where $f$ is the porosity, the sum of the nucleation porosity (nucleation in English) ${f}^{nu}$ and of the growth porosity (growth) ${f}^{\mathit{gr}}$ , and ${d}^{\mathit{co}}$ is the coalescence damage. In the initial (natural) state, one has:

$$
{f}^{nu}(0)=0;{f}^{\mathit{gr}}(0)={f}_{0};{d}^{\mathit{co}}(0)=0\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}f(0)={f}_{0}
$$

With ${f}_{0}$ the initial porosity.

The growth porosity follows from the conservation of the plastic volume:

$$
\dot{{f}^{\mathit{gr}}}=(1-f)\mathrm{tr}\dot{{E}^{p}};{f}^{\mathit{gr}}\ge {f}_{0}
$$

It cannot fall below ${f}_{0}$ but it is reversible (the cavities can be closed again).

As regards the nucleation porosity, two families of models are implemented:

$$
{f}^{nu}={f}_{(1)}^{nu}+{f}_{(2)}^{nu}
$$

The first family adopts an evolution of the porosity related to that of the hardening variable:

$$
{\dot{f}}_{(1)}^{nu}={B}_{n}(\kappa )\dot{\kappa}\text{}\Rightarrow \text{}{f}_{(1)}^{nu}(\kappa )=\underset{0}{\overset{\kappa}{\int}}{B}_{n}(s)\mathit{ds}
$$

In order to cover two models used in the literature, the following function ${B}_{n}$ is chosen:

$$
{B}_{n}(\kappa )=\frac{{f}_{N}}{{s}_{N}\sqrt{2\pi }}\exp\left[-\frac{1}{2}{\left(\frac{\kappa -{\kappa}_{N}}{{s}_{N}}\right)}^{2}\right]+\frac{{c}_{0}}{{\kappa}_{f}-{\kappa}_{i}}{\mathrm{\chi }}_{[{\kappa}_{i};{\kappa}_{f}]}
$$

where ${\chi }_{I}$ equals 1 in the interval $I$ and 0 outside. Taking into account the antiderivatives of these two terms, one deduces:

$$
{f}_{(1)}^{nu}=\frac{{f}_{N}}{2}\left[\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{\kappa -{\kappa}_{N}}{\sqrt{2}{s}_{N}}\right)+\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{{\kappa}_{N}}{\sqrt{2}{s}_{N}}\right)\right]+{c}_{0}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\min\left(\frac{⟨\kappa -{\kappa}_{i}⟩}{{\kappa}_{f}-{\kappa}_{i}},1\right)
$$

where the McCauley brackets $⟨x⟩$ denote the positive part of $x$ .

The second family of nucleation models depends on the cumulative plastic strain, which is defined as follows:

$$
{\dot{E}}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}\equiv \sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{E}}^{p}:{\dot{E}}^{p}}
$$

The nucleation function implemented is proportional to ${E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}$ starting from a threshold:

$$
{f}_{(2)}^{nu}({E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p})={b}_{0}⟨{E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}-{E}_{c}^{p}⟩
$$

As the hardening variable $\kappa$ and the cumulative plastic strain ${E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}$ are increasing by definition, the same is true of the nucleation porosity; its evolution is irreversible.

It remains to define the evolution of the coalescence damage. For this, the definition (usual for GTN) of the porosity in the coalescence regime ${f}^{\text{*}}$ is introduced, where ${f}_{c}$ denotes the porosity threshold at coalescence and ${f}_{F}$ the porosity at failure:

$$
{f}^{\text{*}}=\lbrace \begin{array}{cc}f& \text{si}f<{f}_{c}\\ {f}_{c}+\delta (f-{f}_{c})& \text{si}f\ge {f}_{c}\end{array}
$$

The coalescence damage then obeys the following evolution law:

$$
{d}^{\mathit{co}}(t)=\underset{\tau \le t}{\max}[{q}_{1}{f}^{\text{*}}(\tau )-{q}_{1}f(\tau )]
$$

It too is increasing by definition, and therefore irreversible.

For numerical reasons, a damage threshold at failure ${d}_{f}$ is also introduced, beyond which the point is considered to be broken, that is to say $T=0$ (there is therefore a jump in stress which can disturb the solution of the equilibrium equations but facilitates the integration of the constitutive behaviour). Once a point is broken, it remains so permanently. This mechanism is not activated by default (${d}_{f}=1$ ).

### 2.2 Taking viscosity into account

In the presence of viscosity, the plastic flow is delayed. More precisely, the rate of evolution of the hardening is no longer fixed by the consistency condition () but is henceforth a function of the intensity of the threshold function according to the Norton law:

$$
\dot{\kappa}={\left(\frac{⟨F(T,\kappa )⟩}{\tilde{K}}\right)}^{n}
$$

The other equations of the model remain unchanged. When $\tilde{K}\to 0$ , the elastoplastic model is recovered.

In general, the parameter $\tilde{K}$ coincides with the parameter $K$ of the Norton model. However, when the damage $d$ approaches 1, the gap between ${T}_{\text{*}}$ and $T$ increases (since $T$ tends to zero) and the viscoplasticity can become too abrupt to retain an effective role, in particular in the presence of numerical integration errors. This is why a modification of the usual Norton law is proposed, by coupling the viscoplasticity with the damage, beyond a threshold ${d}_{v}$ :

$$
\begin{array}{cc}\text{si}d\le {d}_{v}& \tilde{K}=K\\ \text{si}d\ge {d}_{v}& \text{}\tilde{K}=K\frac{1-{d}_{v}}{1-d}\end{array}
$$

Finally, viscoplasticity makes it possible to accommodate potential stress jumps by smoothing them in time. It is important to preserve this mechanism, including for broken points. In this case, the cancellation of the stresses (broken point) is delayed in time, in a manner similar to a relaxation mechanism, provided that ${d}_{v}<1$ has been chosen. More precisely, in the viscoplastic regime of a broken point, the plasticity and the stresses obey the following evolution law:

$$
\dot{\kappa}=0;\dot{{E}^{p}}=0
$$

$$
\begin{array}{cc}\text{si}\Vert T\Vert \ge K(1-{d}_{v})& \text{}\frac{\dot{T}}{E}=-{\left[\frac{\Vert T\Vert }{K(1-{d}_{v})}\right]}^{n}\frac{T}{\Vert T\Vert }\\ \text{si}\Vert T\Vert \le K(1-{d}_{v})& \frac{\dot{T}}{E}=-\frac{T}{K(1-{d}_{v})}\end{array}
$$

The change to an exponent equal to 1 when $\Vert T\Vert \le K(1-{d}_{v})$ avoids delaying the erasure of the stress too much.

### 2.3 Taking the hardening gradient into account

A non-local formulation with a gradient of an internal variable is appropriate when the hardening gradient $\nabla \kappa$ becomes large. It consists in introducing an additional term ${\Phi}^{\mathit{grad}}$ into the free energy which reflects the interactions between neighbouring material points, see [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01):

$$
{\Phi}^{\mathit{grad}}(\nabla \kappa )=\frac{1}{2}c{\left(\nabla \kappa \right)}^{2}
$$

Using the augmented relaxed formulation presented in [R5.04.01](doc:r5.04.01), the impact on the constitutive relation results in a modification of the thermodynamic force associated with the hardening variable and therefore, in practice, in a modification of the hardening function:

$$
\tilde{R}(\kappa )=R(\kappa )+r\kappa -\varphi ;\varphi =\lambda +\mathit{ra}
$$

where $\lambda$ is the Lagrange multiplier associated with the relaxation, $r>0$ the augmentation coefficient and $a$ the interpretation of the hardening variable at the scale of the structure. These three quantities are data as far as the constitutive relation is concerned. In the end, the hardening function is corrected by an affine term, which therefore does not add any complexity relative to the numerical treatment of the model. On the other hand, the sign and the amplitude of $\varphi$ not being fixed, this explains why the case of a singular flow for which ${T}_{\text{*}}=0$ must be taken into account.

## 3 Numerical integration

As regards the integration of the constitutive law, that is to say the computation of the internal variables and of the stresses for a given (mechanical) strain history, there is no longer any reason to distinguish whether it is a local formulation or a gradient formulation. Indeed, the impact of the latter is limited to modifying the hardening function according to (). It will continue to be denoted $R(\kappa )$ here without distinction.

On the other hand, the plastic and viscoplastic cases will be distinguished, even though it can be shown that the latter is also limited to a correction of the threshold function in the discretised equations.

### 3.1 Discretised equations in the case without viscosity

The time discretisation of the constitutive equations relies on a staggered scheme: with the damage fixed, an implicit Euler scheme is applied (even in viscoplasticity) in order to compute the plastic variables (plastic strain, hardening variable) as well as the stress, that is to say that the various variables of the problem are expressed at the final time instant of the time step considered. The evolution of the various contributions to the damage is then evaluated, knowing the evolution of the plastic variables. The reader is referred to [Zhang et al., 2018] for more information on the time discretisation and the solution of the corresponding algebraic system.

${Q}_{n}$ denotes the value of a quantity $Q$ at the beginning of the time step, $\Delta Q$ its increment during the time step and (simply) $Q$ its value at the end of the time step. The mechanical state at the beginning of the time step $({E}_{n},{{E}^{p}}_{n},{\kappa}_{n},{f}_{n}^{nu},{f}_{n}^{\mathit{gr}},{d}_{n}^{\mathit{co}},{d}_{n})$ is assumed known, as well as the strain increment $\Delta E$ (and therefore also the strain $E$ ). The increments of the internal variables as well as the stress at the end of the time step $T$ are then to be computed.

Regarding the value of the damage chosen to integrate the plasticity, [Zhang et al., 2018] proposed to adopt that at the beginning of the time step, namely ${d}_{\mathit{ex}}={d}_{n}$ . For reasons of integration accuracy (and therefore of time-step size), this scheme is enriched by extrapolating the damage during the time step on the basis of its rate of evolution at the previous step. More precisely, one defines:

$$
\dot{{d}_{n}}=\frac{{d}_{n}-{d}_{n-1}}{{t}_{n}-{t}_{n-1}};{d}_{\mathit{ex}}={d}_{n}+\theta (t-{t}_{n})\dot{{d}_{n}}
$$

where the value of $\theta$ is fixed by the user under the keyword COMPORTEMENT/ PARM_THETA; the value $\theta =1/2$ is recommended.

Knowing now the extrapolated damage value, one can proceed to the implicit integration of the plasticity. If ${d}_{\mathit{ex}}\ge {d}_{f}$ , one simply switches to the broken-point regime and $T=0$ . Otherwise, the discretisation of the stress–strain relation () is written as follows:

$$
T=K\mathrm{tr}(E-{{E}^{p}}_{n}-\Delta {E}^{p})\mathit{Id}+2\mu \mathrm{dev}(E-{{E}^{p}}_{n}-\Delta {E}^{p})
$$

It is chosen to bring out an elastic stress (or elastic trial stress), denoted ${T}^{e}$ and which is known:

$$
T={T}^{e}-K\mathrm{tr}(\Delta {E}^{p})-2\mu \mathrm{dev}(\Delta {E}^{p});{T}^{e}=K\mathrm{tr}(E-{{E}^{p}}_{n})\mathit{Id}+2\mu \mathrm{dev}(E-{{E}^{p}}_{n})
$$

The discretisation of the threshold function is written simply:

$$
F(T,\kappa )=\frac{{T}_{\text{*}}}{J}-R(\kappa )
$$

And the consistency condition is:

$$
\Delta \kappa \ge 0;F(T,\kappa )\le 0;\Delta \kappa \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}F(T,\kappa )=0
$$

In particular, in plastic loading, $F=0$ , so that the equivalent GTN stress can be deduced as a function of the hardening level:

$$
{T}_{\text{*}}=\widehat{T}(\kappa )\equiv JR(\kappa )
$$

In the case of a regular flow, the time discretisation of () is written:

$$
\mathrm{tr}(\Delta {E}^{p})=3\frac{\lambda}{J}\Theta \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}},\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)\Lambda \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)
$$

$$
\mathrm{dev}(\Delta {E}^{p})=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\lambda}{J}\Theta \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}},\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)\frac{\mathrm{dev}(T)}{{T}_{\text{*}}}
$$

The following functions have been introduced there:

$$
\Lambda (x)=\frac{1}{2}{q}_{2}{d}_{\mathit{ex}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\sinh\left(\frac{3}{2}{q}_{2}x\right);\Theta (x,y)={\left[{y}^{2}+3x\Lambda (x)\right]}^{-1}
$$

By substitution of () and () into (), one deduces:

$$
\frac{{T}_{H}^{e}-{T}_{H}}{3K}=\frac{\lambda}{J}\Theta \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}},\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)\Lambda \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)
$$

$$
\mathrm{dev}(T)={\left[1+3\mu \frac{\lambda}{J}\Theta \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}},\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)\frac{1}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right]}^{-1}\mathrm{dev}({T}^{e})
$$

This last expression indicates that $T$ has the same direction and the same sense as ${T}^{e}$ . Furthermore, by substituting $\Theta$ by its expression coming from (), one obtains the value of :math:`{T}_{\mathit{eq}}$ as a function of ${T}_{H}$ and ${T}_{\text{*}}$ :

$$
{T}_{\mathit{eq}}={T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}{\left[1+\frac{\mu}{K}\frac{{T}_{H}^{e}-{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}\Lambda (\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}})}\right]}^{-1}\equiv {\widehat{T}}_{\mathit{eq}}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})
$$

Taking into account the definition of the equivalent GTN stress (), an implicit link between ${T}_{H}$ and ${T}_{\text{*}}$ can be expressed by substituting () into it:

$$
\widehat{G}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})\equiv {\left[\frac{{\widehat{T}}_{\mathit{eq}}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right]}^{2}+2{d}_{\mathit{ex}}\cosh\left[\frac{3}{2}{q}_{2}\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right]-1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}}^{2}=0
$$

The plastic multiplier, equal to $\Delta \kappa$ , is deduced from ():

$$
\Delta \kappa =\widehat{\lambda}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})\equiv J\frac{{T}_{H}^{e}-{T}_{H}}{3K\Lambda \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}\frac{1}{\Theta \left(\frac{{T}_{H}}{{T}_{\text{*}}},\frac{{\widehat{T}}_{\mathit{eq}}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}
$$

When the flow is singular, ${T}_{\text{*}}=0$ and therefore $T=0$ , which makes it possible to compute $\Delta {E}^{p}$ via (). It then remains to verify that the flow does indeed belong to the cone prescribed by (), that is, after discretisation:

$$
{\Pi}_{N}(\Delta {E}^{p},{d}_{\mathit{ex}})\le \frac{\Delta \kappa }{J}
$$

Knowing the evolution of the plasticity $\Delta {E}^{p}$ and $\Delta \kappa$ , one can now proceed to the integration of the porosity. The nucleation porosity ${f}^{nu}$ is simply computed via the functions () and (); the cumulative plastic strain is evaluated as follows:

$$
\Delta {E}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}\Delta {E}^{p}:\Delta {E}^{p}}
$$

Regarding the growth porosity, a theta-scheme is applied, with the same value of $\theta$ as during the prediction phase. The corresponding discretisation is written:

$$
\Delta f-\Delta {f}^{nu}=(1-\theta f-(1-\theta ){f}_{n})\text{}\mathrm{tr}(\Delta {E}_{p})
$$

One deduces:

$$
\left[1+\mathrm{tr}\Delta {E}^{p}\right]{f}^{\mathit{gr}}={f}_{n}^{\mathit{gr}}+(1-\theta {f}^{nu}-(1-\theta ){f}_{n})\mathrm{tr}\Delta {E}^{p}
$$

Finally, the coalescence damage is simply defined via:

$$
{d}^{\mathit{co}}=\max\left({d}_{n}^{\mathit{co}},{q}_{1}({f}^{\text{*}}-f)\right)
$$

### 3.2 Solution of the discretised problem without viscosity

*Preliminary note*: the equations are solved to a certain accuracy. If $ϵ$ denotes the accuracy supplied by the user via the keyword RESI_INTE_RELA, the following accuracies can be introduced, respectively in terms of stress, of strain and dimensionless for equation ():

$$
{ϵ}_{\sigma}=R(0)ϵ;{ϵ}_{\kappa}=\frac{1}{10}\frac{R(0)}{E}ϵ;{ϵ}_{G}=(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}})ϵ
$$

If $1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}}<{ϵ}^{1/2}$ , the point is considered to be broken: $T=0$ , $\Delta \kappa =0$ , $f={f}_{F}$ (even if ${d}_{\mathit{ex}}<{d}_{f}$ , because at the numerical accuracy requested, the "broken point" solution is indeed acceptable). Otherwise, the discretised problem must be solved.

The latter admits a unique solution. One begins by determining the regime to which it belongs: elastic response, plastic response with singular flow or plastic response with regular flow. The solution is elastic if:

$$
{T}_{\text{*}}^{e}<\widehat{T}({\kappa}_{n})
$$

In this case, $\Delta \kappa =0$ , $\Delta {E}^{p}=0$ and $T={T}^{e}$ .

If the solution is not elastic, the case of a singular plastic flow is examined. In this case, ${T}_{\text{*}}=0$ , $T=0$ , $\Delta {E}^{p}$ is obtained via () and ${\Pi}_{N}^{s}$ denotes the corresponding value of ${\Pi}_{N}$ according to (). One also computes ${\kappa}^{z}$ such that (solution by a Newton method):

$$
\widehat{T}({\kappa}^{z})=0
$$

The flow is singular if ${\kappa}^{z}\ge {\kappa}_{n}$ and if $J\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\Pi}_{N}^{s}\le \Delta {\kappa}^{z}$ according to (); one then has $\kappa ={\kappa}^{z}$ .

Finally, otherwise, the plastic flow is regular and one has in particular $0<{T}_{\text{*}}<{T}_{\text{*}}^{e}$ . It can be noted that, for a given ${T}_{\text{*}}$ , equation () makes it possible to recover the value of ${T}_{H}$ . Denoting ${T}_{H}=p\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{T}_{H}^{e}$ , the function $\widehat{g}:p\to \widehat{G}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})$ is increasing and the solution satisfies the bounds $0\le p\le 1$ . To facilitate the solution, a minimum bound ${p}_{min}>0$ can be computed by majorising the function $\widehat{g}$ . Denoting $r\equiv {T}_{H}/{T}_{\text{*}}$ and $\alpha \equiv \mu /K$ , one has:

$$
\widehat{g}(p)={\left(\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}^{2}{\left(\frac{\Lambda (r)}{\alpha \frac{{T}_{H}^{e}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}+\Lambda (r)-\alpha \phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}r}\right)}^{2}+{d}_{\mathit{ex}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}2\mathrm{chm}(\frac{3}{2}{q}_{2}r)-{(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}})}^{2}
$$

The denominator of the second factor can be minorised analytically and it is denoted ${D}_{min}$ . As furthermore :math:`` , one obtains:

$$
\widehat{g}(p)\le {\left(\frac{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}}{{T}_{\text{*}}}\right)}^{2}{\left(\frac{\Lambda (r)}{{D}_{min}}\right)}^{2}+{d}_{\mathit{ex}}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}2\mathrm{sh}(\frac{3}{2}{q}_{2}r)-{(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}})}^{2}
$$

The root of the right-hand side can be obtained analytically and makes it possible to recover the bound ${p}_{min}$ . A solution of the equation $\widehat{g}(p)=0$ by a Newton method in the interval ${p}_{min}\le p\le 1$ makes it possible to find the solution $p({T}_{\text{*}})$ . Its accuracy is controlled via the convergence condition $\left|\widehat{g}\right|\le {ϵ}_{G}$ .

The incremental problem can thus be reduced to the single unknown $\Delta \kappa$ . Indeed, for a given $\kappa$ , one can compute ${T}_{\text{*}}=\widehat{T}(\kappa )$ . From there, one deduces $p({T}_{\text{*}})$ by solving the previous equation. Knowing $p$ and therefore ${T}_{H}$ , one can compute $\lambda =\widehat{\lambda}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}})$ via (), which reduces the problem to the solution of the scalar equation $\Delta \kappa =\lambda$ .

Thanks to the convexity properties of the equivalent stress ${T}_{\text{*}}$ with respect to $T$ , the analysis of the "return mapping" scheme used shows that, as $\lambda$ increases from 0 to $J\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\Pi}_{N}^{s}$ , ${T}_{\text{*}}$ decreases from ${T}_{\text{*}}^{e}$ to 0. The solution of the problem therefore amounts to finding the intersection of the increasing curve $\Delta \kappa \to {T}_{\text{*}}$ with the decreasing curve ${T}_{\text{*}}\to \lambda$ . A minimum bound on $\Delta \kappa$ is supplied by the maximum of 0 and of $\Delta {\kappa}_{z}$ , because the solution ${T}_{\text{*}}$ must be positive and $\Delta \kappa$ as well. A maximum bound is supplied by the minimum of $J\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{\Pi}_{N}^{s}$ and of $\Delta {\kappa}^{e}$ such that $\widehat{T}({\kappa}^{e})={T}_{\text{*}}^{e}$ (if it exists), because the solution ${T}_{\text{*}}$ must be positive and smaller than ${T}_{\text{*}}^{e}$ .

The equation is solved by a Newton method between these two bounds. The accuracy of the solution is controlled by considering that the algorithm has converged as soon as one of the conditions $\left|\Delta \kappa -\lambda \right|\le {ϵ}_{\kappa}$ or $\left|\widehat{T}(\Delta \kappa )-\widehat{T}(\lambda )\right|\le {ϵ}_{\sigma}$ is reached. It is chosen to initialise the Newton method by the solution $\Delta {\kappa}_{\mathit{VM}}$ of the equivalent von Mises problem (i.e. by setting ${T}_{H}=0$ ) when it respects the bounds, or otherwise by a secant method with respect to the values at the bounds.

Finally one recovers the stress via the following relation:

$$
T={T}_{H}\mathrm{Id}+{T}_{\mathit{eq}}\frac{\mathrm{dev}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}{T}^{e}}{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}}
$$

### 3.3 Solution of the discretised problem in the viscoplastic case

In the presence of viscosity, the consistency condition () is replaced by the Norton evolution law (). Once discretised, the latter is written:

$$
\frac{\Delta \kappa }{\Delta t}={\left(\frac{⟨F(T,\kappa )⟩}{\tilde{K}}\right)}^{n}
$$

And by inverting this relation:

$$
{C}_{\Delta t}{\Delta \kappa }^{q}=⟨F(T,\kappa )⟩;{C}_{\Delta t}=\frac{\tilde{K}}{{\Delta t}^{1/n}};q=\frac{1}{n}
$$

Comme $n>1$ , $0<q<1$ .

It can still be expressed in an equivalent manner in the form of a Kuhn and Tucker relation:

$$
\Delta \kappa \ge 0;{F}_{v}(T,\kappa )\le 0;{F}_{v}(T,\kappa )\Delta \kappa =0
$$

$$
\text{où}{F}_{v}(T,\kappa )=F(T,\kappa )-{C}_{\Delta t}{\Delta \kappa }^{q}=\frac{{T}_{\text{*}}}{J}-{R}_{v}(\kappa );{R}_{v}(\kappa )=R(\kappa )+{C}_{\Delta t}{(\kappa -{\kappa}_{n})}^{q}
$$

After discretisation, taking viscosity into account reduces to the addition of a term (positive and increasing) in the hardening function.

One might be tempted to apply the same solution method as previously but, unfortunately, this new term is not differentiable at $\Delta \kappa =0$ (infinite slope). This is why it is proposed to distinguish two regimes, one with a steep slope, the other with a gentle slope. The "boundary" slope is supplied as a lump sum by $R'(0)$ . More precisely, $V(\Delta \kappa )={C}_{\Delta t}{\Delta \kappa }^{q}$ denotes the viscous term. The boundary value $\Delta {\kappa}_{v}$ corresponds to:

$$
V'(\Delta {\kappa}_{v})=R'(0)
$$

If $\Delta \kappa <\Delta {\kappa}_{v}$ , one is in the steep-slope regime, i.e. dominated by viscosity. Conversely, if $\Delta \kappa >\Delta {\kappa}_{v}$ , one is in the gentle-slope regime, dominated by plasticity. Taking into account the monotonicity of the scalar equation $\Delta \kappa -\widehat{\lambda}=0$ to be solved with respect to $\Delta \kappa$ , the regime of the solution can be determined from the outset and the search interval reduced if necessary.

In the regime dominated by plasticity, the equation in $\Delta \kappa$  is solved as in the previous section; the viscous term does not present any particular numerical difficulties.

On the contrary, in the regime dominated by viscosity, a Newton method on $\Delta \kappa$ is not efficient (slopes too steep, hence very slow convergence). The change of variable $v=V(\Delta \kappa )$ is then carried out (which eliminates the steep slopes in the neighbourhood of 0) and the Newton method is applied with respect to $v$ . The convergence criteria remain unchanged.

### 3.4 Tangent matrix

In the local case, the fourth-order tensor ${H}_{\mathit{TE}}=\delta T/\delta E$ is to be determined. In the non-local case, the second-order tensors ${H}_{T\varphi }=\delta T/\delta \varphi$ and ${H}_{\kappa E}=\delta \kappa /\delta E$ must also be built, as well as the scalar ${H}_{\kappa \varphi }=\delta \kappa /\delta \varphi$ .

The tangent operator depends on the flow regime. In the elastic case, it reduces to:

$$
\delta T=K\mathrm{tr}(\delta E)\mathit{Id}+2\mu \mathit{dev}(\delta E);\delta \kappa =0
$$

In the case of the singular regime, the expression is also very simple:

$$
\delta T=0;\delta \kappa =-{\left(\frac{\partial \widehat{T}}{\partial \kappa }\right)}^{-1}\left[\frac{\partial \widehat{T}}{\partial \varphi }\delta \varphi +\frac{\partial \widehat{T}}{\partial J}\frac{\partial J}{\partial E}\delta E\right]
$$

where the function $\widehat{T}$ contains, where applicable, the terms related to the non-local contribution and to the viscosity.

The regular flow regime leads to a more complex tangent operator. We content ourselves here with describing the broad lines of the method for computing it. This regime is characterised by the solution of the system of equations $\widehat{G}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}};{T}_{H}^{e},{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e},J)=0$ and $\Delta \kappa -\widehat{\lambda}({T}_{H},{T}_{\text{*}};{T}_{H}^{e},{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e},J)=0$ with the relation ${T}_{\text{*}}=\widehat{T}(\Delta \kappa ;J,\varphi )$ . The derivative of the implicit functions makes it possible to express the variations of the unknowns $\delta {T}_{H}$ and $\delta \kappa$ as functions of the variations of the input data of the system (2x2 linear system to be solved). From there, one can recover the variation of the stress by differentiating ().

Finally, it remains to link the variations of the input data to those of the strain $E$ and of $\varphi$ . The only (minor) difficulty comes from the von Mises stress. For this, the following relation is recalled:

$$
\delta {T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mathrm{dev}{T}^{e}}{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}}
\delta {T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{\mathrm{dev}{T}^{e}}{{T}_{\mathit{eq}}^{e}}
$$

### 3.5 Variables computed in post-processing

Four of the internal variables computed in post-processing still need to be defined at this stage:

- The part of the stresses related to hardening:

$$
\text{SIEQ\_ECR =}J(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}})R(\kappa )
$$

- The part of the stresses related to viscosity:

$$
\text{SIEQ\_VSC =}J(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}}){C}_{\Delta t}\Delta {\kappa}^{q}
$$

- The part of the stresses related to the non-local term:

$$
\text{SIEQ\_NLC =}J(1-{d}_{\mathit{ex}})(r\kappa -\varphi )
$$

- The extrapolation error expressed in stresses:

$$
\text{SIEQ\_ERX =}\left(1-\frac{{T}^{\text{*}}(T,{d}_{\mathit{ex}})}{{T}^{\text{*}}(T,d)}\right)\Vert T\Vert
$$
