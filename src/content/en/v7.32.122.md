# v7.32.122 WTNP122 - Modelling a low-linear compressible gas saturated bar (single-phase flow) subjected to pressure shock

**Summary:**

The objective of this test case is to validate:

- finite volume diagrams developed for diphasic flow modelling.
- hydraulic modelling saturated elements finished D_PLAN_HS

The diphasic problem here will be degenerated into a gas monophase problem known to the analytical solution. This is the one-dimensional Modelling of a gas saturated bar subjected to pressure shock.

## 2 analytical solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

The single-phase instationary and one-dimensional problem can be written in a general form of the type:

$N\frac{\partial P}{\partial t}-{K}_{i}\Delta P=0$

$P(t=0)={P}_{0}$

$P(t,x=0)=0$

$\frac{\partial P}{\partial x}(t,x,L)=0$

This problem allows an analytical solution obtained by developing in series of Fourier.

$P={\sum}_{k=0}^{K}\frac{{\mathrm{4P}}_{0}}{(\mathrm{2k}+1)\pi }\exp(\frac{-{K}_{i}}{N}{\omega}_{k}^{2}t)\sin({\omega}_{k}x)$ with ${\omega}_{k}=(k+\frac{1}{2})\frac{\pi}{L}$

The number of $K$ terms in this series is determined as follows:

${n}_{x}$ is the number of ${x}_{i}$ points where the solution is evaluated at a $t$ moment.

We're laying:

${a}_{k}^{i}=\frac{4}{(\mathrm{2k}+1)\pi }\exp(\frac{-{K}_{i}}{N}{\omega}_{k}^{2}t)\sin({\omega}_{k}{x}_{i})$

So the solution can write: $P({x}_{i})={\sum}_{k=0}^{K}{P}_{0}.{a}_{k}^{i}$

Choose $K$ as: $\frac{1}{{n}_{x}}\sqrt{{\sum}_{i=1}^{\mathrm{nx}}{({a}_{k}^{i})}^{2}}<\epsilon$

In practice we took $\varepsilon ={10}^{-10}$ .

### 2.2 Simplification assumptions

The medium is considered to be completely saturated with gas and aster is applied a zero liquid pressure on all nodes. An initial pressure of ${P}_{g}^{\mathit{ref}}$ gas is imposed and boundary conditions is given which corresponds to a variation of this reference pressure, or $\delta {P}_{g}$ this pressure variation. The gas mass conservation equation will be written:

$\frac{\partial (\varphi \delta {P}_{g})}{\partial t}+d(\frac{{K}_{i}{k}_{g}}{{\mu}_{g}}({P}_{g}^{r}+\delta {P}_{g})d({P}_{g}^{r}+\delta {P}_{g}))=0$

Assuming $\delta {P}_{g}$ small ahead of ${P}_{g}^{\mathit{ref}}$, this equation becomes:

$\frac{\partial (\varphi \delta {P}_{g})}{\partial t}+\frac{{K}_{i}{k}_{g}}{{\mu}_{g}}{P}_{g}^{r}\Delta (\delta {P}_{g})=0$

Therefore, $\delta {P}_{g}$ will be identified with the solution of the model analytical equation.

To find the coefficients of the model problem, we will take:

$\varphi =1$

${k}_{g}={\mu}_{g}=1$

and we'll make sure

${K}_{int}{P}_{\mathit{ref}}={10}^{-3}$ .

## 8 Summary of the results

This test case allows testing finished volume diagrams in different configurations on a gas flow model:

- the diagram of the finite volumes decentralized edge;
- on different types of mesh cells (triangles and rectangles).

These same cases are also made with the classic elements finished diagrams. All results are very close to the analytical solution.

From a performance point view, we will recommend using the dice diagram.
