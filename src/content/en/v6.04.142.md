# v6.04.142 SSNV142 - Clean creep test: Granger model

**Summary:**

This test case of quasi-static non-linear mechanics simulates a uniaxial creep test. Its objective is to validate the behavioral relationship of "Granger" BETON_GRANGER, allowing to model the clean creep of concretes. This linear viscoelastic model (a grouping of series Kelvin rheological models) allows to take into account the effects of stress, temperature and hygrometry.

In this test, the stress applied, temperature and hygrometric state are constant.

In modellings:

- A: The cylinder is modeled in axi-symmetric by four elements quadrangles at 8 nodes.
- D: treats the same problem on a $\mathrm{3D}$ cube.

The results obtained by *Code_Aster* are compared with the reference analytical solution.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

Given the nature of the stresses, the solution ($\sigma$ stresses, $\varepsilon$ deformations) is homogeneous.

Either the loading: $(\begin{array}{ccc}0& 0& 0\\ 0& {\sigma}_{d}(t)& 0\\ 0& 0& 0\end{array})$

Granger's own creep model is such that the viscoelastic deformation corresponding to the case of a constant ${\sigma}_{0}$ loading applied at the ${t}_{0}$ moment is: (see [R7.01.01](doc:r7.01.01))

${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{fl}}(t)={\sigma}_{0}\sum_{k=1}^{8}{J}_{k}.(1-\exp\left[-\frac{t-{t}_{0}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right])$

The model also depends on temperature and hygrometry as follows:

${\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{fl}}(t)={\sigma}_{0}h\cdot \frac{T-248}{45}\cdot \sum_{k=1}^{8}{J}_{k}.(1-\exp\left[-\frac{t-{t}_{0}}{{\tau}_{s}}\right])$

but in this test the temperature and d-hygrometry fields are chosen constant and such as $h$ and $\frac{T-248}{45}$ are valid respectively 1.

When the constraint evolves with time then:

${\varepsilon}^{\text{fl}}(t)=\sum_{k=1}^{8}\underset{\tau =0}{\overset{t}{\int}}{J}_{k}.(1-\exp\left[-\frac{t-\tau }{{\tau}_{s}}\right]).\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \tau }.d\tau$

Therefore, in this case:

${\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\text{fl}}=\sum_{k=1}^{2}\underset{\tau =0}{\overset{t={t}_{1}}{\int}}{J}_{k}.(1-\exp\left[-\frac{t-\tau }{{\tau}_{s}}\right]).\frac{{\sigma}_{1}}{{t}_{1}}.d\tau$

the load remaining constant beyond t1. Either:

${\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\text{fl}}={\sigma}_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{k=2}{J}_{k}.(1-\exp(-\frac{t-{t}_{1}}{{\tau}_{s}}))\underset{\text{}\approx 1}{\underset{\underbrace{}}{\frac{{\tau}_{k}}{{t}_{1}}(1-\exp(\frac{-{t}_{1}}{{\tau}_{s}}))}}$

Longitudinal creep deformation is accompanied by transverse deformation such as:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}^{\mathrm{fl}}=-\nu {\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\mathrm{fl}}$

The total uniaxial deformation is: ${\varepsilon}_{yy}={\varepsilon}_{yy}^{\mathrm{fl}}+\frac{{\sigma}_{yy}}{E}$
