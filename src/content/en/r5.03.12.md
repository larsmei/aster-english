## 1 Elasto-visco-plastic model of Chaboche with memory effect and recovery

The most complete elastoviscoplastic model of J.L.Chaboche superposes on the isotropic hardening two kinematic-hardening variables, and makes it possible to take into account the effects of cyclic hardening, creep, recovery and the memory of the greatest hardening. It is therefore well suited to cyclic loadings.

Under certain loading conditions, structures can be subject to a phenomenon of progressive deformation, which may impair the functional capacities of the apparatus. In its original form, the model greatly overestimates the progressive deformation; in order to improve its representativeness, it has been modified by introducing radial-evanescence terms, so as to model this phenomenon better. The resulting model, named VISCOCHAB, is described in this document.

Several studies have consisted in testing this constitutive law with respect to its capacity to model progressive deformations , , by comparing with tests , , ,  and with other models , in particular that of Taheri [r5.03.05](doc:r5.03.05). In particular, identifications of the steels 316L and 304L have been made (respectively in  and ). Recently, the thermal-fatigue studies have required the use of VISCOCHAB, in particular in order to take the memory effect into account.

It will be noted that if C1 or C2 depends on the temperature, the computation of the Jacobian matrix is not exact, which can make the local convergence difficult if C1 or C2 varies strongly with $T$ .

The use of this constitutive law is accessible in the commands STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE by the keyword VISCOCHAB of COMPORTEMENT.

This complete model can be “degraded” by cancelling the effect of certain mechanisms (for example the recovery effect). For example, by cancelling the recovery effect and the radial-evanescence terms of the kinematic hardening (default material parameters), one recovers the Chaboche model with memory effect VISC_CIN2_MEMO .

For details concerning the choice of the material parameters leading to the cancellation of certain effects, one may refer to the documentation of the command DEFI_MATERIAU.

In the remainder of this document, the model VISCOCHAB is described precisely. The detail of its numerical integration is then presented, in connection with the construction of the consistent tangent matrix.

## 2 The model VISCOCHAB in Code_Aster

### 2.1 Description of the model

At any time instant, the state of the material is described by the strain $\varepsilon$ , the temperature $T$ , the plastic strain ${\varepsilon}^{p}$ , the cumulative plastic strain $p$ and the backstress tensor $X$ . The state equations then define, as a function of these state variables, the stress $\sigma ={\sigma}^{H}\mathrm{Id}+\tilde{\sigma}$ (decomposed into hydrostatic and deviatoric parts), the isotropic part of the hardening $R$ and the kinematic part $X$ :

$$
{\sigma}^{H}=\frac{1}{3}\mathrm{tr}(\sigma )=K\mathrm{tr}(\varepsilon -{\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}) \text{ avec } {\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{th}}=\alpha (T-{T}^{\mathrm{ref}})\mathrm{Id}
$$

$$
\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma -{\sigma}^{H}\mathrm{Id}=2\mu (\tilde{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}^{p})
$$

$$
R=R(p)
$$

$$
X=(p,{\varepsilon}^{p})
$$

where $K,\mu ,\alpha$ and the coefficients of $X(p)$ and $R(p)$ are characteristics of the material which may depend on the temperature. More precisely, they are respectively the bulk and shear moduli, the thermal expansion coefficient, and the isotropic and kinematic hardening functions. As for ${T}^{\mathrm{ref}}$ , it is the reference temperature, for which the thermal strain is considered to be zero.

#### 2.1.1 Yield surface — viscoplastic potential

The yield surface associated with the model VISCOCHAB is represented in the space of the principal stresses by:

- its centre $X$ , kinematic-hardening tensor,
- its size ${\alpha}_{R}R+k$ , $k$ being its initial size and $R$ the isotropic-hardening variable giving the evolution of this size, modulated by the coefficient ${\alpha}_{R}$ ,
- its shape given by the Von Mises criterion applied to $\tilde{\sigma}\text{-X}$ . $\tilde{\sigma}$ is the stress deviator.

The evolution of the plastic strain is governed by a flow law normal to a Von Mises plasticity criterion:

$$
F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}-{\alpha}_{R}R(p)-k\text{ avec }{A}_{\mathrm{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}:\tilde{A}}
$$

The viscoplastic dissipation potential for the Chaboche model is written:

$$
{\Omega}^{p}=\frac{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}{\alpha (n+1)}\exp\left[\alpha {\langle \frac{F}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}\rangle }^{n+1}\right]
$$

where $\langle F\rangle$ is the positive part of $F$ .

One deduces from it the evolution law of the plastic strain:

$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{\partial {\Omega}^{p}}{\partial \sigma }=\dot{p}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}
$$

having set:

$$
\dot{p}={\langle \frac{F}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}\rangle }^{n}\exp\left[\alpha {\langle \frac{F}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}\rangle }^{n+1}\right]
$$

Classically, $\dot{p}$ is the cumulative plastic strain rate. The parameters ${K}_{0}$ , ${\alpha}_{k}$ and $\alpha$ relate to the viscosity of the material (Norton viscosity). Equation  can also be written in an equivalent way in the following form:

$$
\dot{p}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}:{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}}
$$

The unit normal to the yield surface is denoted:

$$
n=\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }{\parallel \frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }\parallel }^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}
$$

The evolution laws of the isotropic hardening and of the kinematic hardening are obtained from the thermodynamic potential and from the dissipation potential.

#### 2.1.2 Formulation of the isotropic hardening

The evolution of the isotropic-hardening variable $R$ is given by:

$$
\dot{R}=b(Q-R)\dot{p}+{\gamma}_{r}{\mid {Q}_{r}-R\mid }^{{m}_{r}}\text{sgn}({Q}_{r}-R)
$$

This evolution law of the isotropic hardening involves a first term, linear as a function of the cumulative viscoplastic strain rate $\dot{p}$ . This term is useful for describing the evolution of the loop (softening or hardening) under cyclic loading. This term supplies an asymptotic value of $R$ (corresponding to the stabilised state) equal to the variable $Q$ . This variable $Q$ represents the cyclic hardening (the memory effect of the maximum strains). It is not a constant but it depends on the maximum amplitude of the strain (memory effect):

$$
Q=Q{}_{0}{}^{}\text{}+({Q}_{m}-{Q}_{0})(1-{e}^{-2\mu q})
$$

One defines in strain space a yield surface inside which $Q$ is a constant (non-hardening domain):

$$
f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{2}{3}{({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}_{\text{eq}}-q\le 0
$$

This criterion defines a domain characterising the maximum plastic strains, of which $q$ measures the radius and $\xi$ the centre, computed according to a normality law:

$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}:\frac{\partial f}{\partial {\varepsilon}^{p}}>0
$$

The unit normal to the yield surface is denoted:

$$
{n}^{\text{*}}=\frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }{\parallel \frac{\partial f}{\partial \sigma }\parallel }^{-1}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi }{{({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}_{\text{eq}}}
$$

The evolution laws of the variables $q$ and $\xi$ are given in the form:

$$
\dot{q}=\eta H(f)\langle n:{n}^{\text{*}}\rangle \dot{p}
$$

$$
\dot{\xi}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\eta )H(f)\langle n:{n}^{\text{*}}\rangle \dot{p}{n}^{\text{*}}
$$

The parameter $\eta$ (which does not exist in the initial formulation 5 ) makes it possible to take the memory effect partially into account. If it is equal to 1/2, one recovers the initial formulation. If it is equal to 1, $q$ is equal to the norm of the greatest plastic strain reached. If it is much smaller than 1/2, the memory effect is taken into account only in part.

**Note:**

*The same expressions for* $Q$ *,* $f$ *,* $\dot{q}$ *and* $\dot{\xi}$ *are used in the laws VMIS/VISC_CIN1/2_MEMO*   *which are simplified (and optimised) versions of VISCOCHAB* cf [r5.03.04](doc:r5.03.04).

The evolution law of the isotropic hardening  involves a second term, making it possible to take the effect of recovery into account. The variable ${Q}_{r}$ is given by the equation:

$$
{Q}_{r}=Q-{Q}_{r}^{\text{*}}\left[1-{(\frac{{Q}_{m}-Q}{{Q}_{m}})}^{2}\right]
$$

Note that in the initial model of Chaboche , the coefficient ${m}_{r}$ in equation  is equal to 1.

#### 2.1.3 Formulation of the kinematic hardening

Before giving the expression of the hardening law of the model VISCOCHAB, the various stages which allowed its development are recalled.

The simplest law is a linear hardening of the form:

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}
$$

To this law, one can add a non-linear recall term supplying an evanescent memory effect of the loading path (initial model of Chaboche):

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C\dot{{\varepsilon}^{p}}-\gamma X\dot{p}
$$

with

$$
\gamma ={\gamma}^{0}\left[{a}_{\infty}+(1-{a}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}}\right]
$$

It has been shown that such a law greatly overestimates the phenomenon of progressive deformation. This leads one to introduce a radial-evanescence term (term due to Burlet and Cailletaud):

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-\gamma (X:n)n\dot{p}
$$

In fact, this law now underestimates the phenomenon of progressive deformation. One can then combine the two equations  and  with the weighting parameter $\delta \in \left[0,1\right]$ , in order to estimate the progressive deformations better:

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-\left[\delta (\gamma X\dot{p})+(1-\delta )(\gamma (X:n)n\dot{p})\right]
$$

In the initial model of Chaboche, one also finds an additional term which makes it possible to introduce the effects of recovery into the kinematic hardening, which finally gives the following law:

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-\gamma \left[\delta X+(1-\delta )(X:n)n\right]\dot{p}-{\gamma}_{X}{\left[{(X)}_{\text{eq}}\right]}^{m-1}X
$$

with $\gamma$ given by the equation .

For an exact taking into account of the dependence of the material parameters with respect to temperature, it is necessary to add an additional term in $\dot{T}$ , which gives:

$$
\dot{X}=\frac{2}{3}C{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-\gamma \left[\delta X+(1-\delta )(X:n)n\right]\dot{p}-{\gamma}_{X}{\left[{(X)}_{\text{eq}}\right]}^{m-1}X+\frac{1}{C}\frac{\partial C}{\partial T}X\dot{T}
$$

with $\gamma$ given by the equation .

The model VISCOCHAB proposed in fact comprises 2 kinematic-hardening variables ${X}_{1}$ and ${X}_{2}$ whose evolution laws are given by the equation .

*Summary:*

The evolution law of the hardening is of the form:

$$
\left\lbrace\begin{array}{l}X = X_{1} + X_{2} \\ \dot{X}_{i} = \frac{2}{3} C_{i} \dot{\varepsilon}^{p} - \gamma_{i} \left[ \delta_{i} X_{i} + (1 - \delta_{i}) (X_{i} : n) n \right] \dot{p} \\ - \gamma_{Xi} \left[ (X_{i})_{\text{eq}} \right]^{m_{i} - 1} X_{i} + \frac{1}{C_{i}} \frac{\partial C_{i}}{\partial T} X_{i} \dot{T}, \quad i = 1, 2 \end{array} \right.
$$

with

$$
{\gamma}_{i}={\gamma}_{i}^{0}\left[{a}_{\infty}+(1-{a}_{\infty}){e}^{-\text{bp}}\right]i=1,2
$$

### 2.2 Implicit integration

In order to carry out numerically the integration of the constitutive law, one performs a discretisation in time and one adopts an implicit Euler scheme, reputed to be appropriate for elastoplastic constitutive relations. This is the method used by default. It is also possible to carry out an explicit integration (see r5.03.12-integration-explicite ) by choosing the keyword ALGO_INTE=’RUNGE_KUTTA’.

Henceforth, the following notation will be used: ${A}^{-},{A}^{+}$ and $\Delta A$ represent respectively the values of a quantity at the beginning and at the end of the time step considered, as well as its increment during the step. One therefore has the relation: $\Delta A={A}^{+}-{A}^{-}$ . The problem is then the following: knowing the state at time ${t}^{-}$ as well as the increments of strain $\Delta \varepsilon$ (coming from the prediction phase (cf. STAT_NON_LINE [r5.03.01](doc:r5.03.01)) and of temperature $\Delta T$ , determine the state of the internal variables at time $t$ as well as the stresses $\sigma$ .

#### 2.2.1 System of equations

The implicit discretisation of the problem leads to a system of 27 equations:

Stress-strain relation

$$
\Delta \sigma -H(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}-\Delta p\frac{\partial F}{\partial {\sigma}^{+}})=0 \rightarrow \text{6 équations}
$$

Kinematic hardening

$$
\left\lbrace   \begin{array}{l}   i = 1, 2 \\   \Delta X_{i} - \frac{2}{3} C_{i} \Delta \varepsilon^{p}   + \gamma_{i}^{+} \left[ \delta_{i} X_{i}^{+} + (1 - \delta_{i}) (X_{i}^{+} \colon n^{+}) n^{+} \right] \Delta p \\   + \gamma_{Xi} \left[ (\Delta X_{i}^{+})_{\text{eq}} \right]^{m_{i} - 1} \Delta X_{i}^{+} \Delta t   - \frac{1}{C_{i}} \frac{\partial C_{i}}{\partial T} X_{i}^{+} \Delta T = 0   \end{array}   \right. \rightarrow \text{12 équations}
$$

$$

$$
\Delta R-b({Q}^{+}-{R}^{+})\Delta p-{\gamma}_{r}{\mid {Q}_{r}^{+}-{R}^{+}\mid }^{{m}_{r}}\text{sgn}({Q}_{r}^{+}-{R}^{+})\Delta t=0 \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

$$

$$

In this system, ${\gamma}_{{}_{i}}^{+}$ is given as a function of ${p}^{+}$ by the equation ; ${Q}^{+}$ and ${Q}_{r}^{+}$ are obtained as a function of ${q}^{+}$ by the equations  and .

The 27 unknowns are: $\Delta \sigma$ , $\Delta {X}_{1}$ , $\Delta {X}_{2}$ , $\Delta p$ , $\Delta R$ , $\Delta q$ and $\Delta \xi$ .

#### 2.2.2 General solution scheme

One computes the stress by making the assumption of a purely elastic increment ($\Delta p=0$  ).

$$
\begin{array}{}\Delta \sigma =H\Delta \varepsilon \text{avec}\Delta {\varepsilon}^{p}=\Delta {X}_{i}=\Delta \xi =0\\ \Delta R=\Delta p=\Delta q=0\end{array}
$$

One computes the threshold function ${F}^{+}={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}^{+}-{\alpha}_{R}{R}^{+}-k$ . If ${F}^{+}\le 0$ then the increment is purely elastic and the integration is finished. Otherwise viscoplastic corrections are carried out by solving the following system (S1):

Stress-strain relation

$$
\Delta \sigma -H(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}-\Delta p\frac{\partial F}{\partial {\sigma}^{+}})=0  \rightarrow \text{6 équations}
$$

Kinematic hardening

$$
\left\lbrace   \begin{array}{l}   i = 1, 2 \\   \Delta X_{i} - \frac{2}{3} C_{i} \Delta \varepsilon^{p}   + \gamma_{i}^{+} \left[ \delta_{i} X_{i}^{+} + (1 - \delta_{i}) (X_{i}^{+} \colon n^{+}) n^{+} \right] \Delta p \\   + \gamma_{Xi} \left[ (X_{i}^{+})_{\text{eq}} \right]^{m_{i} - 1} X_{i}^{+} \Delta t   - \frac{1}{C_{i}} \frac{\partial C_{i}}{\partial T} X_{i} \Delta T = 0   \end{array}   \right. \rightarrow \text{12 équations}
$$

Cumulative plasticity

$$
\Delta p-\Delta t{(\frac{{F}^{+}}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}{R}^{+}})}^{n}\exp\left[\alpha {(\frac{{F}^{+}}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}{R}^{+}})}^{n+1}\right]=0  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

Isotropic hardening

$$
\Delta R-b({Q}^{+}-{R}^{+})\Delta p-{\gamma}_{r}{\mid {Q}_{r}^{+}-{R}^{+}\mid }^{{m}_{r}}\text{sgn}({Q}_{r}^{+}-{R}^{+})\Delta t=0  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

Memory effect

$$
\Delta q=0  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

$$
\Delta \xi =0  \rightarrow \text{6 équations}
$$

The 20 unknowns are: $\Delta \sigma$ , $\Delta {X}_{1}$ , $\Delta {X}_{2}$ , $\Delta p$ , $\Delta R$ . One remarks that $\Delta q$ and $\Delta \xi$ are not among the unknowns because their solution is trivial.

One also remarks that, at this stage, one can check that ${F}^{+}>0$ , and since ${K}_{0}>0$ , ${\alpha}_{k}>0$ and ${R}^{+}>0$ , one has formally replaced the brackets (positive part) of equation  by simple parentheses in equation .

One then computes the dual function $f$ “envelope surface of the maximum strains”:

$$
{f}^{+}=\frac{2}{3}{({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}_{\text{eq}}^{+}-{q}^{+}
$$

If ${f}^{+}\le 0$ then the integration is finished. Otherwise, one solves the following system (S2):

Stress-strain relation

$$
\Delta \sigma -H(\Delta \varepsilon -\Delta {\varepsilon}^{\text{th}}-\Delta p\frac{\partial F}{\partial {\sigma}^{+}})=0  \rightarrow \text{6 équations}

One can formally write these systems: $\phi (\Delta Y)=0$ , with :

- $\Delta Y={(\Delta \sigma ,\Delta {X}_{1},\Delta {X}_{2},\Delta p,\Delta R)}^{t}$ for (S1),
- and $\Delta Y={(\Delta \sigma ,\Delta {X}_{1},\Delta {X}_{2},\Delta p,\Delta R,\Delta q,\Delta \xi )}^{t}$ for (S2).

by iterating in $k$ until convergence and by starting with an initial solution ensuring this convergence.

### 2.3 Explicit integration

In order to carry out the explicit integration of the constitutive law, one uses the Runge-Kutta method [r5.03.14](doc:r5.03.14). One therefore integrates directly by this method the following system of 27 differential equations:

Flow

$$
{\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}=\frac{\partial {\Omega}^{p}}{\partial \sigma }=\dot{p}\frac{\partial F}{\partial \sigma }=\frac{3}{2}\dot{p}\frac{\tilde{\sigma}-X}{{(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}}  \rightarrow \text{6 équations}
$$

Kinematic hardening

$$
{\dot{\alpha}}_{i}={\dot{\varepsilon}}^{p}-{\gamma}_{i}\left[{\delta}_{i}{\alpha}_{i}+(1-{\delta}_{i})({\alpha}_{i}:n)n\right]\dot{p}-{\gamma}_{Xi}{\left[{({X}_{i})}_{\text{eq}}\right]}^{{m}_{i}-1}{\alpha}_{i}i=1,2  \rightarrow \text{12 équations}
$$

Cumulative plasticity

$$
\dot{p}={\langle \frac{F}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}\rangle }^{n}\exp\left[\alpha {\langle \frac{F}{{K}_{0}+{\alpha}_{k}R}\rangle }^{n+1}\right]  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

Isotropic hardening

$$
\dot{R}=b(Q-R)\dot{p}+{\gamma}_{r}{\mid {Q}_{r}-R\mid }^{{m}_{r}}\text{sgn}({Q}_{r}-R)  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

Memory effect

$$
\dot{q}=\eta H(f)\langle {\text{n:n}}^{\text{*}}\rangle \dot{p}  \rightarrow \text{1 équation}
$$

$$
\dot{\xi}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}(1-\eta )H(f)\langle n:{n}^{\text{*}}\rangle \dot{p}{n}^{\text{*}}  \rightarrow \text{6 équations}
$$

with :

$\tilde{\sigma}=\sigma -{\sigma}^{H}\mathrm{Id}=2\mu (\tilde{\varepsilon}-{\varepsilon}^{p})$

${A}_{\text{eq}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}\tilde{A}:\tilde{A}}$

$F(\sigma ,R,X)={(\tilde{\sigma}-X)}_{\text{eq}}-{\alpha}_{R}R(p)-k$

$X={X}_{1}+{X}_{2}$

${X}_{1}=\frac{2}{3}{C}_{1}{\alpha}_{1}$ ${X}_{2}=\frac{2}{3}{C}_{2}{\alpha}_{2}$

$Q=Q{}_{0}{}^{}\text{}+({Q}_{m}-{Q}_{0})(1-{e}^{-2\mu q})$

${Q}_{r}=Q-{Q}_{r}^{\text{*}}\left[1-{(\frac{{Q}_{m}-Q}{{Q}_{m}})}^{2}\right]$

$f({\varepsilon}^{p},\xi ,q)=\frac{2}{3}{({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}_{\text{eq}}-q$

${n}^{\text{*}}=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2}}\frac{{\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi }{{({\varepsilon}^{p}-\xi )}_{\text{eq}}}$

### 7.2 Computation of the tangent stiffness

The global iterative Newton scheme (in order to ensure equilibrium) requires knowledge of the tangent operator of the assembled system at the end of each increment. The tangent operator is denoted ${M}_{c}=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }$ . It is possible to determine this operator from the terms of the Jacobian $J$ of the local system, already computed previously (see r5.03.12-expression-matrice-jacobienne).

Indeed, the system $\phi (\Delta Y)=0$ is satisfied at the end of the increment and, for a small variation of $\phi$ , considering this time $\varepsilon$ as a variable, the system remains at equilibrium and one therefore checks $d\phi =0$ .

By differentiation, one obtains:

For the system (S1):

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}d(\Delta \varepsilon )+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \sigma )}d(\Delta \sigma )+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta {X}_{i})}d(\Delta {X}_{i})\\ +\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta p)}d(\Delta p)+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta R)}d(\Delta R)=0\end{array}$

For the system (S2):

$\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}d(\Delta \varepsilon )+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \sigma )}d(\Delta \sigma )+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta {X}_{i})}d(\Delta {X}_{i})\\ +\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta p)}d(\Delta p)+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta R)}d(\Delta R)+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta q)}d(\Delta q)+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \xi )}d(\Delta \xi )=0\end{array}$

In what follows, the presentation is limited to the system (S1), but the approach is identical for the system (S2).

One rewrites the system by putting the terms in $\varepsilon$  on the right-hand side:

$\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \sigma )}d(\Delta \sigma )+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta {X}_{i})}d(\Delta {X}_{i})+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta p)}d(\Delta p)+\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta R)}d(\Delta R)=-\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}d(\Delta \varepsilon )$

With the notation defined previously, this system is written in matrix form:

$J.d(\Delta Y)=-\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}d(\Delta \varepsilon )$ .

Now $\frac{\partial \phi }{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}=(\begin{array}{}\frac{\partial g}{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}\\ \frac{\partial {k}_{i}}{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}\\ \frac{\partial f}{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}\\ \frac{\partial r}{\partial (\Delta \varepsilon )}\end{array})=(\begin{array}{}-H\\ 0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})$ where $H$ represents the elastic modulus.

Whence $J.d(\Delta Y)=(\begin{array}{}Hd(\Delta \varepsilon )\\ 0\\ 0\\ 0\end{array})$ .

The idea then consists in writing this system in blocks, by separating $d(\Delta \sigma )$ from the other variables $Z={(d(\Delta {X}_{i}),d(\Delta p),d(\Delta R))}^{t}$ , which gives:

$\left[\begin{array}{cc}{J}_{\sigma \sigma }& {J}_{\sigma Z}\\ {J}_{\sigma Z}& {J}_{ZZ}\end{array}\right](\begin{array}{}d(\Delta \sigma )\\ Z\end{array})=(\begin{array}{}Hd(\Delta \varepsilon )\\ 0\end{array})$

By computing the Schur complement of ${J}_{\text{ZZ}}$ , one finds that:

$\left[{J}_{\sigma \sigma }-{J}_{\sigma Z}{({J}_{\text{ZZ}})}^{-1}{J}_{Z\sigma }\right]d(\Delta \sigma )=Hd(\Delta \varepsilon )$ ,

whence the expression of the tangent operator:

${M}_{c}=\frac{\partial \sigma }{\partial \varepsilon }=\frac{d(\Delta \sigma )}{d(\Delta \varepsilon )}={\left[{J}_{\sigma \sigma }-{J}_{\sigma Z}{({J}_{\text{ZZ}})}^{-1}{J}_{Z\sigma }\right]}^{-1}H$

**Note:**

*Since* $J$ *is not symmetric, the tangent operator* ${M}_{c}$ *is not symmetric either. It is however symmetrised in Code_Aster.*
