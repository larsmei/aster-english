# v6.01.122 SSNA122 – Contact Validation Benchmark NAFEMS 2: punch (rounded edges)

**Summary:**

This is the second test case in a NAFEMS benchmark for the validation of contact and frangling. Benchmark references are obtained with Abaqus and MARC codes.

This test modeled a contact between a rounded edge punch (off) and a solid axisymmetric. This test case validates the axisymmetric contact and allows to observe the positive effect of a discharge on the singularity of the contact pressure in the vicinity of a sharp angle.

This test includes 4 modellings for testing:

- linear and quadratic elementss,
- ‘CONTINUE' contact treatment formulations without and with friction.
- contact treatment formulations ‘DISCRETE

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The structure is modeled in axisymmetric.

![Figure](/figures/v6.01.122/100000000000027700000368F890D2E59F78C148.png)

$M$ is the point of the foundation located in the upper face on the axis of revolution.

### 1.2 Material properties

**Foundation**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $70000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

**Poin**:

Poisson's ratio: $0,3$

Young's modulus: $210000{\mathit{N.mm}}^{-2}$

The friction coefficient between the block and the cylinder is $\mu =0,1$ **.**

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

Since the structure is axisymmetric and is subject to a load respecting the revolution symmetry, only one slice is represented. $\mathit{DX}=0$ is applied to the revolution axis.

The foundation is clamped at its base:

- $\mathit{DX}=0$
- $\mathit{DY}=0$

The punch shall be subjected to uniform pressure on its upper face:

- $P=100\mathit{Mpa}$

### 3.4 Remarks

![Figure](/figures/v6.01.122/100002010000052D000003832473D0BD5DA14355.png)

Figure 1: Comparison of the tangential constraint between Abaqus, MARC and *Code_Aster*

The results obtained by the continuous formulation in *Code_Aster* with as without friction are in very good agreement with those of the reference codes. The above figure shows for example that the tangential stress on the contact edge raised from the degree of freedom LAGS_F1 coincides with the solution of MARC.

It should be noted, however, that the contact pressure given by the degree of freedom LAGS_C for the point M (located on the revolution axis) is disturbed in continuous formulation. Indeed, the jacobian is zero for the points of the revolution axis. However, for linear elements, the default integration scheme (and recommended) is trapeze (so nodes).

This test illustrates the value of the specific features of continuous formulation that allow for the initial contact (to block rigid body movements) and exclude nodes from friction only (to avoid incompatibilities between boundary conditions and contact-frost conditions).

## 7 Summary of the results

This test validates the axisymmetric Modelling contact-frotting compared to references given by commercial calculation codes (Abaqus and MARC).

There was a very good agreement between the results obtained by *Code_Aster* and the reference results.

It should be noted that the continuous and discreet formulations give identical results with the following restrictions:

- continuous calculation is easier to perform because the vertical rigid body movement is automatically blocked

This test also shows the regularization of the contact pressure by a discharge near sharp angles.
