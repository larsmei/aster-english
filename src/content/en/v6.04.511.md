# v6.04.511 SSNV511 – Block cut by two intersect interfaces with X-FEM

**Summary:**

This test validates the intersection approach with X-FEM. It is a test case where three cracks are introduced. The first crack completely breaks the domain. The other two cracks are defined by the same normal level-set. They are connected on either side of the first one via the JONCTION key word of the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator. The double junction then forms an intersection. The approach is tested with and without contact.

### 2.1 Contactless case

Without contact, each area shall undergo a rigid body movement corresponding to the limit condition imposed on its edge (right or left).

The energy of the structure is therefore:

${E}^{e}=0.$

The analytical solution displacement field is:

$u={u}_{x}(x,y){e}_{x},$

${u}_{x}(x,y)=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-\frac{1}{4}\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left[-5,0\right[\times \left]0,+5\right[,\\ -\frac{1}{2}\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left[-5,0\right[\times \left[-5,0\right[,\\ +\frac{3}{4}\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left]0,+5\right]\times \left]0,+5\right],\\ +1\text{pour}(x,y)\in \left]0,+5\right]\times \left[-5,0\right[,\end{array}$

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}={\int}_{\Omega}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega .$

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}^{2}=25\left(\frac{1}{16}+\frac{1}{4}+\frac{9}{16}+1\right)=25\frac{15}{8}.$

The stress tensor analytical solution is:

$\sigma =-{p}_{x}(y){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-{p}_{y}(x){e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}-\nu ({p}_{x}(y)+{p}_{y}(x)){e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z}$

$\mathit{tr}(\sigma )=-(1+\nu )({p}_{x}(y)+{p}_{y}(x)).$

$\epsilon =\frac{1+\nu }{E}\sigma -\frac{\nu}{E}\mathit{tr}(\sigma )I,$

$\begin{array}{c}\epsilon =-\left(\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu ){p}_{x}(y)}{E}-\frac{\nu (1+\nu ){p}_{y}(x)}{E}\right){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}\\ -\left(\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu ){p}_{y}(x)}{E}-\frac{\nu (1+\nu ){p}_{x}(y)}{E}\right){e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}\end{array}$

$\sigma :\epsilon =\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu )}{E}{\left({p}_{x}(y)\right)}^{2}-2\frac{\nu (1+\nu )}{E}{p}_{x}(y){p}_{y}(x)+\frac{(1+\nu )(1-\nu )}{E}{\left({p}_{y}(x)\right)}^{2}.$

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}{\int}_{\Omega}\left[(1-\nu ){\left({p}_{x}(y)\right)}^{2}-2\nu {p}_{x}(y){p}_{y}(x)+(1-\nu ){\left({p}_{y}(x)\right)}^{2}\right]d\Omega$

Therefore, equation 2.2-1 is used:

$\begin{array}{c}{E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}[{\int}_{{\Omega}_{1}}\left[4(1-\nu )-4\nu +(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{2}}\left[(1-\nu )-2\nu +(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega \\ +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{3}}\left[4(1-\nu )-8\nu +4(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{4}}\left[(1-\nu )-4\nu +4(1-\nu )\right]d\Omega ].\end{array}$

Either:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}\left[\left(5-9\nu \right)\left|{\Omega}_{1}\right|+\left(2-4\nu \right)\left|{\Omega}_{2}\right|+\left(8-16\nu \right)\left|{\Omega}_{3}\right|+\left(5-9\nu \right)\left|{\Omega}_{4}\right|\right].$

So we have:

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{1+\nu }{E}25\left[(5-9\nu )+(2-4\nu )+(8-16\nu )+(5-9\nu ).\right]$

And finally:

${E}^{e}=\frac{25(1+\nu )(10-19\nu )}{E}=1,3975\text{MJ}\times {\text{m}}^{-1}.$

#### 2.2.3 Case 3D

The structure occupies the ${\Omega}_{3D}=\Omega \times [0,1]$ domain. The boundary conditions of the 3D case are the same as the 2D case in the $(X,Y),$ plane a roll condition is imposed in $Z=0$ and the $Z=1$ edge is free of stress. The analytical solution stress tensor is therefore identical to the case of flat stresses ( *cf.* eq 2.2-4):

$\sigma =-{p}_{x}(y){e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-{p}_{y}(x){e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}$

The density of elastic energy is therefore identical to that of the 3D case. The solid is of unit thickness in the direction $Z$ . The expression of the energy of the structure is therefore identical to the case of the flat stresses, but the units are modified. We then have ( *cf.* eq 2.2-5):

${E}^{e}=\frac{1}{E}25(10-9\nu )=1,825\text{MJ}.$

The $u={u}_{x}{e}_{x}+{u}_{y}{e}_{y}+{u}_{z}{e}_{z}$ analytical displacement field is obtained by integrating deformations:

$\begin{array}{c}{u}_{x}={\int}_{-5}^{x}{\epsilon}_{xx}\mathit{dx},\\ {u}_{y}={\int}_{-5}^{y}{\epsilon}_{yy}\mathit{dy},\\ {u}_{z}={\int}_{0}^{z}{\epsilon}_{zz}\mathit{dz},\end{array}$

Because the applied limit conditions are ${u}_{x}(x=-5)=0$ , ${u}_{y}(y=-5)=0$ and ${u}_{z}(z=0)=0$ .

It should be noted that the tensor of deformations is discontinuous. Indeed ( *cf.* eq. 2.2-6):

$\epsilon =\lbrace \begin{array}{c}-\frac{2-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-2\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+3\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{1-\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{1-\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+2\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{2-2\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{2-2\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+4\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ -\frac{1-2\nu }{E}{e}_{x}\otimes {e}_{x}-\frac{2-\nu }{E}{e}_{y}\otimes {e}_{y}+3\frac{\nu}{E}{e}_{z}\otimes {e}_{z},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$

The deformation field is observed to be discontinuous through the $x=0$ and $y=0$ equation lines. It is therefore necessary to distinguish cases according to the sign of the coordinate on which one integrates to explain the value of integrals. The expressions of the components ${u}_{x}$ and ${u}_{y}$ are identical to the case of the plane stresses ( *cf.* eq 2.2-7 and 2.2-8) and one has for ${u}_{z}$:

${u}_{z}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}{\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{3\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{2\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{4\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ {\int}_{0}^{1}\frac{3\nu }{E}\mathit{dz},\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$

Either:

${u}_{z}=\lbrace \begin{array}{c}\frac{3\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ \frac{2\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left[-5,0\right[\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\\ \frac{4\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left]0,+5\right]\times [0,1]\\ \frac{3\nu }{E}z,\text{dans}\left]0,+5\right]\times \left[-5,0\right[\times [0,1]\end{array}.$ éq 2.2-9

The calculation of the square integral of the displacement standard must therefore again use a domain partition according to the $x=0$ and $y=0$ equation interfaces. Therefore:

$\begin{array}{c}{\int}_{{\Omega}_{3D}}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega ={\int}_{{\Omega}_{1}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{2}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega \\ +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{3}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega +{\int}_{{\Omega}_{4}\times [0,1]}\left({u}_{x}^{2}+{u}_{y}^{2}+{u}_{z}^{2}\right)d\Omega .\end{array}$

We finally:

${\int}_{{\Omega}_{3D}}{\Vert u\Vert }^{2}d\Omega =\frac{50(719{\nu}^{2}-1575\nu +1000)}{3{E}^{2}}.$

D'où:

${\Vert u\Vert }_{{L}^{2}}=\frac{5}{E}\sqrt{\frac{2(719{\nu}^{2}-1575\nu +1000)}{3}}\approx 0,99348712456{\text{m}}^{\frac{5}{2}}.$

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

This is a X-FEM model, in flat deformations. Interfaces are defined by level functions (normal level sets rated $\text{LN}$).

The level function equations for horizontal and vertical interfaces are as follows:

$\text{LN}1=Y$                                         éq 3.1-1

$\text{LN}2=X$                                         éq 3.1-2

The horizontal interface is defined in a classic way using the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator with the normal level set $\text{LN1}$ .

To define the vertical interface, the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator is first called DEFI_FISS_XFEM with the normal $\text{LN2}$ level set, defining an point "top" of the horizontal crack for the JONCTION keyword (the point is not necessarily on the level set). This step allows to define the top part of the vertical interface (see Figure 3.1-a in the centre). A second time the DEFI_FISS_XFEM operator is called the same way, but defining an point "below" of the crack (see Figure 3.1-a to the right).

So we called DEFI_FISS_XFEM (creation of 3 crack objects) in total 3 times to define the two interfaces that intersect. From a theoretical point, each crack object adds an enrichment of the Heaviside type. This makes a total of three degrees of freedom Heaviside in addition to the classic degrees of freedom. So we have 4 degree of freedom at the intersection level, allowing to move independently the 4 zones generated by the 2 interfaces.

## 11 Summary of the results

The representation of junctions with $\text{X-FEM}$ allows to model the opening kinematic of the intersection of two interfaces. It is also possible to do the same with crack bottoms on either side of the junction, but care must be taken to keep the bottom away from the intersection (about 2 mesh cells for topological enrichment as we do not yet know how to manage the presence of additional Heaviside for elements Crack-tip. A element Crack-tip can not currently "see" more than one crack at a time).

The approach was validated in 2D for modellings C_PLAN and D_PLAN and elements type QUAD4 and TRIA3. The 3D approach for elements HEXA8 and TETRA4 was also validated with and without contact.
