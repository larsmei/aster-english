# v6.03.160 SSNP160 – Hydrogen diffusion in elastoplastic steel

**Summary:**

This non-linear quasi-static mechanical test simulates hydrogen diffusion in a homogeneous steel volume as a function of plastic deformations.

Modelling A is performed with elements planes (mechanical Modelling C_PLAN, thermal Modelling PLAN_DIAG).

Modelling A is performed with 3D elements (3D mechanical modelling, 3D_DIAG thermal modelling).

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

In document [1], a model for the diffusion of hydrogen atoms in steels is proposed. It considers two types of hydrogen atoms concentration:

- ${C}_{L}$ is the concentration in the crystal network (Lattice)
- ${C}_{T}$ is the concentration in traps or gaps (Traps)

Without taking the whole approach taken by the authors of [1], the proposed wording for the ${C}_{L}$ diffusion equation is:

$\frac{{C}_{L}+{C}_{T}(1-{\theta}_{T})}{{C}_{L}}\frac{\partial {C}_{L}}{\partial t}-\nabla \cdot ({D}_{L}\nabla {C}_{L})+\nabla \cdot (\frac{{D}_{L}{C}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}\nabla {\sigma}_{H})+{\theta}_{T}\frac{d{N}_{T}}{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}}\frac{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}}{\mathit{dt}}=0$         eq1

It is therefore found that the diffusion equation takes into account the local gradient of stress trace (hydrostatic stress ${\sigma}_{H}=1/3\mathit{tr}(\sigma )$) and the equivalent plastic deformation of Von Mises.

The relationships defining the different quantities are:

${\theta}_{L}=\frac{{C}_{L}}{{N}_{L}}$ is the occupancy rate of the crystalline network sites, with ${N}_{T}$ the number of sites per unit volume. ${N}_{L}$ is an estimated constant at ${N}_{L}=5,1{10}^{29}{m}^{\text{-3}}$ for $\alpha$ iron in [1].

${\theta}_{T}=\frac{{C}_{T}}{{N}_{T}}$ is the rate of occupancy of trap sites, with ${N}_{T}$ the density of traps, i.e. the number of sites corresponding to traps per unit of volume.

Unlike ${N}_{L}$, which is a constant, ${N}_{T}$ is a function of the plastic deformation as follows: ${\log}_{10}({N}_{T})={a}_{1}-{a}_{2}\exp(-{a}_{3}{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{p})$ , with: ${a}_{1}=23.26,{a}_{2}=2,33,{a}_{3}=-5,5$ [1].

${D}_{L}=1,27{10}^{\text{-8}}{m}^{2}/s$ ${V}_{H}=2{10}^{\text{-6}}{m}^{3}$ for $\alpha$ iron at room temperature, $R=8,3144J/\mathrm{mol}/K$ is the perfect gas constant, and $T$ is the $°K$ temperature.

${C}_{T}$ still has to be defined in relation to ${C}_{L}$: according to [1] at the balance, which is the case for CSC:

${C}_{T}=\frac{{N}_{T}}{1+\frac{1}{{K}_{T}{\theta}_{L}}}$ , with ${K}_{T}=\exp(-\Delta {E}_{T}/RT)=4,97{10}^{10}$ at room temperature, next [1], using $\Delta {E}_{T}=-60\mathrm{KJ}/\mathrm{mol}$ .

In a similar way to nonlinear thermal, the variational formulation is then written:

$\Omega$ an open ${R}^{3}$, border $\Gamma ={\Gamma}_{1}\cup {\Gamma}_{2}$.

The equation [eq. 1] to ${C}_{L}$ on $\Omega \times ]0,t[$ shall be solved with boundary conditions:

$\lbrace \begin{array}{ccc}{C}_{L}={C}_{L}^{d}& \text{sur}& {\Gamma}_{1}\\ J.n=\phi & \text{sur}& {\Gamma}_{2}\end{array}$                                     éq 3

with $J=-{D}_{L}\nabla {C}_{L}+\frac{{D}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}{C}_{L}\nabla {\sigma}_{H}$

and with initial ${C}_{L}(t=0)$ (and ${C}_{T}(t=0)$) conditions.

$v$ is a sufficiently regular function that cancels on ${\Gamma}_{1}$, the variable formulation of the problem is written:

$\begin{array}{c}\underset{\Omega}{\int}{D}^{\text{*}}({C}_{L})\frac{{\mathit{dC}}_{L}}{\text{dt}}vd\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{D}_{L}\nabla {C}_{L}\cdot \nabla vd\Omega -\underset{\Omega}{\int}\nabla v\cdot (\frac{{D}_{L}{C}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}\nabla {\sigma}_{H})d\Omega =\\ \underset{\Omega}{\int}{r}_{\text{vol}}vd\Omega +\underset{{\Gamma}_{2}}{\int}\phi vd{\Gamma}_{2}\end{array}$                 éq 4

with:

${D}^{\text{*}}({C}_{L})=\frac{{C}_{L}+{C}_{T}(1-{\theta}_{T})}{{C}_{L}}$ $\phi =({D}_{L}\nabla {C}_{L}-\frac{{D}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}{C}_{L}\nabla {\sigma}_{H}).n$ et ${r}_{\mathrm{vol}}=-{\theta}_{T}\frac{d{N}_{T}}{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{p}}\frac{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathrm{eq}}^{p}}{\mathrm{dt}}=0$

The numerical resolution by elements finished is therefore similar to that of nonlinear thermal, and is based on a $\theta$ -method, modulo two peculiarities:

- the source term ${r}_{\mathrm{vol}}$ is non-linear, as in [R7.01.12](doc:r7.01.12) drying modelling, and will be explicitly integrated;
- the term $\underset{\Omega}{\int}\nabla v\cdot (\frac{{D}_{L}{C}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}\nabla {\sigma}_{H})d\Omega$ is non-symmetric, and will also be reported to the second member, as in [1] and explicitly incorporated.

This explicit discretization of these two terms is not binding: the resolution of the equation [eq. 4] is performed at each time step, chained with a mechanical resolution.

On the other hand, the term $\underset{\Omega}{\int}\nabla v\cdot (\frac{{D}_{L}{C}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}\nabla {\sigma}_{H})d\Omega$ requires the application of a temperature gradient $\nabla {T}_{\mathrm{ini}}$ assumed to be uniform in the element. The second elemental member calculated is: ${\int}_{\Omega}\nabla {T}_{\mathrm{ini}}K\nabla vd\Omega$ where $K$ is the tensor of thermal conductivities.

Knowing the initial conditions ${C}_{L}^{0}={C}_{L}(t=0)$ and ${C}_{T}^{0}={C}_{T}(t=0)$, we calculate ${C}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{0}={C}_{L}^{0}+{C}_{T}^{0}$.

and the unknown (concentration) is a dimensioned; the variational formulation is then:

$\begin{array}{c}\underset{\Omega}{\int}{D}^{\text{*}}\frac{{\mathit{dc}}_{L}}{\text{dt}}vd\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{D}_{L}\nabla {c}_{L}\cdot \nabla vd\Omega =\underset{\Omega}{\int}\nabla v\cdot (\frac{{D}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}{c}_{L}\nabla {\sigma}_{H})d\Omega +\underset{\Omega}{\int}{\stackrel{ˉ}{r}}_{\text{vol}}vd\Omega +\underset{{\Gamma}_{2}}{\int}\stackrel{ˉ}{\phi }vd{\Gamma}_{2}\end{array}$

with: ${c}_{L}=\frac{{C}_{L}}{{C}_{\mathrm{tot}}^{0}}$ ${\stackrel{ˉ}{r}}_{\mathit{vol}}=\frac{-{\theta}_{T}}{{C}_{\mathit{tot}}^{0}}\frac{d{N}_{T}}{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}}\frac{d{\varepsilon}_{\mathit{eq}}^{p}}{\mathit{dt}}$ and $\stackrel{ˉ}{\phi }=(-{D}_{L}\nabla {c}_{L}+\frac{{D}_{L}{V}_{H}}{RT}{c}_{L}\nabla {\sigma}_{H}).n$

The ${D}^{\text{*}}$ thermal capacity coefficient is a field represented by a control variable (NEUT1) .

In an isolated volume element ($\nabla {C}_{L}=0$ on the border) loaded so that the stress and deformation state is uniform, the total concentration ${C}_{\mathrm{tot}}={C}_{l}+{C}_{t}$ is constant.

The analytical solution described in [1] is obtained directly as the second degree equation: ${C}_{T}^{2}-B{C}_{T}+{N}_{T}{C}_{\mathrm{tot}}=0$ , with $B={N}_{L}/{K}_{T}+{C}_{\mathrm{tot}}+{N}_{T}$ : one of the two solutions would lead to negative values of ${C}_{L}$ , so the solution is .
