# v6.04.401 SSNV401 – constitutive law BETON_RAG and BETON_BURGER verification: creep phenomenon

**Summary:**

These tests validate the constitutive law BETON_RAG on a compression-supplied cube with either a stress or a deformation. The model's response is compared to solutions obtained by analytically resolving the equations that govern the [R7.01.26](doc:r7.01.26) behavior and identical modellings made with the BETON_BÜRGER [r7.01.35](doc:r7.01.35) behavior model.

Four modellings are proposed:

- Modelization A: compression creep test with a stress imposed on a cube free to deform in the direction perpendicular to the load.
- Modelization B: compression creep test with deformation imposed on a cube free to deform in the direction perpendicular to the load.
- Modelization C: compression creep test with a stress imposed on a confined cube (blocked transverse deformations).
- Modelization D: compression creep test with deformation imposed on a confined cube (blocked transverse deformations).
- Modellings E, F, G and H: the same as A, B, C and D respectively, but using a nonlinear irreversible viscosity model.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

Geometry is a cube of $1\,m$ on the side.

![Figure](/figures/v6.04.401/10000000000002A9000001EAF7840BF799183D95.png)

*Geometry adopted*

### 1.2 Mesh

The mesh is made with 1 HEXA8.

### 1.3 Materials

The material is of type BETON_RAG. The necessary characteristics are those related to the damage and creep phenomena.

**The material characteristics of** "BETON_RAG" **of this test case should not be used for studies. These parameters are not physical and are used only for mathematical verification.**

The material parameters used in this test case are given in v6.04.401-paramsBRAG.

**Note:**

In modellings C, D, F and H, the FLUA_SPH_KR parameter was double-folded to prevent the stresses or displacements from changing signs, making it easier to compare the numerical model to analytical solutions.

A second calculation is made with the BETON_BURGER [r7.01.35](doc:r7.01.35) model for programming quality verification purposes. The material characteristics adopted are:

### 1.4 Boundary conditions, contact and loading

#### 1.4.1 For the free side deformation cube

The following movements are imposed on the faces:

- BAS    : DZ = 0
- GAUCHE    : DY = 0
- ARRIERE    : DX = 0
- HAUT: forced displacement or constraint.

#### 1.4.2 For the contained cube

The following loads are imposed on the faces:

- BAS    : DZ = 0
- GAUCHE, DROIT    : DY = 0
- ARRIERE, AVANT    : DX = 0
- HAUT: forced displacement or constraint.

### 1.5 Modelling

The modelling is 3D.

### 1.6 Calculation time

The calculation is divided into two phases: a first, between times 0 and 1 day, corresponding to the application of the load. The second phase corresponds to the duration between 1 and 35 days, where the load is kept constant.
The load is discretized by v6.04.401-TEMPS commands.

## 11 Annexes

This annex lists the codes that generated the analytical solutions of modellings [A], [B], [C], and [D].

The Python libraries that are used to obtain analytical solutions and calculate them are: `numpy`, `sympy` and `inspect`.

The constant parameters used are defined in the following section of the code:

**Note:**

For C and D test cases, the `k_r_sph` rigidity module must be multiplied by two.

The Analytical Solutions are based on Laplace transforms of the `sympy` module. To do this, you have to define the variables of Laplace, and the symbols used:

The test case ssnv401a [Mod_lization_A] is simple enough to obtain the analytical solution can be obtained by hand. Under constant $\sigma_0$ uniaxial stress, deformation is the superposition of elastic solutions and transient solutions due to creep. Elastic solutions are given to the equation.

$$
{\varepsilon}^{él}_{sph} = \frac{E}{1-2\nu}\frac{{\sigma}_0}{3}

{\varepsilon}^{él}_{dev,\,x} = -\frac{E}{1+\nu}\frac{{\sigma}_0}{3}

{\varepsilon}^{él}_{dev,\,y} = -\frac{E}{1+\nu}\frac{{\sigma}_0}{3}

{\varepsilon}^{él}_{dev,\,z} = \frac{E}{1+\nu}\frac{2{\sigma}_0}{3}
$$

Transitional solutions are given to the equation

$$
{\varepsilon}_{sph}^{f}(t)=\sigma_0\left(\frac{t}{\eta_{sph,\,irr}} - \frac{1}{k_{sph,\,r}}\left[exp\left(-\frac{k_{sph,\,r}t}{\eta_{sph,\,r}}\right)-1\right]\right)

{\varepsilon}_{dev,\,x}^{f}(t)=\frac{-\sigma_0}{3}\left(\frac{t}{\eta_{dev,\,irr}} - \frac{1}{k_{dev,\,r}}\left[exp\left(-\frac{k_{dev,\,r}t}{\eta_{dev,\,r}}\right)-1\right]\right)

{\varepsilon}_{dev,\,y}^{f}(t)=\frac{-\sigma_0}{3}\left(\frac{t}{\eta_{dev,\,irr}} - \frac{1}{k_{dev,\,r}}\left[exp\left(-\frac{k_{dev,\,r}t}{\eta_{dev,\,r}}\right)-1\right]\right)

{\varepsilon}_{dev,\,z}^{f}(t)=\frac{2\sigma_0}{3}\left(\frac{t}{\eta_{dev,\,irr}} - \frac{1}{k_{dev,\,r}}\left[exp\left(-\frac{k_{dev,\,r}t}{\eta_{dev,\,r}}\right)-1\right]\right)
$$

To obtain the analytical solution on an $i\in\lbrace{x,y,z}\rbrace$ component, you need only sum the $i$ spherical and deviatoric components of the elastic participations of the equation and transients of the equation.

For modellings [B], [C], and [D] analytical solutions, boundary conditions, the partition of the partly spherical and deviatoric creep and the application of a constant deformation load complicate the search for a analytical solution by hand. To remove this problem, the `sympy` package is used. This involves writing transfer functions in the Laplace domain, and reverse processing of Laplace.

For B Modelling, the v6.04.401-ssnv401b_soluanaly allows you to calculate and display a function that allows you to calculate the analytical solution.

Similarly, the v6.04.401-ssnv401c_soluanaly and v6.04.401-ssnv401d_soluanaly allow the analytical solution code of the Modelling [C] and [D].
