# v7.31.146 WTNV146- Validation of a cohesive law model for hydromechanical case coupled with XFEM

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to ensure the proper functioning of the "Mortier"-type cohesive zone model associated with hydromechanical elements coupled with XFEM.

In this test, the entire operating regimes of the cohesive zone model for the elements HM-XFEM are tested. The cohesive law used is CZM_LIN_MIX (this is the only one available for the elements HM-XFEM).This test is therefore purely mechanical, all degrees of freedom associated with the fluid phase are blocked at zero. Modelling A is two-dimensional while Modelling B is three-dimensional.

This column is $\mathit{LZ}=5m$ height, $\mathit{LX}=1m$ length and $\mathit{LY}=1m$ width. This column has an interface-type discontinuity in $Z=\frac{\mathit{LZ}}{2}$. The column is thus completely crossed by discontinuity.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Method of calculation

This is an analytical solution. The normal ${t}_{c,n}$ cohesive stress is shown in the Figure, depending on the $[{u}_{n}]$ normal displacement jump for the 'CZM_LIN_MIX' law. For each of the 8 moments of calculation, the position in which you are in the same Figure is represented.The critical displacement jump that corresponds to the loss of cohesion forces is ${\delta}_{c}=\frac{2{G}_{c}}{{\sigma}_{c}}=0,0016363636m$

![Figure](/figures/v7.31.146/10000000000002A400000221AC5EA93FF3A5C1C7.jpg)

Figure 2.1-a: Contrainte cohésive normale en fonction du saut de déplacement normal pour la loi 'CZM_LIN_MIX'

By neglecting the gravity, the overall equilibrium equation is written (in total constraints):

$\text{Div}(\sigma )=0$

In the case of coupled modelling, the total stress tensor is written:

$\sigma =\sigma '-{p}_{1}1$

$\sigma '$ is the stress tensor in the skeleton and ${p}_{1}$ and the pore pressure in the solid. The module of Poisson $\nu$ being zero, and being in the elastic case, we have $\sigma '=Eϵ$.

$\forall x,{p}_{1}(x)=0$ gold so finally $\text{Div}(ϵ)=0$

Given the required displacements and the nullity of the Poisson's ratio $\nu$ , the problem is unidirectional in the $z$ direction . In the solid matrix, the following displacement field $z$ checks:

$\frac{d\mathrm{²}{u}_{z}}{\mathit{dz}\mathrm{²}}=0$

*Instant 1*

The column is compression, the lips of the cohesive interface are in contact. The $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump is therefore zero. Therefore ${{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=g(1)/\mathit{LZ}$ and ${\sigma}_{zz}=E{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=E\ast g(1)/\mathit{LZ}$ . The normal ${t}_{c,n}$ cohesive stress is equal to the ${\sigma}_{zz}$ stress .

Instant 2

The column is in traction. It is assumed that the cohesive interface is in a grip situation. This hypothesis is checked if ${t}_{c,n}\le {\sigma}_{c}$ . If the cohesive interface is adherent, then the lips of the cohesive interface are in contact. The $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump is therefore zero. Therefore ${{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=g(2)/\mathit{LZ}$ and ${\sigma}_{zz}=E{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=E\ast g(2)/\mathit{LZ}$ . The normal cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is then equal to the ${\sigma}_{zz}=0,116\mathit{MPa}\le {\sigma}_{c}$ stress . The initial hypothesis is therefore validated.

Instant 3

The column is in traction. It is assumed that the cohesive interface is in a grip situation. This hypothesis is checked if ${t}_{c,n}\le {\sigma}_{c}$ . If the cohesive interface is adherent, then the lips of the cohesive interface are in contact. The $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump is therefore zero. Therefore ${{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=g(3)/\mathit{LZ}$ and ${\sigma}_{zz}=E{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=E\ast g(3)/\mathit{LZ}$ . The normal cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is then equal to the ${\sigma}_{zz}=1,16\mathit{MPa}>{\sigma}_{c}$ stress . The assumption made initially is therefore false.

It is therefore assumed that the cohesive interface is in damage regime. This hypothesis is verified if $0<[{u}_{n}]\le {\delta}_{c}$ . The cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is then related to the normal $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump by the ${t}_{c,n}={\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{[{u}_{z}]}{{\delta}_{c}})$ relationship . Moreover, the total column elongation is $g(3)=[{u}_{z}]+\mathit{LZ}\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$ . Finally, the normal cohesive stress is equal to the vertical stress ${t}_{c,n}=E\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$ . Finally:

${t}_{c,n}=\frac{E\ast {\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{g(3)}{{\delta}_{c}})}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}\ast {\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

$[{u}_{z}]=\frac{-\mathit{LZ}\ast {\sigma}_{c}+E\ast g(3)}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}\ast {\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

Digitally, we find, $[{u}_{z}]=0,0001230068m<{\delta}_{c}$. The hypothesis originally made is validated.

Instant 4

The column is still in traction, but a lower traction than at the previous moment. So we are in a situation of elastic return in the cohesive zone (unloading during the damage process). Normal cohesive traction is then given by ${t}_{c,n}=\frac{{t}_{c,n}(3)\ast [{u}_{z}]}{[{u}_{z}(3)]}$ and the total elongation of the column is $g(4)=[{u}_{z}]+\mathit{LZ}\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally, the normal cohesive stress is equal to the vertical stress ${t}_{c,n}=E\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally:

$[{u}_{z}]=\frac{E\ast g(4)\ast [{u}_{z}(3)]}{\mathit{LZ}\ast {t}_{c,n}(3)+E\ast [{u}_{z}(3)]}$

${t}_{c,n}=\frac{{t}_{c,n}(3)\ast E\ast g(4)}{\mathit{LZ}\ast {t}_{c,n}(3)+E\ast [{u}_{z}(3)]}$

Instant 5

The column is again in traction, at a level not yet reached. It is therefore assumed that the cohesive interface is in damage regime. This hypothesis is verified if $0<[{u}_{n}]\le {\delta}_{c}$. The cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is then related to the normal $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump by the ${t}_{c,n}={\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{[{u}_{z}]}{{\delta}_{c}})$ relationship. Furthermore, the total elongation of the column is $g(5)=[{u}_{z}]+\mathit{LZ}\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally, the normal cohesive stress is equal to the vertical stress ${t}_{c,n}=E\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally:

${t}_{c,n}=\frac{E\ast {\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{g(5)}{{\delta}_{c}})}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

$[{u}_{z}]=\frac{-\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}+E\ast g(5)}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

Digitally, we find, $[{u}_{z}]=0,000059863325m<{\delta}_{c}$. The hypothesis originally made is validated.

Instant 6

The column is still in traction, at a level not yet reached. It is therefore assumed that the cohesive interface is in damage regime. This hypothesis is verified if $0<[{u}_{n}]\le {\delta}_{c}$. The cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is then related to the normal $[{u}_{z}]$ displacement jump by the ${t}_{c,n}={\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{[{u}_{z}]}{{\delta}_{c}})$ relationship. Furthermore, the total elongation of the column is $g(6)=[{u}_{z}]+\mathit{LZ}\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally, the normal cohesive stress is equal to the vertical stress ${t}_{c,n}=E\ast {{\varepsilon}}_{zz}$. Finally:

${t}_{c,n}=\frac{E\ast {\sigma}_{c}\ast (1-\frac{g(6)}{{\delta}_{c}})}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

$[{u}_{z}]=\frac{-\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}+E\ast g(6)}{E-\frac{\mathit{LZ}{\sigma}_{c}}{{\delta}_{c}}}$

Digitally, we find, $[{u}_{z}]=0,001787699m>{\delta}_{c}$. The hypothesis initially made is wrong. The cohesive interface is no longer in the damage regime, it is broken. Therefore, the cohesive forces are zero ${t}_{c,n}=0$ and the displacement jump is $[{u}_{z}]=g(6)$.

Instant 7

During this time step, the vertical elongation of the column remains fixed. However, a lateral shift is applied to the upper side of the column to verify that the cohesive forces remain zero in the case of shearing in the broken cohesive interface. Thus:

$[{u}_{z}]=g(7)$

$[{u}_{x}]=f(7)$

${t}_{c,n}=0$

${t}_{c,s}=0$

Instant 8

Finally, the column is compressed to ensure that the contact on the lips of the cohesive interface applies even when the cohesive area has been broken. The load is the same as at the moment 1. We have ${{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=g(8)/\mathit{LZ}$ and ${\sigma}_{zz}=E{{\varepsilon}}_{zz}=E\ast g(8)/\mathit{LZ}$ . The normal cohesive stress ${t}_{c,n}$ is equal to the ${\sigma}_{zz}$ constraint .
