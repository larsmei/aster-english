# u4.82.30 Operator POST_K_TRANS

## 1 Purpose

Compute the stress intensity factors of a transient dynamic problem solved by decomposition on a modal basis.

The computation of the stress intensity factors, as functions of time, is carried out from the modal stress intensity factors (obtained from CALC_G [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03)) and from the modal contribution factors obtained from the transient dynamic analysis (operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03)).

This operator may be called in 2D as well as in 3D. The crack may be meshed or unmeshed (X-FEM crack).

This operator is valid only if a linear combination of the modal stress intensity factors can be formed. In particular, the crack must always remain open: contact between its lips is indeed not taken into account.

Produces a data structure of type table_sd_aster.

```text

    # Si: (equal_to("CRITERE", 'ABSOLU'))

◆ : obligatoire
◇ : optionnel
⟐ : présent par défaut
& : ensemble

```

## 3 Operands

### 3.1 Operand RESU_TRANS

Name of a result concept of type tran_gene, the result of the transient dynamic analysis.

### 3.2 Keyword K_MODAL

Factor keyword making it possible to recover the modal stress intensity factors directly from the table TABL_K_MODA, the result of the operator CALC_G.

#### 3.2.1 Operands FOND_FISS / FISSURE

It is mandatory to supply either FOND_FISS (produced by the command DEFI_FOND_FISS [U4.82.01](doc:u4.82.01)) or FISSURE (produced by the command DEFI_FISS_XFEM [U4.82.08](doc:u4.82.08)).

#### 3.2.2 Operand TABL_K_MODA

Result table containing the modal stress intensity factors, produced by the operator CALC_G (option CALC_K_G [U4.82.03](doc:u4.82.03)).

### 3.3 Operands TOUT_ORDRE / NUME_ORDRE / LIST_ORDRE / INST / LIST_INST / PRECISION / CRITERE

These operands are used to select the time instants or order numbers of the post-processing of the operand RESU_TRANS. See [U4.71.00](doc:u4.71.00).

## 4 Principle of the analysis

The displacement $u(x,t)$, the solution of a linear transient dynamic problem, may be approximated by its decomposition on a truncated basis of eigenmodes ${\Phi}_{i}(x)$:

$u(x,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\alpha}_{i}(t){\Phi}_{i}(x)$

This is what is done, for example, when a transient dynamic problem is treated with the operator DYNA_VIBRA [U4.53.03](doc:u4.53.03). Likewise, the modal stress intensity factors may be approximated — with the same degree of accuracy on the result — by the following relation:

${K}_{I}(s,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{M}{\alpha}_{i}(t){K}_{I}^{i}(s)$

where the ${\alpha}_{i}(t)$ are the modal contributions, and ${K}_{I}^{i}(s)$ the modal stress intensity factors (a function of the curvilinear abscissa $s$ in 3D, constant in 2D). The modal stress intensity factors are computed from the eigenmodes of the structure, by the option CALC_K_G of the operator CALC_G.

Since contact is not taken into account, this formula is valid only if the crack remains open at every time instant. This is generally the case for the rotating-machinery applications (blades) that are envisaged, for which the centrifugal loading is predominant.

Thus, the operations carried out by the operator POST_K_TRANS are the following:

- recovery, in RESU_TRANS, of the modal participation factors ${\alpha}_{i}$ obtained from the transient analysis,
- recovery (in TABL_K_MODA) of the modal stress intensity factors,
- recombination and printout of the dynamic stress intensity factors.

The number $M$ of modes in the recombination basis corresponds, by default, to the number of modes ${M}^{\mathrm{trans}}$ used in the transient analysis. If the number ${M}^{\mathrm{tabl}}$ of modes present in the table TABL_K_MODA supplied as input is smaller than ${M}^{\mathrm{trans}}$, an alarm message is issued and the analysis continues, taking $M$ equal to ${M}^{\mathrm{tabl}}$.

## 5 Example

The case treated here is that of a 3D structure subjected to a transient dynamic loading (cf. test case sdls114b [V2.03.114](doc:v2.03.114)). After the mass and stiffness matrices have been built, the eigenmodes of the structure can be computed:

MODE=CALC_MODES( MATR_RIGI=RIG_ASS,

MATR_MASS=MA_ASS,

CALC_FREQ=_F(NMAX_FREQ=60,),);

The displacement of the structure subjected to a dynamic loading can then be computed:

```text
RES_DYNA=DYNA_VIBRA(TYPE_CALCUL='TRAN',BASE_CALCUL='GENE',

MATR_MASS=MASS_GE,

MATR_RIGI=RIGI_GE,

MATR_AMOR=AMOR_GE,

SCHEMA_TEMPS=_F(SCHEMA='EULER',),

INCREMENT=_F(INST_INIT=0.,

INST_FIN=tfin,

VERI_PAS = 'OUI',

PAS=pas,),

EXCIT=_F(VECT_ASSE=CHA_ASS,

FONC_MULT=RAMPE,),

ARCHIVAGE=_F(PAS_ARCH = nbpas,),

IMPRESSION = _F(TOUT='OUI',),);
```

The fracture-mechanics analysis begins with the definition of the crack front:

FF2=DEFI_FISS_XFEM(MODELE=MO,

DEFI_FISS=_F(GROUP_MA_FISS='LEV_SUP',

GROUP_MA_FOND='FN_FS', ),

GROUP_MA_ENRI='VVOLTOT',);

The computation of the functions ${K}_{I}(t)$, ${K}_{\mathit{II}}(t)$ and ${K}_{\mathit{III}}(t)$ is carried out after the prior computation of the modal stress intensity factors by CALC_G:

KT2 = POST_K_TRANS(    RESU_TRANS = RES_DYNA,

> K_MODAL = _F(TABL_K_MODA =GLMO1,
> FISSURE=FF2, ),);
