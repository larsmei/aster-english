# v3.02.314 SSLP314 – Fissure deviated at the interface between two elastic half-planes

**Summary:**

This test case deals with the problem of a crack deviated in 2 dimensions, under the hypothesis of flat stresses. The whole consists of two elastic materials, linear and isotropic, the material 1 being located in the upper half plane.

The crack is defined by two branches. The first branch is located horizontally at the interface of both materials, and is characterized by its length $c$.

The second is in the extension of the first and is inclined to $45°$ in relation to the horizontal, in the hourly direction (characteristic length $a$). This is the end of this branch, totally included in material 1, that we are studying. The deflected crack (branch 1 and 2) is continuous, in an infinite supposed medium, and the load applied to this plate, is a uniform tensile on the upper and lower edges.

For this study, the parameters explored are the Young $\mathit{E2}/\mathit{E1}$ module ratio of $0.25,1.,4.$ and $a/c$ $0.1$ and $1.$ units.

The boundary conditions isostatic blocks the three rigid plane body modes without showing any reactions to the supports.

Under the action of traction, and given the angle of the crack, the second branch opens (mode I) and slides (mode II).

This test case validates the operator's use of calculating the power recovery rate in breakage mechanics for modellings 2D: CALC_G (options G and K).
