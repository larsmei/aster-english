# v2.01.029 SDLD29 - 8 degrees of freedom spring mass transitory and non-proportional viscous damping

**Summary:**

This problem corresponds to a transient analysis by modal recombination of a discrete linear system consisting of 8 degrees of freedom. This system has non-proportional damping. A niche-type transient force is applied in 1 degree of freedom.

In this problem are tested elements DISCRET with modal masses (M_T_D_N), stiffness matrices (K_T_D_L) and damping matrices (A_T_D_L) in a Modelling.

The problem has a reference solution proposed by the VPCS commission. The differences with *Code_Aster* does not exceed 1.8%.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

The numerical integration (approximate) by the direct method using a finite difference numerical integration scheme, the time step used must be small enough to obtain a sufficiently precise solution. With one of the schemas used (improved Newmark method), the time step selected was $\mathrm{0.001s}$.

The improved Newmark method (NEWMARK N. M., "A method of computation for structural dynamics" proceeding ASCE J. Eng. Mech. Div E-3, July 1959, pp. 67-94) uses the following integration scheme:

$\begin{array}{}\left[\frac{1}{{\Delta t}^{2}}[M]+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}[C]+\frac{1}{3}[K]\right]({u}_{n+2})\\ =\frac{1}{3}([{P}_{n+2}]+[{P}_{n+1}]+[{P}_{n}])+\left[\frac{2}{{\Delta t}^{2}}[M]-\frac{1}{3}[K]\right]({u}_{n+1})+\left[\frac{1}{{\Delta t}^{2}}[M]+\frac{1}{2\Delta t}[C]-\frac{1}{3}[K]\right]({u}_{n})\end{array}$

$n$ , $n+1$ , $n+2$ , and $n+2$ are the calculations performed at the time ${t}_{n}$ , ${t}_{n+1}={t}_{n}+\Delta t$ and ${t}_{n+2}={t}_{n}+2\Delta t$ , where $\Delta t$ is the time increment retained. $[M]$ , $[C]$ and $[K]$ are the mass, damping and stiffness matrices respectively, $(u)$ is the displacement vector and $(P)$ is the associated force vector.

## 4 Summary of the results

A relatively good agreement is obtained between the calculated solution and the VPCS solution (<0.7%) except at the time 0.91 (2.4%). The differences are mainly due to the fact that the test moments are given with only 2 significant digits, which does not allow to capture the moment of the extreme sufficiently well.
