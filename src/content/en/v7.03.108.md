# v7.03.108 HPLV108 – Verification of homogenized parameters of a breakthrough plate

**Summary:**

The purpose of this test is to verify some of the parameters calculated by the CALC_MATE_HOMO command on a breakthrough plate. The reference values are derived from R&D studies of 84 and 93.

The calculated properties are:

- Model A: those of an equivalent MASSIF;
- Model B: those of an equivalent PLAQUE;
- Model C: those of a homogeneous PLAQUE;
- Modelling D: those of a homogeneous PLAQUE with transverse CT shear considering the formulations of MINDLIN & TOURATIER;
- Model E: those of an PLAQUE equivalent to a three-layer laminated plate with CT transverse shear and MINDLIN & TOURATIER formulations.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The pierced plates are recurrent elements in industry. The homogenization theory is well suited in such a case because of the periodicity of the bores present on the plate. The base cell is square and symmetrical, only the first octant is taken into account.(v7.03.108-fig-cell-base-pas-carre). For Modelling A thickness is without influence, whereas this is the case for B and C. One considers a thickness of the plate of 534mm.

## 2 Reference solution for MASSIF

$$
\left\{\begin{array}{c}{\mathrm{\chi }}^{\mathit{kh}}\in {V}_{\mathit{pér}}^{\mathit{CA}}\hfill \\ {\int}_{{Z}_{e}}\epsilon (\vec{{\mathrm{\chi }}^{\mathit{kh}}}).A.\epsilon (\vec{\nu})\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\mathit{dz}=-{\int}_{{Z}_{e}}{\epsilon}_{\mathit{kh}}^{0}.A.\epsilon (\vec{\nu})\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\mathit{dz},\forall \vec{\nu}\in {V}_{\mathit{pér}}^{\mathit{CA}}\end{array}\right.
$$

For a 3D linear elasticity problem, the 6 correctors that check the equation are determined:

From the calculation of the correctors, the homogenized coefficients of the matrix are determined according to the following formula:

$$
{\epsilon}_{ij}^{0}.{A}^{\mathit{hom}}.{\epsilon}_{\mathit{kh}}^{0}=\frac{1}{|{Z}_{e}|}{\int}_{{Z}_{e}}{\epsilon}_{ij}^{0}.A.{\epsilon}_{\mathit{kh}}^{0}\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\mathit{dz}-\frac{1}{|{Z}_{e}|}{\int}_{{Z}_{e}}\epsilon (\vec{{\mathrm{\chi }}^{ij}}).A.\epsilon (\vec{{\mathrm{\chi }}^{\mathit{kh}}})\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\mathit{dz}
$$

where ${\epsilon}^{0}$ is the given macroscopic deformation and ${Z}_{e}$ is the total volume of the base cell.

The first integral of the equation is the law of rigidity mixtures, while the second integral is the contribution of the correctors. For the anisotropic case, we will have at most 21 independent elasticity coefficients. In our case of study, this number is reduced due to the symmetries and the invariance according to *Oz* of the base cell, which allows to arrive at 12 relationships on the coefficients of homogenized elastic stiffness:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{A}_{1111}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2222}^{\text{hom}}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{A}_{1133}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2233}^{\text{hom}}\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}};\phantom{\rule{2em}{0ex}}{A}_{3131}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2323}^{\text{hom}}\\ {A}_{1112}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2212}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{1113}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2213}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2223}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{1123}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{3313}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{3323}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{3312}^{\text{hom}}=0\end{array}
$$

Thus, only 9 homogenized coefficients of elasticity have to be determined: homogenized material is elastic orthotropic. However, in , some relationships between homogenized coefficients are shown that reduce the number of independent coefficients.

$$
{A}_{3333}^{\text{hom}}=\frac{1}{|{Z}_{e}|}{\int}_{{Z}_{e}}E\phantom{\rule{0.5em}{0ex}}\mathit{dz}+2{\nu}^{2}.\left({A}_{1111}^{\text{hom}}+{A}_{1122}^{\text{hom}}\right)
$$

$$
{A}_{1133}^{\text{hom}}={A}_{2233}^{\text{hom}}=\nu .\left({A}_{1111}^{\text{hom}}+{A}_{1122}^{\text{hom}}\right)
$$

Similarly, for thermal, three homogenized coefficients are calculated, two of which are independent ${K}_{11}^{\text{hom}}\text{et}{K}_{33}^{\text{hom}}$. In , a relationship is also determined between homogenized transverse shear stiffness coefficients and homogenized thermal coefficients.

$$
{A}_{3\alpha 3\alpha }^{\text{hom}}=\frac{2\mu }{k}{K}_{11}^{\text{hom}}
$$

All these relationships will allow us to verify that the element calculation produces correct results by checking and comparing the values obtained numerically. For homogenized coefficient calculations, our results are compared with those obtained in VOLDOIRE93, which gathers the numerical results of the calculation of the elastic, thermal and thermoelastic behavior, homogenized of the pierced plates.

## 3 Reference solution for an isotropic elastic homogeneous plate

### 3.1 Correctors

The solution of the elementary problems by , in the case of a homogeneous isotropic elastic plate, leads to the correctors in membrane

$$
\chi^{11} = \chi^{22} = \left(0; 0; -\frac{\nu z}{1-\nu}\right)
\textrm{ et }
\chi^{12} = 0
$$

In bending

$$
\xi^{11} = \xi^{22} = \left(0; 0; -\frac{\nu z^2}{2\left(1-\nu\right)}\right)
\textrm{ et }
\xi^{12} = 0
$$

and transverse shearing (with Mindlin formulation)

$$
\phi^{11} = \phi^{22} = 0
\textrm{.}
$$

The problem is only dependent on the vertical position $z$.

### 3.2 Homogenized coefficients

The symmetries require a lack of coupling between the coefficients in membrane, bending and transverse shear.

The homogenized coefficients derived by membrane are linear in $z$

$$
\mathbf{A}^{\text{mm}}
=
\frac{Eh}{1-\nu^2}
\begin{pmatrix}
    1 & \nu & 0 \\
    \nu & 1 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & 1-\nu
\end{pmatrix}
\textrm{,}
$$

in bending, quadratic $z$

$$
\mathbf{A}^{\text{ff}}
=
\frac{Eh^3}{12\left(1-\nu^2\right)}
\begin{pmatrix}
    1 & \nu & 0 \\
    \nu & 1 & 0 \\
    0 & 0 & 1-\nu
\end{pmatrix}
$$

and, in transverse shear, constant

$$
\mathbf{A}^{\text{CT}}_{1313}
=
\mathbf{A}^{\text{CT}}_{2323}
=
\frac{Eh}{1+\nu}
\textrm{.}
$$

### 8.4 Evolution of transverse shear stresses through plate thickness

The contribution of the stresses due to the deformation imposed and the corrector shall be summated to obtain the total stress through the plate subjected to a unit deformation in transverse shear.

![Figure](/figures/v7.03.108/hplv108d_contrainte_totale_CT_XZ_SIXZ.png)

*Evolution of $\sigma_{xz}$ local shear stress $\gamma_{xz}$ transverse shear through plate thickness.*

![Figure](/figures/v7.03.108/hplv108d_contrainte_totale_CT_YZ_SIYZ.png)

*Evolution of $\sigma_{yz}$ local shear stress $\gamma_{yz}$ transverse shear through plate thickness.*

When the Mindlin formulation is applied, it is verified that the transverse shear stress is constant through the plate thickness.

When the Touratier formulation is applied, it is verified that the transverse shear stress changes sinusoidally through the plate thickness.

In addition, it is verified that the shear force obtained is equal for both formulations.
