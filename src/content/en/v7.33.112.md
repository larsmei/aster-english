# v7.33.112 WTNA112 – Thermal pressure of undrained saturated cylindrical test piece

**Summary:**

This is a problem of saturated and elastic THM. The temperature of a non-drained sample maintained at constant containment (constant total stress at the edge) is increased. The resulting water pressure then varies linearly with the temperature according to a coefficient of thermal pressure which is calculated analytically. The solution obtained here is therefore to be compared with an analytical solution.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A $1\mathit{cm}$ radius and $1\mathit{cm}$ height cylinder (i.e. a mesh corresponding to a square domain of $1\mathit{cm}\times \mathrm{1cm}$ , the Modelling being axisymmetric) is considered.

## 2 reference solution

It is called that the water mass input is written: ${m}_{w}=\varphi .{\rho}_{w}.(1+{\varepsilon}_{v})$ , which can be derived in the following form: ${\mathrm{dm}}_{w}=d\varphi {\rho}_{w}(1+{\varepsilon}_{v})+d{\rho}_{w}\varphi (1+{\varepsilon}_{v})+{\rho}_{w}\varphi d{\varepsilon}_{v}$ with $\varphi$ the Eulerian porosity.

If we assume small movements, we will have:

$$
{\mathrm{dm}}_{w}=d\varphi {\rho}_{w}+d{\rho}_{w}\varphi +{\rho}_{w}\varphi d{\varepsilon}_{v}
$$

The variation in porosity is written according to the relationship:

$$
d\varphi =(b-\varphi )(d{\varepsilon}_{V}-3{\alpha}_{0}\text{dT}+\frac{{\text{dp}}_{w}}{{K}_{s}})
$$

With ${\alpha}_{0}$ the linear expansion of the skeleton (similar to the porous medium). It is recalled that the Biot $b$ coefficient and the ${K}_{s}$ solid grain compressibility module are connected to the "drained" compressibility module of the ${K}_{0}$ porous medium, such as:

$b=1-\frac{{K}_{0}}{{K}_{s}}$

The variation in the density of water is also expressed as follows:

$$
\frac{d{\rho}_{w}}{{\rho}_{w}}=\frac{{\text{dp}}_{w}}{{K}_{w}}-3{\alpha}_{w}\text{dT}
$$

with the ${K}_{w}$ water compressibility module and its ${\alpha}_{w}$ expansion module.

Finally, if the constitutive law is elastic, it is recalled that the deformation is related to the actual stress such as:

$$
d{\epsilon}_{V}=\frac{d\sigma '}{{K}_{0}}+3{\alpha}_{0}\text{dT}
$$

Furthermore, the wording in total constraint indicates that:

$d\sigma '=d\sigma +b{\text{dp}}_{w}$ , considering here that the medium is at constant containment, we have therefore:

$d\sigma '=b{\text{dp}}_{w}$ , which gives us the end

$$
d{\varepsilon}_{V}=\frac{b{\text{dp}}_{w}}{{K}_{0}}+3{\alpha}_{0}\text{dT}
$$

Now, you can inject (2), (3), (4) and (5) into equation (1) and you can obtain that:

$$
\frac{{\mathit{dm}}_{w}}{{\rho}_{w}}=(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{(b-\varphi )}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\varphi}{{K}_{w}}){\text{dp}}_{w}+\varphi (3{\alpha}_{0}-3{\alpha}_{w})\text{dT}
$$

Considering that the medium is undrained, we have therefore:

$$
(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{(b-\varphi )}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\varphi}{{K}_{w}}){\text{dp}}_{w}=\varphi (3{\alpha}_{w}-3{\alpha}_{0})\text{dT}
$$

What can be written in the form:

$$
{\text{dp}}_{w}=\Lambda \text{dT}
$$

With $\Lambda$ the thermal pressure coefficient such as:

$\Lambda =\frac{\phi \left(3{\alpha}_{w}-3{\alpha}_{0}\right)}{\left(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{\left(b-\phi \right)}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\phi }{{K}_{w}}\right)}$

It is noted that this coefficient shows the thermal differential $({\alpha}_{w}-{\alpha}_{0})$

### 2.1 Thermal dilation of constant water

In case ${\alpha}_{w}=\mathit{cte}$ the digital application is immediate.

**Digital application:**

With the data defined above, we obtain:

$\Lambda =2,25{10}^{5}\mathit{Pa.}{K}^{-1}$

This gives a pressure variation of $\Delta T=40°C$ (i.e. a pressure of 13.01 MPa) for a temperature change of $\Delta T=40°C$.

### 2.2 Thermal dilation of water temperature function

In this case, we will write

$\Lambda (T)=\frac{\phi \left(3{\alpha}_{w}(T)-3{\alpha}_{0}\right)}{\left(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{\left(b-\phi \right)}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\phi }{{K}_{w}}\right)}$ et

${\text{dp}}_{w}=\Lambda (T)\text{dT}$

To be able to incorporate this formula, thermal expansion of water is considered to be a linear function of the temperature such as ${\alpha}_{w}=\mathit{a.}T+b$ . $\Lambda (T)$ is also a linear function which is incorporated between the initial temperature T0 and the final temperature T1, giving:

$\Delta p=\frac{A}{2}.({\mathit{T2}}^{2}-{\mathit{T1}}^{2})+B(\mathit{T2}-\mathit{T1})$ with

$A=\frac{3\phi a}{\left(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{\left(b-\phi \right)}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\phi }{{K}_{w}}\right)}$

et

$B=\frac{\phi \left(\mathrm{3b}-3{\alpha}_{0}\right)}{\left(\frac{{b}^{2}}{{K}_{0}}+\frac{\left(b-\phi \right)}{{K}_{s}}+\frac{\phi }{{K}_{w}}\right)}$

**Digital application:**

We're taking it here.

$a=2,63{10}^{-6}$ and $b=-7{10}^{-4}$ which corresponds to ${\alpha}_{w}(293)=6,67.{10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$ and ${\alpha}_{w}(333)=1,72.{10}^{-4}{K}^{-1}$ and are realistic values for water.

Any calculation is made, for a temperature variation between 20°C and 60°C, a pressure variation of $\Delta p=10,9\mathit{MPa}$ and therefore a final pressure of $14,9\mathit{MPa}$.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics B

- 'AXIS_THMS' flat Modelling. 'ELAS_ORTH' mechanical law. 'LIQU_SATU' coupling.

$20\times 20$ elements $\mathit{Q4}$ equal width. Anisotropic coupling terms.

This is the same model as before but passing through orthotropy modules (although the test piece is considered isotropic and the characteristics remain the same in each direction). In theory the results should give exactly the same thing.

The model differs slightly here, however, and the analytical solution indicated above is no longer entirely accurate, as it is not the isotropic relationship:

$d\varphi =(b-\varphi )(d{\varepsilon}_{V}-3{\alpha}_{0}\text{dT}+\frac{{\text{dp}}_{w}}{{K}_{s}})$

The relationship used in this case becomes tensory (cf. doc R7.01.11) and is:

$d\phi =B:d\epsilon -\phi d{\epsilon}_{V}-3{\alpha}_{\phi }\text{dT}+\frac{{\text{dp}}_{\text{gz}}-{S}_{\text{lq}}{\text{dp}}_{c}}{{M}_{\phi }}$

with

$\frac{1}{{M}_{\varphi}}=(B-\varphi \delta ):{S}_{0}^{S}:\delta$

> where ${S}_{0}^{S}$ the skeleton flexibility matrix, function of Young's modulus of the solid ${E}^{S}$ matrix and Poisson's ratio of the solid ${\nu}^{S}$ matrix.

Moreover, porosity cannot be integrated analytically here and is therefore explicitly integrated (porosity taken at the previous time).

The purpose of this modelling is to quantify the difference obtained through this modelling. The stinging matrix is also used to test the possibility of this prediction in the orthotropic case.
