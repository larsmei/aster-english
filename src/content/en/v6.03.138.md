# v6.03.138 SSNP138 - 2D inclined crack with X-FEM

**Summary**

This test is intended to validate the calculation of stress intensity factors (${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$) with $\text{X-FEM}$ in $\mathrm{2D}$ , as part of the linear elasticity. This test is also intended to test the volume loadings of FORCE_INTERNE and gravity with the $\text{X-FEM}$ method in $\mathrm{2D}$ .

This test involves a rectangular plate with a straight, inclined central crack, and subjected to traction loading on the lower and upper edges of the plate (modellings $A$ $C$ , $D$ , $E$ ) or subjected to a volume and clamped support on the upper part (modelling $B$ ), or subject to lip pressure (modelling $F$ ).

Different modellingss are considered:

1. $A$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , surface loading on edges, mesh cells QUAD4, geometric enrichment
2. $B$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , volume loading, mesh cells QUAD4. geometric enrichment
3. $C$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , surface loading on edges, mesh cells TRIA3, geometric enrichment
4. $D$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , loading on the edges, mesh cells TRIA3, topological enrichment
5. $E$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , loading on the edges, mesh cells TRIA6, topological enrichment
6. $F$ Modelling: $\text{X-FEM}\mathrm{2D}$ , lip loading, mesh cells TRIA3, topological enrichment

The validation is for stress intensity factors (${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$), compared to analytical solution for modellings $A$ , $C$ , $D$ , $E$ , $F$ and a non-regression solution for $B$ Modelling.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The $\mathrm{2D}$ structure is a rectangular plate ($\mathrm{LX}=0,2m$ , $\mathrm{LY}=0,5m$ ), with a straight central crack, inclined at an angle $\theta$ variable with respect to the horizontal axis (see v6.03.138-fissuree-plate-geometry). The crack length is constant ($a=0,04m$ ). In this test, the $\theta$ angle will successively take the values: $0°$ , $15°$ , $30°$ , $45°$ , $60°$ for $A$ and $0°$ Modelling , $45°$ for $B$ Modelling .

The line in $y=-\mathrm{LY}/2$ and the line in $y=\mathrm{LY}/2$ is called "lower line".

The nodes rated $A$ , $B$ , $C$ and $D$ on the v6.03.138-fig-geometrie-plate-fissuree are used to impose the boundary conditions, which are explained to the v6.03.138-conditions-limits-loads.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.138/10000000000002B7000003C6BFA1BC58A85151FA.png)

*Geometry of the cracked plate and loading of model $A$ .*

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus:            $E=210.{10}^{9}\mathrm{Pa}$

Poisson's ratio: Modelling A $\nu =0.3$ , Modelling B $\nu =0$

Density: Modelling B: $\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

*Modelling* $A$ *:*

The load consists of applying a load set back on the lower and upper lines $p={10}^{6}\mathrm{Pa}$ .

In order to block rigid modes, the nodes $A$ , $B$ , $C$ and $D$ movements are blocked as follows:

- ${\mathrm{DY}}^{A}={\mathrm{DY}}^{B}=0$ ;
- ${\mathrm{DX}}^{C}={\mathrm{DX}}^{D}=0$ .

*Modelling* $B$ *:*

The load consists of applying to the plate an clamped support on the upper line and a volume force of type FORCE_INTERNE or gravity. The same load (following $-Y$) is applied for two successive calculations with these key words: for the first force density imposed by $78000N/{m}^{3}$ and for the second one chooses an acceleration of the gravity equal to $10{\mathrm{m.s}}^{-2}$ and therefore the force density of $10.\rho =78000N/{m}^{3}$.

*modellings* $C$ *,* $D$ *,* $E$ *:*

Same assumptions as $A$ Modelling

*Modelling* $F$ *:*

The traction load on the upper and lower sides is replaced by a pressure on the lips of the crack (the 2 loads are equivalent)

### 1.4 reference solution

*Modelling* $A$ *:*

The analytical expressions of stress factors ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ are functions of distributed force $p$ , length of crack $a$ , width of plate $\mathrm{LX}$ and angle $\theta$ :

$\begin{array}{}{K}_{I}=p\sqrt{\pi \frac{a}{2}}F(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})\mathrm{cos²}\theta \\ {K}_{\mathrm{II}}=p\sqrt{\pi \frac{a}{2}}F(\frac{a}{\mathrm{Lx}})\cos\theta \sin\theta \end{array}$

where the $F$ function can be determined in several different ways. We choose the one obtained by Brown in 1966 bib2 p41, whose accuracy is less than $\text{0,5\%}$ if the ratio between the crack length and the plate width is less than or equal to $0,7$ (in our case, $a/\mathrm{LX}=0,2$ ):

$F(x) = 1 + 0.128x - 0.288x^2 + 1.525x^3$

With the numerical values of the test:

| Reference |  |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\alpha$ (°) | ${K}_{I}({\mathit{Pa.m}}^{-1/2})$ | ${K}_{\mathit{II}}({\mathit{Pa.m}}^{-1/2})$ |
| 0 | 2,5725024656E+5 | 0 |
| 15 | 2,4001774761E+5 | 6,4312561642E+4 |
| 30 | 1,9293768492E+5 | 1,1139262432E+5 |
| 45 | 1,2862512328E+5 | 1,2862512328E+5 |
| 60 | 6,4312561641E+4 | 1,1139262432E+5 |

Theoretically, these values are the same for both cracks.

*Modelling* $B$ *:*

We're doing non-regression tests.

*modellings* $C$ *,* $D$ *,* $E$ *,* $F$ *:*

Reference values of Modelling $A$ are taken

## 2 Modelling A

In this Modelling, the extended finite elements ($\text{X-FEM}$) method is used. A geometric enrichment radius is defined with a layer number of element equal to 3.

### 2.1 Mesh characteristics

The structure is modelled by a regular mesh composed of $100\times 100$ QUAD4, respectively, along the $x,y$ axes. The crack is not meshed.

![Figure](/figures/v6.03.138/10000000000001860000033A3B2D7B4CE7FC8440.png)

*mesh cracked plate*

### 2.2 Tested quantities and results

For each $\theta$ angle value, the ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathrm{II}}$ stress intensity factors given by CALC_G (for both cracks) and the POST_K1_K2_K3 K1 and K2 (for both cracks) are tested.

For the $G-\mathrm{thêta}$ method (CALC_G command), two choices of theta field crowns are tested:

- $\mathrm{C1}$ : ${R}_{\inf}=0,1a$ et ${R}_{\sup}=0,3a$ ;
- $\mathrm{C2}$ : ${R}_{\inf}=h$ et ${R}_{\sup}=\mathrm{3h}$ ;

where $h$ is the characteristic size of mesh cells:

$h=\sqrt{{(\frac{\mathrm{LX}}{\mathrm{NX}})}^{2}+{(\frac{\mathrm{LY}}{\mathrm{NY}})}^{2}}$

For the extrapolation of displacement jumps (POST_K1_K2_K3), the maximum curvilinear abscisse is equal to $0,3a$.

## 8 Summary of results

The objectives of this test are achieved:

- Validate the calculation of stress intensity factors in mixed mode for linear and quadratic elements $\text{X-FEM}$ on a single case
- Test non-regression of the volume forces imposed on a $\text{X-FEM}$ crack

*Blanket on linear elements*

With the CALC_G command, good accuracy is obtained on ${K}_{I}$ and ${K}_{\mathit{II}}$ (2 to 3%) with linear elements (triangles or quadrangles), regardless of the type of enrichment at the bottom of the crack (topological or geometric).

However, with POST_K1_K2_K3 control, the activation of geometric enrichment significantly improves the solution compared to the default topological enrichment (5-6 % → 2 to 3%).

It is therefore recommended to use the default enrichment (topological) and an post-processing with CALC_G. If for any reason, we wish to post-treat with POST_K1_K2_K3, then it is preferable to activate the geometric enrichment.

*A review of quadratic elements*

Quadratic elements (with topological enrichment) allows to find results as accurate as linear elements with geometric enrichment, but for a much larger system size to solve.
