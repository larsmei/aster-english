# v5.06.110 PLEXU06 – Validation of the Code_Aster chaining - Europlexus

**Summary:**

This test is intended to validate the chaining of a nonlinear transient calculation where the first part of the resolution is conducted by Europlexus via the CALC_EUROPLEXUS command, then continues in *Code_Aster* with the use of the DYNA_NON_LINE operator, with an explicit rebalancing step to implicit MACRO_BASCULE_SCHEMA command.

More specifically, the model implemented includes the following specific features:

- elements finished beam type ( POU_D_E ),
- elements Q4GG finished on triangular or quadrangular mesh cells,
- floor springs carpets to model the soil under the foundation,
- load of pressure type imposed variable in time,
- linear zone,
- nonlinear zone with constitutive law GLRC_DAMAGE .

## 1 Description

### 1.1 General description

The structure is of the square reinforced concrete portico type of $10m$ side and $2m$ top with the 4 poles connected to their base to a radier of the same size and resting on a carpet of ground springs.

The radier and roof are represented in elements Q4GG hulls. Only the toe can be nonlinear (constitutive law GLRC_DAMAGE) and it is subjected to variable pressure in time.

![Figure](/figures/v5.06.110/description_generale.png)

The objective of this type of study is to process the fast dynamic part of the response in Europlexus, then switch to *Code_Aster* (implicitly) for the induced shake phase, which is too long in time to allow a resolution in a reasonable time by remaining explicitly in Europlexus.

In post-processing, the displacement, acceleration and oscillator response spectrum (SRO) will be analyzed at the point $\mathit{PP2}$ that is in the middle of the upper slab.

### 1.4 Time limitations CPU

For reasons purely related to a short CPU time for this test case, the total duration that will be simulated numerically during the transient resolution will be deliberately shortened. This will also induce a moment of tilting between the codes that will be located too "at a time": the initial fast dynamic response regime will not have turned into an overall shake mode. The test case will therefore have the disadvantage of tipping from schema to time at an unfavourable moment when non-linearities will still be in the propagation phase, which should not be the case for a real industrial study where the phase of shake-up treated as implicit should remain linear.

Logically, the switch should therefore occur after the return to 0 of the pressure imposed (failure to be in the throttle speed) , so after $0,0171s$ . The CFL condition imposing an explicit time step of the order of $5\mu s$ , the number of time step would be prohibitive for an test case . To reduce the time CPU, you place the switch much earlier, to $0,007s$ , so when the pressure imposed is on its maximum tray . In implicit the time step will be $50\mu s$ .

### 1.5 Europlexus to Code_Aster transition strategy

In this chapter we will discuss two problems:

- rereading and building fields to move from Europlexus resolution (via CALC_EUROPLEXUS) to DYNA_NON_LINE: this step will be validated by comparing calculations explicitly (it remains schematic in similar time between codes),
- the switch from an explicit schema to an implicit schema.

#### 1.5.1 Construction of fields for prosecution

The model has a linear zone and a nonlinear zone. On the linear zone, the fields that we will recover at the last moment calculated with Europlexus are kinematic fields, so only fields to nodes. The constraints will be recalculated in *Code_Aster*.

On the nonlinear zone, two fields at additional Gauss points should be read again for the constraint and internal variables related to the material law used: GLRC_DAMAGE . The nonlinear zone only contains elements type Q4GG on triangular or quadrangular mesh cells.

All fields generated by Europlexus will be re-read from an MED file.

For the continuation with DYNA_NON_LINE (or MACRO_BASCULE_SCHEMA) the following fields are defined as initial state: displacement, speed, acceleration, stress (over the entire model) and internal variables.

#### 1.5.2 Diagram switch

The transient resolution, following Europlexus, is continued in *Code_Aster* starting with the explicit pattern of centered differences, with molten mass matrix, in order to remain in the same assumptions as the Europlexus calculation. Then the transition to an implicit resolution is made with a specific phase of rebalancing within the MACRO_BASCULE_SCHEMA operator. The transient resolution ends in implicit with a consistent mass matrix and for a time step ten times larger than explicit.

## 2 reference solution

The results obtained with a diagram shift will be compared with the results obtained when one diagram is kept in time. In order to quantify the differences between the codes, we will therefore build two reference solutions: the first calculated with Europlexus, the second with *Code_Aster* explicitly.

### 2.1 Baseline

The point $\mathit{PP2}$ is compared to the SRO (with a 5% damping factor) reference solutions in terms of displacement, acceleration and SRO (with a 5% damping factor) in the middle of the top slab. The SRO also compares the results obtained using the OBSERVATION tables, particularly the CALC_EUROPLEXUS generated with the ARCHIVAGE data.

### 2.2 Uncertainty about the solution

Differences due to the schema shift will be compared to differences between the two reference solutions (one obtained with Europlexus, the other with *Code_Aster*). Ideally, the switch should not amplify these differences between the reference solutions, which cannot be reduced.

## 3 Modelling A

The concrete slabs are modeled in elements Q4GG, on quadrangular or triangular mesh cells. The poles are modeled in elements type POU_D_E. The ground springs are, on the other hand, of the K_TR_D_N type.

### 3.1 Analysis of results

In this chapter, we will trace the vertical evolutions of the displacement and acceleration of the point $\mathit{PP2}$ (in the middle of the top slab). Each time, we will superimpose the different responses obtained:

1. resolution with CALC_EUROPLEXUS then continuation with DYNA_NON_LINE explicitly,
2. resolution with CALC_EUROPLEXUS over the entire study interval,
3. resolution with DYNA_NON_LINE explicit over the entire study interval,
4. switch CALC_EUROPLEXUS to DYNA_NON_LINE implicit,
5. DYNA_NON_LINE is explicitly switched to implicit.

This will allow the quantification of:

- accuracy when rereading MED data between codes and good model concordance (curve 1),
- the model differences between Europlexus and *Code_Aster* (curves 2 and 3) with identical timing,
- differences between explicit solution and solution with diagram shift (curves 4 and 5).

Acceleration analysis is expected to amplify the differences, compared to the differences in calculated displacement.

For the following graphs, the simulated time duration was deliberately increased to provide a more relevant analysis. In the test case command file, this simulation time is much shorter to reduce the time CPU.

#### 3.1.2 Comparison of calculated accelerations

![Figure](/figures/v5.06.110/1000000000000AC20000089BA7D8DDCB199F78B5.jpg)

Accelerations have much more chahutized appearances than displacements, but differences between numerical solutions remain moderate. More precisely, we can see the influence of the schema in implicit time that smoothes the response (dissipative pattern type HHT).

![Figure](/figures/v5.06.110/1000000000000AB20000089BF83A2BF179597E44.jpg)

By zooming in on the rocking moment, significant disturbances of acceleration occur. These numerical oscillations cannot be totally annihilated, even by playing on the parameters of the diagram-turning method (choose of the diagram during the balancing phase, parameter of this diagram, choice of the diagram before and after the rocking...). In fact, we have been able to verify that these oscillations are very largely caused by the change in mass matrix: in fact, in explicit use is made of a flambed mass matrix, whereas by implicit it is the consistent mass matrix that is chosen. When the rocking is made, one passes abruptly from one mass matrix to the other and this disturbs the solution. The rebalancing strategy does not completely succeed in eliminating this jump.

A parade could be to gradually switch from one mass matrix to another, using on some time step a variable mass matrix that would be a linear combination of the two matrices (light and consistent). The interest of this algorithmic evolution will still be quantified on this test case, for example.

### 3.2 Test values

We will test the values obtained (movements and accelerations) at the final moment: $0,007s$ .

Each tested value will in fact be the absolute value of the relative difference between the result to be tested and the reference solution considered. This formula must tend to 0 and it is directly a relative value, which explains that in the TEST_FONCTION operator it is specified CRITERE='ABSOLU'car, otherwise it would be sought to render the value relative a second time.

In practice, these tested values can only be set to 0 if the difference between the same calculation with *Code_Aster* or Europlexus is zero, which is not the case. More precisely, the differences for time-shift solutions can only be less than the differences between the two reference solutions (complete calculation with Europlexus and complete calculation with *Code_Aster* explicitly).

So we start by measuring the relative differences between the two reference solutions. On the move, we have: $2,656188{.10}^{-3}$ and acceleration: $0,016270437$

Then we will analyze the relative differences induced by the calculation resume between Europlexus and *Code_Aster* (explicit, so no rocking). On the move, we have: $4,93101{.10}^{-04}$ and acceleration: $0,0380$ .

Differences induced by the time shift should be of the same order as quantity. Indeed, the switch cannot correct these differences inherent in the differences between the codes.

First, we compare the Europlexus to *Code_Aster* implicit. On the move, we have a relative difference from: $5,02195{.10}^{-04}$ and in acceleration: $0,019848325$ .

Finally, the relative differences is given with the solution obtained by explicit toggle to implicit, but always remaining in *Code_Aster* . On the move, we have a relative difference of : $0,011460178$ and acceleration: $0,045500117$ .

It is noted that the acceleration differences is larger than the displacement differences, which is logical because the displacements are more regular than the accelerations, as can be verified on the graphs of the v5.06.110-comparison-deplacement-calculated and v5.06.110-comparison-acceleration-calculated.

Then, it is found that on the movements, the rocking introduces very little disturbance, while on the accelerations, oscillations, albeit damped, appear. After analysis, it turns out that they are mainly due to the abrupt passage of a mass matrix fused to a consistent matrix.

For the calculation of SRO, the difference relative maximum (over the entire frequency range and for an equivalent damping of 5%) will be calculated with reference solution, whether for SRO obtained from OBSERVATION and ARCHIVAGE. These relative differences are in the order of 5 to 8% and are attributable to the time step tilt and differences where the SRO input data are archived.

## 4 Executive summary

This test case validates the continuation of calculations by switching from Europlexus to *code_aster* , on a reinforced concrete building model. Specifically, we begin by validating the field rereading to nodes or Gauss points to continue with an explicit calculation with DYNA_NON_LINE . Then we validate this pursuit by tilting to an implicit time schema.

The observed differences are satisfactory and consistent with the differences in Modelling between the codes (mainly at the level of the mass matrix that can be consistent or fused). post-processings SRO is also valid for ARCHIVAGE or OBSERVATION data.
