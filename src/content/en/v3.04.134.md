# v3.04.134 SSLV134 - Circular fissure in infinite medium

**Summary**

This test allows, after obtaining the displacement field by MECA_STATIQUE, the calculation of the local energy release rate for a circular crack plunged into an infinite supposed medium.

For the first model, only half a space defined by the crack plane is represented. The crack bottom is then a closed curve (a circle) and is defined as such in DEFI_FOND_FISS. The local yield rate is compared to the reference analytical solution.

The following seven modellings allow the calculation of stress intensity factors $\mathrm{K1}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ , in $\mathrm{3D}$ and axisymmetric, calculated by POST_K1_K2_K3 and/or CALC_G (modellings FEM) / CALC_G_XEM (modellings X-FEM).

- The A Modelling tests $G$ for a mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ with closed crack,
- Modelling B tests $\mathit{K1}$ for a mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ ,
- The Modelling C test $\mathit{K1}$ for an axi-symmetric mesh,
- Modelling D tests the combination of $\mathrm{K1}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ for a mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ .
- Modelling H test $\mathrm{K1}$ for a non-mesh crack (method $\text{X-FEM}$)
- Modelling I test $\mathrm{K1}$ for an unmated axi-symmetric crack (method $\text{X-FEM}$)
- The J Modelling tests $G$ for mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ with closed crack for elements incompressible,
- The Modelling K test $\mathrm{K1}$ for an axi-symmetric mesh for incompressible elements,
- The Modelling L test $G$ for a mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ with closed crack ($\text{X-FEM}$ method).
- The M Modelling test $G$ for a mesh $\mathrm{3D}$ with closed crack in large transformations.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The crack is circular (penny shape crack) $a$ radius, in the $\mathrm{Oxy}$ plane. To make the medium count as infinite, the quantities features of the massif are about 5 times higher than the $a$ radius.

### 1.2 Material properties

Young's modulus : $E={2.10}^{5}\mathrm{MPa}$

Poisson's ratio : $\nu =0.3$

Density: $\rho =7850\mathrm{kg}/\mathrm{m³}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

| Lower face | ${\sigma}_{z}=1.\mathit{MPa}$ uniform tensile stress |
| --- | --- |
| Upper face | ${\sigma}_{z}=1.\mathrm{MPa}$ uniform tensile stress |

Following the Modelling, boundary conditionss are also available for symmetry and blocking of rigid body movements.

In model C, the load is supplemented by a pressure applied to the lips of the crack, the same amplitude as the tensile stress to the infinite ${\sigma}_{z}$. By the principle of superposition, this load generates the same energy restitution rates.

In model D where only one quarter of the parallelepiped is represented, boundary conditions antisymmetry is used for torsion loading: they are tangently imposed on one side. Torsion loading is introduced as a tangential superfacial force (distributed shear) applied to the crack lips.

- Upper lip: ${F}_{X}=-\tau \frac{Y}{a}$ and ${F}_{Y}=+\tau \frac{X}{a}$
- Lower lip: ${F}_{X}=+\tau \frac{Y}{a}$ and ${F}_{Y}=-\tau \frac{X}{a}$

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Calculation method used for reference solution

For a circular $a$ radius crack in an infinite medium, subject to uniform $\sigma$ traction as normal in the lip plane, the local $G(s)$ energy return rate is independent of the $s$ curvilinear abscisse and is [bib1]:

$G(s)=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{\pi E}4{\sigma}^{2}a$

then the stress intensity coefficient $\mathrm{K1}$ is given by the formula d-Irwin:

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.134/Object_7.svg)

either ${K}_{I}=\frac{2\sigma \sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{\pi}}$

If this crack is subject to shear distributed on the lips: ${\sigma}_{\theta z}=\tau \frac{r}{a}$

(equivalent to an infinite twist), then we are in pure mode 3 and the corresponding stress intensity factor is:

${K}_{3}=\frac{4\tau \sqrt{a}}{3\sqrt{\pi}}$ by the formula d

In the presence of the two combined modes, we will have:

$G(s)=\frac{(1-{\nu}^{2})}{E}{K}_{1}^{2}+\frac{(1+\nu )}{E}{K}_{3}^{2}$

## 5 Modelling C

This Modelling allows the calculation of $G$, $\mathit{K_I}$ and $K_J$ to be tested using CALC_G and POST_K1_K2_K3 (a method of extrapolation of the movements on the lips of the crack) in axisymmetric.

### 5.1 Modelling characteristics

The model symmetry is used to represent only half a plate.

Two loads are applied in turn: an infinitely tensile load (applied on the upper part of the plate), and a pressure load on the lip. By the principle of superposition, these two loads generate exactly the same $G$ energy restitution rates and the same stress intensity factors $\mathit{K_I}$. The presence of both loads will therefore allow to control the correct consideration of the contributions of edge terms in the calculation of $G$ by the G-theta method.

### 5.2 Mesh characteristics

The mesh is radiating around the crack tip. In the figure below, the lip is represented in orange.

Nodes media of the elements edges affecting the crack tip are moved to the quarter of these edges, to obtain better accuracy.

![Figure](/figures/v3.04.134/maillage_modelisationC.png)

*mesh axi-symmetric*

### 5.3 Tested quantities and results

It is expected that the error on K is twice as small as the error on G.
This is actually what is observed, except for the $\mathit{K_I}$ calculated by CALC_G / option K.
The accuracy is less (here 3% vs. 0.9% expected), as the additional curvature terms in the asymptotic fields are not available for axisymmetric models.

The results are identical for the two loads applied (upper side pull and lip pressure).

### 7.4 Remarks

The accuracy of the results obtained on an unmesh crack ($\text{X-FEM}$ method) and the post-processing POST_K1_K2_K3 is very satisfactory, comparable to the accuracy with a FEM mesh crack. Nevertheless, the results are degraded with CALC_G_XFEM as is generally the case with the curved fronts (also SSVL154 test). The use of this operator is not recommended for the straight fronts with the $\text{X-FEM}$ method, given the rather modest accuracy of this operator (18%).

For CALC_G_XFEM, LAGRANGE smoothings do not allow easy to use results, so a LEGENDRE smoothing is the best option.

It is recalled that the mesh used is linear; using a finer mesh improves the accuracy of the result, but at the expense of the calculation times.

## 13 Summary of results

The conclusions of this test case are as follows:

- The definition and calculation of local $G$ on closed crack bottoms is validated. In particular, the independence of local $G$ from the angle for a crack and a load axisymmetric is verified. There is a difference of less than $\text{2\%}$ on the entire crack bottom for a DISCRETISATION‘LINEAIRE.
- The POST_K1_K2_K3 control, which allows the calculation of stress intensity factors by exploiting the leap of movement on the crack lips, is also validated. This method, less accurate than CALC_G/CALC_G_XFEM, allows the $\mathrm{K1}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ values to be obtained here (with an appropriate mesh: nodes edge media touching the crack bottom moved to the quarter of these edges) from the $\mathrm{K1}$ and $\mathrm{K3}$ values within $\text{2 \%}$ of the reference.

Three methods of interpolation are used and give close results. Method 3 is interesting because it provides a single value of stress intensity factors and not a maximum value and a minimum value.

The use of POST_K1_K2_K3 to study a nodes release crack is tested and gives satisfactory results.

- The calculation of the bilinear form of $G$ and CALC_K_G option of CALC_G_XEM is valid.
- The $\text{X-FEM}$ method allows to evaluate the stress intensity factors $K$ on an uncracked mesh with an error below $\text{20\%}$ with CALC_G_XFEM. With the POST_K1_K2_K3 operator, the accuracy increases considerably, to reach a difference at analytical solution of $\text{2\%}$.
- The calculations are valid for elements 3D_INCO_UPG, 3D_INCO_UP, AXIS_INCO_UPG and AXIS_INCO_UP
