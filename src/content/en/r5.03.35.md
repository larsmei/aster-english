# r5.03.35 Integration of the viscoelastic constitutive relation of Maxwell (VISC_MAXWELL and VISC_MAXWELL_MT)

**Summary**

This document describes the development of the viscoelastic constitutive law of Maxwell. The Maxwell law (keyword VISC_MAXWELL) is a basic viscoelastic law. Its variant (keyword VISC_MAXWELL_MT) includes a variation of its parameters with porosity according to a Mori-Tanaka homogenisation law. This latter law is therefore available only for the poromechanical models (THM).

## 1 Introduction

The implementation of the Maxwell viscoelasticity model (keyword VISC_MAXWELL) is described here. A variant of this model has been added for poromechanics (keyword VISC_MAXWELL_MT), which provides for the evolution of the parameters with porosity according to a Mori-Tanaka homogenisation model. This law was first developed and used within the framework of the thesis of G. Mélot [ 1 ] on bituminous materials.

For each of these models, the material is assumed to be isotropic. They can be used in 3D, in plane strain (D_PLAN) and in axisymmetric conditions (AXIS).

This note presents the constitutive equations of the models and their implementation.

## 2 Continuous relation

The assumption of small perturbations and of an isotropic medium is adopted. The Maxwell model is represented by a purely viscous damper and a Hookean spring in series. This model takes into account a volumetric viscosity and a deviatoric viscosity. The equations are then simply:

$$
\begin{array}{c}{\dot{\epsilon}}_{v}(t)=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{v}}{\sigma}_{\mathrm{m}}(t)+\frac{1}{K}\dot{{\sigma}_{\mathrm{m}}}(t)\\ \dot{{e}_{ij}}(t)=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{d}}{s}_{ij}(t)+\frac{1}{2G}\dot{{s}_{ij}}(t)\end{array}
$$

with:

${\epsilon}_{v}$ : the volumetric strain

${\sigma}_{m}$ : the mean effective stress $\sigma =\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\left(\sigma \right)I$

${e}_{ij}$ : the components of the deviatoric stress tensor ${e}_{ij}={\epsilon}_{ij}-\frac{1}{3}{\epsilon}_{v}{\delta}_{ij}$

${s}_{ij}$ : the components of the deviatoric effective-stress tensor ${s}_{ij}={\sigma}_{ij}-{\sigma}_{m}{\delta}_{ij}$

$K$ : the bulk modulus

$G$ : the shear modulus

${\eta}_{v}$ : the volumetric viscosity

${\eta}_{d}$ : the deviatoric viscosity

## 3 Evolution of the parameters for the law VISC_MAXWELL_MT

The behaviour of a porous medium is strongly affected by the proportion of voids that it contains. For certain materials (for example of bituminous-mix type) whose porosity is going to change considerably, it is important to include this variation in the viscosity. Homogenisation laws make it possible, among other things, to compute the mechanical properties of a porous material from the mechanical properties of the skeleton and from the porosity. The Mori-Tanaka method is appropriate for accounting for the interaction between the pores by modifying the strain boundary conditions at infinity within the framework of the Eshelby problem. In the thesis of G. Melot [ 1 ], this technique is applied, in the case of spherical pores embedded in an isotropic solid matrix, to the elastic part and then to the viscous part. The result is classical in elasticity and gives:

$\begin{array}{c}{K}^{\mathit{mt}}={K}^{s}\frac{4{G}^{s}(1-\mathrm{\phi })}{3\mathrm{\phi }{K}^{s}+4{G}^{s}}\\ {G}^{\mathit{mt}}={G}^{s}\frac{(1-\mathrm{\phi })(9{K}^{s}+8{G}^{s})}{9{K}^{s}(1+\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{\phi })+8{G}^{s}(1+\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{\phi })}\end{array}$

With ${K}^{\mathit{mt}}$ and ${G}^{\mathit{mt}}$ the effective parameters of the porous medium, of elasticity and of shear respectively, which will be used in the constitutive law.

The mathematical derivation classically carried out in elasticity is then performed for the derivative of the strain tensor in place of the strain tensor.

This makes it possible to obtain, in a similar manner, the following equations:

$\begin{array}{c}{\eta}_{v}^{\mathit{mt}}={\eta}_{v}^{s}\frac{4{\eta}_{d}^{s}(1-\mathrm{\phi })}{3\mathrm{\phi }{\eta}_{v}^{s}+4{\eta}_{d}^{s}}\\ {\eta}_{d}^{\mathit{mt}}={\eta}_{d}^{s}\frac{(1-\mathrm{\phi })(9{\eta}_{v}^{s}+8{\eta}_{d}^{s})}{9{\eta}_{v}^{s}(1+\frac{2}{3}\mathrm{\phi })+8{\eta}_{d}^{s}(1+\frac{3}{2}\mathrm{\phi })}\end{array}$

With ${\eta}_{v}^{\mathit{mt}}$ and ${\eta}_{d}^{\mathit{mt}}$ the effective viscosity parameters of the porous medium.

## 4 Integration of the constitutive relation

A finite-difference time discretisation is used in order to obtain the expressions of the components of the effective-stress matrix. The calculation steps leading to the results are reproduced below.

The following notation is used:

$\dot{{\sigma}_{{m}_{}}}=\frac{{\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}-{{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}}{\mathit{dt}}$

Where ${{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}$ and ${{\sigma}_{m}}_{n+1}$ are respectively the mean effective stresses at the previous time and at the current time, and dt the time step.

Using the time discretisation of $\stackrel{´}{{\sigma}_{{m}_{}}}$ and $\stackrel{´}{{s}_{ij}}$ , the expressions of ${\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}$ and of ${s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}$ are sought:

$\dot{{\epsilon}_{{v}_{n+1}}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{v}}{\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}+\frac{1}{K}\frac{{\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}-{{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}}{\mathit{dt}}=\left(\frac{1}{{\eta}_{v}}+\frac{1}{K\mathit{dt}}\right){\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}-\frac{{{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}}{K\mathit{dt}}$

$\dot{{e}_{i{j}_{n+1}}}=\frac{1}{{\eta}_{d}}{s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}^{'}+\frac{1}{2G}\frac{{s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}-{{s}_{ij}}_{n}}{\mathit{dt}}=\left(\frac{1}{{\eta}_{d}}+\frac{1}{2G\mathit{dt}}\right){s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}-\frac{{{s}_{ij}}_{n}}{2G\mathit{dt}}$

Which is equivalent to:

${\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}=\left(\dot{{\epsilon}_{{v}_{n+1}}}+\frac{{{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}}{K\mathit{dt}}\right)\frac{K\mathit{dt}{\eta}_{v}}{{\eta}_{v}+K\mathit{dt}}$

${s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}=\left(\dot{{e}_{i{j}_{n+1}}}+\frac{{{s}_{ij}}_{n}}{2G\mathit{dt}}\right)\frac{2G\mathit{dt}{\eta}_{d}}{{\eta}_{d}+2G\mathit{dt}}$

Which finally is equivalent to:

${\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}=\left(\mathit{dt}\dot{{\epsilon}_{{v}_{n+1}}}+\frac{{{\sigma}_{m}}_{n}}{K}\right)\frac{K}{1+\frac{\mathit{Kdt}}{{\eta}_{v}}}$

${s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}=\left(\mathit{dt}\dot{{e}_{i{j}_{n+1}}}+\frac{{{s}_{ij}}_{n}}{2G}\right)\frac{2G}{1+\frac{2\mathit{Gdt}}{{\eta}_{d}}}$

which can be written in incremental form:

${\sigma}_{{m}_{n+1}}^{'}=\left(\Delta {\epsilon}_{v}(t)+\frac{{{\sigma}_{m}^{'}}_{n}}{K}\right)\frac{K}{1+\frac{\mathit{Kdt}}{{\eta}_{v}}}$

${s}_{i{j}_{n+1}}^{'}=\left(\Delta {e}_{ij}\left(t\right)+\frac{{{s}_{ij}^{'}}_{n}}{2G}\right)\frac{2G}{1+\frac{2\mathit{Gdt}}{{\eta}_{d}}}$
