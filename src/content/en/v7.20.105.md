# v7.20.105 HSNA105 - Expansion of an infinite hollow cylinder with consideration of thermal dissipations due to mechanical deformations

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the setting up of a thermomechanical coupling with consideration of thermal dissipations due to mechanical deformations as a source of heat in the thermal equation. The coupling is carried out by the classical method of the so-called isothermal staggered schematic literature.

This test case has two modellingss that correspond to two distinct physical transients described in the literature [2]:

- Modelling A: Thermoelastic case where the expansion of a cylinder causes its cooling;
- Model B: Thermoelastoplastic case where the plastic dissipation effect compensates for the previous effect and causes the overall warming of the cylinder.

The results obtained are in good correlation.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

An infinite hollow cylinder is considered.

![Figure](/figures/v7.20.105/Object_13.png)

Figure 1: Géométrie du cylindre creux

*Model A:*

Geometrical properties are:

- ${R}_{i}=10\mathit{mm}$ internal radius
- External radius ${R}_{e}=20\mathit{mm}$

*Model B:*

Geometrical properties are:

- ${R}_{i}=100\mathit{mm}$ internal radius
- External radius ${R}_{e}=200\mathit{mm}$

### 1.2 Material properties

The materials used in the calculations use the constitutive law VMIS_JOHN_COOK. Its nutring function is written as follows [1]:

$R(p,\dot{p},T)=(A+B{p}^{n})(1+C\ln(\frac{\dot{p}}{\dot{{p}_{0}}}))(1-(\frac{T-{T}_{\mathit{room}}}{{T}_{\mathit{melt}}-{T}_{\mathit{room}}}))$

We will not use the terms related to the dependence on temperature and the plastic deformation rate. We will then work with:

$R(p)=A+B{p}^{n}$

*Model A:*

An elastic constitutive law is modelled. The following mechanical and thermal parameters are used:

- Young's modulus $E=206900\mathit{MPa}$
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.29$
- Elasticity limit $A=5{.10}^{100}\mathit{Pa}$
- Post-elastic pen $B=2{.10}^{10}\mathit{Pa}$
- Post-elastic power $n=1$
- Density $\rho =7800\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$
- $\alpha =1,5{.10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$ expansion coefficient
- $\lambda =45W/(\mathit{m.K})$ conductivity
- Heat capacity ${C}_{p}=460J/(\mathit{kg.K})$
- Reference temperature ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=293K$

*Model B:*

A constitutive law elastoplastic is modelled equivalent to an VMIS_ISOT_LINE law. The following mechanical and thermal parameters are used:

- Young's modulus $E=70000\mathit{MPa}$
- Poisson's ratio $\nu =0.3$
- Temperature-dependent yield limit $A(T)=A({T}_{\mathit{ref}})(1-w(T-{T}_{\mathit{ref}}))$
- Elasticity limit at reference temperature $A({T}_{\mathit{ref}})=70\mathit{MPa}$
- Linear softening coefficient $w=3{.10}^{-4}{K}^{-1}$
- Post-elastic pen $B=210\mathit{MPa}$
- Post-elastic power $n=1$
- Density $\rho =2700\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$
- $\alpha =2,38{.10}^{-5}{K}^{-1}$ expansion coefficient
- $\lambda =150W/(\mathit{m.K})$ conductivity
- Heat capacity ${C}_{p}=900J/(\mathit{kg.K})$
- Reference temperature ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}=293K$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

*Model A:*

$20\mathit{mm}$ radial displacement is imposed on the inner side of the cylinder at the speed of $\mathrm{1mm}/s$. The temperature of the outer face is imposed on ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}$. The other sides of the cylinder are considered thermally isolated. The behaviour is thermoelastic and the expansion of a cylinder causes its cooling.

*Model B:*

The cylinder surface is considered thermally insulated. The displacement of the cylinder's inner face is $130\mathit{mm}$ applied in $1,3s$. The behaviour is thermoelastoplastic and the plastic dissipation effect compensates for the previous thermo-elastic cooling effect to cause the cylinder to warm up overall.

### 1.4 Initial conditions

The cylinder temperature is equal to ${T}_{\mathit{ref}}$ .

The reference solutions for each of the two problems are derived from the curves presented in the thesis of L. ADAM. These results were obtained using the METAFOR software developed by the University of Liège.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

AXIS_DIAG Modelling for thermal and AXIS_SI Modelling for mechanics are used. The test introducing large deformations, GDEF_LOG formalism is used.

![Figure](/figures/v7.20.105/1000000000000584000002D814D5EF20462D6A71.png)

### 3.2 Characteristics of meshes

Two meshes are used (one for each phenomenon).

For thermal purposes, a linear mesh elements is used:

- 100 elements type QUAD4 .
- 121 nodes .

For mechanics, a mesh of elements is used with quadratic interpolation:

- 100 elements type QUAD8 .
- 341 nodes .

### 3.3 Tested quantities and results

The temperature on the inner cylinder wall is tested at the moment $t=\mathrm{6s}$ , which corresponds to the cylinder cooling peak.

| **Identification** | **Reference type** | **Reference value** | **Tolerance** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal wall temperature | 'SOURCE_EXTERNE' | $261,5$ | 0,2% |

We traced the temperature evolution of the cylinder's inner face during the calculation and compared it with the result of L. ADAM. We can see that we find the same results.

### 3.4 Remarks

The results obtained with the coupling introduced in Code_Aster give good results.
