# u2.07.04 Dynamic condensation of a model by static substructuring

**Summary:**

This document describes a way of using *Code_Aster* to allow the transient analysis by the non-linear transient dynamics operator DYNA_NON_LINE on a model reduced strictly to non-linear subdomains, the linear subdomains being taken into account once and for all by dynamic condensation by means of static macro-elements on the interfaces.

The condensation method acts on all the interface degrees of freedom common to the condensed subdomains and to the subdomains computed by non-linear finite-element analysis. A complete general method requires the computation of all the constrained static interface modes, each associated with a response to a unit imposed displacement for each interface degree of freedom. An alternative method makes it possible to use a reduced basis of interface modes, fewer in number but representative of the displacements of all the interface degrees of freedom, in the expected frequency range.

## 1 Introduction

The objective of the dynamic condensation of a model by static substructuring is to reduce the total number of degrees of freedom of a model made up of several subdomains, by condensing the solution of the subdomains of linear constitutive behaviour onto the degrees of freedom of their interfaces with subdomains of non-linear constitutive behaviour, treated with finite elements.

The idea is then to represent each of these subdomains by a macro-element of static type, so as to be able to use it in a «mixed» reduced model also comprising finite elements for the non-condensed parts of non-linear constitutive behaviour, in order to proceed to non-linear analyses by means of the non-linear analysis operators STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) or DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01).

In the condensation of each linear subdomain, one also wishes to integrate the contribution of the inertia forces, hence the dynamic part of its behaviour obtained by modal analysis, and for this one will therefore use dynamic macro-elements in the same way as the static macro-elements required in static substructuring. This approach is known in the literature under the name: «System Equivalent Reduction-Expansion Process». In the linear regime, it preserves the natural angular frequencies of the initial system in the expected frequency range.

The principle of this condensation and its application is described in the documents [bib1, bib2, bib5].

## 2 Classical condensation problem and principle of the method

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.04/100002000000007A000000C3051F7D7864A8855C.png)

**Figure 2.1: Subdomains of the classical condensation problem.**

In figure 2.1 above are shown schematically the possible subdomains that one may encounter within the framework of a classical condensation problem by static substructuring:

- the subdomains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ : subdomains of a structure of constant linear constitutive behaviour, condensed and modelled by a dynamic macro-element,
- the subdomains of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ : subdomains of a structure of potentially non-linear constitutive behaviour, not condensed and modelled by finite elements,
- the subdomains of type ${E}_{3}^{\mathrm{impé}}$ : soil subdomain represented by a macro-element coming from the conversion of a complex soil-impedance matrix.

The basic principle of condensation by macro-element consists, for each subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ , in partitioning the degrees of freedom $j$ of their interfaces denoted $\Sigma$ with the subdomains of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ , as well as their other degrees of freedom $i$ , said to be «internal».

The stiffness matrices $[K]$ and mass matrices $[M]$ are thus decomposed on these degrees of freedom:

$$
[K]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{K}_{ii}& {K}_{ij}\\ {K}_{ji}& {K}_{jj}\end{array}\right]
[M]=\left[\begin{array}{cc}{M}_{ii}& {M}_{ij}\\ {M}_{ji}& {M}_{jj}\end{array}\right]
$$

The principle of static substructuring amounts, for the matrix $[K]$ for example, to condensing it only onto the degrees of freedom $j$ of the interfaces $\Sigma$ by correcting the initial term ${K}_{jj}$ by a complementary term called the «Schur complement», according to the principle of the so-called Guyan condensation [bib3]:

$$
{\stackrel{ˉ}{K}}_{jj}={K}_{jj}-{K}_{ji}{({K}_{ii})}^{-1}{K}_{ij}={\Psi}^{T}.[K].\Psi
$$

The computation of the new term ${\stackrel{ˉ}{K}}_{jj}$ amounts to a projection of the matrix $[K]$ onto a basis of static modes $\Psi$ of «constrained» type, obtained by the static response to unit displacements imposed at each degree of freedom $j$ of the interfaces $\Sigma$ :

$$
\Psi =\left[\begin{array}{}[{\Psi}_{ij}]\\ \mathrm{Id}\end{array}\right]=\left[\begin{array}{}[-{K}_{ii}^{-1}{K}_{ij}]\\ \mathrm{Id}\end{array}\right]
$$

The condensation onto the degrees of freedom $j$ of the interfaces $\Sigma$ of the mass matrix $[M]$ and of the loads of the subdomains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ will be carried out in an analogous way by the matrix product of the matrix and of the loading vectors onto the basis of static modes $\Psi$ .

One then observes that the terms ${\Psi}^{T}.[K].\Psi$ and ${\Psi}^{T}.[M].\Psi$ can be obtained directly for each subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ by means of the operator for computing a dynamic macro-element MACR_ELEM_DYNA [U4.65.01](doc:u4.65.01) defined on the interface $\Sigma$ , and that the load terms, applied on the subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ , are obtained by the projection ${\Psi}^{T}.F$ by means of the operator PROJ_VECT_BASE [U4.63.13](doc:u4.63.13).

## 3 Methods of dynamic condensation by a complete or reduced basis of motions on the interfaces

One extends the preceding approach by including the condensation of the dynamic behaviour of the subdomains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ with the aid of a reduced modal representation of eigenmodes fixed on the interface $\Sigma$ , chosen so as to be representative of the dynamics in a certain frequency range.

Depending on the way of computing the modes representing the motions of the interfaces, two condensation methods are proposed.

### 3.1 The method of computation by a complete basis of motions at the interface

The motion $U$ of an arbitrary point of a subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ is expressed from the decomposition onto the static modes $\Psi$ and onto a complement brought by the dynamic part of the motion expressed by eigenmodes $\Phi$ , fixed on the interface $\Sigma$ :

$$
U=\sum\Psi {q}_{\Sigma}+\Phi q
$$

With the complete-basis method, on the interface $\Sigma$ , the eigenmodes $\Phi$ have a zero contribution and the static modes $\Psi$ have a unit value, and therefore the displacement ${U}_{\Sigma}$ of a point of the interface is expressed:

$$
U={X}_{\Sigma}={q}_{\Sigma}
$$

In this case, the physical degrees of freedom ${X}_{\Sigma}$ of the interface $\Sigma$ coincide with the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ associated with the static modes $\Psi$ .

As regards the generalised coordinates $q$ associated with the eigenmodes $\Phi$ of the subdomains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ , which are zero on the interface, their solution will be decoupled from that of the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ . In the assembly of the matrices of the condensed problem, the terms of projection onto the eigenmodes ${\Phi}^{T}.[K].\Phi$ and ${\Phi}^{T}.[M].\Phi$ will be juxtaposed. In order to be able to «mix» physical degrees of freedom with generalised coordinates, one uses the device, transparent to the user, of passing the latter off as additional physical degrees of freedom taken from among the internal degrees of freedom of a subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ .

This alias is active in the model in which the domains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ are condensed by macro-elements, but in order to have the true displacement of the degrees of freedom internal to these domains, it is obtained in post-processing after solution of the assembled global problem by means of the operator REST_COND_TRAN [U4.63.33](doc:u4.63.33), by applying the initial relation:

$$
U=\sum\Psi {q}_{\Sigma}+\Phi q
$$

It is necessary to retain a number of eigenmodes $\Phi$ which is an exact multiple of the maximum number of components of the condensed domain ($3$ for solid mechanical finite elements, $6$ for plates and shells, ...) in order to make the generalised coordinates $q$ correspond exactly to components of additional interface nodes.

At the level of the assembly of the degrees of freedom of a subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ , the initial storage represented in figure 3.1-a comprises terms both on internal degrees of freedom and on the interface degrees of freedom, with a fill profile that is not full.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.04/100000000000021C0000019F35AB3B22F9B86841.jpg)

**Figure 3.1-a: Initial assembly of the degrees of freedom of the subdomains E1 before condensation.**

After condensation by the complete method, most of the internal degrees of freedom disappear, but a very small part is devoted to representing the generalised coordinates associated with the eigenmodes, and the terms of the static macro-element are added to the interface degrees of freedom, with a full fill profile this time. The resulting storage is represented in figure 3.1-b below.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.04/10000000000002D50000019F960852E9B3AB90F7.jpg)

**Figure 3.1-b: Assembly of the degrees of freedom of the subdomains** ${E}_{1}^{l}$ **with the complete method.**

The assembly of the terms of the static macro-element is carried out by simple summation on the interface degrees of freedom and does not in general require an additional connection on the interface when one takes into account all the constrained static modes of the interface.

It is not so in the particular case where one decides to carry out a condensation on a single interface node: there, one must add a rigid-body connection relation on the interface degrees of freedom.

In the case of the presence of a subdomain of type ${E}_{3}^{\mathrm{impé}}$ , the post-processing by REST_COND_TRAN is unnecessary, because the displacement has no component on dynamic modes and the displacement at the interface is obtained definitively as soon as the global problem is solved.

### 3.2 The method of computation by modal reduction at the interface

With the modal-reduction method, the motion $U$ of an arbitrary point of a subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ is still expressed from the decomposition onto the static modes $\Psi$ and onto a complement brought by the dynamic part of the motion expressed by eigenmodes $\Phi$ :

$$
U=\sum\Psi {q}_{\Sigma}+\Phi q
$$

But this time, on the interface $\Sigma$ , the eigenmodes $\Phi$ have a non-zero contribution, because the static modes $\Psi$ chosen do not have a unit value on each node and are in number generally much smaller than the number of physical degrees of freedom ${X}_{\Sigma}$ of the interface $\Sigma$ which, in this case, do not coincide with the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ associated with the static modes $\Psi$ . One must therefore distinguish, in the assembly and the solution, the terms associated with the physical degrees of freedom and those associated with the generalised coordinates. In the same way as for the generalised coordinates $q$ associated with the eigenmodes $\Phi$ , one uses the device, transparent to the user, of passing the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ associated with the static modes $\Psi$ off as additional physical degrees of freedom, also taken from among degrees of freedom internal to a subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ .

But this time, the solution of the physical degrees of freedom ${X}_{\Sigma}$ of the interface $\Sigma$ will not be decoupled from that of the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ . In the assembly of the matrices of the condensed problem, the terms of projection onto the eigenmodes ${\Phi}^{T}.[K].\Phi$ and ${\Phi}^{T}.[M].\Phi$ will be juxtaposed. But one will then have to add a connection, and therefore Lagrange degrees of freedom, between the physical degrees of freedom ${X}_{\Sigma}$ of the interface $\Sigma$ and the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ . This connection is expressed by the relation:

$$
{X}_{\Sigma}=\sum\Psi {q}_{\Sigma}
$$

This relation will be expressed by the repeatable keyword LIAISON_INTERF of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01), which makes it possible to connect each subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ to a subdomain of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ (cf. figure 2.1) by generating a particular case of LIAISON_DDL with the coefficients of the preceding linear relation.

Then, in order to have the true displacement of the degrees of freedom internal to the subdomains of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ , one proceeds in the same way in post-processing after solution of the «mixed» global problem by means of the operator REST_COND_TRAN [U4.63.33](doc:u4.63.33), by applying the initial relation:

$$
U=\sum\Psi {q}_{\Sigma}+\Phi q
$$

**Note**:

*Here too, it is necessary to retain a number of eigenmodes* $\Phi$ *and a number of static modes* $\Psi$ *, both exact multiples of the maximum number of components of the condensed domain (* $3$ *for solid mechanical finite elements,* $6$ *for plates and shells, ...) in order to make the generalised coordinates correspond exactly to components of additional interface nodes.*

After condensation by the modal-reduction method, most of the internal degrees of freedom disappear, but a very small part is devoted to representing the generalised coordinates associated with the eigenmodes, and another small part is devoted to the generalised coordinates associated with the interface eigenmodes. This time, the terms of the static macro-element are not added to the interface degrees of freedom, which then retain a fill profile that is not full. But one then obtains a quadrangular block of Lagrange degrees of freedom between the physical degrees of freedom ${X}_{\Sigma}$ of the interface $\Sigma$ and the generalised coordinates ${q}_{\Sigma}$ .

The resulting storage is represented in figure 3.2 below.

![Figure](/figures/u2.07.04/10000000000002D50000019F786802A04A856AC3.jpg)

**Figure 3.2: Storage of the degrees of freedom of the subdomains** ${E}_{1}^{l}$ **with the modal-reduction method.**

One will note that there is no imposed choice of computation for the reduction basis of static interface modes $\Psi$ . One possible choice will be that used in soil–structure interaction [bib4], by considering the restriction to the interface $\Sigma$ of a basis of eigenmodes of the subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ on a bed of springs representative of the stiffness of the adjacent subdomains of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ or ${E}_{3}^{\mathrm{impé}}$ , cf. [U2.06.07, §11,5].

### 3.3 Validation of the use of the dynamic condensation methods

A good approach to validating the dynamic condensation model – before carrying out the non-linear analysis – consists in the comparison of the modal analysis on the first modes between the complete initial model, not reduced, containing all the subdomains, and the final «mixed» reduced model containing the condensed subdomains in the form of dynamic macro-elements as well as the model of subdomains of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ treated in the linear regime.

### 3.4 Respective advantages of using the dynamic condensation methods

The dynamic condensation method makes it possible in general, compared with the non-condensed model, to reduce the size of the matrix systems and of the total number of degrees of freedom, and to obtain gains in terms of memory and performance for the factorisation and the solution.

For the complete method, because of the full storage of the terms of the interface modes, this method is of interest in the factorisation operations, especially if the number of interface degrees of freedom hardly exceeds $1/10$ of the total number of degrees of freedom before condensation.

On the other hand, this method requires the computation of the interface modes for all the degrees of freedom and can be prohibitive in memory for this computation if the total number of degrees of freedom before condensation is greater than 100000 and if the number of interface modes is of the order of 10000.

The condensation method with modal reduction is then of interest if one reduces the number of interface modes to less than $1/10$ of the number of interface degrees of freedom, that is, to less than 1000.

While knowing that, in terms of performance:

- one adds equations linked to the Lagrange degrees of freedom relating to the relations between the degrees of freedom and the reduced interface modes, which is liable to penalise the solution,
- one obtains a gain on the factorisation of the system, because its size will decrease, as well as on the recovery onto the physical basis of each subdomain, in the ratio of the number of degrees of freedom to the number of reduced interface modes.

The complete method seems efficient and sufficient for a total number of degrees of freedom before condensation lying between about 5000 and 50000. The method with modal reduction becomes of interest beyond 50000 degrees of freedom before condensation.

## 4 Summary of the analysis of the «mixed» reduced model with dynamic condensation

The analysis with dynamic condensation requires one to go through the following steps of chaining the commands of *Code_Aster*:

- Classical initial instructions: definition of the complete finite-element mesh of the whole structure and of the mesh-cell groups, in particular the mesh-cell groups and node groups of the interfaces $\Sigma$ , and of the subdomain of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ , definition of the materials, assignment of the materials to the mesh-cell groups.

And for each subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ which will be condensed:

- Definition of the model and assignment of the finite elements to the model, assignment of the materials to the elements, assignment of the elementary characteristics,
- Computation of the eigenmodes on a basis fixed on the interface $\Sigma$ by the command CALC_MODES [U4.52.02](doc:u4.52.02), with the original boundary conditions of this subdomain,
- Definition of the dynamic interface of type CRAIGB on the node group of the interface $\Sigma$ by the command DEFI_INTERF_DYNA [U4.64.01](doc:u4.64.01),
- Computation of the static modes of the dynamic interface defined previously with MODE_STATIQUE [U4.52.14](doc:u4.52.14) in the case of the choice of a complete basis, or by CALC_MODES in the case of the choice of a reduced basis, with the appropriate conditions,
- Definition of a modal basis by the command DEFI_BASE_MODALE [U4.64.02] with the RITZ option, integrating the static modes of the dynamic interface defined previously in order to complete the basis of the eigenmodes on the interface (keywords MODE_INTF and INTERF_DYNA to designate the dynamic interface),
- Computation of the assembled loading vectors (forces, imposed displacements...) of the subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ and projection of these onto the complete RITZ basis, with the associated numbering of the degrees of freedom,
- Assembly of the dynamic macro-element of the subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ on the interface $\Sigma$ by the command MACR_ELEM_DYNA [U4.65.01](doc:u4.65.01), from the RITZ basis defined previously, integrating therein the projected load vectors, or of a dynamic macro-element of a subdomain of type ${E}_{3}^{\mathrm{impé}}$ by converting a soil impedance on the interface $\Sigma$ , cf. the command LIRE_IMPE_MISS [U7.02.32](doc:u7.02.32) and the keyword MATR_IMPE of the command MACR_ELEM_DYNA [U4.65.01](doc:u4.65.01).

Then, for the dynamic analysis of the «mixed» reduced model with the non-linear subdomains:

- Definition and assembly to the initial mesh by DEFI_MAILLAGE [U4.23.01] and ASSE_MAILLAGE [U4.23.03] of super mesh cells each corresponding to a dynamic macro-element of the subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ in order to create a new «mixed» reduced mesh. The nodes of these super mesh cells will carry the degrees of freedom of the condensed subdomain,
- Definition of a «mixed» reduced model assigned to the new «mixed» reduced mesh, integrating the non-condensed subdomain in finite elements of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ as well as the assignments of the super mesh cells by the keyword AFFE_SOUS_STRUC of the operator AFFE_MODELE [U4.41.01](doc:u4.41.01),
- Definition of the specific loads of the model by the keywords LIAISON_SOLIDE or LIAISON_INTERF of the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA [U4.44.01](doc:u4.44.01),
- Call to the non-linear analysis operator STAT_NON_LINE [U4.51.03](doc:u4.51.03) or DYNA_NON_LINE [U4.53.01](doc:u4.53.01) on the «mixed» reduced model, including the material field defined on the subdomain of type ${E}_{2}^{\mathrm{nl}}$ assigned to the «mixed» reduced mesh, the preceding specific loads by the keyword EXCIT, as well as the loading vectors defined on the subdomains which have been condensed and projected onto the macro-elements, via the keyword SOUS_STRUC,
- Finally, one can validate the final «mixed» reduced model, under a fully linear assumption, containing the condensed subdomains in the form of macro-elements, by comparison of natural frequencies with the modal analysis on the first modes of the complete initial model, not reduced, containing all the subdomains, by using the keyword MODE_VIBR of the operator DYNA_NON_LINE.

Then, for each subdomain of type ${E}_{1}^{l}$ apart from those of a soil impedance:

- Recovery onto the physical basis on the model of the subdomain on which the macro-element of the subdomain was built, by the operator REST_COND_TRAN [U4.63.33](doc:u4.63.33).

The command files miss06b.comm and sdnv107b.comm illustrate this type of analysis.
