# v1.01.259 ZZZZ259 - mesh Adaptation and 2D Border Tracking

**Summary:**

This case tests the border tracking functionality during mesh adaptation with HOMARD. It contains 2D borders that are described analytically and 1D borders that are described by a fine mesh.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The field studied is a pricking: two pipes join together to form a connection. In the chosen example, one aims at generality by taking different diameters and non-scathing axes. The geometry is built by Salome. Groups of all edge faces are created. The groups of sharp edges are also created.

With each adaptation of the mesh, nodes will be created on the edge. If you do nothing, the geometry remains facetted. Here you test the ability of HOMARD to replace the new nodes on the curved border.

For 2D borders, surfaces are described analytically, as is the case with the internal and external faces of both pipes.

For 1D boundaries, lines are described by a very fine mesh annex. This is the case of the complex curve which represents the intersection of the two pipes.

For the ends of the cylinders, two cases of appearance occur. For the large pipe, the cutting planes are perpendicular to the axis. It is unnecessary to specify the shape of the intersection because the projection on the cylinder is made in the plane. However, the outer plane of the small pipe is not perpendicular to its axis. Therefore, the curve must be described by a fine annex mesh.

### 1.2 Material properties

The material is defined with:

Thermal conductivity= $40{\mathrm{X.m}}^{-{\mathrm{1.K}}^{-1}}$

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The calculation will simulate the thermal state of the pipes in case two fluids of different temperature circulate inside. The outside is placed in the ambient air.

The inside of the large pipe is blocked at temperature 600.

The inner side of the small pipe is blocked at temperature 400.

The external sides of the two pipes represent a thermal exchange with the outside. The exchange coefficient is 5 and the external temperature is 300.

The other external surfaces on the pipe sections are free. A normal zero flow is voluntarily imposed to ensure that error indicators are calculated correctly if the problem is to be extended.

## 4 Summary of the results

### 4.1 Adaptation No. 1

The transition from the initial mesh to the No.1 mesh is achieved by systematically cutting all the triangles of the inner faces of the pipes. It is noted that the support of these inner faces remains cylindrical.
