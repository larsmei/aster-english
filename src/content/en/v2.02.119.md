# v2.02.119 SDLL119 - Beams harness under fluid-elastic axial excitation

**Summary**

A square step beam of $3\times 3$ aluminium tubes, placed in a rectangular enclosure and subjected to the action of axial water flow. The tubes contain lead pellets and are held between them at half height by piano strings. This material configuration corresponds to the experimental device of Hotta et al. [bib1] which is used to measure the reduced frequency and damping evolutions of the first mode of bending of the beam, according to the average flow speed.

The purpose of this test case is to validate the MEFISTEAU [R4.07.04](doc:r4.07.04) model's resorption to calculate the modal characteristics of a confined axial beam beam, taking into account fluid-elastic excitation.

The following features to test are:

- DEFI_FLUI_STRU [U4.80.08](doc:u4.80.08) operator: definition of parameters for the consideration of fluid-elastic coupling, in the case of a configuration of the "xial flow tube beam" type (factor keyword FAISCEAU_AXIAL),
- CALC_FLUI_STRU [U4.80.03](doc:u4.80.03) operator: calculation of the evolution of modal reduced frequencies and dampings according to the average flow speed, by implementing the MEFISTEAU model.

These features must be tested with the full beam representation and simplified representation.

The numerical results of the Hotta et al. device simulation are validated by comparison with the experimental results. Given the relatively large uncertainties on the experimental values, the reference results for the non-regression of *Code_Aster* are those obtained numerically during the test case return.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The beam tubes are hollow cylinders with the following dimensions:

| length | $L=1,2m$ |
| --- | --- |
| outer diameter | ${\phi }_{\text{ext}}=9,5\mathrm{mm}$ |
| inner diameter | ${\phi }_{int}=8,5\mathrm{mm}$ |

The piano strings now the tubes between them at mid height are assimilated to cylinders full of $\mathrm{2 }\mathrm{mm}$ diameter.

The beam is square step from $\mathrm{12,6 }\mathrm{mm}$. It is composed of $3\times 3$ tubes and is placed in the center of a rectangular enclosure of dimensions $\mathrm{7,8 }\mathrm{cm}\times \mathrm{4,2 }\mathrm{cm}$.

The roughness of the tube walls is $\varepsilon ={10}^{–5}m$.

| --- | --- |

| density | ${\rho}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| --- | --- |
| kinematic viscosity | ${\nu}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1,1{.10}^{-6}{m}^{2}/s$ |

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and loads

The ends of each tube are connected to fixed supports by metal rods. The relative flexibility of these rods frees the degrees of freedom (DDL) of rotation of the ends of each tube. Therefore, it can be estimated that the tubes are rotulated-rotulated, with the metal rods introducing at each end an additional stiffness of rotation.

In addition, these rods allow axial stress to be applied to the tubes, which can thus be prestressed in traction or compression. The configuration studied corresponds to the beam of prestressed tubes in compression by applying an axial force of $\mathrm{50 }N$ at each of the upper ends of the tubes.

## 3 Modelling A

### 3.1 Modelling characteristics

Each of the beam tubes is represented by 50 straight beam elements of Timoshenko (MECA_POU_D_T), supported by as many mesh cells of SEG2 type (segments to 2 nodes).

A element MECA_DIS_TR is added at each end node; these elements allow the metal rods to be modeled by discrete rotational stiffeners.

The elements of the tubes are assigned the characteristics of circular cross-section:

| external radius | ${R}_{\mathrm{ext}}=4,75{.10}^{–3}m$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| thickness | $e={5.10}^{-4}m$ | (see paragraph [§1.1]) |

These elements are also assigned a ELAS behavioral material:

| Young's modulus | $E=6,89{.10}^{10}\mathrm{Pa}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =\mathrm{0,3 }$ |  |
| density | $\rho =20450\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | (see paragraph [§1.2]) |

The discrete elements shall be assigned a single stiffness rotational axis around the two orthogonal axes at the beam direction axis:

${K}_{r}=6,29\mathrm{N.m}/\mathrm{rad}$

This stiffness rotation was adjusted to correctly recover the frequency value of the first double beam bending mode.

Each piano string is represented by an element MECA_POU_D_T. These elements are assigned the characteristic of a full $R={10}^{-3}m$ circular cross section (see paragraph [§1.1]) and a ELAS behavioral material:

| Young's modulus | $E=2,1{.10}^{11}\mathrm{Pa}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Poisson's ratio | $\nu =0,3$ |  |
| density | $\rho =7800\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | (see paragraph [§1.2]) |

Degrees of freedom translation into $y$ and $z$ ($\mathrm{DY}$ and $\mathrm{DZ}$) from nodes at the ends of each tube are blocked. In order to prevent a rigid body movement (axial translation movement), degrees of freedom $\mathrm{DX}$ is also blocked at the lower ends of each tube. Finally, in each node, degree of freedom rotation is blocked to prevent any twisting movement.

A compression axial force of $\mathrm{26,7 }N$ is applied in each nodes at the upper ends of the tubes. The intensity of the stress has therefore been adjusted to correctly recover the frequency value of the first double mode of beam air bending. This readjustment can be explained by the summary Modelling of the metal rods for support and compression.

The elementary vectors of stress are derived from the nodal forces, then a assembled vector which is constructed according to the numbering of the degrees of freedom of the complete structure. The static deformation due to the compression of the tubes is then obtained by multiplying the assembled vector by the inverse of the structural rigidity matrix. Using this static deformation, a stress field is then calculated at the elements, from which a geometric rigidity matrix is derived. This matrix is then added to the structural rigidity matrix to obtain the rigidity matrix after the compression of the tubes, which is finally used for the calculation of the air modes.

The beam is immersed in a rectangular enclosure of $\mathrm{7,8 }\mathrm{cm}\times \mathrm{4,2 }\mathrm{cm}$ dimensions (see paragraph [§2.1]). The density and kinematic viscosity profiles of the surrounding water are constant along the tubes:

| density | ${\rho}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1000\mathrm{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |  |
| --- | --- | --- |
| kinematic viscosity | ${\nu}_{\mathrm{eau}}=1,1{.10}^{-6}{m}^{2}/s$ | (see paragraph [§1.2]) |

The reduced frequency and damping evolutions of the first two beam bending modes are calculated for average flow speeds ranging from $0$ to $\mathrm{10 }m/s$ by $\mathrm{1 }m/s$ steps. An initial reduced damping of 12.3% is taken into account.

### 3.2 Mesh characteristics

The total number of nodes used for this mesh is 459.

The mesh cells are 470 and SEG2 type.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 3.3 Calculation steps

The functions that we want to validate are those of fluid-structure coupling operators, for configurations of the type "failure of tubes under axial flow".

Initially, the parameters for taking into account fluid-elastic coupling are defined, with the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU factor keyword FAISCEAU_AXIAL.

Then, the evolutions of modal reduced frequencies and dampings are calculated according to the average flow speed, with the operator CALC_FLUI_STRU and the implementation of the MEFISTEAU model.

Modelling A allows you to test these features with the full beam representation. In addition to fluid-structure coupling operators, other resolution and mechanical calculation modules are used.

In our case, the nodes displacement field is calculated by inversion of the structural rigidity matrix and multiplication of the inverse matrix obtained by an effort vector assembled with FACTORISER and RESOUDRE operators.

Then, the geometric rigidity matrix is calculated using a stress field at elements with the CALC_MATR_ELEM operator, option RIGI_GEOM.

- a comparison test with experimental measurements on the first mode,
- a test of the first two modes to guarantee the non-regression of the code.

## 4 Modelling B

### 4.1 Modelling characteristics

Model B is identical to model A (see paragraph [§3.1]), but this time a simplified beam representation is used.

A mesh-cell group corresponding to one of the tubes is defined, which defines the only class of equivalence for all the tubes in the beam. The geometry of the beam is defined by giving the coordinates of the centres of the tubes in the orthogonal plane to the beam. It is then assumed, in the resolution of the fluid-structure coupling, that all the tubes have the same modal deformation, which is that of the tube defining the class of equivalence. The mode studied corresponds to a movement of the beam tubes as a whole.

The reduced frequency and damping evolutions of the first two beam bending modes are calculated for average flow speeds ranging from $0$ to $\mathrm{10 }m/s$ by $\mathrm{1 }m/s$ steps. An initial reduced damping of 12.3% is taken into account.

Given the nature of the mode studied (the beam assembly mode), Modelling B should lead to results very similar to those of Modelling A.

### 4.2 Mesh characteristics

The Mesh characteristics of this second model are the same as the model A, i.e.:

459 nodes used and 470 mesh cells type SEG2.

The mesh file is in ASTER format.

### 4.3 Calculation steps

The functions which l-on wish to validate are the same as those listed for Modelling A (see paragraph [§3.3]), but this time using a simplified beam representation.

The parameters defining the fluid-elastic coupling of a simplified beam representation are defined with the operator DEFI_FLUI_STRU, factor keyword FAISCEAU_AXIAL.

The CALC_FLUI_STRU operator shall perform the calculation of the changes in the modal frequency and dampings as a function of the mean flow speed by implementing the MEFISTEAU model based on a simplified beam representation.

## 5 Remarks

The reference values are those obtained by *Code_Aster* when the test case is returned, which will allow the non-regression of the code to be verified later in its evolution.

The results for reduced damping are very slightly different between modellings A and B. This is explained by the fact that Modelling B, which uses a simplified beam representation, assumes that all tubes have a strictly identical modal deformity: the common deformity is that of a tube chosen arbitrarily in the beam. Modelling A, which uses the complete beam representation, takes into account the infinitesimal variations of the deformity of one tube to another. Therefore, it is normal that very slight differences in the results appear.

In addition, the low differences observed on the results indicate the value of using a simplified beam representation when having combined modes. The gain in calculation time is quite substantial.
