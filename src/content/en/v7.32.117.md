# v7.32.117 WTNP117 – Hair Rebalancing of a Bi-materials described by laws of Van-Genuchten Mualem

**Summary:**

The test presented simulates a case of resaturation of one material by another, the 2 materials being described by a model of Mualem Van-Genuchten. This is a classic case of the BO-BG type which allows us to test the hydraulic law called by HYDR_VGM. It also allows the comparison of several numerical diagrams: elements finished and decentralized finished volumes.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

The field studied consists of two media of $0,5m$ each.

![Figure](/figures/v7.32.117/10000ECC000069D500002148E6A1BB3337C93CB8.svg)

The material $\mathrm{M1}$ will be called the open barrier ($\mathit{BO}$ ) and the material $\mathrm{M2}$ geological barrier ($\mathit{BG}$ )

The saturation and permeability curves follow the Mualem-Van-Genuchten (HYDR_VGM) model. Therefore, the $N$, $\mathrm{Pr}$, $\mathrm{Sr}$, $\mathrm{Smax}$ parameters must be defined in the materials.

It is recalled that these models are:

${S}_{\mathrm{we}}=\frac{S-{S}_{\mathrm{wr}}}{1-{S}_{\mathrm{wr}}}$ et $m=1-\frac{1}{n}$

${S}_{\mathrm{we}}=\frac{1}{{\left[1+{\left[\frac{{P}_{c}}{{P}_{r}}\right]}^{n}\right]}^{m}}$

Water permeability is expressed by integrating the prediction model proposed by Mualem (1976) into the Van Genuchten capillary model.

${k}_{r}^{w}=\sqrt{{S}_{\mathrm{we}}}{(1-{(1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}}^{1/m})}^{m})}^{2}$

Gas permeability is expressed in a similar way:

${k}_{r}^{w}=\sqrt{(1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}})}{(1-{S}_{\mathrm{we}}^{1/m})}^{\mathrm{2m}}$

It is recalled that for $S>\mathrm{Smax}$ , these curves are interpolated by a polynomial of degree 2 $\mathrm{C1}$ in $\mathrm{Smax}$ .

### 1.3 Boundary conditions and initials

We are in boundary conditions: zero stream everywhere (default).

$\mathit{BG}$ is saturated ($S=1$) and $\mathit{BO}$ is partially unsaturated ($S=0,77$). In terms of capillary pressure, this translates into $\mathrm{Pc}=0$ in the $\mathrm{BG}$ and $\mathrm{Pc}=89\mathrm{Mpa}$ in the open barrier.

The following figures show the gas pressure, capillary and saturation profiles along the bi-material at different times:

![Figure](/figures/v7.32.117/10000200000001EA0000012FBA65E18634443F87.png)

The gas pressure profiles are also characteristic of this type of problem: a peak gas pressure is observed at the interface of the two materials, which is due to the fact that the gas is compressed by the water that re-restores the open barrier. If the existence of this peak has a physical reality, its magnitude is due to a problem of numerical schema (known problem) as we will see for the $C$ Modelling.

The oscillations observed on saturations are also well known (we will refer to ). The materials are indeed defined to elements and not to nodes. This means that on an node located at the interface, 2 hair pressure curves are defined and different. This point explains that if the capillary pressure continuity is ensured it is not the same for saturation.

## 6 Summary of the results

This test case meets its primary objective: to test the HYDR_VGM functionality. In addition, it allows you to have a classic $«\mathrm{BO}-\mathrm{BG}»$ Modelling problem with stiff front processing. We do not have reference solutions to compare with, however the results obtained have a classic look at this type of resolution. We therefore make it a case of non-regression.

2 numerical diagrams are tested here: the finished elements and the de-de-cented volumes are. If the overall look of the results is the same for both diagrams, we clearly see that the finite volumes decrease very strongly the peak of gas observed at the interface between the materials. If this peak of gas has a physical reality, its magnitude is greatly amplified by the finished elements diagrams.

For this type of problem, the diagrams of the finite volumes decentralized edges (less expensive than the decentralized mesh cells).
