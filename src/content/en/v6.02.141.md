# v6.02.141 SSNL141 - Multifibre and multimaterial P-rings

**Summary:**

The objective of this test is to validate the use of multimaterial cross section for multifibre beams.

The beam consists of two materials.

Two types of calculation are made:

- a static calculation
- two modal calculations, one with the MASS_MECA option and the other with the MASS_MECA_DIAG option

The reference solutions are derived from Euler's beam theory.

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry

A $2m$ length beam is considered to be $X$ oriented.

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.141/10000000000002250000006D1FF5EB494C6D8F4A.png)

### 1.2 Section characteristics

#### 1.2.1 Geometry and fibre

The section under consideration is a square of $10\mathit{cm}$ side. It is composed of 16 fibres, themselves squared.

#### 1.2.2 Materials

The section has two different materials, as described in the following table.

| **Material** | **Beton** | **Storage** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Young's modulus** | $3\times {10}^{10}\mathit{Pa}$ | $2\times {10}^{11}\mathit{Pa}$ |
| **Poisson's ratio** | $0.2$ | $0.0$ |
| **Volume size** | $2500\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ | $7850\mathit{kg}/{m}^{3}$ |
| **Symbol on figure** | $B$ | $A$ |

Materials are affected on fibres as shown in the figure below:

![Figure](/figures/v6.02.141/10000000000001A10000018EE8C465C2FBC0CC77.png)

The advantage of this structure is to keep the centres of rigidity, gravity, torsion, which are the origin of the local section marker, and to be symmetrical with respect to its centre and with respect to the $Y$ and $Z$ axes.

### 1.3 Loading

#### 1.3.1 boundary conditions

**Static calculus:**

The node $A$ is clamped and the node $B$ is left free.

**modal calculus:**

The node $A$ moves are blocked and the rotation around the beam axis is blocked.

$Y$ and $Z$ moves of node $B$ are blocked as well as rotation around the beam axis.

#### 1.3.2 Forced

**Static calculus:**

A constant distributed force of $-1.0E+04N/m$ according to $Z$ is applied to the beam.

**modal calculus:**

None.

## 2 reference solution

### 2.1 Static calculation: clamped beam

The theory of bending beams provides a reference value for maximum displacement. For one clamped beam at one end and free of the other under distributed load, the maximum displacement at the free end called deflection is given by:

$f=\frac{-{\mathit{qL}}^{4}}{{\mathrm{8EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}}$

- $q$: the force distributed in $N/m$.
- $L$: the length of the beam in $m$.
- ${\mathit{EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}$: in the case of single-materials, it is the product of Young's modulus and quadratic moment.

In the multimaterial case, and with the configuration described above, ${\mathit{EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}$ is calculated as follows:

${\mathit{EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}={\int}_{S}E(s){z(s)}^{2}\mathit{ds}$

In *Code_Aster* this calculation is approached by a sum on the fibres:

${\mathit{EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}={\sum}_{i=1}^{{\mathit{nb}}_{\mathit{fibres}}}{E}_{i}{{z}_{i}}^{2}{A}_{i}$

where ${z}_{i}$ is the $Z$ coordinates of the centre of $i$ fiber and where ${A}_{i}$ is its surface.

### 2.2 Modal calculation: single support beam

For a single support beam, as defined in paragraph 1.3.1. , the beam theory provided with reference values for the modal calculation's eigenfrequency.

The first eigenfrequency is: ${f}_{1}=\frac{\pi}{2{L}^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{{\mathit{EI}}_{\mathit{eq}}}{m}}$ .

where $L$ is the length of the beam and $m$ is the linear mass of the beam.

None.
