# r7.04.03 Post-processing according to the RCC-M

**Summary:**

The operator POST_RCCM [u4.83.11](doc:u4.83.11) makes it possible to check the level-0 criteria and certain level-A criteria of chapter B3200 of the RCC‑M, for modellings of 2D or 3D continuous media. The level-0 criteria aim to protect the equipment against the damage of excessive strain, of plastic instability, and of elastic and elastoplastic instability. The level-A criteria aim, for their part, to protect the equipment against the damage of progressive strain and of fatigue.

It also makes it possible to compute the level-A criteria of chapters B3600 and ZE200 in post-processing of piping analyses.

Finally, it makes it possible to evaluate the environmental-fatigue resistance for chapters B3200 and ZE200.

Furthermore, the operator POST_RCCM makes it possible to compute the crack-initiation factor at a singular zone, in the sense of appendix ZD of the RCC-M.

## 1 Introduction

The RCC-M describes the general rules for the analysis of the behaviour of the equipment of nuclear power plants. These rules aim to ensure for the equipment of PWR plants safety margins sufficient with respect to the various types of damage to which they could be exposed as a result of the loadings applied to them: excessive strain and plastic instability, elastic or elastoplastic instability, progressive strain under the effect of repeated loadings, fatigue (progressive cracking), sudden fracture…

In *code_aster*, it is possible to carry out four types of analysis:

- computation of the level-0 and level-A criteria of chapter B3200 in post-processing of analyses on 2D or 3D structures;
- computation of the fatigue criteria of chapter B3600 in post-processing of piping analyses;
- computation of the fatigue criteria of appendix ZE200 in post-processing of piping analyses. The method according to appendix ZE200 is a mixed method which combines simplified equations of B3600 and a detailed analysis of B3200.
- computation of environmental fatigue compatible with chapters B3200 and appendix ZE200 of the RCC-M. The environmental-fatigue resistance was integrated into the RCC-M, 2016 edition, in the form of a Rule in Probationary Phase (RPP).

r7.04.03-generalites recalls general points on chapter B3200 and appendix ZE200 of the RCC-M.

The criteria of B3200 correspond in the operator POST_RCCM to the methods (TYPE_RESU_MECA) ‘B3200’ and 'EVOLUTION'. Their computation is detailed in r7.04.03-type-b3200 and r7.04.03-type-evolution. 'EVOLUTION' makes it possible to compute an initiation factor for geometric singularities and is well suited to cases where there are few situations. Conversely, 'B3200' is well suited to computations for numerous situations, which may be distributed over several operating groups. The earthquake, the passage situations and the environmental fatigue can be taken into account with this TYPE_RESU_MECA.

The criteria of ZE200 correspond in the operator POST_RCCM to the methods ‘ZE200a' and 'ZE200b'. Their computation is detailed in r7.04.03-types-ze200a-ze200b.

The computation of the environmental-fatigue resistance is described in r7.04.03-fatigue-environnementale.

The fatigue criterion of B3600 corresponds in the operator POST_RCCM to the method ‘B3600’. Their computation is detailed in r7.04.03-type-b3600.

## 2 General points

This chapter aims to recall a few basic definitions associated with chapter B3200 and with appendix ZE200 of the RCC-M. *It does not concern chapter B3600.*

This chapter also makes it possible to describe the necessary adaptations carried out in *code_aster*, which are motivated in  and . The criteria of chapter B3200 correspond in the operator POST_RCCM to the methods TYPE_RESU_MECA=‘EVOLUTION’ and TYPE_RESU_MECA=‘B3200’. The criteria of appendix ZE200 correspond in the operator POST_RCCM to the methods TYPE_RESU_MECA=‘ZE200a’ and TYPE_RESU_MECA=‘ZE200b’.

### 2.1 Geometric data

The user of the RCC-M must distinguish in his structure the zones of major discontinuity, the zones of minor discontinuity and the zones comprising geometric singularities. The latter require a specific treatment (described in part 6.5).

**Current zone (outside a geometric singularity):**

The RCC-M defines “support segments” which serve to linearise the stresses. These segments are, outside the discontinuity zones, segments generally normal to the mean surface of the wall, and in the discontinuity zones, the shortest segments making it possible to join the 2 faces of the wall.

The user of *code_aster* must therefore define the set of the sections of the structure where the post-processing computations will be done (it is he who knows whether these sections pass through current zones, or zones of geometric discontinuity). In practice, one works:

- either on a segment existing in the mesh;
- or on a segment defined in MACR_LIGN_COUPE.

One computes all the criteria systematically at the two ends of the segment (computation of Pm, of the usage factor...).

**Geometric singularity:**

The zones of local discontinuities whose geometric contour presents abrupt variations are the seat of sharp stress concentrations. In this case, the classical methods associated with the current zones are unsuitable and one introduces the notion of initiation factor. This parameter must be computed on a circle (of imposed radius, depending on the material) around the singularity. The initiation factor can be computed only with the type 'EVOLUTION'.

The user must therefore define this cutting line. In practice, one works:

- either on a circular cutting line existing in the mesh;
- or on a circular cutting line defined in MACR_LIGN_COUPE.

### 2.2 Material data

The material data necessary for the computation are the following:

- $S_m$: allowable value (tabulated in the RCC-M Appendix ZI).
- $S_y$: conventional elastic limit (tabulated in the RCC-M Appendix ZI 2.1).
- $m$ *,* $n$: material constants for the computation of $K_e$ (defined in the RCC-M B3234.6).
- $E_c$ *,* $E$: elastic moduli (for the correction of the fatigue curve, appendix ZI).
- Fatigue curves of the material: according to the RCC-M appendix ZI.
- Distance $d$ to the geometric singularity and initiation law of the material (as defined and tabulated in appendix ZD2200 of the RCC-M) for the computation of the initiation factor.

### 2.3 Simplifying hypotheses

In the RCC-M, the user must be able to say, after analysis of the results of the computation, whether the principal directions at a given point are fixed or whether they rotate in the course of time. In the command POST_RCCM, one need not make any hypothesis. One will consider only the case where the principal directions are arbitrary.

Moreover, the user must be able to classify the stresses in the following categories:

- General primary membrane: $P_m$
- Local membrane primary: $P_l$
- Primary bending: $P_b$
- Thermal expansion: $P_e$
- Secondary: $Q$
- Peak: $F$

This choice cannot be made by POST_RCCM. Only the user can qualify a stress field (“primary”, “secondary”, or the sum of the two). The criteria which are to be checked are computed from stress fields (constant or a function of time) supplied by the user. It is he who ensures the consistency between the computation of these fields and the criteria applied.

However, to fix ideas, the classification is simpler in the following cases:

- a constant or variable loading with imposed force or pressure is primary, except for certain very particular structures,
- a constant or variable loading with imposed displacement is in principle secondary (except in the case of the “spring effect”),
- a permanent or transient thermal loading is in principle secondary.

On the other hand, the combination of these types of loadings leads to a result which can no longer be qualified as primary or secondary. According to the criteria, the user may therefore be led to decompose his loadings.

### 2.4 Computations carried out by POST_RCCM

One describes in what follows the operation of the command POST_RCCM making it possible to carry out the computation of certain level-0 and level-A criteria of the RCC-M B3200 and of the level-A criteria of appendix ZE200. The implementation described here does not take into account all the criteria of B3200 and may be completed.

The principal datum is the (support) segment where the computations will be carried out. It is the user who chooses the segment and who has the responsibility of finding the one for which the quantities appearing in the criteria are maximal. The automatic search for this segment is a difficult problem, and is not programmed.

After having computed one or more results by MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE, the user must extract the stresses on the analysis segment by POST_RELEVE_T or MACR_LIGN_COUPE. Finally, the user requests the computation of the criterion or criteria by the operator POST_RCCM.

Four types of criteria are accessible, each by the factor keyword 'OPTION':

- level-0 criteria by the keyword PM_PB,
- level-A criteria (excluding fatigue) by the keyword SN,
- level-A fatigue criteria by the keywords FATIGUE (for the types 'B3200', 'ZE200a' and 'ZE200b') or FATIGUE_ZH210 (for the type ‘EVOLUTION’),
- an environmental-fatigue criterion by the keyword EFAT. This criterion is compatible with the options 'B3200', 'ZE200a' and 'ZE200b'.

Furthermore, with the method ‘EVOLUTION’ only, it is possible to check the initiation criterion (level-A criterion) in a singular zone (keyword AMORCAGE).

### 2.5 Level-0 criteria specified by the RCC-M (keyword PM_PB)

The level-0 criteria aim to protect the equipment against the damage of excessive strain, of plastic instability and of elastic and elastoplastic instability. They must be checked for the reference situation (see B3121 and B3151). These criteria require the computation of the equivalent stresses $P_m$, $P_l$, $P_b$ which are defined below and are available for the types 'B3200' and 'EVOLUTION'.

#### 2.5.1 General primary membrane equivalent stress

Given the primary stress of the reference situation (1st category) and a segment situated outside a zone of major discontinuity. At each end point of this segment of length $l$, one computes $P_m$ and the criterion is written (B3233.1):

$$
P_m \leq S_m
$$

$S_m$ is the allowable equivalent stress, tabulated in appendix ZI of the RCC-M. $S_m$ is defined in the computation by the operand SM of the factor keyword RCCM (or RCCM_FO) of DEFI_MATERIAU. It may be a function of the temperature.

#### 2.5.2 Local membrane primary equivalent stress

Given the primary stress of the reference situation (1st category) and a segment **situated in a zone of major discontinuity**, the definition of $P_l$ is identical to that of $P_m$ on this segment.

The criterion is written (B3233.2):

$$
P_l \leq 1.5\, S_m
$$

#### 2.5.3 Primary membrane+bending equivalent stress

Given the primary stress of the reference situation (1st category) and a (oriented) segment. At each end point of this segment of length $l$ (ends corresponding to the external and internal skins), one computes $P_{mb}$ and $P_{lb}$ and the criteria are written (B3233.3):

$$
P_{mb} \leq 1.5\, S_m
$$

$$
P_{lb} \leq 1.5\, S_m
$$

### 2.6 Level-A criteria specified by the RCC-M (keywords SN and FATIGUE/FATIGUE_ZH210)

The level-A criteria aim to protect the equipment against the damage of progressive strain and of progressive cracking. With the methods 'EVOLUTION', ‘B3200’, 'ZE200a' and 'ZE200b', four types of criteria can be checked:

- Amplitude of variation of the linearised stresses $S_n$ (option ‘SN’);
- Amplitude of variation of $S_n*$ (option ‘SN’);
- Computation of the thermal ratchet (option ‘SN’);
- Computation of the fatigue usage factor (options ‘FATIGUE’/‘FATIGUE_ZH210’)

These various parameters and the associated criteria are described below as defined in the RCC-M. In r7.04.03-Hypotheses-simplificatrices, one introduces a simplifying hypothesis before detailing their computation in *code_aster* in chapters 3, 4 and 6.

#### 2.6.1 Computation of Sn and Sn*

One takes into account the primary plus secondary stresses and the stresses resulting from the constrained thermal expansions: $P_l + P_b + P_e + Q$, which therefore represents the linearised stresses associated with the whole loading (mechanical and thermal).

The computation points are the two ends of the segment. At each end point of this segment of length $l$, one computes $S_n$ according to paragraph B3232.6 and the global shakedown criterion is written (B3234.2):

$$
S_n \leq 3\, S_m
$$

$S_m$ being the allowable stress, a function of the material and of the temperature, given by the operand SM of the factor keyword RCCM (or RCCM_FO) of DEFI_MATERIAU.

If this criterion is not satisfied, one can carry out the simplified elastoplastic analysis of B3234.3. One must carry out the following three operations:

- one computes $S_n*$ the amplitude $S_n$ computed without taking into account the bending stresses of thermal origin and one must check the criterion:

$$
S_n* \leq 3\, S_m
$$

- make an elastoplastic correction ($K_e > 1$) in the fatigue analysis,
- check the thermal-ratchet criterion (B3234.8) in the current parts of the cylindrical shells (and pipes) subjected to a pressure and a cyclic temperature gradient.

#### 2.6.2 Computation of the thermal ratchet

The wall of an apparatus subjected simultaneously to a constant pressure and to cyclic variations of temperature can undergo large strains under thermal ratchet. This is a particular mechanism of progressive strain in which the strain increases by approximately the same quantity at each cycle.

The condition to be respected is written below and concerns the maximum allowable value of the amplitude of variation of the stress of thermal origin, cf. B3234.8. It relates to the case of a shell of revolution loaded by a constant internal pressure. One denotes:

$\sigma_\theta$, maximum allowable value of the amplitude of variation of the stress of thermal origin,

$\sigma_m$, maximum of the general membrane (or mean) stress due to the pressure,

$S_y$, conventional elastic limit read on the tables Z I 2.1, for the maximum temperature reached in the course of the cycle.

The criterion is of the form: $\sigma_\theta = f(\sigma_m, S_y)$. Setting $x = \frac{\sigma_m}{S_y}$ and $y' = \frac{\sigma_\theta}{S_y}$, one has

- if the temperature variation is linear through the wall:

> for $0 < x \leq 0.5$
> $y' = 4(1 - x)$ for $0.5 < x < 1$

- if the temperature variation is parabolic in the wall:

> $y' = 5.2(1 - x)$ for $0.615 \leq x < 1$
> and for $x < 0.615$: linear interpolation between the points: $x = 0.3,\ 0.4,\ 0.5$ and $y' = 4.65,\ 3.55,\ 2.7$.

In summary, the membrane stress $\sigma_m$ is therefore computed by linearisation of the pressure stress, then one deduces from it two quantities $\sigma_{\theta_{\mathrm{LINE}}}$ and $\sigma_{\theta_{\mathrm{PARA}}}$ thanks to the equations above. These two quantities are the respective maximum allowable values of $S_{n_{\text{ther}}}$ and $S_{p_{\text{ther}}}$.

#### 2.6.3 Computation of the fatigue usage factor

The general principle of the fatigue computation consists in combining each of the situations two by two and in making sure that the total usage factor defined in this paragraph is less than 1.

$$
FU_{\text{TOTAL}} < 1
$$

##### 2.6.3.1 Algorithm for computation of the total usage factor

Schematically, the algorithm for computation of $FU_{\text{TOTAL}}$ defined in chapter B3200 of the RCC-M is the following:

1. One computes the elementary usage factor of each combination of situations. The combination of the situations $p$ and $q$ rests on the definition of two fictitious transients 1 and 2. The elementary usage factor is the sum of the usage factors due to each of these transients. At the end of this step, one has a symmetric matrix (nxn) of elementary usage factors (n being the number of situations),

$$
FU_{\text{ELEM}}(p,q) = FU_{\text{transitoire1}} + FU_{\text{transitoire2}}
$$

1. One initialises the total usage factor at 0,

$$
FU_{\text{TOTAL}} = 0
$$

1. One identifies the combination of situations k and l the most penalising (maximum elementary usage factor) and one multiplies it by the minimum of the numbers of occurrences of these two situations,

$$
FU_{\text{TOTAL}} = FU_{\text{TOTAL}} + FU_{\text{ELEM}}(k,l) * \min(n_k,n_l)
$$

1. One updates the number of occurrences of the situations $k$ and $l$,

$$
n_k = n_k - \min(n_k,n_l) \qquad
n_l = n_l - \min(n_k,n_l)
$$

1. Return to step 3 until all the occurrences are exhausted.

The definition of the two fictitious transients constitutes a delicate step of this algorithm. The rule is different according to whether the principal directions are fixed or variable.

$FU_{\text{ELEM}}(p,q)$ the elementary usage factor for a combination of situations $p$ and $q$ is computed by introducing into the fatigue curve of the material (Wöhler curve) the amplitudes of variation of the alternating stresses of the two fictitious transients $S^{1}_{\mathrm{ALT}}(p,q)$ and $S^{2}_{\mathrm{ALT}}(p,q)$.

##### 2.6.3.2 Computation of Salt

$S^{1}_{ALT}(p,q)$ and $S^{2}_{ALT}(p,q)$ are defined from the amplitude of variation of the linearised stresses $S_n(p,q)$ and the amplitude of variation of the total stresses of the two fictitious transients $S_{p^{1}}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^{2}}(p,q)$.

Two formulae are proposed (cf. §B3234.6):

- *KE_MECA:*

$$
S^{1}_{ALT}(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{1}}
$$

$$
S^{2}_{ALT}(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{2}}
$$

The reference Young’s modulus ($E_c$) is supplied by the user in DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keyword E_REFE, of the factor keyword FATIGUE. $K_e$ is the elastoplastic concentration factor, a function of $S_n (p,q)$:

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_e(S_n) = \begin{cases}1 & \text{si } S_n \le 3 \cdot S_m \\ 1+\frac{1-n}{n \cdot (m-1)} \cdot \left(\frac{S_n}{3 \cdot S_m}-1\right)
& \text{si } 3 \cdot S_m<S_n<3 \cdot m \cdot S_m \\ \frac{1}{n}
& \text{si } S_n \ge 3 \cdot m \cdot S_m\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

- The parameters $m$ and $n$ are supplied in DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keywords M_KE and N_KE, of the factor keyword RCCM. If the keywords TEMP_REF_A and TEMP_REF_B are present, $S_n$ is interpolated for this temperature (which must correspond to the mean temperature of the transient). Otherwise, $S_n$ is taken at ambient temperature.

*KE_MIXTE:* since the 1997 modification of the RCC-M, one can choose another formula, based on a decomposition of $S_{ALT}$:

$$
S^1_{ALT}(p,q) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{E_c}{E} \cdot \left( K_{e^{meca}}(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{meca,1}} + K_{e^{ther}}(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{ther,1}} \right)
$$

$$
S^2_{ALT}(p,q) = \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{E_c}{E} \cdot \left( K_{e^{meca}}(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{meca,2}} + K_{e^{ther}}(S_n)\cdot S_{p^{ther,2}} \right)
$$

with:

- $K_e^{meca}(S_n)$ is equal to the $K_e$ defined above,

$$
K_{e^{ther}}(S_n) = \max\left(1.86\left(1-\frac{1}{1.66+\frac{S_n}{S_m}}\right),1\right)
$$

- $S_{p^{meca,1}}$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}$ represent the amplitudes of variation of the mechanical part of the total stresses of the fictitious transients 1 and 2
- $S_{p^{ther,1}}$ and $S_{p^{ther,2}}$ are computed from the total stresses $S_{p^1}$ and $S_{p^2}$ from which one subtracts respectively the stresses of mechanical origin $S_{p^{meca,1}}$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}$.

One finally computes $FU_{ELEM}(p,q)$ the elementary usage factor associated with the combination of the situations $p$ and $q$, defined from the fatigue curve of the material $N_{adm}=f(S_{alt})$:

$$
FU_{ELEM}(p,q) = FU_{transitoire1} + FU_{transitoire2} = f\left(S_{ALT}^1\right) + f\left(S_{ALT}^2\right)
$$

1. In order to compute the elementary usage factor of a situation $p$ combined with itself, in KE_MECA,

1. In the RCC-M, the contribution of the sub-cycles is also taken into account in the elementary usage factor. In code_aster, this quantity is not taken into account.

**Note**

$S_{ALT}^1(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e(S_n(p,p))\cdot S_p(p,p)$ and $S_{ALT}^2(p,q)=0$.

In KE_MIXTE,

$S_{ALT}^1(p,q)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot\left(K_{e^{meca}}(S_n(p,p))\cdot S_{p^{meca}}(p,p)+K_{e^{ther}}(S_n(p,p))\cdot S_{p^{ther}}(p,p)\right)$ and $S_{ALT}^2(p,q)=0$.

## 3 Type 'B3200'

### 3.1 Loading data

The type ‘B3200’ is well suited to computations on a component subjected to numerous situations. Several operating groups can also be defined, with possibly passage situations between these groups. Sharing groups and an earthquake can also be taken into account.

A situation is defined by its thermal, pressure and mechanical loading (forces and moments). In code_aster the loadings can be entered in various forms.

- the thermal part can be entered only in the form of a transient $\sigma_{ther}$ ( RESU_THER )

- in the form of a transient $\sigma_{pres}$ ( RESU_PRES )
- in the form of a unit loading $\sigma^p$ with two pressures $P_A$ and $P_B$ for the stabilised states ( RESU_MECA_UNIT, PRES_A and PRES_B ),

**the pressure loadings can be entered in two ways:**

- in the form of a transient $\sigma_{meca}$ ( RESU_MECA )
- with unit loadings (unit global forces and moments applied at the boundaries of the model) with two resultants for the stabilised states (CHAR_ETAT_A and CHAR_ETAT_B). These forces can either be computed with *code_aster*, or come from the OAR database.
- with unit loadings to which one applies a resultant; this resultant is computed by interpolation between two resultants ( CHAR_ETAT_A and CHAR_ETAT_B ) which correspond to the temperatures TEMP_A and TEMP_B and thanks to the temperature in the course of the situation TABL_TEMP.

**the stresses linked to the mechanical loadings (forces and moments):**

*All the types of situations can be combined in code_aster*. For example, the user can enter a first situation of which all the loadings are in the form of a transient, then a second situation of which the thermal part and the pressure are in the form of a transient and the mechanics with unit loadings.

**For the sake of clarity, only the equations in the case where all the situations are described by transient loadings are presented in this chapter**.

Appendix 1 gives the equations in the case where all the situations are described in unit form. Appendix 2 gives the equations in the case where all the situations are described in unit form with interpolation on the temperature.

In this chapter, all the operating situations can be decomposed into transients, that is evolutions of the stresses due to the various loadings as a function of time:

- a transient which is the sum of the transients due to the forces and to the moments (defined under 'RESU_MECA'),
- a transient due to the pressure (defined under 'RESU_PRES'),
- a thermal transient (defined under 'RESU_THER').

- For the branch connections, the user must then enter under RESU_MECA a transient mechanical tensor which is the sum of the two force tensors associated with the body and with the nozzle. Appendix 3 summarises the equations in this case.
- The thermal transient, the pressure transient and the mechanical transient corresponding to a situation must be defined at the same time instants.

**Notes**

### 3.2 Computations carried out with the option 'PM_PB'

For the moment, this option is available if all the mechanical and pressure loadings of the situations are in unit form.

Given the primary stress of the reference situation (1st category) and a segment situated outside a zone of major discontinuity. At each end point of this segment of length $l$, one computes for a situation:

With $\sigma_{ij}=\sigma_{ij,pres}+\sigma_{ij,meca}$

$$
P_m=\max_t\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{moy}\rVert_{TRESCA}
\quad\text{avec}\quad
\sigma_{ij}^{moy}=\frac{1}{l}\int_0^l\sigma_{ij}\,ds
\quad\text{et}\quad
\lVert\tau_{ij}\rVert_{TRESCA}=\max_{I,J}|\tau_I-\tau_J|
$$

$$
(\tau_{II=1,3}\ \text{étant les contraintes principales})
$$

$$
P_b=\max_t\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{fle}\rVert_{TRESCA}
\quad\text{avec}\quad
\sigma_{ij}^{fle}=\frac{6}{l^2}\int_0^l\left(s-\frac{l}{2}\right)\sigma_{ij}\,ds
$$

$$
P_{mb}=\max_t\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{lin}\rVert_{TRESCA}
\quad\text{avec}\quad
\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(s=0)=\sigma_{ij}^{moy}-\sigma_{ij}^{fle}
$$

### 3.3 Computations carried out with the option ‘SN’

The computation points are the two ends of the segment. *For a given situation*, at each end point of this segment of length $l$, one computes $S_n$ according to paragraph B3232.6:

$$
S_n=\max_{t_1,t_2}\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2)\rVert_{TRESCA}
\quad\text{où}\quad
\sigma^{lin}(s=0)=\sigma^{moy}-\sigma^{fle}
\quad\text{et}\quad
\sigma^{lin}(s=l)=\sigma^{moy}+\sigma^{fle}
$$

In the case where the situation is described instantaneously (cf. r7.04.03-donnees-chargement), this definition is applicable directly.

In code_aster, when the situation is defined in a unit manner with two stabilised states and a thermal transient, the formula has been adapted by introducing a method of selection of the time instants. This method is described in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants.

#### 3.3.1 Method of selection of the time instants

In the ‘B3200’ method developed in *code_aster*, a method of selection of the time instants has been implemented ('TRESCA' under the keyword 'METHODE')

One will thus suppose that the time instants which correspond to the extrema of the amplitude of variation of the stresses (linearised or total) of the combination of two situations are also the time instants corresponding to the extrema of the stresses of each of the situations taken alone.

Four time instants $t^{maxSn}$, $t^{minSn}$, $t^{maxSp}$ and $t^{minSp}$ are therefore identified beforehand for each situation. One denotes by $\sigma_{trans}$ the stress tensor which is the sum of the tensors in transient form.

$t^{maxSn}$ and $t^{minSn}$ correspond to the extrema of the linearised transient stress of the situation, in the sense of a Tresca equivalent stress signed by the trace of the stresses:

$$
t^{minSn}=\operatorname*{Argmin}\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t)\rVert\cdot\operatorname{sgn}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t)\right)\right)\right)
$$

$$
t^{maxSn}=\operatorname*{Argmax}\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t)\rVert\cdot\operatorname{sgn}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t)\right)\right)\right)
$$

$t^{maxSp}$ and $t^{minSp}$ correspond to the extrema of the total transient stress of the situation, in the sense of a Tresca equivalent stress signed by the trace of the stresses:

$$
t^{minSp}=\operatorname*{Argmin}\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran}(t)\rVert\cdot\operatorname{sgn}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{tran}(t)\right)\right)\right)
$$

$$
t^{maxSp}=\operatorname*{Argmax}\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran}(t)\rVert\cdot\operatorname{sgn}\left(\operatorname{Tr}\left(\sigma_{tran}(t)\right)\right)\right)
$$

**Note**

There are in reality 2x4 time instants which are identified beforehand for each situation: at the origin and at the end of the analysis segment.

This method of selection of the time instants allows a non-negligible gain in time. It is available by choosing the value 'TRESCA' under the keyword 'METHODE'. The method of selection of the time instants by the signed Tresca is taken by default if the user specifies nothing. But it may lack robustness in the case notably where the frame of the principal stresses rotates.

It is then possible to test all the time instants of the transients, by choosing the value 'TOUT_INST' under the keyword 'METHODE'. One cannot differentiate the method of selection of the time instants for Sn and Sp.

#### 3.3.2 Computation of Sn

One denotes by $\sigma_{tran}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with the situation; and $t^{maxSn}$ and $t^{minSn}$ the extremal time instants as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants.

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$$
S_n=\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{maxSn})-\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{minSn})\rVert
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$$
S_n=\max_{t_1,t_2}\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_2)\rVert
$$

**Note**

In this case, $\sigma_{tran}(t)=\sigma_{ther}(t)+\sigma_{pres}(t)+\sigma_{meca}(t)$.

#### 3.3.3 Computation of Sn*

One denotes by $S_{n^*}$ the amplitude $S_n$ computed without taking into account the bending stresses of thermal origin. One translates this definition by:

- At the origin of the segment:

$$
S_{n^*}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_1)+\sigma_{ther}^{fle}(t_1)-\left(\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_2)+\sigma_{ther}^{fle}(t_2)\right)\rVert
$$

- At the end of the segment:

$$
S_{n^*}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\lVert\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ther}^{fle}(t_1)-\left(\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t_2)-\sigma_{ther}^{fle}(t_2)\right)\rVert
$$

with $\sigma_{ther}^{fle}=\frac{6}{l^2}\int_0^l\left(s-\frac{l}{2}\right)\sigma_{th}\,ds$

#### 3.3.4 Computation of the thermal ratchet

One must have previously defined the conventional elastic limit for the maximum temperature reached in the course of the cycle, either by the operand SY_MAX of POST_RCCM, or by the operand SY_02 of the keyword RCCM in DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01). If no elastic limit is defined, the computation of the thermal ratchet is impossible.

In the table generated by the command there appear, for each end of each analysis segment, for the situations and for the combinations of situations:

- the maximum of the general membrane stress due to the pressure $\sigma_m$ ( 'SIG_PRES_MOY ')
- the linearised amplitude of variation of the stress of thermal origin $Sn_{ther}$ and its maximum allowable value $\sigma_{\theta_{LINE}}$ ( 'SN_THER' and 'CRIT_LINE' )
- the amplitude of variation of the stress of thermal origin $Sp_{ther}$ and its maximum allowable value $\sigma_{\theta_{PARA}}$ ( 'SP_THER' and 'CRIT_PARAB' )

### 3.4 Computations carried out with the option ‘FATIGUE’

Recall that the computation of the elementary usage factor requires beforehand the computation of the amplitude of variation of the linearised stresses $S_n$ and of the total stresses $S_p$ for each of the combinations of situations (see r7.04.03-calcul-facteur-usage-fatigue). This computation is carried out successively for the situations inside each group with or without earthquake, then for the combinations of passage situations between groups of situations.

One then uses a method of cumulation of the elementary usage factors, based on the hypothesis of the linear cumulation of damage, in order to obtain the global usage factor.

#### 3.4.1 Combination of the situations inside each group of situations

##### 3.4.1.1 Computation of Sn

One must not forget the case where the most penalising combination of the linearised stresses corresponds to the two extrema of the same situation. For the combination of the situations $p$ and $q$:

$$
S_n=max\left(S_n(p,p),S_n(q,q),S_n(p,q)\right)
$$

The quantities $S_n(p,p)$ and $S_n(q,q)$ are computed according to r7.04.03-calculs-effectues-sn and the computation of the quantity $S_n(p,q)$ is described in the remainder of this part.

##### 3.4.1.2 Computation of Sn (p,q)

One denotes by $\sigma_{tran,p}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with the situation $p$ and by $\sigma_{tran,q}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with the situation $q$. $t_{p^{maxSn}}$ and $t_{p^{minSn}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $p$ and $t_{q^{maxSn}}$ and $t_{q^{minSn}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $q$ as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants.

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameter $S_n$ for the combination of the situations p and q is defined by:

$$
S_n(p,q)=\max\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}^{lin}\left(t_{p^{maxSn}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,q}^{lin}\left(t_{q^{minSn}}\right)\rVert,\;\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}^{lin}\left(t_{q^{maxSn}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,p}^{lin}\left(t_{p^{minSn}}\right)\rVert\right)
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_n$ for the combination of the situations p and q is defined by:

$$
S_n(p,q)=\max_{t_p,t_q}\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}^{lin}(t_p)-\sigma_{tran,q}^{lin}(t_q)\rVert
$$

**Note**

In this case, $\sigma_{tran}(t)=\sigma_{ther}(t)+\sigma_{pres}(t)+\sigma_{meca}(t)$ .

##### 3.4.1.3 Computation of Sp

One in fact computes the amplitude of variation of the total stresses of the two fictitious transients $S_{p^1}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^2}(p,q)$. One must not forget the case where the most penalising combination of the total stresses corresponds to the two extrema of the same situation. One therefore modifies the definition of $S_{p^1}$ and of $S_{p^2}$ as follows:

$$
S_{p^{1}}=\max\left(S_p(p,p),S_p(q,q),S_{p^{1}}(p,q)\right)
$$

If $S_{p^{1}}=S_{p^{1}}(p,q)$, then $S_{p^{2}}=S_{p^{2}}(p,q)$;

If $S_{p^{1}}=S_p(p,p)$, then $S_{p^{2}}=S_p(q,q)$;

If $S_{p^{1}}=S_p(q,q)$, then $S_{p^{2}}=S_p(p,p)$.

The quantities $S_p(p,p)$ and $S_p(q,q)$ are computed according to Appendix 4 and the computation of the quantities $S_{p^1}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^2}(p,q)$ is described in the remainder of this part.

##### 3.4.1.4 Computation of $S_{p^1}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^2}(p,q)$

One denotes by $\sigma_{tran,p}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with the situation $p$ and by $\sigma_{tran,q}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with the situation $q$. $t_{p^{maxSp}}$ and $t_{p^{minSp}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $p$ and $t_{q^{maxSp}}$ and $t_{q^{minSp}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $q$ as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants.

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameters $S_{p^1}$ and $S_{p^2}$ for the combination of the situations $p$ and $q$ are defined by:

$$
S_{p^{1}}(p,q)=\max\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}\left(t_{p^{maxSp}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,q}\left(t_{q^{minSp}}\right)\rVert,\;\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}\left(t_{q^{maxSp}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,p}\left(t_{p^{minSp}}\right)\rVert\right)
$$

$$
S_{p^{2}}(p,q)=\min\left(\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}\left(t_{p^{maxSp}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,q}\left(t_{q^{minSp}}\right)\rVert,\;\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}\left(t_{q^{maxSp}}\right)-\sigma_{tran,p}\left(t_{p^{minSp}}\right)\rVert\right).
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_{p^1}$ for the combination of the situations p and q is defined by:

$$
S_{p^1}(p,q)=\max_{t_1,t_2}\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_2)\rVert
$$

If $t_{1^p}$ and $t_{1^q}$ are the time instants of fictitious transient 1, then one determines the time instants of fictitious transient 2 $t_{2^p}$ and $t_{2^q}$ according to the method described in Appendix 5 and the quantity $S_{p^2}(p,q)$ is equal to:

$$
S_{p^2}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{2^p})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{2^q})\rVert
$$

- The user has the possibility of entering stress indices under the keyword INDI_SIGM in order to compare the results obtained with the method 'ZE200a' or 'ZE200b'. The corresponding equations are described in Appendix 6.
- In this part, $\sigma_{tran}(t)=\sigma_{ther}(t)+\sigma_{pres}(t)+\sigma_{meca}(t)$ .

**Note**

##### 3.4.1.5 Computation of $Sp^{méca}$ and $Sp^{ther}$

In the case where one uses the KE_MIXTE method, one must decompose the amplitude of variation of the stresses into a mechanical part and a thermal part. For the definition of $S_{p^{ca}}$, the RCC-M (§B3234.6) leaves the choice between (cf. §2.6.3.1):

- taking the mechanical part of the amplitude of the maximum stresses between the two transients;
- taking the maximum value of the amplitude of the mechanical stresses in the course of these transients.

It is this latter method, more conservative but simpler to implement, which has been retained.

One must not forget the case where the most penalising combination of the total stresses corresponds to the two extrema of the same situation. One therefore modifies the definition of $S_{p^{meca,1}}$ and of $S_{p^{meca,2}}$ as follows:

If $S_{p^1}=S_{p^1}(p,q)$, then $S_{p^{meca,1}}=S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}=S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)$.

If $S_{p^1}=S_p(p,p)$, then $S_{p^{meca,1}}=S_p^{meca}(p,p)$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}=S_p^{meca}(q,q)$.

If $S_{p^1}=S_p(q,q)$, then $S_{p^{meca,1}}=S_p^{meca}(q,q)$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}=S_p^{meca}(p,p)$.

The quantities $S_{p^{meca}}(p,p)$ and $S_{p^{meca}}(q,q)$ are computed according to Appendix 4 and the computation of the quantities $S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)$ is described in the remainder of this part. The amplitudes of thermal stress are equal to $S_{p^{ther,1}}=\max\left(0,S_{p^{1}}-S_{p^{meca,1}}\right)$ and $S_{p^{ther,2}}=\max\left(0,S_{p^{2}}-S_{p^{meca,2}}\right)$.

One denotes by $t_{p^{maxSp}}$ and $t_{p^{minSp}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $p$ and $t_{q^{maxSp}}$ and $t_{q^{minSp}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of the situation $q$ as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants.

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameters $S_{p^1}$ and $S_{p^2}$ for the combination of the situations $p$ and $q$ are defined by:

If $S_{p^{1}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{p^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{q^{minSp}})\rVert$, then

$S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{p^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{q^{minSp}})\rVert$

and

$S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{q^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{p^{minSp}})\rVert$.

If $S_{p^1}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{q^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{p^{minSp}})\rVert$, then

$S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{q^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{p^{minSp}})\rVert$

and

$S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{p^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{q^{minSp}})\rVert$.

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the time instants $t_{1^p}$, $t_{1^q}$, $t_{2^p}$, $t_{2^q}$, defining the fictitious transients 1 and 2 (cf. r7.04.03-calcul-sp) also enter into the parameters $S_{p^{meca,1}}$ and $S_{p^{meca,2}}$

$$
S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{1^{p}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{1^{q}})\rVert
$$

$$
S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)=\lVert\sigma_{meca+pression,p}(t_{2^{p}})-\sigma_{meca+pression,q}(t_{2^{q}})\rVert
$$

##### 3.4.1.6 Computation of $S_{ALT}$ and $FU_{ELEM}$

The stress amplitudes $S_n$, $S_{p^1}$ and $S_{p^2}$ ($S_{p^{meca1}}$, $S_{p^{meca2}}$, $S_{p^{ther1}}$ and $S_{p^{ther2}}$ if KE_MIXTE) make it possible to arrive at the stress amplitudes $S_{ALT}^1$ and $S_{ALT}^2$ according to the equations of r7.04.03-calcul-salt.

One deduces from them, via the Wöhler curve f, the numbers of allowable cycles $N_{adm}^{1}$ and $N_{adm}^{2}$ such that $N_{adm}^{1}=f(S_{ALT}^1)$ and $N_{adm}^{2}=f(S_{ALT}^2)$.

The elementary usage factor of the combination of situations is then equal to

$$
FU_{ELEM}=FU_1+FU_2=\frac{1}{N_{adm}^{1}}+\frac{1}{N_{adm}^{2}}
$$

#### 3.4.2 Passage situations

Two situations p and q are combinable only if they belong to the same group or if there exists a passage situation between the groups to which they belong. In this latter case, one will associate with the combination of the situations p and q the number of occurrences of the passage situation. Once this number of occurrences $N_{pass}$ is exhausted then these situations are no longer combinable. An example is given in r7.04.03-stockage.

A passage situation can link at most 20 operating groups. The passage situation must belong to all the groups that it links.

Several passage situations can be declared at the same time. If several passage situations link the same groups, one will take the number of occurrences of the one which, moreover, gives the least penalising elementary usage factor.

#### 3.4.3 Sharing group

Situations belonging to one and the same sharing group share their number of occurrences. This sharing group is numbered under the keyword 'NUME_PARTAGE' and has nothing to do with the operating group under 'NUME_GROUPE'. A given situation can belong to only one sharing group.

#### 3.4.4 Management of the sub-cycles

The method of computation of the stress amplitude with the fictitious transients selects a pair of time instants. One must also take into account the sub-cycles that each situation comprised. The user has this possibility by specifying SOUS_CYCL = 'OUI' but only when METHODE='TOUT_INST'

At the stage of computation of the quantities for the situations alone, once the pair of extremal time instants has been found, one also extracts the sub-cycles of each situation. For example, for a situation $p$, $n_p$ sub-cycles $Sp_{i,p}$ are extracted and for a situation $q$, $n_q$ sub-cycles $Sp_{j,q}$ are extracted.

To the elementary usage factor computed previously, one then adds the contribution of the sub-cycles,

$$
FU_{ELEM}=FU_1+FU_2+FU_{souscycl}
$$

This contribution is a function of the sub-cycles extracted previously and of the equivalent Ke which served for the combination of the situations.

$$
FU_{souscycl}=\sum_{i=1}^{n_p}\left(\frac{1}{N_i}\right)+\sum_{j=1}^{n_q}\left(\frac{1}{N_j}\right)
\quad\text{avec}\quad
N_i=f(S_{ALT,i})
\quad\text{et}\quad
N_j=f(S_{ALT,j}),
$$

$$
S_{ALT,i}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{E_c}{E}K_e\left(S_n(p,q)\right)S_{p_i}
\quad\text{et}\quad
S_{ALT,j}=\frac{1}{2}\frac{E_c}{E}K_e\left(S_n(p,q)\right)S_{p_j}.
$$

#### 3.4.5 Storage

In order to carry out the computation of the total usage factor, the elementary usage factors computed previously and the associated numbers of occurrences are stored in a square matrix containing all the elementary usage factors $FU$ excluding the earthquake, for all the possible combinations of situations, that is to say inside each group of situations, and between two groups if there exists a passage situation. The matrix has as its dimension the sum of the number of situations of all the groups and, being symmetric, one fills it only above the diagonal.

#### 3.4.6 Taking the earthquake into account

When an earthquake is taken into account, one builds a second matrix of the elementary usage factors with earthquake. This matrix is filled in the same way as the matrix without earthquake, taking into account only the possible combinations (groups and passage situations).

Finally, the elementary usage factors with earthquake are the sum of the usage factor of the combination of the situation $p$ and $q$ with earthquake, of the contribution of the sub-cycles of the situations (cf. r7.04.03-gestion-sous-cycles) and of the contribution of the seismic sub-cycles $FU_{ss}$.

$FU_{ELEM}(p,q,S) = FU(p,q,S) + FU_{souscycl} + FU_{ss}$

with $FU_{ss} = (2n_s-1) FU(S)$ where $FU(S)$ is the usage factor due to the earthquake alone and $n_s$ is the number of seismic sub-cycles ('NB_CYCL_SEISME').

If the user has chosen 'SOUS_CYCL' = 'OUI', for the computation of $FU_{souscycl}$, one takes up again the method applied in r7.04.03-gestion-sous-cycles, taking the Ke which depends on the earthquake only.

In order to obtain $FU(p,q,S)$, the second phase consists in computing the stress amplitudes which correspond to the combinations of the situations of a given group, taking the seismic loadings into account.

Depending on the definition of the situations, the earthquake can also be defined in two different ways:

- unit form: it is described by a mechanical state (S) and the corresponding resultant $\left\{F_{X^{s}},F_{Y^{s}},F_{Z^{s}},M_{X^{s}},M_{Y^{s}},M_{Z^{s}}\right\}$ under CHAR_ETAT; the keyword 'RESU_MECA_UNIT' must be supplied.
- with six tensors corresponding to the forces and the moments $\sigma_{FX,S}$, $\sigma_{FY,S}$, $\sigma_{FZ,S}$, $\sigma_{MX,S}$, $\sigma_{MY,S}$, $\sigma_{MZ,S}$

**Note**

For the branch connections, the force resultant goes from 6 to 12 components in unit form and the number of tensors goes from 6 to 12 in instantaneous form.

The seismic loadings are not signed. Each component of the stress tensor can therefore take two values (positive and negative). During the superposition of an unsigned loading with a signed loading, the RCC-M requires that one retain, on each component, a sign such that the computed stress (in fact $S_{ALT}$) is maximised.

The parameters $S_{p^1}(p,q,S)$, $S_{p^2}(p,q,S)$, $S_n(p,q,S)$ and $S_{alt}(p,q,S)$ with earthquake are computed in the same way as without earthquake, but by maximising the amplitude of the stresses with respect to all the possibilities of sign. In order to understand better, an example is given in the remainder of this paragraph; more equations with the earthquake are summarised in Appendix 7.

For example, for the time-instant selection method 'TOUT_INST',

$$
S_{p^{1}}(p,q,S)=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_2)\pm\sigma_{FX,S}\pm\sigma_{FY,S}\pm\sigma_{FZ,S}\pm\sigma_{MX,S}\pm\sigma_{MY,S}\pm\sigma_{MZ,S}\right\rVert
$$

#### 3.4.7 Computation of the total usage factor

If one has N situations, at the end of the preceding steps one therefore has:

- a matrix $[N,N]$ of the elementary usage factors $FU_{ELEM}(p,q,S)$ with earthquake
- a matrix $[N,N]$ of the elementary usage factors $FU_{ELEM}(p,q)$ without earthquake.

One denotes:

| $n_p$ | the number of occurrences associated with the situation $p$ |
| --- | --- |
| $n_q$ | the number of occurrences associated with the situation $q$ |
| $N_s$ | the number of occurrences of the earthquake |
| $n_{pass}$ | number of cycles associated with a possible passage situation between $p$ and $q$ if these situations do not belong to one and the same group |

1. One initialises the total usage factor $FU_{TOTAL} = 0$

  - If $\frac{N_s}{2} > 0$, one looks in the table $FU_{ELEM}(p,q,S)$ for the largest elementary usage factor with earthquake
  - If $\frac{N_s}{2} = 0$, one goes to step 8
3. One multiplies this elementary usage factor $FU_{ELEM}(p,q,S)$ by its number of occurrences

  - $n_{occ}=\min\left\{n_p,n_q, \frac{N_s}{2}\right\}$ in general
  - $n_{occ}=\min\left\{n_p,n_q, \frac{N_s}{2}, n_{pass}\right\}$ if the situations p and q are linked only by a passage situation
4. One obtains the partial usage factor due to this combination $FU_{partiel} = n_{occ} * FU_{ELEM}(p,q,S)$.
5. One increments the total usage factor with the partial usage factor found at the preceding step $FU_{TOTAL} = FU_{TOTAL} + FU_{partiel}$
6. One updates the numbers of occurrences

  - In general, $n_p = n_p - n_{occ}$, $n_q = n_q - n_{occ}$ and $\frac{N_s}{2} = \frac{N_s}{2} - n_{occ}$.
  - If a situation has provided the passage, one also updates $n_{pass} = n_{pass} - n_{occ}$
7. If situation p or situation q belongs to a sharing group, one updates the numbers of occurrences of the situations of this same sharing group. Then one resumes the loop at step 2.
8. One looks in the table $FU_{ELEM}(p,q)$ for the largest elementary usage factor
9. One multiplies this elementary usage factor $FU_{ELEM}(p,q)$ by its number of occurrences

  - $n_{occ}=\min\left\{n_p,n_q\right\}$ in general
  - $n_{occ}=\min\left\{n_p,n_q,n_{pass}\right\}$ if the situations p and q are linked only by a passage situation
10. One obtains the partial usage factor due to this combination $FU_{partiel} = n_{occ} * FU(p,q)$
11. One increments the total usage factor with the partial usage factor found at the preceding step $FU_{TOTAL} = FU_{TOTAL} + FU_{partiel}$
12. One updates the numbers of occurrences

  - In general, $n_p = n_p - n_{occ}$, $n_q = n_q - n_{occ}$.
  - If a situation has provided the passage, one also updates $n_{pass} = n_{pass} - n_{occ}$
13. If situation $p$ or situation $q$ belongs to a sharing group, one updates the numbers of occurrences of the situations of this same sharing group. Then one resumes the loop at step 8 until exhaustion of all the numbers of occurrences of all the situations.

**Note**

Appendix ZI of the RCC-M code defines the Wöhler curves down to a minimum stress amplitude corresponding to a life of 10^6 cycles. If the value $S'_{alt}$ computed for a combination $(i,j)$ of stabilised state is less than this minimum amplitude, the usage factor is equal to 0 for the combination $(i,j)$ considered.

## 4 Types 'ZE200a' and 'ZE200b'

### 4.1 Loading data

The types ‘ZE200a’ and 'ZE200b' are well suited to computations on a piping line or a branch connection subjected to numerous situations. Several operating groups can also be defined, with possibly passage situations between these groups. Sharing groups and an earthquake can also be taken into account.

Each situation is described by two stabilised states and a thermal transient. The stabilised states describe the loadings due to the moments via a resultant $\left\{M_X, M_Y, M_Z \right\}$ .

The pressure can be described in two different ways:

- 'ZE200a': it is associated with the stabilised states, which are then defined by a pressure $p$
- 'ZE200b': it is in the form of a transient under the keyword 'RESU_PRES'.

The use of these options requires the prior computation of the stress fields for each of the thermal transients; these fields are to be supplied on the analysis segment at the time instants of the discretisation of the computation by means of tables.

Additional data are necessary for the computation according to Appendix ZE200 of the RCC-M. These data enter into the simplified equations taken from chapter B3600. They are the following:

- Geometric characteristics of the piping: thickness EP, radius R and moment of inertia I under the keyword TUYAU.
- Stress indices of paragraph B3680 of the RCC-M: C1, C2, C3, K1, K2, K3 under the keyword INDI_SIGM

## 5 Environmental fatigue

The taking into account of the environmental effects on the fatigue resistance in code_aster is available for the types B3200, ZE200a and ZE200b. This taking into account is carried out after the combination of the situations and the computation of the usual usage factor described in r7.04.03-calcul-facteur-usage-total .

### 5.1 Computation of the FEN

The environmental factor for the combination of the situations $p$ and $q$ is expressed as a function of $\Delta\varepsilon$ the strain increment, of $F$ the partial environmental factor and of the time instants $t_k$ and $t_l$ . The index $k$ runs over the time instants of the transient of $p$ and the index $l$ runs over the time instants of the transient of $q$ .

$$
FEN_{comb}(p,q)=\frac{\sum_k ?\,\Delta\varepsilon(t_k)+\sum_l ?\,\Delta\varepsilon(t_l)}{\sum_k F(t_k)\Delta\varepsilon(t_k)+\sum_l F(t_l)\Delta\varepsilon(t_l)}
$$

Whatever the material studied, the general form of the partial environmental factor $F$ is the following:

$$
F(t_k)=\exp\left[\left(A+B\,\dot{\varepsilon}^{*}(t_k)\right)S^{*}O^{*}T^{*}+C\right]
$$

- A, B and C are constants which depend on the nature of the material: ferritic, austenitic, nickel-base (keywords A_ENV, B_ENV and C_ENV),
- $S^*$ is the sulphur content of the metal analysed, therefore common to all the situations (keyword S_ETOILE),
- $O^*$ is the degree of dissolved oxygen in the water in contact with the section analysed. This quantity can be different for each situation (keyword O_ETOILE),
- $T^*$ is a function which depends on the mean temperature T. *The user must therefore supply a table which contains the evolution of the temperature in the course of the transient* (keyword TABL_TEMP).

$$
T=\frac{T(t_k)+T(t_{k-1})}{2}
$$

The function $T^*$ is described below and depends on the thresholds $T_{seuil,sup}$ and $T_{seuil,inf}$ (keywords SEUIL_T_SUP and SEUIL_T_INF) and on the threshold values $T_{sup}$ , $T_{inf}$ , $T_{moy,num}$ and $T_{moy,den}$ (keywords VALE_T_SUP, VALE_T_INF, VALE_T_MOY_NUM and VALE_T_MOY_DEN):

$$
\begin{aligned}
T^{*}=\begin{cases}T_{inf} & \text{si } T>T_{seuil,sup} \\ \frac{T-T_{moy,num}}{T_{moy,den}} & \text{si } T_{seuil,inf}\le T\le T_{seuil,sup} \\ T_{sup} & \text{si } T<T_{seuil,inf}\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

- $\dot{\varepsilon}^{*}$ is a function which depends on the strain rate $\dot{\varepsilon}$ . This function is described below and depends on the thresholds $\varepsilon_{seuil,sup}$ and $\varepsilon_{seuil,inf}$ (keywords SEUIL_EPSI_SUP and SEUIL_EPSI_INF)

$$
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\varepsilon}^{*}=\begin{cases}
0 & \text{si } \dot{\varepsilon}>\varepsilon_{seuil,sup} \\
\ln\left(\frac{\dot{\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon_{seuil,sup}}\right) & \text{si } \varepsilon_{seuil,inf}\le\dot{\varepsilon}\le\varepsilon_{seuil,sup} \\
\ln\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{seuil,inf}}{\varepsilon_{seuil,sup}}\right) & \text{si } \dot{\varepsilon}<\varepsilon_{seuil,inf}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

The strain rate $\dot{\varepsilon}$ is equal to:

$$
\dot{\varepsilon}(t_k)=\frac{\Delta\varepsilon(t_k)}{t_k-t_{k-1}}
$$

The strain increment $\Delta\varepsilon$ enters both into the expression of the environmental factor of the situation $FEN$ and into the partial environmental factor $F(t_k)$ via $\dot{\varepsilon}$ . $\Delta\varepsilon$ is computed from the stress tensor in transient form $\sigma_{tran}$ in the following way: one computes the tensor $\Delta\sigma$ such that

$$
\Delta\sigma(t_k)=\sigma_{tran}(t_k)-\sigma_{tran}(t_{k-1})
$$

After diagonalisation,

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\sigma(t_k)=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_1(t_k)&0&0\\0&\sigma_2(t_k)&0\\0&0&\sigma_3(t_k)\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{avec}\qquad\sigma_1>\sigma_2>\sigma_3
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\begin{split}\Delta\sigma(t_k)=\begin{pmatrix}\sigma_1(t_k)&0&0\\0&\sigma_2(t_k)&0\\0&0&\sigma_3(t_k)\end{pmatrix}\qquad\text{avec}\qquad\sigma_1>\sigma_2>\sigma_3\end{split}
$$

One can then compute $\Delta\varepsilon$ which is a function of the principal stresses, of the $Ke$ of the combination of situations and of Young's modulus $E$ taken at the mean temperature of the time step $T$ . $Ke$ was stored during the computation of the usual usage factor (cf. r7.04.03-calcul-salt ) and $E$ is computed by linear interpolation on the curve $E(T)$ given under the keyword TABL_YOUNG.

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta\varepsilon(t_k)=\begin{cases}0 & \text{si } \sigma_1\le 0 \\ K_e\cdot\frac{\sigma_1(t_k)-\sigma_3(t_k)}{E(T)} & \text{si } \sigma_1>0\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

**Note**

The temperature table entered under the keyword TABL_TEMP must be defined at the same time instants as the tables which contain the stresses in transient form (thermal, pressure, mechanical depending on the computation method).

### 5.2 Computation of the usage factor with environmental effect

For the computation of the usage factor with environmental effect, two quantities still enter at this stage: a criterion on the strain (keyword CRIT_EPSI) and the integrated FEN (keyword FEN_INTEGRE). These two quantities do not depend on the combination of situations; one therefore enters only one value for all combinations of situations.

#### 5.2.1 Criterion on the minimum strain

During the computation of $FEN_{comb(p,q)}$ , if the sum of the strain increments due to situations p and q is less than CRIT_EPSI then the environmental effect is not taken into account for this combination.

More precisely,

$$
\text{si }\sum_k ?\,\Delta\varepsilon_p(t_k)+\sum_k ?\,\Delta\varepsilon_q(t_k)\le\varepsilon_{limite} \quad\text{alors}\quad FEN_{comb}(p,q)=1
$$

Finally, in order to obtain the elementary usage factor with environmental effect, one multiplies the elementary usage factor ${U}^{elem}(p,q)$ (cf. r7.04.03-calcul-facteur-usage-fatigue ) by the FEN of the combination of situations p and q:

$$
U_{env}^{elem}(p,q)=U^{elem}(p,q)\cdot FEN_{comb}(p,q)
$$

The total usage factor with environmental effect ${U}_{env}^{TOT}$ is computed by taking into account ${U}_{env}^{elem}(p,q)$ in place of ${U}^{elem}(p,q)$ but without going again through the algorithm of r7.04.03-Algorithme-calcul-facteur-usage-total .

#### 5.2.2 Global FEN and integrated FEN

A last check is then made: one defines the global FEN such that:

$$
FEN_{global}=\frac{U_{env}^{TOT}}{U^{TOT}}
$$

If ${FEN}_{global}>{FEN}_{integre}$ then the total usage factor with environmental effect ${U}_{env}^{TOT}$ is updated by dividing it by FEN_INTEGRE.

## 6 Type ‘EVOLUTION’

### 6.1 Loading data

‘EVOLUTION’ is well suited to computations on a component subjected to few loading situations and no earthquake. The user of the RCC-M must give the number of occurrences of each operating situation (for example: heating of the boiler, hot shutdown, etc.). An operating situation can be decomposed into transients, that is evolutions of the global operating parameters (pressure, temperature) as a function of time.

In *code_aster*, one deals with mechanical results (produced by MECA_STATIQUE or STAT_NON_LINE), therefore with transients. For each transient, the stress fields are to be supplied on the analysis segment at the time instants of the discretisation of the computation by means of tables created by a call to POST_RELEVE_T or MACR_LIGN_COUPE.

Several types of results may be necessary for each transient: stresses for the thermomechanical loadings (TABL_RESU_MECA), stresses for the thermal loading alone (TABL_SIGM_THER), stresses for the pressure loading alone (TABL_RESU_PRES) and stresses for the singular zones (TABL_SIGM_THETA).

This TYPE_RESU_MECA is the one which leads to the most precise results. It does not indeed require the introduction of any simplifying hypothesis, either on the definition of the loadings, or on the computation of the various level-0 or level-A criteria.

Furthermore, it makes it possible to compute the initiation factor at a singular zone, in the sense of appendix ZD of the RCC-M.

### 6.2 Computations carried out with the option ‘AXIS’ and ‘RAYON_MOYEN’

The treatment of the linearisation of the stresses by means of the 3D method can lead to an erroneous result because of the inhomogeneous radial mass distribution under the axisymmetric hypothesis. For an axisymmetric model, the median axis must be offset outwards by a distance such that the resulting bending moment vanishes on the offset axis.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.03/modele_axisymetrique.png)

*Axisymmetric model and variables involved in the stress-linearisation method. Left: model without curvature; Right: model with a curvature in the XOY plane. (Ref. ANSYS APDL Theory Reference)*

By convention, the model is axisymmetric about the Y axis. The stress tensor having been converted into the local 2D frame, according to the physical design, one can quantify the offset distance in order to linearise the normal stress:

$$
x_f=\frac{M_z}{F_y}=\frac{t^2\cos(\phi)}{12R_c}
$$

Where $M_z$ is the total bending moment in the circumferential direction induced by the normal stress and $F_y$ is the total normal force. The other variables are shown in r7.04.03-fig-modele-axisymetrique.

Likewise, the offset distance for linearising the circumferential stress is expressed as: the offset distance for linearising the circumferential stress is expressed:

$$
x_h=\frac{M_y}{F_z}=\frac{t^2}{12\,\rho}
$$

The main idea of the computation of the membrane stress ${\sigma}_{ij}^{m}$ is to average the total force by the elementary area in the plane which is orthogonal to the stress component. The computation of the bending stress uses the equation in section 3.2 when in the 3D cases. The only difference with the 3D models is the taking into account of the offset axis. Here, the detailed computation is presented as an example for the circumferential stress component, whose formulation is the most complicated.

$$
\sigma_z^m=\frac{F_z}{A_z}=\frac{\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\sigma_z(\rho+x)\Delta\phi\,dx}{\rho\Delta\phi\,t}=\frac{\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\sigma_z\left(1+\frac{x}{\rho}\right)\,dx}{t}
$$

$$
\sigma_z^b=\frac{M_z(x-x_h)}{I_y}=\frac{(x-x_h)\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\sigma_z(\rho+x)\Delta\phi(x-x_h)\,dx}{\rho t\Delta\phi\left(\frac{t^2}{12}-x_h^2\right)}=\frac{x-x_h}{t\left(\frac{t^2}{12}-x_h^2\right)}\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}\sigma_z\left(1+\frac{x}{\rho}\right)(x-x_h)\,dx
$$

The simplified expression of the other linearised stress components can be found in ANSYS APDL Theory Reference.

The code newly developed within the code_aster command POST_RCCM makes it possible for the users to switch to the optimised computation routine if an axisymmetric model is declared, without the original functionalities of the command being disturbed. Furthermore, it has been possible to indicate the curvature of the axis $\rho$ in the XOY plane if necessary, by referring to r7.04.03-fig-modele-axisymetrique. The default value $\rho$ has been set to -1, representing a perfectly straight axis.

### 6.3 Computations carried out with the option ‘PM_PB’

The stress table comprises either a single time step, or a complete transient (*nb_inst* time steps). In this latter case, one will look for the maximum, with respect to the list of the order numbers, of the various terms entering into the criteria.

It is for the user to know whether one computes $Pm$ (general membrane stress: outside the zones of geometric singularity) or else $Pl$ (local membrane stress: in the singularities). From the stress readings supplied, one therefore computes a membrane stress.

The algorithm is the following. Over the set of the order numbers $n=1,\text{nb\_inst}$ :

- extraction of the stresses at the time instant $t$
- On each end of the segment:

> computation of $P_m(t)$, $P_b(t)$,
$P_{mb}(t,s=0)$ and $P_{mb}(t,s=l)$
by integration over the segment

$$
\sigma_{ij}^{moy}(t)=\frac{1}{l}\int_0^l\sigma_{ij}(t)\,ds
$$

$$
P_m(t)=\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{moy}(t)\right\rVert
$$

$$
\sigma_{ij}^{fle}(t)=\frac{6}{l^2}\int_0^l\left(s-\frac{l}{2}\right)\sigma_{ij}(t)\,ds
$$

$$
P_b(t)=\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{fle}(t)\right\rVert
$$

$$
P_{mb}(t,s=0)=\max_t\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{moy}(t)-\sigma_{ij}^{fle}(t)\right\rVert
$$

$$
P_{mb}(t,s=l)=\max_t\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{moy}(t)+\sigma_{ij}^{fle}(t)\right\rVert
$$

- Search for the maximum of $P_m(t)$ , $P_{mb}(t,s=0)$ and $P_{mb}(t,s=l)$
- Output and storage in the result table.

**Note**

The thermal stresses are of secondary type and must therefore not be taken into account in the computation of the level-0 criteria. In POST_RCCM, if TABL_RESU_MECA and TABL_SIGM_THER are present simultaneously, one supposes that the result TABL_RESU_MECA corresponds to the complete thermomechanical loading, and one therefore subtracts from it the stresses of thermal origin.

### 6.4 Computations carried out with the option ‘SN’

#### 6.4.1 Computation of Sn

One denotes by $nb_inst$ the number of time instants selected in the transient considered.

The algorithm for the computation of $S_n$ is the following:

- over the set of the order numbers, $n_1 = 1,\mathrm{nb\_inst}$
- Extraction of the time instant $t_1$
- Computation of $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=0)$ and $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=l)$
- For $n_2$ varying from $n_1+1$ *to* $\mathrm{nb\_inst}$
- Extraction of the time instant $t_2$
- computation of $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=0)$ and $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=l)$ and $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=0)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=0)$ and $\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=l)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=l)$
- computation of the principal directions and of the Tresca criterion: $\left(\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=0)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=0)\right)_{Eq.Tresca}$ and $\left(\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2,s=l)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1,s=l)\right)_{Eq.Tresca}$
- search for the maximum, therefore for $S_n$, at each end
- Output and storage in the result table.

**Note**

The quantity $S_n$ computed here corresponds to an amplitude. It is therefore indispensable that all the states of the system be considered, including the states with zero stress (for example cold shutdown: zero applied pressure and moments and ambient temperature).

#### 6.4.2 Computation of Sn*

This computation is carried out if the operand TABL_SIGM_THER is present. Only the user ensures the consistency of the data, that is to say that this result must be produced by a thermomechanical computation under thermal loading alone, given that the result given by TABL_RESU_MECA may be due to a combination of this thermal loading with other loadings. One must therefore in particular ensure that the time instants of the tables TABL_RESU_MECA and TABL_SIGM_THER correspond.

The algorithm is identical to the preceding one but bears on two stress fields.

#### 6.4.3 Computation of the thermal ratchet

The computation is carried out if the operands TABL_SIGM_THER and TABL_RESU_PRES are present. One must also have previously defined the conventional elastic limit for the maximum temperature reached in the course of the cycle, either by the operand SY_MAX of POST_RCCM, or by the operand SY_02 of the keyword RCCM in DEFI_MATERIAU [U4.43.01](doc:u4.43.01). If no elastic limit is defined, the computation of the thermal ratchet is impossible.

In the result table appear, for each end of each analysis segment, the elastic limit SY, the amplitude of variation of the stress of thermal origin SP_THER, the maximum of the general membrane stress due to the pressure SIGM_M_PRES and two maximum allowable values of the amplitude of variation of the thermal stress computed either by supposing a linear temperature variation in the wall (VALE_MAXI_LINE), or by supposing a parabolic temperature variation in the wall (VALE_MAXI_PARAB).

### 6.5 Fatigue computations with the option ‘FATIGUE_ZH210’

The requirements relating to the computation of the usage factor are defined in §2.6.3.

The method ‘‘EVOLUTION’’ corresponds to appendix ZH210 of the RCC-M. It consists in “forgetting” the notion of situation and in combining directly *loading states*, which are the significant time instants of all the transients where the stresses pass through a local extremum. By default, in *code_aster*, all the computation time instants are used. One associates with each of them the number of occurrences $N_{occ}$ of the transient. The definition is therefore:

*Loading state = {time instant, stress tensor, number of occurrences}.*

Next, one constructs the set of all the loading states by sweeping all the transients. In the end, the notion of transient is forgotten: one works only on a set of loading states. One then computes the elementary usage factors associated with all the combinations taken two by two. One then uses a method of cumulation of the elementary usage factors, based on the hypothesis of the linear cumulation of damage, in order to obtain the global usage factor.

The main advantage of this method is to consider automatically all the possible sub-cycles: it is not necessary to identify the fictitious transients combining the situations with one another. Its drawback is the number of computations to be carried out if one does not restrict the set of the time instants used in the computation.

**Note**

The algorithm described here is similar to that of POST_FATIGUE. More precisely, the algorithm used in POST_FATIGUE is a restriction to the uniaxial case of the ZH210 method. Indeed, the datum of the command POST_FATIGUE is a scalar function of time, whereas POST_RCCM deals with stress tensors functions of time.

#### 6.5.1 Computation of the elementary usage factors

At each end of the segment, for every pair of loading states $k$ and $l$ , one computes the quantities $S_p(k,l)$ and $S_n(k,l)$ defined by:

$$
S_p(k,l)=\left(\sigma_{ij}(k)-\sigma_{ij}(l)\right)_{Eq.Tresca}
$$

For the computation of $S_{alt}$ , two formulae are proposed (cf. r7.04.03-calcul-salt ):

- the original method (KE_MECA) which makes no distinction between the mechanical part and the thermal part:
- the *KE_MIXTE* method introduced in the 1997 modification of the RCC-M, which is based on a decomposition of $S_{alt}$ between the mechanical part and the thermal part.

The fatigue curve $N_{adm}=f(S_{alt})$ is a function defined by DEFI_FONCTION, and introduced into DEFI_MATERIAU by the keyword WOHLER of the factor keyword FATIGUE. It makes it possible to compute the number of allowable cycles $N_{adm}(k,l)$ associated with $S_{alt}(k,l)$ , then the elementary usage factor:

$$
u(k,l)=\frac{1}{N_{adm}(k,l)}
$$

This computation is carried out for each combination of two loading states. One therefore obtains (still for each end of the segment) a symmetric matrix $u(k,l)$ , of order the number of loading states $N_{tot}$ .

#### 6.5.2 Computation of the total usage factor

The algorithm for the computation of the total usage factor, for each of the ends of the cutting line, is the following:

1. $u^{tot}=0$
2. $u^{max}=0$
3. Loop $i=1\ldots N_{tot}$ (search for the maximum in the table)
  If $Nocc(i)>0$ :

> Loop $j=i+1\ldots N_{tot}$

> If $Nocc(j)>0$ and $u(i,j)>u^{max}$ :
> $u^{max}=u(i,j),\; m=i,\; n=j$

4. $Nocc(m,n)=\min\left(Nocc(m),Nocc(n)\right)$
5. $u^{tot}=u^{tot}+Nocc(m,n)*u(m,n)$
6. Updating of the number of occurrences:
  $Nocc(m)=Nocc(m)-Nocc(m,n)$
7. $Nocc(n)=Nocc(n)-Nocc(m,n)$
8. Return to the beginning of the procedure until elimination of all the occurrences.

- If the number of time instants defined for each transient is large, the computation time can be prohibitive. One must therefore be able to restrict it. This is what is done in POST_FATIGUE, by a preliminary sorting of the time instants. One eliminates the time instants such that the scalar function is linear, in order to keep only the ends of the straight-line segments. One also eliminates the very small variations. Here, in a multiaxial situation, the sorting is more delicate. The notion of proportional stresses could be used, but in practice the user can define himself the list of the time instants (keyword NUME_ORDRE).
- By this method, one is sure not to forget any sub-cycle. On the other hand, it is desirable to eliminate the time instants which do not correspond to local extrema, because they could generate spurious sub-cycles, increasing the usage factor (these time instants are used only for the numerical discretisation of the mechanical or thermal problem).
- With the option ‘FATIGUE_ZH210’, the combinations of transients are taken into account in the computation of $S_n$ and of $S_n*$ .

### 6.6 Computations of the initiation factor with the option ‘AMORCAGE’

#### 6.6.1 Principle of the computation of the initiation factor

The zones of local discontinuities whose contour presents abrupt variations are the seat of sharp stress concentrations. In this case, the notion of usage factor defined previously is no longer suitable and it must be replaced by the notion of initiation factor (B3234.7).

The initiation factor is computed from the amplitude of variation of the stress in the structure at a distance $d$ from the singularity, and from an initiation law. The analysis procedure is defined in appendix ZD2200. The distance $d$ and the initiation laws are material characteristics and are tabulated in table ZD2300.

The initiation law defined in the RCC-M is of the form:

$$
\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}(d)=A_{AMORC}\cdot(N_a)^{B_{AMORC}}
$$

with $N_a$ the number of acceptable cycles and $\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}$ the amplitude of variation of the tangential stresses, in the local frame, at the distance $d$ from the singularity.

The initiation law developed in the operator POST_RCCM takes into account the load ratio $R$ of the loading, as recommended in the RSE-M (ref 15):

$$
\Delta\sigma_{eff}(d)=A_{AMORC}\cdot(N_a)^{B_{AMORC}}
$$

with the following relation between the real amplitude of variation of the stresses $\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}$ and the effective one $\Delta\sigma_{eff}$ :

$$
\Delta\sigma_{eff}=\frac{\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}}{1-?}
$$

**Note**

In order to use an initiation law such as defined in the RCC-M, that is without taking the load ratio into account, it is sufficient to define a large R_AMORC (1000 for example).

#### 6.6.2 Computation in code_aster

The parameters of the initiation law (A_AMORC, B_AMORC, R_AMORC) and the distance to the singularity D_AMORC are to be defined under the factor keyword RCCM of DEFI_MATERIAU.

The table expected as input, under the keyword TABL_SIGM_THETA, corresponds to the reading of the stresses on a **circular cutting line** (of radius D_AMORC) around the singularity. The stresses must be expressed in the **local frame**.

![Figure](/figures/r7.04.03/1013EFC4000069D500004D690EB23ABDC7EC016C.svg)

*definition of the local frame*

Such a table can be created by means of the command MACR_LIGN_COUPE. As in the fatigue computation (cf. §6.4), one considers that all the time instants supplied correspond to extrema of the transient. Furthermore, the notion of transient is forgotten and one works only on a set of loading states. One denotes by $N_{occ}(i)$ the number of occurrences associated with the loading state $i$ and by $N_{tot}$ the total number of loading states.

The computation algorithm is then the following:

Loop over the points of the cutting line

- Check that the point is at the distance $d$ from the singularity
- Loop over the points of the cutting line

  - Check that the point is at the distance $d$ from the singularity
  - Loop $i=1\ldots N_{tot}$

    - Extraction of $\sigma_{\theta\theta}(i)$
    - Loop $j=i+1\ldots N_{tot}$

      - Extraction of $\sigma_{\theta\theta}(j)$
      - Computation of

$$
\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}(i,j)=\left|\sigma_{\theta\theta}(j)-\sigma_{\theta\theta}(i)\right|
$$

      - Computation of

$$
R(i,j)=?
$$

      - Computation of

$$
\Delta\sigma_{eff}(i,j)=\frac{\Delta\sigma_{\theta\theta}(i,j)}{1-?}
$$

      - Computation of the number of allowable cycles and of the elementary initiation factor

$$
N_a(i,j)=(?)^{1/B_{AMORC}}
$$

$$
FA(i,j)=?
$$

At the end of this first part, one therefore has a matrix of the initiation factors of the set of the combinations of loading states. The size of the matrix is $N_{tot} x N_{tot}$ but only the part above the diagonal is filled in.

The algorithm for the computation of the total initiation factor, for a given point on the cutting line, is then the following:

1. $FA^{max}=0$
2. $FA^{tot}=0$
3. Loop $i=1\ldots N_{tot}$ (search for the maximum in the table)
  If $Nocc(i)>0$ :

> Loop $j=i+1\ldots N_{tot}$

> If $Nocc(j)>0$ and $FA(i,j)>FA^{max}$ :

$$
FA^{max}=FA(i,j),\quad m=i,\quad n=j
$$

$$
Nocc(m,n)=\min\left(Nocc(m),Nocc(n)\right)
$$

$$
FA^{tot}=FA^{tot}+Nocc(m,n)\times FA(m,n)
$$

6. Updating of the number of occurrences:

$$
Nocc(m)=Nocc(m)-Nocc(m,n)
$$

$$
Nocc(n)=Nocc(n)-Nocc(m,n)
$$

7. Return to the beginning of the procedure until elimination of all the occurrences.

At the end of this algorithm, one thus has the initiation factor for each of the points (i.e. for each of the angles) of the cutting line.

## 7 Type 'B3600'

In code_aster, it is possible to evaluate level-A criteria (fatigue) according to chapter B3600 of the RCC-M. It is customary in B3600 to define each situation as the passage from a stabilised state A (corresponding to a given internal pressure in the piping line, a given uniform temperature, and fixed mechanical loadings) to a stabilised state B (with constant loadings different from the preceding ones). One associates with this situation a thermal transient. Loadings of thermal origin can also be taken into account in the computation.

The treatment which is described here is carried out for each node of each mesh cell of the piping line considered. The result obtained will therefore be a usage factor (total or partial) for each node of each mesh cell requested by the user.

### 7.1 Prior computation of all the loading states

For each node of each mesh cell, the present step consists in computing, for all the situations, the moments relating to each stabilised state (by cumulating the various loadings which enter).

#### 7.1.1 Computations of the static loading states

One processes the results of the static analyses (field EFGE_ELNO or SIEF_ELNO) for the stabilised states of the list of the situations undergone by the line.

A stabilised state can be defined by a list of load cases, each load being signed. In this case, the resultants of the stabilised state are obtained by algebraic summation of the resultants of each of the load cases:

$$
M_i=M_{i_{{CHAR}^1}}+M_{i_{{CHAR}^2}}+\cdots\qquad i\in(z)
$$

The loadings are for example the constrained thermal expansion, the anchor displacement.

#### 7.1.2 Computation of the seismic loadings

The seismic loading is decomposed into two parts:

- An inertial part

It is computed by imposing on the set of the anchors the same motion characterised by the envelope spectrum of the various floor spectra, in the horizontal directions $X$ and $Y$ on the one hand, and vertical $Z$ on the other hand (in the global frame). To this end, one uses the command COMB_SISM_MODAL, which produces generalised forces which correspond to each earthquake direction as well as the quadratic combination of these forces.

The inertial contribution of the earthquake to the component $i$ of the moment is written:

$$
M_{i_{S\_DYN}}=\sqrt{\sum_j\left(M_{i_{S\_DYN}}\left(spectre_j\right)\right)^2}\qquad i,j\in((x;y;z);(X;Y;Z))
$$

with $M_{i_{S\_DYN}}(spectrej)$ the moment in the direction $i$ resulting from the dynamic loading in the direction *j*. This combination is done directly by COMB_SISM_MODAL.

- A quasi-static part

It is estimated by imposing static differential displacements corresponding to the maxima of the differences of the seismic motions of the anchor points in the course of time. The computations are therefore carried out for each unit loading (one computation per displacement in a given direction for one end of the line).

One denotes by $N_{ANC}$ the number of anchor points of the structure. The quasi-static contribution of the differential anchor displacements to the component *i* of the moment is written:

$$
M_{i_{S\_ANC}}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ANC}}\left(M_{i_{S\_ANC}}^{k}\right)^2}
$$

with $M_{i_{S\_ANC}}^{k}$ the $i$-th component of the moment corresponding to the $k$-th anchor displacement.

##### 7.1.2.1 Combination of the inertial and differential components due to the earthquake

The $i$-th resulting component is obtained by the root-mean-square of the $i$-th inertial and differential components:

$$
M_{i_S}=\sqrt{\left(M_{i_{S\_ANC}}\right)^2+\left(M_{i_{S\_DYN}}\right)^2}\qquad i\in(x;y;z)
$$

which in fact amounts to carrying out the root-mean-square of all the inertial and differential moments,

$$
M_{i_S}=\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ANC}}\left(M_{i_{S\_ANC}}^{k}\right)^2+\left(M_{i_{S\_DYN}}\right)^2} \qquad i\in(x;y;z)
$$

For the user, the earthquake situation is defined by the list of the results corresponding to the inertial response and to the responses to the displacement of the $N_{ANC}$ successive anchor points. The recombination by root-mean-square is done directly by the operator POST_RCCM.

#### 7.1.3 Computation of the thermal transients

The loadings of the type "thermal gradient through the thickness" are decomposed into three parts, cf. r7.04.03-fig-decomposition-distribution :

- a constant value which is the mean value of the temperature:

$$
T_{moy}=\frac{1}{t}\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}T(y)\,dy
$$

where $t$ corresponds to the nominal thickness of the wall.

- a linear distribution of zero mean (moment of order 1):

$$
V=\frac{12}{t^2}\int_{-t/2}^{t/2}y\cdot T(y)\,dy
$$

- a non-linear distribution of zero mean and of zero moment with respect to the mean fibre.

For each of the transients and each piping section of the line (and each junction), one therefore carries out beforehand, depending on the geometric complexity of the problem studied, a 2D or 3D thermal analysis.

Each analysis is then post-processed so as to extract, for each time instant of the transient, the temperature on the chosen section and the mean values (moments of order 0 and 1). This operation can be done for example by means of two calls to POST_RELEVE_T (OPERATION=‘EXTRACTION’ and OPERATION = ‘MOYENNE’).

In the case of a material discontinuity or of a junction, one computes the mean temperature (denoted $T_a$ and $T_b$ ) on the two sides of the junction. In practice, the zones $a$ and $b$ will correspond to segments chosen by the user in POST_RELEVE_T, and the tables produced will be associated with the two adjacent mesh cells having in common the node which corresponds to the junction.

### 7.2 Computations of the amplitudes of variation of the stresses

#### 7.2.1 Principle of the method

The amplitudes of variation of the stresses are defined in paragraph B3653 of the RCC-M for combinations between two time instants or two loading states. Denoting by $t_i$ and $t_j$ these two time instants, one has schematically for the amplitude of variation of a quantity $S$:

$$
S(t_i,t_j)=S^{mecanique}(t_i)-S^{mecanique}(t_j)+S^{thermique}(t_i)-S^{thermique}(t_j)
$$

In the method as developed in *code_aster*, the situations are defined in a simplified way by two stabilised states and a thermal transient: it is then not possible to work directly on each of the time instants of the situations and hypotheses must be introduced.

One therefore considers the set of the combinations $(i,j)$ with $(i,j)\in(1,2,..,N,1,2,..,N)$ , $N$ being the number of stabilised states excluding the earthquake (that is 2 times the number of situations of the group). Let two stabilised states, $i$ and $j$ , belonging respectively to the situations $p$ and $q$ . The amplitude of variation $S$ will then be computed in the following way:

$$
S(i,j)=S^{mecanique}(i)-S^{mecanique}(j)+\max\left(\Delta S^{thermique}(p),\Delta S^{thermique}(q)\right)
$$

denoting by $S^{thermique}(p)$ the amplitude of variation of the thermal stress of the transient $p$ .

1. It is important to note that the amplitude of variation of the stresses is obtained by maximising the amplitude of the thermal stresses for each thermal transient independently of one another. The computation method for the case ‘B3600’ is therefore different from that adopted for the case ‘B3200_UNIT’.
2. As indicated in paragraph B3653.2, all the states of the system must be considered, including the states with zero stress (for example cold shutdown: zero applied pressure and moments and ambient temperature).

**Notes**

#### 7.2.2 Computation of the loading combinations inside each group

The objective is to construct, for each group of situations, a symmetric square matrix containing the set of the amplitudes of variation of the alternating stress $S_{alt}'(i,j)$ , with $i$ and $j$ two stabilised states associated respectively with the situations $p$ and $q$ . This computation requires the prior computation of the quantities $S_p$ (amplitude of the total stress) and $S_n$ (amplitude of the linearised stress).

##### 7.2.2.1 Notations and definitions

One denotes:

| Symbol | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| $C_1,\ C_2,\ C_3, K_1,\ K_2,\ K_3$ | Stress indices supplied in §B3680 of the RCC-M |
| $E$ | Young's modulus of the piping at ambient temperature |
| $\nu$ | Poisson's ratio |
| $\alpha$ | Expansion coefficient of the piping at ambient temperature |
| $E_{ab}$ | Mean Young's modulus between the two zones separated by a discontinuity at ambient temperature |
| $D_o$ | Outside diameter of the piping |
| $t$ | Nominal thickness of the wall |
| $I$ | Moment of inertia of the piping: $I=\frac{\pi}{64}\left(D_o^2-\left(D_o-2t\right)^2\right)$ |
| $M_i(i,j)$ | Variation of moment resulting from the various loadings of the situations to which the stabilised states i and j belong: $M_i(i,j)=\sqrt{\left(M_X(i)-M_X(j)\right)^2+\left(M_Y(i)-M_Y(j)\right)^2+\left(M_Z(i)-M_Z(j)\right)^2}$ |
| $P_o(i,j)$ | Pressure difference between the states i and j |
| $T_a(t_k,t_l), T_b(t_k,t_l)$ | Amplitude of variation of the mean temperatures in the zones a and b between the time instants $t_k$ and $t_l$ |

| $\Delta T_2(t_k,t_l)$ | Non-linear part of the distribution through the wall thickness of the amplitude of variation of the temperature between the time instants $t_k$ and $t_l$ $\Delta T_2(t_k,t_l)=\max\begin{cases}\left\|T_o(t_k,t_l)-T_{moy}(t_k,t_l)\right\|-\frac{1}{2}\Delta T_1 \\ \left\|T_i(t_k,t_l)-T_{moy}(t_k,t_l)\right\|-\frac{1}{2}\Delta T_1 \\ 0\end{cases}$ |

##### 7.2.2.2 Computation of Sp

The amplitude of variation of the total stresses $S_p$ for the piping is defined in equation 11 of §B3653 of the RCC-M. One computes:

$$
S_p(i,j,t_p)=K_1\cdot C_1\cdot\frac{|P_o(i,j)|\cdot D_o}{2\cdot t}+K_2\cdot C_2\cdot\frac{D_o}{2\cdot I}\cdot M_i(i,j)+\frac{1}{2\cdot(1-\nu)}\cdot K_3\cdot E\cdot\alpha\cdot\Delta T_1+K_3\cdot C_3\cdot E_{ab}\cdot\left|\alpha_a\cdot T_a(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})-\alpha_b\cdot T_b(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})\right|+\frac{1}{1-\nu}\cdot E\cdot\alpha\cdot\Delta T_2
$$

$(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})$ denote any two time instants of the transient associated with the situation $p$ . In the case of a material discontinuity or of a junction, the terms $\Delta T_1$ and $\Delta T_2$ to be retained are those associated with the most penalising section.

One likewise computes $S_p(i,j,t_q)$ with the thermal transient associated with the situation $q$ . The amplitude $S_p(i,j)$ for the combination $(i,j)$ is then:

$$
S_p(i,j)=\max\left(\max_{(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})}\left(S_p(i,j,t_p)\right),\max_{(t_i^{kq},t_i^{lq})}\left(S_p(i,j,t_q)\right)\right)
$$

##### 7.2.2.3 Computation of Sn

The amplitude of variation of the linearised stresses $S_n$ for the piping is defined in equation 10 of §B3653 of the RCC-M. One computes:

$$
S_n(i,j,t_p)=C_1\cdot\frac{|P_o(i,j)|\cdot D_o}{2\cdot t}+C_2\cdot\frac{D_o}{2\cdot I}\cdot M_i(i,j)+\frac{1}{2\cdot(1-\nu)}\cdot E\cdot\alpha\cdot\Delta T_1+C_3\cdot E_{ab}\cdot\left|\alpha_a\cdot T_a(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})-\alpha_b\cdot T_b(t_i^{kp},t_i^{lp})\right|
$$

$$
S_n(i,j,t_q)=C_1\cdot\frac{|P_o(i,j)|\cdot D_o}{2\cdot t}+C_2\cdot\frac{D_o}{2\cdot I}\cdot M_i(i,j)+\frac{1}{2\cdot(1-\nu)}\cdot E\cdot\alpha\cdot\Delta T_1+C_3\cdot E_{ab}\cdot\left|\alpha_a\cdot T_a(t_i^{kq},t_i^{lq})-\alpha_b\cdot T_b(t_i^{kq},t_i^{lq})\right|
$$

$$
S_n(i,j)=\max\left(\max_{(t_i^{kP},t_i^{lP})}\left(S_n(i,j,t_p)\right),\max_{(t_i^{kQ},t_i^{lQ})}\left(S_n(i,j,t_q)\right)\right)
$$

One then computes $S_n(p,q)=\max_{i,j} S_n(i,j)$ , for $i$ and $j$ running over the set of the stabilised states of the two situations $p$ and $q$ (4 possible combinations).

##### 7.2.2.4 Computation of Salt

Two formulae are proposed in order to define the amplitude of variation $S_{alt}'$ between the states $i$ and $j$

- KE_MECA: this is the original method, the only one available in the versions prior to version 7.2:

$$
S_{alt}'(i,j)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e\left(S_n(p,q)\right)\cdot S_p(i,j)
$$

with:

- $E_c$ : reference Young's modulus for the construction of the Wöhler curve, supplied by the user in DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keyword E_REFE, of the factor keyword FATIGUE.
- $E$ : the smaller of the Young's moduli used for the computation of the states $i$ and $j$ , that is evaluated at the temperatures of these stabilised states.

$$
\begin{aligned}
K_e(S_n(p,q))=\begin{cases}1 & \text{si } S_n(p,q)\le 3\cdot S_m \\ 1+\frac{1-n}{n\cdot(m-1)}\cdot\left(\frac{S_n(p,q)}{3\cdot S_m}-1\right) & \text{si } 3\cdot S_m<S_n(p,q)<3\cdot m\cdot S_m \\ \frac{1}{n} & \text{si } S_n(p,q)\ge 3\cdot m\cdot S_m\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$

with $m$ and $n$ depending on the material, and supplied by the user in DEFI_MATERIAU, under the keywords M_KE and N_KE, of the factor keyword RCCM. If the keywords TEMP_REF_A and TEMP_REF_B are present, $S_n$ is interpolated for this temperature (which must correspond to the mean temperature of the transient). Otherwise, $S_n$ is taken at ambient temperature.

- KE_MIXTE: since the 1997 modification of the RCC-M, one can choose another formula, based on a decomposition of $S_{alt}$ :

$$
S'_{alt}(i,j)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot\left(K_{e^{meca}}(S_n(p,q))\cdot S_{p^{meca}}(i,j)+K_{e^{ther}}(S_n(p,q))\cdot S_{p^{ther}}(i,j)\right)
$$

with:

- $K_{e^{meca}}(S_n(p,q))$ is equal to the $K_e$ defined above.
- $K_{e^{ther}}(S_n(p,q))=\max\begin{cases}1.86\left(1-\frac{1}{1.66+\frac{S_n}{S_m}}\right) \\ 1\end{cases}$
- $S_{p^{meca}}(i,j)$ represents the amplitude of variation of the mechanical part of the quantity $S_p$ , between the states $i$ and $j$ . It is computed on the basis of the loadings of mechanical origin: pressure, self-weight, earthquake (inertial and anchor displacements), thermal expansion.
- $S_{p^{ther}}(i,j)$ the amplitude of variation of the thermal part of the quantity $S_p$ , between the states $i$ and $j$ (terms depending on $T_a$, $T_b$, $\Delta T_1$ and $\Delta T_2$ in the definition of §7.2.2.2).

Case of the sub-cycles

The sub-cycles correspond either to the taking into account of the sub-cycles linked to the earthquake, or to situations for which the keyword COMBINABLE=’NON’ has been filled in. In both cases, one computes the stress amplitude by bringing in only the stresses linked to these sub-cycles (no combination of loading states outside this situation). For the computation of $S_{alt}'$ , one must use the factor $K_e$ which corresponds to the main situation from which the sub-cycle originates.

#### 7.2.3 Computation of the loading combinations for the passage situations

Two loading states are combinable only if they belong to the same situation or if there exists a passage situation between the groups to which they belong. In this latter case, one will associate with the combination $(i,j)$ the number of occurrences of the passage situation. In the case where the passage situation belongs to one of the two groups (which is not excluded a priori), it is naturally combined with the other situations of this group, then serves for the combination of the situations of its group with the situations of the related group.

For each passage situation from one group to another, one therefore considers the set of the combinations $(i,j)$ with $i$ belonging to the first group (of dimension $N$ ) and $j$ belonging to the second group (of dimension $M$ ). For each combination, $S_{alt}'(i,j)$ is obtained in the same way as previously and one associates with it the number of occurrences of the passage situation. One constructs a (rectangular) matrix containing all the $S_{alt}'(i,j)$ .

### 7.3 Computation of the usage factor

One denotes:

| $n_k$ | number of cycles associated with the situation $p$ to which the stabilised state $k$ belongs |
| --- | --- |
| $n_l$ | number of cycles associated with the situation $q$ to which the stabilised state $l$ belongs |
| $N_s$ | number of occurrences of the earthquake |
| $n_s$ | number of sub-cycles associated with each occurrence of the earthquake |
| $n_{pass}$ | number of cycles associated with a possible passage situation between $p$ and $q$ if these situations do not belong to one and the same group, but if there exists a passage situation between the two |

**For the set of the combinations of loading states (inside a group of situations or associated with a passage situation):**

**If** $N_s > 0$ **, one selects the combinations of stabilised states** $k$ **and** $l$ **the most penalising, that is the combinations** $(k,l)$ **leading to the greatest values of** $S_{alt}'(k,l)$ **.**

For each of these combinations:

1. Superposition of the moments of seismic origin and of the combination $(k,l)$ :

- One superposes the earthquake loadings on the variation of moment resulting from the various loadings of the stabilised states $k$ and $l$ :

$$
M_{X_s}(k,l)=M_X(k)-M_X(l)\pm\Delta M_{S_X}
$$

with:

- $M_X(k)$ and $M_X(l)$ : components in the direction $X$ ($X\in(1;2;3)$) of the moments associated with the states $k$ and $l$ ;
- $\Delta M_{S_X}$ : total amplitude of variation in the direction $X$ of the moments due to the earthquake ($\Delta M_{S_X}=2\,M_{X_s}$ where $M_{X_s}$ is the total resulting moment (inertia and anchor displacements) as defined in §7.1.2).
- One then computes $S_p$ and $S_n$ as defined previously with the new value of $M_i$ (denoted respectively $S_{p_s}$ and $S_{n_s}$) and one computes:

$$
S'_{alt}(k,l)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e\left(S_{n_s}(m,n)\right)\cdot S_{p_s}(k,l)
$$

- One computes the number of allowable cycles $N(k,l)$ for the stress amplitude $S'_{alt}(k,l)$ by means of the Wöhler curve associated with the material.
- One finally computes $u_1(k,l)=\frac{1}{N(k,l)}$

1. Taking into account of $2n_s-1$ seismic cycles considered as sub-cycles:

- Amplitude of variation of the seismic stress alone:

$$
S'_{SC}(k,l)=\frac{E_c}{E}\cdot K_e\left(S_{n_s}(k,l)\right)\cdot K_2\cdot C_2\cdot\frac{D_o}{4\cdot I}\cdot\sqrt{\Delta M_{S1}^2+\Delta M_{S2}^2+\Delta M_{S3}^2}
$$

- One computes the number of allowable cycles $N_{SC}(k,l)$ for the stress amplitude $S_{alt}^{SC}(k,l)$. It must be noted that one uses the value $K_e\left(S_{n_s}^{s}(k,l)\right)$ previously computed for the main cycle.
- One finally computes:

$$
u_2(k,l)=\frac{2\,n_s-1}{N_{SC}(k,l)}
$$

One repeats this computation until exhaustion of the most penalising combinations.

The computation of the usage factor is then continued **without taking the earthquake into account**.

**If** $N_s=0$ **, or after having taken the earthquake into account for the most unfavourable combinations:**

- One selects the combination $(k,l)$ leading to the maximum value of $S_{alt}'(k,l)$ , over the set of the combinations, such that the number of occurrences $n_0$ is non-zero, with:

$n_o=\min\left(n_k,n_l,n_{pass}\right)$ if $n_{pass}$ is non-zero

$n_o=\min\left(n_k,n_l\right)$ if $n_{pass}$ is zero

- One computes the number of allowable cycles $N(k,l)$ for the stress amplitude $S_{alt}'(k,l)$ , by means of the Wöhler curve associated with the material.
- One then computes the elementary usage factor: $u(k,l)=\frac{n_o}{N(k,l)}$ .
- One finally replaces:

$n_k$ by $(n_k-n_0)$

$n_l$ by $(n_l-n_0)$

if it is a passage situation, $n_{pass}$ by $n_{pass}-n_0)$

then:

if $n_k=0$ , the column and the row corresponding to the stabilised state $k$ of the matrix $S_{alt}'(i,j)$ are set to 0.

if $n_l=0$ , the column and the row corresponding to the stabilised state $l$ of the matrix $S_{alt}'(i,j)$ are set to 0.

The loop is repeated until exhaustion of the number of cycles.

**Note**

Appendix ZI of the RCC-M code defines the Wöhler curves down to a minimum stress amplitude corresponding to a life of $10^{6}$ cycles. If the value $S_{alt}'$ computed for a combination $(i,j)$ of stabilised state is less than this minimum amplitude, the usage factor is equal to 0 for the combination $(i,j)$ considered. This amounts implicitly to considering the existence of an endurance limit at $10^{6}$ cycles.

## 8 Appendix 1: B3200 equations for situations in unit form

Each stabilised mechanical state is described from a pressure $p$ and a force resultant $\left\{F_X,F_Y,F_Z,M_X,M_Y,M_Z\right\}$ defined under the keyword 'CHAR_MECA'. The stress tensors are reconstructed by linear combination from the stress tensors associated with each of the unit loadings. For example, one denotes by $sigma^{F_x}$ the stress tensor associated with the unit force loading in the direction $X$ . The computation of the stress tensor corresponding to a mechanical loading belonging to a stabilised state is then obtained in the following way:

$$
\sigma=F_X\cdot\sigma^{F_X}+F_Y\cdot\sigma^{F_Y}+F_Z\cdot\sigma^{F_Z}+M_X\cdot\sigma^{M_X}+M_Y\cdot\sigma^{M_Y}+M_Z\cdot\sigma^{M_Z}+P\cdot\sigma^{P}
$$

The use of this option requires the prior computation of the stress fields for the 7 unit loadings and of the stress fields for each of the thermal transients; the unit fields are to be supplied on the analysis segment by means of tables under the keyword 'RESU_MECA_UNIT'.

**Note**

For the branch connections, it is also possible to define two force tensors associated respectively with the body and with the nozzle. The force resultant goes from 6 to 12 components and one goes from 7 to 13 unit loadings. Appendix 5 summarises the equations in this case.

### 8.1 Computations carried out with the option 'PM_PB'

For the moment, this option is available if the data of the situations are in unit form only, not in instantaneous form.

Given the primary stress of the reference situation (1st category) and a segment situated outside a zone of major discontinuity. At each end point of this segment of length *l*, one computes for a situation:

P_m=\max_t\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{moy}\right\rVert_{TRESCA}\quad\text{avec}\quad\sigma_{ij}^{moy}=\frac{1}{l}\int_0^l\sigma_{ij}\,ds\quad\text{et}\quad\left\lVert\tau_{ij}\right\rVert_{TRESCA}=\max_{I,J}\left|\tau_I-\tau_J\right|

P_{mb}=\max_t\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{lin}\right\rVert_{TRESCA}\quad\text{avec}\quad\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(s=0)=\sigma_{ij}^{moy}-\sigma_{ij}^{fle}

### 8.2 Computations carried out with the option ‘SN’

#### 8.2.1 Computation of Sn

The computation points are the two ends of the segment. *For a given situation*, at each end point of this segment of length $l$ , one computes $S_n$ according to paragraph B3232.6:

$$
S_n=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(t_2)\right\rVert_{TRESCA} \quad\text{avec}\quad \sigma_{ij}^{lin}(s=0)=\sigma_{ij}^{moy}-\sigma_{ij}^{fle}
$$

$$
\sigma_{ij}^{lin}(s=l)=\sigma_{ij}^{moy}+\sigma_{ij}^{fle}
$$

One denotes by $\sigma_A$ and $\sigma_B$ the mechanical stresses associated with the two stabilised states of the situation and by $\sigma_{ther}(t)$ the thermal transient associated with this situation. One then has,

$$
\sigma_{A^{lin}}-\sigma_{B^{lin}}=(F_{XA}-F_{XB})\,\sigma^{F_X}+(F_{YA}-F_{YB})\,\sigma^{F_Y}+\ldots+(M_{ZA}-M_{ZB})\,\sigma^{M_Z}+(P_A-P_B)\,\sigma^{P}
$$

Depending on the method of selection of the time instants chosen (see r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants), one obtains the amplitude Sn.

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', $t^{maxSn}$ and $t^{minSn}$ being the extremal time instants of this transient as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants, the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$$
S_n=\max\left(\left\lVert\sigma_{A^{lin}}-\sigma_{B^{lin}}+\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{maxSn})-\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{minSn})\right\rVert,\left\lVert\sigma_{B^{lin}}-\sigma_{A^{lin}}+\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{maxSn})-\sigma_{tran}^{lin}(t^{minSn})\right\rVert\right)
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$S_n=\max\left(S_{n1},S_{n2}\right)$ ,

with $S_{n1}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_{A^{lin}}-\sigma_{B^{lin}}+\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_2)\right\rVert$

and $S_{n2}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_{B^{lin}}-\sigma_{A^{lin}}+\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_2)\right\rVert$ .

S_{nB}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_{q^{lin}}-\sigma_{p^{lin}}+\sigma_{tran,p}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran,q}^{lin}(t_2)\right\rVert
$$

#### 8.3.2 Computation of Sp

One denotes by $\sigma_p$ (respectively $\sigma_q$ ) the mechanical stresses associated with a stabilised state of situation p (respectively of situation q).

One denotes by $\sigma_{tran,p}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with situation p and by $\sigma_{tran,q}(t)$ the transient tensor associated with situation q. $t_{p^{maxSn}}$ and $t_{p^{minSn}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of situation p and $t_{q^{maxSn}}$ and $t_{q^{minSn}}$ the extremal time instants of the transient of situation q as defined in r7.04.03-methode-selection-instants .

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameters $S_{p^1}$ and $S_{p^2}$ for the combination of situations p and q are defined by:

$$
S_{p^{1}}(p,q)=\max\left(S_{pA},S_{pB}\right)
$$

$$
S_{p^{2}}(p,q)=\min\left(S_{pA},S_{pB}\right)
$$

By maximising over the four possible combinations of stabilised states $(\sigma_p,\sigma_q)$ ,

$$
S_{pA}=\max\left(\left\lVert\sigma_p-\sigma_q+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{p^{maxSp})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_q^{minSp}})\right\rVert,\left\lVert\sigma_q-\sigma_p+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{p^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_q^{minSp})\right\rVert\right)
$$

that is 8 possibilities.

$$
S_{pB}=\max\left(\left\lVert\sigma_p-\sigma_q+\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{q^{maxSp})-\sigma_{tran,p}(t_p^{minSp}})\right\rVert,\left\lVert\sigma_q-\sigma_p+\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{q^{maxSp}})-\sigma_{tran,p}(t_p^{minSp})\right\rVert\right)
$$

that is 8 possibilities.

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_{p^1}$ for the combination of situations p and q is defined by:

$$
S_{p^{1}}(p,q)=\max\left(S_{pA},S_{pB}\right)
$$

with

$$
S_{pA}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_p-\sigma_q+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_2)\right\rVert
$$

$$
S_{pB}=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma_q-\sigma_p+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_1)-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_2)\right\rVert
$$

If $t_{1^p}$ and $t_{1^q}$ are the time instants of fictitious transient 1 $S_{p^1}(p,q)$ , then one determines the time instants of fictitious transient 2 $t_{2^p}$ and $t_{2^q}$ according to the method described in Appendix 2 and the quantity $S_{p^2}(p,q)$ is equal, by maximising over the four possible combinations of stabilised states $(\sigma_p,\sigma_q)$ , to:

$$
S_{p^{2}}(p,q)=\max\left(\left\lVert\sigma_q-\sigma_p+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{2^{p}})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{2^{q}})\right\rVert,\left\lVert\sigma_p-\sigma_q+\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{2^{p}})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{2^{q}})\right\rVert\right)
$$

**Note**

In this part, $\sigma_{tran}(t)=\sigma_{ther}(t)$ .

#### 8.3.3 Computation of $Sp^{meca}$

One denotes by $\sigma_{p1}$ (respectively $\sigma_{q1}$ ) the mechanical stresses associated with the stabilised states of situation p and of situation q which have maximised the quantity $S_{p^1}$ . One denotes by $\sigma_{p^2}$ (respectively $\sigma_{q^2}$ ) the mechanical stresses associated with the stabilised states which have maximised the quantity $S_{p^2}$ .

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA' and 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST' :

$$
S_{p^{meca,1}}(p,q)=\left\lVert\sigma_{p1}-\sigma_{q1}\right\rVert
\quad\text{et}\quad
S_{p^{meca,2}}(p,q)=\left\lVert\sigma_{p2}-\sigma_{q2}\right\rVert
$$

## 9 Appendix 2: B3200 equations for situations in unit form with interpolation on the temperature

Each situation is defined by two stabilised mechanical states A and B. Each state is described from a pressure $p$ and a force resultant $\left\{F_X,F_Y,F_Z,M_X,M_Y,M_Z\right\}$ defined under the keyword 'CHAR_MECA' and corresponds to a temperature (TEMP_A or TEMP_B). *In this example, the situations have no pressure loading.*

The user must also enter the temperature profile as a function of time in the course of the situation (keyword 'TABL_TEMP' under the factor keyword ' SITUATION' )

The stress tensors are then reconstructed by linear interpolation from this temperature as a function of time and from the two resultants. The use of this option requires the prior computation of the stress fields for the 6 unit loadings ('RESU_MECA_UNIT') and of the stress fields for each of the thermal transients ('RESU_THER').

For the moment, the computation of PM_PB is not available if the data of the situations are in unit form with interpolation on the temperature.

### 9.1 Computation of Sn for a situation

One denotes by $\sigma_{A^{mom,lin}}$ and $\sigma_{B^{mom,lin}}$ the linearised mechanical stresses associated with the moments of the two stabilised states of the situation and by $\sigma_{ther}(t)$ the thermal transient associated with this situation. One then has,

$$
\sigma_{A^{mom,lin}}(T=TEMP_A)=F_{XA}\,\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_{YA}\,\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+F_{ZA}\,\sigma^{lin,F_Z}+M_{XA}\,\sigma^{M_X,lin}+M_{YA}\,\sigma^{M_Y,lin}+M_{ZA}\,\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

$$
\sigma_{B^{mom,lin}}(T=TEMP_B)=F_{XB}\,\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_{YB}\,\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+F_{ZB}\,\sigma^{lin,F_Z}+M_{XB}\,\sigma^{M_X,lin}+M_{YB}\,\sigma^{M_Y,lin}+M_{ZB}\,\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

At the time instants $t_1$ and $t_2$ , one has $T(t_1)=TEMP_1$ and $T(t_2)=TEMP_2$ . If $TEMP_A < TEMP_B$ , by interpolation one has

$$
F_X(t_1)=\frac{F_{XB}-F_{XA}}{TEMP_B-TEMP_A}\cdot TEMP_1+\frac{F_{XA}TEMP_B-F_{XB}TEMP_A}{TEMP_B-TEMP_A}
$$

$$
F_X(t_2)=\frac{F_{XB}-F_{XA}}{TEMP_B-TEMP_A}\cdot TEMP_2+\frac{F_{XA}TEMP_B-F_{XB}TEMP_A}{TEMP_B-TEMP_A}
$$

The equations are similar for the five other components. One deduces from them the expression of the linearised mechanical stresses due to the moments at the time instants $t_1$ and $t_2$ .

$$
\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_1)=F_X(t_1)\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_Y(t_1)\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+\ldots+M_Z(t_1)\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

$$
\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_2)=F_X(t_2)\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_Y(t_2)\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+\ldots+M_Z(t_2)\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$$
S_n=\max_{t_1,t_2}\left\lVert\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_1)-\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_2)+\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_1)-\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_2)\right\rVert
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TRESCA', the parameter $S_n$ for the situation is defined by:

$$
S_n=\left\lVert\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_{maxSn})-\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_{minSn})+\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_{maxSn})-\sigma_{ther}^{lin}(t_{minSn})\right\rVert
$$

### 9.2 Computations carried out with the option ‘FATIGUE’

#### 9.2.1 Computation of Sn for a combination of situations p and q

One denotes by $\sigma_{Ap}^{mom,lin}$ and $\sigma_{Bp}^{mom,lin}$ the linearised mechanical stresses associated with the moments of the two stabilised states of situation p and by $\sigma_{ther,p}$ the thermal transient associated with this situation.

One denotes by $\sigma_{Aq}^{mom,lin}$ and $\sigma_{Bq}^{mom,lin}$ the linearised mechanical stresses associated with the moments of the two stabilised states of situation q and by $\sigma_{ther,q}$ the thermal transient associated with this situation.

For the time instant $t_p$ belonging to $\sigma_{ther,p}$ , one has $T(t_p)=TEMP_p$ and if $TEMP_{Ap}<TEMP_{Bp}$ , by interpolation one has

$$
F_X(t_p)=\frac{F_{XBp}-F_{XAp}}{TEMP_{Bp}-TEMP_{Ap}}\cdot TEMP_p+\frac{F_{XAp}TEMP_{Bp}-F_{XBp}TEMP_{Ap}}{TEMP_{Bp}-TEMP_{Ap}}
$$

For the time instant $t_q$ belonging to $\sigma_{ther,q}$ , one has $T(t_q)=TEMP_q$ and if $TEMP_{Aq}<TEMP_{Bq}$ , by interpolation one has

$$
F_X(t_q)=\frac{F_{XBq}-F_{XAq}}{TEMP_{Bq}-TEMP_{Aq}}\cdot TEMP_q+\frac{F_{XAq}TEMP_{Bq}-F_{XBq}TEMP_{Aq}}{TEMP_{Bq}-TEMP_{Aq}}
$$

The equations are similar for the five other components. One deduces from them the expression of the linearised mechanical stresses due to the moments at the time instants $t_p$ and $t_q$ .

$$
\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_p)=F_X(t_p)\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_Y(t_p)\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+\ldots+M_Z(t_p)\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

$$
\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_q)=F_X(t_q)\sigma^{lin,F_X}+F_Y(t_q)\sigma^{lin,F_Y}+\ldots+M_Z(t_q)\sigma^{M_Z,lin}
$$

With 'METHODE' = 'TOUT_INST', the parameter $S_n$ for the combination of situations p and q is:

$$
S_n=\max_{t_p,t_q}\left\lVert\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_p)-\sigma^{mom,lin}(t_q)+\sigma_{ther,p}^{lin}(t_p)-\sigma_{ther,q}^{lin}(t_q)\right\rVert
$$

#### 9.2.2 Computation of Sp for a combination of situations p and q

One denotes by $\sigma_{Ap}^{mom,lin}$ and $\sigma_{Bp}^{mom,lin}$ the mechanical stresses associated with the moments of the two stabilised states of situation p and by $\sigma_{ther,p}$ the thermal transient associated with this situation.

One denotes by $\sigma_{Aq}^{mom,lin}$ and $\sigma_{Bq}^{mom,lin}$ the mechanical stresses associated with the moments of the two stabilised states of situation p and by $\sigma_{ther,q}$ the thermal transient associated with this situation.

For the time instant $t_p$ belonging to $\sigma_{ther,p}$ , one has $T(t_p)=TEMP_p$ and if $TEMP_{Ap}<TEMP_{Bp}$ , by interpolation one has

$$

For the time instant $t_q$ belonging to $\sigma_{ther,q}$ , one has $T(t_q)=TEMP_q$ and if $TEMP_{Aq}<TEMP_{Bq}$ , by interpolation one has

$$
F_X(t_q)=\frac{F_{XBq}-F_{XAq}}{TEMP_{Bq}-TEMP_{Aq}}\cdot TEMP_q+\frac{F_{XAq}TEMP_{Bq}-F_{XBq}TEMP_{Aq}}{TEMP_{Bq}-TEMP_{Aq}}
$$

The equations are similar for the five other components. One deduces from them the expression of the mechanical stresses due to the moments at the time instants tp and tq.

$$
\sigma^{mom}(t_p)=F_X(t_p)\sigma^{F_X}+F_Y(t_p)\sigma^{F_Y}+\ldots+M_Z(t_p)\sigma^{M_Z}

## 10 Appendix 3: Equations for a piping junction (branch connection)

### 10.1 Type ’B3200’

*Situation of unit type*

One denotes by $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ the mechanical stresses associated with the two stabilised states of the situation. Only the definitions of $\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_2$ change with respect to the case of a single set of external resultants.

For a component, each stabilised mechanical state is described from a pressure $p$ and a force resultant $\left\{F_X,F_Y,F_Z,M_X,M_Y,M_Z\right\}$ defined under the keyword 'CHAR_MECA'. For a piping junction, one supplies two force resultants:

$$

$$

$$
\left\{F_{X,tubu},F_{Y,tubu},F_{Z,tubu},M_{X,tubu},M_{Y,tubu},M_{Z,tubu}\right\}
$$

The stress tensors are reconstructed by linear combination from the stress tensors associated with each of the unit loadings. For example, one denotes by $\sigma=F_{X,tubu}$ the stress tensor associated with the unit force loading in the direction x for the nozzle. The computation of the stress tensor corresponding to a mechanical loading belonging to a stabilised state is then obtained in the following way:

$$
\sigma=F_{X,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{F_{X,tubu}}+F_{Y,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{F_{Y,tubu}}+F_{Z,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{F_{Z,tubu}}+M_{X,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{M_{X,tubu}}+M_{Y,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{M_{Y,tubu}}+M_{Z,tubu}\cdot\sigma^{M_{Z,tubu}}+F_{X,corp}\cdot\sigma^{F_{X,corp}}+F_{Y,corp}\cdot\sigma^{F_{Y,corp}}+F_{Z,corp}\cdot\sigma^{F_{Z,corp}}+M_{X,corp}\cdot\sigma^{M_{X,corp}}+M_{Y,corp}\cdot\sigma^{M_{Y,corp}}+M_{Z,corp}\cdot\sigma^{M_{Z,corp}}+P\cdot\sigma^{P}
$$

The computation of the quantities $S_n$ and $S_p$ is then identical to that for a single component.

*Situation of instantaneous type*

For a piping junction, one must enter under RESU_MECA the tensor sum of the tensors of the body and of the nozzle.

$\sigma_{meca}(t)=\sigma_{meca,corp}(t)+\sigma_{meca,tubu}(t)$

The computation of the quantities $S_n$ and $S_p$ is then identical to that for a single component.

## 12 Appendix 5: Method of computation of fictitious transient 2 with ’TOUT_INST’

One denotes by $\sigma_{tran,p}$ the tensor sum of the transients associated with situation p and by $\sigma_{tran,q}$ the tensor sum of the transients associated with situation q.

One notes: if $t_{1^p}$ and $t_{1^q}$ are the time instants of fictitious transient 1 $S_{p^1}(p,q)$ , then one determines the time instants of fictitious transient 2 $t_{2^p}$ and $t_{2^q}$.

One looks for $t_{2^p}$ which maximises the quantity $S_{pA}$ such that

$$
S_{pA}=\max_{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{1^p})-\sigma_{tran,p}(t)\right\rVert=\left\lVert\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{1^p})-\sigma_{tran,p}(t_{2^p})\right\rVert
$$

One looks for $t_{2^q}$ which maximises the quantity $S_{pB}$ such that

$$
S_{pB}=\max_{t}\left\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{1^q})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t)\right\rVert=\left\lVert\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{1^q})-\sigma_{tran,q}(t_{2^q})\right\rVert
$$

The time instants $t_{2^p}$ and $t_{2^q}$ are then used for the computation of the quantity $S_{p^2}(p,q)$ whatever the TYPE_RESU_MECA chosen ( ZE200a, ZE200b, B3200 ).
