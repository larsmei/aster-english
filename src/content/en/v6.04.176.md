# v6.04.176 SSNV176 – Law Identification ENDO_ORTH_BETON

**Summary:**

The ENDO_ORTH_BETON law tests are presented here on a single element to identify the model parameters. Since there is no empirical formula for calibrating the parameters, the user can use some of the test cases presented here to adjust his parameters. The study of the model parameters is in the [R7.01.09](doc:r7.01.09) documentation. The 5 tests proposed are as follows:

1. single traction
2. single traction with steering
3. simple compression
4. simple compression with control
5. single traction, simple compression and a biaxial test

## 1 Reference problem

### 1.1 Geometry and boundary conditions

L的element used is a tetrahedron to a gauss point. There is therefore no problem of homogeneity of the fields in the element.

The blocking conditions and linear relationships between the nodes to be applied are summarized in [Figure 1.1-a]. The $\mathrm{N0N1}$ , $\mathrm{N0N2}$ and $\mathrm{N0N3}$ edges are of length 1.

Given the geometry of the element, blocking conditions and linear relationships, deformation is directly related to the displacements of the nodes:

${\varepsilon}_{xx}=\mathit{DX}(\mathit{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{yy}=\mathit{DY}(\mathit{N2})$

${\varepsilon}_{zz}=\mathrm{DZ}(\mathrm{N3})$

${\varepsilon}_{xy}=\mathit{DX}(\mathit{N2})=\mathit{DY}(\mathit{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{xz}=\mathit{DX}(\mathit{N3})=\mathit{DZ}(\mathit{N1})$

${\varepsilon}_{yz}=\mathit{DY}(\mathit{N3})=\mathit{DZ}(\mathit{N2})$

If we work with imposed deformation, we need only impose the appropriate nodes displacements.

If you want to work forcefully, as is the case for Modelling E, you must impose the following loads (see [Figure 1.1-a] for the definition of the $\mathrm{F1}$ , $\mathrm{F2}$ , $\mathrm{F3}$ and $\mathrm{F4}$ faces ):

${\sigma}_{xx}>0$ : $\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F1}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FX}<0$ (traction according to $x$ )

${\sigma}_{xx}<0$ : $\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F1}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FX}<0$ (compression according to $x$ )

${\sigma}_{yy}>0$ : $\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F2}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FY}<0$ (traction according to $y$ )

${\sigma}_{yy}<0$ : $\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F2}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FY}<0$ (compression according to $y$ )

${\sigma}_{zz}>0$ : $\mathrm{FZ}$ on $\mathrm{F3}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FZ}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FZ}<0$ (traction according to $z$ )

${\sigma}_{zz}<0$ : $\mathrm{FZ}$ on $\mathrm{F3}$ and $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FZ}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FZ}<0$ (compression according to $z$ )

### 1.2 Material properties

The material characteristics are identical for the 5 tests that are presented.

The elastic characteristics of the materials are as follows:

$E=32000\mathit{Mpa}$ ; $\nu =0.2$

The stress of failure in traction and compression are:

${\sigma}_{\mathit{rupture}}^{\mathit{traction}}=3.2\mathit{MPa}$ ; ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rupture}}^{\mathit{compression}}=-31.8\mathit{MPa}$

## 7 Modelling E

### 7.1 Modelling characteristics

3D modelling

Element MECA_TETRA4.

### 7.2 Mesh characteristics

Number of nodes : 4

Number of mesh cells and types : 1 TETRA4

### 7.3 Tested Features

The constitutive law ENDO_ORTH_BETON is tested here in 3 cases of loading:

1. ${U}_{1}$ : Simple Traction
2. ${U}_{2}$: Compression
3. ${U}_{3}$ :Biaxial charging (traction in direction $y$ , compression in direction $x$ , with fixed stress ratio: ${\sigma}_{yy}=-0.2{\sigma}_{xx}$

This test case allows you to check that the set of parameters chosen by the user complies with the following data:

1. tensile fracture stresses,
2. compression bursting stresses,
3. no bursting of the bursting envelope for biaxial tests. This consists of checking that the maximum tensile stress ${\sigma}_{yy}$ of the biaxial test is less than the single tensile fracture stress.

### 7.4 Loading journey

Unlike modellings A, B, C and D, it is the force, not the displacement, that is imposed here. The method of piloting the PRED_ELAS load is used, because the behavior is softening. The following loads are applied:

- ${U}_{1}$ : $\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F1}$ , $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FX}<0$ (Traction)
- ${U}_{2}$ : $\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F1}$ , $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FX}>0$ (Compression)
- ${U}_{3}$: $\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F1}$ , $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FX}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , $\mathrm{FX}>0$ (Compression on $x$ axis );

$\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F2}$ , $-1/\sqrt{3}\mathrm{FY}$ on $\mathrm{F4}$ , with $\mathit{FY}=-0,2\mathit{FX}$ (Traction on $y$ axis ).

1. $\mathit{U1}$ : ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rupture}}^{\mathit{traction}}=3.20684\mathit{MPa}$
2. $\mathrm{U2}$ : ${\sigma}_{\mathit{rupture}}^{\mathit{compression}}=-31.8\mathit{MPa}$
3. $\mathrm{U3}$ : ${\sigma}_{\mathit{U3}}^{\mathit{traction}}=-0.2{\sigma}_{\mathit{U3}}^{\mathit{compression}}=0.2\times 14.2038\mathit{Mpa}$ et ${\sigma}_{\mathit{U3}}^{\mathit{traction}}<{\sigma}_{\mathit{rupture}}^{\mathit{traction}}$

**Warning 1**: The number of time step may not be sufficient to reach the softening phase. Therefore, the user will verify that for $\mathit{U1}$ and $\mathrm{U2}$ calculations, the $\mathrm{U3}$ calculation is subject to an additional warning (see **Warning 2** ), it is well in the softening phase (decrease of the steering parameter). The maximum absolute stress should not be reached for the last time step. Otherwise, the calculation should continue until the softening phase.

**Warning2**: For some sets of parameters, it is possible to observe convergence difficulties for $\mathrm{U3}$ calculation during the softening phase. Indeed, the constitutive law ensures the existence and uniqueness of the imposed deformation solution, but not in imposed force. These convergence problems only appear in the softening phase, the user can consider the highest value of the control parameter reached, equal to the greatest compression stress

## 8 Summary of the results

The objective of the modellings presented in this document is to identify the parameters of the ENDO_ORTH_BETON law. As there is no empirical formula for the values of the parameters to be used, the user will have to calibrate his parameters step by step on the various tests proposed. The method for calibrating the parameters, which is contained in the [R7.01.09](doc:r7.01.09) document, can be summarized as follows:

1. $\mathrm{ALPHA}$ choice: (0.85 to 0.9),
2. Calibration of $\mathrm{K0}$ , $\mathrm{ECROB}$ on modellings A, C or E (calculation $\mathrm{U1}$ ). Once these parameters are calibrated, they should not be changed in the calibration phase of the other parameters,
3. $\mathrm{K1}$ , $\mathrm{K2}$ and $\mathrm{ECROD}$ calibration on modellings B (or D) and E. In fact, E Modelling ($\mathrm{U2}$ and $\mathrm{U3}$ calculations) is sufficient. It allows to verify the value of the single compression break stress, and to ensure that the break envelope for biaxial tests does not inflate. It is not necessary to calibrate the $\mathit{K2}$ parameter in a very fine way because it results from a qualitative argument, and no experimental data is ever available to identify it.
